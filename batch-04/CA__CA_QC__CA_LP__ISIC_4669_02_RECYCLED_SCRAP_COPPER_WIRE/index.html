Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada has operated without exchange controls since the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, so neither the Bank of Canada nor any other agency must approve profit remittance, capital repatriation, or currency conversion involving the CAD. For the Quebec Limited Partnership, the practical hurdles are limited to: (1) Part I income tax on each non-resident partner's share of the partnership's Canadian-source business income, because the LP is a permanent establishment of each partner for tax-treaty purposes; (2) Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced) on payments that are in substance interest, royalties, or management fees; (3) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting by the bank for any cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more received or sent within a 24-hour period; and (4) bank-side source-of-funds and source-of-wealth verification. There is no Quebec-specific convertibility or repatriation restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds. The LP may retain USD or EUR proceeds in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely and convert at the prevailing market rate. Mandatory non-surrender obligations remain: (1) export reporting to the CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System for commercial shipments, including HS 7404.00 copper waste and scrap; (2) bank-filed FINTRAC EFT reports for international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within 24 hours; and (3) proper accounting for foreign-exchange gains and losses on the LP's Canadian-dollar books. Exports of HS 7404.00 scrap are not on Canada's Export Control List, so no Global Affairs Canada permit is required except where the goods or destination are sanctioned.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control regulator. The central bank operates monetary policy, currency issuance, the Retail Payment Activities Act registration function, and payment-system oversight, while the Department of Finance and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administer cross-border fund-transfer reporting and AML/CTF obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM, strictly conditional. A foreign or offshore EMI (for example, Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or MultiPass) may lawfully move CAD on behalf of the Quebec LP only if the EMI (1) is a registered money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, (2) holds the Quebec money-services business licence of the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) where it carries on business in Quebec, and (3) holds a payment service provider (PSP) registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act if it provides open-loop payment functions. No offshore EMI can issue or confirm documentary letters of credit or provide true escrow-grade clearing for HS 7404.00 scrap cargoes. The EMI may function only as a supplementary FX/liquidity gateway; the statutory LC, escrow, and CAD-clearing rails must remain with a Schedule I bank or a licensed Canadian credit union for this trade profile.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No Bank of Canada or CRA authorization is required.
