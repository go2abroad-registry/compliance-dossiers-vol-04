Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons), operating under the Federal Commercial Register Authority (Federal Office of Justice); final registration is published through the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht), Art. 620 et seq., specifically Art. 621 (Content of Articles), Art. 626 (Minimum capital), Art. 632 (Registered share capital), Art. 634 (Payment of contributions), Art. 635 (Validity of registration), Art. 716 and 716a (Non-transferable board powers), Art. 717 (Duty of diligence); also Federal Act on Commercial Register Registration (e.g. Art. 15 of the Ordinance on the Commercial Register).

Formation / constitutional filings

Public Deed of Incorporation including the Articles of Association (Statuts / Statuten), executed before a Swiss notary public, followed by an application for registration on Form ZH/Commercial Register extraction.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum registered share capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be effectively paid-in before registration, deposited in cash with a Swiss bank and subject to a blocking/confirmation obligation until the Commercial Register proceeds. Share capital contributions exceeding paid-in minimum may be made in non-cash assets under Art. 628 CO; the deed must fully establish payment and blocking certificates.

Funding & unlock path: For office-only use (no factory or sound stage): obtain local fire safety confirmation from the municipal fire inspector (based on building code VKF/AEAI), ensure structural load capacity for installed computer racks/servers, and carry out the occupational health checklist under SUVA (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund) requiring ergonomic workstation assessment, first-aid provisions, and fire extinguisher compliance. If premises exceed standard office worker capacities, cantonal labour law clearance may apply under Art. 20 of the Swiss Labour Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss tax law does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. The AG is inherently governed by entity-level taxation at the federal, cantonal and communal levels. Relief mechanisms include the tax-neutral reorganisation provisions (VU 2020) and the possibility of obtaining an advance tax ruling (Vorbescheid / ruling fiscal) from the competent cantonal tax authority to confirm application of tax laws to specific facts. Switzerland also implements ATAD-driven hybrid mismatch rules preventing double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion; no elective transparency regime exists for AGs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover from supplies and own-use (for resident legal entities; non-established suppliers are generally subject to VAT registration without a threshold if their supplies are taxable in Switzerland and no exemption applies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods, zero-rating applies when goods are physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination and the export is evidenced by a confirmed customs export declaration (AUS-2 / redevance d'exportation). For intellectual and digital services (e.g., royalty-free stock music licensing), Swiss VAT is not chargeable when the supply is deemed to take place outside Switzerland: in B2B transactions at the recipient's business establishment and in B2C transactions at the consumer's habitual residence. Such services are 'zero-rated' or outside Swiss VAT scope with the supplier entitled to input VAT deduction where connected to exported supplies. The supplier must obtain and retain adequate documentary evidence of the recipient's foreign location, including recipient VAT number, IP/geolocation data, client contracts, invoices and payment/bank settlement records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (AUS-2)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing transport to foreign territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Foreign Currency / payment proof) evidencing actual international payment flow

Switzerland does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. B2B e-invoicing is not mandatory in the private sector, but electronic invoices are accepted if they comply with the Swiss VAT Act requirements on authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility. Mandatory e-invoicing (Peppol BIS 3.0) applies for invoicing the federal administration under public procurement rules. For all Swiss payment slips, the QR-bill format is mandatory, requiring a QR reference or ISO 11649 reference, but this does not constitute fiscal clearance.

The FTA may retroactively reclaim the zero rating and disallow input VAT deduction if the exporter fails to provide conclusive proof of export, recipient foreign status, or receipt of proceeds within the expected commercial timeframe. Interest runs from the original due date of the VAT. Intentional VAT evasion is subject to a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; negligent tax shortfall is fined up to 10% of the tax omitted. Fraudulent or unnecessary claim of export zero-rating or refunds also triggers criminal prosecution and clawback of the full beneficial amount plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Switzerland maintains an open capital account with no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements, and no restrictions on the free movement of capital or services under the Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital and the Free Movement of Services. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including dividend distributions, loan repayments, royalty payments, and equity injections, may be executed freely in any convertible currency. Cross-border profit remittance is subject only to ordinary corporate tax settlement and, for dividends only, Swiss anticipatory withholding tax at 35%, which may be reduced under the applicable double tax treaty. Royalty and licence fees paid to non-residents are generally not subject to Swiss withholding tax. The practical impediments to convertibility are therefore confined to bank-level anti-money-laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) source-of-funds verification, not to foreign-exchange permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation requirement for export receipts. The Swiss AG may retain foreign-currency earnings offshore or hold them on Swiss bank accounts in any denomination without conversion duties. The only statutory burden is statistical: Swiss residents holding cross-border assets, liabilities, or payment flows may be required to report balance-of-payments and direct investment data to the SNB, typically triggered by SNB surveys or where aggregate cross-border transactions exceed approximately CHF 1 million in a reporting period or where foreign direct investment participation exceeds 10% of the AG's share capital. These reporting duties are analytical and non-restrictive; they do not require any surrender or conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, acting in coordination with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary treasury rail; Low as the primary transactional account. Swiss-licensed fintech banks and neo-banks offer partially digital onboarding, but for a non-resident-owned AG with foreign directors, enhanced due diligence and physical or notarised identification are generally still required. Offshore digital EMI licenses (UK FCA e-money, EU PSD2 e-money institutions) are lawful to use as collection conduits but are not supervised by FINMA, do not qualify as 'banks' for Swiss deposit-protection or withholding-tax purposes, and may cause acceptance friction with Swiss correspondent banks on inbound wires. The recommended architecture is a FINMA-supervised Tier-1 Swiss bank as the principal treasury account, with Stripe, PayPal, or an offshore EMI strictly limited to marketplace settlement collection and swept daily or weekly into the CHF operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A compliant capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the shareholder's bank to the AG's Swiss Capital Escrow Account in CHF, EUR, or USD. The payment reference must identify the purpose as paid-in share capital (Aktienkapital) using the appropriate Swiss payment reason code for equity contribution. The receiving Swiss bank will require a statutory declaration of source of funds; where the shareholder is a legal entity, it must provide certified corporate resolutions, register extracts, and authority documents demonstrating the signatory power for the injection. No exchange-control authorisation is required. However, the transaction may appear in SNB balance-of-payments and direct investment reporting where the foreign participation exceeds 10% of the AG's share capital or the transaction exceeds the SNB's statistical thresholds (commonly CHF 1 million). For capital contributions above CHF 1 million, the Swiss bank may additionally require an updated Form A, the notarial deed covering the share capital increase, and confirmation that the minimum paid-in capital requirement has been satisfied.Outbound settlements use the Swiss SIC retail payment system and SEPA credit-transfer rails for domestic and euro-area payments; SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with full structured payment details for high-value international settlements; SWIFT MT202/COV for correspondent-bank cover transfers where interbank clearing is required; and documentary instruments such as irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 and documentary collections where physical distribution of audio products, print-on-demand merchandise, or master-licence deliverables requires proof of payment. The AG should maintain an EUR-denominated account at the Swiss bank or a SEPA member institution to avoid conversion friction on marketplace payouts, and should configure automated daily sweeps from Stripe and PayPal settlement balances into the CHF treasury account. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Ordinary (full) audit is required if the AG exceeds two of three thresholds for two consecutive fiscal years: balance-sheet total above CHF 20 million; net turnover above CHF 40 million; annual average more than 250 full-time employees. Listed companies, issuers of debt instruments and regulated financial intermediaries are always subject to ordinary audit. Other companies are subject to limited audit unless they validly opt out with unanimous shareholder consent and no more than 10 employees; a royalty-free stock-music licensing AG normally qualifies for the opting-out exemption.
