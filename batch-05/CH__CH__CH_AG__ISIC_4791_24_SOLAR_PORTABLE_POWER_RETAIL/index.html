Minimum total share capital is CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 of that amount must be fully paid-up in cash before registration. The paid-in capital must be blocked with a Swiss bank in a dedicated capital-blocking account until the Commercial Register enters the company. In-kind contributions are subject to a review and must be disclosed in the notarial deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for the registered premises, warehouse, and any occupied office, including compliance with cantonal fire protection norms (VKF guidelines) and installation of approved fire extinguishers and smoke alarms. Occupational clearance under Swiss labour law requires a working environment memorandum, accident prevention measures pursuant to the Swiss Accident Insurance Law (UVG), and, if employees are engaged, registration with the SUVA-appointed prevention institution. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC) VAT Division and Corporate Tax Division, Bern; official e-portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch (e-services for VAT registration, UID register, and e-dec customs). Cantonal and municipal tax authorities (e.g., Tax Office of Canton Zug) administer direct corporate income and capital taxes for the registered seat of the AG.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft with a Swiss registered office (minimum share capital CHF 100,000, at least 20% paid-up, minimum CHF 50,000) and obtain entry in the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister) with notarised articles of association. Step 2: The Federal Statistical Office automatically allocates the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) in format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX; verify the UID entry via https://www.uid.admin.ch. Step 3: Register for Swiss VAT online via the ESTV e-service using the VAT registration form (Art. 61 VAT Act) once annual worldwide taxable turnover from Swiss supplies exceeds CHF 100,000; foreign entities without a Swiss domicile may register directly online since 1 January 2018 and must designate a Swiss service address (a fiscal representative is optional unless required by the FTA). Step 4: The FTA issues the VAT/UID number with suffix MWST/TVA/IVA (e.g., CHE-116.033.548 MWST) and sets the tax period, generally quarterly; simultaneously apply for spontaneous registration with the Cantonal Tax Office for income and capital tax purposes, which issues a separate cantonal tax account/reference. Step 5: Obtain e-dec customs access codes from the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (BAZG/FOCBS) to clear export shipments electronically and retain compliance files for the statutory 10-year retention period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: a Swiss-resident AG is fully taxable on worldwide income at federal, cantonal and municipal levels; a non-resident AG is limitedly taxable on Swiss-source income only. The AG is a separate corporate taxpayer; there is no pass-through or check-the-box classification. No general tax-exempt zone applies; Zug offers a competitive combined effective corporate rate of approximately 14.6% including the federal 8.5% headline statutory rate (federal corporate tax effective at approximately 7.83% after deduction and Zug cantonal/capital taxes). Profits from online retail of solar portable power goods are ordinary business income taxed at entity level; capital gains, dividend and IP income may benefit from participation relief or the patent box.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box elections exist under Swiss law; tax classification is determined by legal form. Available elections and reliefs: (i) participation exemption election for dividends and capital gains on qualifying shareholdings (holding of at least 10% of share capital or market value of at least CHF 1 million); (ii) optional patent box under the 2020 Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF), applying up to 90% reduction of net qualifying intellectual property income under the OECD modified nexus approach; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) on excess high-risk equity available in certain cantons including Zug; (iv) election to form a VAT group (fiscal unity) under Art. 13(4)(a) VAT Act for closely controlled entities; (v) no hybrid mismatch election exists, but Switzerland applies its own qualification rules, the modified nexus concept, and respects the OECD BEPS reports; the former mixed/domicile company status was abolished as of 1 January 2020 and is no longer electable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies made in Switzerland (Art. 10(2)(a) VAT Act). Domestic enterprises must register when taxable supplies exceed CHF 100,000; foreign suppliers of goods via distance selling to non-VAT-registered Swiss recipients must register once the CHF 100,000 threshold is exceeded; foreign suppliers of digital/electronic services to Swiss private consumers are also mandatorily registered above CHF 100,000; where the Swiss customer is a VAT-registered business, the reverse charge mechanism eliminates the supplier registration requirement. Voluntary registration below the threshold is possible only in limited circumstances and requires prior FTA approval.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate with full input VAT deduction applies to: (1) supplies of goods physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination where the goods leave Swiss customs territory within 12 months (or 5 years in authorised processing situations) under Art. 23(1)(a) VAT Act and Art. 8 VAT Ordinance; direct online retail of portable battery generators and solar panels qualifies when the goods are dispatched from a Swiss fulfillment centre to a foreign customer and the export movement is evidenced by customs export declaration and transport/tracking documentation; (2) services supplied to foreign recipients or services performed abroad (including software, digital content and remote professional services) under Art. 8(1) VAT Act, which are zero-rated and taxable in the destination country; the Swiss supplier must document the customer residence, contract, delivery method and payment trace. Bundled goods-in-transit and e-commerce marketplace transactions require clear consignment and title documentation to support the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / e-dec summary or Passar filing) evidencing departure of the goods from Swiss customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and for postal e-commerce parcels the CN22/CN23 dispatch declaration and carrier tracking receipt proving foreign delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank account statement evidencing that full export proceeds were credited to the AG's Swiss bank account within 180 days

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping or mandatory digital stamping applies in Switzerland. E-invoicing is mandatory for supplies to the Swiss federal government (B2G) using ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, SIX eBill or EDI; B2B/B2C e-invoicing is voluntary. Every invoice issued by a Swiss VAT taxpayer must include the UID/MWST number, issuer and recipient identifiers, date, goods description, and VAT rate; invoices to Swiss residents must quote a Swiss QR-bill (QR-IBAN) for payment. Export shipments must be declared electronically through the BAZG/FOCBS e-dec or Passar customs platform, including CN22/CN23 declarations for postal export parcels; digital records and substantiation must be retained for 10 years under Art. 85(2)(d) VAT Act.

Swiss law does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day time limit is the standard banking, Letters of Credit and Export Risk Guarantee (SERV) benchmark and is applied by the FTA as a documentary completeness expectation for export substantiation. Failure to prove the physical export or recovery of sales proceeds triggers retrospective clawback: the 0% rating is reversed, Swiss VAT at 8.1% is assessed on the full consideration, default interest of 4.5% per annum runs from the original VAT due date, and input VAT credits are recovered proportionally. Legal penalties under Arts. 94-97 MWSTG: regulatory fines up to CHF 8,000 for procedural and record-keeping breaches; tax evasion fines of up to four times the VAT evaded (Art. 96); qualified evasion involving falsified customs or banking documents (Art. 97) is subject to imprisonment of up to two years (five years in commercial or repeated cases) in addition to fines. The FTA and BAZG/FOCBS routinely scrutinise e-commerce export documentation, compare delivery records with export declarations, and apply a 10-year limitation period for concealment together with a 10-year statutory record-retention requirement under Art. 85 VAT Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has been an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction since 1992 and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Swiss franc is freely floated and thousands of fully tradable with no exchange-control licence, prior approval requirement, or repatriation restriction for dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Profit-remittance hurdles are purely fiscal rather than regulatory: a 35% withholding tax on dividends applies and may be reduced or eliminated under applicable double-taxation treaties; standard corporate income tax obligations also apply. Banks must report external assets and liabilities to the SNB for monetary statistics and must comply with AMLA, FinSA, and FINMA due-diligence rules, but no capital-export permit or motive test exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender duty, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline under Swiss law. Export receipts in Swiss francs or foreign currency may be retained abroad indefinitely without SNB consent. The Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (WZG) grants only emergency protective powers for exceptional crises, and such powers are not currently activated. Ordinary trade payments require no export licence, no percentage conversion, and no central-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse) is the central monetary authority of Switzerland. The Federal Council, the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) hold residual external-financial policy powers; the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) administers embargoes and financial sanctions relevant to cross-border wire clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-B (conditional). Switzerland is not an EU or EEA member state, so an EU or UK EMI licence does not passport into Switzerland. A foreign digital EMI cannot act as the company's principal deposit-taking treasury bank without a FINMA licence or a binding Swiss partner-bank arrangement. Legally viable models: (i) a FINMA fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (minimum capital CHF 300,000, public-deposit ceiling CHF 100 million, no deposit-taking beyond the ceiling); (ii) a white-label settlement structure in which a Swiss SIC-member bank provides the IBAN and settlement layer while the EMI supplies the user interface and FX execution; or (iii) use of a foreign merchant acquirer or payments facilitator such as Stripe, Airwallex, or Adyen purely as settlement facilitator, with treasury accounts maintained at a tier-1 Swiss bank. Key constraints: the EMI must not solicit Swiss clients without compliance under FinSA and AMLA; all funds reaching the Swiss AG remain subject to AML source-of-funds review; crypto-origin capital is generally rejected by tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must arrive via a compliant SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit from a regulated bank into the company's Swiss capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) before the auditor's liberation confirmation is issued. No SNB permit or FX quota applies, but the bank requires a clear structured remittance purpose text, for example 'Non-commercial capital contribution - paid-up share capital (Liberierung) in favour of CH_AG, UID CHE-xxx', supported by the notarised incorporation resolution and capital-increase minutes. A shareholder loan must be documented by a written arm's-length loan agreement and remitted with the purpose text 'Shareholder loan agreement dated [x] - interest-bearing, non-commercial' to prevent re-characterisation as equity. The escrow is released only upon the auditor's written confirmation of liberation and the subsequent Commercial Register entry. The payment origin must be a bank account held in the name of the ultimate beneficial owner or the contributing parent company; cryptocurrency balances and unlicensed PSP funds are not acceptable. Amounts above CHF 15,000 equivalent trigger AML identification obligations, and larger inflows are captured by SNB monthly external-position statistics without prior approval.Permitted clearing rails are: (1) CHF domestic payments and instant SIC5 (24/7 ISO 20022 pain.001) for local supplier settlements; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and SCT Inst for EUR-denominated European payments; (3) SWIFT MT103 with BIC and IBAN for USD and other cross-border supplier wires executed through the bank's correspondent network, including Fedwire and CHIPS for USD; (4) a documentary trade cycle via SWIFT MT700 (letters of credit) and MT752 (acceptance) under UCP 600 for solar-panel and portable-battery imports; (5) bank payment obligations (BPO) under URBPO for eligible purchase-order invoices; and (6) RMB cross-border clearing through the CIPS network where both the bank and the customer are participants, available at institutions such as UBS and ZKB. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-B (conditional). Switzerland is not an EU or EEA member state, so an EU or UK EMI licence does not passport into Switzerland. A foreign digital EMI cannot act as the company's principal deposit-taking treasury bank without a FINMA licence or a binding Swiss partner-bank arrangement. Legally viable models: (i) a FINMA fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (minimum capital CHF 300,000, public-deposit ceiling CHF 100 million, no deposit-taking beyond the ceiling); (ii) a white-label settlement structure in which a Swiss SIC-member bank provides the IBAN and settlement layer while the EMI supplies the user interface and FX execution; or (iii) use of a foreign merchant acquirer or payments facilitator such as Stripe, Airwallex, or Adyen purely as settlement facilitator, with treasury accounts maintained at a tier-1 Swiss bank. Key constraints: the EMI must not solicit Swiss clients without compliance under FinSA and AMLA; all funds reaching the Swiss AG remain subject to AML source-of-funds review; crypto-origin capital is generally rejected by tier-1 banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 1,000,000 (annual aggregate value of cross-border transactions with related parties) triggers mandatory Local File; Master File required if group turnover exceeds CHF 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a formal ESR regime, but requires economic substance for tax residency and to avoid treaty abuse. The company must have a registered office, board meetings, and actual business activities in Switzerland. Anti-abuse rules (e.g., GAAR) apply to deny treaty benefits if substance is lacking.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to CHF 250,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years; additional civil penalties of 1–3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly sign false returns; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and retroactive taxation at 35% withholding; fines up to CHF 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the sham structure; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CHF 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally prosecuted if they fail to implement AML controls; travel bans and extradition risks.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax, and interest; penalties up to 20% of the adjustment amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
