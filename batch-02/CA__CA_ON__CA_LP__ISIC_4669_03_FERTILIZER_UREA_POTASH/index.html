E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership (LP) must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Business Number Registration', or by filing Form RC3481 'Non-Resident Corporation and Non-Resident Partnership Registration'. The BN is a 9-digit number with program identifier 'RC' for GST/HST. Application may be mailed, faxed, or submitted electronically through a representative; proof of foreign registration and Ontario Limited Partnership Act registration may be required. The GST/HST account must be activated separately and requires a Canadian agent or representative if the LP has no place of business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box election regime. An Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; partners report their allocable share. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s.97(2) election to transfer property to a partnership at fair market value; (ii) s.96(3) election for a non-calendar fiscal period; (iii) election under s.96(5) for a partnership to be deemed to have a fiscal period. No hybrid mismatch 'check-the-box' election exists; the OECD hybrid mismatch rules are implemented through income tax provisions rather than elective regimes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of prilled urea and potassium chloride qualify as zero-rated under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act where (i) the seller supplies the goods by way of sale, (ii) the goods are delivered by the seller or on the seller's behalf outside Canada, (iii) the goods are exported within a reasonable time, and (iv) the seller obtains documentary evidence of export in the required form and content. Offshore services supplied to non-residents may qualify under ETA s.7 or s.8 if the services are performed exclusively outside Canada or are to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A eManifest or paper B13A as transmitted to Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical export and destination
  • Commercial Invoice and international sale contract (SPA, LOI, Powerv of Attorney notices)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank account statement evidencing proceeds credit
  • Packing list / export cargo manifest and terminal receipt
  • Proof of export timing and destination (e.g., port of exit stamps, CTMS data)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. GST/HST returns and remittances must generally be filed electronically if the registrant's threshold exceeds specified CRA e-filing requirements, but invoices remain paper/commercial. No Quebec-specific EDI requirement applies in Ontario.

CRA and CBSA may jointly audit exporters. If zero-rating is claimed without sufficient export documentation or proceeds are not repatriated within a reasonable period, HST at 13% becomes payable on the export invoice with interest from the date of zero-rated supply, and penalties may apply under ETA s.280.1 (penalty not exceeding $250 for each failure) and s.285 (false statements) where the penalty is the greater of $100 and 25% of the tax shown to be understated. Fraudulent exports carry criminal liability under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act, including prosecution, restitution, and potential clawback of any claimed input tax credits. CRA may also apply the general anti-avoidance rule if a series of transactions inappropriately creates an export zero-rating result.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada's current and capital account transactions are fully liberalized under the IMF's Article VIII commitments. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible into USD and other major currencies for capital injections, dividends, profit distributions, and repayment of intercompany debt by an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident partners. No permit, license, or central-bank approval is required for any cross-border transfer; practical restrictions arise only from AML/KYC obligations, reporting thresholds (FINTRAC for transfers ≥ CAD 10,000 or non-cash equivalents), and sanctions-compliance screening. Repatriation of profits from a fiscally transparent LP is subject to the income tax withholding regime (if any) but not to FX convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a prescribed timeline, nor any mandatory conversion ratio. Wholesale traders of urea and potash can hold funds in CAD, USD, or other currencies in operational accounts. Nevertheless, the LP must comply with (i) Statistics Canada's International Merchandise Transactions and Balance of Payments surveys for exports exceeding CAD 30,000 in any reporting period; (ii) FINTRAC reporting obligations for each cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, including a declaration of the purpose and beneficial owner; and (iii) standard documentary evidence requirements for all export invoices and bills of lading.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) pursuant to the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, in policy coordination with the Department of Finance (Minister of Finance); however, Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control administration akin to a 'SAFE'. Effective FX regulatory supervision derives from the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) over banks and money-services business registration and reporting via FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low (legal). Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are neither chartered banks nor authorized payment service providers under Canadian law. To accept CAD deposits or effect cross-border payments, a fintech/EMI must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, adhere to Canadian AML/KYC standards, and apply for an interbank settlement arrangement. In practice, most offshore EMIs cannot provide a compliant corporate account for an Ontario LP with heavy commodity trade flows; they are acceptable only for ancillary small/mid-value disbursements where the EMI has Canadian MSB registration and provides clear audit trails. Capital injection routing and trade settlements must reside in Tier 1 bank accounts to maintain security and regulatory credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident partners must be executed through the SWIFT network as MT103 direct wire or MT202 cover when a correspondent bank is used, with the beneficiary account being the Ontario LP's operational account at RBC, TD, or Scotiabank. The remitting institution must use the IMF Balance of Payments standard purpose code; for equity capital into a Canadian LP, the appropriate BOP code is 'Capital contribution — Direct Investment: Equity (Formation of Enterprise, code 5314)' or, for intercompany advances to fund receivable turnover, 'Other Investment: Loans (code 6310)'. The narration field must read 'CIP — Capital Injection into Ontario Limited Partnership' and reference the LP's name, account number, and possibly the partner's contribution docket number. Financial institutions are obligated under the PCMLTFA to verify the source of funds for one-time transfers over CAD 10,000 and to report such transfers to FINTRAC, so the LP must provide the remitter with a complete source-of-wealth questionnaire (or letter) before settlement. The LP's internal ledger must match each inbound wire to the capital account schedule and issue a non-lapsing contribution receipt to each partner to satisfy Ontario Securities Act / tax elections records.For high-value maritime charters and landed-cost compensation to overseas fertilizer producers, use a documentary letter of credit (Commercial LC / UCP600) opened by the Ontario LP's bank with the beneficiary in the supplier's country. For daily operational payments (freight forwarders, welding/dunnage, port fees, inspection charges, and third-party administration), instruct a SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details and a purpose code consistent with the import / offshore trade (e.g., ISIC 4669 / NAICS 424910). For CAD-denominated supplier invoices, settle through the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Interac-to-RTR under the Canadian Payments Act. Each outbound wire payment must be supported by an identifiable purchase order, transport contract, or invoice with a definitive counterparty reference, and Treasury must maintain a confirmation checklist (know-your-customer's-customer, sanctions screening, and transaction-pattern check) for every transfer exceeding CAD 10,000; all settle into foreign banks via a recognized correspondent chain to prevent freezing at clearing fence.Outright CAD/USD FX forward contracts (vanilla) for settlement of expected USD inflows and outflows on a fixed date with a Tier 1 Canadian bank under the ISDA 2023 Canadian AnnexNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., BRL, INR, TRY) to hedge freight or procurement exposure when CAD/USD contracts are insufficient, executed under a bank-approved NDF master agreementCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) matching CAD funding costs with USD revenue streams for multi-month trade cycles, excluding leveraged speculative dealings and subject to a written hedge-policy approval
