Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax; Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial income tax, and corporate registrations. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec's CliquezAffaires (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporating or registering to carry on business in Quebec obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, followed by program accounts: GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and corporate income tax (RC). Registration is simultaneous via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper (RC1 form). A Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and QST account (RM/ND) are then obtained from Revenu Québec; the Registraire des entreprises (Quebec Corporate Register) transmits registration data to Revenu Québec. For cloud/digital services (ISIC 6201), where the seller is a non-resident without a Canadian establishment, simplified GST/HST registration and QST registration are available for zero-rated or taxable digital supply, triggered by Canadian-source revenue thresholds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canadian tax law; classification is fixed by legal form under the Income Tax Act (ITA). A Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I federal tax and provincial income tax. Available elections affecting filing: (i) section 216 election for non-residents earning rents/royalties; (ii) joint election to file a corporate group consolidated tax return is not permitted, but a joint election under subsection 256(3) can deem associated corporations; (iii) election to capitalize paid-up capital versus contributed surplus; (iv) for hybrid instruments, the hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.2/18.3) operate as an automatic disallowance of a deduction where an offshore payment is not included in the recipient's income within a reasonable period, thereby overriding contractual characterizations — no elective opt-out is available, though the rules contain a written agreement timing exception. A QST registration election may be exercised by non-resident digital suppliers; the registered representative for GST/HST and QST purposes is mandatory for non-resident digital vendors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.14975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small supplier threshold of CAD $30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies for resident suppliers and non-resident digital suppliers whose Canadian supply value exceeds CAD $30,000 in a calendar quarter or trailing four quarters. The same CAD $30,000 threshold applies for QST small-supplier relief; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, irrespective of profit motive.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 23 of Part V, Schedule VI of the ETA applies to supplies of software, data infrastructure, database monitoring, and API cloud services (ISIC 6201) where the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, the service is not supplied to a person in Canada, and the supply is not in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For either, the supplier must hold documentary evidence of the recipient's non-residency. For B2B supplies of digital services to a Canadian GST/HST registrant, zero-rating may also apply if the recipient is registered and acquires the service for use in Canada exclusively in its commercial activities; for QST, a similar zero-rating provision applies to supplies of intangible personal property made to a non-resident or to a QST registrant for use exclusively in commercial activities. Zero-rating is a supply-level determination; the supplier must substantiate each invoice to preserve the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods; generally inapplicable to cloud/digital services, but preserved for hybrid transactions involving hardware or media)
  • Bill of Lading (where any tangible component, such as pre-installed infrastructure or encoding devices, is exported)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign currency payment received from the overseas client, matching invoice currency, bank reference, and payment date)
  • Signed service agreement or master subscription contract stating the recipient's country of residence and place of use of the supplied tool
  • Client KYC documentation such as a foreign tax ID number (e.g., EIN, VAT ID) to evidence non-residency
  • Supporting technical logs or usage attestations showing the service is consumed outside Canada where applicable for API/monitoring services
  • QST-specific proof: for non-resident recipients, a declaration of non-residency and, where replying on the recipient's registration, a copy of the GST/HST or QST certificate of registration

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-clearance exists at the federal GST/HST level or under Quebec's QST regime. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept conventional electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) provided the invoice contains prescribed information (supplier name/BN, recipient name, date of invoice, total amount, tax rate and amount, and for QST, the QST number). Quebec has enacted a phased mandatory e-invoicing framework under the Revenu Québec 'FACTURATION ÉLECTRONIQUE' roadmap: from 2026, certain large enterprises and their suppliers must exchange structured e-invoices through the Peppol network, extending to all taxable persons by 2027–2029; enforcement will be through audit and administrative penalty for non-compliance, not through fiscal pre-clearance. Until then, issuance and retention of prescribed invoice data in electronic form satisfies the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and Tax Administration Act (Quebec) record-keeping obligations.

The CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit zero-rated IT/cloud exporters; the absence of complete documentation triggers reassessment at the standard rate (5% GST + 9.975% QST) for each unsupported invoice, with interest at the prescribed rate compounded daily (currently approximately 8% to 10% annual effective for overdue amounts) running from the original remittance date. Gross negligence penalties of 25% of the net additional tax apply under ETA s. 285.1 for knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence making false statements; false or deceptive invoices, misstatement for tax evasion, and the knowingly obtaining of a refund to which the taxpayer is not entitled attract criminal penalties under ETA s. 327 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act, including fines of up to 200% of the amount sought and imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years for indictable offences. Retroactive clawbacks are unlimited where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established; otherwise a four-year normal reassessment window applies. Where a zero-rating claim is reversed, the taxpayer also becomes liable for remittance of uncollected QST due to Revenu Québec, including a strict liability penalty of 15% of the net tax for failure to file and civil penalties for accessing refunds under false pretence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and financial transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted movement of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, and trade receipts, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax withholding, transfer-pricing rules, corporate-law approvals, lender restrictions, and bank transaction monitoring. No general approval is required for foreign ownership of a Quebec corporation or for ordinary repatriation of post-tax profits. Dividends must be declared under applicable corporate law and supported by solvency requirements; non-resident payments may require Canadian withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty. Intercompany loans, management charges, royalties, and service fees should be supported by written agreements, arm's-length pricing, beneficial-ownership evidence, and applicable Canadian tax filings. Foreign-exchange derivatives are generally available through regulated financial institutions, subject to counterparty onboarding, suitability, documentation, collateral, and applicable derivatives regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts. The corporation may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Banks may require invoices, contracts, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and explanation of payment purpose. Large international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, including aggregated transfers within the applicable reporting period, can trigger FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution. Foreign affiliates, foreign accounts, and foreign property may create Canadian tax and information-reporting obligations depending on ownership, residency, and asset characteristics.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with federal anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), prudential oversight of federally regulated banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and Quebec financial-market oversight by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and operating workflows, but not a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank. Offshore electronic-money institutions and platforms such as Mercury or similar providers may restrict Canadian-incorporated entities, Quebec residents, non-resident beneficial owners, certain currencies, or particular payment purposes. Availability is subject to the provider's licensing perimeter, country policy, correspondent-bank access, sanctions screening, source-of-funds review, and safeguarding model. An offshore EMI generally cannot accept deposits as a Canadian bank, provide unrestricted domestic Canadian banking, or be represented as a Canadian deposit institution. Stripe is a payment processor rather than a bank and should be connected to a controlled Canadian settlement account. Funds should not be routed through an offshore EMI to evade Canadian reporting, tax, sanctions, or AML requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital through an account held in the corporation's legal name at a Canadian bank or an appropriately licensed payment institution. Use a wire reference identifying the transaction as equity subscription, paid-up capital contribution, or shareholder loan; do not describe a loan as equity or use unexplained third-party remitters. Before receipt, maintain the subscription or loan agreement, board and shareholder approvals where required, capitalization table, investor and beneficial-owner records, source-of-funds evidence, and banking instructions. The receiving bank may request a purpose statement, remitter identification, ownership evidence, and confirmation that funds are not escrowed for an undisclosed party. Canada has no universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system; banks may use internal purpose classifications and should receive accurate commercial descriptions. Record the foreign-exchange rate, fees, Canadian-dollar equivalent, accounting treatment, and whether the amount is debt or equity. Review corporate solvency, thin-capitalization, interest-deductibility, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and non-resident reporting consequences before deployment.Use Canadian bank operating accounts for supplier, cloud-hosting, software, payroll, tax, and other legitimate business payments. A SWIFT customer wire, commonly processed using legacy MT103 terminology or the applicable ISO 20022 equivalent, should identify the legal payer, beneficiary, bank details, invoice number, currency, payment purpose, and any required intermediary-bank data. For recurring or material imports and cross-border services, retain contracts, purchase orders, invoices, delivery or service-acceptance evidence, tax treatment, and sanctions-screening results. Commercial letters of credit may be issued or advised through a Canadian bank where documentary trade risk justifies the cost; define documentary conditions, Incoterms, insurance, inspection evidence, discrepancy handling, governing rules such as UCP 600 where adopted, and authorized signatories. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment collection and operating workflows, but not a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank. Offshore electronic-money institutions and platforms such as Mercury or similar providers may restrict Canadian-incorporated entities, Quebec residents, non-resident beneficial owners, certain currencies, or particular payment purposes. Availability is subject to the provider's licensing perimeter, country policy, correspondent-bank access, sanctions screening, source-of-funds review, and safeguarding model. An offshore EMI generally cannot accept deposits as a Canadian bank, provide unrestricted domestic Canadian banking, or be represented as a Canadian deposit institution. Stripe is a payment processor rather than a bank and should be connected to a controlled Canadian settlement account. Funds should not be routed through an offshore EMI to evade Canadian reporting, tax, sanctions, or AML requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident per taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation and Form T106 filing; Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding C$1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act ss. 245-246) and the doctrine of economic substance in transfer pricing (s. 247) to ensure that intercompany agreements reflect actual commercial conduct. Transactions with no real business purpose are recharacterized and tax benefits are denied, with penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (summary conviction) or up to 14 years (indictable); additionally, civil gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or participate in fraudulent filings may be held personally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted withholding taxes, with civil restitution, travel bans, passports withdrawn, asset freezes, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under section 247(2) to adjust transfer prices to arm's length; transfer pricing penalty under 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment for misrepresentation or failure to provide documentation; additional interest from the reassessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign materially false transfer-pricing documentation without reasonable investigation are exposed to negligence penalties and, in cross-border cases, travel restrictions and seizure of assets; director liability may extend to unpaid Canadian tax where the corporate structure is a sham.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245) denies the tax benefit; the shell entity's transactions are recharacterized as directed to the Canadian corporation; criminal fraud charges under Criminal Code section 380 may apply where deception is used; combined penalties and interest may exceed 200% of the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the shell is a mere facade; directors may be ordered to pay broad civil restitution, face travel bans and passport denials during investigation, and be imprisoned for fraud or forgery.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA of up to C$2 million per violation; criminal penalties for laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code section 462.31 include fines up to C$500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; corporate assets may be forfeited and bank accounts frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable if they knowingly fail to establish a PCMLTFA compliance program; penalties include personal fines, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment; FINTRAC publishes individual sanction lists, severely damaging cross-border mobility.
