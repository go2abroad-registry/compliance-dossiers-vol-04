Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for provincial tax policy, with GST/HST administered by the CRA)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident / foreign entity engaged in taxable activity in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA, which serves as the GST/HST registration ID. Registration can be completed online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number', or by mailing/faxing the equivalent paper application. Supporting documentation includes proof of foreign incorporation, foreign tax identification number, director/officer details, principal Canadian business premises, and estimated Canadian taxable revenue. The BN is used for all federal program accounts (income tax, GST/HST, payroll, import/export). A foreign entity whose supplies are exclusively zero-rated may elect to register voluntarily to claim input tax credits on related expenses.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification regime; a Business Corporation is automatically a separate taxable entity at the corporate level. Statutory elections relevant to cross-border trading structures include: (i) the Excise Tax Act s.156 election to treat certain supplies among closely related corporations as made for nil consideration for GST/HST purposes; (ii) the Income Tax Act s.216 election for a non-resident to file and pay Canadian income tax on a net basis for rental / timber income; (iii) the s.93(2) election to deem dividends from a foreign affiliate to be received out of taxable surplus; and (iv) association elections under s.256 of the Income Tax Act affecting allocation of small business deduction limits. Hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000 (the small supplier threshold — the value of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded over a calendar quarter, mandatory registration is required).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods that meet the definition of a 'qualified export' under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act (e.g., ETA s.1, s.2, s.3), including goods exported directly by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export, and to certain intangible personal property supplied to non-residents for use solely outside Canada. Services are zero-rated under Schedule VII where they are supplied to a non-resident and are not for consumption in Canada (subject to exceptions for services in connection with Canadian real property or goods). The supplier must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence proving export or offshore supply within the required evidentiary window; otherwise the supply is recharacterized as taxable at the HST rate.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic filing through the Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS)
  • Bill of Lading (straight or order) or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Evidence of export issued by the carrier or customs office (e.g., cargo control document, terminal receipt, customs stamp)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank wire transfer confirmation showing foreign currency payment
  • Certificate of origin where applicable

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently in force for GST/HST. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices provided they are legible, complete, and stored in a CRA-compliant format. Ontario does not maintain a separate clearance system. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing standard (Peppol) is voluntary.

If the CRA determines that an export zero-rating claim was unsupported, the supply is reclassified as taxable and the taxpayer is liable for the unremitted HST (currently 13% in Ontario) plus interest under Excise Tax Act s.280. Civil penalties include failure-to-register and false-statement penalties under ETA s.285 and gross negligence penalties under s.285.1, which can be up to 50% of the tax sought. CRA may claw back GST/HST input tax credits claimed on inputs relating to the failed export, for a period of up to four years (or longer in the case of misrepresentation or fraud). Fraudulent export zero-rating may also lead to criminal prosecution under ETA s.397, with fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment. Although Canadian law does not prescribe a statutory repatriation deadline for export proceeds, the 180-day benchmark is commonly used as a documentary evidence-preservation period for corroborating payment and export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada complies with IMF Article VIII and imposes no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders are freely permitted, subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% statutory, reduced to 5% on dividends under the Canada–[Resident Country] tax treaty with form NR301/NR302 filing) and to FINTRAC reporting for each wire transfer equal to or above CAD 10,000. No prior regulatory approval is required for the conversion of CAD into foreign currency or for the crediting of foreign currency to domestic or offshore accounts, provided the bank-facing documentation supports the underlying trade or investment purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert export receipts in Canada; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic, cross-border, or offshore accounts. The only compliance obligations are: (i) a Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) B13A export declaration for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000, submitted within 15 days after the export event; (ii) an International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) / Statistics Canada classification of the inward remittance as 'trade settlement' or 'direct investment' as requested by the receiving bank; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of any single cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, in addition to ongoing record‑keeping and beneficial-ownership transparency obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; foreign-exchange administration is functionally shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential conduct and with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border payment monitoring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), together with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy under the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment streams and Low for principal trade settlements. Non-bank fintechs and digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) in Canada under FINTRAC registration, but they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, are not members of the Lynx/ACSS clearing system, and hold funds in non-bank pooled/omnibus accounts that do not meet escrow-grade segregation expectations of Canadian and foreign dairy counterparties. They are legally constrained by the Bank Act (prohibition on engaging in the business of banking without a Schedule I/II charter) and by PCMLTFA requirements for MSB registration; therefore, their use should be limited to low-value disbursements to service providers, not to LC-based commodity purchase contracts from ISO/IEC-accredited dairy processors.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial ownership structure involving foreign nominees or a trust holding the Ontario corporation's shares

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership structure involving foreign nominees or a trust holding the Ontario corporation's shares

Defense evidence: Ontario Business Information Report (corporate registry extract from the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery), trust deed and trustee declarations, ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) attestation form, organizational chart tracing at least 25% beneficial ownership, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, audited financial statements of the subscriber), and a Canadian legal opinion confirming no bearer shares or unregistered holdings.
High-frequency cross-border wires to/from jurisdictions perceived as higher-risk for dairy commodity trade

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border wires to/from jurisdictions perceived as higher-risk for dairy commodity trade

Defense evidence: Executed sales and purchase agreements for whole and skimmed milk powder, commercial invoices and packing lists, bills of lading and air waybills, certificates of origin and phytosanitary certificates, product specification sheets, CFIA export certification, marine/transit insurance policies, and a correspondent bank reference letter from the foreign buyer's/seller's financial institution.
Absence or inconsistency of statutory licenses for dairy commodity export

Root cause: Absence or inconsistency of statutory licenses for dairy commodity export

Defense evidence: Current Safe Food for Canadians Act (SFCA) Licence from the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA), Canadian Dairy Commission registration or export permit for butter/skim milk powder quotas, import-country health certificates and pre-export inspection reports, Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) certification, and the company's dairy dealer agreement with a licensed processor.
Requests for third-party payments where the remitter is not the named commercial counterparty

Root cause: Requests for third-party payments where the remitter is not the named commercial counterparty

Defense evidence: Back-to-back supply chain agreements, subrogation or assignment notices, invoice genealogy demonstrating the third-party payment mirrors an outstanding receivable/payable, board-approved treasury policy permitting directed payments, reconciliation ledger between invoice numbers, payment amounts, and SWIFT reference numbers, and a signed indemnity from the third-party remitter.
Sanctions screening hit or politically exposed person (PEP) status of a shareholder, director, or counterparty

Root cause: Sanctions screening hit or politically exposed person (PEP) status of a shareholder, director, or counterparty

Defense evidence: Negative-media search results from a reputable due-diligence provider, verification against the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions sanctions lists, Global Affairs Canada and United Nations sanctions registries, PEP self-declaration and source-of-wealth/income documentation, and a documented compliance rationale establishing that the screened entity is not a target or is subject only to unsanctioned dealing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

To inject equity or shareholder-loan capital into the Ontario corporation, route funds via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the corporation's CAD or USD account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, quoting the corporate name and Ontario incorporation number in field 59 and using the remitter's bank draft references that align with the share subscription agreement. Canada maintains no statutory 'purpose code' registry, but the receiving bank will apply a Statistics Canada ITRS transaction classification; the remitter should specify 'Direct Investment – Equity' or 'Intercompany Loan' on the wire instruction. The verification bundle must include the signed share subscription agreement, board resolution authorizing issuance, Notice of Articles or share register update, a Canadian legal opinion confirming that the injection is characterized as equity or quasi-equity for Part XIII/Part I tax purposes, and the CRS/CBCR entity classification form. For payments exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will generate an electronic FINTRAC report; for amounts above the reporting threshold, the remitter must also provide a completed SWIFT FinRem or equivalent tax-certification form if the shareholder resides in a reportable jurisdiction.Use SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for direct disbursement to foreign dairy suppliers and logistics providers, with the underlying commercial invoice number and commodity description in the payment narrative, and settle in USD or CAD via the Lynx/ACSS real-time systems for domestic legs or through the bank's correspondent network for cross-border legs. For principal commodity procurement, use documentary letters of credit issued by a Schedule I Canadian bank under ICC UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT710), payable at sight or with deferred payment tenor, with the L/C naming the Ontario company as applicant and the foreign exporter as beneficiary; confirmations by a first-class international bank are recommended for milk-powder suppliers in jurisdictions with elevated country risk. All outbound payments must be supported by (i) the purchase contract, (ii) the pro-forma invoice, (iii) inspection certificates, (iv) CFIA export/import certifications, and (v) transport documents, and must be screened against the Canadian sanctions regime and counterparty embargo lists before transmission.Deliverable outright forward contracts in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/CNY, and CAD/CHF with a Schedule I bank or an eligible credit-corporation counterparty under ISDA documentation, used to lock the CAD value of projected milk-powder export receipts and import payables for tenors up to 24 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) structured by a Canadian bank or its foreign affiliate, net-settled in CAD, for hedging exposure to currencies of importing jurisdictions with limited onshore convertibility (e.g., CNY if subject to settlement restrictions), provided the NDF is documented as a hedge of a genuine commercial receivable.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) exchanging fixed or floating CAD payments for USD/EUR/CHF payments, covering principal and interest cash flows on intercompany loans or on trade-finance lines, subject to an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and a collateral arrangement acceptable to the bank.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX options (call/put structures) on CAD/USD and CAD/EUR, including collars and zero-premium risk reversals, purchased for hedge-only purposes and not for speculative trading.Exchange-traded currency futures and options on CAD and USD listed on CME Group or B3, cleared through a registered futures commission merchant and endorsed by the corporate treasury policy, primarily used for shorter-tenor tactical hedging.Forward rate agreements (FRAs) and interest rate swaps used in conjunction with the FX hedges where the underlying trade-finance facility carries a floating-rate exposure, to neutralize the cross-currency basis risk in the overall treasury portfolio.
