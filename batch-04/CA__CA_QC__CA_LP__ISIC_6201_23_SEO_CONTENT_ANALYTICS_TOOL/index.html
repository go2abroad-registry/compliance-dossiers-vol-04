Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are freely fixed by the partnership agreement and may consist of money, property, or services for the general partner; a limited partner must contribute money or property only (art. 2259 C.c.Q.). No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain a fire safety certificate or attestation of conformity per the Règlement sur la sécurité incendie (Quebec Fire Safety Regulation) for the registered office fit-out; structural load inspection if server racks/load-bearing installations exceed office floor capacity; occupational clearance: register with the CNESST and carry out a hazard identification; maintain first-aid boxes, safety signage and an emergency evacuation plan; for premises with 20+ employees, adopt a workplace health and safety program under the LSST (Loi sur la santé et la sécurité du travail) and establish a joint committee.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are freely fixed by the partnership agreement and may consist of money, property, or services for the general partner; a limited partner must contribute money or property only (art. 2259 C.c.Q.). No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), GST/HST program and International Tax Services Office, portal: CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); and Revenu Québec, portal: 'Mon dossier d'entreprise'. For a business with a permanent establishment in Quebec, the federal GST/HST is administered and collected by Revenu Québec under the Canada-Quebec Tax Collection Agreement, while the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) is a separate provincial VAT administered solely by Revenu Québec.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Register the Limited Partnership in the Quebec enterprise register (Registraire d'entreprises) to obtain the provincial 'Numéro d'entreprise du Québec' (NEQ), filing the declaration of a limited partnership or amendment identifying the general partner. STEP 2: Apply to the CRA for the federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, indicating a Quebec permanent establishment and attaching the partnership agreement extracts that designate the representative partner. STEP 3: Register separately with Revenu Québec for the QST account (account type 'RM') after confirming that the LP has ceased to qualify as a small supplier or expects to exceed the threshold; Revenu Québec requires the NEQ, the BN, the RC1 reference number, and particulars of non-resident partners for the TIN/QST cross-reference. STEP 4: Execute the statutory election under ETA s. 273(1) appointing the general partner as representative for GST/HST and QST administration, and file the applicable Revenu Québec e-registration declaration. STEP 5: Obtain the CRA confirmation letter and the Revenu Québec registration certificate, then begin issuing invoices carrying the BN, GST/HST registration number, and QST registration number; total processing time is typically 2 to 10 business days. Non-resident registrants without a Canadian residence may use the CRA International Tax Services Office and Revenu Québec's non-resident unit.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box classification regime for limited partnerships. The relevant statutory elections available to this LP are: (i) ETA s. 273(1) election designating a partner as the representative for the partnership's GST/HST and QST registration, filing, and payment obligations; (ii) ITA s. 96(1.1) and (1.2) elections fixing the allocation of partnership income and losses among partners independent of the distribution year; (iii) ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to file a Part I Canadian income tax return with respect to taxable Canadian income from the LP rather than being subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding; (iv) corresponding QST Act elections made to Revenu Québec aligning the GST/HST and QST registration and filing framework; and (v) for US federal tax purposes only, an IRC s. 7701 check-the-box election permitting the Canadian LP to be disregarded or treated as an association taxable as a corporation, which has no effect on the Canadian or Quebec tax classification of the entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory registration for both GST/HST (ETA small supplier rule, s. 148(4)) and QST (parallel rule under the QST Act). For a non-resident LP not carrying on business in Canada, the same CAD 30,000 threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies; once the LP's taxable supplies made through a Quebec establishment or deemed made in Quebec under the place-of-supply rules for digital services exceed CAD 30,000, registration with Revenu Québec for QST and the GST/HST becomes compulsory. An LP whose only Canadian supplies are zero-rated exports may elect to register even below the threshold to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under the ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1 to 3 where tangible personal property is exported from Canada in accordance with the statutory export evidence rules. Services and digital products (the LP's SEO analytics SaaS): zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, is not registered under the ETA at the time the supply is made, and the service is not rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service enjoyed or performed in Canada; the place-of-supply rules in ETA s. 144.1 and the corresponding QST place-of-supply rules determine whether the supply is made in Canada. For QST, supplies that are zero-rated under the ETA are correspondingly zero-rated under the QST Act unless specifically recharacterized by Quebec. The vendor must evidence non-residence through the subscription contract, billing address, IP/geolocation logs, and payment-rail records (Stripe/Paddle) demonstrating that the customer's use and enjoyment occurred outside Canada.

  • For goods: Canadian Export Declaration filed electronically with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian export reporting system, including Form B13A for controlled goods; the Customs Act and Export Control List commodity classifications must be reflected
  • For goods: original transport document proving exportation - ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or road manifest (CMR) - naming the Canadian vendor as exporter; for pure digital/SaaS supplies this is substituted by server access and session logs evidencing offshore geolocation and foreign use of the platform
  • Commercial invoice, executed SaaS subscription agreement, and customer non-residence declaration showing the application of the 0% rate, the foreign billing address, the customer's status as a non-registered non-resident, and the LP's GST/HST and QST registration numbers
  • Inward remittance evidence: Canada does not issue a formal 'Inward Remittance Certificate'; the accepted substitute is the bank statement, SWIFT transfer advice, or payment-rail settlement report (Stripe/Paddle) evidencing receipt of funds from abroad within the documented period
  • Internal zero-rating substantiation worksheet cross-referencing the statutory provision relied upon and the six-year retention file

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada or Quebec for SaaS analytics services. Electronic filing is mandatory for registered persons: GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE XML/API (Biz2File) and QST returns through Revenu Québec's electronic services. Invoicing must comply with the ETA and QST record-keeping rules (seller and recipient identification, registration numbers, date, description, tax consideration, rate applied, or zero-rating indication), and all records must be retained for six years. Revenu Québec has announced under its enterprise tax modernization project (Quebec Budget 2025-2026) a phased mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing regime intended to apply to large businesses (annual turnover above CAD 10 million) in the late 2020s; no such obligation is currently in force for this LP.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the normal three-year period (four years for QST) where zero-rating is claimed on the basis of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud; fraudulent export zero-rating attracts penalties of 25% for gross negligence and up to 200% of the tax evaded for wilful evasion, under ETA ss. 280 and 285, with compounded daily interest; criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 327 and 328 and ITA s. 239 may result in fines up to CAD 200,000 or between 100% and 200% of the tax sought, plus imprisonment up to five years. Revenu Québec applies parallel penalty and interest provisions under the QST Act and the Taxation Act and may jointly void registrations, demand immediate payment of reassessed net tax, and pursue the general partner personally where the LP is used as a facade. Although no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline exists in Canada, the 180-day benchmark is maintained as a prudent compliance standard; remittance evidence gathered after that date may be treated as weakening the documentation chain and can justify denial of the zero-rating, reopening of the limitation period, and clawback of input tax credits previously refunded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. No exchange-control approvals, registration, or prior authorizations apply to inbound capital injections, foreign-currency financing, or repatriation of profits through distributions from a Quebec limited partnership. The sole material impediment to profit remittance is tax-driven: under section 194 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), a partnership that credits or pays income to a non-resident partner must withhold Part XIII tax of 25%, subject to treaty reduction (e.g., 15% under the Canada-US Convention for the portion treated as a distribution to a US resident beneficial owner) or a section 115 net-basis election arrangement. Subsections 216 and 217 elections are available for specific income classes but would typically not be the operative route for SaaS business income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds, including software subscriptions, SEO data-crawler licensing, or analytics-as-a-service revenue. The partnership may retain USD and other foreign-currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts without Bank of Canada or OSFI authorization.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains an open capital account and does not operate a dedicated foreign exchange administration body. Prudential supervision of federally incorporated banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF regulation in FINTRAC, and payment-system oversight in Payments Canada. Commercial bank onboarding and foreign-currency operations are governed by the Bank Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and applicable provincial consumer-protection statutes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — permissible only as a complementary collection and FX-conversion rail, never as the primary treasury account provider. The European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence is not recognized in Canada; global fintech firms (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) must operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA and are not authorized to accept demand deposits like Schedule I banks. A Quebec LP may lawfully use such platforms to receive USD payouts from Paddle or Stripe and to convert currency at commercial rates, but the capital escrow account, statutory distribution clearing, and operating liquidity must ultimately reside with a Canadian regulated bank to preserve LVTS/Real-Time Rail access and to avoid isolated PCMLTFA facilitation risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions should be remitted by the partner via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire where same-day USD settlement is required) directly to the LP's CAD or USD account at a Quebec Schedule I bank, with a payment narrative reading 'Capital Contribution - [investor name] - [LP name] - NEQ [registration number]' and an ISO 20022 purpose code of 'CAPC' (capital payment). The bank is required to report any electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, so the partnership must retain the capital commitment letter, the confirmation copy of the MT103, and the capital-account entry to allow seamless audit. USD-to-CAD conversion may be executed with the account bank as a deliverable spot or forward outright; no prior Bank of Canada clearance, licence, or repatriation filing is required.For trade and operating settlements, the recommended clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor and contractor payments, with ISO purpose code 'TRAD' or 'SUPP' and the corresponding invoice reference; (ii) domestic CAD clearing through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for Canadian suppliers; (iii), commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are not necessary for a recurring SaaS licensing model but remain available if the SEO-data-crawler hardware or colocation contracts require documentary settlement; (iv) distribution remittances to US partners should be executed by MT103 with purpose code 'DIVP' and accompanied by a withholding evidence package proving Part XIII treaty-reduced tax compliance; and (v) Stripe and Paddle sales receipts should be instructed to settle in USD into the LP's USD sub-account to eliminate embedded FX spreads on each payout.Deliverable FX forwards (plain vanilla and average-rate forwards) for hedging USD-denominated subscription receivables from Stripe/Paddle, executed with the account bank under an ISDA 2002 or Canadian Master Agreement with typical tenors of up to 24 monthsFX options (European-style calls and puts) and zero-cost collars structured on USD-to-CAD conversion of forecast revenueCross-currency swaps (CCS) where a partner loan or intercompany financing is extended in USD against CAD operating liquidity, executed under an ISDA 2002 Collateral AnnexNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible in Canada but are economically redundant for CAD/USD, and would be considered only for ancillary exposure to non-convertible emerging-market currencies that arise from the SEO analytics tool's multi-currency invoicing

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium — permissible only as a complementary collection and FX-conversion rail, never as the primary treasury account provider. The European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence is not recognized in Canada; global fintech firms (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) must operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA and are not authorized to accept demand deposits like Schedule I banks. A Quebec LP may lawfully use such platforms to receive USD payouts from Paddle or Stripe and to convert currency at commercial rates, but the capital escrow account, statutory distribution clearing, and operating liquidity must ultimately reside with a Canadian regulated bank to preserve LVTS/Real-Time Rail access and to avoid isolated PCMLTFA facilitation risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%) general corporate rate; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level corporate income tax, with income allocated to partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Treaty-reduced rates: e.g., Canada-US Treaty 15% portfolio / 5% for shareholdings of at least 30% voting control; Canada-UK Treaty often 15% / 5%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation required when cross-border transactions with non-resident related persons exceed CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value (goods/services), with CAD 250,000 threshold for intangibles and financing transactions; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000. Canada has no de jure Local/Master File threshold, but CRA expects OECD-aligned documentation for material transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the OECD Economic Substance Regulations used in Caribbean centers. CRA enforces the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s.245) and the substance-over-form doctrine. A Quebec LP must maintain genuine economic presence—physical office, local employees, management decisions, and business operations in Quebec—to preserve partnership status and claim treaty benefits. A hollow or shell LP can be re-characterized as a sham, its tax attributes ignored, and its partners treated as taxable in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty s.163(2): 50% of understated tax plus interest; equivalent Revenu Québec penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and general partners can be assessed personally for withholding and source deductions and civil restitution. Criminal travel bans, passport surrender, and asset forfeiture may be imposed; principals face personal bankruptcy and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices under s.247(2) and impose a penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable documentation is absent; interest on reassessments applies; secondary adjustments may be re-characterized as deemed dividends subject to Part XIII WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing documentation or create sham intercompany agreements may be personally fined up to CAD 50,000 on summary conviction, face civil restitution, and non-resident executives can be subject to entry bans during criminal investigations.
Shell Company / Artificial Transaction / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Denial of the claimed tax benefit under s.245(3); 25% GAAR penalty under s.245(5) in addition to tax and interest; possible re-characterization of the LP as a corporation; Revenu Québec may apply similar anti-avoidance rules.

Director personal liability: General partner liability is unlimited; CRA may pierce the partnership veil and impose joint and several liability on directors and the general partner for tax, interest, and penalties; travel bans may be issued in egregious cross-border schemes.
AML / FINTRAC / Payment Processor Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for unreported transactions; violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal money-laundering conviction with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally liable for failing to discharge AML obligations; personal fines up to CAD 500,000 for indictable offences, 5-year travel ban, asset restraint, and imprisonment for complicity in money laundering.
