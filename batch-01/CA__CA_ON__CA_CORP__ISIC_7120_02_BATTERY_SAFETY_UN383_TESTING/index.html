Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — competent authority for federal GST/HST, corporate income tax (including Ontario's harmonized corporate income tax), and payroll accounts. Ontario Ministry of Finance administers Employer Health Tax; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the entity under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or Ontario Business Corporations Act) to obtain the federal incorporation number or Ontario file number. 2) Register with the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); the BN is the statutory tax identifier and supports separate program accounts: RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RC (payroll), and RM (import/export). 3) For a non-resident-owned Canadian corporation, provide articles of incorporation, director information, a Canadian agent for service if directors are non-resident, and, where applicable, security arrangements under ITA s. 116(5) for future dispositions. 4) Activate the GST/HST (RT) account; because Ontario participates in the HST, the 8% provincial component of the 13% HST is remitted through the same CRA GST/HST account; no separate Ontario sales-tax registration exists. 5) If battery samples will cross borders for UN38.3 testing, activate the RM (import/export) program account and obtain a CBSA importer/exporter identifier. 6) For Ontario corporate income tax, no separate registration is required: Ontario corporation tax is administered by the CRA on a harmonized basis. 7) Registration becomes mandatory when the $30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available using Form GST1/RC1 and, for non-residents not carrying on business in Canada, Form GST 524.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is deemed resident in Canada for tax purposes (ITA s. 250(4)(a)) and is taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income. There is no pass-through or check-the-box election for corporations in Canada. Ontario corporate income tax is imposed on the same taxable income base and administered by the CRA (Ontario general rate 11.5% today; small-business rate applies to qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations; federal general rate 15%, federal small-business rate 9%). For GST/HST, the corporation is a separate 'person' required to collect net tax on supplies made in Canada. Non-resident shareholders are taxed on dividends under Part XIII withholding tax (subject to the Canada-US Treaty where applicable); the entity remains a resident Canadian corporation regardless of the residence or ownership of its shareholders.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; business corporations are universally entity-level taxpayers under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections and mechanical choices include: (i) election to adopt a fiscal year other than the calendar year (ITA s. 249.1); (ii) joint filing of an allocation agreement to distribute the federal and Ontario small-business deduction limits among associated corporations (ITA s. 125(7)); (iii) election to waive the carry-back of non-capital losses (ITA s. 111(9.2)); (iv) for non-resident owners of a Canadian corporation, treaty-based return elections under Part XIII (e.g., s. 215(3) deemed-recovery filings) to obtain a refund of excess withholding; (v) anti-hybrid mismatch elections under ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 and related MLI notifications where debt-equity classification is contested; and (vi) GST/HST voluntary registration election for non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada (ETA s. 240(3)). No election permits a standard corporation to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for Canadian tax purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada has eliminated exchange controls since 1951 and adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations. Current-account and capital-account transactions are equally unrestricted: equity injections, share subscriptions, shareholder loans, profit remittances, dividends, trade disbursements, and loan servicing require zero regulatory authorization. No surrender, allocation, prior-approval, or licensing regime exists for CAD conversion. The only treasury-relevant constraints are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on outbound dividends/interest/royalties (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%–15% under the Canada–U.S. and Canada–EU conventions); and (ii) FINTRAC cross-border reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000). A battery-safety testing entity (ISIC 7120 / NAICS 541380) owned by a non-resident parent may therefore invoice in USD, EUR, or CAD and convert proceeds freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any currency, in domestic or offshore accounts, at the company's sole discretion. For the physical export of battery samples for UN38.3 testing, an export declaration must be filed through CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) where the goods exceed the CAD 2,000 threshold, but this is customs-compliance only and carries no forex obligation. Testing services exported electronically or by report are outside customs documentation entirely. No G13/G14 survey filing is required unless the company receives a Statistics Canada survey request; all wire receipts above CAD 10,000 nevertheless trigger FINTRAC reporting by the receiving bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); bank supervision: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision: FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada). Canada maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Control Administration and has no applicable exchange-control statute.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility for Wise Business as an auxiliary rail only. Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences; foreign fintech EMIs offering payment services to Canadian clients must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and satisfy Ontario registration rules. Wise is operationally viable for USD/EUR/CAD collections and low-value disbursements, and it aligns with the company's stated target rails; however, it is not a substitute for a Schedule I operating account because banks and CRA may treat an MSB balance as secondary credit, and high-value equipment payments require correspondent-banked SWIFT credit. Testing-lab fee receipts from EU battery clients (EUR) and U.S. clients (USD) can be received into a multi-currency Wise balance and auto-converted to CAD at the mid-market rate, but for corporate governance, monthly reconciliation, and audit evidence, a domestic bank account must remain the primary treasury anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital must arrive by SWIFT MT103 or the faster-payment network to the Ontario operating account, denominated in CAD. The beneficiary line must combine the entity's legal name, account number, and the bank's institution/transit code and BIC (e.g., RBC, institution 003). Field 70 (Remittance Information) should state 'EQUITY INJECTION – SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – OBCA/CBCA' to distinguish the amount from trade remittances. The sending bank must apply an internationally coherent Balance-of-Payments purpose code: United States wires should use the T-code for equity (e.g., T-equity/CAP eq) and EU wires the ECB/BoP capital transfer subcategory (F6). The receiving bank will clear the credit only after automated screening against OFAC, UN, and FINTRAC sanctions lists; for amounts above CAD 100,000, the bank will require a completed beneficial-ownership notice and board resolution before releasing the funds. Practical acceleration measure: file the corporate KYC pack and board resolution with the relationship manager before the wire so the credit is released intraday rather than after a compliance hold.Use Lynx (Canada's real-time gross-settlement system, successor to LVTS) for domestic CAD disbursements; use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border settlements. USD payments should be routed through the bank's New York correspondent; EUR payments should route via the bank's European branch or correspondent into TARGET2/SEPA, using MT103 with the beneficiary's IBAN. For high-value procurement of battery-testing equipment (drop towers, electromechanical shakers, climatic chambers, RF shielding rooms), a documentary commercial letter of credit under UCP600 (irrevocable, confirmed if sourced from high-risk jurisdictions) is the strongest clearing rail; the required document set includes commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and the certificate of quality. For ordinary subcontract payments to ISO/IEC 17025 partner laboratories, use MT103 remittance wires with the PO number stated in field 70 to preserve audit and CRA transfer-pricing records under subsection 247 of the Income Tax Act. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility for Wise Business as an auxiliary rail only. Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences; foreign fintech EMIs offering payment services to Canadian clients must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and satisfy Ontario registration rules. Wise is operationally viable for USD/EUR/CAD collections and low-value disbursements, and it aligns with the company's stated target rails; however, it is not a substitute for a Schedule I operating account because banks and CRA may treat an MSB balance as secondary credit, and high-value equipment payments require correspondent-banked SWIFT credit. Testing-lab fee receipts from EU battery clients (EUR) and U.S. clients (USD) can be received into a multi-currency Wise balance and auto-converted to CAD at the mid-market rate, but for corporate governance, monthly reconciliation, and audit evidence, a domestic bank account must remain the primary treasury anchor.
