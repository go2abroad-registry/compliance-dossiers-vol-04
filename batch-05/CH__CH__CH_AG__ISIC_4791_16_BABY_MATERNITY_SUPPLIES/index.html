Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum authorized share capital CHF 100,000; minimum paid-up capital CHF 50,000 (at least 20% of each share's par value) must be deposited in a blocked account (Sperrkonto) held with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank; the bank issues an escrow-capital confirmation; free disposal of funds is permitted only after Commercial Register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Cantonal Fire Safety Inspectorate for leased office and warehouse premises, including evacuation plans, extinguisher coverage and fire-load documentation; structural load and building compliance evidenced by the landlord's building permit; occupational health and workplace clearance under SUVA and SECO covering first-aid, ergonomics, electrical safety and staff safety instruction for office and warehouse operations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum authorized share capital CHF 100,000; minimum paid-up capital CHF 50,000 (at least 20% of each share's par value) must be deposited in a blocked account (Sperrkonto) held with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank; the bank issues an escrow-capital confirmation; free disposal of funds is permitted only after Commercial Register entry.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/AFC), Value Added Tax Division, Bern; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; electronic filing via the SIP taxpayer portal.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation the AG is automatically assigned a Swiss Business Identifier (UID) by the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The AG must separately register as a VAT taxpayer with the ESTV through the online SIP portal using Form 'Registration as a VAT taxpayer'. Procedure: (1) submit company incorporation deed and certified Commercial Register extract; (2) designate Swiss domicile address and board representation; (3) if non-established abroad, appoint a Swiss fiscal domicile/representative for VAT; (4) ESTV issues the VAT number (MWST-Nr./UID) within approximately 3-5 weeks. Registration is mandatory once taxable turnover reaches the statutory threshold; late registration attracts procedural fines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; the AG is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available elections: VAT group election (Organschaft, Art. 21 MWSTG); simplified budget method (Saldomethode) vs. net tax method; participation relief (Beteiligungsabzug) election for qualifying capital gains and dividends; cantonal notional interest deduction (NID) elections under TRAF reforms; no domestic hybrid-mismatch election - BEPS/MLI measures apply through treaty provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover effective 1 January 2025 (formerly CHF 100,000); non-established foreign e-commerce suppliers crossing the threshold from Swiss consumer sales are registrable, while sub-threshold suppliers may opt for voluntary registration to claim input VAT recovery

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% with input VAT credit) applies to goods physically exported from Switzerland, including cross-border e-commerce deliveries of maternity and infant care products to foreign consumers, provided the goods leave Swiss customs territory within 30 days of supply and proof of export is retained. Services directly connected to exported goods or performed wholly abroad are also zero-rated. Domestic online sales without genuine export documentation attract the standard 8.1% rate (or 2.6% reduced rate for qualifying baby food/medical supplies).

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / customs exit receipt)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment proof evidencing foreign buyer payment

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate in Switzerland. Swiss QR-bill (since 2020) is the preferred structured e-invoice format; PEPPOL/EBInterface exchange is used in B2B/B2G. VAT returns are filed electronically via the SIP portal or certified tax software on quarterly/semi-annual/annual cycles; no third-party clearance is required.

The ESTV audits export zero-rating claims and may retrospectively recharacterize the supply as domestic once export proof is insufficient; the withheld VAT (8.1%/2.6%) is clawed back with default interest of 4.5% p.a. Penalties: procedural fines up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 92 MWSTG); evasion surcharge up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 93 MWSTG); criminal prosecution for forged customs export documents under Swiss customs law (VStrR) with imprisonment up to 10 years in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland affords unrestricted convertibility for both current account and capital account operations. No exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior authorizations apply to cross-border fund transfers. The regulatory framework is anchored in the Swiss Constitution's free movement of capital, the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments, and Switzerland's adherence to the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Profit remittance is permitted without foreign-exchange restriction; Swiss withholding tax (35% on dividends) applies but is subject to treaty relief. Only statistical thresholds under the Swiss Payment Statistics Ordinance require banks to report payments exceeding CHF 3 million in a month or CHF 250,000 for certain non-resident transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion regulations exist for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances freely, settle trade debts in any currency, and repatriate funds without central-bank permission. Swiss money laundering reporting obligations require identification of beneficial owners for transactions above CHF 15,000 in monetary instruments, but treasury and corporate banking operations are not subject to any forced conversion regime.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank, in collaboration with the Federal Department of Finance and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex can serve as operational clearing rails for e-commerce disbursements only. They are not viable for capital escrow or treasury reserve purposes in Switzerland absent a Swiss FINMA fintech licence (Art. 1b Banking Act) or a full banking licence. These entities cannot legally hold client funds in segregated accounts for third-party treasury functions unless they maintain a Swiss branch with FINMA authorization. Their use is therefore restricted to secondary payment gateways, subject to bank-grade AML/KYC reviews by the primary Swiss relationship bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections into CH_AG, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 through a prime correspondent bank with explicit field 23B (Code: CRED), field 50K (originating beneficial owner), field 59 (beneficiary name and account at Swiss bank), and a purpose clause stating 'Capital Increase – Share Premium – Art. 632 CO'. Swiss banks require the submission of a capital increase attestation from the commercial register and the notarised bank confirmation of full subscription. For inbound capital transfers exceeding CHF 1 million, the Swiss National Bank's statistical reporting may be triggered, and the bank will require a short census form identifying the sender, beneficiary, and economic transaction code.Trade settlement disbursements are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 direct transfers or by documentary letter of credit (MT700), under UCP600. For low-value recurring supplier payments in Europe, SEPA Credit Transfer (everyday SEPA) is available because Switzerland participates as a non-EU SEPA member. For cross-border inventory procurement outside Europe, SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT GPI tracking is preferred, accompanied by commercial invoices and packing lists for bank-side value-chain verification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Swiss Federal Direct Corporate Income Tax: 8.5% charged on the after-tax (assessed) profit, equivalent to approximately 7.83% on the pre-tax base; in addition, each canton and commune levies corporate income tax, producing a combined effective headline rate for a commercial AG of approximately 11.9% (Canton of Zug) to approximately 21.1% (Canton of Zurich), depending on the registered seat and place of effective management.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) levied on the gross amount of cash dividends, stock dividends and constructive distributions made by the Swiss AG; capital gains realised by shareholders on disposal of the AG's shares are generally exempt from Swiss income tax for non-qualifying private shareholders under the treaty context.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within six months of the financial year-end; for a calendar-year AG the statutory standard deadline is 30 June of the following year, and an extension to approximately 30 November is routinely granted on request; late settlement of instalments bears default interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File, per OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines) must be prepared and maintained before the filing date where the annual volume of controlled cross-border transactions entered into by the Swiss AG exceeds CHF 2 million per tax year; Country-by-Country Reporting and notification obligations apply only to multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue is at least CHF 900 million (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply ESR-style substance codes and has no general statutory anti-avoidance rule, but economic substance is constitutive for tax residence (registered seat and place of effective management per Art. 50 DBG), for treaty entitlement (beneficial ownership and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test), for the participation exemption, and for avoidance of deemed-dividend withholding tax upon termination or relocation of the tax domicile. A Swiss AG without premises, staff, operational decision-making or commercially real inventory is exposed to classification as a domiciliary or shell company, denial of treaty relief, 35% withholding tax on the accumulated distributable reserves, and administrative strike-off by the commercial register authority. The Swiss Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax implements OECD Pillar Two for in-scope groups with consolidated revenue above the EUR 750 million threshold from 1 January 2024.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and concealed revenue (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the unpaid shortfall tax ('Nachsteuer') with default interest; a penalty equal to 33% to 100% of the evaded tax for ordinary negligence, rising to up to 300% for aggravated and systematic concealment; criminal prosecution under Art. 175 and Art. 186 DBG with fines up to CHF 1,000,000, publication of the conviction and confiscation of the evaded amounts; the discretionary assessment procedure under Art. 130 DBG adds a surcharge margin.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto manager are jointly and severally liable to the tax authorities in civil recovery where they wilfully or negligently failed to maintain proper books and records (Art. 754, 957 and 958 CO); personal criminal prosecution for tax evasion and, where forged invoices or false certifications are used, tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG carries imprisonment of up to three years; investigative measures may include asset seizure and a travel ban against the directors.
Constructive dividends and transfer pricing deviations (hidden profit distribution)

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing Swiss taxable profit with default interest; the recast payment is recharacterised as a constructive dividend and the 35% withholding tax is assessed on the AG, which remains liable where it failed to withhold; treaty relief for the excess payment is denied, and stamp duty consequences may arise on deemed contributions; administrative fines follow Art. 62 DBG and ESTV transfer pricing practice.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the non-arm's-length contracts are jointly and severally liable to the company for the resulting tax damage and penalties (Art. 754 CO); civil restitution may be ordered against them for the excessive fees, and where the deviation is part of a criminal plan involving the Hong Kong-linked buyer or agent, directors may face prosecution for qualified tax evasion or money laundering with imprisonment and extradition-arrest risk.
Shell company and absence of economic substance (domiciliary company)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration disregards the company's Swiss residence, applies treaty abuse rules and the general anti-avoidance doctrine, denies the participation exemption, withdraws DTA gross-up and refunds, and imposes a deemed dividend on the accumulated distributable reserves at 35% upon termination or relocation of the tax domicile; the administrative dissolution procedure of the commercial register (Art. 164 ff. HRegV) may strike the company off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the entire 35% withholding tax on the deemed distribution plus interest and penalties (Art. 12 VStG and Art. 754 CO); where the shell structure is used for cross-border tax evasion or money laundering, directors face criminal complicity charges, seizure of personal assets and travel bans pending the criminal investigation.
AML and unlicensed payment intermediary activity

Statutory sanction: FINMA enforcement action with discontinuation of operations, confiscation of unlawful profit, and criminal fines of up to CHF 500,000 under Art. 44 FinIA and the VStrR; conduct reaching the threshold of money laundering is prosecuted under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code with imprisonment, fines and confiscation of the laundered funds; the merchant accounts are frozen and the bank relationship terminated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permitted third parties to use the payment accounts are prosecuted as co-perpetrators of qualified money laundering and face imprisonment of up to three years, increased to five years for professional or band activity, together with disqualification from financial business activities; civil liability follows under Art. 754 CO for the resulting fines and sanctions, and the criminal authorities may impose international arrest warrants and travel bans against the responsible directors.
