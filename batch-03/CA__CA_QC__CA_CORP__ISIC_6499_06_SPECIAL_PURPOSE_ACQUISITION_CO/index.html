Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax (T2) and GST/HST, administered through the CRA 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca); and Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax, and payroll contributions, administered through the Revenu Québec 'Espace client' portal (revenuquebec.ca). Federal and provincial registrations are integrated with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec's Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and the CRA Business Number (BN).

E-government filing portal

A foreign sponsor must: (1) incorporate the SPAC under the CBCA or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises; (2) apply through CRA Business Registration Online for a BN with a GST/HST (RT) program account and a corporation income tax account; (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V and the same BN/NEQ; (4) open a payroll (RP) account if directors' fees are paid. Registration is fully electronic, is typically completed within 5-10 business days, requires no Canadian physical presence, and must be supported by the certificate of incorporation, the SPAC trust agreement, and identification of directors and officers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The CBCA/QBCA business corporation is a separate juridical person and a 'taxable Canadian corporation' resident in Canada, taxed under ITA Part I on worldwide income at a combined statutory headline rate of approximately 26.5% (federal general rate 15% plus Quebec general rate 11.5%). A listed SPAC is a 'public corporation' under ITA s. 89(1) and cannot elect CCPC status or claim the small business deduction; trust-escrow investment income is taxed at the corporate level, and non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% for portfolio dividends under the Canada-US treaty). Territorial Scope: no territorial exclusion applies to a resident corporation; foreign-source income is fully reportable on the T2 return. — A foreign sponsor must: (1) incorporate the SPAC under the CBCA or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises; (2) apply through CRA Business Registration Online for a BN with a GST/HST (RT) program account and a corporation income tax account; (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V and the same BN/NEQ; (4) open a payroll (RP) account if directors' fees are paid. Registration is fully electronic, is typically completed within 5-10 business days, requires no Canadian physical presence, and must be supported by the certificate of incorporation, the SPAC trust agreement, and identification of directors and officers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The CBCA/QBCA business corporation is a separate juridical person and a 'taxable Canadian corporation' resident in Canada, taxed under ITA Part I on worldwide income at a combined statutory headline rate of approximately 26.5% (federal general rate 15% plus Quebec general rate 11.5%). A listed SPAC is a 'public corporation' under ITA s. 89(1) and cannot elect CCPC status or claim the small business deduction; trust-escrow investment income is taxed at the corporate level, and non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% for portfolio dividends under the Canada-US treaty). Territorial Scope: no territorial exclusion applies to a resident corporation; foreign-source income is fully reportable on the T2 return.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification: corporate tax identity is statutory, not elective. Relevant mechanisms for a SPAC include: (i) voluntary GST/HST/QST registration under ETA s. 240(1) even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; (ii) the ETA s. 156 election to treat closely related Canadian corporations as a single GST/HST registrant; (iii) elections for annual or fiscal-year GST/HST reporting periods where the registrant qualifies; (iv) hybrid mismatch and earnings-stripping rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.6, which afford no protective exclusion for SPAC financing structures; and (v) section 216/217 elections available only to qualifying non-resident investors, not to the resident SPAC itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the identical GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold: registration is compulsory once worldwide taxable supplies (excluding exempt financial services) exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters. A SPAC's escrow interest income is not consideration for a taxable supply, but sponsor arrangement and trustee oversight fees may be taxable and can trigger the registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V and the corresponding QSTA provisions where the supplier exports goods from Canada and retains prescribed proof-of-export documentation within two years. For a SPAC, the operative rule is ETA Schedule VI Part IX s. 7: a supply of a financial service to a non-resident recipient (e.g., underwriting, sponsor-acquisition advisory, or trust-escrow management services billed to a non-resident target or investor) is zero-rated where the financial service is not for consumption in Canada; the SPAC must obtain contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (Form RC67-V, tax-residence certificate, or incorporation records), and CRA practice applies a 180-day benchmark for receipt of the foreign consideration.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or the CBSA CERS electronic export report) and carrier-issued Bill of Lading / waybill proving physical export
  • Commercial invoice identifying the non-resident buyer and the HS classification of any exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT wire records, or bank statements proving receipt of the foreign currency consideration
  • Non-resident status and purpose evidence: Form RC67-V (GST/HST certificate for non-resident recipients) or equivalent, plus the SPAC's trust agreement, underwriting agreement, and board minutes documenting the offshore supply

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-level API pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime (contrast the CFDI/NF-e models). Mandates include: (1) registrants must issue a prescribed tax invoice under ETA s. 221(1) and QSTA s. 235 showing the supplier's BN/NEQ, date, recipient identification for supplies above CAD 150, description, and tax amounts; (2) electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns is compulsory for corporate registrants and for those with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million, using CRA NETFILE/XML and Revenu Québec's electronic services; (3) both authorities rely on post-filing risk assessment and data matching rather than pre-clearance; (4) the federal Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (Peppol BIS 3.0) is available for B2B/B2G e-invoicing through certified service-provider APIs but remains voluntary as of 2025.

Export zero-rating claims are a priority audit area. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years (unlimited where misrepresentation is shown), retroactively claw back zero-rating and input tax credits, and charge interest under ETA s. 280. Civil penalties include the ETA s. 285 failure/incorrect-filing penalty, the ETA s. 327.1 false-statement penalty of 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded, and the ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax avoided. Criminal exposure arises under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud), with fines and imprisonment; Revenu Québec applies parallel penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act and may suspend or revoke the QST registration. Directors and the SPAC sponsor are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST/QST under ETA s. 323 and the equivalent Quebec regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; no exchange-control law, capital-inflow licensing, or profit-remittance approval exists. Dividends, redemption proceeds, and escrowed SPAC trust funds may be remitted cross-border freely, subject only to Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) CRS/FATCA reporting and Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25 percent base rate, treaty-reducible). The floating CAD regime implies that the SPAC treasury must budget for spot-rate volatility on USD/CAD trust rebalancing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable - Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or other foreign receipts. A Quebec-domiciled SPAC may retain, convert, and disburse export or investment proceeds in any currency without Bank of Canada authorization.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency; monetary policy, payment-system oversight, and banking supervision are distributed among the Bank of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for Schedule I chartered banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border electronic-fund-transfer and AML/ATF reporting, and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) of Quebec as principal securities regulator for the SPAC reporting issuer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary banking rail; permissible only as an ancillary FX and collection utility. Canada does not recognize offshore electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses; platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex may provide multi-currency wallets and spot conversion, but they are not Schedule I chartered banks and cannot act as escrow agent or custodian for a reporting-issuer SPAC trust, nor satisfy AMF/TSX escrow conditions. Any fintech usage must be confined to non-trust operational disbursements with segregated client funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% plus Quebec 11.5% for a combined general statutory rate of 26.5% (subject to provincial abatement and allocation adjustments; effective combined rate varies by province and active business income level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII withholding tax: 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to reduction under the Canada-Quebec income tax treaty to 15% generally, 5% for corporate shareholders holding at least 10% voting control, and 0% for certain treaty exemptions; a 25% Quebec withholding tax also applies on distributions to non-residents outside treaty protection.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal T2: no later than two months after the tax year-end for most corporations, or three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) with a qualifying tax year-end; a six-month extension of filing deadline is available to CCPCs only. Quebec CO-17: due on the last day of the third month following the taxation year-end for CCPCs, otherwise on the last day of the second month following the taxation year-end. Payment of balance due is generally due the same day as the filing deadline without extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in annual gross revenue from transactions with non-resident related parties (Quebec and federal) triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File required when total group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million. Aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 5 million require full contemporaneous documentation under CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and Revenu Québec's TP guidance.

Economic substance scope: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec equivalent under section 1079.1 of the Quebec Taxation Act may recharacterize a SPAC arrangement lacking economic substance. Case law (e.g., Canada Trustco, Copthorne, and the 2020 amendments broadening GAAR) applies a two-step test: a tax benefit exists and the transaction is an avoidance transaction that defeats the object, spirit, or purpose of the relevant provisions. For a Quebec SPAC, Economic Substance Regulations similar to those in other provinces are not codified, but Quebec's anti-avoidance provisions and the federal 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine in tax matters require genuine commercial activity or investment purpose; a SPAC that is a bare holding company without any real Canadian office, employees, or investment activities may be deemed a sham and subject to reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus interest; criminal prosecution under section 238 of the Income Tax Act and 75.2 of the Quebec Taxation Act subject to a fine of not less than 50% of the evaded tax and up to 200% thereof, and imprisonment of between 2 and 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for withholding tax deductions and unpaid corporate tax liabilities under section 227.1 ITA and 30.0.6 of the Quebec Taxation Act; civil assessment of up to 100% of the unremitted amounts, travel bans under Quebec tax enforcement procedures, and imprisonment for willful non-remittance.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the tax benefit, recharacterization of the SPAC's income as the shareholder's income, 50% gross-negligence penalty, and potential application of the 'factual entity' doctrine permitting the CRA or Revenu Québec to ignore the separate identity of the shell corporation.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the entire corporate tax deficiency if the corporation is found to be a sham or if directors signed false returns; Quebec civil liability under the Civil Code of Quebec (art. 317) permits an order for restitution and punitive damages; criminal exposure up to 5 years imprisonment.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering and Unlicensed Investment Business

Statutory sanction: Provincial securities commission cease-trade orders, administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 15,000,000 per violation under the Quebec Securities Act, or CAD 5,000,000 per contravention under federal AML law; criminal fines of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for failure to report suspicious transactions or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors of a SPAC that executes an unlawful merger without regulatory approval may be personally liable for investor losses; the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) may impose lifetime bans on serving as officers or directors and enforce personal monetary sanctions under the Quebec Securities Act.
Failure to File or False Statements

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty equal to 5% of the unpaid amount plus 1% per month (maximum 12 months), repeat-filing penalty increased to 10% plus 2% per month; false-statement penalties under section 163(2) of 50% of the understated liability; Quebec penalties under section 1079.1.1 of up to 200% of the amount evaded, and criminal fine of up to CAD 200,000.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign tax returns with knowledge of false particulars are personally liable for the penalties and may be prosecuted criminally; travel bans issued by Revenu Québec can prevent departure from Canada for delinquency; imprisonment of up to 2 years for evasion and 12 months for failure to comply with a production demand.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Canadian resident corporation is liable for the entire unwithheld amount under subsection 215(1) of the Income Tax Act and Quebec equivalent, plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the unremitted amount; the non-resident shareholder may also be assessed directly and subjected to garnishment of Canadian assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve undisclosed dividend distributions without an appropriate withholding remittance are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under section 227.1 ITA; in egregious cases, directors personally owe the full tax, interest, and penalties, and may be prohibited from trading or leaving Canada until payment is secured.
