Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance (administering the Ontario 8% provincial component of HST under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement, with the CRA as sole collector and administrator).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian business corporation obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN), which serves as the statutory TIN, through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service. A GST/HST account (BN with 'RT' suffix) is opened simultaneously or subsequently via BRO or Form RC1/RC1A. Non-resident incorporated entities operating in Canada must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) and, where carrying on business in Canada, register for GST/HST once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; non-resident suppliers of 'specified supplies' (digital services and intangibles such as SaaS) are subject to the simplified registration framework (ETA s. 240.1) once total worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or preceding four calendar quarters. Registration requires a Canadian business address or, for non-residents, a Canadian representative/agent, plus identification of directors and material ownership. The CRA assigns the corporate income tax account (denoted 'RC' and remittance file RC0001), and, where applicable, payroll ('RP') and customs/import-export ('RM') accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; classification of a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act is statutory and mandatory. Available elections include: (i) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election, permitting a qualifying corporation to compute Canadian tax in US dollars or euros; (ii) GST/HST voluntary registration election by a small supplier (ETA s. 240(2)); (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (iv) designation of 'associated corporations' under ITA s. 256 for allocation of the small business deduction; and (v) hybrid-mismatch compliance under Part XVI.2 ITA and the Part VI.1 Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses (EIFE) limitation regime (effective for 2024+ taxation years), which follow statutory anti-avoidance designations, not elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. For resident and non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada, the 'small supplier' threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding financial services and supplies of capital property) in the preceding four calendar quarters or the current quarter; non-residents soliciting orders or selling digital services to Canadian recipients are also caught. For the simplified non-resident registration regime (ETA s. 240.1), the threshold is CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies of the person and associated persons in the current or preceding four calendar quarters, with no Canadian permanent establishment required. Registration is mandatory immediately upon breaching the threshold; voluntary registration is available below the threshold, subject to CRA approval.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of the fintech SaaS application (a supply of intangible personal property) to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10 ETA where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident; (b) the recipient is not registered under Subdivision d of Division V of Part IX ETA; and (c) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the supply is made. Ancillary analytics/data-processing services exported to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 ETA, subject to the same Canadian-property exclusions. The supply of software as such is taxable (not exempt as a 'financial service' under Schedule V, Part VII), so zero-rating is available only on export substantiation. The supplier must document that the recipient is non-resident; mere knowledge or ordinary business practice is insufficient for CRA audit tolerance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS) where goods are contemporaneously exported, and corresponding Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Executed SaaS Master Subscription Agreement / Service Contract stating the non-resident recipient, governing law, and absence of Canadian situs of performance
  • Commercial invoice expressly indicating 'Zero-rated — Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act' with the supplier's 9-digit Business Number and the recipient's foreign address
  • Non-resident status evidence (foreign business registry extract, tax-residence certificate, foreign IP/billing address records, or W-8-style self-certification)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT-ACH bank records evidencing actual conversion of export proceeds at 0% GST/HST within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API-based transaction clearance, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal or Ontario level. Canada is voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on the Peppol/EN 16931 standard. Taxpayers must retain electronic records in a readable and reproducible format for six years (CRA e-audit practice), and GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or the CRA 'My Business Account' API where turnover exceeds prescribed thresholds; no transaction-level reporting to a government clearance node is required.

CRA may reassess within 4 years, extended to 10 years in the case of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default or fraud. Where zero-rating documentation is insufficient, the CRA reverses the rate and assesses 13% Ontario HST on the full consideration, plus compound daily interest at the prescribed rate (corporate rate, currently 9% per annum), and penalties: ETA s. 280 failure-to-register penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 2% of taxable supplies made during the unregistered period; ETA s. 285 gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; and ETA s. 327 criminal tax-evasion penalties of up to CAD 25,000 fine and 2 years imprisonment on summary conviction, or CAD 100,000 fine and 5 years on indictment. Export records must be retained for 6 years; clawback additionally exposes the taxpayer to transfer-pricing reassessments under ITA s. 247 (10% to 25% penalties on net transfer-pricing adjustments) and, for excessive leveraging tied to export financing structures, Part VI.1 EIFE disallowances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions. Canada does not operate a foreign exchange rationing system or capital control approval mechanism. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayments, and capital repatriation to non-residents are lawful, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (25% statutory rate, reduced under the Canada-US Tax Treaty) and to the standard filing and reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act, the Canada Business Corporations Act, and FINTRAC thresholds. No residual currency conversion permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion timeline. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain foreign currency revenue in foreign currency bank accounts located in Canada or abroad. Any physical transfer of monetary instruments or precious assets exceeding CAD 10,000 across the border must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations. Receipts from digital services sales are treated as export-like revenue for GST/HST purposes but are not subject to any surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising federally regulated deposit-taking banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administering cross-border currency and monetary instrument reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate for auxiliary USD/EUR payout uses, but not as a primary CAD clearing rail. Non-Canadian electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital wallets are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot provide direct Bank of Canada settlement access. To offer money-transfer services into Canada, a foreign EMI must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC or use a Canadian payment service provider licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). For this wealth-tracking fintech, an EMI can handle low-value global disbursements, while all CAD settlement and tax-related treasury flows should remain in Schedule I bank accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (for services) or CAD 5 million (for goods). For lower thresholds, a simplified TP disclosure (Form T106) is required if transactions exceed CAD 1 million in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial substance, and the 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) status requires active business income to qualify for SBD. A shell company with no real operations or employees may be deemed to have a permanent establishment in Canada and be subject to full taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax (gross negligence penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, reassess income to the beneficial owner, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax avoided. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; may face civil penalties and potential criminal prosecution.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to $500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years if they knowingly fail to implement AML compliance programs.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, plus penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000 or 10% of gross revenue). Interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading TP documentation; civil liability for restitution of corporate tax shortfalls.
