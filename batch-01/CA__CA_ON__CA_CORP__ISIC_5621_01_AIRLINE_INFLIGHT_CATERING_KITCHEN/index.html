Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account / MyBA and GST/HST NETFILE portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial HST revenue reconciliation through the federal CRA administration

E-government filing portal

For an Ontario-incorporated or federal-incorporated business corporation, obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by registering online through the CRA Business Registration Online service, by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or by using Form RC459 for non-resident applicants. The BN is the statutory tax identifier (TIN); add program accounts RC (corporation income tax), RP (GST/HST), and RT (payroll) as needed. A foreign-owned Canadian corporation will require a Canadian resident director or in certain cases may apply for a BN before incorporation via the provincial/federal registry. CRA generally requires the corporation’s Articles of Incorporation and registered office address for Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; a corporation is always taxed as a separate entity under the Income Tax Act. Elections available include the section 85 income tax rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of eligible property to a taxable Canadian corporation, the section 125(7) election/status determination for Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) classification, and the section 216 election only for particular non-resident earnings streams. Hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 apply automatically; no elective opt-out is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters for small supplier exception; registration becomes mandatory once the supplier exceeds the CAD 30,000 threshold unless the supplies are all zero-rated or the supplier is a small supplier. Non-resident carriers and zero-rated exporters may still elect to register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% GST/HST where a registered supplier sells tangible food products, beverages, and in-flight commissary stock to a non-resident or international airline carrier for export from Canada, and the goods are physically exported before any use or consumption in Canada. For an airport bonded in-flight catering kitchen supplying international flights, the zero-rating is sustainable if the meal inventory is loaded onto the outbound international aircraft pursuant to an air waybill or cargo manifest, titling passes to the airline customer, and payment is collected from the foreign counterparty. Domestic flight catering supplies are subject to Ontario 13% HST. Offshore services performed outside Canada for a non-resident person may also be zero-rated under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration or authorized commercial invoice equivalent for non-controlled goods
  • Air Waybill / Bill of Lading evidencing carriage to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement/wire statement evidencing receipt from the foreign airline
  • Aircraft loading manifest and CRA proof-of-export records
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and catering delivery confirmation

Canada does not mandate real-time continuous transaction controls, API-based fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Electronic invoices are legally acceptable if retained in legible, CRA-accessible format. GST/HST returns are remitted via CRA electronic filings (GST34/GST/HST NETFILE), and EDI/PDF invoices are sufficient provided the supplier maintains an adequate audit trail evidencing export status.

CRA may reassess retroactively within four years, extended to ten years for misrepresentation or fraud, and will deny the zero-rating entirely where export documentary evidence is incomplete or internally inconsistent. False export claims attract interest, civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act including 50% gross negligence penalties on refundable or recoverable amounts, and possible criminal sanctions for knowingly making false or misleading statements. If zero-rating is reversed, the taxpayer must remit the 13% HST, interest, and penalties, resulting in a full clawback of input tax credits previously claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance (dividends, interest, royalties, proceeds from share sales) is unrestricted from an FX perspective, but is subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25% unless reduced by tax treaty, e.g., 5% to 15%), corporate solvency and board approval tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA), and mandatory FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers or cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000. No special clearance or FX conversion certificate is needed to repatriate funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts at Canadian financial institutions. For statistical/customs compliance, goods exported from Canada with a value of CAD 2,000 or more must be reported through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). The reports are non-monetary and do not require proceeds to be salted or converted into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) is the central bank and official issuer of Canadian dollars; foreign-exchange administration is distributed among the Minister of Finance (Currency Act), the Bank of Canada (management of official international reserves), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (banking supervision and prudential liquidity rules), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/CTF surveillance). Canada has no dedicated central foreign-exchange-control authority, and no administrative approval is required for capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for a primary treasury. Canada does not confer European-style EMI status. Non-bank payment service providers must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and generally must partner with a Schedule I bank to offer account-like services. A foreign offshore EMI without Canadian MSB registration cannot legally maintain virtual accounts on behalf of a Canadian business. Use of such EMI may be tolerated for ancillary supplier cards, but the core cross-border liquidity clearing, capital injection, and trade settlement must run through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a licensed Canadian MSB to satisfy AML/KYC and contractual aviation-trade requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the foreign shareholder/bank account into the Canadian operational account. The receiving Canadian bank will require clear remittance instructions: beneficiary bank and account, beneficiary legal name exactly matching the corporate agreement, purpose code provided in the Option F field or in the payment narrative (e.g., 'Equity Injection', 'Shareholder Loan', 'Capital Contribution'). There is no Canadian statutory purpose-code framework analogous to SAFE; the bank's AML system will verify the source of funds, the payer identity, and the stated purpose. For a shareholder loan, the borrower must ensure the loan documentation is dated and signed prior to receipt, the CBCA/OBCA solvency test is satisfied, and the loan proceeds are not deemed a taxable benefit or deemed dividend under Income Tax Act subsections 15(2) and 80.2. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate income tax (Federal Part I 15% + Ontario 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents. Treaty relief may lower this to 0% (certain foreign-parent structures that meet 5% ownership) to 15% (portfolio dividends) depending on the applicable tax convention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents (applicable threshold for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation across a Local File and Master File).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the economic substance doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s.245) and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting framework. Intercompany arrangements must be commercially rational, the catering kitchen must have real employees, premises, and business risk; transactions that are circular, artificially shifted, or lacking a profit motive can be recharacterized and subject to the GAAR penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty under ITA s.239: 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus interest, criminal fines up to $200,000, and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment (or 2 years on summary conviction).

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.160 for taxes never assessed to the corporation where they orchestrated the evasion. CRA may issue director compliance orders, freeze assets, and institute travel bans in egregious circumstances; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment of the responsible director personally.
Shell Company / Abusive Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR penalty under ITA s.245(7) of 50% of the tax benefit recharacterized, in addition to full reassessment and interest. The CRA can also apply the newly-expanded mandatory disclosure rules for abusive planning.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a shelter scheme lose the protection of the corporate veil. They can be held personally liable for the GAAR penalty under s.245(6) if they did not deal at arm's length; civil asset freezing and non-citizen travel bans under the Offshore Tax Integrity legislation may apply.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) of up to CAD 500,000 per violation for a corporation; additional penalties for each day of continued failure.

Director personal liability: Senior officers may be individually fined up to CAD 100,000 and imprisoned for up to 6 months for failure to report or for obstruction; directors who authorized non-arms-length cash flows can be subject to asset forfeiture and travel restrictions, piercing the usual limited liability shield.
