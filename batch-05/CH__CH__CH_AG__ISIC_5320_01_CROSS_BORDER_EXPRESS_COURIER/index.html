Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of the Company's seat (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce du canton de Zoug), operating under the aegis of the Federal Commercial Register Office in the Federal Office of Justice and the cantonal Office for Economic Affairs and Labour.

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Certified deed of incorporation (öffentliche Urkunde / acte authentique) comprising the Articles of Association (Statuten / statuts) and the subsequent commercial registration application (Anmeldung / réquisition) submitted electronically via the Swiss Commercial Register portal (EHRA).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000. At incorporation no less than CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up in cash and deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto / compte bloqué) maintained with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank; the bank's escrow confirmation must be filed. The remaining contribution may consist of contributions in kind, supported, where required, by a sworn auditor's report under article 635 CO.

Funding & unlock path: Cantonal fire-safety certification issued by the fire-police (Feuerpolizei) covering extinguishing systems, escape routes and dangerous-goods storage; structural-load inspection of conveyor systems and racking under structural norms SIA 260–265; occupational-health and workplace clearance confirmed by the competent cantonal labour inspectorate and the employer-liability insurer (Suva) under the Labour Act (ArG / LTr) and EKAS Guidelines.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV/FTA), Federal Department of Finance. VAT registration portal: ESTV VAT e-Service (https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/vat-online-services).

E-government filing portal

A foreign express-courier entity must apply to ESTV for a Swiss VAT number (MWST/TVA/IVA) when its worldwide taxable turnover from non-exempt activities exceeds CHF 100,000 or when it supplies services in Switzerland and is required to charge Swiss VAT. The application is made through ESTV eService and, for foreign entities without a Swiss registered office, must be accompanied by a power of attorney appointing a Swiss tax representative (fiscal domiciliary agent). If the entity is not already in the Swiss commercial register with a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), ESTV will issue a special VAT-UID. Registration must occur within 30 days after the threshold is exceeded; SFTA issues a written registration confirmation with effect from the beginning of the calendar year following the qualifying turnover (or from the actual start of taxable activity).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss tax law contains no US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections; an AG is statutorily subject to entity-level income tax. Available elections include: (i) VAT group taxation (Art. 21 VAT Act) for closely linked partners, subsidiaries, or branches to form one taxable person; (ii) simplified VAT accounting methods (Art. 34 VAT Act) for businesses with a maximum turnover of CHF 250,000 in the previous year; (iii) the small-business exemption election (Art. 37 VAT Act) for entities with annual Swiss and worldwide turnover below CHF 100,000; (iv) option to tax supplies otherwise exempt from VAT if the exemption leads to distortion of competition, under certain cantonal practice; and (v) the qualified domestic top-up tax election (Global Minimum Tax Act) for Pillar Two purposes effective 1 January 2025.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover from non-exempt supplies (Art. 10 VAT Act); a non-established supplier is separately required to register if it provides electronically supplied services to Swiss consumers and the worldwide remuneration from such supplies in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000; voluntary registration is available below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with input VAT deduction) applies to direct export of goods from Switzerland to foreign countries, including parcels transported out of Switzerland by express courier where the recipient is located abroad and has the right of disposal over the goods outside Switzerland. It also covers the supply of international transport services and services directly connected with exported goods (e.g., international freight, parcel consolidation, customs brokerage and documentation) where the service is supplied to a foreign business or is an essential cost of the export movement. The supplier must prove the goods physically left Swiss customs territory and that the service remuneration is attributable to that export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss customs export declaration via e-dec/NCTS, containing the MRN and exit confirmation for the consignment)
  • Commercial invoice and transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or express courier shipment manifest with tracking scan showing foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement/payment proof evidencing receipt of the export consideration from abroad
  • Order/contract and proof of recipient foreign status (contract, destination address, and evidence that the recipient is a business or private person outside Switzerland with right of disposal)
  • Export proof per written consignment where customs administration requires item-by-item exemption evidence (aggregated periodical export summaries may be accepted under approved procedures)

Switzerland does not operate a real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for VAT invoices. There is no B2B/B2C electronic-invoicing mandate; however, suppliers to Swiss federal administrative units must follow the Federal e-Invoicing specifications (eBill/Peppol-based) on applicable procurement contracts. Invoices must preserve authenticity of origin and integrity of content; the QR-bill and Swiss eBill formats are accepted electronic invoice channels. No pre-clearance, API-based tax clearance, or continuous transaction controls apply. Documentary records must be archived for 10 years and be ready for retrospective VAT/customs audits.

The Federal Tax Administration and Federal Customs Administration audit export zero-rating retrospectively; the burden of proof lies on the supplier. If the export documentation or the characteristic of export is missing or incorrect, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT is assessed at 8.1% on the gross consideration, plus default interest at 4.5% per annum from the date VAT was due. Deliberate or wilful false zero-rating, forged customs declarations, or intent to obtain an unlawful VAT refund constitutes VAT tax evasion under Art. 70 and Art. 71 VAT Act, carrying a monetary fine of up to CHF 30,000 (or up to three times the evaded tax in serious cases) and, in customs matters, penalties under the Customs Act and Swiss Criminal Code for forgery, potentially including imprisonment. The retention record obligation is 10 years; without an inward remittance within the customary 180-day documentary window, the SFTA may treat the proceeds as not received and disallow input VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under IMF Article VIII and the OECD Code of Liberalisation. There are no exchange controls, no import/export clearance requirements, no restrictions on payments, and no profit remittance approval mechanism. Dividends and branch profits are freely remitted in any currency; however, dividend distributions from a Swiss AG remain subject to Swiss federal withholding tax at 35%, with relief available under an applicable double-tax treaty or by tax-resident refund procedure. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections, are also unrestricted and do not require SNB exchange permission. The SNB monitors cross-border financial flows for statistical purposes and may levy negative interest on sight deposits held at the SNB, but this does not restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit for bringing export proceeds into Switzerland. Export receipts may be retained abroad indefinitely in any currency, converted freely, used for offshore working capital, or settled through Swiss banks. There is no SNB or Federal Administration obligation to file conversion declarations for ordinary trade receivables, although cross-border direct investment and balance-of-payment reporting thresholds may require statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque Nationale Suisse

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — not viable as the primary settlement bank. FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech companies under Banking Act Art. 1b may take in restricted deposits up to CHF 100 million and offer payment, FX, and card services; they can be useful supplementary rails, but they do not grant SIC membership, capital escrow, or commercial lending. Foreign/offshore digital EMIs are generally not passported into Switzerland and must obtain a Swiss FINMA license or be limited to non-Swiss, non-account-linked services; they cannot legally hold the AG's CHF treasury deposits on an operational basis without Swiss banking/correspondent arrangements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal rate 8.5% on net profit after federal tax; combined federal/cantonal/communal ordinary rate approx. 11.9%–21.6% by canton (Zug approx. 11.9%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax on gross dividends

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within 6 months after fiscal year-end; statutory extension available upon written request to cantonal tax authority

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold; OECD-compliant TP documentation recommended for any cross-border related-party transaction, and mandatory in practice when transactions exceed CHF 3 million gross

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as enacted in GAAR jurisdictions are not directly applicable in Switzerland; Swiss anti-abuse doctrine, MLI/principal purpose test, and substance requirements under treaty benefit entitlement impose analogous substance checks on courier logistics entities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Back taxes plus interest; administrative penalty of up to 3x evaded tax; criminal prosecution with monetary fine up to CHF 30,000

Director personal liability: Civil restitution to tax authority; travel bans; potential custodial sentence up to 3 years under Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of profit to owner jurisdiction; unwinding of Swiss non-recognition and possible re-assessment at source

Director personal liability: Direct claims for unpaid taxes where artificial arrangements are found; prohibition from acting as directors for up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's length profit; hidden profit distribution (also from shareholder perspective) causing additional 35% withholding tax liability on deemed dividend

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability for withholding tax on constructive dividend if not reported and withheld
AML / Payment Processing Breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under AMLA up to CHF 500,000 for institutions; suspension of courier license; confiscation of illicit proceeds

Director personal liability: Personal fines up to CHF 5 million for committed AML breaches; mandatory disqualification from senior roles in Swiss financial intermediaries
