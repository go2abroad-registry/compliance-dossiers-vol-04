Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration via the My Business Account portal (canada.ca/revenue-agency); Ontario Ministry of Finance administers the Ontario HST component (harmonized with CRA), Ontario Retail Sales Tax (RST 8%) and Employer Health Tax (EHT); Ontario Business Registry (ServiceOntario) is the provincial business registration body; federal Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada and Ontario Office of the Integrity Commissioner administer lobbying registration statutes, not tax.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident LP tax identity acquisition in 8 steps: (1) appoint a Canadian representative (individual, accounting firm or customs broker) under ETA s. 221.1(1) where the LP lacks physical presence in Canada; (2) register with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account number (BN suffixed with 'RT'); (3) concurrently file an Ontario Extra-Provincial LP declaration with the Ontario Business Registry under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act to obtain an Ontario registration number; (4) file CRA Form RC59 (Non-Resident Business Registration) or the non-resident T2/T5013 package and appoint a 'designated member' for service of documents; (5) mandatory GST/HST registration if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, otherwise voluntary unless the LP solicits business in Canada or has a permanent establishment; (6) register the lobbying business with the federal Lobbyists Registry (Commissioner of Lobbying) and the Ontario Lobbyists Registry (OLRA) if the LP conducts paid lobbying of federal or Ontario public office holders; (7) open a Canadian settlement bank account (or use a foreign correspondent account) to remit HST through CRA My Payment and to receive retainer wires; (8) file initial GST/HST return (Form GST34) electronically within one month after the end of the first reporting period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: for income tax purposes, the Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96 - the LP itself pays no federal or provincial income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocable share of income. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is a separate 'person' under ETA s. 272 and must independently register, collect, and remit HST on its taxable supplies. Territorial scope: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income allocable through the LP and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on management or advisory fees paid by the LP. No tax-exempt zone or Ontario tax holiday applies to cross-border trade policy analysis or government-affairs lobbying services.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; partnership fiscality is mandatory for Canadian partnerships. Available statutory elections for the LP: (i) ETA s. 156 election - the LP and a closely related registered entity may elect to treat inter-entity taxable supplies as nil for GST/HST purposes; (ii) ITA s. 249.1(4) election - the LP may adopt a non-calendar fiscal period if the business cycle justifies CRA approval, affecting the allocation of income to partners; (iii) non-resident partners may file an NR6 form and elect to compute Canadian-source business income tax on a net basis under ITA ss. 216/217 instead of flat Part XIII withholding, or claim treaty relief under an applicable tax treaty; (iv) hybrid mismatch deeming rules (OECD BEPS Action 2, implemented through ITA ss. 18.3, 18.4, 94, 95) apply automatically to deny deductions for payments that are deductible in Canada but excluded or deferred in the foreign partner's jurisdiction - no affirmative election is required; (v) for GST/HST, the LP may elect under ETA s. 167(1) to treat a sale of the business as a taxable supply, and under ETA s. 156 to streamline inter-entity HST flows with a closely related parent. Ontario offers no separate provincial elections.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada complies with IMF Article VIII and operates a floating exchange-rate regime with fully liberalized current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no exchange-control approval, prior-license requirement, or surrender obligation for non-resident capital subscriptions into an Ontario Limited Partnership, nor for the conversion of CAD into foreign currency at market rates. The only substantive frictions are fiscal: (i) Canadian-source business income allocable to non-resident partners is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act, generally via a T5013 partnership return and NR-4/NR-6 filings; (ii) certain remittances (management fees, distributions, interest, rents, royalties) may attract Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, reduced by an applicable income tax treaty; and (iii) the Ontario Limited Partnership itself, although tax-transparent, must be properly disclosed to the bank for financial reporting and to FINTRAC for beneficial-ownership diligence. No exchange-control act restricts profit remittance, and no waiting period or repatriation tax applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or conversion ratio on export proceeds or operating receipts. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no successor regime requires a timeline for converting foreign-currency receivables into CAD. Exporters and cross-border service providers (including a lobbying/consultancy partnership) may retain receipts in USD or other currencies indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at any authorized Canadian Schedule I bank, with 0% mandatory conversion and no deadline. The only reporting obligation is FINTRAC's cross-border EFT and terrorist-property reporting threshold (CAD 10,000 or greater within a 24-hour period), which is a compliance-reporting duty rather than a convertibility restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in agency on behalf of the Minister of Finance for the Exchange Fund Account and for the vaulting and settlement of Canadian-dollar finality under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act; the day-to-day enforcement of cross-border currency movement reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), while the Minister of Finance retains residual authority under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury use, MEDIUM for supplementary payout rails. A foreign digital EMI (for example, Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer) is not a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank and cannot provide settlement finality on CAD LVTS rails. Under the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers serving Canadian clients are being brought under Bank of Canada registration and oversight with a phased transition; an Ontario-registered LP using a foreign EMI as its principal account would fail the banks' correspondent 'KYC-on-KYC' expectations and FINTRAC's expectation of a documented domestic account relationship. The viable structure is to maintain the LP's treasury spine at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using a regulated EMI only for fractional payout automation (small contractor disbursements, foreign-currency localization, procurement cards) through an API-linked feeder, with no more than 5% of monthly treasury volume running through EMI rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident partner's capital injection into the Ontario LP account: the remitting partner instructs its bank to send a SWIFT MT103 to the LP's account at the designated Canadian Schedule I bank, quoting the LP's legal name and Ontario Limited Partnership Declaration number in Field 59 and the General Partner's legal name in Field 50K/52a; Field 70 must include a payment descriptor such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / PARTNERSHIP SUBSCRIPTION / CAPITAL CALL NO. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury use, MEDIUM for supplementary payout rails. A foreign digital EMI (for example, Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer) is not a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank and cannot provide settlement finality on CAD LVTS rails. Under the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers serving Canadian clients are being brought under Bank of Canada registration and oversight with a phased transition; an Ontario-registered LP using a foreign EMI as its principal account would fail the banks' correspondent 'KYC-on-KYC' expectations and FINTRAC's expectation of a documented domestic account relationship. The viable structure is to maintain the LP's treasury spine at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using a regulated EMI only for fractional payout automation (small contractor disbursements, foreign-currency localization, procurement cards) through an API-linked feeder, with no more than 5% of monthly treasury volume running through EMI rails.
