Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and the CRA Import-Export (RM) program account gateway. The Ontario Ministry of Finance sets Ontario corporate income tax policy under the Ontario Tax Administration Agreement, but the CRA administers, collects and enforces all Ontario corporate income tax and HST; no separate Ontario tax identifier is issued to a Business Corporation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA or register online through Business Registration Online (BRO), the one-stop federal-provincial portal. Step 2: A non-resident foreign lessor must provide its certificate of incorporation or equivalent constitutional documents, a certified list of directors (Form RC451), a Canadian address for service or an appointed agent for service, and a description of the proposed aircraft-engine leasing activities in Canada. Step 3: The CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import/export), each denoted by a two-letter suffix. Step 4: Register for GST/HST before the first taxable dry-lease supply is made in Canada if total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or if the non-resident carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits on engine acquisition costs. Step 5: Use the BN for CBSA export reporting (Form B13A / Automated Export Declaration) and for GST/HST returns filed electronically. Step 6: The CRA administers Ontario corporate tax on the same BN; a permanent establishment in Ontario creates an Ontario Part I tax allocation obligation but no separate provincial ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp., ELF 8615) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada); it is not a pass-through entity and no check-the-box classification exists. Resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income, including lease rentals from engines situated or used in Canada. A non-resident corporation carrying on a Canadian business is subject to Part I tax on its Canadian business income and an additional 25% Part XIV branch tax (treaty-reducible) on the after-tax branch earnings. Dividends paid to shareholders are separately taxed; the entity itself does not flow income through to owners for Canadian tax purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. Available statutory elections and deeming mechanisms include: (1) s. 216(1) ITA election by a non-resident to compute Canadian tax on net rental income from real or leased tangible property instead of Part XIII withholding; (2) s. 217 ITA election for non-resident employees; (3) s. 219(2) ITA election to reduce Part XIV branch tax under an applicable tax treaty; (4) s. 115.1 ITA election for a tax-deferred rollover of Canadian business property into a Canadian corporation; (5) ss. 91(5) and 95(2) ITA foreign-affiliate and FAPI grouping elections affecting hybrid entity determinations; and (6) GST/HST group registration elections under ss. 156-157 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are addressed through the s. 95(1) hybrid entity rules, the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) regime and the general anti-avoidance rule under s. 245 ITA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ss. 148(1) and 240 of the Excise Tax Act, computed on the aggregate value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada and taxable supplies made outside Canada in the course of carrying on a business during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident lessor making a taxable dry lease supply of an aircraft engine in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded or if it carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; below the threshold, registration is voluntary but is commonly elected to recover input tax credits on Canadian engine acquisition and maintenance costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property, including a dry lease of a turbofan aircraft engine, is zero-rated under the export provisions of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the engine is exported by the lessor, or delivered to a common carrier in Canada for export, and is exported as soon as reasonably possible after the supply. Aircraft and aircraft parts supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada, or in international commercial aviation, may also qualify for zero-rating. The lessor must establish that the dry lease is a supply of tangible personal property, the lessee is a non-resident, the engine is not consumed, used or installed in Canada, and no part of the supply is made in Canada. An engine never brought into Canada falls outside ETA scope and bears no HST; an engine physically delivered in Ontario generates 13% HST on the rental consideration unless a specific zero-rating provision is satisfied. The lessor must obtain contemporaneous documentary proof of export and no-Canadian-use within a reasonable period after the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Form B13A or the Automated Export Declaration (AEI) transmitted electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency before departure of the engine from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by a licensed carrier evidencing physical export of the aircraft engine from Canada to the non-resident lessee's jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 advice issued by the Canadian bank evidencing repatriation of lease rental proceeds into the Canadian lessor's account within the repatriation deadline

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory government invoice clearance platform, and no digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian Peppol interoperability standard. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants using NETFILE / GST/HST e-file and to T2 corporate income tax returns for corporations with gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million. Every GST/HST registrant must maintain books and records in Canada, in English or French, for six years, including invoices, bills of lading, export declarations, lease contracts, and bank records supporting each zero-rated export.

The CRA may reassess and claw back export zero-rating relief, together with reverse-charged 13% HST, compound arrears interest, and penalties, within 4 years of an assessment, or within 10 years where neglect, misrepresentation or fraud is shown. Penalties include: Excise Tax Act s. 280 - a 25% false-statement penalty on the amount claimed; s. 285 - a gross-negligence penalty; s. 327.23 - criminal tax evasion fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; and the denial of input tax credits attributable to the engine purchase. The lessor bears the evidentiary burden of proving continuous export, non-Canadian use, and timely repatriation of lease rentals. Failure to produce the B13A declaration, bills of lading and bank MT103 records within the 180-day administrative benchmark will, in practice, trigger a reassessment, reversal of zero-rating, and recovery of the full 13% HST together with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible and fully tradable in global markets. Canada maintains no general exchange control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act. Profit remittances by an Ontario business corporation may be made without exchange control approval, but are subject to normal Canadian withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII on dividends and Canada-based payments to non-residents) unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. FATCA and CRS reporting obligations apply to certain cross-border accounts and transfers. The functional currency should be CAD for domestic treasury reporting, with USD or EUR sub-accounts accepted for aviation leasing revenue in foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. There is no requirement for an Ontario aircraft-engine lessor to repatriate or convert export receipts or foreign-currency lease rentals into Canadian dollars. Foreign-currency earnings may be retained in Canadian or foreign bank accounts and used for offshore operating expenses, debt service, or on-lending. Normal tax compliance requires the recognition of accrued foreign-currency income in Canadian-source returns, and Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) requires arm's-length transfer pricing documentation for related-party cross-border leases under section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential bank regulation exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate for ancillary treasury flows, Low for primary aviation-lease settlement and capital escrow. Foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) may provide commercial payment services to Canadian customers, but any entity engaged in money-services business activities in Canada must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. A non-resident EMI without Canadian registration or physical nexus is not a permissible clearing channel for capital injections, lease settlement, or escrow release. For the aircraft-engine dry-leasing business, fintech EMIs should be limited to convertibility workarounds, small-dollar disbursements, or non-core expense payments. Primary treasury rails must remain with Schedule I Canadian banks or their regulated foreign bank branches to satisfy banking confidentiality, source-of-funds audit trails, and counterparty KYC requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the parent or investor's bank to the Ontario-incorporated company's segregated capital escrow account at the Canadian clearing bank. The remittance information field (Field 70) must state the business purpose, e.g., 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION FOR CA_ON CORPORATE LEASING ENTITY' and reference the CRA Business Number. Canadian banks have no mandatory domestic purpose-code taxonomy, but they require the beneficiary account number, beneficiary tax number, ultimate source of funds declaration, and confirmation that the sending institution is subject to equivalent AML/KYC standards. For investments in a Canadian business controlled by a non-Canadian parent, the Canadian entity must assess whether the Investment Canada Act (ICA) notification or net-benefit review applies, and provide the bank a copy of the ICA filing if a transaction crosses established financial thresholds.
