Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration and business registration portal (Business Registration Online, My Business Account: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax administration (Mon dossier d'entreprise / Clé d'accès: https://www.revenuquebec.ca). The 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) is Canada's statutory TIN; the QST program account is established as the BN suffix 'TQ 0001'.

E-government filing portal

Corporate domestic protocol: (1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the 9-digit NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) from the Registraire d'entreprises du Québec (REQ); no minimum paid-in capital is required. (2) Register with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN); activate the RT program account for federal corporate tax (T2) and the RC program account for GST/HST. (3) Register the BN with Revenu Québec via Mon dossier d'entreprise to open the TQ (QST) account and the CP account for Quebec corporate income tax; QST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Quebec exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold plus the worldwide small supplier test. (4) For a foreign-incorporated entity with no Canadian residence, apply for CRA's non-resident GST/HST registration under the simplified cross-border digital services regime, and simultaneously file the corresponding Revenu Québec QST registration for non-resident suppliers of intangible personal property/services; registration is compulsory once the non-resident's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, even absent a Canadian permanent establishment. (5) Maintain an active annual registration: update the NEQ/BN within 30 days of address/director changes and file the required information returns with the Registraire d'entreprises and Revenu Québec to keep the tax identity enforceable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act. It pays federal corporate income tax on worldwide income if a Canadian resident; Quebec provincial tax applies to the same taxable income base with provincial adjustments. No pass-through of tax attributes to shareholders occurs; distributions are taxable as eligible/non-eligible dividends. The corporation is not eligible for the small business deduction if it is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), which occurs when 50% or more of the equity is controlled by non-residents; this does not change Entity_Level_Taxation status. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable — no free-zone income exemption exists for cloud/SaaS income in Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box-style elective entity reclassification; a corporation is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Available elections relevant to this LegalTech SaaS structure: (1) Closely-related-corporations election under ETA s. 156 and the corresponding QST grouping election — permits intra-group supplies of e-signature/cloud services to be deemed consideration-free, eliminating provincial/federal VAT leakage; (2) functional currency election under ITA s. 261 allowing USD-denominated tax returns if U.S. export revenue is material; (3) ITA s. 93(1) paid-up-capital election on dividends repatriated from a foreign affiliate; (4) ITA s. 216 net-basis election for non-resident recipients of royalty/licence income; (5) Hybrid-mismatch alignment — OECD anti-hybrid rules in ITA ss. 94, 94.2, 94.3 and s. 18(5.1) deny double deductions for hybrid entity/arrangement structures; no elective carve-out exists, and the taxpayer must self-assess under the Canadian hybrid-mismatch ordering rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — annual worldwide taxable supplies threshold for the GST/HST small supplier exemption (ETA s. 148) and the mirrored QST small supplier threshold in Quebec. For cloud document automation and e-signature SaaS, once cumulative worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or trailing four-quarter period, mandatory registration with CRA and Revenu Québec begins. The same CAD 30,000 worldwide threshold applies to non-resident digital suppliers under the simplified cross-border GST/QST registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of cloud-based document automation, e-signature authentication, and legal contract SaaS qualifies for 0% GST/QST export zero-rating under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act (supply of intangible personal property) and the mirrored QST provisions, provided: (1) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada for GST/QST purposes; (2) the supply is not made to an individual physically present in Canada at the time of supply; (3) the digital service does not relate to real property situated in Canada and is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; (4) the service is delivered wholly or substantially through remote electronic access, with no employee or server activity physically performed in Canada for the specific supply; and (5) the supplier retains contemporaneous export substantiation evidencing the non-resident status of the subscriber and the place of use. Pure digital delivery requires no CBSA customs export declaration; the exporter must instead retain transactional and technical logs sufficient to demonstrate the zero-rated character of the supply.

  • Non-resident status documentation: statutory declaration or Certificate of Residence for the subscriber/affiliate (e.g., treaty Form NR-303 available to the non-resident recipient) establishing the recipient's tax residence outside Canada
  • Invoice and contract bundle: fully executed SaaS subscription / EULA / legal contract automation agreement plus commercial invoice marked '0% GST/QST — Export of digital service under ETA Schedule VI Part V and QST mirror provisions' (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541199)
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103, Stripe settlement ledger, or credit card acquiring receipts demonstrating foreign-currency payment from the non-resident recipient and settlement into the Canadian corporation's bank account
  • Service delivery audit log: e-signature authentication metadata, server access logs, geo-IP records, and timestamping authority certificates showing user authentication events and service performance occurred outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance API, or digital stamping is imposed on GST/QST invoices; Canada uses a periodic self-assessment model. GST/HST returns (Form GST34-1) and QST returns (Form QST-1) must be transmitted electronically via CRA and Revenu Québec E-file/API channels within one month after the reporting period; no line-item invoice validation occurs at submission. Electronic invoice mandates are limited: (i) Quebec B2G — suppliers invoicing Quebec public bodies must transmit compliant electronic invoices through the provincial electronic invoicing platform integrated with Revenu Québec; (ii) federal Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing standard — voluntary B2B adoption; (iii) no tax-authority clearance or digital stamping applies to B2B/B2C SaaS invoices. However, the zero-rated export status must be evidenced at the invoice level and documents are demandable by CRA and Revenu Québec on audit.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess an export zero-rating claim within the normal 4-year limitation period, extended to 7 years for negligent misrepresentation, and without limitation where fraud is established. Civil penalties under ETA s. 280/281 impose not less than CAD 5,000 and up to 20% of the tax evaded; Quebec's false-statement penalty under the Tax Administration Act can reach 30% of the QST evaded; prescribed arrears interest is compounded daily and cannot be waived for gross negligence. Upon audit rejection, the invoice is recomputed as a taxable Quebec supply at 14.975% combined GST/QST, input tax credits previously claimed on related inputs are denied, and the resulting tax plus penalties is fully clawed back retroactively. Willful evasion exposes officers/directors to fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years under ETA s. 282, with Quebec penal provisions authorizing fines up to CAD 50,000 for repeat offenders; a false claim of zero-rated export constitutes tax fraud under Criminal Code s. 380(1) punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day field serves as our internal compliance benchmark — export balances outstanding beyond 180 days trigger a formal documentation refresh and heightened transfer-pricing scrutiny of the intercompany settlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity contributions, external borrowing, dividend declarations, or profit repatriation. The principal limitations on remittance are fiscal rather than monetary: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies to dividends paid to non-residents at a statutory rate of 25%, generally treaty-reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-U.S. and similar treaties, and transfer pricing or thin capitalization rules may apply. Dividend remittance must be documented through a corporate board resolution and form filed with the Canada Revenue Agency for any treaty-based relief claim.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline within which export receipts must be repatriated or converted to Canadian dollars. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in authorized foreign-currency bank accounts. However, export transactions must be properly documented for corporate income tax, GST/HST purposes, customs valuation, and any applicable Canadian economic sanctions and export-control screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is Canada's sole monetary authority and currency issuer. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; exchange-control-related regulatory oversight is exercised by the Minister of Finance and operationalized through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary collection rail and Medium-Low as the primary treasury settlement anchor. A foreign electronic-money institution is not recognized as a deposit-taking institution under Canadian banking law, cannot accept regulated deposits, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and does not carry CDIC deposit insurance. To operate lawfully, a foreign EMI or digital payment provider must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian designated compliance officer, maintain equivalent Canadian AML/KYC procedures, and require a Canadian Schedule I bank to act as the settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for inbound treasury is: foreign parent bank to an international correspondent, then onward to the Canadian corporate account at National Bank or RBC via SWIFT MT103 for direct payment instructions or MT202 for bank-to-bank treasury cover. Remittance messages must identify the Canadian corporation by legal name, bank account number, SWIFT/BIC, and beneficiary account IBAN or Canadian transit number, and include a purpose narrative such as 'Unsolicited capital contribution / share subscription' or 'Related-party loan advance'. Canada does not maintain a mandatory purpose-code schedule, but FINTRAC reporting applies to electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.
