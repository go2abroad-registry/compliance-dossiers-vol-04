Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The Ontario limited partnership is statutorily a flow-through entity for income tax purposes; partners report their share of income and losses directly. Available elections include the partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(1) of the Income Tax Act, optional Part XIII tax waiver elections for non-resident partners under section 216, and GST/HST partnership registration elections contemplated by the Excise Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch provisions (subsections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act) may recharacterise certain deductible/creditable arrangements involving hybrid entities or instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding financial services) made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to mandatory registration once this small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act applies to qualifying exports of tangible personal property sold to a non-resident and exported as soon as reasonably possible after delivery, and to specified offshore services supplied to non-resident persons where the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For cold-chain warehousing services, storage or warehousing performed in Canada in respect of goods physically present in Canada is generally taxable at 13% HST; however, qualifying international freight transportation and certain logistics services may be zero-rated. Documentation must establish the physical export and non-resident recipient status.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Canada Border Services Agency export confirmation
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank proof of foreign currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract
  • Packing list
  • Proof of non-resident status of purchaser

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime currently applies in Canada or Ontario. GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenues exceeding CAD 1.5 million are generally required to file GST/HST returns electronically using NETFILE or approved EDI. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records for audit and refund substantiation, but there is no digital fiscal stamp.

CRA may deny zero-rating and claw back input tax credits or refunds with statutory interest if export documentation is incomplete or inconsistent. Gross negligence penalties of 25% of the tax amount at issue may apply under the Excise Tax Act; knowingly making false statements or evading GST/HST is an offence punishable by fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. Retroactive reassessments within the normal four-year limitation period, or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, are available to CRA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating, IMF Article VIII fully convertible currency. There are no exchange-control laws, no FX licensing requirements, no surrender obligations, and no quantitative limits on capital-account or current-account transactions. Non-resident investors may freely subscribe to limited-partnership interests in an Ontario LP, inject capital by SWIFT wire, convert CAD to USD or any other currency, and repatriate principals, profits, and loan repayments without Bank of Canada approval. The only material frictions are (i) FINTRAC and CBSA reporting thresholds for EFTs and cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.) on outbound dividends, interest, rents, and royalties, subject to applicable tax treaties; (iii) CRS/FATCA self-certification for beneficial owners; and (iv) the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 28, 1st Supp.) notification requirement, under section 12, for a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business; a greenfield Ontario cold-storage warehousing LP should file the ISED Notice of Establishment promptly after launch. The Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. L.6) imposes no additional exchange or convertibility restrictions on foreign-limited-partner contributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio to CAD, and no requirement to deposit export receipts with a designated bank. A Canadian LP may retain foreign currency offshore or onshore in a CAD/USD multi-currency operating account, choose to compute taxable income under a functional-currency election pursuant to section 261 of the Income Tax Act, and is subject only to: (a) FINTRAC EFT reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (b) CBSA Form E311/GBR reporting for physical currency or monetary instruments crossing the border at CAD 10,000 or more; (c) Statistics Canada merchandise-export reporting on the Canada Border Services Agency and International Trade Division schedules; and (d) normal Part I income-tax recognition of foreign-exchange gains or losses on revenue-account or capital-account dispositions. No anti-hoarding or antibrexit-style forced conversion rules exist.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), which carries out foreign-exchange reserve management and payment-system oversight; the Department of Finance Canada, which holds statutory responsibility for the Exchange Fund Account and international financial-policy permissions; and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), which supervises all federally chartered banks and compliance with the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), anti-money-laundering directives, and liquidity standards.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary disbursement overlay, but Low as the primary liquidity or escrow institution. Canada does not issue an EMI licence analogous to the EU electronic-money institution regime. Non-bank money-services businesses operate only under FINTRAC registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and may not take deposits in the manner of a bank, may not obtain balances protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, and have no direct access to Lynx/Payments Canada RTGS. Offshore EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) can be used lawfully for small-value foreign-vendor remittances and cross-border payroll as a supplementary rail, but the LP must maintain its principal treasury account at a Schedule I bank or a credit union to satisfy Ontario lending covenants, tax-remittance channels, and CDD expectations. Any movement of funds between the bank account and an EMI should be documented as a treasury disbursement, not as a capital structure transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound route is a SWIFT MT103 customer transfer from the non-resident investor's bank to the LP's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The MT103 field 50K must name the investor as order-institution, field 59 must name the LP and the Ontario account number, and field 70 must state: CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO ONTARIO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP - COLD CHAIN WAREHOUSING. Canada uses no statutory purpose-code system comparable to China's SAFE codes; each Canadian bank maintains an internal classification, typically recording the narrative as a capital-account transaction with sub-codes such as CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT / EQUITY INFUSION. The bank will verify that the sending institution is the same entity as the subscription-agreement payer; if the GP contributes on behalf of an investor, the GP must provide its resolution and a detailed partnership capital schedule. For USD funds, the Canadian bank's New York correspondent receives the wire through Fedwire or CHIPS and credits the Canadian ledger internally same-day; for CAD, the investment is settled through Lynx, the high-value real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada. Incoming transfers of CAD 10,000 or more automatically trigger a FINTRAC large-value EFT report by the bank, and the LP must additionally file any Investment Canada Act notice within the required statutory window. All capital-account entries should be recorded in the LP's capital accounts and reconciled against T5013 Schedule 1 filings to satisfy the Canada Revenue Agency.Outbound settlement operates through four distinct rails. First, domestic CAD invoices, including Ontario trucking, utilities, and payroll-tax remittances, use Payments Canada's Lynx for same-day high-value wires and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch low-value pre-authorized debits, which is the Canadian functional equivalent of ACH. Second, USD payments to US and cross-border refrigeration-equipment vendors clear through SWIFT MT103 for customer transfers or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank covers, with the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent settling via Fedwire, with FINTRAC records retained for every outbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more. Third, for capital-intensive procured equipment, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued per UCP600 (MT700/MT707) provides the appropriate rail, supported by commercial invoices, bills of lading, and CFIA- or CBSA-compliant customs declarations. Fourth, any disbursement to a non-resident payee that constitutes interest, rent, royalty, or a dividend-type payment requires the LP to withhold Part XIII tax and remit through the CRA bank-integrated payment channel; the bank will expect the LP to maintain a valid tax-residency self-certification file for each such payee. The LP should also document all intercompany management-fee payments under section 247 of the Income Tax Act with contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation to prevent tax-disallowance and treasury-account clawback risk.CAD/USD spot contracts and plain-vanilla deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with tenors of up to twelve months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX Master Agreement, provided the notional does not exceed forecast operating expenses and capital expenditures.FX swaps (CAD/USD) used to match the debt-service profile of a non-resident shareholder loan to the LP, including swap of principal at drawdown and principal re-exchange at maturity.Over-the-counter FX options, specifically CAD-call/USD-put and USD-call/CAD-put structures, purchased at a known premium for acquisition or capital-import hedging, subject to a board-approved derivatives policy and premium paid solely from the operating account.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic currencies of cold-chain customers or suppliers, such as Pakistani rupee, Egyptian pound, or Nigerian naira, traded through the Schedule I bank's offshore NDF desk; NDFs are permissible for non-CAD crosses because the Canadian dollar itself is freely convertible and does not require an NDF overlay.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge foreign-currency-denominated equipment leases or debentures, with mandatory transaction reporting to the Canadian Depository for Securities where the LP is a financial end-user; a non-financial Ontario LP may rely on the end-user exemption from mandatory clearing.CME-listed CAD/USD futures as a margined exchange-traded alternative, permitted for a Canadian limited partnership that qualifies as an eligible contract participant, with an ISDA-cleared margin account or futures commission merchant account executed at arm's length from the operating bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary disbursement overlay, but Low as the primary liquidity or escrow institution. Canada does not issue an EMI licence analogous to the EU electronic-money institution regime. Non-bank money-services businesses operate only under FINTRAC registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and may not take deposits in the manner of a bank, may not obtain balances protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, and have no direct access to Lynx/Payments Canada RTGS. Offshore EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) can be used lawfully for small-value foreign-vendor remittances and cross-border payroll as a supplementary rail, but the LP must maintain its principal treasury account at a Schedule I bank or a credit union to satisfy Ontario lending covenants, tax-remittance channels, and CDD expectations. Any movement of funds between the bank account and an EMI should be documented as a treasury disbursement, not as a capital structure transaction.

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. A limited partnership does not pay dividends; distributions of partnership income are allocated and taxed as business income of the partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident triggers mandatory Form T106 filing. There is no statutory documentation threshold, but CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under Income Tax Act s.247 for all non-arm's length transactions; OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation are generally expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 20,000,000 or where material intercompany services, assets, or financing exist.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime similar to low-tax offshore centers. However, CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s.245 ITA, the doctrine of substance-over-form, and treaty limitation-of-benefits clauses to deny tax treaty benefits where the LP lacks genuine Canadian commercial substance. A cold-storage LP must carry on real warehouse operations with employees, inventory, commercial contracts, assets, and management in Ontario.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.163(2): gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; plus interest. Criminal Code s.380(1) fraud against the Crown: fine at the discretion of the court and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties under s.160 ITA; courts may impose travel restrictions, restrain assets, and ordering restitution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s.245 ITA may recharacterize the structure and deny the tax benefit; a 25% GAAR penalty applies under s.245(6) to the amount of the benefit unless the taxpayer proves reasonable diligence.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced, the general partner and directing directors are personally liable for the denied benefits and sanctions; CRA may reassess the LP and the partners directly.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(3): transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net understatement of taxable income or overstatement of loss, unless the taxpayer made reasonable efforts to document arm's length pricing; late T106 penalties apply separately.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct the recording of false intercompany invoices are exposed to liability under s.230 context and may, in exceptional cases, be assessed for corporate penalties if they acted as the directing mind.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax and Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.215(1): the transferring resident is liable for the tax that was withheld; s.227(1) penalties of 10% for failure to withhold/remit, plus monthly interest, and the amount payable remains a personal debt of the taxpayers.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutorily liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s.227.1 ITA, including civil recovery from personal assets; travel bans and bankruptcy proceedings may be triggered by continued non-remittance.
