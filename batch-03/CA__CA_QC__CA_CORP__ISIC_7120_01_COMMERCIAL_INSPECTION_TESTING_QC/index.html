Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for legitimate commercial and investment transactions. Canada does not generally impose capital-account approval, mandatory conversion, capital-repatriation restrictions, or export-proceeds surrender requirements. A Quebec corporation may generally remit dividends, interest, principal, service fees, and other permitted payments to non-residents, subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, contractual restrictions, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, withholding-tax obligations, beneficial-ownership and treaty-entitlement requirements, anti-avoidance rules, sanctions, and bank compliance review. The general Canadian non-resident withholding rate is 25 percent unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or other statutory relief. Dividend payments require sufficient corporate-law capacity and should be supported by directors' resolutions, financial records, and evidence of the recipient's legal and beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate or convert export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. The corporation must nevertheless maintain accurate books, report taxable income in Canadian dollars, comply with applicable customs and tax filings, preserve commercial invoices and shipping or service evidence, and respond to bank, tax, sanctions, AML, Statistics Canada, or balance-of-payments information requests. Foreign-currency balances and transactions should be translated and accounted for under the applicable Canadian accounting framework and tax rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Relevant supplemental authorities include the Department of Finance Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency, and applicable Quebec corporate and tax authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Offshore electronic-money institutions and payment platforms, including Wise Business where eligibility is accepted, may support selected inbound and outbound payments and local account details, but they are not substitutes for a Canadian deposit-taking bank, Canadian corporate escrow account, or full treasury relationship. Availability depends on the entity's Canadian incorporation, registered address, directors, beneficial owners, business activity, expected volumes, jurisdictions, source of funds, and platform risk policy. The corporation must verify whether the provider is a Canadian registered payment service provider or otherwise lawfully servicing Canadian users under the applicable framework. Platform balances may not have Canadian deposit insurance and may be subject to account suspension, transaction limits, restricted-country rules, intermediary-bank screening, and documentary requests. Offshore EMI use does not eliminate Canadian AML, sanctions, tax, recordkeeping, or withholding obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unclear or layered beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, bearer-like arrangements, or inability to identify every individual who owns or controls the corporation.

Root cause: Unclear or layered beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, bearer-like arrangements, or inability to identify every individual who owns or controls the corporation.

Defense evidence: Current articles and corporate profile; certificate of incorporation; shareholder register; securities or ownership ledger; organization chart to the ultimate natural-person beneficial owners; passports and address evidence for all relevant individuals; director and officer register; ownership declarations; trust or nominee documentation where applicable; signed beneficial-ownership certification; board resolution authorizing the account and signatories.
Foreign parent or shareholder funding with no clear distinction between equity, shareholder loan, service payment, and customer revenue.

Root cause: Foreign parent or shareholder funding with no clear distinction between equity, shareholder loan, service payment, and customer revenue.

Defense evidence: Executed subscription agreement or loan agreement; board and shareholder approvals; capitalization table; bank statements showing source and path of funds; remittance instructions; tax analysis; transfer-pricing support; beneficial-owner evidence; general-ledger entries; purpose-of-payment explanation; expected-funds-flow schedule; confirmation that the remitter is not a sanctioned or restricted party.
Commercial inspection and factory-audit activity involving overseas factories, intermediaries, trading companies, sanctioned jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or public-sector counterparties.

Root cause: Commercial inspection and factory-audit activity involving overseas factories, intermediaries, trading companies, sanctioned jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or public-sector counterparties.

Defense evidence: Detailed business model memorandum; service descriptions; customer and supplier lists; signed engagement letters; inspection reports or sample deliverables; invoices; shipment and factory-location information; sanctions and export-control screening procedures; end-use and end-user controls; anti-bribery policy; conflict-of-interest policy; customer risk-rating methodology; explanation of why the corporation does not take title to goods, if applicable.
Expected cross-border volume or transaction geography inconsistent with a newly incorporated Quebec corporation or with the stated low-risk business profile.

Root cause: Expected cross-border volume or transaction geography inconsistent with a newly incorporated Quebec corporation or with the stated low-risk business profile.

Defense evidence: Twelve-month transaction forecast by currency, country, counterparty type, and payment purpose; management accounts; contracts and purchase orders; customer pipeline; staffing and premises evidence; website and marketing materials; tax registrations; historical statements from existing accounts; treasury policy; explanation of seasonality and peak payment periods.
Use of personal accounts, third-party payment accounts, unrelated remitters, cash-intensive settlement, or frequent pass-through transfers.

Root cause: Use of personal accounts, third-party payment accounts, unrelated remitters, cash-intensive settlement, or frequent pass-through transfers.

Defense evidence: Written prohibition on personal and third-party accounts; approved payment matrix; segregation-of-duties controls; corporate bank statements; invoice-to-payment reconciliation; remitter relationship evidence; exception-approval procedure; cash policy; accounting-system extracts; documented return or rejection process for unexplained funds.
Inability to evidence Canadian operating nexus or a mismatch between Quebec registration and actual management, staffing, premises, or revenue-generating activity.

Root cause: Inability to evidence Canadian operating nexus or a mismatch between Quebec registration and actual management, staffing, premises, or revenue-generating activity.

Defense evidence: Quebec corporate registration and registered-office evidence; lease or serviced-office agreement; utility or occupancy evidence; payroll or contractor records; Quebec tax registrations where applicable; management service agreements; employee and director information; customer contracts; insurance; professional-license evidence if required for the specific inspection activity; business plan and operating budget.
Adverse media, sanctions exposure, politically exposed person status, corruption concerns, or an unexplained relationship with government-linked customers or suppliers.

Root cause: Adverse media, sanctions exposure, politically exposed person status, corruption concerns, or an unexplained relationship with government-linked customers or suppliers.

Defense evidence: Enhanced due-diligence questionnaire; sanctions-screening results; PEP declarations; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds evidence; beneficial-owner biographies; anti-corruption compliance program; gifts and hospitality register; government-counterparty explanation; independent background reports; legal opinion or compliance memorandum addressing the identified risk.
Payment descriptions that are vague, inconsistent with invoices, or suggest regulated financial activity, money transmission, customs evasion, or trade-based money laundering.

Root cause: Payment descriptions that are vague, inconsistent with invoices, or suggest regulated financial activity, money transmission, customs evasion, or trade-based money laundering.

Defense evidence: Standardized payment-purpose taxonomy; invoice and contract cross-reference; approved invoice workflow; service-delivery evidence; customs and shipment documentation where relevant; written confirmation that the company does not accept deposits, transmit money for third parties, operate a payment service, or provide unlicensed financial services; compliance escalation records.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a Canadian Tier 1 bank into the corporation's designated operating or capital account using a traceable bank-to-bank transfer. The payment instruction should identify the exact legal remitter, Canadian beneficiary, account number, currency, amount, and purpose, such as 'share subscription,' 'capital contribution,' or 'shareholder loan.' Canada does not prescribe a universal mandatory FX purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires; the bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification, source-of-funds evidence, and supporting corporate documents. Before receipt, provide the bank with the subscription or loan agreement, board resolutions, capitalization table, beneficial-ownership records, remitter bank statement, and expected-funds-flow schedule. Reconcile the incoming wire to the general ledger and issue shares or record the loan only after legal and accounting review. Avoid third-party remitters, split transfers designed to avoid review, unexplained overfunding, and transfers through personal accounts. Large or unusual transactions may be reported by the financial institution under Canadian AML requirements without prior notice to the corporation.Use the Canadian operating account for supplier, contractor, inspection, travel, and customer-refund payments. Standard bank wires may be transmitted through SWIFT, historically using MT103 customer-credit-transfer messaging and increasingly through ISO 20022 payment messages, subject to the sending bank's implementation. For documentary trade transactions, use a commercial letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or open-account transfer according to counterparty risk. A commercial LC should specify applicant, beneficiary, issuing and advising banks, amount, currency, expiry, presentation period, Incoterms where relevant, required commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, insurance document, and applicable sanctions or compliance conditions. For service-only factory inspection assignments, retain the engagement letter, inspection report, invoice, acceptance evidence, and tax treatment rather than manufacturing documents that do not exist. Screen all beneficiaries, banks, vessels, goods, factories, and jurisdictions before release; ensure withholding tax, GST/QST treatment, transfer pricing, customs, sanctions, and foreign-exchange conversion costs are properly addressed.Deliverable CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, and other currency forwards entered into with a regulated bank or appropriately authorized dealer, supported by an identifiable forecast or committed receivable or payable.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currency, counterparty, documentation, and settlement structure are legally and operationally available; NDFs are settled by net cash difference and require enhanced counterparty, market, and enforceability review.Cross-currency swaps and currency swaps for recurring or long-dated foreign-currency funding, subject to dealer documentation, collateral, valuation, accounting, and counterparty-credit requirements.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and participating forwards may be used for defined exposures, provided the corporation understands premium, margin, break-cost, liquidity, and potentially asymmetric-loss characteristics.Hedging should be documented under an approved treasury policy with delegated limits, authorized counterparties, ISDA or equivalent master documentation where appropriate, confirmations, exposure records, effectiveness testing, valuation controls, and segregation between payment initiation and hedge approval. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined statutory general corporate rate = federal Part I 15% + Quebec general corporate rate 11.5%; a qualified CCPC (Canadian-controlled private corporation) is taxed at 12.2% combined on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% combined statutory Part XIII rate on dividends paid by a Quebec-resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada. The federal Part XIII rate of 25% is structurally reduced to 11% for corporations resident in Quebec under the Federal-Provincial tax collection agreement, while Revenu Quebec levies an additional 14% Part IV Quebec tax, producing a total statutory burden of 25%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; the balance of federal tax payable is due within 2 months after year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months for CCPCs; quarterly instalments apply for the second and subsequent years when taxes exceed CAD 3,000

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 due within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; the balance of Quebec tax payable is due within 2 months after year-end for general corporations and 3 months for qualified SMEs; instalments are required when the Quebec tax exceeds CAD 1,200

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions in the fiscal year exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (Income Tax Act s.247(4)); CRA and Revenu Quebec apply the same threshold for the Quebec documentation obligation under s.1087.0.1 of the Taxation Act; the OECD Master File and Country-by-Country Report (Form RC5101 / RC5100) become mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 1.1 billion (EUR 750 million equivalent); documentation must exist before the return filing date and be produced within 3 months of request

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the form enacted in the UAE or Gulf jurisdictions; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test incorporated in Canada's DTAs, and the common law beneficial ownership doctrine require real personnel, physical premises, independent decision-making, and genuine operational activity in Quebec. A CA_CORP shell that invoices inspection fees without local laboratory files, staff, or client-facing operations will be reassessed as an artificial transaction, denied treaty relief, and subjected to the full 25% Part XIII withholding on stripped amounts; for this testing agency, retain payroll records, inspection site reports, client engagement contracts, and equipment ownership evidence in Quebec to demonstrate substantive presence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Federal (ITA s.239): summary conviction or indictment - fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated amount plus compounding daily interest; Quebec (Taxation Act s.158.1 et seq.): penalty up to 50% of evaded tax, fine of CAD 5,000 to CAD 50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s.227.1 ITA for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding, including interest and penalties; courts may issue asset freezes, travel bans, bank account seizures, and extradition requests; directors who sign false declarations expose their personal criminal liability, and a foreign parent board may be prosecuted for aiding and abetting
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR s.245 reassessment disallowing all tax benefits, plus a 50% penalty for gross negligence where applicable; denial of DTA withholding reduction under the Principal Purpose Test, resulting in retroactive assessment of the full 25% Part XIII tax on distributions; recharacterization of dividends as fees or royalties subject to 25% withholding; compound daily interest from the original filing date

Director personal liability: Directors are assessed personally for unpaid withholding where the company failed to remit; courts pierce the corporate veil where no separate board decisions exist, treating the entity as the alter ego of the foreign owner; the Quebec tax authority may certify the directors for travel bans, and criminal fraud against the Crown charges can extend to passive directors
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: s.247(2) upward transfer pricing adjustment plus a strict 10% consequential penalty on the net adjustment where contemporaneous documentation is absent (s.247(3)); interest runs from the reassessed year; CRA can extend the normal reassessment period to 10 years; Revenu Quebec imposes a matching adjustment under s.1087 of the Taxation Act

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable where the corporation fails to satisfy the reassessed liability and distributes its retained earnings; the 10% TP penalty is not shielded by business judgment where directors personally executed the TP policy; the Quebec AMF and CRA can commence criminal prosecution for wilful transfer pricing misrepresentation
Non-Resident Withholding Tax (Part XIII) Default

Statutory sanction: The full 25% Part XIII amount is assessed against the payer, including the 14% Quebec portion administered by Revenu Quebec, plus daily compound interest and penalties; misapplied treaty rates trigger the gross negligence penalty; a non-resident cannot be assessed, so the entire burden falls on the Canadian corporation

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s.227.1 ITA for the unreported withholding, including the 10% director's liability imposed where the corporation fails to remit; the CRA and Revenu Quebec may garnish personal assets, impose travel bans, and prohibit departure; failure of the board to implement a proper withholding system for cross-border payments is treated as gross negligence
AML, Beneficial Ownership Registry, and Corporate Registration

Statutory sanction: Quebec Business Corporations Act penalties: CAD 200 to CAD 50,000 for defective declarations; federal beneficial ownership registry rules (effective January 2024) impose fines up to CAD 200,000 for individuals and CAD 800,000 for corporations, with imprisonment up to 6 months; the corporation may be administratively dissolved, extinguishing the owners' limited liability

Director personal liability: Directors are designated as responsible persons for registry accuracy; non-compliance empowers the court to strike the corporate registration retroactively, exposing directors personally to client tort claims; Quebec anti-money-laundering officers (AMF) can impose administrative penalties against the directors' personal property where the entity is insolvent, and foreign directors can be subject to entry and travel restrictions
