Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions may consist of money, property, or services and are set freely in the partnership agreement; no escrow or deposit account is required. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and income tax, portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec for QST and provincial filings, portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec limited partnership must first determine whether it carries on taxable commercial activity in Canada and Quebec. It then applies to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts', and must add the RT program account for GST/HST if taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold. Because the entity is an LP, the BN also supports the T5013 Partnership Information Return. For Quebec QST, the LP must register separately with Revenu Québec through its business registration system (including the GST/HST and QST combined registration form for non-residents) and is assigned a QST registration number. Foreign entities may be required to provide details of beneficial ownership and appoint an authorized representative or mandatary for service of documents in Quebec. There is no minimum capital requirement for the LP under Canadian tax law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Not applicable in the U.S.-style check-the-box sense. Canadian income tax law treats a limited partnership as a transparent flow-through entity by statute: the LP files an information return but is not itself liable to federal income tax; partners are taxed on their allocated share. There is no statutory election to convert the LP into an entity-level taxpayer. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2, 18.3 and 95(2) apply automatically to deny inappropriate double-deduction outcomes and do not require an election. An LP may, where permitted, make joint elections with partners for partnership fiscal-period alignment, but such elections do not affect entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the small-supplier threshold is exceeded when worldwide taxable supplies in Canada determine GST/HST registration; in Quebec, QST registration is required once taxable supplies in Quebec exceed CAD 30,000 in the calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of road feeder trucking services for international airport bonded cargo qualifies as zero-rated if the service is a supply of transportation of goods to a place outside Canada and the goods are not to be delivered in Canada. The zero-rating applies to the physical export movement of bonded freight from a Canadian airport or bonded warehouse to a foreign destination, including transshipment traffic in transit through Canada. Substantiation must demonstrate that the goods left Canada and that the supply was performed by the LP as a carrier in the course of a commercial transportation service.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted via CERS or, where applicable, a B13A Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Cargo Manifest / eManifest evidence showing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire / FX transfer confirmation evidencing payment
  • Commercial invoice or export sales invoice tied to the specific shipment
  • Bonded carriage authorization, carrier customs bond, or transit authorization for the customs-bonded truck movement

Canada has no national real-time electronic invoicing, API-based fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate for GST/HST or QST. Electronic invoices are accepted and must contain the prescribed information. For customs and export control purposes, the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) requires electronic filing of export declarations, and CBSA eManifest rules require advance electronic cargo data for bonded truck movements. No digital fiscal stamp is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating, claw back refunds and reassess GST/HST and QST, including interest and penalties, where export documentation is insufficient or the goods were not in fact exported. The normal reassessment period is four years, extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. False-statement and gross-negligence penalties may be imposed, and CRA may pursue criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion. The LP may also face Customs Act penalties, carrier license suspension, revocation of bonded carrier privileges, and monetary penalties for incomplete or false customs documentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating and fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII. No regulatory approval or central bank ratification is required for current account payments, trade settlements, capital remittances, profit distributions, or partnership redemptions. Profit remittance to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted but must address Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced under applicable tax treaties) and Quebec provincial withholding on income allocations (T5013/TP-600.1); returns of capital and dissolution proceeds are not subject to withholding if properly documented and traced to original contributed capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline. Export receipts from bonded truck feeder services (ISIC 5222) may be retained freely in CAD or foreign currency in multi-currency operating accounts. Compliance obligations are limited to CBSA export declarations for cargo movements, Statistics Canada reporting where thresholds apply, and FINTRAC reporting of cross-border EFTs equal to or exceeding C$10,000. No ratio of export proceeds is required to be converted into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); ancillary regulatory functions exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); broader currency policy authority rests with the Department of Finance Canada. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange administration because it has fully liberalised current and capital account transactions.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Federal plus Quebec on taxable income of a corporate partner; Canada general federal rate is 15% (38% statutory less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction), Quebec general rate is 11.5%. Because the LP is fiscally transparent, the headline rate applies at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding and an identical 25% Quebec dividend withholding applied to dividends paid by a corporation resident in Quebec to non-residents. For an LP, distributions to non-resident partners are allocated business income rather than dividends and are taxed under Part I, subject to treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 2,000,000 aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions in the taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 filing, contemporaneous Local File documentation and supporting records. Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000,000 and reportable cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate ESR statute; however, section 245 GAAR, section 250.2(3) place-of-management rules and the treaty Principal Purpose Test deny treaty benefits where an LP lacks economic substance. A Quebec bonded trucking LP must demonstrate: a Quebec-registered general partner with real employees; a functioning head office in Quebec; a CBSA Bonded Carrier Permit; a CTQ operator's licence under the Road Transportation Act; GPS-sealed fleet telemetry and eManifest records; and substantial physical and managerial presence in Quebec to preserve the partnership's tax attributes and treaty eligibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine up to CAD 100,000 plus 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec Taxation Act section 1901: fine up to twice the amount of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and the corporate general partner are personally liable under ITA section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and withholding; CRA may obtain asset injunctions and travel bans under ITA section 222.1; officers and directors face criminal imprisonment and disqualification from board service for up to 5 years.
Sham / Shell Partnership and GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallowance of the deduction and recharacterization of the arrangement under ITA section 245; denial of partnership loss allocation; gross negligence penalty under ITA section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; section 160 joint assessment against directors and connected persons.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner face 100% personal liability for unremitted Part XIII and source withholdings; criminal liability for false statements under section 380 of the Criminal Code; Quebec authorities may suspend the bonded carrier permit and require a security bond before the LP continues operations.
Abusive Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: ITA section 247(2) adjusts income to arm's length terms; ITA section 247(3) adds a flat 10% adjustment penalty on the increase; CRA may apply the gross negligence penalty of 50% on the understated tax plus interest on both the adjustment and penalty; the amounts are joint and several.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally liable for TP penalties unless they participated in concealment; veil-piercing applies when the corporate general partner has no separate assets and the limited partnership is a sham; Revenu Quebec may also rely on section 130.1 of the Labour Standards Act to impose director liability for unpaid source deductions.
AML / CBSA / Customs Non-Compliance and Capital Flight

Statutory sanction: Customs Act: fines up to CAD 25,000 per violation or seizure of bonded goods; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: corporate penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for non-registration or unreported cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000; Quebec Taxation Act: parallel administrative fines and prohibition orders restricting outbound travel.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability under the PCMLTFA for knowingly failing to report or for laundering freight revenues; personal asset forfeiture under section 462.37 of the Criminal Code; travel bans enforced by Canada Border Services Agency; suspension of the Customs Bonded Carrier Permit; denial of clearance certificates for directors until all outstanding tax obligations are satisfied.
