Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program administered via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec – QST, QPIP/CNT and payroll accounts administered via Mon dossier d'entreprise/ClicSÉQUR portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca); Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Québec Enterprise Registrar) for the limited partnership's NEQ (Québec Enterprise Number).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Constitute the CA_LP under Quebec partnership law and register it at the Registraire des entreprises (attorney-in-fact/service agent for foreign non-resident partners), obtaining the NEQ. Step 2: Apply online to CRA for a BN with GST/HST account (Form RC1/RC1A where application by mail) and, if applicable, payroll/mport accounts. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec via ClicSÉQUR for the QST account using the federal BN as base identifier. Step 4: Because the LP is fiscally transparent, no income tax account is opened for the partnership itself; instead the LP files the annual partnership information returns (federal T5013; Quebec TP-600) and issues T5013/TP-600 slips to each partner. Step 5: Non-resident general partners must obtain a Canadian NR account number (BN-15) to file non-resident tax returns and to support treaty relief claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian law does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election; entity classification follows the Quebec Civil Code/Income Tax Act s. 96 partnership rules. Available elections include: (i) a partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(7) to adopt a non-calendar year-end where all members agree; (ii) the s. 97(2) ITA election permitting tax-deferred transfer of contributed IP/ad-tech contracts into the LP; (iii) the CRA 'small supplier' voluntary registration election for GST/HST and QST (ETA s. 240(1); Quebec QST Act equivalents), permitting the LP to register before the CAD 30,000 threshold and claim refundable ITCs; and (iv) OECD BEPS hybrid-mismatch compliance — imported hybrid and reverse-hybrid structures are subject to ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 (in force July 2022), under which foreign partners may elect to align substantive characterization to avoid deducted-but-not-included (D/NI) outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (the 'small supplier' threshold) — mandatory GST/HST registration once exceeded (ETA s. 240(1)); an identical CAD 30,000 threshold applies to QST (Quebec Taxation Act); voluntary registration available before the threshold to recover input tax credits; supply-based registration requirements apply, not asset-based tests; for a foreign-owned LP, the threshold is measured by the global turnover of the person and related entities, requiring careful aggregation analysis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST: under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated where the recipient is not registered under the ETA and the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. QST mirrors this result for supplies made to a non-resident not registered for QST. For the ASO/SEO localization agency: app-store optimization, multilingual keyword localization, organic search audits, metadata translations, and campaign-performance reporting supplied to non-resident app publishers qualify as zero-rated exports when the contractual recipient is outside Canada; CRA does not require preapproval, and the fact that the foreign publisher's end-users are in Canada does not by itself defeat zero-rating (CRA 'advertising services' policy). To substantiate, the LP must document the recipient's non-resident status (foreign certificate, registered office, tax ID), the exported nature of the service, and remittance from offshore accounts. Pure service exports do not require CBSA customs declarations; however, if ASO deliverables include licensed digital goods or media copyrights crossing borders, the supply is treated as intangible personal property and remains zero-rated under s. 10(2)? (intellectual property supplied to non-resident, Schedule VI Part V s. 10), for which assigned contract and license records are the primary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA CERS record — applicable only if digital-media kits, promotional devices, or physical props are shipped; not required for pure remote services.
  • Bill of Lading / carrier airway bill — similarly goods-only evidence, retained if any physical deliverables are exported.
  • Inward remittance certificate — Wise_B2B or Payoneer payment statements, SWIFT/MT103 records, and bank credits evidencing settlement in USD/EUR from non-resident counterparties.
  • Executed service agreement containing governing law, Quebec/non-resident status representations, and scope-of-work defining exported services.
  • Commercial invoice pack stamped 'Zero-rated export — ETA Schedule VI s.7 / QST equivalent', quoting the LP's GST/HST and QST registration numbers and the recipient's foreign tax ID.

No real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based Continuous Transaction Control, and no digital stamping are mandated federally or in Quebec. Invoices issued by the LP must contain: supplier name and BN/GST-HST registration number, date, description, consideration, and applicable taxes. CRA encourages but does not require PEPPOL e-invoicing. Revenu Québec's QTIP (Québec Tax Information Platform) reporting obligations apply only to digital platform operators settling gig-economy payments, not to an ASO/SEO agency's B2B cross-border invoices; records must be retained for six years from the end of the fiscal year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no general foreign-exchange controls, no restrictions on foreign-currency deposits, and no requirement for prior exchange-control approval for inbound or outbound payments. The Bank of Canada does not impose surrender or repatriation requirements on export receipts or service revenues. Profit remittances from a Quebec Limited Partnership to non-resident partners remain subject to Canadian tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable distributions such as interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and certain management fees; however, business income allocated to non-resident partners is generally taxed under Part I and remittance must be supported by Form NR4, fileable by the partnership or its general partner, with treaty-based rate reductions available. Payment rails must comply with money-laundering reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including FINTRAC reports for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for a Quebec Limited Partnership. Export and cross-border service receipts may be retained in the original foreign currency, converted at commercial bank rates, or used directly for trade disbursements. The only obligations are: (i) accurate accounting in Canadian dollar equivalent for tax filings; (ii) reporting by a registered Canadian money services business if converting funds on behalf of third parties; and (iii) FINTRAC large-transaction and SWIFT/CAD-related transfer reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Minister of Finance for policy matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with legal constraints. Canadian law does not recognise the European-style 'electronic money institution' designation as a chartered bank; such entities are generally treated as money services businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC if operating in or targeting Canada, appoint a chief AML officer, and implement a compliance program. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise B2B and Payoneer can be used as secondary disbursement and receivables-concentration rails, but cannot legally be the sole treasury infrastructure for a Quebec LP because they do not provide a Canadian deposit account, lack CDIC deposit insurance protection, are not licensed loan or deposit-taking corporations under the Bank Act, and may require the underlying corporate entity to maintain a separate bank account. Where the offshore EMI is itself a regulated money transmitter in its home jurisdiction, the Quebec LP should provide the EMI with tax residency evidence, ultimate beneficial owner declarations, and a copy of the limited partnership registration from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be wired in the partner's local currency or Canadian dollars via SWIFT MT103 using the partnership's beneficiary institution code and Canadian bank transit/institution number. For a Canadian recipient, use the 9-digit routing numeric identifier (institution number + 5-digit branch transit) and the partnership's account number; Canada does not operate an IBAN system, so all cross-border wires require BIC/SWIFT of the receiving Canadian chartered bank. The MT103 must include a clear purpose code such as 'Capital contribution to limited partnership', 'Equity injection', or 'Foreign direct investment (FDI) inflow' as required by the receiving bank's external-payments code list. For partner contributions from the United States, the payer may use the existing USD clearing hub (Fedwire) with ABA routing through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; for contributions from Europe, the sending bank will route via SWIFT with no IBAN but with full beneficiary legal name, registered address, and CA/QC partnership identifier. Verification procedures include: (i) pre-notification by the general partner; (ii) matching of the remitter information against the partnership register; (iii) source-of-funds documents; (iv) confirmation of no sanctions-prohibited jurisdictions; and (v) posting of the contribution to the partnership capital account with contemporaneous accounting entry.Outbound disbursements for trade settlement are executed over SWIFT MT103 for international telegraphic transfers, via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Lynx high-value payment rails for cross-bank CAD settlement, and via bank-to-bank ACH or EFT for recurring CAD vendor payments. For USD payments to U.S. publishers and advertising platforms, Canadian Tier 1 banks use U.S. dollar clearing through a U.S. correspondent bank, either by Automated Clearing House (ACH) for low-value recurring settlements or Fedwire for larger or time-critical disbursements. Commercial letters of credit are technically available and appropriate for physical-goods transactions, but for an app growth and localization agency they are not the standard rail; instead, confirmed SWIFT MT103 wire transfers, commercial invoicing with credit terms, and payment platform settlement files are the primary outbound rails. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services, interest, royalties, and other transactions with non-resident related parties

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires genuine economic substance. The partnership must have real business activity, employees, and assets. No specific ESR, but substance is required to avoid GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion. Travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may recharacterize transactions, disallow deductions, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for the tax benefit and penalties; limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly participate in non-compliance.
