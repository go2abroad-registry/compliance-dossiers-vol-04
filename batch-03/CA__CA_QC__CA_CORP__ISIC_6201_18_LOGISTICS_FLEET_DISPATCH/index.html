Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Revenu Québec; federal portal: My Business Account (cra-arc.gc.ca); Quebec portal: Revenu Québec CLIC (revenuquebec.ca)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian corporation must first incorporate federally or provincially under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act. After incorporation, the corporation must register with the CRA for the federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts, and simultaneously register for the GST/HST account, payroll deductions account, and corporate income tax account. In Quebec, the corporation must also register with Revenu Québec to obtain a Quebec Business Number (NEQ), which can be done through the Registraire d'entreprises or the CLIC portal, and separately register for the Quebec sales tax (QST) account, Quebec payroll source deductions, and the Quebec income tax account. Non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted; however, a resident director requirement applies under the Quebec Business Corporations Act unless an extra-provincial or federal corporation is used. The BN and NEQ must be obtained before filing any GST/HST or QST returns or claiming input tax refunds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable entity subject to federal corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act and Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act. The corporation is not a pass-through entity. It is resident in Canada if its central management and control is exercised in Canada; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident for federal purposes. Quebec-resident corporations are subject to Quebec tax on worldwide income. Branches of non-resident corporations are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source business income and may be subject to Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings remitted abroad. The Quebec tax regime imposes an additional branch tax on Quebec-allocated branch profits of non-resident corporations. For the SaaS logistics fleet dispatch business, income is generally allocated to permanent establishments based on a formulaic attribution of gross revenue and payroll in Quebec.

Available election forms

The Canadian income tax system does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election comparable to the U.S. regime. A business corporation is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Available elections include: (i) a section 216 election under the Income Tax Act for a non-resident to file a Canadian income tax return on gross rental or timber royalties income instead of being subject to Part XIII withholding tax; (ii) a section 216.1 election for non-resident service providers to file a return and pay tax in lieu of 15% withholding on certain service payments; (iii) a joint election under subsection 256(9) to deem associated corporations for the purpose of the small business deduction; (iv) a late-filing election under subsection 256.1(4) to correct corporate control or ownership filings; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act, which deny deductions in respect of certain hybrid mismatch arrangements involving foreign entities. For QST purposes, no equivalent check-the-box election exists; the corporation is mandatorily a QST registrant if it carries on a taxable supply in Quebec and is not a small supplier, or voluntarily if it carries on a commercial activity in Quebec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports and supplies of financial services) per calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident that does not carry on business in Canada but supplies digital services in Canada must register regardless of threshold unless the supply qualifies as a 'supply made through a digital platform' where the platform operator collects the tax. For QST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Quebec over a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident supplier of specified digital supplies in Quebec must register and collect QST without regard to the small-supplier threshold, subject to the platform collection mechanism for certain platform-facilitated supplies. The corporate registration threshold for Quebec payroll and provincial corporate income tax is not turnover-based; registration is required as soon as the corporation has an establishment and employees in Quebec. For a SaaS logistics fleet dispatch business supplying telematics services on a subscription basis, the relevant threshold is GREATER of the GST/HST and QST thresholds, effectively CAD 30,000 of taxable worldwide and Quebec supplies, but mandatory registration applies for digital platform-facilitated supplies and for supplies of what the CRA and Revenu Québec characterize as 'advertising services' or 'digital services' to Quebec consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier maintains evidence of exportation and the goods are delivered outside Canada or to a common carrier for export. For services, zero-rating applies to: (i) a supply of a service performed wholly outside Canada; (ii) a supply of freight transportation services from a location in Canada to a destination outside Canada; (iii) a supply of transportation of goods by a common carrier from a location in Canada to a destination outside Canada; (iv) a supply of a service in respect of tangible personal property that is exported from Canada, if the property is not acquired for use in Canada; and (v) a supply of a 'digital good' or 'telecommunication service' supplied by a non-resident registrant to a recipient in Canada that is not a consumer, or supplied to a recipient outside Canada. For the logistics fleet dispatch SaaS, zero-rating eligibility depends on whether the service is supplied to a recipient that is not resident in Canada and the service is not delivered to a person in Canada. Software-as-a-service using telematics data is generally treated as a supply of an intangible that is 'made outside Canada' when the recipient is outside Canada and does not use the service in Canada. If the recipient is a GST/HST registrant in Canada, the supply is generally subject to GST/HST at the place-of-supply rules, unless the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered and the service is not consumed in Canada. Quebec QST zero-rating applies on a similar basis to zero-rated exports under section 16 of Schedule I of the QST rule where the supply would be zero-rated under the GST regime; the QST zero-rating election (Form VD-33) may be filed by a registrant to treat certain supplies to non-residents as QST zero-rated. The supplier must obtain and maintain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and of the supply being made to a non-resident recipient for the purpose of consumption outside Canada.

  • Canada Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS export declaration or Form B13A) when required by the Canada Border Services Agency for regulated or controlled goods or for exports exceeding the prescribed threshold, plus the Customs Self-Assessment (CSA) export coding if applicable
  • Commercial invoice reflecting the arm's length sale and noting zero-rated export supply and the supplier's GST/HST registration number
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or other transport document showing delivery to a carrier for dispatch outside Canada and foreign destination
  • Proof of export including customs broker export documentation and Canada Border Services Agency CERS confirmation / ACI export confirmation (when applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing that the export proceeds were received from a non-resident bank account or a Canadian bank account of a non-resident purchaser, as well as FX conversion record
  • Non-residency evidence of the customer (e.g., certificate of incorporation, foreign VAT registration number, or address of principal place of business outside Canada) for the zero-rating of services
  • Contract or purchase order of the international logistics fleet dispatch subscription identifying the recipient and the performance outside Canada or delivery to a non-resident located outside Canada
  • Quebec QST zero-rating support, including the QST registration number and the recipient's attestation that the supply is not for use in Quebec

Canada does not currently mandate a national continuous transaction control (CTC) electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime comparable to Mexico or Brazil. The CRA accepts original, corrected, and amended invoices in paper or EDI/XML formats. Since 2022, the federal government has been developing a 'GST/HST E-invoicing' framework based on the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (PEPPOL) standard, with voluntary adoption. Quebec, however, has implemented a mandatory electronic invoicing regime for public-sector procurement (e.g., invoices to Quebec government ministries and agencies) through the Quebec Electronic Tendering System (SEAO) and the Trans Québec et fédéral (TQF) contract portal, and requires Phase 2 EDI or PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 compliant e-invoices for suppliers to the Gouvernement du Québec and certain public bodies. For the private-sector SaaS logistics fleet dispatch business, no real-time API fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required. Revenu Québec mandates that a 'tax invoice' contain specific information in French (or with French version available), including supplier and recipient identification, the date of supply, a description of the supply, the consideration, the GST/HST and QST rates and amounts, and the QST registration number. All invoices issued to Quebec customers must be retained in a legible format and made available upon request; electronic records must remain accessible and preserved for six years from the end of the last year to which they relate, and the use of non-repudiation and digital signatures is accepted for audit purposes.

The CRA and Revenu Québec impose severe penalties for fraudulent or erroneous zero-rating claims. Under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, knowing participation in a false or deceptive statement for GST/HST purposes is subject to penalties of CAD 100 plus 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, and criminal fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for each count. For corporate taxpayers, Part I of the Income Tax Act and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act authorize reassessment beyond the normal four-year limitation period where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation that amounts to fraud or neglect, permitting retrospective clawback of unreceipted refunds and assessments of GST/HST, QST, penalties, and interest for an unlimited or extended period. If exported goods or services are subsequently brought back into Canada or consumed in Canada after being zero-rated, the supplier or recipient is liable for GST/HST on the value of the supply and the input tax credit is denied. The CRA's 'export integrity' program audits zero-rating documentation; failure to produce the mandatory evidence bundle within the prescribed 30-day or reasonable request period results in disallowed zero-rating and reassessment, even absent intentional fraud. Revenu Québec separately enforces articles 1047 to 1080 of the Quebec Taxation Act and sections 811 to 815 of the QST rules for QST fraud, imposing civil penalties of 50% of the evaded QST and, upon conviction, fines and imprisonment. For a logistics SaaS vendor, a common clawback risk arises when the customer uses the telematics services partly in Canada (fleet management for Canadian motor carriers); the supplier must allocate and collect QST/HST on the Canadian portion, and the zero-rating claim is limited strictly to the foreign-use portion demonstrated by the customer's usage records, contract, and IP address/geolocation evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control restrictions, no surrender requirements, and no central approval regime for cross-border payments. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and proceeds from a disposition of shares are freely remittable, subject only to transactional reporting thresholds under Canadian anti-money-laundering rules and to tax withholding obligations (e.g., Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and certain payments to non-residents). For Quebec entities, in-bound foreign direct investment is permitted, with no Quebec-specific conversion restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose a statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency and at any financial institution; reporting is limited to FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or more, or convertible equivalent) and, where applicable, Canada Revenue Agency international-reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control authority. Foreign-exchange administration is distributed among prudential regulators and tax/revenue agencies, with financial-integrity supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to High, subject to dual Canada-Quebec registration. Canada does not issue an 'Electronic Money Institution' licence as in the EU; a non-resident digital EMI must become a registered money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if operating in Quebec, may need to assess Quebec's money-services-browser requirements and provincial consumer-protection registration. A foreign-licensed EMI can otherwise provide payment-processing and multi-currency treasury accounts as a third-party service provider, but it cannot establish a directly licensed deposit-taking institution without a Canadian Schedule I bank charter.

Root cause: Beneficial-owner opacity as a consequence of a layered offshore parent structure

Defense evidence: Provide a consolidated organizational chart, register of ultimate beneficial owners, declaration of non-PEP status, FATCA/CRS self-certificates, and, if applicable, accountant-prepared annual filings confirming the ultimate natural-person owners.
Sanctions or heightened-risk jurisdiction involvement in payment counterparties

Root cause: Sanctions or heightened-risk jurisdiction involvement in payment counterparties

Defense evidence: Prepare transactional counterparty screening reports, end-use and end-user certificates, corroborating bills-of-lading or electronic-service deliverables, and a sanctions-compliance policy executed by management to demonstrate that restricted jurisdictions and embargoed carriers are excluded.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity and intercompany loans must be wired in USD or CAD to the Quebec operational or escrow account using standard SWIFT MT103 messages. The originating institution requires a proper purpose code, typically 'capital contribution', 'intercompany loan', or 'direct investment in equity', and the receiving Canadian bank performs a FINTRAC large-transaction report if the amount is at least CAD 10,000. Verification includes sourcing documents such as the executed share subscription, loan deed, and board resolution; for amounts exceeding threshold levels, banks will obtain an advance FATCA/CRS certification and confirm no U.S. withholding issues. Direct wire with a receivable wire transfer protocol from a recognized bank is preferred over crypto intermediaries.Outbound disbursements for logistics SaaS procurement, hosting, subprocessors, and fleet hardware are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for vendor payables and MT202 for inter-entity transfers; the Canadian bank will enforce the OFAC-equivalent Canadian sanctions framework via the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) list and may screen all counterparties. For larger trade settlements, a commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) may be opened through a Canadian Schedule I bank for confirmed hardware procurement, with agile disbursements from the escrow account upon presentation of compliant transport documents. All remittances over C$10,000 must be reported internally and retained as supporting records under FINTRAC client-information and transaction-reporting rules.Deliverable Canadian-dollar forward contracts (CAD/USD forwards) executed with a Canadian Schedule I bankCross-currency swaps (CAD/USD fixed-for-fixed or floating-for-fixed) for intercompany loan principal and interest hedgesVanilla foreign-exchange options on CAD/USD to hedge contingent SaaS contract receiptsOvernight and tom-next FX swaps for treasury liquidity managementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally unnecessary because CAD is fully convertible, but may be offered by overseas counterparties for CAD exposure under certain restrictive counterparty regimes; they remain permissible only if the Canadian entity transacts through an internationally licensed dealer and demonstrates appropriate risk-management approval

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate to High, subject to dual Canada-Quebec registration. Canada does not issue an 'Electronic Money Institution' licence as in the EU; a non-resident digital EMI must become a registered money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if operating in Quebec, may need to assess Quebec's money-services-browser requirements and provincial consumer-protection registration. A foreign-licensed EMI can otherwise provide payment-processing and multi-currency treasury accounts as a third-party service provider, but it cannot establish a directly licensed deposit-taking institution without a Canadian Schedule I bank charter.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act with fines up to CAD 1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties and interest

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for willful evasion; potential criminal charges, fines, and disqualification from office
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Penalties for sham transactions under Income Tax Act; potential revocation of corporate status and back taxes

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced; directors face personal restitution, fines, and possible imprisonment

Director personal liability: Officers personally liable for fines and potential jail time for reckless or willful AML breaches
