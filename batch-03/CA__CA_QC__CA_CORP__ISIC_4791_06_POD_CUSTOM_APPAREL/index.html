Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Quebec-specific: Revenu Québec – https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities carrying on business in Canada must register for a federal Business Number (BN) with CRA using the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or Form RC1. The BN is a nine-digit unique identifier. For GST/HST, the BN with an 'RT' program account suffix serves as the GST/HST registration number. For Quebec, a separate QST registration number must be obtained from Revenu Québec. Documentation includes proof of incorporation in the home jurisdiction, identification of directors, and information on Canadian activities. Registration is required if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a fiscal year, or if other registration triggers apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act. It is not a pass-through entity; profits are taxed at the corporate level, and dividends are taxed separately on shareholders. Federal and Quebec provincial corporate tax rates apply, with a preferential rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations where applicable.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or entity classification election is available under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Tax classification is determined by statute and legal form. Certain elections exist (e.g., s.216 rental income election, associated corporations allocation), but they do not alter entity classification. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.12.6, s.18.4) disallow deductions for certain hybrid instruments; no elective mitigation is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital property, zero-rated exports) for both GST/HST and QST; applicable to POD retailers once threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada by the supplier qualify as zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, provided they are delivered to a non-resident outside Canada and proof of export is retained. For POD custom apparel shipped to a foreign customer via a commercial carrier, the supply is zero-rated. Services supplied to a non-resident that are performed entirely outside Canada may also qualify. The supplier must maintain documentary evidence to substantiate the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (G13/Exit) or commercial invoice with customs proof
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill or courier tracking evidence of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt for foreign currency settlement

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance for B2B or B2C transactions. Electronic invoicing is permitted if it satisfies CRA and Revenu Québec evidentiary requirements; no continuous transaction controls, API requirements, or digital stamping are statutorily required. B2B e-invoicing mandates are anticipated but not yet implemented.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively for up to four years (longer in cases of misrepresentation). Improper zero-rating results in assessment of uncollected GST/QST, interest, penalties of 10% of net tax, and up to 50% of the tax for negligence. Fraudulent claims can lead to criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment. Anti-avoidance rules under the Excise Tax Act apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no exchange-control legislation, no restrictions on current-account transactions, and no restrictions on capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, repatriation of capital, and intercompany payments may be freely effected in Canadian dollars or any foreign currency, subject only to standard corporate solvency and dividend-eligibility rules under the Canada Business Corporations Act and the Quebec Business Corporations Act. No prior central-bank approval, foreign-exchange license, or repatriation tax clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any government authority. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, converted at any authorized financial institution, or used directly for settlement of foreign-currency liabilities at the discretion of the exporter.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential banking regulator; Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange control authority or exchange-control licensing board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. Foreign electronic money institutions and non-resident fintech payment service providers are not permitted to take deposits or carry on deposit-taking business in Canada without being authorized as a bank, a federal credit union, or a payment service provider registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Offshore digital EMIs may offer payment facilitation through Canadian partner banks or local money-services-business licensees, but a Quebec-based operating entity should primarily rely on regulated Canadian Schedule I banks and authorized payment processors for treasury clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting entity to the Quebec corporation's Canadian-dollar operating account at a Schedule I bank. The wire should carry a clear ISO 20022 remittance information field describing the transaction as 'share capital contribution', 'contributed surplus', or 'non-resident shareholder loan', supported by corporate resolutions and subscription documents. Canada has no central-bank foreign-exchange purpose-code regime and does not require prior approval for inward FDI; however, the receiving bank may request a source-of-funds declaration, the Quebec corporate registry extract, and beneficial-ownership confirmation to satisfy FINTRAC and OSFI guidance. Large settlements may additionally require the remitting institution to use its Canadian correspondent bank and the Payments Canada Lynx settlement system.Outbound supplier and fulfillment payments should use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers, with commercial letters of credit presented via SWIFT MT700/MT707 where the POD supplier requires documentary assurance. Canadian-dollar domestic disbursements clear through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system or the Real-Time Rail for faster retail payments. United States dollar payments to POD fulfillment partners may be settled through Canadian banks' US clearing correspondents or through the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) / Fedwire using a US-dollar operating account.
