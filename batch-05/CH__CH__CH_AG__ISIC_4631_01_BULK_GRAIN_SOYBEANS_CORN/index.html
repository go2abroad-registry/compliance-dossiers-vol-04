Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug) acting under the supervisory authority of the Federal Commercial Register Office (Schweizerisches Handelsregisteramt) at the Swiss Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP); electronic filing conducted through the notary via the eGRIS system.

Governing statute

Switzerland: Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Articles 620–643 (formation, capital, registration of the Aktiengesellschaft), Articles 626 and 629 (mandatory contents of the deed and articles of association), Article 633 (paid-up capital confirmation), Article 727a (audit exemption), and Articles 640–643 (commercial register entry and attainment of legal personality).

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister – certified application for entry into the Commercial Register, together with the notarized deed of incorporation (Errichtungsurkunde), the articles of association (Statuten), and the paid-up capital confirmation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000 (Art. 621 OR); a minimum of CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a licensed Swiss bank. The bank must issue an unconditional paid-up capital confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) before the commercial register application can be filed; the funds remain blocked and become freely available to the company only upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Standard office workplace clearance under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and accident prevention rules of Suva; fire safety certification must be issued by the Cantonal Building Insurance of Zug (Gebäudeversicherung Zug) following an inspection of the registered office; structural load clearance applies only to physical grain storage installations, which are not present in the current brokerage scope.

Paid-in capital rule: Nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000 (Art. 621 OR); a minimum of CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a licensed Swiss bank. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial license is mandated for commission-based bulk grain and soybean brokerage under ISIC 4631. Where the company transitions to physical import or storage, the relevant authority becomes the Swiss Federal Office for Agriculture (FOAG) for agricultural trade quotas and the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (FSVO) for feedstuffs regulation.

Category: None required for the current pure-brokerage scope. Optional registrations: agricultural import quota registration with FOAG and feed business operator registration with FSVO should physical grain handling, storage, or importation be added.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC); online portal: www.estv.admin.ch and dedicated VAT e-portal (Suche / VAT access) at estv.admin.ch for filing and registration.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is automatically assigned a Swiss Business Identification Number (UID/IDE) upon Commercial Register incorporation at the cantonal register. VAT registration is a separate mandatory step with the Federal Tax Administration: the AG (or a foreign non-established entity) must file Form 'Registrierung für die Mehrwertsteuer / Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuerpflicht' (VAT registration notification) through the FTA e-portal. Non-resident entities supplying goods or services in Switzerland must register once their annual worldwide turnover from Swiss-taxable supplies reaches CHF 100,000, and must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative unless they have a Swiss branch or permanent establishment. The FTA issues a 9-digit UID followed by MWST/IVA number; registration becomes effective ex nunc and requires bookkeeping evidence of turnover thresholds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not permit US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections and regimes: (i) VAT group registration election (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) under Art. 12 of the Swiss VAT Act for closely affiliated companies; (ii) small-business exemption under Art. 11 VAT Act for turnover below CHF 100,000 with right to opt into standard taxation; (iii) Group Taxation (profitable group taxation) for corporate income tax from fiscal year 2020 at cantonal/federal level; (iv) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains under Art. 69-70 of the Federal Act on Direct Taxation; (v) hybrid mismatch neutralisation under Swiss domestic rules implementing BEPS Action 2 — no elective carve-out is available for mismatching structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies with a Swiss place of supply; a de facto zero threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services and certain B2B services subject to reverse charge; simplified registration applies for foreign businesses making only occasional deliveries in Switzerland.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported by the supplier or on its behalf out of Swiss customs territory, evidenced by the customs export declaration (Ausfuhrdokument). For an AG acting as bulk vessel broker for soybeans and yellow corn (ISIC 4631), zero-rating of brokerage commission services applies when the recipient is a foreign-resident business and the services relate directly to goods in transit or to international transport of the goods, or when the services are economically used abroad; goods stored in Swiss free ports remain outside the Swiss VAT territory and are exempt under bonded-warehouse rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / ZEBIS electronic record with customs clearance stamp or electronic transit reference T1)
  • Bill of Lading (shipped-on-board negotiable maritime bill for bulk grain/corn cargo) or CMR/CIM contract of carriage for overland transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence of foreign currency receipt for the underlying commodity sale) and the cash-sale/contract confirmation showing title transfer and foreign destination

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based transactional clearance mandate. E-invoicing and QR-bills are voluntary alternatives to paper invoices. VAT returns (formular 1010) must be filed electronically through the FTA e-portal on a quarterly or annual basis depending on taxpayer volume, with SIX/Multipay integration for payment. Swiss customs clearances via the e-dec / ZEBIS system serve as the operational export-control interface.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-checked by the FTA against customs exit records, commercial contracts, shipping manifests, and banks' inward remittance files. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating triggers immediate reassessment at 8.1% VAT plus default interest at 4% per annum and administrative fines up to four times the evaded tax under Art. 182 VAT Act; structured sham export schemes are prosecuted as tax fraud under Art. 186 VAT Act and Art. 146 Swiss Criminal Code, with custodial penalties possible and personal director liability; clawback is unlimited for fraud and gross negligence and subject to a ten-year limitation only for ordinary negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland grants full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no central-bank permit, exchange licence, or currency-cover requirement applies to trade or capital remittances. The CHF floats freely; the exchange-rate floor of 15 January 2015 was permanently removed. Profit repatriation is legally unrestricted, but a Swiss AG paying dividends to its foreign shareholder faces the 35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) unless a double-taxation treaty exemption or refund path applies. Banks additionally enforce AMLA origin-of-funds duties, so first deposits can be delayed while the economic background of the contributing shareholder is verified. Practical limitation: the SNB requires Swiss banks to report any counterparty transaction flow equal to or exceeding CHF 10 million in a calendar month for balance-of-payments statistics; the corporate must provide sufficient purpose and trade data for that filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist in Switzerland. Export proceeds from bulk grain, soybeans, and corn sales (USD, EUR, BRL, or other currencies) may be retained indefinitely in domestic Swiss bank accounts, in any currency, with no mandatory conversion into CHF and no statutory repatriation deadline. The sole statutory duty is the bank's own reporting to the SNB under the Ordinance on Cross-Border Payment Statistics for payments reaching CHF 10 million per counterparty per month; the exporting AG must merely supply accurate payment reference and counterparty details to enable the bank to comply.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse, Börsenstrasse 15, Zurich); complemented by the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for external financial policy. The SNB is the sole monetary authority, retains the right to intervene in the free-floating franc market, and operates the cross-border payments statistical surveillance system; it does not issue foreign-exchange operating licences.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low for the principal treasury and LC-backed trade settlement; marginal as a collection-only sub-rail. A Swiss AG must keep core treasury funds with a FINMA-authorised bank or a licensed payment institution under the FinIA and AMLA; offshore digital EMIs (UK, Lithuanian, Caribbean, or other non-FINMA licences) cannot issue letters of credit in the name of the Swiss AG, cannot act as prime treasury counterparties, and are automatically graded high-risk by Swiss banks. A Swiss-licensed fintech holding a payment-institution licence (for example a FINMA-authorised Zug-based payment institution) may be used for small-value collections and pre-screening of inbound FX receipts, but all grain settlement and working-capital flows must run over SWIFT and LC rails through an SRO-member bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital is transferred by SWIFT MT103 or MT199 from the parent's or subscriber's own bank account to the Swiss AG's account at UBS Switzerland AG (SWIFT: UBSWCHZH80A), ZKB (SWIFT: ZKBKCHZZ80A), or the Zuger Kantonalbank escrow; the instruction must state the purpose as 'Kapitalerhöhung' (capital increase) or 'Zeichnungsschein' subscription and the bank requires a notarised board minute, the share-register update, and confirmation that the remitter is the exact subscriber. Intercompany loans are routed by MT103 with reference 'Intercompany-Darlehen' together with the executed loan facility, an arm's-length interest schedule, and an AMLA Art. 4 origin-of-funds statement. This AG will issue equity injection documentation in either CHF, USD, or EUR; no SNB exchange-control permit is required. Any aggregate counterparty flow above CHF 10 million per calendar month is automatically disclosed to the SNB by the bank under the Cross-Border Payments Statistics Ordinance. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In CHF: Local File is required if the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds CHF 250,000 for goods or services, or CHF 100,000 for interest, per tax year. Master File threshold: if the multinational group's total consolidated turnover exceeds CHF 900 million, a Swiss Master File statement and country-by-country reporting notification are mandatory.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply the EU's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a domestic doctrine, but the Federal Act on the Implementation of International Tax Transparency Standards and the revised Circular 4 (ESTV, Steuervertretung) impose substance requirements under the tax avoidance provisions. A Swiss AG must maintain: actual office premises in Switzerland, qualified directors or employees performing substantive management activities, board meetings in Switzerland, and full accounting records. A 'letterbox company' or shell structure with only nominee directors will be disregarded under the spiritual theory of management (statutarischer Sitz), and profits may be reattributed to the place of effective management. The OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) minimum standards also require the brokerage function to be supported by real personnel who conclude and negotiate contracts, manage logistics contracts, and bear commodity price risk if claiming entitlement to trading profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Swiss Federal Tax Act, Art. 186)

Statutory sanction: A fine of up to 250% of the tax evaded; if the evasion is committed by intentional use of forged documents, false records, or a criminal conspiracy, imprisonment up to three years for responsible directors and tax advisors. Penalty interest at 4.5% per annum is mandatory, and a criminal conviction is entered in the Swiss criminal record.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded taxes if they signed the unlawful tax return or participated in the scheme. Additionally, the Federal Administrative Criminal Law Act (VStrR) permits seizure of the director's Swiss assets and a travel ban or exit prohibition (Ausreisesperre) while the criminal tax investigation is pending. In severe cases, extradition procedures under the mutual legal assistance treaty may be triggered; a director resident in Switzerland may face preventive detention pending trial.
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by ESTV under the place of effective management doctrine; all attributed profit is taxed as hidden profit distribution at 35% anticipatory tax plus cantonal/federal direct taxes. Capital tax may be reassessed at a deemed minimum equity of 10% of assets, and the company's commercial register entry may be struck off with forced liquidation if the corporate form is abused.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure a minimum physical, personnel, and operational presence can be personally liable under Swiss company law (Art. 754 OR) for damage to the tax authority and creditors. Directors may be required to repay any distributions made while the company was insolvent; a personal criminal fine is possible under Art. 86 para. 2 of the Criminal Code for failure to maintain proper books and records.
Anticipatory Tax Reimbursement Fraud

Statutory sanction: According to Art. 14 of the Anticipatory Tax Act (VStG), claim taxes are subject to a penalty equal to the unlawfully refunded amount, an additional fine of up to 100% of the claim, and an interest surcharge of 5%. Criminal penalties for subsidy fraud under Art. 14 VStrR extend to imprisonment up to five years, and banks or intermediaries involved lose their safe-harbor protection.

Director personal liability: The board members must sign personal declarations that the facts stated in Form 82 are true; deliberate fraud causes direct liability of the signatory with no corporate indemnification under Swiss law. The ESTV may publish the conviction and impose a banking freeze on personal accounts held in Switzerland.
AML / Money Laundering (AML-FINMA)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Art. 10 of the Swiss AML Act of up to CHF 500,000 for negligent money laundering; aggravated money laundering under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code carries imprisonment of up to five years. FINMA licensing infractions for unregistered financial intermediaries may lead to a fine of up to CHF 1 million and a prohibition from operating in Switzerland.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable under the Swiss Corporate Criminal Liability Act if the offense arises from the lack of adequate organization (Art. 6 VStrR). Personal imprisonment up to one year is possible for directors who knowingly approve trades where the grain collateral documents are fraudulent; a travel ban is commonly imposed while money laundering investigations proceed.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Procedural fine up to CHF 10,000 plus a record-keeping penalty of 0.1% of the inappropriate transaction volume per month of delay; the tax authority may estimate the arm's length price based on its own benchmark and issue a hidden profit distribution assessment, resulting in additional corporate income tax of up to 18% plus 35% anticipatory tax on the deemed distribution.

Director personal liability: Under the Swiss Tax Ordinance, the duty to produce books and records rests on the management; directors can be fined up to CHF 10,000 personally for refusing to produce documentation, and if the resulting assessment causes insolvency, directors may be held liable for negligent loss of corporate assets under Art. 754 OR.
Criminal Anticipatory Tax Non-Withholding

Statutory sanction: Corporate penalty equal to the unpaid anticipatory tax; estate tax claim remains enforceable against the company. Directors are guilty of an offense under Art. 24 VStG and are individually subject to a fine equal to the unpaid tax if the company is insolvent. Criminal fines up to CHF 30,000 plus costs.

Director personal liability: Personal liability without limitation for the unpaid anticipatory tax due to human error or willful disregard; the board's own assets can be attached in execution proceedings initiated by ESTV. Directors have a duty to notify the general meeting to withhold; failure to do so is a serious breach of fiduciary duty.
