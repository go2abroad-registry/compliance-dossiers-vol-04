Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — administers Part I corporate income tax and Part IX GST/HST; digital portal: My Business Account / GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Quebec provincial: Revenu Québec — administers the QST, Quebec provincial corporate income tax and employer remittances; portal: Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). Company registries: Corporations Canada (federal incorporation) and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporation: incorporate the Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal incorporation number) or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act, then register the legal person with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the NEQ. Step 2 — Federal tax ID: file CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts — RC (T2 corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and, if applicable, RD (customs/import-export). Step 3 — Provincial tax ID: using the NEQ, register with Revenu Québec for the QST (TVQ) account, Quebec corporate tax account, and employer health-services/payroll accounts; QST registration is triggered when the taxable supply threshold is exceeded. Step 4 — Non-resident variant: a foreign entity not incorporated in Canada but carrying on business in Quebec must still obtain a BN from CRA (Form RC363 with a resident agent) and an NEQ/TVQ registration from Revenu Québec before invoicing Canadian counterparties or claiming export zero-rating relief.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Quebec/Canadian Business Corporation is a 'corporation' under ITA s. 89(1) and a legal person under the Taxation Act (Quebec); it is taxed at entity level on worldwide income if resident in Canada (residence established at incorporation for federal or Quebec provincial corporations), with no check-the-box or pass-through classification available. A non-resident foreign-incorporated corporation is taxed under Part I only on Canadian-source business income, including branch profits under Part XIV, allocated to Quebec if a permanent establishment exists. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Quebec/Canadian Business Corporation is a 'corporation' under ITA s. 89(1) and a legal person under the Taxation Act (Quebec); it is taxed at entity level on worldwide income if resident in Canada (residence established at incorporation for federal or Quebec provincial corporations), with no check-the-box or pass-through classification available. A non-resident foreign-incorporated corporation is taxed under Part I only on Canadian-source business income, including branch profits under Part XIV, allocated to Quebec if a permanent establishment exists. Copper cathode wholesale arbitrage income is ordinary business income subject to the general federal and Quebec corporate rates; the small business deduction depends on active-business characterization and Quebec concordance rules.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form. Available elections for a Business Corporation: (i) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 (with the Quebec Taxation Act mirror for provincial purposes); (ii) associated-corporations designation and small-business-limit allocation agreement under ITA s. 125(3.1)-(3.5); (iii) section 85 rollover election on transfer of trading assets/contracts and receivables into the newly incorporated entity; (iv) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to deem supplies for nil consideration with the QST mirror in AQLST s. 449; (v) ETA s. 273 qualifying joint-venture election to designate a GST/HST operator, with the corresponding QST election; and (vi) permanent establishment designation and Quebec income allocation agreements. Hybrid mismatch: no election mechanism applies; ITA ss. 18.68-18.75 and the BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules (including 2025 imported hybrid amendments) operate mechanically to deny duplicate deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold: a registrant must register for GST/HST when taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240). The identical CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST registration (AQLST s. 417). Zero-rated export supplies count as taxable supplies for the threshold; once registered, all supplies are taxable unless zero-rated, and exporters generally register voluntarily to claim input tax credits on acquisition costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST: 0% applies to goods supplied by a registrant that (a) exports the goods itself, (b) ships them to a destination outside Canada, or (c) sells goods to a non-resident who exports them, provided the goods leave Canada within the ordinary course of business and documentary export evidence is retained — ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1-3. QST: equivalent export zero-rating under AQLST s. 44 et seq. for corporeal movable property (copper cathodes) delivered outside Quebec/Canada. Related services supplied to non-residents for use entirely outside Canada may also be zero-rated (ETA Sch. VI s. 7; QST mirror). Actual physical removal from Canada is mandatory: a book-entry transfer of an LME warrant from a Canadian vault without shipment abroad does not constitute an export and remains taxable at 14.5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration — filing via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), successor to CAED/Form B13A, required for shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more unless exempted by CBSA rules
  • Bill of Lading / multimodal transport document or cargo manifest naming the non-resident consignee and the foreign destination port/warehouse
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or documentary letter of credit settlement evidencing receipt of the foreign currency proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API stamping or digital stamp mandate exists at the federal or Quebec level. Invoicing rules require prescribed content and GST/HST and QST registration numbers on the face of each invoice (ETA s. 223(1); AQLST ss. 462-464); Quebec invoices must be available in French under the Charter of the French Language. Electronic invoices in EDI/XML/PDF are lawful without pre-clearance; tax returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec e-services), and books/records must be retained in readable electronic form. A voluntary PEPPOL-based pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard exists for public procurement only; it is not mandatory for B2B commodity trading.

On audit, CRA and Revenu Québec verify the full export trail: if exported goods are not substantiated or the invoice misstates the export, zero-rating is reversed and output tax is reassessed at 5% GST plus 9.5% QST with compound interest. Reassessment windows are generally four years under ETA s. 298, but remain open indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect or fraud. Penalties include: false statements/omissions — greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the tax assessed (CAD 500 and 10% on repeat), ETA s. 285; failure to produce records — escalating penalties under ETA ss. 283 and 286; and criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 with fines up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment. Revenu Québec applies concordant penalties under the AQLST and may claw back QST refunds and deny input tax refunds. There is no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the schema-controlled trade-finance and AML benchmark, and unreasonable delay in inward remittance may be treated as artificial financing or a non-arm's-length pricing adjustment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally imposes no exchange-control licensing, blocked-currency regime, mandatory central-bank approval, or general capital repatriation restriction. A Canadian corporation may receive foreign currency, convert it through a regulated financial institution, pay foreign suppliers, repay documented shareholder or third-party debt, distribute dividends subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, and remit profits to non-resident shareholders. Practical restrictions arise from bank anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, source-of-funds review, tax reporting, transfer-pricing rules, withholding tax on certain outbound payments, non-resident withholding on dividends and specified interest or other amounts, customs and trade documentation, and provincial securities or derivatives rules. Dividends must be declared in accordance with the Canada Business Corporations Act or applicable Quebec corporate statute and cannot be paid where statutory solvency tests are not satisfied. Related-party funding and treasury transactions should be supported by arm's-length agreements, transfer-pricing documentation, corporate approvals, and evidence of commercial purpose. Foreign-exchange transactions involving sanctioned persons, sanctioned jurisdictions, terrorism financing, or unexplained third-party payments may be refused, blocked, reported, or escalated by the financial institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, convert a prescribed percentage of export receipts into Canadian dollars, or repatriate export receipts within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may ordinarily remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through foreign correspondent banks, subject to contractual bank controls, sanctions screening, tax and accounting requirements, and accurate customs and trade records. The corporation should retain sales contracts, invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin, import or export declarations where applicable, warehouse or LME warrant records, proof of delivery, payment confirmations, and reconciliation evidence. Large or unusual international electronic funds transfers may trigger reporting by the reporting entity under Canadian anti-money-laundering rules. Foreign affiliates, foreign bank accounts, and specified foreign property may create Canadian tax information-reporting obligations, including T1135 reporting where applicable. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Federal foreign-exchange, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, customs, and prudential requirements are also administered or supervised by the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and applicable provincial securities regulators, including the Autorité des marchés financiers in Quebec.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection, foreign-exchange execution, expense management, and limited treasury services; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank for a physical copper-trading business. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution generally cannot conduct Canadian deposit-taking, represent itself as a Canadian bank, or provide unrestricted Canadian payment services without complying with applicable Canadian federal and provincial requirements. Foreign providers must address Canadian retail-payment regulation where applicable, FINTRAC registration and obligations where they conduct regulated money-services activities in or from Canada, sanctions screening, correspondent-bank requirements, data and privacy controls, and Canadian-dollar settlement access. Client funds may be safeguarded or pooled rather than held as insured Canadian bank deposits. Offshore EMI accounts are unsuitable as the sole account for customs payments, payroll, tax remittances, documentary-letter-of-credit issuance, LME-related settlement, or high-value third-party commodity flows. Use a Canadian chartered bank as the primary operating and settlement account and a regulated fintech only as a documented supplemental rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity subscriptions and shareholder loans from the identifiable investor or lender directly to the Canadian corporation's account at a Canadian chartered bank through SWIFT or an equivalent regulated bank-to-bank channel. Use a payment reference stating the legal entity name, contribution type, and purpose, such as equity subscription or shareholder loan, and avoid unexplained third-party remitters. Before receipt, provide the bank with the executed subscription or loan agreement, board and shareholder approvals, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, tax-residency details, and expected use of proceeds. Reconcile the incoming wire to the corporate ledger and record whether the amount is share capital, contributed surplus, or debt. Do not describe an equity subscription as ordinary trade revenue or use personal accounts. Canada generally does not require a central-bank capital-account registration or a statutory foreign-exchange purpose code; however, the bank may require internal purpose classifications, regulatory declarations, sanctions information, and supporting commercial documents. Large international electronic funds transfers are subject to applicable reporting by the reporting institution, and suspicious activity may be reported to FINTRAC. For capital received from a related non-resident, preserve arm's-length loan terms, interest support, withholding-tax analysis, thin-capitalization analysis, transfer-pricing documentation, and beneficial-ownership evidence.Use the Canadian operating account for supplier payments, customs charges, freight, insurance, inspection, warehouse, exchange, and customer-refund obligations. Standard bank-to-bank wires may be transmitted through SWIFT MT103 or successor ISO 20022 payment messages, subject to the sending and receiving banks' formats, sanctions screening, correspondent-bank requirements, and complete originator and beneficiary information. For copper purchases, require a three-way match among the purchase contract, commercial invoice, and logistics or title documents before release. Commercial documentary letters of credit may be issued, advised, confirmed, transferred, or discounted by a bank subject to credit approval, collateral, margin, UCP 600 terms where incorporated, and applicable sanctions controls. Specify acceptable transport, warehouse, assay, origin, inspection, insurance, and title documents; prohibit document substitution and uncontrolled third-party payment; and align LC presentation terms with the actual shipment and title chain. For LME-related transactions, use an approved member, broker, clearing bank, or custodian and verify warrant ownership, warehouse status, title transfer, settlement obligations, and applicable exchange rules. Maintain payment sanctions screening, dual authorization, callback verification for changed instructions, segregation of duties, daily cash reconciliation, and documented exception approval. Outbound dividends, interest, management fees, royalties, and related-party payments require corporate approvals, withholding-tax review, treaty documentation, transfer-pricing support, and evidence of services or indebtedness.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a regulated Canadian bank or appropriately registered dealer, used to hedge documented receivables, payables, inventory commitments, or forecast exposures and supported by a board-approved treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards may be used for currencies that are not freely deliverable or where settlement is contractually cash-settled, subject to dealer eligibility, applicable Canadian derivatives requirements, trade reporting, collateral, close-out, and sanctions controls. An NDF does not create a legal right to bypass the foreign-exchange rules of the reference-currency jurisdiction.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps with a regulated bank or eligible derivatives dealer may be used for documented funding or currency-mismatch exposures, subject to confirmations, collateral or credit-support terms, valuation, accounting, regulatory reporting, and Quebec or other applicable provincial derivatives requirements.Foreign-exchange options and collars may be considered for asymmetric copper-trade cash-flow exposures, subject to suitability, documented risk limits, premium accounting, dealer disclosures, and senior approval. Speculative trading unrelated to the corporation's commercial exposure should be prohibited.Hedging counterparties should be selected through credit limits, ISDA or equivalent master agreements, confirmations, netting and close-out provisions, collateral documentation where applicable, sanctions screening, independent valuation, and periodic effectiveness testing. The corporation should maintain exposure schedules, hedge-designation records where hedge accounting is used, and controls preventing hedges from exceeding the underlying commercial exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment collection, foreign-exchange execution, expense management, and limited treasury services; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank for a physical copper-trading business. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution generally cannot conduct Canadian deposit-taking, represent itself as a Canadian bank, or provide unrestricted Canadian payment services without complying with applicable Canadian federal and provincial requirements. Foreign providers must address Canadian retail-payment regulation where applicable, FINTRAC registration and obligations where they conduct regulated money-services activities in or from Canada, sanctions screening, correspondent-bank requirements, data and privacy controls, and Canadian-dollar settlement access. Client funds may be safeguarded or pooled rather than held as insured Canadian bank deposits. Offshore EMI accounts are unsuitable as the sole account for customs payments, payroll, tax remittances, documentary-letter-of-credit issuance, LME-related settlement, or high-value third-party commodity flows. Use a Canadian chartered bank as the primary operating and settlement account and a regulated fintech only as a documented supplemental rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.95% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.70% net of federal abatement, effective provincial rate 11.95% before general reduction; combined general corporate rate in Quebec is 26.95% for non-manufacturing income after federal abatement and Quebec deduction), applicable to general business income of a non-resident-owned Canadian corporation conducting wholesale commodity trading in Quebec

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory 25% Part XIII withholding tax on all taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, plus no separate Quebec statutory dividend withholding tax for non-residents (Quebec levies a parallel Part XIII-style 25% withholding on dividends paid to non-residents; combined federal and Quebec withholding is administratively consolidated at 25% when federal treaty relief mechanisms are procedural, but the effective combined statutory imposition remains 25% federal plus 25% Quebec for amounts not covered by a simplified protocol; practical application books through a single 25% remittance by the payer). Canada–Quebec fiscal administration applies the federal tax treaties on behalf of Quebec; treaty-reduced rates generally reduce the combined remittance to 5% for portfolio dividends and 15% for substantial holding dividends, subject to Canadian treaty policy
