Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax administration, with the My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; in Quebec, Revenu Québec administers the Québec Sales Tax (QST), provincial income tax, and the Registre des entreprises du Québec (NEQ) registration, with the Clic ReQ portal at https://www.revenuquebec.ca. A federal/provincial business corporation also files through Corporations Canada and, for Quebec operations, with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to carry on business in Canada and Quebec must first obtain a CRA federal Business Number (BN) using the CRA online business registration service. The application requires certified evidence of legal existence, description of the business activity, directors and officers, address of the Canadian registered office or agent for service, and authorization of the filing officer. Once the BN is issued, the entity registers for the GST/HST program code. For Quebec, the entity must additionally register in the Registre des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ and register with Revenu Québec for the QST number; Revenu Québec generally uses the CRA BN as a base identifier and requires evidence that the entity has an establishment, inventory, or taxable supply nexus in Quebec. A non-resident entity must appoint a representative or have sufficient documentary proof of authority before tax IDs are activated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election comparable to the United States. The default classification of a business corporation is entity-level taxation. Relevant statutory elections include designation as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), designation under subsection 89(11) deeming a corporation not to be a CCPC, and an election under subsection 256.1(1) governing fair market value treatment in connection with public corporation status. For hybrid instrument or hybrid entity arrangements, Canada applies automatic anti-hybrid rules under subsections 18.2, 18.3, and 20.2 of the Income Tax Act; no elective carve-out is available, and the rules operate to disallow a deduction or require a corresponding income inclusion to neutralize deduction/no-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies, including sales made through the Canadian/Quebec business, is the statutory small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST. Registration is mandatory once total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; below the threshold registration is voluntary, except that non-resident suppliers with supplies to Canadian persons may be registrant for GST/HST purposes under specified provisions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registrant may zero-rate exported tangible personal property, including pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base bricks, where the supply is a sale made to a non-resident or to a person contracting to export the goods, the goods are shipped from Canada by the supplier, the recipient, or a commercial carrier as part of a continuous unbroken export journey, and the goods are not further manufactured, processed, or consumed in Canada before export. Evidence must show the goods left Canada and the destination is outside Canada. Supplies of related offshore services to a non-resident may also qualify as zero-rated where the services are exclusively for consumption outside Canada. In Quebec, the QST Act provides equivalent zero-rating for exported goods and services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / Canadian Export Declaration, and the associated export report from the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Commercial invoice / export invoice showing exporter, consignee, description of the hotpot soup base, quantity, unit price, currency, HS/Schedule classification, and Incoterms
  • Through Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing delivery to a foreign destination
  • Carrier manifest, cargo control document, courier receipt, or terminal receipt showing the continuous movement of the goods outside Canada
  • Foreign exchange payment record, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, foreign remittance advice, or bank inward remittance certificate where available

Canada and Quebec do not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted in EDI, PEPPOL, XML, or PDF formats, but the supporting books and records must be retained in Canada or Quebec for audit. CRA requires electronic filing for GST/HST registrants above the prescribed revenue thresholds, using NETFILE/GST34 APIs; Revenu Québec imposes analogous electronic filing and remittance obligations for QST registrants through its secure online services.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating claims and reassess output tax, interest, and penalties if export documentation is incomplete or the goods are found not to have been actually exported. The normal reassessment period is four years, but longer or unlimited reassessment applies in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a 25% false-statement or omission penalty on the net tax amount, gross-negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution for knowing evasion or wilful misreporting. Quebec imposes comparable civil penalties and administrative sanctions under the Tax Administration Act. Corporate officers and directors may also be held jointly and severally liable for amounts collected but not remitted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada sustains full current- and capital-account convertibility of the Canadian dollar consistent with IMF Article VIII; there are no statutory gates, quotas, settlement licences or central-bank permissions for dividend remittances, repayment of trade debt, intercompany charges or capital repatriation. Profit-remittance hurdles are therefore non-FX in nature: (i) the CBCA / Quebec Business Corporations Act solvency and capital-impairment tests that must be met before declaring a dividend; (ii) Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act at 25% statutory, reduced by the Canada-Hong Kong agreement to 5% for a corporate shareholder with at least 10% voting power and by the Canada-China agreement to 10% for a corporate shareholder with a substantial direct shareholding; and (iii) bank-side source-of-funds verification for any distribution that follows a cross-border capital injection. Under the Investment Canada Act, establishment of this new Quebec wholesale-distribution business by a WTO investor is notifiable only and does not require a net-benefit review; the financing quantum is also far below the enterprise-value threshold relevant to any acquisition-of-control scenario.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender window, no mandatory repatriation ratio and no forced conversion of export proceeds into Canadian dollars. A Quebec exporter may lawfully hold USD, CNY, HKD or EUR proceeds indefinitely in a foreign-currency account with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank and settle foreign sales without any exchange-approval filing. The only compliance constraints are indirect: accrual recognition of export receivables under sections 9 and 23(1) of the Income Tax Act, the optional functional-currency reporting election under section 261, FINTRAC Large-Value International Electronic Fund Transfer reports for CAD 10,000 or more, and Part XVII transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 where the Chinese supplier or the overseas distributor is related. As a treasury policy matter, the blueprint nevertheless requires a 30-day export-receipt collection standard and a 60-day maximum open FX position so that bank monitoring flags remain negative.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign-exchange administration, so the functional supervisory complements are the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential licensing, FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for transaction surveillance, and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act for foreign investment screening where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury institution and MEDIUM as an ancillary disbursement conduit. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RCRA), a non-bank payment service provider may register with the Bank of Canada to perform clearing, settlement and payment processing for Canadian end-users only after the transitional regime matures fully in 2025; however, retail deposit-taking remains statutorily reserved to Schedule I/II chartered banks under the Bank Act and to Quebec-authorized financial-services cooperatives under the Act respecting financial services cooperatives. An offshore digital EMI (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer or similar) cannot hold demand deposits for a Quebec commercial client without a Canadian bank partner or a trust-account structure, and funds so held are outside Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI) dispute regime. For a food-export wholesaler whose payment rails are SWIFT MT103 and commercial letters of credit, an EMI is unsuitable for the main operating account; it may be used only for low-value incidental cross-border payments (e.g., overseas marketing spend aggregators) after the main chartered-bank account is established and after the EMI has provided a registration attestation under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder capital from a Hong Kong or PRC remitter is wired via SWIFT MT103 as a customer transfer, moved on the Canadian receiving side through Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS) for high-value credits or ACSS for ordinary commercial batches. The sender must include a precise purpose code in the remittance field: 'Share Capital Contribution / Subscription for Common Shares', together with the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), the share certificate or authorized-capital resolution reference, and the full ultimate-originator name/address as required by FINTRAC EFT rules. The receiving bank's AML desk will classify the funds to the paid-in capital ledger and will release them for trade disbursements only after matching the amount against the corporate-law records and completing source-of-funds verification. If the inflow is structured as a shareholder loan instead, the wire purpose must state 'Intercompany Shareholder Loan Advance' and be backed by a signed loan agreement on file to avoid mischaracterization as taxable equity. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury institution and MEDIUM as an ancillary disbursement conduit. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RCRA), a non-bank payment service provider may register with the Bank of Canada to perform clearing, settlement and payment processing for Canadian end-users only after the transitional regime matures fully in 2025; however, retail deposit-taking remains statutorily reserved to Schedule I/II chartered banks under the Bank Act and to Quebec-authorized financial-services cooperatives under the Act respecting financial services cooperatives. An offshore digital EMI (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer or similar) cannot hold demand deposits for a Quebec commercial client without a Canadian bank partner or a trust-account structure, and funds so held are outside Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI) dispute regime. For a food-export wholesaler whose payment rails are SWIFT MT103 and commercial letters of credit, an EMI is unsuitable for the main operating account; it may be used only for low-value incidental cross-border payments (e.g., overseas marketing spend aggregators) after the main chartered-bank account is established and after the EMI has provided a registration attestation under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate for non-CCPC active business income in Quebec (Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); 12.2% combined small business rate where the corporation is Canadian-controlled and eligible for the small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited to non-resident shareholders. This applies automatically to any dividend paid by the Canadian corporation to its foreign parent unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions triggers the T106 filing and contemporaneous Local File obligation. CRA records-based transfer pricing review applies at the same threshold; a Master File is recommended where the foreign group exceeds CAD 100,000,000 gross revenue, and CRA may request Master File data under ITA s.247(4).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code as in Gulf jurisdictions; instead, substance is mandatory under (i) ITA s.247 arm's length recharacterization, (ii) ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, (iii) the permanent establishment analysis in the Canada-OECD treaties, and (iv) the common-law business purpose test. The Canadian wholesale subsidiary must have actual procurement negotiation authority, inventory title, warehouse handling, credit risk, invoicing, and Canadian decision-makers for the hotpot base distribution; otherwise CRA can reallocate the profit to the foreign affiliate, deny treaty benefits for the residual income, or apply the GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Wilful Omission or False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to five years; Quebec Tax Administration Act applies similar fines and penalties. CRA's Criminal Investigations Program and Revenu Quebec's anti-economy investigation unit may jointly prosecute.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and in-house counsel who authorized, acquiesced, or directed the scheme are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax under ITA s.227.1 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; criminal conviction exposes personal fines, asset seizure, travel-bank restrictions, and extradition risk under the Canada-China mutual legal assistance treaty.
Shell Company / Conduit Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 denies the tax benefit, imposes a 25% penalty on the amount of the benefit, plus prescribed-rate interest; CRA may alternatively apply s.247(2) to adjust the arm's length price and, where the treaty interposes, recharacterize the Quebec entity as a mere investment vehicle causing the profit to be taxed as Canadian-source income of the foreign shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors remain personally exposed under s.227.1 only for unremitted withholdings, but courts have pierced the veil in tax matters where the subsidiary is a mere facade; civil restitution orders, disqualification as directors, and Quebec travel bans under the Tax Administration Act for unpaid personal or corporate assessments are available.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3): penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment with no upper cap; CRA is entitled to the 7-year normal reassessment period for misstatement arising from gross negligence; daily compound interest at the prescribed rate plus 4 percentage points runs from the date the amount would otherwise have been payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally signed false transfer-pricing declarations may be subject to tax evasion prosecutions; under the directors' liability provisions of the Canada Business Corporations Act and analogous Quebec law, they can be ordered to make restitution to the corporation for the amount of the CRA penalty and interest where their negligence caused the financial loss.
Withholding Tax and Profit Repatriation Stripping

Statutory sanction: ITA s.212(1): full 25% Part XIII tax remains payable by the non-resident, and under s.215 the Canadian payer is assessed for the amount not withheld; the Section 215 assessment is not dischargeable by bankruptcy; the CRA may also disallow the deduction of the payment under s.212 where the recipient is not the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly liable under ITA s.227.1 for the unremitted amounts and any related interest/penalties even if they delegated payroll or accounts payable functions; Revenu Quebec obtains judgment against directors personally and can, under the recovery statute, restrict the directors from leaving Canada where the debt is significant; deliberate schemes also trigger conspiracy charges and director imprisonment under the Criminal Code.
