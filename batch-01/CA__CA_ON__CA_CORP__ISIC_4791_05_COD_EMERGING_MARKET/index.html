Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full Article VIII current-account convertibility and open capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. CAD is fully convertible into all major currencies, and foreign-currency accounts may be held both domestically and abroad. The only constraints are reporting thresholds: every incoming or outgoing international electronic transfer equivalent to CAD $10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC, and any cash/currency import or export of CAD $10,000+ requires a declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes withholding taxes on certain cross-border distributions, but this is a tax obligation, not a convertibility or liquidity restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receivables indefinitely, convert at a time of their choosing, and hold proceeds in Canadian or offshore accounts. There is no statutory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Under the Foreign Exchange Control regime (abolished), financial institutions are required to collect and report business number, particulars, and transaction details for any international electronic funds transfer of CAD $10,000 or more, but this is an anti-money-laundering measure rather than a surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Minister of Finance and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border monetary and AML/ATF oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed Canadian banks and cannot maintain accounts at the Bank of Canada, but they can settle into Canadian partner banks if the EMI is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and licensed in the relevant province (e.g., Ontario under the Money Services Business Licensing Act). The Canadian operating entity may use such EMIs to initiate and receive cross-border payments for COD settlement, but the EMI must accept CDD documentation consistent with Canadian AML/CFT rules. A purely foreign EMI with no Canadian MSB registration will be unable to access the Canadian LVTS/ACH rails and may be limited to SWIFT (MT103) payment corridors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) to the Canadian bank's correspondent account, referencing the corporate entity name and account number. The remittance instruction must include a purpose code (IMF Balance-of-Payments category) such as 'EQ' for equity injection, 'D300' for intercompany loans, or 'D763' for trade credit; the bank will perform OFAC and Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening. For equity injections, the following must be available to the receiving bank: (i) board resolution approving issuance; (ii) signed shareholder subscription agreement; (iii) updated share register and UBO disclosure form; (iv) evidence that the funds originate from the declaring shareholder's own account; and (v) FATCA/CRS self-certifications for the remitter. If the injection is in foreign currency, the bank will credit a USD or GBP sub-ledger or automatically convert to CAD; conversion to CAD is at the applicable market rate with no exchange-control limit.Outbound trade and logistics disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 open-payment transfers for supplier invoices; irrevocable confirmed commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) through a Canadian bank's trade finance desk are used for larger procurement from emerging-market sellers; for COD logistics, Airwallex's multi-currency settlement API is recommended for smaller-value last-mile remittances to local couriers, provided each leg is tagged with the originating order ID. All outbound transfers must carry a valid purpose code (e.g., '400' for goods, '687' for logistics services) and be screened against the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, OFAC SDN List, and United Nations Security Council sanctions. Canadian-dollar domestic legs clear same-day through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), while USD and other currencies are cleared via SWIFT/CHIPS/ACH. Non-resident corporations must report only income reasonably attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment under ITA s.115, including Canadian COD sales routed through local logistics platforms. Late T2 filing penalty under ITA s.162(2): 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delinquency up to 12 months, with elevated 10% plus 2% per month for repeat offenders; daily interest under ITA s.161 also accrues on unpaid amounts.
Line 300 — Taxable Income and Schedule 400 — Part I Tax Calculation Computation of taxable income after loss carryovers and deduction of the SBD where eligible, then application of Part I federal tax and Ontario provincial corporate tax under the integrated T2 schedules. Apply the applicable income tax treaty's business profits article, claim treaty relief where no permanent establishment exists, and compute Part XIII withholding on any payments to the foreign parent. Late T2 filing penalty under ITA s.162(2): 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delinquency up to 12 months, with elevated 10% plus 2% per month for repeat offenders; daily interest under ITA s.161 also accrues on unpaid amounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 total annual value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties across property, services, intangibles, or interest; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared under ITA s.247 and CRA Information Circular 87-2R.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no generic Economic Substance Regulations comparable to certain offshore centres; however, CRA applies the arm's-length standard under ITA s.247(2), the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245, and beneficial ownership analysis to deny treaty benefits where transactions or corporate structures lack demonstrable economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsification of Books and Records

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): on summary conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine of 100%–200% of tax evaded; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years plus the same financial penalties; late-filing penalties and arrears interest also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized or acquiesced in the false filing may be personally liable for civil restitution and, under s.227.1, for unpaid withholdings. Criminal conviction may result in travel bans, seizure of personal property, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR under ITA s.245(2) to deny the tax benefit and impose a penalty of 50% of the benefit; transfer pricing recharacterization under s.247(2) may adjust amounts; reassessments may be extended by three years beyond the normal period.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the conduit arrangements may be liable for unpaid corporate taxes, subjected to civil fraud penalties, and exposed to criminal liability for evasion if the arrangement is found to be a sham.
AML / Cash on Delivery Structuring

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) violations carry administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000; Criminal Code s.462.31 money laundering offence carries imprisonment up to 10 years and criminal forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or knowingly facilitated money laundering through the corporate COD structure face personal criminal liability, restitution orders, asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and permanent disqualification from serving as directors.
