Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The QBCA does not impose any minimum paid-up capital. A share may be without par value; directors must fix the lawful consideration and must not issue a share until it is fully paid in cash, property or past services. No escrow account, capital deposit or bank confirmation is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax and GST/HST, operating through the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec for Quebec provincial income tax, QST (TVQ) and other provincial levies, operating through the CLIC portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations; Corporations Canada (federal) and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec for corporate registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity first incorporates as a business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act, obtaining a Quebec NEQ (Numero d'entreprise du Quebec) through the Registraire des entreprises. The corporation then obtains a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, together with program accounts: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, and RP for payroll. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST even without a permanent establishment; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits. For QST, the corporation must file an initial registration with Revenu Quebec (via the one-stop registraire process) and obtain a separate QST (TVQ) account. The corporation must appoint a director resident in Canada (or a provincial nominee if Quebec-elected), maintain a registered office, and may be required to provide a Canadian resident agent for service. Forms and filings are administered jointly by CRA and Revenu Quebec, with the tax identity becoming effective upon issuance of the BN/NEQ.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box pass-through election; business corporations are per se taxable entities. Relevant elections and mechanisms include: GST/HST closely-related corporation election under ETA s. 156 (nil-consideration intra-group supplies); QST equivalent election for closely-related corporations under Quebec QST legislation; ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents to compute Part I tax on net rental and royalty income instead of Part XIII withholding; ITA s. 219 branch profits tax election for Canadian branches of foreign corporations; ETA s. 179 election permitting non-resident registrants to claim an immediate refund of GST/HST; and hybrid mismatch compliance via ITA ss. 18.2-18.6 including the EIFEL limitation, which permits a group-level filing approach rather than elective entity reclassification. No retroactive entity-classification election is available, and treaty-based reductions require proving treaty entitlement and filing the appropriate waiver or reduced-withholding certificate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier threshold calculated on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, but excluding financial services, supplies of goodwill and certain real property supplies) made in the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters. A one-calendar-quarter exceedance of CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies also triggers mandatory registration effective the first day of the following month. For QST, the equivalent threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Quebec over four consecutive quarters, with the same one-quarter exceedance rule. Export supplies, while zero-rated, are counted for threshold purposes, so an active bulk-cement exporter will likely exceed the threshold in its first fiscal quarter and must register from inception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bulk hydraulic portland cement and clinker supplied for export are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V as supplies of tangible personal property exported by or on behalf of the supplier, provided that: (i) the goods are shipped from Canada by ocean vessel as soon after the supply as is reasonable, with CRA generally accepting a 60-day export window; (ii) title transfers to a non-resident recipient for use or consumption outside Canada; (iii) the goods are not substantially transformed or consumed in Canada prior to export; and (iv) the supplier obtains and retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export, including the vessel departure date and foreign port of discharge. Ocean freight and related services supplied by the registrant for international carriage of those goods are separately zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part VII. QST zero-rating applies by reference to equivalent export rules under the Quebec QST Act for goods exported from Canada by the supplier. Where the goods are exported after more than 60 days or the documentary evidence is incomplete, the supply is taxable at 14.975% and the supplier must self-assess and remit the uncollected tax.

  • CBSA Form B13A Export Declaration filed via CERS/CAED with electronic cargo control number and customs receipt confirmation
  • Original or certified true copy of the ocean Bill of Lading showing the exporting shipper, outbound vessel call, port of loading in Canada, foreign port of discharge, and bulk commodity description
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the exporter's Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, GST/HST and QST registration numbers
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire transfer confirmation from the foreign buyer evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the contractual payment term
  • Draft survey report, weight certificate, certificate of quality/quantity, and independent inspection certificate for the bulk clinker cargo

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or pre-clearance API regime exists in Canada or Quebec as of 2025. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 network, with CRA and Revenu Quebec progressively participating and publishing transacting-party onboarding requirements. GST/HST and QST invoices must satisfy substantive content requirements under ETA s. 223 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act, including registrant name and BN/NEQ, invoice date, description of the supply, consideration, and either the applicable tax amount or a clear 'tax included' notation. For export shipments, the exporter must file the CBSA B13A export declaration electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the legacy CAED system, citing the cargo control number of the ocean vessel, and retain the customs receipt number for audit trail purposes.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within four years of the original assessment; the limitation period is unlimited where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation, committed fraud, or wilfully defaulted. If export zero-rating is denied for deficient documentation or unreasonable export delay, the full GST/QST becomes payable, plus compound daily prescribed-rate interest from the date the tax was required to be collected. Civil penalties include the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the unremitted tax for knowingly making false statements or gross negligence under ETA s. 285 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated income tax under ITA s. 163(2). Criminal conviction for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; however, CRA may scrutinize open export receivables exceeding normal commercial terms as a transfer-pricing indicator under ITA s. 247, and a non-arm's-length receivable outstanding beyond the reassessment window may trigger both provincial and federal currency-translation adjustments and penalty exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for intangibles (annual aggregate with non-residents).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; no specific ESR regime, but transactions must have commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit; civil penalties and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment; denial of treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if company is a sham; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply.
