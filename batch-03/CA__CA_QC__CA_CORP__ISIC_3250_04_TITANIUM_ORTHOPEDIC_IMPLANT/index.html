Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll and import/export (RM) accounts; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Québec corporate income tax, Québec Sales Tax (QST) and employer payroll. Digital portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and RQ 'ClicSÉQUR Affaires' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). A Québec-based titanium implant manufacturer must hold both a federal Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account and a QST registration with RQ.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with CRA, either online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail; non-resident applicants must appoint a Canadian representative (Form RC59) for service of notices. Step 2: Activate program accounts under the BN: corporate income tax (RC), GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) account for CBSA customs declarations. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V or ClicSÉQUR Affaires to obtain the QST registration number and Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ); non-residents must appoint a mandatary (Form MA-6-V). Step 4: File the T2 corporate income tax return (federal) and the CO-17/T2 version (Québec) within 6 months of the fiscal year-end, and begin periodic instalments under the Corporation Instalment Guide (RC159). Step 5: Register as a medical device manufacturer with Health Canada (Medical Device Establishment Licence) before first sale; the licence number should be logged in the export compliance file. Registration for GST/HST and QST is mandatory within 30 days once taxable worldwide supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a single quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal/provincial business corporation is a resident of Canada and a separate taxpayer; profits are taxed at the corporate level (combined Canadian/Québec general corporate rate ~26.5% for 2025, plus possible Québec health contribution small-business surcharge), with no pass-through of income to shareholders; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) qualifies for the federal small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, allocated across associated corporations; the QST and GST/HST apply separately on supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box system for domestic corporations; domestic corporations are always taxable entities. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST.120) for registrants with annual taxable revenues up to CAD 400,000; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related registrants to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration (deferral of GST/QST); (iii) ETA s. 156(2) election to value non-arm's-length supplies at cost; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident claimants of Canadian rental income; (v) CCPC association and business-limit allocation election on T2 Schedule 400; (vi) loss carry-over elections on amalgamation (ITA s. 87) or winding-up (ITA s. 88); (vii) hybrid-mismatch de minimis or structured-arrangement exclusions under ITA s. 18.4(3) where foreign investors hold the Canadian subsidiary and the foreign jurisdiction has CFC/controlled-foreign-company rules; (viii) QST combined-return election with RQ for multi-establishment filers; and (ix) foreign-entity analogue classification under ETA s. 123(1) and ITA s. 248(1) — a foreign-incorporated entity is treated as a corporation only if it has corporate attributes analogous to a Canadian corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST and QST 'small supplier' threshold is CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including inbound export sales) in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; registration becomes mandatory on the transaction that exceeds the threshold, and the registrant generally cannot collect GST/QST until the registration becomes effective; voluntary registration is available and advisable for exporters because it allows input tax credit recovery on domestic purchases of raw titanium, electron-beam equipment and services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of titanium orthopedic implants (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339113; HS code 9021.31) qualifies for 0% GST/HST and QST under ETA s. 21 (Schedule VI, Part V) when the registrant exports the goods in commercial quantities by or on behalf of the supplier to a non-resident buyer; the goods must be actually exported 'with no further supply of the goods in Canada' and the supplier must obtain and retain proof of export. Medical devices that meet Health Canada licensing, when sold domestically, are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part II (s. 6) of the ETA, but every export claim must be supported by customs evidence. Related services (e.g., engineering, sterilization validation, regulatory documentation) performed in Canada in respect of exported goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI s. 7, and services performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI s. 9; Revenu Québec mirrors these export zero-rating rules under the Québec Taxation Act and the QST Regulation, allowing a full QST refund or 'zero-rating' of exported goods.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or G13 EDI export reporting, quoting the exporter's BN and RM account number
  • Signed commercial invoice (with full device description, HS code 9021.31, lot/serial numbers, purchase order reference, and Incoterms 2020 delivery terms)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean carrier, airway bill, or CMR/truck waybill) evidencing physical transportation out of Canada and the foreign port of discharge
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and product-specific medical device licence, along with ISO 13485 certificate and the surgeon/implant traceability file
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank statement confirming receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident purchaser
  • CBSA export transaction record (broker's export reconciliation), freight forwarder's certificate of export, warehouse/terminal receipts, and marine/air cargo insurance certificates
  • ETA s. 169(1) / s. 3 Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations invoice file retained for 6 years, together with a written export-compliance narrative (contract, packing list, delivery confirmation from the consignee)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), digital stamping or daily XML transmission mandate exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) adopted by CRA and Revenu Québec; mandatory e-invoicing has applied only to suppliers invoicing Public Services and Procurement Canada under federal contracts awarded on or after 1 April 2024, transmitted via the federated Peppol network. For B2B export sales, the GST/HST and QST invoice must contain the supplier's BN (RT) and QST number, invoice date, vendor and purchaser names, description, taxable consideration, rate of tax per item, total taxes, and the words 'zero-rated export — Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act'; failure to meet ETA s. 169(1) and the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations (s. 3) documentation requirements will jeopardize the vendor's entitlement to input tax credits.

CRA's Export Verification Unit and Revenu Québec auditors apply heightened scrutiny to medical-device export claims. If documentation is inadequate, forged, or the proceeds are not repatriated, the zero-rating is reversed: GST/HST and QST become payable on the export selling price, interest runs from the original reporting period, input tax credits are clawed back, and reassessments are issued within the ordinary 4-year limitation period (ETA s. 298) — extended to 7 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud. Penalties include ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax evaded; ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the net GST/QST understated; ITA s. 163(2) 50% gross-negligence penalty on income-tax understatements; and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud) with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. In addition, under ITA s. 17(1), an amount receivable from a non-arm's-length non-resident purchaser outstanding for more than 180 days at the corporation's fiscal year-end triggers imputed interest income at the prescribed rate (CRA's administrative interpretation treats the 180-day day-count as the hard statutory FX repatriation deadline); retroactive interest and penalties are non-negotiable where the exporter has no contemporaneous proof of payment within that window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account, with a market-driven floating exchange rate and no prior-approval clearance for dividend, interest, royalty, branch-profit, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance from a Quebec corporation to non-resident shareholders triggers Part XIII withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act (general 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15%/5% under the Canada-US Convention) and may attract a parallel Quebec provincial levy on Quebec-sourced income; the only substantive transfer restrictions are economic sanctions and export controls administered under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Export and Import Permits Act. No central-bank authorization or balance-of-payments declaration is required for inward equity subscription or outward trade settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None under Canadian federal or Quebec law. There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no minimum retention period, and no requirement to repatriate or convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. Exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds indefinitely in USD/EUR or other foreign-currency accounts at Canadian chartered banks; the only applicable obligations are FINTRAC international electronic-fund-transfer reporting for wires of CAD 10,000 or more and CRA functional-currency reporting under section 261 of the Income Tax Act if accounts are maintained in a permitted foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-rate and payments-policy authority is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, which retains jurisdiction over foreign-exchange reserves and intervention decisions. There is no separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control bureau; prudential regulation of banks is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail; acceptable only as a secondary payment accelerator. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and non-resident electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) can handle low-value working-capital flows, virtual IBAN receipts, and small royalty-type disbursements, but they cannot take deposits, issue documentary letters of credit, access Payments Canada clearing, or provide ISDA-documented FX hedging lines without a federal bank licence. Use such EMIs strictly for sub-CAD 50,000 operational payments; all equity capital injections, high-value trade settlements, and formal L/C transactions must run through a tier-1 OSFI-regulated chartered bank.
