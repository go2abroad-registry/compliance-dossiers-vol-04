Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), GST/HST Directorate; digital portal: My Business Account (MyBA) and GST NETFILE e-filing service at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Ontario's 13% Harmonized Sales Tax is administered federally by the CRA for the Province of Ontario under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Ontario must: (1) obtain Ontario extra-provincial or domestic LP registration under the Limited Partnerships Act (if not already registered); (2) register with the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) provide the LP's legal name, Ontario LP registration number, principal place of business in Canada, mailing address, full name and residency of the general partner and any Canadian agent for service, a statement of commercial activity (online retail of furniture), estimated annual taxable supplies, and the requested program accounts; (4) the CRA assigns a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program account suffixes ('RT0001' for GST/HST, 'PD' for payroll, 'RC' for corporate income tax, 'RM' for import-export accounts). Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold, and the RT account must be quoted on all tax invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Ontario must: (1) obtain Ontario extra-provincial or domestic LP registration under the Limited Partnerships Act (if not already registered); (2) register with the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) provide the LP's legal name, Ontario LP registration number, principal place of business in Canada, mailing address, full name and residency of the general partner and any Canadian agent for service, a statement of commercial activity (online retail of furniture), estimated annual taxable supplies, and the requested program accounts; (4) the CRA assigns a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program account suffixes ('RT0001' for GST/HST, 'PD' for payroll, 'RC' for corporate income tax, 'RM' for import-export accounts). Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold, and the RT account must be quoted on all tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' election analogous to U.S. IRC 7701(f); a domestic Limited Partnership is automatically fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96. Relevant elections: (i) partners may allocate partnership income or loss in any reasonable manner under the partnership agreement subject to the anti-avoidance rule in ITA s. 103; (ii) non-resident partners may elect under ITA s. 216 to be taxed on a net basis on Canadian-source rental income (rarely relevant to furniture e-commerce); (iii) under ETA s. 272.1, a limited partnership may make specific elections with respect to deemed supplies in financial-services or investment contexts; (iv) anti-hybrid mismatch disallowance rules under ITA ss. 12(1)(x) and 18(t) apply automatically and are not elective, neutralizing double-dip outcomes where payment characterization differs between Canada and a treaty partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1); GST/HST registration is mandatory as soon as the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (including those of associated persons) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; below the threshold registration remains optional.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, provided the goods are shipped to a destination outside Canada in the course of shipment and proof of export is retained (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1). Goods sold to a non-resident recipient and shipped directly abroad by the recipient also qualify under s. 2. Services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., delivery logistics, warehousing arrangements, customer support) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where all consideration is received from the non-resident, the service is not performed in Canada for a Canadian GST/HST registrant, and no Canadian real property or tangible personal property exception applies. Incidental freight, handling, and transportation services relating to exported oversized furniture are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 3–5, preserving full input tax credit recovery.

  • B13A Customs Export Declaration filed electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for any exported goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more
  • Commercial Invoice (Canada Customs Invoice) showing vendor, consignee, Incoterms, merchandise value, HS codes, and country of origin for the furniture
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement from a Canadian port or terminal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank wire/SWIFT/foreign exchange record demonstrating receipt of foreign currency consideration from the offshore buyer
  • Carrier/Freight Forwarder Proof of Delivery (POD) or foreign customs import entry showing arrival at the destination country

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval e-invoicing, API clearance, or digital stamping regime (no equivalent of CDFI or SDI). Registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million and all monthly remitters must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST NETFILE; other registrants may file paper. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed information under ETA s. 223: the supplier's BN and RT account number, date of invoice, supplier and recipient names, description, consideration, and GST/HST charged. The CRA accepts XML/Peppol-based e-invoices on a voluntary basis under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework; however, registrants must maintain a complete electronic audit trail of invoices, export documents, and clearing records for CRA inspection.

CRA places the entire burden of proof on the registrant to substantiate export with contemporaneous documentation; failure results in deeming the supply taxable at 13% HST, with net tax reassessed, interest running from the original due date, and a four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298) which becomes unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Monetary penalties: ETA s. 285 for false statements or omissions — the greater of CAD 8,000 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; ETA s. 280 for failure to file or remit — CAD 100 plus up to 6% of the unreported net tax; ETA s. 285.1 for gross negligence — 25% of the understated net tax. Wilful evasion is prosecutable as a criminal offence under ETA Part VII (ss. 327–340), and directors, general partners, and controlling persons may be held jointly and severally liable under CRA collection rules. CRA exporter-compliance audit programs specifically target cross-border e-commerce furniture sellers for verified review of export documentation, foreign payment traceability, and chain-scope of supply evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII and maintains a free-floating CAD with no current-account or capital-account approval regimes, no prior exchange authorization for trade invoicing, and no profit-remittance license. Residual remittance frictions are tax-driven: deductible payments of interest, royalties, rent, or management fees made by the Ontario LP to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless treaty-reduced, and the non-resident payee must hold current certificate-of-residence documentation; allocations of net Canadian-source business income to non-resident limited partners are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding but remain taxable under Part I, exposing the bank to confirming both source and treaty position before executing large outbound distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or export-proceeds beding applies in Canada. The Ontario LP may lawfully hold USD and other settlement currencies in authorized Tier 1 accounts and convert at its discretion. Inbound and outbound wire transfers individually exceeding CAD 10,000 are automatically captured in FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) filed by the financial institution, and all such flows are screened against Canadian sanctions lists under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange administrative functions delegated in part to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI for prudential FX exposure) and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for cross-border transfer surveillance).

Feasibility: Medium as a secondary settlement rail, Low as the primary treasury depository.
