Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital and no escrow account requirement for a Québec société en commandite. However, each limited partner's contribution (money, property or a right of enjoyment; services are excluded) must be stated in the partnership contract and in the REQ declaration (P-44.1, s. 29); contributed capital must be turned over to and remain in the partnership assets for the benefit of creditors until dissolution (CCQ arts. 2242–2246). In practice, initial capital is deposited into a Canadian bank account in the partnership's name before the NEQ attestation is used for KYC and payment-rail on-boarding.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and income tax administration via Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topic/registering-your-business.html); Revenu Québec - Quebec QST and provincial tax administration via clicSURF and Registraire des entreprises (revenuquebec.ca/en).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Quebec limited partnership must: (1) determine if the partnership is an 'extra-provincial limited partnership' requiring Quebec extra-provincial registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises; (2) register with Registraire des entreprises via a Declaration of Registration to obtain the Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (3) register the partnership with CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online, selecting the 'Partnership' entity type and filing Form RC18 or its online equivalent, attaching the certificate of limited partnership, the partnership agreement, and a power of attorney/appointment of an agent for service; (4) activate the GST/HST account; (5) register with Revenu Québec for the QST, either simultaneously with NEQ registration or through clicSURF, and obtain the QST account number; (6) file the initial partnership information return and any TP-600/CO-17 partnership remittance forms required by Revenu Québec; (7) provide notarized corporate and partnership documentation, certified translations if not in English/French, and a statutory declaration of non-resident status for each foreign partner; and (8) maintain the NEQ, BN, and QST numbers in all invoicing and tax filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing a Quebec limited partnership must: (1) determine if the partnership is an 'extra-provincial limited partnership' requiring Quebec extra-provincial registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises; (2) register with Registraire des entreprises via a Declaration of Registration to obtain the Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (3) register the partnership with CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online, selecting the 'Partnership' entity type and filing Form RC18 or its online equivalent, attaching the certificate of limited partnership, the partnership agreement, and a power of attorney/appointment of an agent for service; (4) activate the GST/HST account; (5) register with Revenu Québec for the QST, either simultaneously with NEQ registration or through clicSURF, and obtain the QST account number; (6) file the initial partnership information return and any TP-600/CO-17 partnership remittance forms required by Revenu Québec; (7) provide notarized corporate and partnership documentation, certified translations if not in English/French, and a statutory declaration of non-resident status for each foreign partner; and (8) maintain the NEQ, BN, and QST numbers in all invoicing and tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian limited partnerships are statutorily pass-through entities; no elective check-the-box corporate classification is available. Available elections and mechanisms include: (i) partnership fiscal period selection under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act (ITA) to adopt a non-calendar taxation year; (ii) a partnership or its members may make an election for one partner or a qualifying representative to act as the GST/HST/QST registrant and self-assess on behalf of the partnership under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and the Quebec Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; (iii) where the LP has both resident and non-resident partners, a hybrid mismatch election is not elective - ITA Part VI.1 and the hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically to disallow duplicate deductions; (iv) for purposes of a relevant tax treaty, the partnership may submit a fiscally transparent entity declaration under Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention or equivalent treaty provision to align entity classification; and (v) an election to have the partnership treatment of certain jointly-owned properties under ITA subsection 96(1) where applicable. No check-the-box under US Subchapter K or Canadian equivalent exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable worldwide supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) before the GST/HST and QST registration thresholds are exceeded; for non-resident suppliers, the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada and in Quebec, respectively, unless a special rule for digital services or specified registrant applies. Once exceeded, mandatory registration for GST/HST and QST with no dollar limit for the obligation to collect on subsequent taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI and the corresponding QST rules, a supply of a newsletter-hosting/transactional email API service to a non-resident recipient (an individual or entity not resident in Canada and not registered for GST/HST/QST) is zero-rated as an exported service if the supply is not made in Canada and is not in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The service must be supplied for acceptance outside Canada, and the supplier must retain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (name, address, place of business, residency declarations, and documentary proof such as foreign tax identification number or certificate of foreign status) and evidence that the service was in fact delivered to the foreign recipient. Where the service is used partly in Canada or by a Canadian branch, only the export portion qualifies for zero-rating. If the recipient is a Canadian non-resident registered for GST/HST/QST, the supply is taxable in Canada, not zero-rated. No export physical shipment document is required for digital services; the required bundle is the equivalent documentary chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13 or electronic equivalent) if tangible goods or media containing software is physically shipped; for pure email/SaaS services, this is typically not required and a written explanation of non-applicability is expected
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document for physical exports; for digital exports, include the signed B2B service contract, the invoice from the Canadian LP to the foreign recipient, delivery/server logs, IP geolocation records, customer onboarding records, and support/usage records demonstrating provision outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment processor settlement statement proving receipt of consideration from abroad, including foreign currency conversion records, Trader Identification Number if using a Canadian bank, and the underlying remittance reference

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance or fiscal stamping system for B2B supplies. Electronic invoices are permitted if all prescribed information is visibly displayed, including supplier name, BN/GST/HST registration number, QST number, date of invoice, description, amount, and the taxes charged. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic filing of returns via GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's online portals. Voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing standard (Peppol-based) is available but not mandatory; API integration is not required for tax clearance. For email-infrastructure SaaS supplies, the presence of an e-invoice is sufficient if stored in the CRA/Revenu Québec required format and made available on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit export zero-rating claims with high scrutiny, requiring complete tracing of the supply chain and payment. Failure to substantiate the zero-rating results in immediate reassessment of the tax at 14.5%, collection of the tax plus arrear interest from the date the return was originally due, and revocation of input tax credits/refunds. Penalties for a false or fraudulent zero-rating claim include: (i) a civil penalty under section 280 of the ETA of the greater of CAD 2,500 and 10% of the overstated refund or input tax credit; (ii) additional penalties under section 285.1 for false statements; (iii) if the claim is connected to income tax or partnership reporting, a gross-negligence penalty under ITA section 163.2 equal to 50% of the understated income or undeposited tax; (iv) criminal prosecution under ETA section 239 and ITA section 239 for willful falsification, with a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; and (v) for limited partnerships, general partner and limited partners who participated in the claim may be jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST/QST liabilities to the extent of their involvement. A 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline is used as a documentary benchmark for payment receipt; Canada does not impose a positive statutory deadline but accepts evidence of foreign settlement within this period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; no exchange-control approvals are required for investment or profit remittance. Non-resident distributions from a Quebec limited partnership (including dividends and partnership allocations) are generally subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, but no FX convertibility impediment exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation applies to export proceeds earned by Canadian residents. Receipts may be retained in any convertible foreign currency; however, bookkeeping must reflect the Canadian-dollar equivalent for tax reporting under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) or Accounting Standards for Private Enterprises (ASPE).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for market conduct and anti-money-laundering supervision. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not a defined license category in Canada; any entity offering payment services or holding client funds must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and may require provincial money-transmitter licensing in Quebec under the Money-Services Businesses Act (chapter M-30.1). Offshore fintech EMIs cannot offer bank accounts in Quebec without partnership with a Schedule I Canadian bank and must remain outside the core escrow/treasury structure. Robust compliance and a local MSB representative are required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Incoming capital contributions from foreign limited partners must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's home bank to the Quebec LP's bank account. The payment reference must include 'capital contribution to CA__CA_QC__CA_LP' and the LP's Canadian tax identification number. Banks will accept standard purpose codes such as 'FDI' (Foreign Direct Investment) or, for loan contributions, '120' (Trade Finance), but FINTRAC may request proof of the underlying partnership agreement. For USD-denominated expenses, using a U.S. dollar clearing account at the Canadian bank with FEDWIRE/CHIPS links minimizes conversion fees. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec corporate rate of 26.5% (federal 15.0% + Quebec 11.5%). A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay entity-level income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocable share of partnership income at the applicable corporate or personal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid to non-residents under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA). For a limited partnership, payments of income to non-resident partners are generally not characterized as dividends; they are treated as business income earned in Canada. Where the partnership receives Canadian-source dividends and distributes them to non-resident partners, the partnership is deemed to have paid the dividend, and the 25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to the distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal T5013 must be filed no later than five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. Quebec RL-15 follows the same filing date. If the fiscal year-end does not coincide with the calendar year for individual partners, the allocation is reported in the partner's taxation year containing the partnership year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal T2 due within two months of corporate fiscal year-end; for corporations with Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status and taxable income not exceeding thresholds, six months is allowed. Quebec CO-17 is due on the same date as the federal T2 (or six months where eligible).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under ITA §247(4), a Canadian-resident partnership or its partners are required to prepare and retain transfer pricing documentation for cross-border transactions between the partnership and non-arm's-length entities when the total value of transactions exceeds $1,000,000 CAD in the fiscal year. A master file is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenues exceed $10,000,000 CAD and the value of the Canadian participants' intercompany transactions exceeds $5,000,000 CAD. These thresholds align with OECD BEPS Action 13 recommendations as adopted by the CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime such as the Cayman Islands ESR. However, the Income Tax Act includes a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245, a statutory substance-over-form doctrine, and the CRA may re-characterize transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. A Quebec limited partnership must have a real place of business, substantive employees, and substantial business activity in Quebec to avoid being classified as a pure flow-through shell, especially for purposes of the Canada-Quebec integration rules and the availability of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Federal court prosecution under ITA §239; conviction leads to fines of at least 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec taxation act imposes additional fines and up to 7 years imprisonment. Civil penalties include 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner are jointly and severally liable with the partnership for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if the corporation fails to deduct or withhold required amounts (ITA §227.1). Individual general partners may also be personally liable for partnership tax obligations if the partnership is a limited partnership but they participate in control; travel bans and asset freezes can be ordered by the Canada Revenue Agency in serious cases.
Lack of Economic Substance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and reassesses the full tax liability plus interest. A penalty of 25% of the tax benefit is imposed under ITA §245(5). Quebec has a similar penalty equal to 25% of the avoided provincial tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a GAAR-tainted plan can be assessed for penalties if they prepared or participated in the false statement. CRA may seek to pierce the veil where the LP operates as an alter ego of a single dominant partner; personal liability can extend for unpaid taxes due to the partnership under provincial partnership law.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to $250,000 for individuals and $500,000 for entities per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Quebec has its own AML framework for financial services with fines up to $1,000,000 (Québec Securities Act), and criminal proceedings can result in imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who fail to exercise due diligence to prevent an AML breach can be liable for the administrative penalty if they directed or acquiesced in the conduct. The Crown can seek restitution orders; directors may be banned from managing corporations and are subject to asset forfeiture orders.
Failure to Withhold and Remit on Distributions to Non-Residents

Statutory sanction: Under ITA §227(9), a failure to withhold results in liability for the amount not withheld, plus penalties up to 10% of the unreported amount (if withholding was attempted but not remitted) and 10% again for negligence. Interest accrues on the unpaid amount. Revenu Québec has mirroring rules for provincial withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors of the partnership (if corporate) or managing partners are personally liable for the unremitted withholding amount and penalties if the amount is not paid. The director must reimburse the tax authority even if the partnership is insolvent. Civil recovery measures include garnishment of personal bank accounts and travel bans issued under the Canada-Quebec Reciprocal Measures Act.
