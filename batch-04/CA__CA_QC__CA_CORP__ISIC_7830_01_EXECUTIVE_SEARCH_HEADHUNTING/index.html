Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/businesses.html) and Revenu Québec via the CLICK business portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca); legal entity registration is effected through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca), which issues the NEQ (Québec Enterprise Number) used as the master business identifier for all tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally or provincially and complete legal publicity before the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ and certificate of registration. 2) Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts (RC corporate income tax, RT GST/HST, RP payroll) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; a Canadian-incorporated corporation is resident for tax purposes and obtains the BN before filing its T2 return. 3) File Form LM-1-V or register online in CLICK with Revenu Québec to open the Quebec tax dossier, which integrates the QST account, Quebec corporate income tax account (CO-17), and source deduction accounts under the same BN. 4) For a foreign entity carrying on business in Quebec, first complete extra-provincial registration and obtain an attestation of registration; a foreign corporation may then obtain a BN and register for QST/GST without maintaining a physical establishment in Canada, but must appoint an agent for service. 5) Revenu Québec administers the federal GST/HST for Quebec-based businesses under the federal-provincial harmonization agreement, so the first-time registrant completes a single registration file covering both GST and QST. 6) Re-open or amend program accounts through CRA Business Registration Online and Revenu Québec CLICK whenever ownership, directors, address, or payroll status changes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian resident corporation taxed on worldwide income under ITA s. 2 with integrated federal and Quebec corporate income tax; executive search and headhunting fees are taxable business income; no territorial remittance exemption for the head office; the corporation may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation, entitling it to the small business deduction on active business income up to the business limit).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification is statutory. Available elections include: non-calendar fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(1)(b); GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156 eliminating tax on intercorporate supplies; mirror QST closely related person election under the QSTA; GST/HST quick method election under ETA s. 227 for small suppliers; QST quick method (méthode rapide) election for small businesses; ITA s. 85 rollover elections for transfers of property to the corporation; FAPI designation and s. 93(1) dividend election for hybrid-mismatch alignment with controlled foreign affiliates; and ITA s. 216 election for rental or timber income of non-residents where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies threshold over any four consecutive calendar quarters; this small-supplier threshold applies to both GST/HST and QST and includes taxable supplies of associated persons; once exceeded, the executive search corporation must register for GST/HST and QST and collect 14.975% combined tax on taxable Canadian services, with a corresponding obligation for non-resident persons carrying on taxable activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of executive search and headhunting services to a non-resident client is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes under Part V of Schedule VI, section 7(a)(i) of the Excise Tax Act when the client is not registered for GST/HST, the service is not supplied to the client while physically present in Canada, and the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; the corresponding QST zero-rating rule applies to the same non-resident supply under the mirror provisions of the QSTA. Zero-rating is also available where the non-resident recipient is registered if the service is acquired exclusively for use in a commercial activity carried on outside Canada. Domestic search placements, candidate introductions, and retainer services performed for Canadian employers or Canadian subsidiaries of foreign clients attract full GST plus QST at 14.975%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or ACI eManifest) - generally not applicable to cross-border services, but required if any ancillary physical deliverables are exported
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill - only for physical documentary or marketing materials accompanying the service
  • Inward remittance certificate and SWIFT/MT103 or Wise B2B payment trace proving receipt of the foreign retainer without domestic on-invoicing
  • Signed executive search contract or statement of work identifying the non-resident client, its foreign jurisdiction, and the fee currency
  • Invoice marked zero-rated export supply under ETA Schedule VI Part V section 7 and the QSTA non-resident service provision
  • Evidence of where the recruitment services were performed, including candidate location assessments and remote delivery records
  • Full retention of the above documentation for a minimum of six years under ITA s. 230 and ETA s. 286

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices exists in Canada. Quebec is phasing in mandatory Peppol-based e-invoicing and e-reporting: from September 1, 2025, enterprises with annual revenue above CAD 100 million must accept e-invoices, and from September 1, 2026, they must issue e-invoices through certified service providers using Peppol BIS 3.0; mandatory phases for mid-market enterprises (CAD 10 million to 100 million) and smaller businesses are scheduled to follow from 2027 to 2028. A specialized executive search agency below these thresholds may continue to issue paper or PDF invoices, but must retain transactional data to support future e-reporting and must comply with Revenu Québec record-keeping standards; GST/HST and QST registration numbers must appear on all invoices issued in Canada.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reverse zero-rating on audit where services are in fact consumed in Canada, such as where the foreign client is a conduit for a Canadian operation or where the headhunting activity places candidates with a Canadian affiliate; reassessments are retrospective to the date of the original claim and attract full GST and QST, compounded daily interest, and penalties. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but documentary evidence must exist at the time of the claim and be retained for six years; failure to repatriate or substantiate may be treated as an inaccurate claim. Gross negligence carries a 50% penalty on the tax amount under ETA s. 285(1) and the QSTA equivalent, wilful evasion carries a 100% civil penalty, the limitation period is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, and criminal prosecution is possible under ETA s. 239 and the QSTA; recovery may be enforced against corporate assets through Revenu Québec legal hypothec and collection powers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a freely floating exchange-rate regime; there are no exchange controls restricting conversion, remittance, or foreign-currency pricing. Profit remittance via dividends is unrestricted but subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at a statutory 25% rate, generally reduced by tax treaty (e.g., 5–15% under the Canada-US or Canada-France treaties) and dependent on timely T106/T1134 informational filings where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. Cross-border service revenues may be retained in any currency; however, financial institutions automatically report every transaction over CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, and Canadian-controlled corporations with foreign-currency accounts or foreign property costing over CAD 100,000 must file the T1135 form with their corporate income tax return.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC); with OSFI as banking prudential regulator and FINTRAC as the AML/CFT and cross-border wire report authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for B2B settlement via Wise Business (Wise Payments Ltd., registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC). Legal constraints: Wise is not a Schedule I/II bank, holds no CDIC deposit coverage, and multi-currency balances are pooled/non-segregated; thus Wise is suitable only as a secondary collections/disbursement rail, not as a capital-escrow or regulatory-capital depository.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Canadian federal (15% net general rate after abatement and general rate reduction) and Quebec (11.5% general corporate rate); a qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) claiming the federal and Quebec small business deductions is taxed at an effective combined rate of 14% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-residents, reduced by tax treaty to typically 5% for a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting power of the Canadian corporation and 15% for portfolio dividends; Quebec does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax, as Part XIII is administered federally.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 and CO-17 returns are due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end is due by June 30 of the following calendar year); the final balance of federal and Quebec income tax payable is due two months after fiscal year-end, extended to three months for a qualifying CCPC with an active business limit; instalment payments are generally due on the 15th day of each month where prior-year federal/Quebec tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a particular non-resident triggering mandatory Form T106 filing); for Country-by-Country Reporting the threshold is CAD 750,000,000 of consolidated group revenue; CRA may require contemporaneous Local File and Master File-style documentation and comparability analysis under Information Circular IC 87-2R for any controlled transaction, irrespective of transaction size, upon audit.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on domestic operating corporations; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits of transactions lacking economic substance, and the sham and substance-over-form doctrines permit CRA and courts to disregard artificial contractual arrangements. An executive search agency must demonstrate genuine operational substance: physical office, full-time recruiters on payroll, active solicitation contracts, Canadian client-facing invoicing, corporate records, and board decisions made in Canada. Corporate residence is determined by central management and control; a Quebec-incorporated entity managed from abroad is still tax-resident in Canada for domestic purposes, but treaty interpretation may allocate the profits to a foreign PE. For non-resident shareholders, treaty benefits require real commercial substance in the residence state and compliance with anti-conduit and LOB provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment for all years not statute-barred; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2) ITA and Quebec Taxation Act equivalents; criminal prosecution under s. 239 ITA, carrying fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years (summary) or five years (indictable); CRA may also impose penalties for failure to file T106 and failure to withhold Part XIII.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, QST, and Part XIII withholdings under ITA s. 227.1, Excise Tax Act s. 323, and Quebec Tax Administration Act; where evasion involves deliberate concealment, Quebec's Civil Code allows piercing of the corporate veil (art. 317 CCQ) for fraud and abuse of rights, exposing directors' personal assets to civil restitution and freezing orders.
Shell Company and Sham

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 re-characterizes or disallows the tax benefit; assessment of the full 26.5% federal/Quebec rate on the disallowed income; 50% gross negligence penalty; potential cancellation of corporate registration under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act for failure to file annual returns; consequential loss of treaty entitlements and possible criminal exposure for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders are personally liable for the unpaid taxes where the corporation is a 'persona ficta' facade; CRA can collect against the shareholder who receives the diverted income; courts may issue asset freezes and civil restitution orders; directors of federally registered companies may be disqualified or barred from serving as directors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under s. 247(2) ITA; penalty under s. 247(3) of 10% of the net adjustment, minimum CAD 5,000 (maximum penalty does not apply where reasonable efforts and contemporaneous documentation exist); late T106 filing penalties of CAD 300 for each month of non-filing up to 25 months? (In practice, prescribed penalties under s. 162(10.1) apply); denial of expense deduction, reallocation of income, and potential reassessment of withholding tax on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently approved the non-arm's length arrangements may be held personally liable for penalties and interest under the Quebec Tax Administration Act and for gross negligence under the Income Tax Act; where the inflated fees were routed personally to the director, the CRA may pierce the veil and attach the director's assets.
Failure to Withhold and Remit

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec collect the gross amounts unremitted plus prescribed statutory interest compounded daily; penalty for non-remittance of 10% of the deficiency when more than once in a 24-month period; criminal charges for non-remittance of source deductions under s. 227(4.1) ITA carry fines of up to CAD 50,000 and/or two years' imprisonment for a first offence; Quebec administrative fines are imposed under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the entire amount of unremitted source deductions under s. 227.1 ITA and s. 323 ETA, without the protection of limited liability; the 'due diligence' defence is narrowly construed; CRA may issue a director's assessment, register a lien, seize personal property, and enforce garnishments; travel restrictions and exit bans are possible where a director has been charged with a criminal tax offence.
