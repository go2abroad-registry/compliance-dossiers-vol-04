Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax coordination under the federal tax collection agreement

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts. Standard procedure: file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA or use the Business Registration Online (BRO) service; register for GST/HST (RT account) when taxable sales exceed the small supplier threshold; for Ontario corporate tax, complete the Ontario corporate tax registration via the CRA's provincial services; non-resident online sellers may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process. Supporting documentation includes articles of incorporation, foreign registration proof, director and shareholder particulars, and a description of the Canadian commercial activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal tax law; entity classification follows the incorporating statute and treaty residence rules. Statutory elections affecting tax identity include: the closely related corporation election under ETA s.156 (nil consideration for GST/HST between eligible related corporations); the joint venture election under ETA s.273; and the s.216(1) election for non-residents to file a Canadian return respecting rental income. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4–18.6 apply mandatorily and do not provide an elective mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property (TPP) qualifies for the 0% GST/HST rate under Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 of the Excise Tax Act when the supplier transfers ownership and exports the property as part of the supply, ordinarily within 60 days after delivery, and retains contemporaneous export evidence. Online retail supplies shipped to a foreign destination by a common carrier to a destination outside Canada are zero-rated if incidental freight records and customs export declarations are retained. Supplies delivered within Ontario are subject to HST at 13%.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1952 and maintains a free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD). There are no exchange controls, no capital-account authorization requirements, and no restrictions on payment or transfer for current international transactions. Profit remittance by dividend is unrestricted but subject to federal Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, treaty-reduced (e.g., 5% under the Canada-United States tax treaty for qualifying corporate shareholders and 10% under the Canada-China tax treaty); repatriation of original paid-up capital is normally free of Canadian withholding tax and does not require Bank of Canada approval. Ontario imposes no sub-national exchange controls, and a Canadian business corporation may lawfully hold CAD, USD, EUR, or CNY-denominated bank accounts in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and Canada imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory surrender timeline, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. Cross-border proceeds from online retail (ISIC 4791) may be retained in foreign currency, swept to a USD concentration account, or converted at the corporate treasury's discretion. The only binding compliance triggers are: (i) FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for each remittance or deposit of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting completed by the bank using underlying invoice, HS classification, and mode-of-delivery data supplied by the corporate; and (iii) the bank's sanctions screening obligations under the United Nations Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the Criminal Code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential regulation of Schedule I and Schedule II banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada); AML/CFT supervision is shared between FINTRAC and OSFI. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and no exchange-control statute; the Minister of Finance retains residual powers under the Bank Act, the Investment Canada Act, and the Special Economic Measures Act, and the Bank of Canada administers the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime for non-bank payment service providers effective for PSP registrations in 2025.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada has no bank-agency or EMI charter class, and non-bank payment service providers (Wise Payments Canada Ltd., Airwallex, Payoneer) operate as FINTRAC-registered money service businesses or as PSPs under the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime rather than as deposit-taking institutions. Such providers may lawfully offer multicurrency receiving accounts and outbound USD/CNY sweeps, but their balances are not CDIC-insured (CDIC insurance applies only to regulated bank deposits up to CAD 100,000), they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and they cannot provide the full treasury evidence required by OSFI-regulated bank due diligence. Deployed use-case: secondary sweep/flip rail only, for low-value supplier disbursements and speed-to-beneficiary payments, with the primary account, escrow, trade finance, and FX hedging relationships maintained at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred Inbound route is a subscription for newly issued common shares funded by a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 corporate credit transfer from the non-resident investor to the bank's correspondent account, with the remitter's name in field 50K and the transfer documented as 'Direct Investment - Equity Injection under BPM6 financial account'. The bank must be instructed to credit the Capital Escrow Account pending deployment. Incorporation of this new Canadian business corporation does not trigger Investment Canada Act review; review thresholds apply only to acquisitions of control of an existing Canadian business and are adjusted periodically for WTO and non-WTO investors. For intercompany debt funding, the corporate must execute a written demand promissory note, charge interest at or above the CRA prescribed rate to avoid imputed-interest rules, and keep the debt-to-equity ratio at or below 1.5:1 against non-resident shareholder debt to preserve interest deductibility under the thin-capitalization rules in ITA s. 18(4) through s. 18(6), thereby avoiding a shareholder benefit and Part XIII withholding exposure. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services) in a fiscal year; Master File required if consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. For e-commerce retail, physical presence and operational substance in Canada are required to avoid deemed permanent establishment issues.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalties and interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s.227.1 if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 10% of tax benefit (s.163.2), and potential criminal charges for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the arrangement; asset seizure and personal bankruptcy risk
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for individuals; business license revocation

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; travel bans and extradition risk
