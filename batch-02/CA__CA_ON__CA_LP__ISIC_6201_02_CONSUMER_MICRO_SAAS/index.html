Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – 'My Business Account' portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax programs administered separately (where applicable)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA by submitting Form RC1 or using the Business Registration Online service. The BN is obtained in the partnership's legal name, and the general partner acts as the representative partner. If the LP is required to collect GST/HST on Canadian taxable supplies, it must register for a GST/HST account using the same BN; CRA then issues an RZ (Registrant) account number. In Ontario, no separate provincial tax ID is required for HST, as HST is administered by CRA under the Excise Tax Act. Additional Ontario registrations may apply only if the employer health tax or other provincial levies are triggered.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident Limited Partnership must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA by submitting Form RC1 or using the Business Registration Online service. The BN is obtained in the partnership's legal name, and the general partner acts as the representative partner. If the LP is required to collect GST/HST on Canadian taxable supplies, it must register for a GST/HST account using the same BN; CRA then issues an RZ (Registrant) account number. In Ontario, no separate provincial tax ID is required for HST, as HST is administered by CRA under the Excise Tax Act. Additional Ontario registrations may apply only if the employer health tax or other provincial levies are triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. A Limited Partnership is statutorily pass-through under Income Tax Act s. 96; the partnership is not a taxable entity and each partner reports its allocable income. Hybrid mismatch timing adjustments may apply under Income Tax Act s. 18.6 for amounts paid through an entity that is fiscally transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another. GST/HST elections are available under the Excise Tax Act to treat certain supplies among the partnership and its partners as made for no consideration, and to permit the partnership to be included in a closely related group or jointly elect with a non-arm's length partner for streamlined registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (as measured over all four preceding calendar quarters); non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers must register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime once this threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) supplies of goods that are exported from Canada, provided the supplier obtains and retains satisfactory evidence of export and delivery outside Canada; (ii) supplies of services made to a non-resident person where the service is not in respect of real property located in Canada, tangible personal property ordinarily situated in Canada, or certain intangible rights that are not eligible; and (iii) supplies of intangible personal property, including SaaS subscriptions, made to a non-resident person who is not registered under the Excise Tax Act, under Schedule VI, ss. 6, 7, and 9 to the Act. A consumer Micro-SaaS subscription sold to non-Canadian end-users may qualify for the 0% rate, provided the supplier retains commercial records establishing the non-resident status of the recipient and the foreign destination of the supply.

  • Customs export declaration (B13A or ACI e-manifest / CAFTS² export report)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill showing destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice evidencing the sale and consideration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment receipt evidencing collection from the foreign buyer
  • Proof of delivery / carrier certificate confirming export
  • GST/HST Schedule VI zero-rating substantiation and non-resident evidence for the recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital seal regime is imposed in Canada. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing the supplier's name, address, BN/GST-HST registration number, date of supply, description of the supply, and tax payable. Electronic invoices are valid if the CRA's records-integrity rules are observed; CRA accepts EDI, XML, PDF, and other machine-readable formats without government pre-clearance in Ontario.

CRA may retrospectively reassess a supply as taxable, disallow input tax credits, and recover unpaid GST/HST with statutory interest from the due date. Civil penalties may be imposed under the Excise Tax Act, including up to 50% of tax evaded for gross negligence, and higher penalties for knowingly making false statements. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment. Reassessments can generally issue within four years of an assessment, with no limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, no withholding tax on dividends or interest paid to non-residents (subject to treaty reductions), and profit remittances are unrestricted. A limited partnership may freely receive and disburse foreign currency; repatriation of Canadian dollar profits to foreign partners faces no exchange control hurdles. The only practical considerations are exchange rate margins, Canadian securities registration exemptions for the issuance of partnership interests, and compliance with the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act if the LP has US partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate receipts within a specified period. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely. For a consumer micro-SaaS business, subscription receipts from foreign customers are classified as software export services and may be received in USD, EUR, or other currencies without any surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (for monetary policy and payment system oversight) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering compliance; day-to-day foreign exchange administration falls under the Minister of Finance and the Canada Border Services Agency in relation to trade data, but no standalone foreign exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) viability is Medium. Canada does not have an EMI legal category comparable to Europe's e-money directive. Offshore digital EMIs cannot acquire a banking licence in Canada and cannot solicit retail deposit business without registration as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). An offshore digital EMI may provide payment processing services to a Canadian LP through an agent or white-label agreement with a Canadian Schedule I bank, but the LP will need a domestic Canadian bank account for settlement and tax compliance. Using an unlicensed foreign EMI as the primary treasury rail is legally prohibited for deposit-taking transactions and poses a material compliance risk under FINTRAC.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership and control chain opacity for a limited partnership with foreign investors

Root cause: Beneficial ownership and control chain opacity for a limited partnership with foreign investors

Defense evidence: Execute the updated Ontario limited partnership declaration, prepare an organizational chart listing all general partners, limited partners, and ultimate beneficial owners with over 25% economic interest, provide notarized passports and proof of residential address, obtain a Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) proof of business registration, and include a detailed source-of-funds statement for all capital contributions.
Anticipated cross-border subscription revenue with high transaction velocity from low-ticket SaaS users

Root cause: Anticipated cross-border subscription revenue with high transaction velocity from low-ticket SaaS users

Defense evidence: File a written business plan describing the micro-SaaS product, target markets, and payment processors (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy), produce a contractual framework for each payment remitter, maintain a transaction volume projection consistent with a low-risk SaaS profile, and implement a screening procedure to ensure no counterparty appears on OFAC, Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, or FINTRAC sanctions lists.
Non-face-to-face due diligence concerns arising from remote director or general partner interviews

Root cause: Non-face-to-face due diligence concerns arising from remote director or general partner interviews

Defense evidence: Provide a certified true copy of the partnership agreement, a board/general partner resolution authorizing the account opening, completed FINTRAC forms for record keeping, a valid Quebec/Ontario extra-provincial registration if applicable, and a signed declaration confirming the economic purpose of the account. Where remote opening is used, the bank will require Liveness Check, geolocation, and independent source-of-funds documentation.
Capital escrow account funding from foreign jurisdictions with insufficient origin clarity

Root cause: Capital escrow account funding from foreign jurisdictions with insufficient origin clarity

Defense evidence: Accompany each inbound wire with a legally certified sender disclosure, bank statement evidencing the source of funds, a notarized capital commitment letter from the limited partner, and confirmation that the sending jurisdiction has not been designated high-risk by Financial Action Task Force (FATF). If the capital originates from a jurisdiction with weaker AML standards, the bank may require a legal opinion on the legitimacy of the funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Canadian limited partnership, inbound capital from foreign limited partners must use an international wire transfer denominated in CAD or a freely convertible currency. The routing protocol is SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian bank via its correspondent network. On the creditor side, the remitter must include the Partnership's full legal name, unincorporated registration number from the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services, and the bank account number. In the SWIFT MT103, the remitter should use a payments purpose code consistent with equity contribution. Canada has no specific customs declaration for inbound funds, but banks must record the transaction under PCMLTFA. The recommended purpose code is 'A' / 'Equity participation in a resident enterprise' where the standard OECD Balance of Payments classification is used; in SWIFT messaging, use field 50K for remitter and field 70 for remittance information stating 'LP Capital Contribution'. Banks will require the LP to certify that the funds are not derived from criminal activity and that no portion constitutes suspicious proceeds. Inbound transfers from non-treaty jurisdictions may trigger enhanced review, so prepare the partnership agreement, subscription documents, and capital account statements to confirm non-resident ownership percentage and ensure the LP does not inadvertently become a reporting issuer under Canadian securities laws.Outbound trade disbursements for a micro-SaaS LP will primarily be operating expenses, marketing payments, and distributions to limited partners. The recommended clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor payments and partner distributions requiring full remittance details; (ii) commercial letters of credit only for significant hardware, colocation, or licensing contracts where a documentary credit is required; and (iii) Canadian wire system via Payments Canada and the Real-Time Rail, with same-day processing for domestic CAD disbursements. For cross-border payments, the LP should maintain USD, EUR, and CAD accounts to avoid double conversion. Disbursements must include appropriate SWIFT purpose codes, and payments to non-resident service providers are subject to Canada Revenue Agency reporting under Part XIII withholding tax if the payment constitutes 'management or administrative fee' or 'rent/royalty', unless a treaty exemption is documented. For recurring platform fees to Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, remit via MT103 with purpose code 'Software maintenance services' and annex the corresponding invoice. For partner distributions, use MT103 with purpose code 'Dividends and distributed branch profits', and if the LP is not carrying on business in Canada, no Part I income tax is owed at the entity level; instead, withhold tax on profit shares to non-residents under the Canada-US or applicable tax treaty where the limited partner is treaty protected.Outright forward contracts on CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP transacted with a Schedule I Canadian bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for settlement in CAD against a notional foreign currency for currencies where CAD deliverability is unavailable at the counterparty's booking centerCross-currency swaps between CAD and USD to manage cashflow mismatches from recurring subscription revenues in USD and operating expenses in CADPlain-vanilla foreign currency options to hedge customer subscription refund exposure and repatriation timing riskBank-issued forward rate agreements, permissible without registration under Canadian derivatives regulation provided the LP qualifies as an end-user and the transactions are not speculative

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total value of transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under CRA guidelines)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. For treaty benefits, the CRA may deny relief if the entity lacks economic substance (e.g., no real business activity, no employees, no office).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Ontario Corporations Tax Act. Penalties: 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans (via court orders), and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits and re-characterize the entity. Penalties under GAAR: 25% of the tax benefit, plus interest. The partnership may be deemed a sham, leading to full tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be held personally liable for the tax liability of the partnership if they were the directing mind behind the sham. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals. Criminal penalties for non-compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) include fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for AMPs if they knowingly failed to implement AML compliance programs. Civil liability for damages, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful blindness.
