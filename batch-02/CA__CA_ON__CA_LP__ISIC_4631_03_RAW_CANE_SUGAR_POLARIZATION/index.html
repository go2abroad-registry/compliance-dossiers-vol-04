Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/account-information-online.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is a Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, typically by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The BN is the key identifier for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. For GST/HST purposes, the non-resident limited partnership must complete Form RC1 or RC1A (Request for a CRA Business Number – Non-Resident) and may be required to appoint a Canadian resident agent for service. If the LP is not required to register for GST/HST, it may still obtain a BN voluntarily to claim input tax credits or to export zero-rated goods. Online registration is available through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The LP must provide its partnership agreement, proof of foreign registration (if any), and details of its Canadian permanent establishment. If the LP has no Canadian residency but has a Canadian source of income, it may also need to file a partnership information return (T5013) and obtain a BN for that filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' corporate classification regime. A Limited Partnership formed in Ontario is statutorily treated as a pass-through entity for federal and provincial income tax purposes unless an election is made to be taxed as a corporation under section 142.2 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (rare). For cross-border hybrid mismatch purposes, a foreign entity may elect under paragraph 128.1(4)(b) or subsection 128.1(4) to deem certain property/liability transfers on becoming resident, but no general entity classification election exists. Non-resident limited partners may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) to file a Canadian return to report rental income on a net basis, but that is irrelevant to wholesaling. The LP may, however, choose to be part of a group of corporations for provincial tax purposes only if it is a corporation; partnerships cannot consolidate. GST/HST rules allow an election (Form RC4592) to have a 'qualifying partner' be the designated registrant for the partnership, and a 'partner representative' can be elected for reporting. A non-resident LP may also elect to register for GST/HST voluntarily under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act (Canada).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; CAD is freely tradable and unrestricted, and there is no central-bank approval or repatriation permit required for inbound capital contributions, outbound trade disbursements, or profit remittance from the Ontario LP. The relevant compliance hurdles are non-convertibility items: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at a 25% statutory rate on income-like remittances to non-resident partners, reducible under an applicable tax treaty with documentary backing; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border wire reporting for all international transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (iii) Statistics Canada international-transactions classification of the flows; (iv) ITA thin-capitalization and foreign-affiliate-dumping rules applicable to the corporate general partner and any Canadian-resident partners. None of these constitute an exchange-control barrier, and proceeds from the physical raw-sugar arbitrage may be freely remitted worldwide.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. USD receipts from the sale of VHP raw cane sugar may be retained indefinitely in the LP's USD operational account at a Schedule I bank, held in a non-resident USD account, or converted into CAD at the LP's discretion. The only binding requirements are FINTRAC recordkeeping and reporting at the CAD 10,000 cross-border threshold; there is no obligation to convert, liquidate, or bring export proceeds onshore within any prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank; prudential regulation of clearing banks is vested in OSFI; AML/ATF transaction monitoring and cross-border reporting are vested in FINTRAC; the Minister of Finance holds residual currency-management powers under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), the Currency Act, and the Retail Payment Activities Act. Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and imposes no licensing, permit, or quota system on cross-border currency movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary trade-settlement rail; MEDIUM for ancillary operational payments. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU e-money institutions, payment service providers, or crypto-FX platforms) cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, negotiate bills of exchange, or accept deposits by way of business, as those are banking powers reserved under the Bank Act (s. 545 ff.); they are therefore structurally incapable of clearing a Revolving LC. To act in or into Canada, an EMI must register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business and, for retail payment activities, obtain registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; unregistered activity carries criminal exposure and enforcement debarment. The Ontario LP may utilize an EMI only for spot USD/CAD conversion, sub-CAD 10,000 expense wires, and travel-and-settlement payments; the commercial LC, SWIFT MT103/MT202, and bulk trade disbursements must terminate at, and be cleared by, a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%) general corporate tax; the LP is fiscally transparent and the tax is assessed at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Canada Part XIII). Distributions of Canadian-source business income from the LP to non-resident partners are generally taxed as business income under Part I, not as dividends; however, any deemed dividends or excessive distributions from a registered LP may attract the 25% statutory rate, reduced to 5% under certain DTAs for eligible corporate beneficial owners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (aggregate fair market value of transactions with a non-resident related party in one fiscal year) requiring contemporaneous TP documentation, Form T106, and supporting local/master files. Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting threshold is CAD 900 million group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Ontario does not apply standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions; however, Canada's GAAR and the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI can deny treaty benefits where a limited partnership lacks commercial substance, such as no actual employees, premises, or vessel-holding functions in Ontario.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus 50% gross negligence penalty; criminal conviction under Income Tax Act sections 238-239 with fines up to 200% of the tax attempted to evade and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's federal and provincial tax debts; limited partners who take part in control forfeit limited liability; directors of a corporate general partner may be personally assessed for withheld amounts and may face travel bans and asset freezing orders pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under PPT, reassessment and GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit, administrative penalties for willful false statements, and possible annulment or revocation of active registration with the Ontario Ministry.

Director personal liability: The CRA may ignore the partnership and assess the general partner of a sham, piercing the corporate veil; directors/officers can be held personally accountable for the resulting tax debt and penalties, and are disgorged of any transferred assets.
AML / FINTRAC reporting

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for institutions; criminal convictions for laundering proceeds of crime (Criminal Code) with up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct these actions are exposed to personal fines and imprisonment; the 'corporate veil' is not a shield for criminal conduct; relevant banking relationships are terminated and assets may be restrained.
