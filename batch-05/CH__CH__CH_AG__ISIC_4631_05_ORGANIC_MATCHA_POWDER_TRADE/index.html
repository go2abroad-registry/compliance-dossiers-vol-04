Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch and https://www.zefix.ch for UID registration.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) Incorporate and register in the Swiss Commercial Register; (2) obtain the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID) automatically from the Swiss Federal Statistical Office / Federal Tax Administration; (3) if taxable turnover exceeds the VAT registration threshold or an elective registration is made, file Swiss VAT registration through the ESTV ePortal (Form 700 / VAT registration); (4) the VAT identifier (MWST-Nr / TVA-Nr) is issued by the ESTV in the format CHE-123.456.789 MWST. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered establishment must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative when applying for VAT registration and provide a signed power of attorney and proof of foreign registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss-resident stock corporations are subject to mandatory entity-level taxation at federal, cantonal, and municipal levels on worldwide income, with relief for foreign permanent establishments and real estate under double tax treaties. — For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) Incorporate and register in the Swiss Commercial Register; (2) obtain the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID) automatically from the Swiss Federal Statistical Office / Federal Tax Administration; (3) if taxable turnover exceeds the VAT registration threshold or an elective registration is made, file Swiss VAT registration through the ESTV ePortal (Form 700 / VAT registration); (4) the VAT identifier (MWST-Nr / TVA-Nr) is issued by the ESTV in the format CHE-123.456.789 MWST. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered establishment must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative when applying for VAT registration and provide a signed power of attorney and proof of foreign registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss-resident stock corporations are subject to mandatory entity-level taxation at federal, cantonal, and municipal levels on worldwide income, with relief for foreign permanent establishments and real estate under double tax treaties.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box or hybrid pass-through election system. An AG is always taxed as a corporation. Available VAT elections include formation of a VAT group for closely integrated Swiss entities, and small-business election to register voluntarily despite turnover below the statutory threshold. Tax rulings may be obtained from cantonal and federal tax authorities for advance certainty on transactional tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide gross taxable turnover for businesses established in Switzerland. Foreign suppliers performing taxable supplies in Switzerland are generally required to register for Swiss VAT without a turnover threshold if no reverse-charge exemption applies. Importation of goods into Switzerland is exclusively subject to import VAT irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination and documentary proof of export is furnished to the ESTV. For the cross-border distribution of organic matcha powder, the exporter must demonstrate the goods left Switzerland, the export order was issued by a foreign party, and the transport completed to the foreign destination. Certain services supplied to foreign recipients are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Switzerland, provided the recipient is established abroad and the service is actually used abroad.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / AES declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice describing organic matcha powder with HS/ISIC classification
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill as transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer payment proof
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the importing country)
  • Organic product certificate for the matcha consignment

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or API tax clearance regime exists in Switzerland. Electronic invoices are permitted provided they ensure authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability as required by Article 26 of the Swiss VAT Act. Practical electronic invoicing formats include ZUGFeRD, EDIFACT, XML, and Swiss QR-bill-enabled invoices; VAT returns are submitted to the ESTV through its ePortal with machine-readable interfaces.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may retroactively review export zero-rating claims, and the statutory limitation period allows reassessment for several years after the tax year. If the required export evidence is missing, false, or not produced on time, the zero-rated treatment is denied, the VAT becomes immediately payable, and default interest is charged from the date the tax was originally due. Wilful or negligent false claims may trigger administrative fines under the Swiss VAT Act, with monetary penalties up to a maximum of CHF 800,000 for severe tax evasion cases, plus criminal prosecution where warrants.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised currency regime for both current and capital account transactions under the Foreign Exchange and Currency Act; profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation by non-residents are unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests and withholding tax compliance (final withholding tax at source), with no requirement for central bank approval or quantitative limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the SNB or any authorised institution; export receipts may be held freely in CHF or foreign currency, credited to resident or non-resident accounts, and disposed of without mandatory conversion or a prescribed holding period.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for fully offshore digital EMI reliance: Switzerland recognises only FINMA-authorised fintech (banking-like) licensees for domestic payment services; an EU/UK EMI operating purely offshore cannot clear CHF payments natively or maintain a Swiss IBAN, and would require a Swiss banking partner or a licensed Swiss fintech subsidiary; legal constraints under the Financial Services Act (FinSA) and Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) effectively mandate a regulated Swiss nexus for material treasury operations.

Line 4 - Deduction of participation relief (Art. 69-71 DBG) Qualifying dividends and capital gains from a shareholding of at least 10% held for at least one year are fully exempt after a 5% deemed non-business expense adjustment. Relief remains available notwithstanding non-resident shareholding, provided the Swiss AG itself meets the participation threshold and substance conditions. Late filing: reminder fees of CHF 30-250; coercive administrative fines up to CHF 30,000; default interest of 4.0% p.a.; penalties for incomplete/misleading filing range from CHF 100 to 3x the evaded tax; criminal complaints are possible in aggravated cases.
Line 6 - Losses carried forward Deductible business losses limited to the 7-year carryforward window under Art. 211 DBG and cantonal harmonisation. Losses from foreign permanent establishments or transparent foreign entities are not deductible in the Swiss return. Late filing: reminder fees of CHF 30-250; coercive administrative fines up to CHF 30,000; default interest of 4.0% p.a.; penalties for incomplete/misleading filing range from CHF 100 to 3x the evaded tax; criminal complaints are possible in aggravated cases.
Line 8 - Taxable net profit (IFD) Taxable earnings after statutory adjustments; subject to the flat 8.5% federal direct tax without progression. Retained earnings are not attributed to non-resident shareholders; Swiss tax applies exclusively at the AG level until distribution. Late filing: reminder fees of CHF 30-250; coercive administrative fines up to CHF 30,000; default interest of 4.0% p.a.; penalties for incomplete/misleading filing range from CHF 100 to 3x the evaded tax; criminal complaints are possible in aggravated cases.
Box B - Taxable capital and reserves (cantonal tax) Paid-in share capital, disclosed legal reserves, and other open reserves; participation deductions and IP-Box carve-outs reduce the base. Cantonal capital tax and a 0.001%-0.5% rate apply irrespective of the non-resident ownership percentage. Late filing: reminder fees of CHF 30-250; coercive administrative fines up to CHF 30,000; default interest of 4.0% p.a.; penalties for incomplete/misleading filing range from CHF 100 to 3x the evaded tax; criminal complaints are possible in aggravated cases.

File Form 820 (refund) or Form 820-A (relief at source) together with the Tax Residence Certificate of the ultimate shareholder to avoid double taxation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss TP documentation is required when cross-border intercompany transactions exceed CHF 10 million per annum; a Master File/Local File obligation is triggered for MNE groups with consolidated turnover exceeding CHF 100 million; even below these thresholds, a simplified TP study is mandatory for intra-group management services.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as in zero-tax jurisdictions; nonetheless, the FTA enforces substance requirements through (i) participation relief requiring a 10% participation held at least one year, (ii) intra-group management services taxed at a minimum margin of 8%-10% on costs under FTA Circular No. 4, and (iii) Country-by-Country Reporting for groups with consolidated income above CHF 900 million. Insufficient local substance triggers denial of treaty and participation benefits and reclassification of retained profits as hidden distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Criminal Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Federal direct tax penalty of up to 3x the evaded tax and a criminal fine of up to CHF 30,000 (Art. 175 and 186 DBG); VAT evasion penalties of up to 5x the evaded amount and a maximum CHF 2,000,000 fine (Art. 103 VAT Act); imprisonment up to 2 years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for damages under Art. 754 Code of Obligations; the FTA may pierce the corporate veil where capital is demonstrably inadequate; administrative travel bans and exit restrictions may be imposed on directors as enforcement measures; personal imprisonment possible for criminal evasion.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of participation relief and treaty refunds; retroactive 35% Swiss withholding tax on hidden profit distributions; prohibition of favourable tax treatment for a period of up to 5 fiscal years; mandatory capital tax recapture; potential administrative liquidation of the registered office.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for uncollected withholding tax and penalties (Art. 12 DBG); Swiss courts may declare the director personally responsible for restitution of treaty benefits wrongfully claimed; travel bans and seizure of personal assets in insolvency proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Upward tax adjustment of taxable profit; penalty of up to 3x the under-declared tax; default interest of 4% p.a. on the additional assessment; application of the hidden dividend doctrine leading to 35% WHT exposure on transfer pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign non-compliant returns or implement artificial pricing structures face civil liability for restoration of corporate assets under Art. 678 OR, joint liability under Art. 754 OR, and criminal sanctions in case of fraudulent intent.
AML and International Trade Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty for aggravated money laundering under Art. 305bis/305ter Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 5 years and a corporate fine up to CHF 5,000,000; confiscation of assets under Art. 70 SCC; fines under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) up to CHF 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors implicated in laundering operations face imprisonment and mandatory exclusion from equivalent business for 10 years; personal monetary restitution is ordered pursuant to Art. 58a SCC; international arrest warrants and Swiss asset freezes are coordinated through mutual legal assistance channels.
