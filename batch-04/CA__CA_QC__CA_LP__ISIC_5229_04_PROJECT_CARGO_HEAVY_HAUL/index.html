Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) for federal GST/HST and federal income tax; Revenu Québec (RQ) for QST and Quebec provincial income tax; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs/export declarations. Federal portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial portal: Revenu Québec ClicSÉUR Affaires (revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/clicseur-affaires).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Quebec limited partnership (LP) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) and a Quebec establishment number; this is the provincial master identification for all subsequent Quebec tax accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96 and Income Tax Folio S4-F1-C1): income, losses and gains are computed at the partnership level but taxed in the hands of partners according to their proportionate shares. The limited partner enjoys limited liability; the LP itself is not a Canadian taxpayer for income tax purposes. However, for GST/HST and QST purposes the LP is a distinct 'person' and is separately registrable/adjudicated as a registrant supplier. For treaty purposes, Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(7) (fiscally transparent entities) governs partner-level benefits where a US or other non-resident partner is involved.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' election regime; entity classification is determined by common law, CRA administrative guidance (Income Tax Folio S4-F1-C1) and treaty provisions. Elections commonly available to a Quebec LP include: (i) ITA s. 97(2) election for tax-deferred transfer of partnership property to a newly formed Canadian corporation (useful for EPC asset/equipment migration); (ii) ITA s. 85(1) corporate rollover elections on transfer of business assets; (iii) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-resident partners to file a Canadian return on Canadian-source rental income and s. 217 election regarding pension/employment-type income; (iv) ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 hybrid mismatch elections to neutralize deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for cross-border interest/royalty flows; (v) Pillar Two global minimum tax elections under the OECD/G20 GloBE rules implemented by Canada for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2024 (inclusive of qualified domestic top-up tax payment elections); and (vi) GST/HST and QST elections permitting a separately reporting branch/division under ETA ss. 271/273 and Quebec equivalents. For non-resident subcontractors, a payer must also consider Regulation 105 withholding (15% on fees for services rendered in Canada) and treaty-based waiver applications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — small-supplier threshold common to GST/HST (Excise Tax Act s. 148) and QST (Quebec Taxation Act); registration is compulsory for an entity, including a non-resident-owned Quebec LP, where taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed C$30,000. Registration is also mandatory without regard to the threshold for non-residents supplying specified digital services or supplies relating to Canadian real property (e.g., warehousing/terminal heavy-haul staging services situated in Quebec), and for any person electing to register voluntarily to claim input tax credits. The threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies for small-supplier qualification, but Canadian-source supplies determine the registration trigger.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST and QST registrant supplying 'heavy-haul out-of-gauge project cargo logistics' may zero-rate: (i) supplies of tangible personal property that is exported from Canada in accordance with ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 (export of goods by a registrant, excluding goods consumed or altered in Canada); (ii) transportation services to a place outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part VII, section 1) — including cross-border heavy-haul trucking/ocean freight of OOG project cargo from Canadian origin; (iii) services performed on exported goods that are delivered to non-residents and used exclusively outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, sections 2–4), e.g., rigging/load-securing engineering for export cargo; and (iv) supplies of goods/services to a non-resident for use otherwise than in Canada where export documentation is complete. The Quebec QST zero-rating provisions mirror the GST rules (Title II, Chapter 3, Division IV of the QST Act). Eligibility requires that the supplier retains records proving the goods were delivered to a carrier for direct export, exported promptly, and not returned to Canada; the same documentation supports the exporter's input tax credit (ITC) and refund position.

  • CBSA export evidence: completed Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) filing or equivalent AESDirect/CCI record for controlled or regulated OOG cargo, including the export permit where required
  • Transport document: full-set original or ISO-certified copy of the bill of lading, CMR waybill or intermodal rail/ocean waybill evidencing movement from the Canadian Quebec staging point to a foreign destination, with the foreign destination named and no intermediate Canadian discharge
  • Commercial substantiation: signed commercial invoice, packing list, transport contract/freight booking confirmation, and inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice demonstrating repatriation of foreign-currency sale proceeds into the LP's Canadian financial institution account

None — Canada operates no real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal-invoice stamping, or mandatory CTRX/API e-invoice validation regime comparable to Mexico/Italy/Brazil. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically where the registrant exceeds prescribed revenue thresholds (mandatory e-filing has been phased in for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1.5 million; Revenu Québec similarly requires electronic QST filing and remittance via ClicSÉUR Affaires for most registrants). Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 electronic invoicing is available on a voluntary basis under the national e-invoicing service; CBSA provides API-based eManifest and ACE export/import filing channels for customs data but these are not fiscal-clearing controls. Digital stamping and real-time tax authority clearance do not apply in Quebec or federally.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims retroactively (generally 4 years; extended to 8 years where gross negligence exists; unlimited in cases of fraud). Where export documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or the cargo never physically departed Canada, CRA will re-characterize the supply as taxable at 5% GST (and RQ at 9.975% QST), deny input tax credits, and recover the tax with arrears interest at prescribed rates; the gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 (25% of the net tax amount) and parallel QST penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act apply for false statements or omissions. Criminal exposure arises under ETA ss. 327/329 and the Criminal Code for wilful evasion or fraud in export refund schemes. Although Canada imposes no hard statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day window is the CRA's evidentiary benchmark for matching export invoices to inward remittances; unrepatriated proceeds beyond 180 days trigger heightened audit scrutiny, forensic tracing, and potential denial of zero-rating unless a documented credit-period/trade-finance rationale (e.g., standby letter of credit) is provided. Records supporting zero-rated exports must be retained for at least six years from the end of the fiscal year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, profits, or loan repayments for non-resident partners. However, non-resident limited partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on certain Canadian-source payments (e.g., distributions characterized as interest, rents, or management fees), unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. No prior regulatory approval is required for capital inflows or outflows; reporting obligations exist under the Census and Statistics Act for large transactions and under the Income Tax Act for certain foreign investments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds surrender. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign accounts, subject to general record-keeping rules under the Income Tax Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For project cargo logistics, standard practice is to invoice in the contract currency and settle via normal banking channels without any requirement to convert receipts into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); also supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential matters and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF compliance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited. Foreign-incorporated electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating as unregulated entities in Canada must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC if they engage in money transfers or payment services. Even when registered, EMIs generally lack direct access to the Canadian clearing and settlement infrastructure (e.g., the Large Value Transfer System, ACSS). Consequently, the reliance on an offshore EMI for project cargo logistics settlements is not recommended as a primary treasury rail. For auxiliary payments (e.g., disbursing minor invoice payments), a compliant MSB/EMI registered with FINTRAC may be viable only if it maintains a Canadian presence or a correspondent banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital contributions from foreign limited partners into the Quebec LP, the appropriate clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfer (or Canadian LVT via CLS for CAD). The recommended purpose code for cross-border inflow is S010 (Investment in Equity Capital) under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) or, if in USD/CAD, F101 (Investment). The bank remittance instruction must include: (a) the partnership's full legal name as recorded at REQ; (b) account number; (c) a compliance reference line identifying the limited partner's identity and the capital call notice number; (d) the ISO 20022 message source with the proper purpose code; and (e) for funding of the Capital Escrow Account, an escrow disbursement schedule signed by the general partner. The bank will require a copy of the capital call letter and a certificate confirming the LP has satisfied its subscription obligations.Preferred clearing rails for trade settlements are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cash wire payments if counterparties accept direct bank transfers, with a reference to the underlying invoice and beneficiary bank BIC; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) issued through a Canadian chartered bank (e.g., RBC, BMO) for high-value milestone payments, particularly for transportation of OOG cargo where the seller's bank requires a documentary presentation; (3) Contractual milestones can be structured under a standby L/C performance guarantee or advance payment guarantee, using the ICC UCP 600 and URDG 758 rules. For CAD-denominated settlements, the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and Real-Time Rail (RTR) are used for domestic clearing. For foreign-currency disbursements (USD, EUR, GBP), banks route via SWIFT and use correspondent banking networks; the LP may also leverage a Canadian dollar prepayment account to avoid conversion spreads.Vanilla FX forward contracts (CAD/USD or CAD/EUR) permitted through Canadian Schedule I banks, non-deliverable, with ISDA or bank master agreement, subject to IFRS hedge accounting if documented as a cash flow hedge.Currency swaps (cross-currency interest rate swaps) for multi-year project cash flow matching, available via CSA clearing obligations only if above the five-year benchmark thresholds; otherwise bilaterally under ISDA credit support annex.FX options (put/call on CAD) for protecting against adverse currency movements in project bids; OTC options permitted with Canadian dealers, embedded in the Bank of Canada's market guidelines.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for CAD as the currency is fully convertible and NDF markets are informal; instead, banks quote deliverable forwards with outright settlement.Foreign exchange swaps (swap-forward combinations) for managing temporary cash positions, subject to the bank's counterparty credit limits and the LP's treasury risk policy.

Foreign-incorporated electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating as unregulated entities in Canada must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC if they engage in money transfers or payment services. Even when registered, EMIs generally lack direct access to the Canadian clearing and settlement infrastructure (e.g., the Large Value Transfer System, ACSS). Consequently, the reliance on an offshore EMI for project cargo logistics settlements is not recommended as a primary treasury rail. For auxiliary payments (e.g., disbursing minor invoice payments), a compliant MSB/EMI registered with FINTRAC may be viable only if it maintains a Canadian presence or a correspondent banking relationship.
