Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA), CQLR c. Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the QBCA. Shares may be issued with a low par value or no par value; any subscribed-but-unpaid share consideration becomes a receivable of the corporation. A private business corporation has no escrow account requirement; escrow deposit rules apply only to specific immigration-investment or public-listing structures.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal occupancy permit and local fire safety certificate for the registered office and any transloading yard; CNESST registration and compliance with the Quebec Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains near-total convertibility for both current and capital account items. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) establishes the Canadian dollar as legal tender, but contractual settlement in any freely convertible foreign currency is permitted. No exchange-control restrictions impede profit remittance, dividends, loan repayments, or capital repatriation for a Quebec business corporation, subject to solvency tests under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1) and any applicable non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest (typically 15-25% reduced under tax treaties). The project cargo entity may freely invoice and receive in CAD, USD, EUR, or other currencies. Inbound equity and outbound trade payments do not require Bank of Canada approval. GST/HST and Revenu Québec registration are separate tax obligations, not FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has not imposed foreign exchange surrender or repatriation requirements for export proceeds since 1951. An out-of-gauge heavy-haul logistics company is not required to repatriate foreign-currency earnings within any statutory timeline, nor convert any percentage into Canadian dollars. Export invoices must be retained for tax, statistical, and customs purposes under the Customs Act and Census and Statistics Act, but there is no central-bank conversion mandate. All settlement may remain in the currency of the underlying contract.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is exercised jointly by the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada under Canada's flexible exchange-rate regime, with FINTRAC administering AML/ATF obligations. There is no dedicated FX control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (SC 2000, c 17) and FINTRAC regulations, any offshore digital EMI carrying on business in Canada must register as a money services business (MSB) in Canada. An unregistered foreign EMI cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Canadian-bound project account. Offshore EMI accounts are not deposit accounts with chartered-bank protections and cannot issue MasterCard/Visa settlement reliably for high-value milestones. They may be used only for ancillary low-value collections if the EMI is FINTRAC-registered and prepared to accept the funding-wire documentation. The primary treasury, capital injection, LC, and clearing needs must be handled through a Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank. A treasury policy should therefore designate the EMI as a wallet-type sub-ledger, never as the bank clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV from the shareholder's bank to the Canadian bank's clearing account. The wire instruction must include the beneficiary account number (CIS), the ISO payment-purpose code for direct investment (e.g., 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' or 'Equity Injection'), a narrative stating 'Share capital injection for Quebec project cargo corporation', and full remitter identification. For amounts above CAD 10,000, Canadian banks will verify the source of funds and may request a closing balance confirmation from the remitting bank. The funds will post to the operational account only after FINTRAC sanctions screening and against the corporate subscription ledger. If the injection is structured as shareholder debt, the instruction should include a loan agreement and transfer price documentation to avoid tax recharacterisation. Dividends, management fees, and royalty disbursements are non-controllable, but withholdings under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act must be remitted by the entity at source.Trade settlements for heavy-haul equipment and project-cargo subcontractor milestones should be executed through the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier and subcontractor payments, including the beneficiary's full account details and the trade-invoice reference; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for purchase of oversized equipment or milestone-based EPC disbursements; (3) Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) using SWIFT MT400/MT422 for progressive payment terms; (4) CAD settlement in excess of CAD 50,000 is cleared through Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) operated by Payments Canada using the bank's clearing code and settlement account; (5) USD settlement is cleared via the correspondent bank network, CHIPS, or Fedwire, depending on the downstream bank. Each outbound wire must be referenced to the underlying commercial invoice and the Canadian bank's AML model must match the payee against OSFI/DPR sanctions lists.
