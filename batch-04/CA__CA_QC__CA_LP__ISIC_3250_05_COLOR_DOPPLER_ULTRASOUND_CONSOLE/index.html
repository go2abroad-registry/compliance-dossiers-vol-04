Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec (Revenue Quebec) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax, and payroll-related levies. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec CLIC online services (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the Limited Partnership in Quebec by filing a declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Numéro d'entreprise du Québec (NEQ), a prerequisite for QST registration. Step 2 — Obtain the federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); non-resident applicants may use CRA's International Tax Services Office (+1-800-959-5525; outside North America +1-613-940-9247). Step 3 — Activate the GST/HST program account (BN-RT0001) for the partnership's commercial activity. Step 4 — Register separately for QST with Revenu Québec by filing Form LM-1-V or via CLIC using the NEQ and CRA registration data. Step 5 — Complete registration within 30 days after the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; non-resident partners must designate a Canadian representative (Form RC59) and may be required to post security under Excise Tax Act s. 240(2). Supporting due diligence: certified partnership agreement, foreign registration certificate, and notarized powers of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Quebec LP is fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax: the LP files an information-only T5013 (and Quebec equivalent) and does not pay income tax at the entity level; each partner, including non-resident partners, is liable on the allocated share of Canadian-source business income. For GST/HST and QST, the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act treat the partnership as a separate person, so the LP must maintain its own GST/HST and QST registrations and remit net tax in the partnership's name. A non-resident partner's Canadian tax exposure is limited to income attributable to the LP's Quebec carrying-on-business, subject to treaty sourcing rules; the LP remains an entity-level registrant for indirect taxes and a withholding vehicle for payments to non-resident partners.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; pass-through is the statutory default for eligible LPs. Relevant elections: (i) a joint election under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Convention allows the LP and its US-resident partners to treat the LP as a corporation solely for treaty benefits (filing accompanies the T5013 or the partners' returns); (ii) Income Tax Act s. 96(3) joint election to be recognized as a Canadian partnership when all partners are Canadian residents, enabling tax-deferred transfers; (iii) for GST/QST, the quick method of accounting (Form GST74) and quarterly or annual filing elections are available to qualifying registrants; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 apply de jure and are not elective, denying deductions on hybrid instruments and disregarded payments between Canadian and foreign related entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold is reached when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters, or in the current calendar quarter where the person has no prior business. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory within 30 days (ETA s. 240); the parallel QST registration threshold before Revenu Québec is also CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies. Registrants making exclusively zero-rated exports must nevertheless register to recover input tax credits on export-related purchases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 1 provided the registrant supplier, as part of the supply, ships the goods to a destination outside Canada: goods must be delivered to a common carrier for export or exported by the supplier within the statutory time window (generally 60 days after the supply, unless the Minister allows a longer period), and the exporter must retain evidence meeting the Exporters of Records Regulations (SOR/99-164). Supplies of services to non-residents are zero-rated under Part V, item 23 unless the service relates to real or tangible personal property in Canada or is a personal service; installation and training performed in Canada on the ultrasound console are taxable and should be separately invoiced. The reciprocal QST zero-rating under the Quebec Taxation Act mirrors these rules. The recipient must be a non-resident and the goods must not be acquired for consumption or use in Canada; a B13A export declaration processed through the CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System substantiates the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR); mandatory for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000, with a low-value shipment exemption below that amount)
  • Commercial Invoice (full description, HS tariff classification 9018.12 or 9018.13 for ultrasound devices, unit value, currency, terms of sale, destination, and consignee identity)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) naming the Canadian exporter and the foreign consignee, evidencing outbound movement
  • ACI eManifest confirmation (Advance Commercial Information transmitted by the carrier to the CBSA before departure)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or TT credit advice evidencing that export proceeds have been or will be repatriated within the 180-day compliance window)
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence and Export Certificate under the Medical Devices Regulations (SOR/98-282), where applicable for the destination market
  • Packing list, calculation-of-origin statement, and certificate of origin (e.g., CETA, CPTPP, USMCA) where an FTA preference is claimed

Canada and Quebec do not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a mandatory B2B e-invoicing API. GST/HST remittances are filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE (or Form GST34); Revenu Québec requires electronic transmission of QST remittances for registrants above its filing thresholds. Voluntary e-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard, which is mandatory for suppliers billing the federal government through PSPC where the contract requires e-invoicing. Invoices must be retained for six years in a readable format and must display the prescribed text 'GST/HST included at 5%' and 'QST included at 9.975%' where applicable; no government pre-approval or stamping regime exists.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the four-year normal reassessment period for GST/QST (three or four years for income tax depending on partner status), with no time limit where misrepresentation or fraud is shown (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). Denial of export zero-rating — due to late exportation, defective bills of lading, shell foreign consignees, or unsubstantiated inward remittances — triggers assessment of the full GST (5%) and QST (9.975%) on the invoice value plus prescribed compound interest. Penalties include: ETA s. 285 gross-negligence penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax avoided); ETA s. 280 failure-to-register penalty; late-filing and late-remittance penalties (1% of the balance plus 0.25% per month, maximum 12 months); CBSA AMPS monetary penalties up to CAD 25,000 per export misdeclaration; and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. CRA export-task-force audits of medical-device exporters routinely require the full documentation bundle to be produced within 30 days of request.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada applies full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls requiring central bank approval, no CAD repatriation limits, and no restriction on paying dividends, partnership distributions, or export proceeds to non-residents, provided (i) the partnership is in good standing under provincial and federal laws, (ii) withholding requirements under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) or Part I (Quebec) are addressed, and (iii) payments are not blocked by sanctions or U.S. dollar clearing-house restrictions. The medium- to long-term funding position of the LP should nevertheless be aligned with transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio applies under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Exporters and manufacturers may retain invoices in foreign currency and operate foreign-currency bank accounts. All exports of medical devices must nevertheless be reported for customs/statistics under the Customs Act and the Export and Import Permits Act; the exporter should declare the transaction to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) if required. Where proceeds are received, FINTRAC large-cash transaction reporting thresholds apply to receipts of CAD 10,000 or more and to cross-border wire transfers, but no repatriation deadline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Department of Finance administer related oversight, but Canada maintains no specialized foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Canada does not regulate EMIs as a separate category; non-bank fintechs require money-services business (MSB) or foreign-money-services business (FMSB) registration with FINTRAC when carrying out any transfer/payment activity on behalf of the LP. Offshore digital EMIs are generally not acceptable conduits for the structural capital escrow or for regulatory-required commercial settlements; their viability is Medium as a secondary collection/payment initiation channel only where the EMI is FINTRAC-registered or holds a Quebec/Canadian money-services license, and Low where it relies solely on a foreign e-money authorization without a Canadian nexus.

Incoming equity/debt contributions should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for CAD clearing, using the bank's correspondent arrangement through Lynx (Canada's high-value payment system). Recommended wire instructions include the LP's Canadian bank account, full legal name, Quebec enterprise number, and purpose narrative 'Capital contribution to limited partnership' or 'Foreign direct investment - equity participation'. The remitter must ensure originator information conforms to FINTRAC wire-transfer rules; the bank will verify source of funds and may request the partnership agreement and capital commitment schedule. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC large-cash transaction reporting and electronic funds transfer reports may apply.For trade settlements, preferred rails are (i) direct SWIFT MT103 for T/T payments in USD/CAD/EUR, and (ii) commercial letters of credit issued or applicable under UCP600 for the sale of color Doppler ultrasound consoles. The bank will clear CAD domestically through Lynx and cross-border in USD through its U.S. correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire). Documentary compliance must include commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, and medical device export compliance evidence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Canada does not regulate EMIs as a separate category; non-bank fintechs require money-services business (MSB) or foreign-money-services business (FMSB) registration with FINTRAC when carrying out any transfer/payment activity on behalf of the LP. Offshore digital EMIs are generally not acceptable conduits for the structural capital escrow or for regulatory-required commercial settlements; their viability is Medium as a secondary collection/payment initiation channel only where the EMI is FINTRAC-registered or holds a Quebec/Canadian money-services license, and Low where it relies solely on a foreign e-money authorization without a Canadian nexus.
