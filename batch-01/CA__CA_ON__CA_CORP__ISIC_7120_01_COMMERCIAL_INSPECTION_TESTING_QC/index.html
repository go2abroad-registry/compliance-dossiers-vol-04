Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) — national tax administration, Business Registration Online (BRO), and the 'My Business Account' portal. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal–Provincial Tax Collection Agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Finance operates the Ontario Business Registry for provincial matters, including the Employer Health Tax (EHT) account. GST/HST is administered federally by the CRA, with the 8% Ontario component of the 13% HST remitted to Ontario under the Harmonized Sales Tax Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or the Ontario Business Corporations Act. Step 2: Obtain the statutory nine-digit Business Number (BN) and tax identifier via CRA Business Registration Online or paper Form RC1. Step 3: Activate the GST/HST account (RT program) upon exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or electively to recover input tax credits; activate the corporate income tax account (RC program) for T2 filings. Step 4: Register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for the Employer Health Tax where Ontario payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Step 5: Non-resident shareholders require no separate registration unless the non-resident itself carries on business in Canada, in which case CRA's Non-Resident Program (Form RC476) applies. Registration is fee-free, no minimum capital is required, and the BN/TIN is issued immediately upon application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a corporation incorporated in Canada (federal or Ontario) is resident in Canada under ITA s.250(4) and taxed at Part I rates on worldwide income; no pass-through election exists. Combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate is approximately 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%; Ontario manufacturing/processing 10.0%). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) qualifies for the small business deduction (federal 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, plus Ontario 3.2% small business rate). The corporation is outside any tax-exempt zone and enjoys no territorial tax limitation on Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (1) eligible dividend designation election (ITA s.89(11)); (2) functional currency election to compute Canadian tax in a foreign currency such as USD (ITA s.261); (3) ETA s.156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as non-taxable; (4) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for small registrants; (5) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA Part 6.1, ss.18.2/18.3, effective 1 July 2022) are mandatory and cannot be elected out, although statutory carve-outs exist for arm's-length and derivative transactions; (6) treaty-based Part XIII withholding relief elections for cross-border dividends, interest, and royalties paid by the Ontario corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no exchange controls on current-account transactions, capital movements, dividend distributions, or profit repatriation. Inward equity injections and outward trade payments are unrestricted, subject to standard CDD, FINTRAC reporting thresholds, tax filing obligations, and Part XIII withholding tax on qualifying cross-border remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receivables in correspondent or multi-currency accounts. FINTRAC money-service-business and large-transaction reporting obligations apply at CAD 10,000 or more for cash or certain remittance flows. Foreign-currency holdings must be translated and reported under the Income Tax Act for Canadian tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the national monetary and financial stability authority; exchange-control and AML/ATF functions are delegated to OSFI and FINTRAC respectively, with no separate foreign-exchange control administration in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions have no automatic passport into Canada. A foreign EMI must either hold a Canadian money-services-business licence under FINTRAC and, for Ontario, comply with the Ontario Money Services Business Act, or operate solely through a federally regulated Schedule I bank sponsor. For CAD clearing, LVTS access is restricted to regulated institutions, so an offshore EMI alone is insufficient for high-value treasury flows.

False reporting or omissions can trigger separate civil penalties of 50% of the federal tax understatement and criminal indictment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of all reportable cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; OECD-aligned local-file and master-file documentation must be contemporaneously prepared for transactions over this threshold. Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches CAD 800,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act will demolish transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose or real economic substance. CRA also invokes the common law sham doctrine and may recharacterize artificial corporate arrangements, impose a 25% GAAR penalty, and apply anti-thin-capitalization or foreign affiliate rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal indictment under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty equal to 50% of the tax understatement; CRA may reassess beyond the normal limitation period where fraud or misrepresentation is established.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where a director has participated in fraud; directors are jointly assessed for unremitted source deductions under section 227.1; travel bans and imprisonment are possible following criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 10% transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) where the net price adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000 and required records are not produced within 3 months; reallocation of income and disallowance of deductions; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding taxes arising from mischaracterized cross-border invoicing; personal monetary exposure arises only where participation in a fraudulent scheme is proven.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the corporate entity or individual contracts under the sham doctrine and GAAR, reassess income in the hands of the economic owner, levy the 25% GAAR penalty, and, if intentional tax evasion is proven, pursue criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners cannot shelter behind the corporate veil when the corporation is a fraudulent facade; directors risk civil restitution, personal tax assessments, and criminal exposure for false certifications.
Withholding Tax Violations on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The payer is assessable for the full amount of the unwithheld tax plus interest; penalties up to 20% of the amount not withheld may apply; criminal exposure exists for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes regardless of corporate dissolution, and CRA may collect through garnishment, asset seizure, or travel-control measures in serious cases.
AML/CTF and FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for record-keeping and reporting failures; facilitation of sanctions evasion can lead to separate federal prosecution and asset freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly receive or circulate proceeds of crime or direct suspicious transactions face personal criminal liability, extradition, and travel restrictions in connection with serious money-laundering offenses.
