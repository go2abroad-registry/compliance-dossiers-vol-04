Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the registered-office canton, acting with a cantonally recognized notary public for the public deed; supervised by the Federal Office for the Commercial Register (FEDRO).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft), as revised effective 1 January 2023; Swiss Civil Code Art. 52 (legal personality); Federal Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV, SR 221.411); Federal Act on Labour in Industry, Commerce and Trade (Arbeitsgesetz, ArG), Art. 7.

Public notarial deed of incorporation (öffentliche Beurkundung des Errichtungsakts) with the Articles of Association (Statuten), submitted through the Commercial Register application form (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) and published in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SHAB).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The AG requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (Art. 632 OR). At incorporation, at least CHF 20,000 must be paid up in cash and deposited on a blocked share-capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank; the bank's confirmation that the funds are at the free disposal of the Board is a mandatory filing condition. The unpaid balance remains callable by the Board of Directors and must be fully paid in the event of insolvency.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV / AFC), VAT Division, Schwarztorstrasse 50, CH-3003 Bern; centralized Swiss VAT registration and compliance portal at https://estv.admin.ch (SuisseTax eServices). Switzerland applies a unitary national VAT system; cantonal and communal authorities administer direct profit taxes only and have no VAT competence.

E-government filing portal

For the resident CH_AG: after commercial register incorporation, the FTA issues the UID business identification number (format CHE-116.345.890), which is converted ex officio into a VAT number by adding the suffix 'MWST' once the AG notifies the start of taxable activity (Form 1425 via FTA e-services) and meets the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold or opts for voluntary registration. VAT registration is effective from commencement of the taxable activity; the MWST ID is typically issued within 2 to 4 weeks. Non-Swiss entities without a Swiss establishment appoint a Swiss tax representative (jointly and severally liable, Art. 67 para 2 MWSTG) and apply through the FTA portal; foreign suppliers of electronic B2C services may instead use the simplified national notification procedure applicable since 1 January 2025.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - The Aktiengesellschaft (AG / SA, Swiss stock company) is a resident capital company subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide profits and capital: federal profit tax at 8.5% on after-tax profit, plus cantonal/communal profit tax (effective aggregate approximately 12% to 24% depending on canton). No pass-through or fiscal transparency applies. Territorial scope: profits attributable to foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt at the cantonal/communal level with progression and relieved under double taxation treaties at the federal level; for VAT, the AG is a Swiss taxable person with an establishment for all goods and services supplied in Switzerland. — For the resident CH_AG: after commercial register incorporation, the FTA issues the UID business identification number (format CHE-116.345.890), which is converted ex officio into a VAT number by adding the suffix 'MWST' once the AG notifies the start of taxable activity (Form 1425 via FTA e-services) and meets the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold or opts for voluntary registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Aktiengesellschaft (AG / SA, Swiss stock company) is a resident capital company subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide profits and capital: federal profit tax at 8.5% on after-tax profit, plus cantonal/communal profit tax (effective aggregate approximately 12% to 24% depending on canton). No pass-through or fiscal transparency applies. Territorial scope: profits attributable to foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt at the cantonal/communal level with progression and relieved under double taxation treaties at the federal level; for VAT, the AG is a Swiss taxable person with an establishment for all goods and services supplied in Switzerland.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, partnership classification or S-corp election exists in Swiss law. Relevant elections for a custom-manufacturing AG: (1) VAT group taxation for closely linked enterprises (Art. 52 MWSTG, binding for at least five tax periods) to consolidate the keycap production entity with sister sales companies; (2) opt-in to VAT liability below the CHF 100,000 threshold (Art. 11 para 4 MWSTG); (3) option to subject real estate letting to VAT (Art. 22 para 2 MWSTG); (4) OECD hybrid mismatch deduction-limitation rules (in force since 1 January 2020) disallow deductions where the counterparty does not include the corresponding income; (5) OECD Pillar Two GloBE top-up tax (IIR/UTPR at 15%) applies from fiscal year 2024 to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies linked to Switzerland (Art. 10 para 2 lit. a MWSTG). The threshold applies to Swiss-resident and non-resident suppliers alike; exports of goods (0% supplies) count toward the threshold. Enterprises below the threshold are exempt but may register voluntarily (Art. 11 MWSTG).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are zero-rated under Art. 23 para 1 MWSTG when: (i) the goods physically leave Switzerland to a foreign destination; (ii) dispatch/transport is performed by the supplier, the buyer or a third party; (iii) the recipient is established outside Switzerland or the goods terminate in a foreign customs territory; and (iv) the exporter holds verifiable customs, transport and payment evidence. CH_AG's double-shot PBT keycap shipments to non-Swiss final customers and distributors qualify as 0% supplies upon proof of export; ancillary services directly connected with the export (freight, handling, commissions) also benefit from the 0% rate under Art. 23 para 2 MWSTG. Deliveries to Swiss customers, supplies consumed in Switzerland, and goods merely warehoused in Switzerland before a later re-export remain taxable at the ordinary 8.1% or reduced 2.6% rate; EU/UK distance sales may trigger foreign VAT registration and import schemes.

  • Swiss customs export declaration in e-dec/NCTS with Movement Reference Number (MRN) proving physical exit from Swiss customs territory
  • Commercial invoice stating exporter UID/MWST number, consignee, Incoterms, HS code (e.g., 3926.90 or 8471.60), quantity, value and the declaration 'Export supply, zero-rated under Art. 23 MWSTG'
  • Transport document - original bill of lading, air waybill (AWB), CMR consignment note or rail waybill evidencing delivery to the foreign consignee and destination
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT transfer instruction / card-settlement statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds in the CH_AG's bank account
  • Recipient evidence - foreign VAT ID, company register extract or residence/IP proof for private buyers confirming non-Swiss status
  • Transaction file - purchase order, sales contract, insurance certificates and delivery acceptance notes forming the complete audit trail

No mandatory continuous transaction control, no real-time fiscal clearance and no digital stamping regime exists. Invoices may be paper or electronic; mandatory content under Art. 26 MWSTG applies to invoices exceeding CHF 400 (supplier UID, recipient, nature/date/amount of supply and applicable VAT rate or zero-rating reference). The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung, SIX standard) is the mandatory structured payment instrument in the Swiss receivables workflow since 30 June 2020; voluntary B2B e-invoicing via Peppol BIS 3.0 and B2C Swiss eBill is permitted. Export invoices must reference the customs MRN and state 'Export supply, zero-rated under Art. 23 MWSTG'; no API clearance or government pre-approval is required.

Switzerland imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark stated herein is an internal control threshold for timely export substantiation and aligns with FTA audit practice of matching export declarations against bank receipts within the accounting year. FTA post-clearance export audits may reassess VAT at 8.1% on the invoice value where zero-rating is unproven, plus default interest at 5% per annum and clawback of any input VAT allocated to the fictitious export. Criminal exposure: intentional tax evasion under Art. 58 MWSTG is punishable by a fine up to CHF 100,000 (negligent: up to CHF 10,000); fraudulent use of forged export documents constitutes Abgabebetrug under Art. 59 MWSTG and Swiss customs criminal law, with imprisonment up to five years (up to ten years in qualified aggravated cases with large amounts). The normal five-year limitation for VAT assessment is extended to ten years in cases of evasion or fraud (Art. 42 para 3 MWSTG). Export vouchers and books must be retained for ten years (Art. 89 MWSTG).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. The Swiss franc is freely convertible, with no restrictions on remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, or returns of capital to non-residents. Profit repatriation by an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is effected through board-approved dividend distributions, subject to the statutory annual general meeting approval process, applicable statutory profit availability tests, and, where relevant, Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at 35% on distributions to non-qualifying shareholders; a reduced rate may apply under a double taxation treaty. There is no statutory foreign-exchange allocation, priority-rationing system, or MAC-clause blocking of dividend transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender rule exists under Swiss law. Exporters are not required to repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within any statutory timeline and are not required to convert export revenues into CHF. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency, netted, or converted at the company's discretion, subject only to general bookkeeping and Swiss Federal Tax Administration reporting of taxable income.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque Nationale Suisse, Banca Nazionale Svizzera); currency and monetary policy is implemented without an exchange-control authority and there is no separate foreign-exchange administration in Switzerland. The State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and FINMA share policy and supervision functions, respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low direct viability for offshore digital EMIs acting as primary treasury accounts. A Swiss AG requires an account with a bank or FINMA-licensed fintech institution holding a banking licence or deposit-holding authorisation. Offshore EMIs not licensed or passported into Switzerland cannot legally maintain customer accounts in CHF or provide settlement infrastructure without an onward clearing relationship. A licensed Swiss fintech such as a FINMA 'fintech licence' holder or a Swiss bank with a full banking licence may be used as a low-cost digital treasury intermediary, but the legal settlement layer must remain a regulated Swiss financial intermediary. For Shopify Payments and Stripe receiving channels, the merchant account will be routed through Stripe/Shopify's respective acquiring banking partners; this does not replace the need for a Swiss-authorised settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound CHF or foreign-currency equity injections shall be wired via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank) to the Swiss clearing bank using standard SIC/SEPA mechanics. The payment order must reference the company full legal name, the corporate account number, and the purpose code for direct investment / equity capital (e.g., 'CAP/ EQU-01', 'Direct Investment' or 'BOP 4078' if requested by the receiving bank; in Switzerland there is no mandatory central-bank purpose code but commercial banks may use OECD/IMF classification for statistics). Receiving bank documentation shall include board resolution, share issuance minutes, capital adequacy calculation, UBO declarations, and the certified share register. No prior exchange-control approval is required, but violation-free onboarding requires the capital injection to be neat, traceable, and line-by-line reconcilable with the company's treasury file.Trade settlements for injection-moulded keycaps will be executed through SIC-CH/EuroSIC or the SWIFT network. For Swiss and European payroll, rents, and domestic operating expense, SEPA Direct Debit and CHF direct clearing through SIC are mandatory. Supplier invoices below USD 50,000 shall be paid by MT103 within the standard SWIFT window; invoices above USD 50,000 or requiring legal internet will use the Commercial LC corridor with documents against payment. For Shopify Payments and Stripe receiving channels, the merchant account will be routed through Stripe/Shopify's respective acquiring banking partners; this does not replace the need for a Swiss-authorised settlement account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% flat federal corporate income tax on net profit; combined Zug cantonal and communal headline rate approximately 11.8% for an Aktiengesellschaft.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) withheld at source on gross dividend distributions from the AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within six months after the close of the commercial fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers the Zug administration generally requires filing by 31 March and routinely grants an automatic extension to 30 September.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law establishes no codified documentation threshold; in practice the Zug administration expects contemporaneous OECD Local File documentation for controlled cross-border transactions exceeding CHF 1,000,000 per counterparty per fiscal year and Master File documentation for multinational groups above CHF 50 million consolidated turnover; absence of documentation shifts the burden of proof and increases penalty exposure on transfer pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE regime, but the OECD substance-over-form doctrine, BEPS Action 6 principal-purpose test, Swiss anti-avoidance case law, and Verrechnungssteuer substance requirements apply. The AG manufacturing custom mechanical keyboard caps must maintain genuine premises and qualified employees in Zug, hold board decisions locally, control procurement, logistics, and IP functions in Switzerland, and document the functions, assets, and risks supporting its arm's-length manufacturing margin.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the evaded federal and Zug cantonal tax, default interest, and an administrative fine of up to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and additional court fines under federal and cantonal tax law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns, instruct the concealment, or knowingly benefit from evasion are prosecuted as individual offenders and can face detention pending trial, travel restrictions, civil restitution orders, and forced personal assets seizure.
Constructive Dividend (Verdeckte Gewinnausschuettung)

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the expense, upward profit adjustment, recharacterization as a distribution triggering 35% Swiss withholding tax, plus interest and penalties on the deemed dividend.

Director personal liability: Board members who authorized the unauthorized advantage are jointly and severally liable for the un-withheld WHT and for restitution to the company in insolvency under Swiss withholding tax and company law; discharge is not accepted in tax fraud investigations.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result, secondary adjustments recharacterizing the excess as a constructive distribution with 35% WHT, reassessment interest, and fines for failure to keep proper business records.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve misleading transfer pricing policies can be sued by the company for damages, held liable for the extra tax assessed and penalties where they acted against published OECD guidance, and personally fined when they sign false declarations to the Zug authorities.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reallocation of profits to the jurisdiction of effective management, denial of DTA benefits under the principal-purpose test, revocation of the participation exemption, withholding tax on deemed distributions, and reassessment for open limitation periods.

Director personal liability: Founders and directors remain civilly liable for unpaid tax and are exposed to criminal prosecution for tax omission; international cooperation and search warrants may lift Swiss banking secrecy and trigger travel restrictions pending the criminal tax case.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Proceeds-of-Tax-Crimes Violations

Statutory sanction: Confiscation of proceeds, disqualification from commercial filings, fines under the Swiss Criminal Code, and aggravated money-laundering charges where the director knew the funds derived from tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are prosecuted as natural persons, risk imprisonment, and face asset freezes and travel bans until the penalty and confiscation order is secured; the corporate veil is ineffective in laundering investigations.
