Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) - Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec (www.revenuquebec.ca) - Mon dossier d'entreprise portal, administering Quebec corporation tax, QST, and (for Quebec-established businesses) the federal GST/HST under the CRA-RQ single-administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA) and obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ). Step 2: Register with the CRA via Business Registration Online to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts, notably RC0001 (federal corporate income tax) and RT0001 (GST/HST). Step 3: File the single GST/HST-QST registration (form LM-1) with Revenu Québec, which issues the QST number using the NEQ base and the QST program suffix, eliminating a separate federal GST/HST registration for Quebec-established businesses. Step 4: Maintain separate federal T2 and Quebec CO-17 corporate income tax filings, and reconcile Apple App Store / Google Play merchant-of-record financial reports against the registrant's revenue accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canada/Quebec business corporation is a resident taxpayer subject to federal Part I and Quebec corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no pass-through or S-corporation equivalence, and the entity is a per se corporation for US check-the-box purposes (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-2(b)(8)); foreign-source digital revenue is taxable in Canada with foreign tax credit relief and controlled-foreign-affiliate (FAPI) rules applying to downstream affiliates.

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box or pass-through election exists for business corporations. Available elections affecting this structure: (i) non-calendar fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1) subject to CRA concurrence; (ii) GST/HST group registration election (Excise Tax Act s. 273) and the corresponding QST group election for closely related Quebec corporations, permitting consolidated indirect-tax compliance; (iii) associated-corporation small-business-deduction allocation under ITA s. 125(7); (iv) Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules, which disallow deductions on hybrid instrument and entity mismatches without any elective opt-out. A US check-the-box election is legally unavailable because Canada is on the per se corporation list.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold for both GST and QST; registration is compulsory within 30 days after taxable supplies (including zero-rated export sales) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; app-store subscription revenue from Canadian consumers counts toward the threshold, and Apple/Google IAP commission statements must be netted according to the merchant-of-record agreement for threshold computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital web-novel subscriptions supplied to non-resident (non-Canadian) recipients are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (supply of services and intangible personal property to non-residents) and are correspondingly zero-rated for QST, provided the supply is not in respect of Canadian real or tangible property and the subscriber's habitual residence and device app-store territory are outside Canada. Qualifying zero-rated exporters remain GST/QST registrants and may claim full input tax credits and refunds on Canadian business expenses, making substantiation of non-resident status essential. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, but the 180-day settlement benchmark in this dossier governs the completeness of payment documentation.

  • Zero-rated supply register with per-transaction non-resident evidence: subscriber contract, billing address, IP geolocation, and device app-store territory (country code)
  • Apple App Store and Google Play monthly financial reports and payout statements evidencing foreign-territory sales and any app-store-remitted GST/HST/QST
  • Foreign-currency settlement and remittance records: FX conversion reports, bank deposit advices, and payout receipts from Apple/Google payment rails
  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A) and transport documents (air waybill or bill of lading) - required only where tangible goods are exported; inapplicable to intangible web-novel subscription exports
  • Six-year retention of all books, vouchers, and electronic records in Canada as required by ETA s. 286 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act

Canada and Quebec do not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or continuous transaction controls. Mandatory electronic filing applies where thresholds are met: CRA requires e-filing of GST/HST returns and T2 corporate returns for large filers, and Revenu Québec requires electronic QST filing for registrants above prescribed turnover thresholds. Books, vouchers, and electronic records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act), and monthly reconciliation of Apple/Google Play financial reports to GST/QST accounts is required for audit readiness.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess unsubstantiated zero-rated exports as taxable supplies within the four-year normal reassessment window, or indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud, clawing back unremitted GST/QST plus interest and late-filing and late-remittance penalties (up to 6% of the unpaid net tax plus daily compound interest). Gross-negligence false-statement penalties reach 25% of the tax evaded under ETA s. 285 (50% under ITA s. 163(2), with Quebec equivalents under the Tax Administration Act), and knowing falsification may lead to criminal prosecution. Repeated failures may also trigger denial of input tax credit and refund claims, enhanced audit scrutiny of the app-store revenue reconciliation, and publication of sanctions by Revenu Québec.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; there are no capital controls, no surrender requirements, and no profit-remittance licensing. A Quebec commercial corporation (federal or provincial) may receive foreign equity injections, repatriate profits, and distribute dividends without Bank of Canada approval. The only compliance friction is tax-based: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding at 25% on dividends, royalties, and management fees paid to non-residents (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% dividends / 10% royalties under the Canada–US treaty, frequently 0% on royalties under other applicable treaties); (ii) the GST/HST (5%) and Quebec QST (9.975%) place-of-supply rules for digital services; (iii) the W-8BEN-E filing requirement with US withholding agents, as Apple/Google pay through US or related entities and may treat app-store proceeds as royalties or business income under IRC 871/881 absent treaty relief. No capital-account permission is required from any Canadian authority for inbound investment or outbound profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation timeline for export receipts; the platform may keep earnings in USD in a domestic USD sub-account or offshore account without restriction. The only enforceable constraints are reporting: (i) importing/exporting currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to CBSA under PCMLTFA Regulation 19; (ii) cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, with the reporting obligation attaching to the financial intermediary, so the platform should pre-notify its account manager; (iii) any CAD 10,000+ cash or EFT transaction conducted with a foreign-exchange dealer is subject to large transaction and beneficial ownership reporting. Apple/Google monthly settlement wires of CAD-equivalent value above 10,000 will consequently trigger automated FINTRAC reporting by the receiving Canadian bank, which the treasury must reflect in its compliance calendar.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as monetary authority, currency issuer for the Canadian dollar (CAD), and retail payments supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c. 23; PSP registration regime effective 16 November 2024). It does not operate a foreign exchange control or licensing regime. FX-related supervision is compartmentalized: OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially regulates the Schedule I banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including EFT and large virtual currency transaction reporting; and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) enforces cross-border currency reporting. The federal government's Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List and Criminal Code sanctions regimes must be screened before every cross-border transaction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI passport; foreign digital-bank/EMI rails (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) may be used only as secondary collection/payout channels once the operator verifies that the PSP is registered as an MSB with FINTRAC and, for money transmission, with Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers under the Money-Services Businesses Act (CQLR c E-12.000001). All non-resident directors/signatories lacking a Canadian presence or using private mailbox addresses

Root cause: All non-resident directors/signatories lacking a Canadian presence or using private mailbox addresses

Defense evidence: Certified true copies of passports (bilingual notary); notarized proof of residential address (utility bill, bank statement dated <90 days); banker's reference from their home-country bank addressed to the Canadian account manager; video-KYC dual-process completion per FINTRAC guidance; appointment of a Quebec resident director (for QBCA) or Canadian resident director (for CBCA) with a current REQ extract proving the appointment; a signing authority matrix with limitation amounts.
Business model flagged due to in-app tipping / donation mechanics resembling unlicensed money services or gambling

Root cause: Business model flagged due to in-app tipping / donation mechanics resembling unlicensed money services or gambling

Defense evidence: Apple App Review approval letter and App Store category certificate; Terms of Use and End-User Licence Agreement defining virtual coins as non-redeemable content-access tokens with zero cash-out value; platform content moderation and age-gate policy; a legal opinion (Quebec counsel) that the subscription/tipping model is not a lottery or wagering transaction under Criminal Code Part VII; an MSB regression checklist demonstrating that the platform never transmits funds on behalf of third parties outside the server-payment framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI passport; foreign digital-bank/EMI rails (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) may be used only as secondary collection/payout channels once the operator verifies that the PSP is registered as an MSB with FINTRAC and, for money transmission, with Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers under the Money-Services Businesses Act (CQLR c E-12.000001). Since 16 November 2024, retail payment services supplied to Canadians are also subject to Bank of Canada supervision under the RPAA, so foreign PSPs serving Quebec users or the platform's payout flows must hold or be affiliated with a designated PSP registration. Legal constraints: (i) Apple/Google settlement proceeds must never be routed to a foreign EMI as the primary treasury; Canadian tax filings and QST registration require a Canadian bank account; (ii) EMI balances are not CDIC-insured; (iii) the platform must not operate an unlicensed money-transfer business for author payouts, meaning each payment must be supported by a specific underlying content royalty obligation and funds must not be commingled beyond 24 hours without bank consent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is triggered for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value; a Local File is expected where related-party transactions are material to the Canadian entity. A Country-by-Country Report and Master File obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not operate an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA and Revenu Québec apply substance-over-form, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, and transfer-pricing adjustment rules under section 247. A Quebec web-fiction corporation must demonstrate real office premises, operational decisions, employees, servers, content management, and economic risk in Canada. A conduit or brass-plate corporation that merely invoices subscription revenue from Quebec while bearing no substance is vulnerable to recharacterization, treaty denial, permanent-establishment assertion, and related withholding assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act of 50% of the amount of tax that would have been understated, plus interest. Criminal conviction under section 239(1) carries a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment for up to two years, restitution orders, and publication in CRA tax-evasion conviction bulletins. Revenu Québec imposes equivalent penalties under the Tax Administration Act and may suspend or revoke registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize the false reporting may be charged jointly under criminal law, liable for restitution, and personally subject to imprisonment. In criminal proceedings, bail conditions may include surrender of passports and travel bans; CRA and Revenu Québec may also seek civil enforcement against director assets where corporate formalities are used as a sham.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full amount of tax that should have been withheld under section 215 of the Income Tax Act, plus arrears interest and penalties. A gross-negligence penalty of 50% may be added if the omission is intentional. Revenu Québec may reassess the same amount where the payment was deducted in computing Quebec taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII tax under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and commence civil recovery against personal bank accounts and property; wilful evasion in respect of withholding gives rise to criminal liability, including fines, imprisonment, and departure restrictions pending prosecution.
Transfer Pricing and Profit-Shifting Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA may re-determine taxable income to arm's-length amounts under section 247(2), deny the deduction for excessive payments, apply the transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable efforts are not demonstrated, and impose gross-negligence penalties, arrears interest, and treaty-claim disallowance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation, pressure finance staff to fabricate comparability benchmarks, or direct payment flows to shell entities may be assessed as abettors, made parties to criminal fraud, or held liable for unpaid withholding taxes arising out of the recharacterization. Criminal Code fraud convictions for dishonest earnings through artificial pricing carry imprisonment up to 14 years and court-ordered restitution.
Shell Corporation, Sham, and Economic-Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The CRA and Revenu Québec may apply the GAAR under section 245 to disallow the tax benefit and reallocate income to the real economic owner. The courts may treat the arrangement as a sham and deem a permanent establishment where substance is absent, resulting in full federal and Quebec tax reassessment, Part XIII withholding on distributions, interest, and up to 50% gross-negligence penalties. Anti-money-laundering authorities may also report the corporate vehicle to FINTRAC and impose monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Director personal liability: Courts will carefully pierce the corporate veil when a company is used for fraudulent or dishonest purposes. Directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for restitution, including disgorgement of profits, court-ordered compensation to the tax authorities, and, in aggravated fraud cases, imprisonment. Travel restrictions may be imposed by the court at the bail stage, and CRA may obtain third-party garnishment and asset-freeze orders against director assets.
