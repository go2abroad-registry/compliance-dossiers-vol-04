Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec – ClicSÉUR Affaires portal for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial employer/agent registrations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) formed outside Canada but carrying on business in Quebec must obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RC (GST/HST) account by filing the RC1 through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO). The LP is treated as a 'person' for GST/HST purposes (Excise Tax Act s. 240(1)) if it carries on a commercial activity in Canada. Step 2: the LP must register separately with Revenu Québec for QST under the Quebec Taxation Act using form LM-101-V or via ClicSÉUR Affaires. Step 3: if the LP has no physical establishment in Canada, it should appoint a representative for service of process using CRA Form RC59 and Revenu Québec Form LM-107-V. Step 4: the LP must open a CRA customs account (RM) for export/import reporting and maintain a T5013 partnership information return process for partner allocations. Step 5: each foreign partner's identity, residency, and participation percentage must be documented, as the LP is fiscally transparent and non-resident partners become subject to Canadian income tax reporting and potential Part XIII or Part XIV withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax (income and losses flow through to partners) but is an independent registrant for GST/HST/QST when it conducts commercial activity. Available elections include: (i) a non-calendar partnership fiscal year-end election under ITA s. 249.1(4) where all members are non-residents or where a natural business period applies; (ii) GST/HST annual or quarterly filing elections and the quick method option for small suppliers under ETA s. 227(3); (iii) commensurate QST elections under the Quebec Tax Administration Act; and (iv) the election to limit partnership joint and several liability for GST/HST under ETA s. 272.1. No US-style check-the-box classification or blanket hybrid mismatch election is available under Canadian law; hybrid mismatch consequences are instead applied automatically under ITA ss. 18.4-18.9.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (GST/HST and QST) of worldwide taxable supplies for resident small suppliers; however, a non-resident limited partnership that carries on business in Canada is mandatorily registrable under ETA s. 240(1) and the Quebec QST regime regardless of the C$30,000 threshold where it makes taxable supplies in Canada. Registration is performed in the LP's own name and requires a Canadian business address or a designated representative.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST and QST rate may be claimed on the sale of used hydraulic earthmovers and rollers when: (i) the supply is made by the LP to a non-resident purchaser who is not registered under Part IX ETA; (ii) the heavy machinery is delivered or shipped by the LP (or at the LP's risk) for direct export from Canada; (iii) the goods are exported as soon as is reasonably practicable after the sale; and (iv) the LP retains contemporaneous commercial, customs, and banking evidence sufficient to demonstrate that the goods physically left Canada and that title passed to the foreign buyer. Sales structured as buyer's-own-export under a foreign purchase order and delivery to a Canadian carrier or customs-bonded warehouse are equally zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 1 ETA and corresponding QST provisions. Quoted export transactions must also respect CBSA export reporting under the Customs Act.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form A6/CAED electronic filing) evidencing the HS code and declared value of the used excavator
  • Detailed commercial invoice with make, model, serial number, hour meter reading, year of manufacture, and sale price in CAD
  • Ocean or air Bill of Lading (or highway waybill) naming the non-resident consignee and destination
  • Outbound carrier cargo report or customs broker house manifest proving physical exit from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 payment instruction evidencing foreign-source settlement of the invoice
  • Title and acquisition records (purchase invoice, prior import documentation) establishing the LP's cost base and chain of ownership
  • Proof of delivery to the carrier at a port of exit, border crossing, or bonded warehouse

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API tax-authority clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST/QST. Suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information (ETA s. 221(1); QTA s. 434), including the seller's registration number, the date of supply, the total consideration, and the amount of tax. Electronic invoices are permitted where integrity and reliability of the audit trail are maintained. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol PINT model) is voluntary as of the knowledge cutoff date. Books and records must be kept in Canada unless CRA or Revenu Québec has authorized retention outside Canada.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess an improperly zero-rated export within the normal 4-year reassessment window, extended to 10 years where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is demonstrated. If export substantiation is deficient, the sale is recharacterized as taxable in Quebec, resulting in 5% GST and 9.5% QST becoming payable, with compound interest. ETA s. 280(1) imposes a gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of C$100 and 25% of the net tax that would have been payable had the false statement not been made; egregious cases may trigger criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 327-335, exposing the LP and responsible partners to fines up to C$500,000 and imprisonment for up to five years, with parallel offence provisions under Quebec's Tax Administration Act. Partners of an LP may be jointly and severally liable for GST/HST/QST debts under ETA s. 272.1 and applicable Quebec law. Although Canadian federal law imposes no general statutory deadline for foreign-exchange repatriation in connection with zero-rating, CRA audits of cross-border heavy equipment traders require demonstrable evidence that export proceeds were received from outside Canada; failure to repatriate within the 180-day commercial benchmark may increase the risk that a purported export is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale with full clawback of input tax credits previously claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible floating currency. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control legislation; both current-account and capital-account transactions are substantially liberalized for non-resident persons and partnerships. Profit and capital remittances are not subject to exchange-control approval, but outbound distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, normally up to 25% or a reduced treaty rate where an applicable tax treaty applies. Payment flows are also subject to FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and sanctions screening, but there are no convertibility, surrender, or Central Bank pre-approval rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, mandatorily convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a fixed timeframe. Used heavy machinery exporters may lawfully hold foreign currency in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts. The only material statutory duties are: (a) FINTRAC reporting of all electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada within 24 hours of execution, including the purpose code; (b) CBSA reporting of physically transported currency and monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; and (c) maintaining underlying trade documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration) sufficient to evidence an export transaction for banking and tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential and payments oversight by OSFI, anti-money-laundering compliance by FINTRAC, and cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to Medium for pure offshore digital EMI-only onboarding. Canadian payment service providers must be regulated by OSFI as banks, federal credit unions, or authorized foreign bank branches; pure digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) can provide virtual IBANs and multi-currency corridors but are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and generally cannot independently clear CAD-denominated CAD-A debit obligations. In Quebec, an entity providing payment services on an ongoing basis must hold or operate under a license under the Payment Services Act (Quebec) administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Recommended structure is to use a Canadian Schedule I bank as the primary operating depository and an EMI only as an inbound/outbound collection vehicle, with funds passing through the Canadian bank within 48 hours.

Use SWIFT MT103 for all capital injections from non-resident partners to the Canadian Schedule I bank account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium for pure offshore digital EMI-only onboarding. Canadian payment service providers must be regulated by OSFI as banks, federal credit unions, or authorized foreign bank branches; pure digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) can provide virtual IBANs and multi-currency corridors but are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and generally cannot independently clear CAD-denominated CAD-A debit obligations. In Quebec, an entity providing payment services on an ongoing basis must hold or operate under a license under the Payment Services Act (Quebec) administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Recommended structure is to use a Canadian Schedule I bank as the primary operating depository and an EMI only as an inbound/outbound collection vehicle, with funds passing through the Canadian bank within 48 hours.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec corporate rate: 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% provincial). For a Limited Partnership, income is allocated to partners and taxed at their respective marginal rates; the LP itself is not subject to corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable for LP distributions (pass-through). For corporate dividends, statutory rate is 25% (federal Part XIII) plus provincial surtax, but treaty relief may reduce to 5%–15% depending on jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for other transactions with non-residents. Documentation must include Local File, Master File, and Country-by-Country Report (if applicable).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The CRA may recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance, especially for partnerships with non-resident partners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Taxation Act (Quebec). Penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, plus fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the CRA's director liability provisions. For partnerships, general partners are jointly and severally liable for partnership debts, including tax liabilities.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the non-compliance. For LPs, general partners are personally liable for partnership obligations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties for gross negligence. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face personal liability for taxes and penalties if the entity is deemed a sham. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under tax enforcement.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Criminal charges possible.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined or imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply. For LPs, general partners are exposed.
