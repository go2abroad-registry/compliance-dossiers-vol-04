Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up prior to registration, with payment evidenced by an escrow/blocked capital certificate in a Swiss bank within the cantonal registry's accepted list. Shareholder loans or contribution in kind are not permitted to satisfy the paid-up minimum without an audited asset valuation.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical office leased by the company: a fire safety clearance (Feuerpolizeiliche Abnahme) for use of business premises, structural load inspection for server racks/electrical panels above 100kg/m2, work safety and health directives of Suva (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund) are required before full operational scale; if employees work from home only, a written occupational safety stress-risk assessment is required per the Swiss Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (VUV/ArbG 3 et seq.).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Swiss Federal Tax Administration (SFTA), Berne, Switzerland; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch, including the VAT, UID-Register and company tax e-Services portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the Swiss Federal Statistical Office UID register, then file Form 11 (application for VAT registration for non-established businesses) with the ESTV main VAT department using the official e-Service platform. The application requires certified company incorporation documents, proof of foreign registration, particulars of Swiss place of business or Swiss tax representative, description of activities, expected Swiss turnover, and bank details. VAT registration is mandatory if the annual worldwide turnover generated from taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000; voluntary registration is permitted for non-residents (Art. 11 and 14 MWSTG). The ESTV issues the UID and MWST-Nummer within approximately 5 to 10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax (8.5% on profit after tax, equivalent to roughly 7.8% pre-tax) plus cantonal and communal profit taxes, giving standard all-in effective rates of approximately 11.9% to 15.0% depending on canton (e.g., Zug); taxation is blended territorial/worldwide for resident companies; global active business income is taxable with credit/participation relief, but passive foreign income is generally taxable. No pass-through treatment applies to the AG, and no US-style check-the-box classification is available. — A foreign entity must apply for a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the Swiss Federal Statistical Office UID register, then file Form 11 (application for VAT registration for non-established businesses) with the ESTV main VAT department using the official e-Service platform. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax (8.5% on profit after tax, equivalent to roughly 7.8% pre-tax) plus cantonal and communal profit taxes, giving standard all-in effective rates of approximately 11.9% to 15.0% depending on canton (e.g., Zug); taxation is blended territorial/worldwide for resident companies; global active business income is taxable with credit/participation relief, but passive foreign income is generally taxable. No pass-through treatment applies to the AG, and no US-style check-the-box classification is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Swiss law. Available statutory mechanisms include: group taxation / tax consolidation under Art. 59 para 5 DBG (federal) and matching cantonal rules, permitting intra-group profit and loss offset between Swiss resident group companies; VAT group registration (elected group treatment) for units closely linked by economic, organisational and financial relationships (Art. 21 MWSTG); small business VAT exemption election for turnover below CHF 100,000 (Art. 11 MWSTG); election to apply domestic reverse-charge or to zero-rate export services upon documented foreign place of use; election to withhold or op to be treated as an invoice-based taxpayer; hybrid mismatch adjustments under Art. 59a DBG are mandatory, not elective, and apply against foreign hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover (exclusive of VAT) from taxable supplies performed in Switzerland (Art. 10, 11, 14 MWSTG); non-established suppliers of digital, telecommunication and e-commerce B2C services are taxable from the first franc of Swiss supplies if that CHF 100,000 threshold is reached; separate reduced registration threshold of CHF 50,000 applies only to businesses established in Switzerland deriving supplies of professional/institutional services to other VAT payers; no registration threshold applies to non-established businesses deriving supplies of services and goods located or used in Switzerland unless excluded by tax treaty rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% Swiss VAT is available for: (i) exported movable goods, including digital content stored on physical media; (ii) services that relate to movable property that will be exported; (iii) services supplied directly to non-resident customers and properly attributable to use or benefit outside Switzerland (including distance B2B and B2C digital learning subscriptions where the customer's residence and place of use is abroad); and (iv) services relating to immovable property located abroad. For EdTech subscriptions sold through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing to Swiss consumers, the non-established app store operator is deemed the supplier under Art. 20a MWSTG and must charge Swiss VAT; the Swiss AG cannot zero-rate those supplies. Zero-rating for the AG applies only where the customer is a non-established business or private user abroad supplied on an own-account basis without platform intermediation.

  • Swiss export declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration / GemData drop-off declaration or REX certificate for corresponding shipments)
  • Commercial invoice referencing 0% export rate, contract terms, and delivery conditions (Incoterms)
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from Switzerland: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or postal/courier consignment note
  • Proof of receipt abroad: customs clearance confirmation of destination country, signed delivery note, or import declaration
  • For digital services: contractual documentation, IP address or geolocation evidence of the customer, unique customer identification, and confirmation of foreign establishment or residence
  • Payment documentation showing foreign-source settlement (e.g., SWIFT credits, remittance advices)

Switzerland operates no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control, no unitel API, and no digital stamping of e-invoices. Swiss law mandates the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) for payment slips attached to invoices in CHF; the vendor may issue ordinary paper/PDF invoices if VAT data fields comply with Art. 26 MWSTG. E-bill (SIX eBill) and other EDI/e-invoicing formats (e.g., Peppol BIS, Faktur-X) are voluntary commercial standards. Mandatory fiscalisation is limited to cash register software meeting the Federal Department of Finance's technical safety requirements for retail transactions; cloud-based EdTech subscription billing is outside this regime.

The SFTA may retrospectively disallow zero-rating, reassess turnover at 8.1% standard rate, disallow related input VAT deductions, and claim interest of 5% per annum. Where evasion is established under Art. 69 MWSTG, the penalty is up to three times the evaded tax, with a maximum of CHF 250,000, and attempt or obstruction is punishable by fines up to CHF 10,000 under Art. 70 MWSTG. Missing, contradictory, or late-procured export documentation at the time of filing invalidates the zero-rating claim and exposes the AG to a reassessment up to 10 years after each VAT period. Intentional misapplication of export zero-rating, including the use of false residence certificates in B2C EdTech subscriptions, triggers criminal prosecution, publication of sanctions in the official gazette, and suspension from the VAT electrical network. Swiss law imposes no statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark is used by the SFTA to test commercial bona fides; unrepatriated export proceeds with no payment evidence beyond 180 days are treated as indicia of fictitious exports and lead to immediate clawback.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. The Swiss franc is freely convertible in spot and forward markets; neither import, export, nor capital-movement licences are required. Profit remittance, dividends, and loan interest may flow freely to non-residents, but the board must allocate legal reserves at 5% of paid-in capital and 20% of share capital before dividends. No exchange control clearance is needed for remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender obligations exist. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, converted at any time, and no repatriation deadline or minimum conversion ratio is imposed by the SNB or Swiss FX authorities. The only obligation is statistical reporting for Swiss balance of payments purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for offshore EMIs. Offshore digital e-money institutions — such as UK-licensed Wise or EU-licensed Revolut — may hold CHF via partner banks, but for a Swiss AG exercising capital escrow obligations they cannot replace a licensed Swiss bank. Legal constraints: FINMA prohibits accepting public deposits without a banking licence; an EMI's safeguarding arrangement does not constitute statutory depositor protection; and the tax authorities and commercial registry generally require an official Swiss confirmation of capital payment. For operating current accounts, an offshore EMI is viable, but regulatory subjection of the EdTech company only arises if the company itself forwards money; the company must not act as an agent for onboarding third-party customers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax: 8.5% of net taxable profit after tax, equivalent to an effective federal rate of 7.83% on pre-tax profit; combined federal, cantonal and communal effective rate varies by canton — approx. 11.9% in the Canton of Zug (chosen seat) to approx. 21% in high-tax cantons.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) applied at source on all shareholder dividend distributions of a Swiss AG (Art. 1, 13 VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax authority sets a filing deadline of at least 30 days after formal request (Art. 124/125 DBG); in ordinary administrative practice for calendar-year companies in the Canton of Zug, the combined return is due by 30 June of the following year, with a routinely granted extension to 30 September; the federal direct tax assessment follows the cantonal determination.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Swiss threshold is codified; the ESTV administrative practice expects contemporaneous OECD-compliant Local File documentation where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed CHF 500,000 (goods, services, IP, financing); the Master File is expected for groups above CHF 50 million consolidated revenue; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for Swiss-headquartered or Swiss-included MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of CHF 900 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland abolished the privileged holding/domicile/mixed company regimes in the 2019 Tax Reform and the Administrative Measures Act (STAF); there are no formal ESR filing regulations, but an anti-abuse economic-substance doctrine applies under Art. 21 VStG (withholding tax), Art. 6 LHID and DTA mutual agreement practice: the Swiss AG must have its administrative offices, decision-making management, qualified personnel, banking relationships and operational records in Switzerland; a passive shell relying on fiduciary office services is denied treaty benefits, WHT refunds and the patent box, and may be re-qualified as tax-transparent with profits taxed at the shareholder level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false accounting

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the entire evaded federal/cantonal tax, default interest of 3.5% p.a., penalty of 25% to 100% of the evaded tax in simple cases, and up to 200% in aggravated cases; tax fraud (use of falsified documents) carries an additional criminal fine up to CHF 30,000 and, in serious cases, publication of the decision.

Director personal liability: Directors and de-facto managers face imprisonment up to 3 years for aggravated tax fraud (Art. 186 DBG); administrative fines apply to the company and responsible officers under Art. 6 VStrR; the personal assets of directors may be seized to settle the company's WHT and VAT debts where the company is insolvent (Art. 14 VStG, Art. 52 MWSTG); no travel ban is imposed for tax offences alone, but international arrest warrants and entry bans can follow repeated tax fraud.
Shell company with artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the DTA-reduced dividend rate and retroactive 35% anticipatory tax plus interest; re-qualification of intercompany royalty/licence payments as deemed dividends; TP adjustment of the profit base with penalty of 25%-100%; the corporate veil is pierced for tax collection against shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the unpaid anticipatory tax if the distribution was made and the tax not remitted; criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion; confiscation of management fees paid to the fiduciary; travel restrictions may be imposed by criminal authorities in cross-border investigation proceedings.
Transfer pricing mispricing of IP and financing

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to the taxable profit, recalculation of the taxable base with a penalty of 25%-100% of the tax differential; reclassification of excess shareholder loans as hidden equity with 35% anticipatory tax on deemed interest; per-diem fines for documentation default.

Director personal liability: Directors' criminal exposure under Art. 186 DBG for signing false statements; civil restitution claims of the tax authority against the board in insolvency proceedings; personal liability for the due-but-unpaid withholding tax on deemed dividends if the board authorised the distribution without deducting the 35% tax.
