Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec provincial income tax administration. Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online / My Business Account (CRA) and Revenu Québec Clic Revenu / Registraire des entreprises.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must first determine whether it carries on business in Canada or derives Canadian-source income from real property. The LP must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online portal, opening accounts for GST/HST (type RT) and, if applicable, customs (RM) and payroll (RP). In Quebec, the LP must register separately with Revenu Québec using the Form LM-1 Registration Application or through the Registraire des entreprises, obtaining a QST account and a Quebec business number (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec, NEQ). A non-resident partnership earning Canadian rental income should file a section 216 election with CRA and corresponding Revenu Québec forms to report net rental income on a Canadian tax return. The partnership agreement, ownership details, and a Canadian representative for service are required; there is no minimum capital requirement for LP formation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax purposes; tax liabilities arise at the partner level. The LP itself is generally not subject to income tax, although a trust-qualifying REIT with units may be taxed at the trust level if it does not deduct distributions. For a limited partnership holding real estate, rental income and capital gains flow through to domestic and foreign partners in proportion to their interests. Quebec respects the partnership as a flow-through entity and does not levy entity-level income tax on the partnership.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. Statutory elections relevant to the structure include: (1) partnership fiscal period election under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act to maintain an off-calendar year-end; (2) election under subsection 216(4) for non-resident landlords to compute Canadian rental income on a net basis; (3) GST/HST elections under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related entities to treat taxable supplies as having been made for nil consideration; (4) joint elections to file combined GST/HST returns for corporate groups; and (5) corresponding QST elections under the Quebec Tax Administration Act. Hybrid mismatch rules are legislated through sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act, with no elective exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST small-supplier status; QST registration threshold aligns with CAD $30,000 for the same period. For commercial real property rentals in Quebec, registration is mandatory regardless of the small-supplier threshold because the tenant is typically an GST/HST and QST registrant and the supply is taxable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier obtains documentary evidence of export within the prescribed timeframe. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the service relates to real property situated outside Canada or the service is an advisory/consulting or other qualifying service provided to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST. Rents from commercial real property located in Canada are taxable at GST and QST; residential rents are exempt; supplies of real property located outside Canada are outside the scope of Canadian tax. The REIT must defensively document the non-resident status of recipients and offshore property location to sustain any zero-rating claim.

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory government-issued invoicing clearance exists in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE for registrants above a prescribed threshold, and Revenu Québec requires electronic transmission of QST returns through Groupe TVQ for certain registrants. A Pan-Canadian Electronic Invoicing Standard (PCIUS) is in voluntary adoption, and some public-sector procurement requires e-invoicing. Invoices must show the supplier's BN and QST number, and GST/QST must be separately disclosed. No API fiscal stamping or sequential digital clearance regime is currently imposed.

CRA is authorized to reassess a GST/HST return within four years from the date of original assessment; the limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Improper zero-rating claims are disallowed with full tax reassessment, arrears interest, and a penalty under subsection 280(2) of the Excise Tax Act not exceeding 25% of the amount claimed. Fraudulent export claims may expose the LP and its operators to criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, liable to fines up to C$500,000 and imprisonment up to two years. Revenu Québec imposes analogous QST assessments, penalties, and interest under the Quebec Tax Administration Act, and export documentation must be retained for six years from the end of the reporting period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The CAD floats freely and there is no restriction on conversion for rent, dividends, interest, capital contributions, loan repayments, or property-disposition proceeds. Profit remittance is not exchange-controlled but is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory; treaty-reduced, e.g., US treaty rates on arm's-length interest and dividends; real-property rental income may be withheld at 25% absent a section 216 net-income election), reportable on NR4 forms and remitted monthly. FINTRAC imposes only reporting duties on international EFTs equal to or above CAD 10,000. Sanctions restrictions (Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act) may block specific counterparties but are not currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts or foreign-currency rental income. A REIT may retain, borrow against, or convert foreign currency at will in onshore or offshore accounts. The only obligations are FINTRAC reporting for cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more and ordinary CRA reporting of foreign-currency gains/losses and foreign income (Form T1135 where thresholds apply).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (Ottawa) is the monetary and financial-stability authority for the free-floating Canadian Dollar; external payment oversight is shared with OSFI (prudential supervision of banks), FINTRAC (AML/ATF and cross-border EFT reporting), and, in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for securities and derivatives conduct. No dedicated foreign-exchange control administration exists; Canada has had no exchange-control board since 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for core treasury, Medium as a payment conduit. Canada has no EU-style EMI passport; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) cannot take deposits or hold escrow/trust funds under the Bank Act and are not members of Payments Canada for Lynx/ACSS settlement. They may be used only as registered PSPs under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA transitional regime) for low-value distribution payouts, rent collection, or FX execution, provided the LP maintains a chartered-bank master account and all PSP movements are fully reconcilable. Legal constraint: unitholder capital and acquisition proceeds must sit in a Canadian prudentially regulated bank account to satisfy fiduciary and escrow requirements.

Legal constraint: unitholder capital and acquisition proceeds must sit in a Canadian prudentially regulated bank account to satisfy fiduciary and escrow requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate + 11.5% Quebec provincial rate = 26.5% combined for a standard Canadian corporation; 12.2% combined for Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income below the federal small business deduction limit. The Quebec Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level income tax; partners are subject to tax on their allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source income at their respective rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident shareholders is 25% for dividends paid by Canadian corporations. A Quebec LP's allocation of profits to non-resident partners is not a corporate dividend; it is taxed as Canadian-source income under Part I or Part XII.2. If the LP receives dividends from a Canadian company and allocates them to a non-resident partner, the dividend payer remains responsible for the 25% Part XIII withholding, subject to a treaty reduced rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no absolute de minimis threshold on the requirement to prepare transfer pricing documentation for non-arm's length transactions with non-resident related parties. Section 247 of the Income Tax Act requires contemporaneous documentation at the time the return is filed; CRA administrative practice treats any annual aggregate of related-party transactions above CAD 5,000,000 as requiring a full Local File. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for an MNE group with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Quebec does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council's Economic Substance Regulations. The anti-avoidance architecture comprises the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and its Quebec analogue under section 1079.1 of the Taxation Act. A REIT-style LP must demonstrate real property management, active rental operations, employee substance, bank accounts in Canada, and board decision-making in Quebec. A passive shell LP with circular cross-border financing or tax-motivated conduit arrangements will be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, a person convicted of tax evasion on summary conviction is liable to a fine of 50% to 100% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or up to 5 years imprisonment. The civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax. Quebec imposes parallel fines and imprisonment under the Taxation Act (CQLR c. I-3, s. 42).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted tax, source deductions, and interest under section 159 of the Income Tax Act. Criminal conviction exposes responsible officers to personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, and court-ordered civil restitution. CRA may freeze the LP's bank accounts and permit recovery directly against the general partner's personal assets.
Transfer Pricing and Management Fee Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price under section 247(2), assess additional tax and interest, and impose a section 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the applicable threshold under the statute (generally the greater of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue). Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation removes the 'reasonable efforts' defence and makes the adjustment an automatic penalty exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who sign false or incomplete transfer pricing documentation, or who knowingly participate in a scheme to shift income to a related offshore entity, may be assessed personally to the extent of the penalty. CRA can pierce the corporate veil where the participants acted outside any legitimate business purpose, and the related party debt can be recharacterized as equity.
Shell Company / Sham / GAAR

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 disallows the tax benefit and imposes a 25% GAAR penalty on the tax benefit amount; the sham doctrine permits recharacterization of the transaction at law. If the arrangement is a treaty-shopping conduit, Canada may deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who orchestrated an artificial conduit structure can be held personally liable for any disallowed tax benefit, with civil restitution, injunctions, and monetary penalties. The court may impose travel bans pending asset tracing and require a personal financial statement under the tax enforcement process.
AML / Unregistered Financial Activity / Failure to Maintain Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the PCMLTFA, administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 500,000 for a reporting entity (CAD 1,000,000 for repeat or serious violations). Criminal money laundering under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years. FINTRAC may impose compliance agreements, revoke registrations, and order the disgorgement of fees.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner are exposed to prosecution if they knew or ought to have known of laundering activity. Personal sanction includes jail terms, financial penalties, travel bans, and mandatory disgorgement orders. The corporate veil will not protect persons who knowingly allow the LP to be used to launder the proceeds of unrecorded real estate transactions.
