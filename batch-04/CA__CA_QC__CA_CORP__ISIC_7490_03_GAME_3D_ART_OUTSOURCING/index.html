Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (www.canada.ca/cra) and Revenu Québec (www.revenuquebec.ca); Quebec administers the federal GST/HST for businesses registered in Quebec. Provincial corporate registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) is issued automatically by the Registraire des entreprises if incorporated provincially. Step 2: Obtain the federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by filing Form RC1; for CBCA corporations the BN is created upon filing articles of incorporation. Step 3: Register for GST/HST and Quebec Sales Tax (QST) through Revenu Québec using the combined registration process (TPS/TVQ), which provisions the BN accounts to CRA. Step 4: Register for the federal corporate income tax account (RC account) and Quebec corporate tax account (CO account) with Revenu Québec; if hiring employees, also register for payroll source deductions (CRA and Revenu Québec DP/JP accounts). Step 5: Keep the BN/QST registration effective from the earlier of (a) the first taxable supply in Canada or Quebec and (b) the day the $30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists; a Canadian business corporation is per se a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA) and Part IV of the Quebec Taxation Act and is resident by incorporation (ITA s. 250(4)(a)). Relevant elections: (i) GST/HST s. 156 election and corresponding QST election for supplies between closely related Canadian corporations (nil-consideration treatment); (ii) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents earning Canadian-source rents or royalties on a net basis (relevant if the studio licenses its 3D art IP to Canadian licensees); (iii) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident personal services; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 apply ex lege, not by election, and deny interest deductions where the corresponding amount is deductible in a hybrid foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ITA-equivalent small-supplier rule; ETA s. 240 and QSTA s. 28.0.1). A Quebec resident corporation exceeding this threshold must register for GST and QST. Non-resident suppliers must register if they carry on business in Canada and exceed the same threshold, unless their supplies are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of services to a non-resident client (e.g., a foreign game publisher) are zero-rated under s. 7, Part V, Schedule VI of the ETA where the services are not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Transfers of intangible personal property (3D assets, rigs, character models, licences, source files) for use outside Canada are zero-rated under s. 10 of Part V, Schedule VI. QST applies parallel zero-rating under ss. 27.1 and 28 of the QSTA Regulation. The recipient must be a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA/QSTA and who is not acquiring the service for consumption in Canada; digital place-of-supply rules (ETA s. 142.2) deem the supply to be made in Canada if the recipient's establishment is in Canada. The exporter must not zero-rate supplies to Canadian residents, registered non-residents, or Canadian establishments of foreign clients.

  • Not applicable: no customs export declaration, bill of lading, or inward remittance certificate is generated for intangible digital exports; the following documentary set is the acceptable substitute evidence.
  • Signed Service Agreement / Statement of Work identifying the non-resident client, the scope of 3D art services, and the transfer or licence of intangible property rights.
  • Zero-rated tax invoice containing the supplier's Business Number and QST registration number, an explicit 'zero-rated export supply' notation, and a precise description of the digital deliverables.
  • Proof of digital delivery: encrypted file-transfer logs, handover certificates, email chains, or platform extraction records showing transmission to an address outside Canada.
  • Proof of settlement from a foreign bank account: SWIFT/IBAN wire transfer records, foreign currency receipts, or PayPal transaction logs.
  • Evidence of the client's non-resident status: foreign corporate registry extract, foreign tax identification number, or a signed declaration that the client is not registered for GST/HST/QST.
  • If the client is a related party, transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247 and a legal memorandum on place-of-supply analysis.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST and QST invoices must contain prescribed content (seller name, BN, date, description, tax amounts; s. 169 and s. 223 ETA; s. 34 of the QST Regulation). E-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the Pan-Canadian Peppol ecosystem; Revenu Québec requires registered businesses to issue invoices on request and to make electronic books and records available for audit in Quebec.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit export zero-rating claims and reassess within the normal 4-year period, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect or carelessness, and without time limit for fraud. Where zero-rating is overturned, the supplier must remit the full 14.975% GST/QST on the consideration plus daily compound interest (ETA s. 280.1; QSTA s. 69). Civil penalties include the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated tax for false statements (ETA s. 285; analogous QSTA penalty), plus recovery of any input tax credits or rebates claimed in respect of the export. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion (ETA s. 280) may lead to fines and imprisonment. The 180-day settlement benchmark is an internal credit-control practice rather than a statutory CRA deadline; unduly deferred withheld receivables from non-residents may attract ITA s. 15(2) shareholder-benefit or s. 80.4(2) imputation assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange rate regime. There are no general exchange controls under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act; current account transactions and capital account transactions are freely permitted. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject to corporate law solvency tests and ordinary tax compliance (e.g., withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief). Non-resident investors face no statutory FX approval hurdles; payments are processed through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and SWIFT.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists. Canadian law does not impose a conversion ratio or statutory timeline for repatriating export receipts. Exporters may hold foreign currency accounts domestically or offshore and are required to report transactions under FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., $10,000 CAD or equivalent for large cash or cross-border transfers) and maintain books and records in accordance with the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administration oversight is coordinated with Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary settlement volume, Low for primary treasury banking. Authorized foreign fintechs or EMIs with a Canadian money-services business (MSB) registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) may provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment rails, but they cannot replace a Schedule I bank due to OSFI oversight requirements, CDIC insurability, and credit facility access. Non-registered offshore EMIs are legally prohibited from providing payment services to Canadian resident businesses; any such arrangement would trigger regulatory enforcement and bank de-risking risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); 14.0% combined rate for qualified Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents of Canada, subject to income tax treaty reduction. Under the Canada-United States treaty, the rate is generally 15%, reduced to 5% for dividends paid to a corporate beneficial owner that owns at least 10% of the voting shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada prescribes no general CAD monetary threshold exempting non-arm's length cross-border transactions from transfer pricing documentation. ITA section 247(4) requires contemporaneous documentation to be prepared before the T2 filing date. Country-by-Country Reporting and the associated Master File become mandatory when the multinational enterprise group has consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000; CRA may request a Local File for controlled transactions of any size.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions, but ITA section 245 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) and section 247 require genuine commercial substance and arm's length pricing. A 3D-art outsourcing studio must demonstrate real employees, active project management, owned or leased production infrastructure, substantive client contracts, and identifiable commercial risk; a shell arrangement will be disregarded and recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Under ITA section 239, tax evasion is punishable on indictment by a fine of at least 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. A gross negligence penalty under ITA section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax may also be assessed, plus daily compounded arrears interest. Revenu Québec imposes parallel sanctions under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or participate in evasion may lose limited liability protection via the corporate-veil doctrine, become jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest, and face asset seizure, court-ordered travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Non-Filing and Source Deduction Remittance Failures

Statutory sanction: The corporation is liable for late-filing penalties of 5% to 10% of unpaid tax plus monthly surcharges of 1% to 2%, daily prescribed-rate interest, and penalties for unremitted source deductions of up to 20% of the amount. Quebec assessments follow the same principles.

Director personal liability: Under ITA section 227.1 and corresponding Quebec legislation, directors are personally liable for 100% of unremitted source deductions and associated interest and penalties, even if the corporation is dissolved or bankrupt; the due-diligence defence requires proof of reasonable supervision of payroll and remittance systems.
Transfer Pricing and Bare Shell Structures

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to the arm's length price, impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under ITA section 247(3), assert gross negligence penalties under ITA section 163(2), and apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny tax benefits. Interest continues to run until payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements or control sham pricing may be treated as the directing minds of a fraudulent arrangement; the corporate veil is pierced, making them personally liable for civil restitution, and criminal prosecution may include imprisonment and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering and International Wire Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Violations of the PCMLTFA can trigger administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation for entities and CAD 1,000,000 for individuals; criminal money laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to ten years, forfeiture of proceeds, and ancillary orders restricting travel.

Director personal liability: A director who directs routing of illicit funds, approves undocumented cash-equivalent payments, or participates in laundering faces criminal prosecution, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and loss of corporate limited-liability protection.
