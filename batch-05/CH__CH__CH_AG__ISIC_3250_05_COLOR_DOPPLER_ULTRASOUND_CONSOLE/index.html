Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000. At least 20% of the aggregate nominal value, or CHF 50,000, plus any share premium, must be paid up in cash into a blocked capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank; the bank issues a blocked-funds confirmation that must accompany the registration application. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) – ESTV / AFC, portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration via the FTA’s online portal or the Swiss Federal Tax Administration’s Suisa eService platform

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities supplying goods or services in Switzerland that are subject to Swiss VAT must register with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration. Registration is performed electronically via the FTA’s VAT registration form or through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) to obtain an UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) followed by the suffix 'MWST' for VAT purposes. Proof of foreign company registration, a local representative (if required), and details of Swiss activities must be submitted. The UID is assigned within a few days, and the VAT registration number is issued accordingly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities supplying goods or services in Switzerland that are subject to Swiss VAT must register with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration. Registration is performed electronically via the FTA’s VAT registration form or through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) to obtain an UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) followed by the suffix 'MWST' for VAT purposes. Proof of foreign company registration, a local representative (if required), and details of Swiss activities must be submitted. The UID is assigned within a few days, and the VAT registration number is issued accordingly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss corporate income tax is imposed at federal, cantonal, and communal levels with no check-the-box pass-through election for Aktiengesellschaft (AG). The AG is a separate taxable entity, subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income unless treaty or domestic provisions apply. Hybrid mismatch elections are restricted by Swiss domestic anti-abuse rules and the OECD BEPS minimum standards; no elective classification for Swiss resident AGs is available under Swiss tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies unless such supplies are exclusively export-related or foreign-to-foreign supplies, in which case voluntary registration is possible; for non-resident suppliers, a mandatory registration obligation arises as of the first taxable supply in Switzerland regardless of turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Switzerland to non-Swiss territories qualify for 0% VAT where the seller proves that the goods have left Swiss customs territory and entered a foreign destination. Services effectively used and enjoyed abroad may be zero-rated or not subject to Swiss VAT, provided the recipient is located outside Switzerland and the benefits are realized abroad. The supplier must retain reliable commercial evidence of the offshore supply, including contracts, transport documents, and customs confirmation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung)
  • Commercial Invoice/Proforma Invoice
  • Proof of Transport (CMR / Air Waybill / Bill of Lading)
  • Bank or Payment Evidence (Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103)
  • Proof of Foreign Recipient and Tax Status (e.g., foreign VAT registration or residence certificate)

Switzerland does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control. However, electronic invoicing is recognized, and the Swiss QR-bill standard is mandatory for corporate invoice presentation. While there is no mandatory e-invoicing clearance API, all invoices must meet Swiss VAT invoice requirements (e.g., VAT number, issuer, recipient, type of supply, tax rate and amount). The FTA is progressively adopting digital exchange but no pre-clearance stamping regime is in force.

The FTA may scrutinize export zero-rating through documentary audits. Where zero-rating was improperly claimed, the VAT becomes due and payable, interest starts accruing, and a fine of up to 100% of the evaded VAT may be imposed (VAT Act Art. 96). In serious cases, tax evasion penalties and criminal confiscation may apply. Retrospective clawback is generally limited to ten years for VAT assessment periods, and additionally any falsely documented export claim may trigger penalty proceedings under Swiss customs and VAT legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible and Switzerland maintains no exchange controls. Current and capital account transactions are equally liberalized; no prior authorization, no licensing, no blocked-account requirements, and no restriction on profit remittance or dividend repatriation is imposed by Swiss exchange-control law. The only relevant frictions are Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends and debt remuneration where applicable, transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany payments, and prudential AML/KYC checks by banks. Inbound and outbound payments may be structured freely through any FINMA-regulated bank, subject solely to Swiss sanctions law and the Swiss Criminal Code anti-money-laundering rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland does not impose any export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or holding-period requirement for foreign-currency receipts. Exporters are permitted to retain invoices and collections in any freely convertible currency, maintain foreign-currency working capital accounts with Swiss banks, and remix settlement proceeds without Central Bank approval. Swiss banks only carry out statistical reporting of cross-border payments to the Swiss National Bank under the Swiss Payment Transactions statistics framework; there is no company-level reporting or repatriation duty.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera); no separate foreign exchange administration; competences include Swiss monetary policy and statistical balance-of-payments reporting, while the State Secretariat for International Finance and SECO handle sanctions and financial market matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment flows, Low for primary treasury and trade-clearing functions. FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech companies and digital banks can open CHF and foreign-currency collection accounts for a CH-AG, receive SIC payments, and facilitate online FX conversions; however, they generally do not provide commercial documentary credit services, SWIFT MT700 issuance, or escrow/capital block accounts. EMIs from the EEA or the UK cannot passport into Switzerland; they require Swiss FINMA recognition or a local licensed agent to lawfully onboard a Swiss entity. Offshore EMI-only structures are therefore not a compliant substitute for a Tier-1 corporate banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 transfer from the parent or shareholder’s bank to the Tier-1 Swiss beneficiary account, with CHF settlement, full remittance reference stating “Share capital increase – CH_AG” and a clear economic purpose. No Swiss FX licence is required and no prior Central Bank approval is necessary; the receiving bank assigns the applicable statistical balance-of-payments code for a capital transaction (typically direct-investment equity). The bank will request: the notarized capital-increase resolution, the formal subscription declarations of the new shares, the statutory minimum share capital deposit confirmation, and the intended commercial register filing. Remittance instructions must identify the transferring shareholder exactly as the beneficial owner; third-party wires from a non-UBO account will be rejected unless supported by a written loan or assignment agreement and a compliant corporate resolution.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for wire payments and MT700/MT707 documentary credit messages issued under UCP600 for import and export trade settlements. For confirmed irrevocable letters of credit involving a Swedish, EU, UK or Chinese counterparty, the issuing Swiss bank can add confirmation through a correspondent bank in the target clearing zone. Same-day CHF settlement is executed through Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC); EUR payments use SEPA/EURO1 or TARGET2 via correspondent networks; USD payments use the US correspondent clearing system (Fedwire/CHIPS). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory corporate income tax rate of 8.5%; combined cantonal and communal rates bring the normal aggregate headline rate to approximately 11%–16% depending on the canton (e.g., Zug for substance-heavy commercial operators).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% final Swiss dividend withholding tax imposed at source on cash and in-kind distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Reference tax period equals the financial year; the return must generally be filed within 6 months after fiscal year-end, with cantonal extensions commonly granted up to 12 months after year-end without penalty if properly requested.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Payable on declaration when profits are distributed; in practice the 35% withholding tax must be declared within 30 days of the dividend resolution by the board of directors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Although Swiss law does not prescribe a fully statutory local-file threshold in the same manner as other OECD countries, the ESTV and leading cantonal tax authorities expect a complete Labour and Substance File as soon as cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 3 million in any particular year; full local-file is mandatory where the transaction value is material and the taxpayer applies for treaty relief or requests mutual agreement assistance.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but under the '15% limitation' and the OECD Pillar Two rules, an AG claiming treaty benefits or holding privileges must demonstrably maintain real personnel, physical presence, decision-making capacity, and a functioning medical-device trade business in Switzerland; artificial entry transactions without operational activity will be recharacterized as a purely passive wealth-preservation vehicle and denied treaty relief and partial participation deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understatement of Cross-Border Sales Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to three times the amount of tax evaded plus interest; significant procedural fines imposed by the cantonal tax administration; publication of the tax evasion in the cantonal official gazette in highly aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax declarations become personally liable for the full amount of the tax evaded, are subject to criminal fines, and can be subject to travel-ban orders and a maximum prison term of five years in serious fraudulent tax evasion cases.
Shell Company / Lack of Real Substance

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax administration will pierce the corporate veil under the transparent-company doctrine and allocate all income to the foreign beneficial owner, triggering retroactive assessment of federal and cantonal corporate income tax, withholding tax, and VAT, plus penalties of up to three times the tax evaded; the AG's commercial registry status may also be suspended.

Director personal liability: The board of directors of the empty AG can be individually held for restitution of the unpaid withholding tax (up to CH 1 million in extreme cases), and the register office may impose a travel restriction when foreign-principal directors use Swiss corporate form solely to evade foreign tax.
AML / Sanctions / Export-Control Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Swiss Money Laundering Act up to CHF 500,000 for entities, plus seizure of assets, revocation of the commercial register entity, and regulatory debarment; export-control violations under Embargogesetz are punishable by imprisonment of one to five years and heavy fines.

Director personal liability: An individual director or officer who knowingly conceals the ultimate beneficial owner's identity or orders the transaction can be held criminally liable, with fines and custodial sentences, and the director cannot be shielded by corporate limited liability.
Incorrect / Fictitious Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Fraudulently false TP documentation leads to a reestablished arm's-length computation by the Swiss FTA; taxpayer loses 'reasonably careful taxpayer' exemption and receives a procedural penalty of up to CHF 30,000 plus supplementary corporation tax plus default interest; in fraudulent-case procedure, the tax authority may impose criminal fines of 30%–150% of the hidden additional tax.

Director personal liability: The statutory representative (Geschäftsführer) who contributes to false TP documentation can be personally held jointly and severally for the penal amount if the company cannot pay, and may face disqualification from acting as director of any Swiss AG for up to five years.
Late Dividend Distribution / Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: The Swiss AG will owe the full 35% withholding tax from its own funds, plus 4.5% aging interest and a delay penalty; continued non-remittance constitutes a claim over the estate that can accelerate bankruptcy proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who issue dividend instructions without reserving funds for withholding tax become jointly liable to the tax authority for the unpaid withholding obligation, and in repeated cases can face criminal prosecution for unauthorized disposal without coverage.
