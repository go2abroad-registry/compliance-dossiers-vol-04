Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online administration via My Business Account, Business Registration Online (BRO), and the CRA Digital Economy portal. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System and eManifest systems supporting export zero-rating substantiation.

E-government filing portal

An Ontario limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent and does not hold a corporate income TIN; the operational statutory identifier is the nine-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) with a dedicated GST/HST (RT) program account. Procedure for a foreign-owned LP: (1) file the LP declaration and annual return with ServiceOntario through the Ontario Business Registry (ORBIS) and obtain the Ontario Business Identification Number; (2) register with the CRA via Business Registration Online (or Form RC1) to obtain the BN and request the GST/HST RT account when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or cumulatively in the preceding four quarters, or earlier voluntarily to recover input tax credits; (3) record each partner's foreign tax identification number, country of tax residence, and permanent establishment data for the T5013 partnership information return and partner slips; (4) a non-resident partner that itself carries on business in Canada through the LP may require its own BN program accounts for payroll, import/export, or Part I tax compliance; (5) the CRA does not issue a separate VAT ID distinct from the BN; the BN and RT program account together constitute the statutory VAT/GST identifier for the LP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the Ontario LP is not subject to entity-level federal or Ontario income taxation; income, losses, and tax credits are allocated to and taxed in the hands of partners under Income Tax Act (Canada) sections 96 through 103. This pass-through classification is mandatory under Canadian law and is not contingible by a US-style check-the-box election. For GST/HST purposes the LP is a distinct person under the Excise Tax Act and must register and remit HST on taxable supplies made in Canada when the registration threshold is exceeded; non-resident ownership is permitted, subject to Ontario LP registration and CRA information reporting.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box legislative mechanism; entity classification follows the legal form of the Ontario LP. Available elections relevant to a foreign-owned LP: (a) Income Tax Act s. 97(2) rollover election tax-deferring the contribution of eligible property to the LP by a partner; (b) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA ss. 96(3) and 249.1 allowing alignment of the LP's fiscal period with the partner's taxation year, subject to CRA administrative approval; (c) Excise Tax Act s. 156 closely-related-person election permitting taxable inter-party supplies within a related group to be valued at nil for GST/HST; (d) selected listed financial institution elections where the LP's activities are predominantly financial within the meaning of the ETA; (e) the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (reverse-hybrid income inclusion and hybrid arrangement deduction-denial provisions implementing BEPS Action Item 2) apply automatically and cannot be displaced by a partnership-level election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (Excise Tax Act s. 148(1)): an LP ceases to be a small supplier, and registration becomes mandatory, when the consideration for its worldwide taxable supplies, other than supplies of financial services, capital property, and goodwill, exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or cumulatively in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration takes effect on the first day of the month following the quarter in which the threshold is exceeded. Voluntary registration is available before the threshold is reached to claim input tax credits on Canadian business inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate when the supplier obtains and retains documentary proof of export in accordance with the Export and Commercial Goods Regulations. Digital services, including real-time video-matching and video-chat platform services supplied to non-resident users, are zero-rated as services supplied to a non-resident person under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, provided the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant using the service in its commercial activity and the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. The supplier must contemporaneously capture non-residence evidence such as billing address, credit-card country, IP geolocation, contractual residency declaration, and identification documents; where payment is processed through Apple IAP or Paymentwall, the records must identify the underlying end-customer tax residence rather than relying solely on the payment intermediary's status.

  • Canada Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) / Canadian Export Reporting System proof of report, applicable to any physical goods exports
  • Commercial Invoice / Bill of Sale stating the exported supply value, tariff classification, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or highway) or waybill evidencing the Canadian point of loading and foreign delivery point
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident payer
  • Non-resident user verification records (IP geolocation, billing address, payment-card country, contract of supply, passport or tax-ID evidence) for zero-rated digital platform services

Canada does not operate a real-time transaction-level clearance system, digital fiscal stamping, or a continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. The CRA prescribes minimum invoice content under the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations, including the supplier BN, date of supply, description, consideration, and tax amount. Electronic invoices are valid, and records may be stored digitally, provided they are complete, legible, and retrievable for CRA audit during the seven-year retention period. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants above the CRA electronic-filing threshold; non-resident digital-economy suppliers use the CRA digital registration portal and simplified reporting. Canada participates in the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework, but no API-based fiscal authorization or clearance requirement has been enacted to date.

On audit, the CRA will deny the zero-rating where the LP cannot produce contemporaneous export or non-resident-recipient documentation; the reassessment restores HST at 13% on the full consideration, including the Ontario 8% provincial component, plus daily compound interest at the prescribed rate. Excise Tax Act penalties include: s. 280 failure to register (greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the consideration for taxable supplies during the non-registration period); s. 280.1 failure to file a return; and s. 285.1 false statements or gross negligence (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax or input tax credit amount misstated). Retrospective clawback extends to all input tax credits allocated to the zero-rated supplies, including input tax credits embedded in Apple IAP and Paymentwall commission fees. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely in cases of fraud, neglect, or misrepresentation attributable to wilful default. While no federal GST/HST statute prescribes a specific foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is adopted as the standard contractual control in the payment-rails agreements with Apple IAP and Paymentwall to ensure Inward Remittance Certificates and bank credit advices can be produced for CRA and OECD-style documentation review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under both current-account and capital-account lines; no central-bank licence, exchange-control approval, or surrender requirement applies to cross-border capital movements, service payments, dividends, or partnership distributions. The practical hurdles are fiscal-regulatory: (i) FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer and large-cash reporting at CAD 10,000; (ii) CRA Form T1135 foreign-property reporting by Canadian-resident partners; (iii) transfer-pricing documentation for related-party remittances; (iv) Part XIII non-resident withholding on interest, royalties, management fees, and certain distributions (25% statutory, treaty-reduced); and (v) sanctions screening of counterparties. Profit remittance by the Ontario LP is achieved through discretionary or contractual distributions to limited partners; to the extent the distribution represents contributed capital or previously taxed non-capital income, it is non-taxable in Canada, but partner-level characterisation must be documented to prevent recharacterisation as debt interest or a taxable benefit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada abolished statutory currency surrender with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; there is no obligation to convert, and no statutory timeline, for repatriating export or service proceeds. The LP may retain 100% of revenue in any currency, hold foreign-denominated accounts at domestic authorised banks, and remit earnings at its discretion. The only binding compliance gates are tax measurement under ss. 39 and 261 of the Income Tax Act (foreign-currency gains and losses recognised on the Bank of Canada daily rate), FINTRAC reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, and T1135/CRS reporting of foreign-denominated financial assets held directly or indirectly.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and financial-system authority; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration because Canada operates an unrestricted capital account. Banking and FX counterparty supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/FX transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and cross-border sanctions enforcement by Global Affairs Canada in coordination with the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium/Low for treasury-critical functions. A non-resident fintech EMI (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, or similar) is not a bank within the meaning of the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and cannot hold the LP's capital escrow or settlement balances in Canada unless regulated as a money-services business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) with FINTRAC registration and, in Ontario, licensed under the Money Service Business Act, 2020. EMIs are viable only as auxiliary collection or FX-conversion rails for the Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlement flows, not as the depository of operating liquidity.
