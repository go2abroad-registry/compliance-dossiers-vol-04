Competent tax authority

For Quebec, Revenu Québec administers the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), Quebec corporate income tax, and, in most cases, GST and Quebec Sales Tax (QST) registration and returns. A corporation may also have separate federal or Quebec corporate registry filing obligations.

E-government filing portal

Obtain the applicable federal or Quebec corporate incorporation/registry number first. Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts, including a corporate income tax account (RC) and, when required, a GST/HST account (RT), through Business Registration Online or the applicable CRA non-resident registration process. A non-resident without a Canadian business number may apply using CRA Form RC1 or the prescribed non-resident process. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate legal entities and do not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership. Canada has no general entity-classification election allowing a corporation to opt into pass-through treatment. Specific elections may apply to transactions, accounting methods, or statutory regimes, but they do not generally change the corporation's classification as a taxpayer. Cross-border hybrid mismatch rules may deny or adjust deductions or other tax attributes; they are not elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over a single calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters for the general small-supplier test. Special rules apply to certain non-residents, digital supplies, taxi and ride-sharing operators, and other prescribed suppliers. The 5% figure is the federal GST rate; Quebec's general QST rate is 9.975%, subject to separate registration and place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including that the goods are exported from Canada and the supplier retains prescribed evidence of export and satisfies applicable delivery, purchaser, and non-use conditions. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the specific Exported Services provisions of the Excise Tax Act apply; exclusions and conditions include, among others, services relating to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada and services supplied to individuals in specified circumstances. Freight, logistics, and clinical-supply-chain services require transaction-specific analysis; a foreign customer or foreign-currency payment alone does not establish zero-rating. Quebec QST treatment must be assessed separately under Quebec legislation.

  • Commercial invoice and contracts identifying the supplier, recipient, goods or services, destination, and consideration
  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration and related proof of export, where an export declaration is required
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier documentation evidencing shipment and delivery outside Canada
  • Customs, freight-forwarder, and delivery records linking the exported goods to the invoice
  • For services, evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, service scope and performance, and compliance with the applicable statutory exported-service conditions
  • Foreign-exchange receipts and bank records, where available, as corroborative evidence; they are not a generally prescribed condition of GST zero-rating

Canada has no general real-time electronic-invoice clearance, mandatory digital-stamping, or API pre-clearance regime for ordinary GST/QST invoices issued by a freight or clinical-logistics corporation. Invoices and supporting records must meet applicable statutory content and retention requirements. Quebec's mandatory billing and sales-recording requirements apply to specified sectors and transactions, not generally to freight logistics. Electronic filing and payment requirements may apply based on taxpayer status and the relevant tax account.

Canada imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Zero-rating must be supported by contemporaneous records and the applicable statutory conditions. The CRA or Revenu Québec may reassess tax, deny input tax credits or refunds, assess interest, and impose applicable penalties for incorrect returns, failures to keep records, false statements, or gross negligence. Penalties may include statutory amounts under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec tax legislation; exposure depends on the conduct and provision applied. A refund or input tax credit obtained on an ineligible or unsupported basis may be recovered through reassessment, with interest and penalties where applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange control regime, no requirement for central bank approval for foreign direct investment, dividend repatriation, capital redemption, or loan servicing. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency. Profit remittances are subject only to normal withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and, where applicable, the Canada-Quebec reciprocal taxation framework, plus standard banking documentation such as a tax residency certificate, W-8 series forms, and audited financial statements supporting the source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion ratios in Canada. Exporters may lawfully retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts at domestic banks or abroad. Funds received into Canadian bank accounts settle through standard SWIFT, Lynx, EFT, or ACH rails without any forced conversion into Canadian dollars.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment flows, Low for primary treasury and escrow settlement. Foreign electronic money institutions and pure-play fintechs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada). They cannot access Canadian clearing systems (Lynx, Payments Canada ACH, Real-Time Rail) directly unless sponsored by a member bank. A foreign EMI operating in Quebec must be registered as a money services business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, when active in Quebec, licensed as a money services business under the Quebec Money-Services Businesses Act. For a capital escrow account, a Tier-1 Canadian bank is strongly preferred.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate income tax rate of 15% (computed as 38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction) plus Quebec provincial general corporate rate of 11.5%, producing a combined statutory headline rate of 26.5% for the 2024 taxation year. A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) that allocates the C$500,000 business limit may access the federal small business deduction (9% federal rate) and the Quebec SME reduced rate (3.2%), yielding a combined 12.2% rate on qualified active business income.
