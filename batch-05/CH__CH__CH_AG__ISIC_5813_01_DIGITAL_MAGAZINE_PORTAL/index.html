Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 is required, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up in cash before registration; the paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked Swiss bank account and released only after the Company obtains its Commercial Register excerpt; non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) require a licensed audit review and a verification report filed with the registration application.

Funding & unlock path: Occupational safety and health in any office premises must comply with the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and the Workplace Regulation (Verordnung 4 über Arbeits- und Schutz); a fire-safety certificate from the cantonal building insurance authority is required for commercial premises, and the employer must remit civil-defense-related social contributions to the cantonal compensation fund; no separate civil-defense clearance is required for a digital-only business operation.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 is required, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up in cash before registration; the paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked Swiss bank account and released only after the Company obtains its Commercial Register excerpt; non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) require a licensed audit review and a verification report filed with the registration application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/AFC – Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions), VAT Main Division; electronic filing portal 'Mein ESTV / Mon AFC' at www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal/communal tax offices assess corporate income tax and capital tax (e.g., Zug/Zurich/Bern Cantonal Tax Office).

E-government filing portal

The CH_AG receives a statutory business identifier (UID – Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) upon entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) issued by the Federal Statistical Office (BFS); VAT liability is then triggered by separate registration with the ESTV via the 'Mein ESTV' e-portal (VAT form 'Registrierung für die MwSt'). A foreign entity (non-resident supplier) applies for a Swiss VAT number (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx MWST) with a Tax Representative based in Switzerland, also through the same portal; registration is immediate from the first taxable turnover franc in the absence of a Swiss registered seat.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no US-style check-the-box regime; the AG is an opaque per se corporation subject to federal, cantonal, and communal income tax plus capital tax. Material statutory elections include: (a) VAT grouping election for Swiss-based affiliates (Art. 21 MWSTG); (b) participation relief election on dividend and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings (Beteiligungsabzug); (c) Swiss tax transparency (look-through) election only for certain Swiss SICAV/partnership structures, not for an AG; (d) hybrid mismatch neutralizing elections under the Swiss implementing rules for BEPS Action 2 (anti-hybrid provisions); (e) deemed-liquidation tax election upon migration of the AG out of Switzerland.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding investment income) for Swiss-resident businesses; no de minimis threshold for foreign suppliers of electronically supplied B2C digital services who must register from the first CHF; CHF 10,000 threshold for acquisition tax on imported services and supplies of goods by foreign suppliers under certain conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital magazine sales abroad qualify for the 0% export rate (or are exempt from Swiss VAT) where the place of supply is outside Switzerland: for B2B subscriptions and sponsored-media placements, the place of supply is the customer's place of business (Art. 18(2) MWSTG) — thus no Swiss VAT is charged; for B2C private subscribers residing abroad, the place of supply is their country of habitual residence, provided the digital content is effectively consumed abroad. Google AdSense/Stripe proceeds are treated as consideration for online marketing/royalty-like services supplied to non-resident digital platforms and qualify for 0% VAT subject to proof that the platform (e.g., Google Ireland) is outside Switzerland and the reverse-charge/recipient-accounting applies. Input VAT recovery is preserved when the transaction is substantiated as an export of services per Art. 23 MWSTG.

  • Swiss export customs declaration / bill of lading (CAD or NCTS) for any physical media shipments of magazines or USB storage devices
  • Subscriber/IP/geo-location foreign-residence report exported from the digital magazine platform (CMS logs), demonstrating consumption outside Switzerland
  • Payment gateway reconciliation reports (Stripe Adyen/AdSense payout summaries) showing offshore card acquirer BINs and remittance countries
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swiss bank SWIFT MT940-credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign proceeds within 180 days
  • License or advertising insertion orders with place-of-performance clauses and foreign customer due-diligence files

No real-time fiscal clearance/API clearance mandate exists in Switzerland; domestic VAT invoices are issued offline, but must satisfy statutory data requirements (Kwittung/TVA invoice requirements). Switzerland participates in the Peppol framework for B2G (supply to federal administration) e-invoicing on an opt-in basis; from 2026, digital e-invoicing standards are being phased in for B2B on a voluntary basis, with no digital tax stamping requirement.

In cases of erroneous or fraudulent export zero-rating, the ESTV retroactively assesses Swiss VAT (8.1% or applicable reduced rate 2.6% for qualifying electronic magazines) plus default interest at 5% p.a.; deliberate or reckless VAT evasion is sanctioned with a fine of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 96/97 MWSTG), and wilful tax fraud may lead to imprisonment. The reassessment statute of limitations is 5 years for ordinary corrections and 10 years for fraudulent suppression; clawback for wrongly deducted input VAT also triggers interest and penalties and bars the AG from future export zero-rating refunds pending resubstantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland grants full convertibility on both capital and current accounts under Art. 27 of the Swiss Federal Constitution and the Federal Act on Money and Payment Instruments. There are no exchange-control approvals, no prior licensing for foreign-currency purchases, and no restrictions on repatriation of share capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. The only practical constraints for the digital magazine portal are SECO and FINMA sanctions/embargo screening duties plus CRS/FATCA reporting; the AG may freely hold, invoice, and disburse in CHF, USD, EUR, or any other currency, making profit remittance legally frictionless.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender rules, statutory timelines, or conversion ratios for export proceeds. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts, converted at market rates, or netted internally. The sole residual obligation is threshold-based statistical reporting to the SNB under the National Bank Act where total foreign assets exceed CHF 100 million; digital advertising and subscription receipts below this threshold carry no reporting duty, and no conversion obligation applies to Stripe or AdSense inflows.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse), operating under the Federal Act on the National Bank and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF); no separate foreign-exchange-control authority exists, as exchange regulation is embedded in SNB monetary policy and statutory statistical-reporting powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as primary banking anchor and High as auxiliary pay-in/pay-out collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA EMIs, Lithuanian/Luxembourg e-money institutions) may lawfully serve the AG as pass-through settlement processors for Stripe and AdSense payouts, but they cannot issue the blocked-account confirmation (girierte Einzahlung / Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) that the Swiss Commercial Register requires as proof of the paid-up CHF 100,000 minimum share capital; that instrument must originate from a FINMA-licensed bank or FinTech with a Swiss establishment. FINMA guidance further prohibits deposit-taking offshore EMIs above CHF 100 million public funds absent a Swiss FinTech licence, and EMIs do not have direct access to Swiss SIC clearing, necessitating a licensed correspondent arrangement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law does not impose a fixed statutory Local File/Master File threshold; administrative practice expects a Local File when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 1 million and a Master File for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above CHF 500 million. Contemporaneous documentation must be available in all material intercompany transactions to avoid audit adjustments.

Economic substance scope: The Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations do not apply; however Swiss anti-abuse rules, substance-over-form case law, the OECD Multilateral Instrument beneficial ownership test, and the effective management doctrine impose substance requirements on a Swiss AG (physical office, trained personnel, substantive board meetings, local decision-making, and proper accounting records) for corporate residence, treaty access, and transfer pricing defense.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of evaded tax plus default interest, and fine up to three times the tax evaded under Art. 175 DBG; repeated or collusive conduct escalates to criminal tax prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a false return are personally exposed to fines and criminal sanctions; Swiss veil-piercing principles and Art. 754 OR permit recovery of corporate tax debts from wilful or negligent board members.
Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years and/or fine under Art. 186 DBG; loss of all statute-of-limitation defenses; full tax re-assessment and denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Executive board members are criminally liable as principals; civil restitution orders, seizure of personal assets, and travel bans may be imposed during proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Miscalculation

Statutory sanction: Tax audit adjustment increasing Swiss taxable income, 3.5% default interest, and administrative penalties; serious intentional misstatement leads to criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for incomplete transfer-pricing documentation face civil liability for additional taxes and audit costs; negligence under Art. 754 OR may also support third-party creditor claims.
Shell / Letterbox Company

Statutory sanction: Rebuttal of corporate seat/effective management; retroactive domicile shift abroad; denial of DTA relief; reassessment of hidden reserves and deemed dividends; administrative fines up to CHF 10,000 and possible dissolution proceedings.

Director personal liability: Under Swiss 'Durchgriff' doctrine, aggrieved tax authorities and creditors may pierce the corporate veil and pursue directors and shareholders personally; individuals may face travel bans and forced-liquidation cooperation orders.
AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine up to CHF 500,000 under GwG for entities; aggravated violations carry imprisonment up to five years; FINMA may strike financial-intermediary registrations; internal controls are deemed deficient.

Director personal liability: AML officers, compliance directors, and board members acting with wilful blindness face direct criminal conviction, personal fines, asset freezes, and civil restitution obligations toward creditors and the bankruptcy estate.
