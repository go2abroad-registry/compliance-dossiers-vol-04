Competent tax authority

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss-resident Aktiengesellschaft is fully opaque and subject to federal corporate income tax at an effective rate of approximately 7.83%, plus cantonal and communal profit taxes at a combined typical burden of 11.9% to 21.0% depending on the canton; the AG is also liable for cantonal capital tax; no pass-through to shareholders). Territorial_Scope: Swiss-resident companies are subject to worldwide taxation, but profits from foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt under applicable double-taxation treaties with full exemption method; a Swiss participation exemption (95% of qualifying dividends and capital gains) reduces the effective burden on foreign franchise subsidiaries and royalty-holding structures.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election is available; an AG is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. However, the following statutory elections are available: (i) VAT group election under Art. 21 of the Swiss VAT Act, allowing the Swiss franchise holding company and its domestic subsidiaries to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) participation exemption election for 95% of dividend income and capital gains from qualifying foreign and Swiss subsidiaries; (iii) deemed interest/notional-interest deduction on surplus equity under the 2019 Swiss tax reform; (iv) partial direct federal tax election for liquidation proceeds; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (implemented per OECD BEPS Action 2) are mandatory and deny deductions for payments that are deductible in Switzerland but not taxed in the recipient state, with no elective opt-out; (vi) both foreign and domestic entities may elect the small-business VAT procedure under CHF 100,000 turnover, limited to Swiss-resident suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover for Swiss-resident enterprises (Art. 10 VAT Act); non-resident suppliers with taxable supplies in Switzerland are liable from the first franc unless the recipient is liable and the reverse-charge mechanism applies; a CHF 150,000 threshold applies only to non-profit and charitable associations; voluntary registration is permitted above the threshold only for legitimate business purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods to destinations outside Switzerland is zero-rated under Art. 23(1) of the Swiss VAT Act provided the goods physically leave Swiss customs territory; services directly connected with exported goods (logistics, processing of bubble-tea syrups and packaging for foreign franchisees), transport services, and services of intermediaries acting in the name of the customer may be zero-rated. Franchise royalty and licensing services supplied to foreign franchisees are zero-rated if the service is deemed provided abroad (Art. 18(2) B2B territoriality rule) and the foreign franchisee is the taxable person receiving the benefit; intra-group service charges to a Swiss-registered central supply hub remain standard-rated at 8.1%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss e-dec/NCTS declaration) with the customs exit confirmation stamp or electronic proof of release into exportation
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, CIM/smgs, or Air Waybill evidencing physical transport of the goods out of Swiss territory
  • Commercial invoice with the exporter's UID/VAT number, the zero-rating reference, and a clear description of the exported goods
  • Franchise agreement, purchase order, delivery note, and inventory records linking the exported syrup batch to the overseas franchisee
  • Bank inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 confirmation demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds in a Swiss bank account

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transaction-control (CTC) system, or government digital stamping mandate. Since 2020, the QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory for all invoice payments using Swiss reference numbers (ISR) and is the de facto standard for B2B franchise supply chains; the format must embed the creditor UID/VAT number and QR-IBAN. XML-based electronic invoices (FacturX, Swiss eBill) are accepted for audit purposes, but there is no central tax authority API clearance database or automatic e-invoicing validation before issuance.

The ESTV conducts standard documentary audits of export zero-rating claims; if the exporter cannot substantiate physical export within the audit files, the VAT is reassessed at 8.1% standard rate plus interest from the supply date. Deliberate misdeclaration to obtain an unjustified VAT benefit constitutes tax evasion under Art. 96 of the VAT Act, punishable by a fine of up to four times the evaded tax, seizure of the clawback, and publication of the decision; grossly negligent errors trigger a fine of up to one-quarter of the evaded tax. Repeated violations place the taxpayer in the ESTV KONTEST high-risk database, suspending VAT refund privileges and triggering mandatory banking disclosures to the Federal Tax Administration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has no general exchange-control regime. The Swiss franc is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and royalty payments to non-residents are unrestricted, subject only to corporate law solvency requirements, treaty anti-abuse provisions, and Swiss withholding tax at 35% (recoverable or reduced under tax treaties). No authorization from the SNB is required for foreign direct investment, inbound equity injections, or outbound settlement. Capital flows are only subject to statistical reporting obligations under the Financial Statistics Ordinance (Finanzstatistikverordnung, FStatV) when cumulative cross-border payments reach CHF 1 million within a 30-day period, which is satisfied via the resident bank's reporting duty rather than by the corporate client.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio requiring mandatory surrender of export proceeds in Switzerland. Exporters are free to hold export receipts in foreign currency or convert them through any licensed bank. The only mandatory obligation is statistical in nature: movements exceeding CHF 1 million per counterparty per month must be reported to the SNB via the clearing bank using prescribed balance-of-payments codes. For the bubble tea franchise model, franchise royalty inflows from Swiss stores are classified as service receipts (royalty/licensing income) and are not subject to any conversion or repatriation restriction.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Eidgenössische Finanzmarktaufsicht FINMA

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for licensed Swiss fintechs and FINMA-licensed payment institutions; Low for offshore digital EMI operators that lack a FINMA licence or Swiss banking foothold. Swiss corporate treasury policy for a CH_AG franchise royalty and syrup supply business should prefer FINMA-supervised entities (e.g., licensed fintech banks under Banking Act Art. 1b) for cross-border collections and disbursements. Offshore EMIs (e.g., non-EU/EEA digital wallets, unlicensed payment aggregators) can legally be used as collection overlay in limited amounts, but they create AML concentration risk, may be viewed unfavourably by Swiss bankers, and cannot provide Swiss IBAN-based clearing for domestic store credit-card POS settlement without a banking licence or partnership with a licensed Swiss bank. For franchise royalty collection from global franchisees, SWIFT-based commercial bank rails remain the primary compliant option; EMIs are viable only as supplementary collection vehicles for low-value payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal headline rate, equal to 7.83% on a pre-tax basis; combined effective burden with cantonal and communal taxes approximately 11.6% in Zug (Canton of Zug) and 19.7% in Zurich, with a Swiss national average between 11% and 21% depending on the commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividend distributions, payable by the Swiss paying company before distribution; a 10-day quarantine period applies to dividend coupons after which refund liability lapses (VStG Art. 13).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax returns must generally be filed within 6 to 8 months after the close of the fiscal year (calendar-year taxpayers in most cantons face an initial deadline of 30 June or 30 September). Extensions are granted on reasoned request; failure to obtain an extension triggers reminder procedure and compulsory assessed taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no statutory Local File threshold, but FTA and cantonal practice require full transfer pricing documentation (Local File equivalent) for taxpayers with annual domestic turnover exceeding CHF 80 million or intra-group revenues exceeding CHF 20 million when raised in an audit; CbC reporting obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain a Gulf-state style Economic Substance Regulation, but the OECD MLI and the TRAF anti-abuse rules impose a substantive de facto presence requirement to obtain treaty benefits, participation relief and WHT refunds. The Swiss franchise royalty company must maintain: a physical registered office with a local director, employees with actual technical and managerial skills, board meetings held in Switzerland, independent decision-taking, adequate bank accounts and a documented commercial rationale for the intellectual property development and licensing. An AG that is merely a letterbox conduit without these attributes is treated as fiscally transparent or as a sham, with all foreign passive income re-attributed to the ultimate foreign shareholders and 35% WHT clawed back together with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 3 times the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated commercial tax fraud carries imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 186 DBG); unpaid tax plus statutory default interest at 4.5% p.a. is assessed within a certificate of debt (Rechtsöffnung) and enforceable through cantonal debt collection.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable as principals or accessories; the federal tax assessment is transposed into a personal claim against the responsible director for the evaded amount; Swiss criminal conviction triggers automatic asset seizure, possible travel restriction within the Schengen visa system, extradition exposure and disqualification from board membership for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of participation relief and of DTA treaty protection; re-qualification of accumulated profits as hidden distributions subject to 35% WHT; adjustment of taxable income by the FTA using a discretionary assessment, plus interest and penalties; forced dissolution of the company without substance is obtainable by the cantonal registration authority.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability to the tax administration under Art. 754 OR for unlawful reduction of assets; criminal proceedings for fiscal misrepresentation expose board members to fines and up to 3 years imprisonment; UBOs behind nominee directorships are exposed to criminal restraint orders, impoundment of Swiss real estate, and revocation of Swiss residence permits.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis StGB: imprisonment up to 3 years (5 years in aggravated cases of laundering a criminal organization's assets); corporate liability under Art. 102 StGB: fines up to CHF 5 million; administrative fines under the GwG/AMLA up to CHF 40,000 per violation; confiscation of laundered assets and proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually criminally accountable in their operational capacity; penalties are not insurable and are borne personally; non-resident directors face immediate arrest warrants by Swiss authorities, travel bans, international Red Notice requests, and extradition from Hong Kong or third states; civil liability under the Art. 102 StGB compliance provision pierces the corporate veil, allowing confiscation of personal bank accounts of the director held in Switzerland.
