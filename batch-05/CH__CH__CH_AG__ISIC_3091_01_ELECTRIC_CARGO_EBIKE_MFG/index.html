Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni), Schwanengasse 3, CH-3003 Bern; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; electronic VAT service channel: SukV / VTA-Electronic via estv.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

Procedure for a foreign-owned Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) to obtain statutory tax identifiers: (1) Notarize the incorporation before a Swiss public notary; minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 must be fully subscribed with at least CHF 50,000 paid in; statutory organs (board of directors and auditor) must be identified. (2) Submit the registration application to the Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt); upon entry, the Swiss Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) in the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX; the UID is the master tax and business identifier used for all federal, cantonal, and statistical purposes. (3) Apply for VAT registration with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration under Art. 67 of the Swiss VAT Act using the FTA registration form and the SukV electronic channel; the FTA assigns the VAT 'UID-Referenz' (MWST-Nummer), specifies the filing cycle (10.9 quarterly or annual), and issues instructions for electronic VAT return filing. (4) Register as an employer with the cantonal compensation office (AHV-Ausgleichskasse) to obtain the social security employer number. (5) The cantonal tax administration of the registered office automatically opens the cantonal and federal corporate tax account upon Commercial Register notification and dispatches the annual corporate tax return packages; no separate TIN application is required because the UID is the unique identifier. (6) A foreign-controlled AG with no effective domestic management is required to appoint a Swiss tax representative for VAT purposes; foreign shareholders and directors are permitted without domestic residency restrictions. The complete registration sequence typically takes 2 to 4 weeks after notarization.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no US-style check-the-box election and no pass-through classification for an Aktiengesellschaft; the AG is a separate corporate person taxable at the entity level on worldwide net income at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Available statutory elections and hybrid-mismatch tools: (a) VAT group taxation election (Art. 15 VAT Act) for economically integrated Swiss enterprises; (b) option under Art. 19 VAT Act to waive the exemption on real estate rental supplies in order to recover input VAT; (c) voluntary VAT registration for enterprises below the CHF 100,000 threshold (Art. 11 para 2 VAT Act); (d) participation deduction election (Art. 70 DBG and cantonal equivalents) eliminating income tax on dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings of at least 10%; (e) cantonal IP-box / patent-box regimes and the notional interest deduction (NID) where election is available; and (f) hybrid mismatch consequences transposing OECD BEPS Action 2 via general anti-abuse rules, without any elective hybrid-neutralizing mechanism. No transparent or mixed-equity status is available to a Swiss AG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) of taxable supplies and services rendered by the enterprise within Swiss customs territory, plus foreign digital/telecommunications services supplied by foreign enterprises to Swiss recipients (Art. 10 para 1 and Art. 11 of the Swiss Value Added Tax Act, SR 641.20).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The zero rate applies under Art. 23 para 1 of the Swiss VAT Act to goods, including electric cargo bikes and spare parts of ISIC 3091 / HS 8711.60 and 8714.10, when the goods are physically exported from Swiss customs territory by the supplier, by the foreign customer, or by a third party on the customer's instructions, and the export is declared to Swiss customs and proven by the customs declaration certificate (Ausfuhrschein / export proof). The zero rate also covers services strictly ancillary to exported goods, such as freight, logistics, pre-export inspection, and warranty repairs on goods intended for export. Services supplied to recipients with a place of residence, establishment, or habitual residence abroad are outside the Swiss VAT scope or zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules (Art. 8/9) and Art. 23 paras 2 and 3 VAT Act. To claim recovery of input VAT embedded in the manufacturing chain, the AG must possess the export documentation package and demonstrate that the goods have not been diverted into the Swiss domestic market; the FTA accepts the zero rate only where export turnover is declared in the periodic VAT return and the transaction value is consistent with the commercial invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Ausfuhrschein (official Swiss customs export certificate evidencing the zero-rated export)
  • Commercial Invoice with HS classification (HS 8711.60 / 8714.10) and reference to ISIC 3091 electric cargo bike manufacturing
  • Sales contract / purchase order naming the foreign buyer and the applicable Incoterms delivery clause
  • Transport document (CMR consignment note, air waybill, or carrier's certificate) demonstrating physical dispatch out of Swiss customs territory
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1/ATR or non-preferential certificate) when required by the destination country

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance model, no national digital stamping, and no invoice pre-approval API. The mandate is the issuance of invoices under Art. 26 of the Swiss VAT Act: every invoice must contain all mandatory particulars (full suppliers and buyer identity, supplier VAT/UID number, date, description of goods, quantity, tariff, consideration, VAT rate, and VAT amount) and must be issued no later than 3 months after the taxable supply. E-invoices are legally equivalent to paper; the dominant machine-readable standards are SIX eBill (pain.001/pain.002, CI-Plus) and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X. For invoices with a payment slip or payment reference, the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory. Federal public procurement suppliers must transmit structured electronic invoices under the OeRE Ordinance. All invoices, customs declarations, vouchers, and accounting records must be archived for 10 years under Art. 70 VAT Act; VAT returns must be filed through the FTA's electronic SukV/VTA-Electronic channel.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV) may audit export zero-rating claims for up to 10 years and typically cross-checks customs extraction data, transport records, inventory movement, and the banking trail of the export proceeds. If the export proof is missing, the goods are diverted into the Swiss domestic market, or the transaction is recharacterized through an undisclosed commissionnaire or back-to-back letter-of-comfort arrangement, the FTA assesses VAT at the standard 8.1% rate on the full consideration with statutory default interest (5% per annum), denial or clawback of input VAT deductions, and retrospective reassessment of prior tax periods. Administrative fines under Art. 55/56 of the VAT Act reach up to four times the tax evaded for intentional evasion and up to one time for gross negligence; criminal prosecution under Art. 59 VAT Act may result in fines or imprisonment for responsible officers. Abusive round-tripping or fabricated export documentation also triggers the publication of judgments and the activation of international administrative-assistance and customs cooperation mechanisms. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark represents the Swiss tax administration's arm's-length settlement norm for OEM and intercompany trade accounts; Switzerland maintains full free convertibility of the Swiss franc without an express statutory repatriation deadline.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland is in IMF Article VIII status. The CHF is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions, with no quantitative ceilings, no prior SNB licensing, no minimum holding periods, and no balance-of-payments registration. Profit remittances from CH_AG to foreign shareholders are unrestricted under exchange-control law; the only material friction is the 35% Swiss anticipatory (withholding) tax on dividends and assimilated distributions, which is subject to treaty reduction or elimination under double taxation agreements and to group-autonomous relief under the revised Swiss withholding tax rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Swiss law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds derived from electric cargo e-bike sales. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in CHF or foreign currency (EUR, USD, CNH) at Swiss or foreign banks; banks classify such flows for SNB statistical purposes and AML monitoring only.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank; French: Banque nationale suisse), the central bank with exclusive CHF note-issuance and monetary policy authority. Banking supervision and enforcement are delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) under the Swiss Banking Act of 1934 and the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), including delegated ordinances (AMLO-FINMA) on client due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal rate 8.5% of profit after tax, equivalent to 7.83% of pre-tax profit (Art. 68 DBG). With Zug cantonal and communal profit tax added, the aggregate effective rate for an ordinary manufacturing Aktiengesellschaft in the Canton of Zug is approximately 11.9%–14.6% depending on municipality and financing structure; this is below the Swiss national average of roughly 15%–17%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) levied on distributions of the Swiss AG to all shareholders, resident and non-resident alike (Art. 13 VStG). The AG is obliged to withhold and remit the tax before the dividend is paid; payment without withholding shifts the tax liability to the company and its board.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the close of the business year (Art. 125 para. 1 DBG). For a 31 December balance-sheet date, the return and annexes must be delivered to the Zug tax authority by 30 June of the following year; extensions are granted on written application until 30 September or later. If the return is not filed within the statutory period after a reminder, the authority may estimate the taxable base at its discretion (Art. 130 para. 2 DBG) and impose a late-filing fine.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no separate TP documentation statute, but the ESTV's Kreisschreiben Nr. 13 (12 June 2020) obliges taxpayers to apply the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and to prepare contemporaneous documentation. The ESTV expects a Local File where annual related-party transactions exceed approximately CHF 5 million (goods and services) or CHF 1 million (financing transactions); a Master File is expected for significant intra-group flows, and a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of CHF 900 million or more. The Zug-based e-bike manufacturer with cross-border component purchases and IP licences should prepare and file the transfer pricing documentation with the annual tax return once the thresholds are met, and earlier if any related-party transaction is material, to pre-empt recharacterisation of income.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Swiss ESR code exists, but economic substance is legally decisive in four fields. (i) Seat and management: under Art. 56 ZGB and Art. 643 OR, the AG must maintain a real registered office in Switzerland and its effective management (board meetings, strategic decisions) must occur in Switzerland; absence of these facts shifts the residence of the company and the taxing right abroad. (ii) Eligibility for tax incentives: the patent box, R&D super-deduction and NID require the taxpayer itself to perform the qualifying R&D or to finance the qualifying assets with substance performing a business purpose. (iii) Treaty entitlement: letterbox companies are denied the DTA benefits under the MLI principal purpose test and the specific anti-abuse clauses of the Swiss DTA network. (iv) Capital tax: the Zug capital tax assessments will verify that equity is genuinely employed in operating assets and not merely held to claim the NID. For a manufacturing AG with physical plant, production employees and board meetings in Zug, substance is established per se; using a nominee board, an empty registered address, or an artificial cash box would fail these tests and trigger reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Fines of 1–3 times the amount of the evaded federal tax (Art. 175 DBG); additional cantonal fines in Zug; default interest on the full underpaid amount; where false or forged documents are used to deceive the tax authority, criminal tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG carries imprisonment of up to 2 years, while VAT fraud under Art. 105 MWSTG carries imprisonment of up to 5 years. The entire reassessment may cover the full 10-year limitation period.

Director personal liability: Board members, the CEO and the responsible CFO face personal criminal liability; the corporate veil is pierced for deliberate evasion. Sanctions include personal fines, disgorgement of unlawful profits, asset seizures, travel bans, and imprisonment. In addition, the executives are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax and for the company's tax debt attributable to their breach of fiduciary duties (OR Art. 754).
Shell Company / Abusive Letterbox Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief: the 0% or 5% dividend/interest/royalty treaty rate is revoked and the full 35% anticipatory tax becomes finally exigible; the participation exemption is refused; cantonal and federal reassessment for up to 10 years; additional fines for negligent or intentional tax evasion of up to 3 times the additional tax; the commercial register may also initiate compulsory dissolution of the abusively structured entity.

Director personal liability: The signatories of the tax return are personally liable for the non-withheld 35% anticipatory tax under Art. 12 VStG. Directors who certify false substance or sign abusive rulings are exposed to criminal liability for false certification under Art. 251 StGB (imprisonment up to 3 years) and to civil restitution actions of the tax authority. Travel bans and freezing orders apply in pending investigations.
Hidden Profit Distribution and Non-Arm's Length Related-Party Flows (Verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung)

Statutory sanction: The excess amount is recharacterised as a deemed dividend: (i) the Swiss AG loses the deduction, (ii) the 35% anticipatory tax becomes payable on the deemed distribution, and (iii) interest and evasion penalties of 1–3 times the evaded tax under Art. 13 VStG apply. Transfer pricing corrections are simultaneously imposed at federal and Zug cantonal level, including capital tax recalculations.

Director personal liability: The board members are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid 35% withholding tax (Art. 12 VStG). Deliberate structuring of such payments constitutes disloyalty and criminal mismanagement under Art. 158 StGB, punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years; the shareholders are subject to civil restitution of the distributed amounts, and the executives to professional bans and asset seizure.
AML and Unlicensed Payment or Crypto-Facilitation Activity

Statutory sanction: Violation of the AMLA constitutes a criminal offence: fines of up to CHF 500,000 for negligence and up to CHF 1,000,000 plus imprisonment of up to 3 years for intentional breach (Art. 39–40 AMLA). FINMA may revoke the registration, order cessation of business, appoint an investigating agent, and in DNAB proceedings require the disgorgement of profits. The entity may be struck from the commercial register if it operates without the required licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer are personally subject to criminal prosecution under the AMLA and StGB Art. 165; FINMA bans the individuals from exercising management activities in the financial sector, imposes personal disgorgement, and may refer the matter to the criminal authorities with the consequence of travel bans, asset blocking and imprisonment up to 3 years. Directors who knowingly allow unlicensed crypto acceptance are additionally liable for all resulting damages under company law (OR Art. 754).
