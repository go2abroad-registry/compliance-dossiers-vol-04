Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under the QBCA; a corporation may be incorporated with no issued shares and may subsequently allot shares for cash, property, or promissory consideration in accordance with s. 28 QBCA.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: municipal fire prevention certificate (certificat de securite incendie) issued after inspection of warehouse exits, sprinklers, and storage classification; structural clearance: conformity with the Quebec Construction Code (Code de construction du Quebec, Chapter I — Building, Part 9) for storage mezzanines and racking loads; occupational clearance: CNESST workplace health check and first-aid/CPR equipment compliance under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under the QBCA; a corporation may be incorporated with no issued shares and may subsequently allot shares for cash, property, or promissory consideration in accordance with s. 28 QBCA.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — via My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec (RQ) for provincial QST and provincial corporate income tax filings

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity incorporates federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincially in Quebec under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA); apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Revenu Québec for a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST account; an agent or representative may file the application with supporting constitutional documents

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a resident taxpayer subject to Part I federal corporate income tax and Part IV refundable tax; Quebec levies a separate corporate income tax on the same taxable income with tax-on-income allocation — Foreign entity incorporates federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincially in Quebec under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA); apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Revenu Québec for a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST account; an agent or representative may file the application with supporting constitutional documents

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a resident taxpayer subject to Part I federal corporate income tax and Part IV refundable tax; Quebec levies a separate corporate income tax on the same taxable income with tax-on-income allocation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; statutory elections include the S.85 rollover election on asset transfers into a corporation, S.89(11) or 89(12) paid-up capital elections, and a fiscal year-end election; the Canada-US treaty contains hybrid-mismatch tie-breaker rules, and TCC (tax classification) may differ from US LLC rules for treaty purposes

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies per calendar quarter or over a single quarter for both GST/HST and QST; registration is voluntary below the threshold; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register regardless of the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or a commercial carrier directly to a destination outside Canada qualifies for 0% GST/HST and 0% QST under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act and Quebec's equivalent; services relating to exported goods or performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated if the recipient is not resident in Canada

  • B13A Export Declaration or exporter-reported electronic export report (if export value > CAD 2,000 or covered under permit)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill showing foreign destination
  • Proof of export date and destination (carrier's certificate, forwarder's confirmation)
  • Payment evidence (SWIFT MT103, bank advice, or equivalent showing foreign remittance)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; CRA accepts electronic invoices under EDI or PEPPOL interoperability standards on an opt-in basis; Revenu Québec requires registered businesses to retain transaction records in a legible electronic format providing an unbroken audit trail

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible, and Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely permitted, with no separate fiat approval for non-resident equity injections or debt instruments. Dividend and profit remittances are unrestricted from an FX perspective; only the Income Tax Act imposes a non-resident withholding tax on dividends and certain payments (generally 25%, with treaty-reduced rates).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement exists. Export receipts may be retained in CAD or invoicing currency and converted at any time at market rates. Statutory timeline: not applicable. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange-control policy coordinated by the federal Department of Finance and prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium only as a supplemental settlement conduit; Low as the sole primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada and must comply with FINTRAC anti-money-laundering obligations to hold, transmit or settle funds on behalf of a Canadian entity. Without such registration, an offshore EMI cannot lawfully serve as a principal clearing channel for this entity. A Canadian Schedule I bank remains structurally preferable for capital escrow and high-volume trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity from the subscriber is received as a SWIFT MT103 in CAD or USD through the beneficiary bank. The wire must carry the corporate legal name, the entity's CBCA/QBCA registration number, and a clear purpose detail such as 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CA_CORP'. Source-of-wealth evidence is furnished to the bank for any inward credit exceeding CAD 10,000. No exchange control permit is required. The receiving bank records the transaction under FINTRAC/OSFI expectations, and the company separately completes any corporate and tax filings triggered by the equity issuance.Open-account supplier settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 messages. Documentary trade flows are supported by commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600. Each outbound wire must identify the underlying invoice number, the Harmonized System commodity code for finished garments (e.g., 6204 series), and the precise beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC to avoid false hits. For USD cross-border payments, an ABA-correspondent SWIFT path is mandatory; ACH Direct is not a cross-border rail.
