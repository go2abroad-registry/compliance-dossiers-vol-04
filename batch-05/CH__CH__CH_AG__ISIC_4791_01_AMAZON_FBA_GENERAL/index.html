Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and e-filing portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/home/mehrwertsteuer.html; Swiss business identifier (UID) register administered by the Federal Statistical Office via https://www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Determine nexus: a foreign entity must register for Swiss VAT if its annual worldwide turnover reaches CHF 100,000 or if its taxable supplies in Switzerland exceed CHF 100,000 per calendar year (Art. 10 para 2(a) VAT Act, MWSTG). For Amazon FBA sellers without a Swiss establishment, the marketplace deemed-supplier rules from 1 January 2025 may in certain consignment-stock and low-value import scenarios shift the Swiss VAT liability to Amazon. Step 2 – Appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (Art. 67 MWSTG): a foreign company not resident in Switzerland and without a Swiss permanent establishment must appoint a domiciled representative in Switzerland (typically a fiduciary or tax adviser) who is jointly and severally liable for the VAT. Step 3 – Obtain a Swiss UID business identifier (format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX) for the foreign entity via the UID register; no Swiss commercial register (Handelsregister) entry is required for the foreign entity itself. Step 4 – File the VAT registration application with the Federal Tax Administration through the official VAT e-services portal, attaching: the UID confirmation, a home-country commercial register extract (with certified German/French/Italian translation if needed), a power of attorney granted to the Swiss fiscal representative, a description of the marketplace e-commerce activity (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 454110), and projected Swiss turnover. Step 5 – After approval, the FTA issues the VAT number consisting of the UID plus the 'MWST' suffix (e.g., CHE-123.456.789 MWST); the taxpayer then files periodic (usually quarterly) VAT returns electronically. The procedure typically takes two to four weeks; registration is retroactive to the date the obligation arose.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) is a standalone legal person subject to Swiss corporate income tax at the federal, cantonal and communal levels on its worldwide net profit (federal rate 8.5% on profit after federal income tax, equivalent to approximately 7.83% effective), with exemption-with-progression for profits attributable to foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. The AG has no pass-through eligibility; shareholders are separately taxed on dividends (partial taxation/participation relief). Territorial_Scope: Swiss resident companies are taxed on worldwide profits, but income from foreign PEs and foreign immovable property is exempt in Switzerland by treaty or domestic practice. Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable; Switzerland maintains no general tax-haven, free-zone or offshore entity regime for active e-commerce operations.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification system; legal form exclusively governs tax status. Available elections: (1) Optional VAT registration for small businesses with annual worldwide turnover below CHF 100,000 (Art. 11 MWSTG), often elected to recover input VAT. (2) Election between the effective input-VAT-deduction method and the flat-rate input-tax method (Saldosteuersatzverfahren, Art. 37 and 38 MWSTG), appropriate for sellers whose input tax is hard to determine precisely. (3) Option under Art. 22 MWSTG to subject otherwise-exempt supplies (e.g., proceeds from sale of immovable property or financial services) to VAT. (4) With respect to hybrid mismatches, Swiss domestic law contains no unilateral entity-election or check-the-box mechanisms; Switzerland addresses hybrid situations primarily through its double-tax-treaty network, the BEPS Multilateral Instrument and published FTA administrative practice, and no domestic 'hybrid election' is available in the Swiss VAT or corporate income tax laws.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 per calendar year of worldwide turnover (Art. 10 para 2(a) MWSTG); CHF 150,000 for non-profit and amateur-sport entities (Art. 10 para 2(d) MWSTG). Non-established foreign sellers must register when their taxable supplies to persons in Switzerland exceed CHF 100,000 p.a. From 1 January 2025, portal/marketplace operators (including Amazon) are deemed to make the underlying supply for Swiss VAT purposes in respect of (i) distance sales of imported goods with a value not exceeding CHF 150 (low-value consignment relief) and (ii) goods introduced into Switzerland under a consignment-stock scheme; the marketplace operator becomes the VAT debtor for those transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated Swiss VAT (0%) applies to physical exports of goods when the seller or a third party on its behalf exports the goods and the buyer obtains the power of disposal abroad (Art. 18 para 2 MWSTG), including Amazon FBA cross-border shipments to non-Swiss customers. Selected services are likewise zero-rated: services relating to movable tangible property that is exported, services relating to foreign immovable property, and B2B services performed abroad for foreign recipients. The supplier must prove the export by the customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / export data released by the Swiss customs system) or, for hand-carried exports, by the customs officer's written confirmation. Digital and e-services supplied to Swiss private consumers remain taxable at 8.1% (or 2.6% where the reduced rate applies) and cannot be zero-rated merely because the seller is foreign. Refund/input-VAT credit mechanisms apply on the Swiss VAT return where the seller is registered; non-registered foreign sellers may recover Swiss input VAT through the FTA's refund procedure for foreign businesses under Art. 107 MWSTG only, subject to reciprocity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) or electronic customs export data release
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (ocean, air, postal or combined transport proof to the non-Swiss consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the FBA shipment
  • Amazon seller central shipment records, inbound/outbound warehouse data and final delivery confirmation

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, no government pre-clearance of e-invoices and no mandatory invoice-by-invoice API clearance system. Structured e-invoicing (Peppol BIS 3.0 / B2G) is mandatory only for suppliers issuing invoices to the federal administration since 1 January 2020; B2B e-billing (Peppol network or eBill) remains voluntary. VAT returns must be filed electronically via the FTA's VAT portal (XML/file-transfer/API-based batch submission supported for large taxpayers) within 60 days after the end of the reporting period. Amazon FBA sellers must nonetheless retain full electronic order, dispatch, shipment-tracking and payment XML records sufficient for audit reconstruction.

Swiss law imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for Amazon FBA export proceeds; the 180-day figure is adopted as a documentary audit benchmark consistent with standard export payment terms and FTA practice in reviewing completed export transactions. The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may within ten years (five years in good-faith cases) retrospectively reassess incorrectly zero-rated supplies and claw back any input VAT previously credited; in cases of tax evasion the limitation period is ten years and may be indefinite for fraudulent concealment. On reassessment the taxpayer must pay the under-declared VAT plus default interest (administrative interest of 5% p.a. adjusted periodically) and, if input VAT was improperly credited, reimbursement of that credit. Administrative fines range up to the amount of the evaded tax in simple cases and up to four times the evaded amount in serious cases under Art. 96-101 MWSTG. Intentional VAT fraud is additionally prosecuted under Art. 14 of the Swiss Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR), carrying imprisonment of up to three years or a monetary penalty, and the responsible officers and the Swiss fiscal representative may be held jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland imposes no exchange controls; the Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. Dividend, profit, interest, and royalty remittances to non-residents are freely permitted without FX authorization, subject only to ordinary corporate tax, 35% dividend withholding tax (reduced under applicable double tax treaties), and standard bank compliance documentation. No capital account restrictions, retrocessional limitations, or inward investment approval regimes apply to inbound share capital or outbound distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Switzerland. Exporters may freely retain receipts in any foreign currency and maintain multi-currency accounts. The only binding obligation is incremental statistical reporting to the SNB under the Financial Statistics Ordinance (SR 951.1) for balance-of-payments compilation, with de minimis thresholds and no repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank), operating under the National Bank Act (SR 951.11) and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (SR 941.10), with supporting oversight by the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMIs and PSPs (e.g., Airwallex, Stripe, Wise) may be engaged as supplementary payment facilitators for marketplace collections, multi-currency conversion, and payout routing, provided they operate through a FINMA-regulated correspondent or partner bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the parent or investor with clear purpose coding (e.g., share capital contribution or equity injection), referencing the reserved share capital resolution of the board. No SNB approval or exchange control permit is required; banks request proof of source of funds, the share register, and the notarized capital increase deed. Multi-currency inbound wires may be credited to a CHF or foreign-currency account and converted intra-bank at commercial rates.
