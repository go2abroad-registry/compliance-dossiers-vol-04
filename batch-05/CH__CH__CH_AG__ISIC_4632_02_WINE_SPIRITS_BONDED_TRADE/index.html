Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV; Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC; Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni – ACF), official portal https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT Main Division and Swiss Business Identification Number (UID) Register via the Federal Statistical Office portal https://www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Switzerland must file Swiss tax registration Form 701 (Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuer) with the ESTV, either on paper or via secure SwissLogin e-services, before commencing the taxable activity; the Federal Statistical Office then allocates a Swiss Business Identification Number (UID, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX) which serves as the VAT ID; registration is effective from the date of economic activity commencement, and the cantonal tax authorities are notified separately for income and capital tax purposes if a permanent establishment exists, with the UID being the statutory reference in all VAT filings, invoices and customs declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/Société Anonyme) is taxed at entity level on worldwide profits if statutorily resident in Switzerland, comprising Federal direct tax (statutory rate 8.5%) plus cantonal and communal profits and capital taxes; companies without a Swiss registered office are taxed at entity level only on Swiss-source profits attributable to a Swiss permanent establishment; no pass-through or transparent classification exists for VAT or corporate income tax purposes. — A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Switzerland must file Swiss tax registration Form 701 (Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuer) with the ESTV, either on paper or via secure SwissLogin e-services, before commencing the taxable activity; the Federal Statistical Office then allocates a Swiss Business Identification Number (UID, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX) which serves as the VAT ID; registration is effective from the date of economic activity commencement, and the cantonal tax authorities are notified separately for income and capital tax purposes if a permanent establishment exists, with the UID being the statutory reference in all VAT filings, invoices and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/Société Anonyme) is taxed at entity level on worldwide profits if statutorily resident in Switzerland, comprising Federal direct tax (statutory rate 8.5%) plus cantonal and communal profits and capital taxes; companies without a Swiss registered office are taxed at entity level only on Swiss-source profits attributable to a Swiss permanent establishment; no pass-through or transparent classification exists for VAT or corporate income tax purposes.

Available election forms

Available elections: (i) VAT group taxation ('Organschaft' under Art. 77 MWSTG) covering financially, economically and organizationally integrated Swiss entities; (ii) voluntary VAT registration when annual taxable turnover is below the CHF 100,000 statutory threshold, permitting input VAT deduction; (iii) election between actual input VAT deduction and approved lump-sum/flat-rate input tax schemes (Arts. 28 and 29 MWSTG) for specific trades; (iv) no US check-the-box classification regime applies and hybrid mismatch outcomes follow the mandatory Swiss BEPS Action 2 implementing rules without elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover derived from taxable supplies and services in Switzerland and abroad (Art. 10(1) MWSTG); the threshold excludes exempt supplies, reverse-charge items and pure financing transactions; foreign-situs turnover is included when the business activity has a Swiss nexus through supplies of goods or services performed in Switzerland.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for zero-rating (exemption with input VAT deduction) under Art. 23(1)(1) MWSTG when goods are physically transported from Switzerland to a foreign destination or into a foreign customs territory, including removal from an authorized Swiss bonded warehouse, in which case the customs outbound declaration must prove the removal has not occurred for Swiss domestic consumption; wines and spirits may be zero-rated only if the bonded goods are directly re-exported under customs supervision, and services directly benefiting those exports are likewise zero-rated when rendered to the foreign purchaser (Art. 23(1)(2)-(3)).

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) processed in the Swiss electronic customs system e-dec or the EU NCTS export system with validated export reference number
  • Commercial invoice, purchase contract and bonded-transfer order identifying the foreign buyer and warehouse departure record (Lagerabmeldung)
  • Transport documents evidencing physical border-crossing (CMR waybill, bill of lading, air waybill or rail consignment note including foreign customs stamps or electronic arrival confirmation)
  • Bonded warehouse authorization number and warehouse outbound/seal record showing the goods left the customs warehouse under transit or re-export procedure
  • Inward foreign exchange remittance certificate (Swift wire or bonded-transfer settlement advice) from the Swiss payment bank proving settlement of the export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping exists in Switzerland. Invoicing must comply with Art. 26 MWSTG content requirements (seller and recipient identification incl. UID, date, type and scope of supply, consideration and VAT rate); the Swiss QR-bill (payment slip with QR code) is the mandatory settlement standard for domestic invoice payment traffic; electronic invoices in standardized XML or PDF format are accepted and retained for the statutory 10-year VAT record-keeping period.

On audit, missing, false, backdated or fraudulent export evidence triggers retroactive reclassification of the supply as taxable at 8.1%, with arrears plus default interest of 5% per annum (Art. 84 MWSTG) and disallowance of related input VAT deduction; intentional VAT evasion carries fines up to CHF 400,000, negligent evasion fines up to CHF 100,000 (Arts. 197-198 MWSTG), plus criminal seizure of unjust enrichment; recovery claims remain subject to a 5-year general assessment limitation, extended to 10 years where objective or intentional evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Switzerland imposes no exchange-control legislation, no capital-repatriation restrictions, and no prior approval requirement for inbound or outbound cross-border payments. Dividend and profit remittance to foreign shareholders is unrestricted provided corporate-law solvency requirements under Art. 680 CO are satisfied and Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is duly declared and paid. For the newly incorporated Aktiengesellschaft, the capital-injection minimum is CHF 100,000 total nominal capital with at least CHF 50,000 paid in, being 20% of the nominal value of each issued share, pursuant to Art. 632 CO.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The SNB does not impose surrender, conversion, repatriation timelines, or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds. The bonded-trade entity may retain EUR, GBP, and USD receipts in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. The sole regulatory duty is statistical: Swiss residents must declare cross-border payment transactions to the SNB when monthly payment totals exceed CHF 10 million or when the number of declared individual transactions exceeds 25, in accordance with the SNB Payment Transactions Statistics Ordinance.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank), Börsenstrasse 15, CH-8022 Zurich and Bundesplatz 1, CH-3003 Bern.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for FINMA-licensed Swiss fintechs; Low for foreign or offshore digital EMIs used as primary settlement accounts. Under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (Fintech Licence), entities such as Yapeal, Neon, and Alpian may accept client deposits up to CHF 1 million with a lighter authorisation, but none offers the full documentary trade, excise-settlement, and customs-guarantee suite required for a bonded wine and spirits wholesaler. The EU/UK EMI passport does not extend into Switzerland; a foreign EMI operating as a principal clearing account would constitute unlicensed deposit-taking under FINMA practice. Recommended architecture: a Swiss-licensed commercial bank as the sole settlement spine, with a FINMA-regulated payment-technology layer or white-label API used solely for treasury automation and FX execution.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (plus cantonal/communal direct taxes making the combined average effective rate generally 11%-21%; Zug and Zurich commonly at the lower end)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on all Swiss-source dividend distributions to shareholders, irrespective of residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the close of the commercial fiscal year; cantonal tax authorities may grant extensions of up to an additional six months upon written request.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit CHF statutory threshold for ordinary Local File/Master File; Swiss tax authorities expect contemporaneous arm's-length documentation whenever cross-border related-party transactions occur. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to Swiss parent or subsidiary members of MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of CHF 900,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of offshore regimes. However, a Swiss AG engaged in bonded wine/spirits trade must maintain real physical presence, local management, employee capacity, and decision-making power in Switzerland. Otherwise the tax authorities may deny treaty benefits under beneficial ownership/limitation-on-benefits clauses, reclassify transactions, or challenge the entity as a letter-box company under Swiss abuse-of-law principles and anti-strip rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 1.0-1.5 times the evaded federal tax plus accrued interest; cantonal penalties of similar magnitude; administrative default assessment and forfeiture of false storage records; in aggravated cases criminal proceedings under Swiss Criminal Code with fines and possible imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly approve false stock records are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax and penalties; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment (up to 3 years), confiscation of assets, and, for high-value cross-border schemes, travel bans and extradition matters.
Failure to Withhold Swiss WHT (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: The debtor is personally liable to the Swiss tax authority for the un-withheld 35%; recovery interest and procedural fines apply; criminal sanctions for intentional failure can include fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly authorise gross distributions without WHT can be personally assessed as joint debtors for the withheld amounts; conviction under tax criminal law can impose monetary penalties and prison sentences on responsible officers, not just the company.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax re-assessment at arm's-length prices, disallowance of expense deductions, reclassification of pricing differences as hidden dividends subject to 35% WHT, late interest, and penalties of 20%-40% of the tax adjustment; deliberate conduct escalates to evasion penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who set or approve non-arm's-length intercompany pricing with knowledge of tax consequence face personal joint and several liability for unpaid tax and WHT; criminal liability as accomplices to tax evasion may result in imprisonment and personal asset seizure.
Shell-Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief, retroactive refusal of Swiss WHT refunds, denial of tax residence status, reclassification of the entity as transparent, and recovery of all underpaid taxes plus interest and penalties; substantial economic taxes may be asserted on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors may be held personally liable for tax debts of a liquidated shell company under Swiss insolvency principles; criminal liability for fraudulent registration or false declarations can include imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from board positions.
Money Laundering and Lack of Due Diligence

Statutory sanction: Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis punishes money laundering with imprisonment up to 3 years (or up to 5 years in qualifying cases) and a fine; Art. 305ter penalises negligent failure to verify beneficial owners with a fine up to CHF 500,000; asset confiscation is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual or constructive knowledge of tainted funds face criminal conviction, extradition risk, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and exclusion from managing regulated entities; corporate veil-piercing exposes directors to civil restitution for amounts laundered through the AG.
Bonded Warehouse and Excise Duty Diversion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full excise duty and VAT, forfeiture of goods, administrative fines up to three times the evaded duty, and criminal prosecution for customs/consumption tax evasion; duty suspension status is revoked and the licence may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives of the licensed bonded warehouse operator are personally liable for the duty debt to the extent they directed or tolerated the diversion; criminal sentence can impose imprisonment for intentional smuggling or duty evasion, alongside personal confiscation of profits.
