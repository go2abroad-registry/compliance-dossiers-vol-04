Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the seat canton under the supervisory authority of the Federal Office of the Commercial Register (Eidgenössisches Amt für das Handelsregister, EAR); operational supervision by the cantonal Commercial Register Supervisory Commission.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Articles 621, 626-629, 632, 643, 716a-b; Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV); Federal Act on the Commercial Register (ANAG/HReg-PV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation (öffentliche Beurkundung) including the Articles of Association (Statuten), filed electronically by the notary with the Cantonal Commercial Register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value per share, but not less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, must be paid up in cash (Art. 632 OR); cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the company in formation at a Swiss bank and remain blocked until legal personality is acquired by entry in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense or structural clearance is required for a pure e-commerce head office; for leased office or warehouse space, the Company must obtain fire safety certification from the cantonal fire inspectorate, structural load approval for racked storage, and occupational-safety confirmation covering lighting, ventilation, sanitation, and emergency exits under the Swiss Labour Act (Arbeitsgesetz) and SUVA accident-prevention regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value per share, but not less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, must be paid up in cash (Art. 632 OR); cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the company in formation at a Swiss bank and remain blocked until legal personality is acquired by entry in the Commercial Register.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 4791 electronic retailing of pet supplies; if physical warehousing or pick-and-pack premises are deployed, the Cantonal Labour Inspectorate (Kantonales Arbeitsinspektorat), the municipal building authority (Bauamt), and the cantonal fire inspectorate become competent.

Category: No sector-specific operating license required for pure e-commerce retailing; a municipal business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) will suffice for any warehouse or office premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), Effingerstrasse 20, 3003 Bern, Switzerland; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and UID management via the ESTV eServices portal; cantonal corporate income tax administered by the cantonal tax administration of the registered office (e.g., Zug or Zürich).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned AG established in Switzerland: (i) incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft and register it in the commercial register (Handelsregister) of the canton of registered office; (ii) the Swiss Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the business identification number (UID, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX), which serves as the unified statutory TIN for tax, VAT, customs and statistics; (iii) file the VAT registration with the ESTV (form 744e for foreign-controlled entities; online via the ESTV ePortal) once annual net taxable turnover from Swiss supplies exceeds CHF 100,000; the application must include the notarized articles of incorporation, commercial register extract, certificate of capital paid in (minimum CHF 100,000, at least CHF 50,000 paid up), shareholding structure, proof of registered office and place of effective management, and, where no Swiss residence is maintained, designation of a Swiss tax representative (Art. 67 MWSTG); (iv) for foreign distance sellers of pet supplies into Switzerland without a Swiss registered office, the simplified ESTV distance-selling registration procedure applies once Swiss distance-sales turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, with the same UID/VAT number being used for customs clearance and electronic reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss AG is a separate resident tax subject under Art. 58 ff. of the Federal Tax Harmonization Act; no pass-through treatment is available to an AG; corporate income tax is imposed at federal (effective ~8.5% incl. surcharge), cantonal, and communal levels with combined effective rates of approximately 11.9% in Zug and 19.7% in Zürich; residence is established by statutory seat or place of effective management; worldwide income is taxable with territorial carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and Swiss real estate; for VAT, CH_AG is a taxable person (Art. 10 MWSTG) upon exceeding the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold; Switzerland has no tax-exempt zone applicable to e-commerce entities.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election. Applicable statutory elections for a CH_AG engaged in pet-supplies e-commerce: (i) VAT group registration (Art. 21(2) MWSTG) permitting related Swiss undertakings to be treated as one taxable person; (ii) net tax rate / Saldo method election (Art. 36a and 37 MWSTG) available to taxpayers with annual taxable turnover not exceeding CHF 5 million, applying a sectoral flat VAT rate (for retail trade of goods, currently 2.6% of turnover) in lieu of input VAT deduction; (iii) participation exemption (Art. 69 DBG and cantonal statutes) applicable to qualifying dividend and capital-gain income (10% substantial participation), applied as a statutory deduction; (iv) voluntary VAT registration from the first franc of turnover, and (v) election of the business fiscal year (calendar or 12-month period) as VAT settlement period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual net taxable turnover (excluding VAT) on Swiss supplies for Swiss-resident businesses (Art. 10(2)(a) MWSTG); for foreign distance sellers of goods into Switzerland (including e-commerce of pet supplies), a CHF 100,000 Swiss distance-sales turnover threshold applies (Art. 10(2)(b) MWSTG); voluntary registration is available from the first franc; marketplace platform operators may be deemed the supplier where the underlying foreign merchant has no Swiss UID and the platform controls the delivery or invoicing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign country are zero-rated under Art. 23(2)(1) MWSTG, provided the exporter proves by customs and transport documentation that the goods left Swiss territory and the consignee is located abroad. For the ISIC 4791 pet-supplies e-commerce model, the 0% rate applies to sales where CH_AG ships goods via postal or courier services to foreign customers, subject to the export proof bundle described below. Services related to export or foreign movable goods, such as storage, handling, and transport, may qualify for the 0% rate under Art. 23(1) MWSTG. Off-store border trade, deliveries to Swiss customs warehouses without onward export, and intragroup consignments of Swiss stock do not qualify. Swiss VAT law does not stipulate a fixed statutory FX repatriation deadline for zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark is used as the internal best-evidence period for completed export consideration and for retaining export documentation, consistent with accounting international practice.

  • Customs export declaration: electronic TARES/e-dec declaration number and the Swiss Customs export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / export confirmation) proving that the goods crossed the Swiss border
  • Transport document proving physical export: bill of lading (B/L), CMR waybill, air waybill, courier manifest, postal dispatch certificate, or multimodal transport document showing dispatch from Switzerland to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice / VAT zero-rated invoice issued by CH_AG stating the exported pet supplies, quantity, customs value, Incoterms, destination country, and recipient details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or banking evidence of foreign-currency payment for the exported goods, supporting the consideration and exchange-rate treatment
  • Proof of receipt abroad, such as a signed delivery note, consignee confirmation, or courier track-and-trace record evidencing final delivery to the foreign customer

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance API, mandatory continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime applies in Switzerland. Electronic invoices in PDF, XML, EDI, or other system-readable formats are recognized under Art. 26 MWSTG provided the statutory invoice content is present (name and address of supplier and recipient, date, type and quantity of goods, consideration, tax rate or VAT exemption notice). The Swiss market standard is the SIX eBill framework and the QR-bill payment slip, which is mandatory for paper payment invoices since 1 January 2021; the QR-bill replaces the traditional ISR payment slip and must contain the IBAN and QR code. B2G e-invoicing via the eBill platform is increasingly required by Swiss cantons and communes for public procurement but is not yet a universal federal mandate.

The ESTV treats zero-rating claims as high-risk and audits e-commerce exporters intensively. If the export documentation bundle is missing, incomplete, or internally inconsistent, the 0% rate is retrospectively denied, VAT is reassessed at the 8.1% standard rate, input VAT deductions are clawed back with default interest, and the exporter is exposed to evasion penalties. Intentional or attempted tax evasion triggers administrative fines of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 57 VStrR) and criminal monetary or custodial sanctions; the limitation period extends to ten years, or fifteen years in case of intentional evasion. False distance-selling filings and incorrect customs declarations may simultaneously be sanctioned by the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (Oberzolldirektion) under the Customs Penal Act, with additional penalties and publication of final decisions in severe cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates as a fully liberalised capital and current account economy; CHF is freely convertible and export/import payments face no conversion licensing, retrenchment restrictions, or profit-remittance ceilings. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are legally unrestricted; only the 35% Swiss withholding tax applies and may be reduced or reclaimed under the relevant double-taxation agreement. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft can therefore hold and remit worldwide proceeds without central-bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion deadlines, and no quota obligations. Export receipts from electronic pet-supplies retail may remain in the original currency, be retained in foreign bank accounts, or be converted into CHF at free-market rates. Swiss banks only report cross-border payment flows to the SNB for statistical balance-of-payments purposes, and no repatriation or conversion ratio is ever triggered.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) with regulatory oversight from the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking conduct; the Swiss State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) handles cross-border financial market policy. Switzerland maintains no exchange-control authority or approval mechanism for routine commercial payments.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax headline rate 8.5% on net after-tax profit, equal to approximately 7.83% effective on pre-tax profit. Combined federal and cantonal/municipal rates for a Zug-registered AG are approximately 14.3%–14.6%; a Zurich AG is approximately 21.1%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed by the Swiss AG, unless the non-resident shareholder qualifies for an exemption under a double taxation agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax law does not impose a separate mandatory Local File/Master File filing threshold in the annual corporate return. However, Swiss tax authorities apply OECD transfer pricing guidelines and require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation upon audit once cross-border related-party transactions reach commercial significance; a de facto threshold for maintaining a local file is annual related-party turnover or charges above CHF 1,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for Swiss-headquartered groups with consolidated group revenue of CHF 900 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nonetheless, Swiss courts and the FTA apply an abuse-of-law doctrine that requires genuine economic substance to respect the corporate form, grant DTA relief, and allow withholding tax refunds. A Swiss AG must maintain a real seat and effective management in Switzerland, have premises, a resident board or management, personnel, books, bank accounts and decision-making relevant to the online pet-supplies business. A letterbox company with no warehouse, no employees and no actual management substance is disregarded, its income reattributed to the real beneficial owner, and treaty/WHT benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Federal Act on Direct Tax (Art. 174–175 and Art. 186), the taxpayer must pay the evaded tax, default interest and a procedural fine up to CHF 10,000; evasion penalties range from 25% to 150% of the evaded tax. Where forged books, false certificates, or deceptive documents are used, the offense is tax fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to three years or a criminal fine, plus confiscation of illicitly gained assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFOs, and anyone who signed or prepared a false return are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax and associated penalties under Swiss company law (Art. 754 CO). Criminal tax fraud can lead to personal criminal fines, travel bans, asset freezes, and exclusion from serving as a board member.
Letterbox/Shelter Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The FTA and cantonal tax authorities may disregard the corporate entity under the Swiss abuse-of-law doctrine, reattribute the taxable income to the actual entrepreneur or foreign beneficiary, deny treaty benefits and withholding tax refunds, impose a 35% withholding tax on deemed dividends, and assess evasion penalties. The Commercial Register can also strike off the company for lack of legal organization under Art. 731b CO.

Director personal liability: Directors who lend their names to a letterbox AG are personally exposed to administrative fines, civil restitution, criminal liability for false declarations, travel restrictions, and Swiss mutual legal assistance proceedings; the corporate veil can be pierced where the shell is used to commit tax fraud or money laundering.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax administration will apply the OECD arm's length standard and adjust taxable income, disallow excessive deductions, recharacterize the excess as a constructive dividend subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax, and impose default interest plus tax evasion penalties of up to 150% of the additional assessed tax. Willful or falsified transfer pricing documentation can elevate the case to criminal tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length contracts knowing they are designed to evade Swiss tax are personally liable for the resulting tax deficit and penalties; in cross-border cases, directors may face asset freezes, mutual legal assistance, travel bans, and extradition or criminal prosecution in the foreign jurisdiction.
AML/Payment-Rails Abuse in E-Commerce

Statutory sanction: Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis (money laundering) and Art. 305ter (lack of financial diligence) impose imprisonment up to three years, raised to five years in aggravated cases, and fines up to CHF 500,000. Criminal assets are subject to confiscation under Art. 70 et seq.; payment providers may terminate merchant contracts, freeze funds, and report suspicious transactions to the Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (MROS).

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately ignore red flags, facilitate laundering through online payment rails, or personally benefit from disguised transactions face criminal prosecution, confiscation, civil damages to the company, professional disqualification, travel bans, and, in serious cases, extradition or mutual legal assistance.
