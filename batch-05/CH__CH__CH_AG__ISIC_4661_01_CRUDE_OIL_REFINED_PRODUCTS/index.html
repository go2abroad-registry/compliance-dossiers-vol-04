Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The AG requires minimum share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of each registered share, i.e. minimum CHF 20,000 in aggregate, must be paid in cash before registration, evidenced by a blocked-funds confirmation from a Swiss bank; no escrow account is otherwise separately imposed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and civil-defence clearance must be issued by the Geneva Cantonal Fire Prevention Service for the registered trade premises and any fuel-handling area; a structural integrity inspection is required for tank installations; occupational workplace clearance must be obtained from the Cantonal Labour Inspectorate and SUVA confirming compliance with Swiss occupational safety standards.

Paid-in capital rule: The AG requires minimum share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of each registered share, i.e. minimum CHF 20,000 in aggregate, must be paid in cash before registration, evidenced by a blocked-funds confirmation from a Swiss bank; no escrow account is otherwise separately imposed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; Official Portal: www.estv.admin.ch; e-services portal: www.estv.admin.ch/eservices

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity without a Swiss registered office or permanent establishment applies directly to the ESTV for Swiss VAT registration using the official form 'Registration as a taxable person (MWST/IVA/TVA)'. The application is filed electronically via the ESTV e-service or by post; the ESTV verifies the Unified Business Identification Number (UID - Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) register and issues a Swiss VAT/UID number bearing the suffix 'MWST'. The UID becomes legally effective upon notification; no personal appearance is required and a Swiss commercial register entry is not mandatory for a foreign entity without a Swiss branch. For a Swiss-incorporated Aktiengesellschaft (CH_AG), registration is initiated through the cantonal commercial register (Handelsregister), which allocates the UID; the UID is subsequently used for federal, cantonal, municipal and VAT filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — An Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) is a mandatory entity-level taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax on worldwide income with treaty relief, and to cantonal and communal profit and capital taxes that partially apply the territorial principle, exempting foreign permanent establishments and foreign immovable property. Pass-through treatment and US-style check-the-box classification are not available under Swiss law.

Available election forms

No general entity-classification election exists. Available elections: (1) VAT-group election under Art. 13 of the Swiss VAT Act for closely interlinked economic, financial and organisational units; (2) tax-neutral restructuring rollover under the Swiss Merger Act (Fusion Act); (3) election to create untaxed replacement reserves on reinvestment of capital gains; (4) option for tax-privileged reserves and hidden-reserve build-ups permitted by cantonal practice; (5) hybrid mismatch: Swiss law applies mandatory anti-hybrid rules aligned with BEPS/MLI; no elective de-hybridisation mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover for Swiss-established businesses (Art. 10 para. 1 of the Swiss VAT Act). Non-established foreign suppliers are registrable without a de minimis threshold when they perform taxable supplies in Switzerland; voluntary registration remains available to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (Art. 23 para. 1 of the Swiss VAT Act) when the supplier proves that the goods left Switzerland or were delivered to an overseas consignee. Marine bunker fuel and ship stores supplied to seagoing vessels are zero-rated under Art. 23 para. 2 of the VAT Act. Services connected with exported goods, including freight, logistics, brokerage, and physical wholesale brokerage of crude oil and refined products, qualify for the 0% rate where the place of supply is abroad under the B2B place-of-supply rules (Art. 18 of the VAT Act) or where the services are directly and economically attributable to exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Swiss customs declaration and NCTS/EU transit document where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Transport and insurance documents, tank-farm warehouse receipts and delivery records evidencing physical removal

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and predominantly Peppol BIS 3.0; public-sector invoices are processed via the Swiss e-Bill infrastructure and mandatory e-procurement channels. All Swiss-franc payment slips must display the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) as of 30 June 2020. Swiss VAT returns are filed electronically through the ESTV portal and are subject to post-audit review rather than pre-clearance.

Swiss customs and fiscal authorities retrospectively audit export substantiation; absent full and consistent proof, zero-rating is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply and the exporter must remit Swiss VAT at 8.1% plus default interest. Fraudulent claims, forged shipping documents or inflated quantities constitute criminal offences under the Swiss VAT Act (Art. 87 para. 2 and Art. 94), punishable by administrative fines up to CHF 100,000 and criminal money penalties up to three times the evaded tax, with a 10-year limitation period for VAT evasion. Swiss exchange-control law imposes no separate statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day timeline reflects the standard documentary credit and standby letter-of-credit (SL&C) cycle and banking due-diligence expectations in commodity trading, and a mere delay in repatriation does not by itself trigger a VAT clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible with no distinction between current account and capital account transactions, consistent with the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Current Invisible Operations and Capital Movements; profit remittance to foreign shareholders is unrestricted and requires no SNB approval; dividends distributed maintain the statutory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at 35% unless refundable under an applicable double tax treaty or mitigated by the Swiss participation relief, which does not affect convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts; the energy trading company may lawfully retain USD/EUR receivables and cash balances in Swiss or foreign bank accounts; only statistical monthly balance-of-payments reporting to the SNB is triggered for cross-border receivables and payables above the statutory threshold, without prior clearance or authorization.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, headquartered in Berne and Zurich, is the monetary authority and custodian of the Swiss franc (CHF); prudential AML governance and bank licensing supervision rest with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF); Switzerland does not operate any exchange-control department, and the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (BAZG) administers only customs and embargo integrity, not currency surrender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not Swiss licensed banks and cannot legally provide escrow, documentary credit, or commercial guarantee services for high-risk ISIC 4661 commodity trading; FINMA FinTech licences under Art. 1b of the Banking Act permit deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million only with plain, interest-free deposits and no securities custody or credit business, and the AML High_Risk_Special_License classification of an oil broker will trigger enhanced due diligence and almost certain rejection for core treasury clearing.

Wholesale petroleum trading, given its high-risk profile, typically undergoes a full audit as a matter of prudent governance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Federal statutory corporate income tax on net profit (effective Federal rate 7.83% after deductibility of the tax itself); combined Federal + cantonal + communal headline rate for a trading AG seated in Zug approximates 14.6% effective, with a national effective range of roughly 11.9% to 21.6% depending on the canton of registered seat.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé), which the distributing CH-AG must withhold and remit to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration within 30 days of the distribution date.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within 6 months after the close of the fiscal year (Art. 124 DBG); for a 31 December year-end the base deadline is 30 June of the following year, with canton-granted extensions generally available up to 12 months; assessed tax is payable within 30 days of the assessment notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Swiss TP Documentation Ordinance (VPD, SR 651.703, in force from tax year 2020): Local File is mandatory where the taxpayer's annual aggregate gross value of transactions with related parties exceeds CHF 1,000,000; Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CHF 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where the group's consolidated annual revenue reaches CHF 900,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the offshore sense, but the Swiss tax authorities apply the arm's length principle (Art. 58 DBG), statutory anti-abuse rules, and case law-based substance-over-form doctrines. A commodity trading or holding AG must demonstrate genuine economic activity: adequate qualified personnel, physical premises, independent risk management, and actual decision-making in Switzerland. Financing, IP-holding, and intermediary structures must maintain substance documentation (staff lists, board minutes, service agreements) to defend treaty access and avoid classification as a shell company; the LOB/PEP provisions of the Switzerland–PRC DTA deny treaty benefits where the enterprise's business is performed mainly outside its state of residence without genuine connection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion – Under-Declaration of Trading Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG) plus late interest at 4.5% p.a.; taxation by estimation at an increased rate; publication of final criminal judgments; in aggravated cases involving forgery, prosecution under Art. 186 DBG and Art. 146 StGB (fraud).

Director personal liability: Board members and authorized signatories who knowingly sign defective returns face personal criminal fines, confiscation of personal assets, imprisonment up to 3 years, civil restitution to the tax administration, and disqualification from board office; travel alerts may issue during mutual assistance proceedings.
Constructive Dividend / Hidden Profit Distribution & WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the excess amount as taxable profit and as a dividend subject to 35% WHT; the CH-AG is the debtor of the unwithheld tax; administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 and, in tax evasion cases, a criminal penalty up to three times the WHT evaded; interest accrues at 4.5% p.a.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for taxes withheld in fact but not remitted; individual criminal fines; personal seizure of assets; coercive imprisonment for failure to pay assessed penalties; per Art. 54 VStG rules, the creditors of the company may be joined in liability.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief under LOB/PEP, full Swiss taxation of attributed income, 35% WHT on deemed distributions, withdrawal of favorable tax rulings, and fines for misleading authorities; the company may be struck from the commercial register if found to lack legal substance.

Director personal liability: Directors and UBOs face personal assessments on reattributed income, personal fines for false attestation of substance, criminal proceedings for aiding tax evasion, asset freezes and travel restrictions ordered in international concordat proceedings, and civil liability to shareholders and creditors for false representations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Full discretionary TP adjustment by the tax administration with consequential tax and 4.5% p.a. interest; penalty fine up to CHF 10,000 for procedural non-compliance; for deliberately misleading TP documentation, penalty up to three times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Individual responsibility for signing the TP-D1 declaration; personal fines for false declarations; criminal exposure for certified tax advisers who knowingly prepared non-compliant documentation; director disqualification and personal restitution in group insolvency scenarios.
Anti-Money Laundering Failure – High-Risk Commodity Trading

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine for the financial intermediary or merchant up to CHF 500,000 under Art. 37 GwG; FINMA enforcement action including prohibition from trading; criminal conviction under Art. 305bis StGB for money laundering with imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years in qualified cases) and liability under Art. 305ter StGB for negligent failure to exercise due care; confiscation of profits.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers named in criminal proceedings face personal fines, imprisonment up to 5 years for qualified money laundering or criminal organization participation, travel bans and pre-trial asset freezes, personal civil liability for losses caused, and disqualification from serving on any Swiss board.
