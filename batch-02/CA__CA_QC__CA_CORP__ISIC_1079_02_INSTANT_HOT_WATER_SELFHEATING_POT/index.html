Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) portal; Revenu Québec – CLIC Revenu Québec registration and remittance portal for QST, GST/HST, and Quebec provincial corporate tax.

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint a Canadian resident authorized representative (registered agent or accountant) if no place of business yet in Canada. 2) Obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) together with certified corporate documents (foreign certificate of incorporation, directors' list, proof of identity). 3) Register with Revenu Québec for the QST and provincial corporate accounts under the single business registration mechanism (Form LM-1); this registration automatically includes the federal GST/HST account, eliminating separate CRA GST/HST enrolment. 4) Provide a Canadian financial institution account, and if non-resident, post security/deposit as demanded by tax authorities. 5) Obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and the CRA program accounts (GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxable person; resident in Canada due to incorporation; subject to worldwide income taxation under Income Tax Act, with foreign tax credit and treaty relief. It is not a pass-through entity and not tax-exempt.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification regime exists in Canada; legal incorporation status is conclusive. Available elections include the functional-currency election under s.261, section 216 election for non-residents to file on net basis, CCA elections, associated corporation elections, and GST/HST election under s.273 to treat supplies among closely related corporations as nil consideration hybrids. For hybrid mismatch, limited transitional timing elections exist under ss.18.4(4)-(5) where an imported mismatch payment is adjusted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supply revenue (per calendar quarter and preceding four quarters) for both GST/HST and QST small-supplier thresholds; mandatory registration for non-residents carrying on business in Canada, even below threshold, if they must collect tax or wish to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of self-heating instant meals and related goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act if sold to a non-resident for delivery outside Canada and actually exported pursuant to a contract at arm's length. Conditions: goods must leave Canada within a reasonable time (generally 60 days); supplier must retain documentary evidence constituting 'proof of export' per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4-3-3; zero-rating applies automatically to both GST and QST for export shipments.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration / CAED or ACI)
  • Bill of Lading (airway bill, ocean bill, or truck cargo control document with Canadian exit stamp)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit confirmation showing foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice clearly marked 'Export Sale', with basis of sale and origin details
  • Packing list describing the exothermic self-heating components and food products
  • Carrier certificate or proof of delivery to an overseas destination (if requested by CRA)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing exists in Canada or Quebec. CRA requires mandatory electronic filing and remittance for GST/HST from large registrants (annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million) and monthly filers; e-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the PEPPOL interoperability framework.

CRA and Revenu Québec deny zero-rating on audit when proof of export is incomplete; the supply is re-assessed as taxable at 14.975% (GST+QST), with arrears interest and penalties. Gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the understated tax, and criminal prosecution for fraud, apply. Reassessment period extends to four years normally, but is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraudulent documentation is shown, and secondary directors' liability may arise.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under the Currency Act and the repealed exchange-control framework; both current-account (trade settlements, royalties, dividends) and capital-account (equity injections, shareholder loans, M&A) transactions are unrestricted without central bank approval. Profit remittance barriers are tax-only: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, reduced to 5%-15% under applicable tax treaties) and zero-rated GST/QST treatment for exported goods under the ETA and the Quebec Taxation Act. No Section 116 clearance is required for routine dividend or loan-repayment remittances, but it may apply to dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds in foreign currency may be retained offshore or held in Canadian foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, CNY). FINTRAC regulations require reporting of cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction, but this is a surveillance record, not a convertibility constraint.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and lender-of-last-resort authority, with foreign exchange oversight shared by the Department of Finance Canada, OSFI (supervisory licensing), Global Affairs Canada (sanctions administration under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act), and FINTRAC (cross-border wire surveillance). Canada maintains no exchange-control licensing agency; banks perform sanctions and AML screening autonomously.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with structural constraints. Canada has no statutory EMI license; an offshore digital EMI cannot independently hold Canadian deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts. To clear CAD or USD flows it must partner with a Canadian FINTRAC-registered money services business and, in Quebec, obtain a licence under the Money-Services Businesses Act (CQLR c. M-30.1) from the Autorite des marches financiers. Practical use is therefore limited to low-value supplier payouts and FX conversion; the primary treasury anchor must remain a Schedule I bank account to satisfy escrow discipline, trade finance, and lender counterparty expectations.
