Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Ontario is mandatory for an Ontario corporation and must be a physical address in Ontario where documents can be served; a post-office box alone is insufficient. A federal corporation may maintain its registered office in the province stated in its articles and must comply with the Canada Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual-office provider may be used if it supplies a genuine service address, accepts service of process, maintains reliable records and permits timely access by the corporation and authorities. A mail-forwarding address or nominal address with no operational access is inadequate. For a single-family investment office holding only family assets, a virtual office may be legally acceptable but creates enhanced bank, custodian, tax-residency and substance scrutiny. If the entity provides investment management, portfolio management, dealing, advising, custody or services to third parties, securities registration, anti-money-laundering obligations and additional substantive premises requirements may arise.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required under the OBCA for a non-offering corporation. The articles may authorize an unlimited number of common shares; subscription consideration may be nominal, and shares are validly issued when the board resolves the allotment and receives consideration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax and GST/HST administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario tax policy; CRA My Business Account portal and ServiceOntario for provincial corporate registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business or receiving Canadian-source investment income must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. For non-resident corporations, registration is made by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or electronically through My Business Account / Represent a Client. The primary accounts established are RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST) and, if applicable, RP (payroll). An Ontario extra-provincial license and provincial account are obtained through ServiceOntario and the Ontario Ministry of Finance; Ontario corporate income tax is collected by the CRA under a federal-provincial administration agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canadian federal law; a Business Corporation is taxed as a corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act and is not eligible for pass-through or disregarded-entity election. Available elections are limited to statutory filings such as the capital dividend election under subsection 83(2), treaty-based reductions on Part XIII non-resident withholding, and fiscal-period-year-end elections for Canadian-controlled private corporations. Hybrid mismatch and other anti-deferral rules operate automatically without a taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or across the four preceding consecutive fiscal quarters, unless the person is a 'small supplier'. Non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada and exceeding the threshold must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act where the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada or exports them as commercial exports, with export documentation retained. Services must be supplied to a non-resident and be performed wholly outside Canada, or be directly connected to real estate located outside Canada. For the Single-Family-Office scope, private banking custody, securities administration and investment advisory services are usually exempt financial services rather than zero-rated; such financial services do not attract GST/HST but also deny recovery of input tax credits on related costs.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), including B13A Canada Export Declaration or Canada Customs Automated Data Exchange record
  • Transport documentation (original Bill of Lading or airway bill) showing the goods left Canada and were received abroad
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order evidencing the consideration paid by the non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire confirmation showing foreign proceeds entering the exporter's Canadian bank account

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed using CRA-approved software, GST/HST NETFILE, or My Business Account on a monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle; payments must be remitted electronically. There is no province-specific e-invoicing mandate in Ontario.

CRA may challenge export zero-rating claims during a four-year normal reassessment period; in fraud or wilful misrepresentation, the window is unlimited. Adverse reassessments restore tax, claw back input tax credits, and add interest. Gross negligence penalties of 25% of the misstated net tax and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act can apply for false export documentation or counterfeited proof of export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, with no exchange-control legislation, no surcharge, and no ministerial authorization requirement for dividend, interest, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance from an Ontario business corporation to a non-resident shareholder is subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (general rate 25%, reducible under applicable income tax treaties, e.g., 5-15% for corporate shareholders), payable via the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) NR4 remittance process. There are no capital-control quotas, di minimis transfer caps, or compulsory FX conversion rules; however, all cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution, and the client must provide source-of-funds and legal-purpose documentation

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export proceeds and investment income may be retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely transferable currency at the discretion of the entity. Obligations are limited to FINTRAC record-keeping (5-year retention) and reporting thresholds: large cash transactions and cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000+ in a 24-hour window, plus reporting of any suspicious transaction regardless of amount. No central bank surrender, repatriation deadline, or foreign-income conversion ceiling applies to the Single Family Office's portfolio receipts

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with statutory foreign-exchange policy and sanctions administration allocated to the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of authorized banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT transaction reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force since 2024), payment service providers operating in Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada; a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer does not permit the Canadian entity to obtain LVTS-class settlement, escrow-grade fund segregation, or investment-grade creditworthiness. For a Single Family Office with medium-risk/enhanced due diligence status, a foreign EMI may be used only as a secondary low-value disbursement channel, not as a primary treasury or capital-escrow rail. Canadian MSB/EMI substitutes (e.g., WS/Wealthsimple Business, Koho, or affiliated provincial-licensed PSPs) lack the risk absorption capacity for cross-border SFO holdings. The mandatory Bank of Canada registration, FINTRAC EFT reporting, and provincial MSB licensing (where applicable) render pure digital-EMI onboarding legally viable but operationally inferior to Schedule I banks

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38.67% combined federal and Ontario rate on contributed investment income (federal Part I 28.67% after 10% provincial abatement plus Ontario corporate rate 10%); 25.0% combined general rate on active business income (federal 15% + Ontario 10%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate of 25% on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident individual or corporation, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous penalty-protection documentation required where cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate per taxation year; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue over CAD 750,000,000; substantiation of services (Form RC4564) is required for management-fee allocations

Economic substance scope: Canada applies economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(4)), the sham doctrine, and the 'Farner/single-purpose' analysis. A single-family office must demonstrate real commercial activity: employee/business decisions, independent board control, custody outside the family, and a coherent risk structure. CRA can disregard transactions without significant economic effect that frustrate the purpose of the Income Tax Act, and the SFO must maintain contemporaneous board minutes, service agreements, and asset-management evidence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees and senior officers are criminally liable as parties to the evasion; conviction triggers immediate travel bans (Interpol red notice), asset forfeiture under dual criminality, and civil restitution to the Crown.
Gross Negligence and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax (subsection 163(2)); CRA may reassess returns beyond the normal reassessment period (3 years) for misrepresentation (6 years)

Director personal liability: Sections 163.1 and 227.1 personal liability for unpaid amounts and penalties arising from negligence; directors may be sued for corporate breaches and barred from professional indemnity cover.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding

Statutory sanction: Part XIII arrears plus 100% penalty on the unremitted amount (where the amount was deducted but not remitted — section 227); interest compounded daily at prescribed rates

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII, payroll, and GST/HST amounts under subsection 227.1(1); CRA may issue director's liability certificate, enforce against personal assets, impose travel/liquidation restrictions, and prosecute the director for failure to deduct (section 227.1(4)).
Transfer Pricing Contravention

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount (subsection 247(3)), full reassessment at fair market value, plus accumulated interest; no offset for the penalty if another country also adjusts

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise non-arm's length pricing without economic analysis incur personal exposure for the penalty where the corporation is insolvent; CRA applies the corporate veil exceptionally in fraud or imprudent monitoring cases.
AML/PCMLTFA (Money-Laundering) Non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under FINTRAC up to CAD 1,000,000 per infringement for corporations and CAD 1,166,000 for certain categories; criminal penalties for knowingly placing proceeds of crime — imprisonment up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who deliberately fail to register the MSB, file STRs, or implement the compliance program face disqualification orders, personal AMPs, and criminal prosecution for aiding laundering; the 'Designated Individual' of the SFO can be criminally liable for omissions.
