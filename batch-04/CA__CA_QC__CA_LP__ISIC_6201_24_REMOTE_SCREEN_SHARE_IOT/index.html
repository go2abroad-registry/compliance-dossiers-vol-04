Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online service, My Business Account, and Represent a Client portals; and Revenu Quebec, including the clicSÉQUR and online tax-services portals for Quebec tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec limited partnership or foreign partnership carrying on business, earning income, employing personnel, or having a Canadian tax filing obligation should obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or the CRA non-resident registration process. The entity must add the applicable program accounts, including GST/HST (RT), payroll deductions (RP), and, where applicable, corporation income tax or information-reporting accounts. A partnership may also have to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return, generally using the partnership's BN. For Quebec operations, the entity should register with Revenu Quebec for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), payroll source deductions, and other applicable accounts. Registration is generally completed using the entity's formation or partnership documents, principal business and Canadian activity details, responsible-person information, ownership and partner information, authorization documents, and non-resident contact details. A non-resident supplier may use the CRA simplified GST/HST registration regime for qualifying digital-economy supplies to Canadian consumers, but that regime does not generally replace full GST/HST registration where the supplier is carrying on business in Canada or making supplies requiring the regular regime. A QST registration may similarly be required under Quebec's specified digital economy rules or the general QST regime. A Canadian BN, GST/HST account, and QST number are separate from a Quebec enterprise number issued by the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: partnership income or loss is calculated at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and Canadian tax rules, with partners generally taxed on their allocated shares. Canada has no general US-style check-the-box election that converts a Canadian limited partnership into a corporation or disregarded entity for Canadian tax purposes. A partnership may use specific statutory elections where conditions are met, including rollover elections under section 97 of the Income Tax Act for qualifying transfers of property to a partnership, partnership-level and partner-level elections under applicable tax provisions, and elections concerning the tax treatment of certain financial or depreciable property. A qualifying partnership may also be eligible for limited partnership loss rules, foreign-affiliate and treaty-based positions, or other specialized regimes, subject to statutory conditions. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or alter income inclusions for arrangements involving foreign entities or instruments treated inconsistently across jurisdictions; these rules are not elective entity-classification elections. US tax classification is determined under US law and does not change the Canadian legal or default tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, determined under the applicable GST/HST small-supplier rules, generally measured over the relevant preceding or immediately following four-quarter period. QST registration generally applies at the corresponding CAD 30,000 threshold for taxable supplies, subject to Quebec rules and specified digital-economy provisions. The threshold is not a general exemption where the supplier voluntarily registers or is otherwise required to register because of Canadian business activities, non-resident digital-economy rules, or other statutory nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST purposes, exported goods may generally be zero-rated where they are supplied for export and the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including export from Canada within the applicable time and evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to a non-resident may generally be zero-rated where the recipient is non-resident and the service is not excluded by the place-of-supply and exported-service exceptions. Software access, remote-support software, cloud functionality, and telemetry services require an analysis of the legal nature of the supply, customer residence, user location, performance location, contractual rights, and whether the service relates to real property, tangible personal property, or services performed in Canada. A non-resident customer alone does not automatically establish zero-rating. Supplies made to or for a Canadian resident, or supplies used or consumed in Canada, may be taxable. QST zero-rating and exemption rules must be analyzed separately under Quebec legislation. Registered businesses may generally claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST paid and input tax refunds or credits for eligible QST paid, subject to documentary support, business-use requirements, restrictions, and filing compliance. A non-resident digital supplier using a simplified registration generally has restricted input-tax recovery compared with a registrant under the regular regime.

Canada and Quebec do not generally impose a universal real-time electronic invoicing or pre-clearance mandate for ordinary B2B or B2C software supplies. GST/QST invoices must contain prescribed information when required, including supplier identity and registration numbers, supply description, consideration, applicable tax rates, and tax amounts. Electronic invoices and records are generally acceptable if they preserve reliability, integrity, accessibility, and auditability. CRA and Revenu Quebec may require electronic filing for specified returns, remittances, information returns, and payroll obligations, but these requirements do not constitute real-time fiscal clearance. A supplier using Stripe, Paddle, or another merchant-of-record arrangement must establish whether it or the merchant is the legal supplier, importer, or tax collector and must retain transaction, customer-location, tax-rate, exemption, refund, and payment records. No general digital tax stamp is required for the stated software activity.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or mandatory 180-day remittance deadline for export proceeds. CRA and Revenu Quebec may audit the transaction, deny zero-rating, reassess GST/HST or QST, assess interest, and recover improperly claimed input tax credits or refunds where export, customer-residency, place-of-supply, or documentary conditions are not proven. Tax may be reassessed for the statutory reassessment period and for an unlimited period in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud. False statements and omissions may attract statutory penalties, including gross-negligence or other prescribed penalties, in addition to tax and interest. Failure to register, collect, remit, file, or maintain records may result in separate administrative penalties. Deliberate tax evasion, fraud, obstruction, or use of false documents may result in prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. A fraudulent zero-rating claim may also trigger denial of refunds, recovery of amounts already paid, audit expansion to related supplies, and partner-level or director-level exposure where applicable under Canadian law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts without surrender, conversion, or remittance-approval requirements. Profit repatriation to foreign limited partners is unrestricted at the exchange-control level: a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its allocated partnership income, and routine distributions of allocated income are not subject to Part XIII gross withholding. However, payments of interest, royalties, rent, or management fees to non-resident partners attract 25% Part XIII withholding subject to treaty relief. Practical compliance gates are FATCA/CRS self-certification, FINTRAC transaction reports for cash or large virtual currency transactions at or above CAD 10,000, and Form T1135 foreign property reporting by resident partners whose specified foreign property cost exceeds CAD 100,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or forced repatriation of export proceeds. Software export receipts for remote desktop access and unattended IoT telemetry licensing are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes as zero-rated supplies of digital goods and services. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained, swept, and hedged freely; the only obligations are ordinary CRA and Revenu Québec record-keeping, accurate reporting of partnership income, and foreign-source disclosure where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (monetary authority and retail payment supervision), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority because the historical Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail; NOT acceptable as the primary banking layer. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury embedded accounts can legally support multi-currency collections and vendor payouts, but any provider serving Canadian users must be registered or be in the transitional registration window under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. EMIs do not provide CDIC deposit insurance, credit facilities, or Quebec provincial regulatory comfort. The LP must therefore maintain its principal operating and capital accounts with a Schedule I bank and use EMIs only to optimize cross-currency conversion, merchant payouts, and last-mile vendor payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) corporate tax rate; not levied at LP entity level due to flow-through taxation

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; for distributions of a limited partnership, profit allocations to non-resident partners are not dividends. Such allocations are subject to Part I net-basis taxation at graduated rates, and no Part XIII withholding tax applies to active business income of the partnership if the non-resident partner files a Canadian return and is entitled to treaty protection.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reporting is mandatory for cross-border non-arm's-length transactions if the aggregate fair market value exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Comprehensive Canadian transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is expected by CRA where controlled transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; a Master File and Country-by-Country report are generally required once consolidated group revenue reaches CAD 750,000,000 (roughly aligned with the OECD EUR 750 million threshold).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf-cooperation style; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) and the statutory transfer-pricing rules under section 247 require that transactions be commercially rational and that profits be allocated according to economic substance, functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed. A Quebec limited partnership with no employees, no office, and no business activity in Quebec will be disregarded or re-characterized; the CRA may assess the non-resident general partner directly and deny the flow-through status. For remote screen share and IoT software, the partnership must demonstrate real personnel, decision-making, and support operations in Quebec, or the income source and beneficial ownership will be re-attributed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act for tax evasion carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment; civil penalty under subsection 163(2) is equal to 50% of the gross understatement (reduced to 25% where the filing was made based on adequate disclosure). Revenu Quebec imposes the same penalties for provincial evasion, with additional late-interest accumulation.

Director personal liability: General partners of the LP are personally and jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax and penalties; officers/directors of a corporate general partner may be assessed personally under subsection 226(1) for unremitted amounts and can face travel restrictions, asset-freezing orders, and restraint orders in serious cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR, the CRA may deny tax benefits and reassess the partnership for the full amount plus interest; a penalty under subsection 245(4) of 25% of the tax benefit is applied unless a reasonable justification is shown. Revenu Quebec may disallow the LP's Quebec nexus and impose a 100% diligence penalty for failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be subject to a court order for restitution if they authorized the sham arrangement; personal liability can arise under contract law, corporate law, or the Quebec Tax Administration Act for allowing the partnership to operate without honest commercial purpose. In egregious cases, an Order for a travel permit may be required before an individual may leave Canada.
Anti-Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) imposes administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for an entity; a criminal offence can lead to a fine of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for a person; directors/officers can be charged independently for failing to maintain an AML compliance program.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers/directors can be criminally prosecuted under the PCMLTFA for knowingly directing, authorizing, or acquiescing in the offence; they may be subject to a personal administrative penalty of up to CAD 500,000 and a court-ordered travel ban if they attempt to leave Canada while charges are pending.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The CRA will adjust the consideration to arm's length and assess taxes on the upward profit adjustment; the penalty under subsection 247(5) is 10% of the amount of gross adjustment (without reference to net tax, and without deduction for offsetting adjustments) for each year in which the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts at documentation. Interest is compounded daily and no penalty relief is available for the statutory 10% unless the taxpayer proves due diligence.

Director personal liability: The directors, officers, and agents of the corporate general partner who knowingly or negligently caused the failure to document are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties to the extent they participated in the decision; they may be assessed personally under Quebec's civil lift-the-corporate-veil doctrine if the LP is insolvent and the transaction was a sham.
