Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating currency, and no exchange control filings are required for inbound foreign direct investment, non-resident limited partner capital contributions, or outbound profit and partnership distributions. Remittance frictions are limited to tax compliance: Part XIII withholding tax on certain Canadian-source payments to non-resident partners (e.g., interest, royalties, management fees) and Part XIV branch tax on unremitted Canadian business income, subject to treaty relief under the Canada–US Tax Convention and other bilateral treaties. Quebec imposes no provincial exchange controls but requires the limited partnership to maintain active registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and to satisfy Revenu Québec tax registration and reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export receipts. An air cargo charter broker may freely retain proceeds in foreign currency. Mandatory reporting obligations that apply are limited to: (i) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reports for each incoming or outgoing wire transfer of C$10,000 or more; (ii) International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) data collection by financial institutions for balance-of-payments statistics; and (iii) Canada Revenue Agency foreign-asset and foreign-income reporting, including Form T1135 where foreign account balances exceed prescribed thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transfer monitoring by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No standalone foreign exchange administration exists because Canada maintains complete current and capital account convertibility with no exchange control approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury banking rail and Medium-to-High as a supplementary payments channel. An offshore digital electronic money institution (EMI) may not take deposits or carry on banking business in Canada unless licensed as a Schedule I or Schedule II bank under the Bank Act. Offshore EMIs may operate only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, in Quebec, may require additional registration or licensing from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for money-services and currency-exchange activities. An offshore EMI can provide payment initiation, digital wallet settlement, and commercial card services through a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank partner, but it cannot offer insured CAD deposit accounts or direct access to Lynx RTGS. For the ISIC 5229 air cargo charter broker model, the viable fintech structure is a combined treasury architecture: a Canadian Schedule I bank as the settlement and escrow backbone, with a FINTRAC-registered EMI used for merchant payments, fuel-card settlement, and small-value cross-border disbursements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident partners concentrated in tax-haven or high-risk jurisdictions in the beneficial-ownership cascade

Root cause: Non-resident partners concentrated in tax-haven or high-risk jurisdictions in the beneficial-ownership cascade

Defense evidence: Certified true copies of passports; tax residency certificates issued by the relevant tax authorities; a complete beneficial-ownership chain table depicting each general and limited partner through to the ultimate natural persons; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statements; the signed limited partnership agreement; and written legal opinions from the foreign counsel of each non-resident partner.
Third-party payments or channeled funds that appear inconsistent with the air-cargo charter brokerage revenue model

Root cause: Third-party payments or channeled funds that appear inconsistent with the air-cargo charter brokerage revenue model

Defense evidence: Signed aircraft charter agreements, brokerage service contracts, proforma invoices, air waybills and bills of lading, operator invoices, fuel-supplier invoices, and ground-handling invoices; a transaction-velocity narrative explaining expected payment flows; and a rolling 12-month business plan with volumes and counterparty names.
Use of a virtual office, registered-agent address, or bare post office box as the partnership's principal place of business

Root cause: Use of a virtual office, registered-agent address, or bare post office box as the partnership's principal place of business

Defense evidence: An executed commercial lease in Quebec bearing the LP's legal name; utility and internet-service bills; payroll and employee records; the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ) registration certificate; and any applicable provincial transport or freight-forwarding licence supporting the ISIC 5229 scope.
Capital contribution sourced from a corporate partner active in cryptocurrency, gambling, or other high-risk sectors

Root cause: Capital contribution sourced from a corporate partner active in cryptocurrency, gambling, or other high-risk sectors

Defense evidence: Bank statements and audited financial statements of the contributing partner; a written legal opinion confirming the absence of prohibited sources of funds; a full transaction-history reconciliation; flow-of-funds charts demonstrating the original economic origin of the contribution; and executed undertakings to maintain ongoing compliance with the PCMLTFA and Quebec AML directives.
Unusually large or unexplained pre-revenue deposits deposited before the first commercial charter contract is executed

Root cause: Unusually large or unexplained pre-revenue deposits deposited before the first commercial charter contract is executed

Defense evidence: Notarized partners' resolutions documenting the capital call and the escrow-purpose designation; the executed escrow agreement and term sheet; a capitalization table; the LP's audited or reviewed financial statements; and a forward 12-month treasury projection validated by the LP's external accountant and tax advisor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the capital contribution as a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the contributing partner's remitting bank to the LP's Canadian bank account. In the remittance information field, identify the payment as 'Capital contribution to limited partnership interest in CA_LP' and, where the sending or receiving bank requires a regulatory purpose code, assign the direct-investment / equity-participation classification consistent with that bank's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) reporting parameters. For CAD-denominated receipts, the domestic leg settles with finality through Lynx, Canada's real-time gross settlement system; USD and EUR receipts are cleared through Fedwire/CHIPS or TARGET2 respectively via the receiving Canadian bank's correspondent network. Pre-funding verification requirements: the LP must hold an active Quebec enterprise registration (NEQ); the partnership agreement and certificate of LP registration must be current; the bank mandate with authorized signatories must be executed; and source-of-funds documentation for the contribution must be pre-positioned with the bank's financial-crimes unit. The receiving bank will credit the Capital Escrow Account until the capital call subscription documentation, partnership register, and supporting KYC deliverables are fully validated.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for cross-border disbursements to aircraft operators, handling agents, fuel suppliers, airports, and navigation service providers; for high-value foreign-currency flows requiring cover payments, SWIFT MT202COV is used through the correspondent bank. CAD-denominated domestic disbursements clear via the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch EFT or via Lynx RTGS where same-day finality is required. USD-denominated outbound payments may clear through Fedwire or CHIPS via the bank's US correspondent.
