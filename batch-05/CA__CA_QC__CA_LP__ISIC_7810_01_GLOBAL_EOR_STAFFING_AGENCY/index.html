Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for entity classification: Canada does not offer a US-style check-the-box election or a general hybrid-mismatch election for limited partnerships. A Quebec LP is mandatorily flow-through for income tax and is treated as a separate person for GST/HST/QST purposes. Limited partnership fiscal-period and partner-representative designations may be filed under applicable tax acts, but these do not alter entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated supplies, over the current calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; the same C$30,000 threshold applies for QST. A Quebec LP engaged in employer-of-record and payroll services should nevertheless register promptly because it is making taxable supplies and must remit payroll source deductions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated where the service is not rendered to an individual while that individual is in Canada, is not in relation to real property situated in Canada, and is not in relation to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Global EOR and cross-border contractor payroll administration supplied by the Quebec LP to a foreign end-client generally falls within this exported-services rule if the recipient is a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST and the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada or tied to Canadian real or tangible property. QST applies an equivalent zero-rating rule for services supplied to non-residents. The LP must obtain and retain contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the place of supply to support the 0% rate.

  • Signed global EOR/contractor payroll services agreement citing the non-resident recipient and the place of supply.
  • Invoice issued under GST/HST/QST rules showing the client's foreign address and the 0% rate applied.
  • Non-resident status evidence, such as foreign corporate register extract, foreign tax identification number, or certificate of residence.
  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) and bill of lading or air waybill where any physical goods are bundled with the services.
  • Bank remittance or inward-remittance certificate demonstrating receipt of cross-border payment within the FX repatriation period.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API authorization applies in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST and QST invoices must contain the supplier's registration number, transaction date, description, consideration, tax rates, and tax amounts. Invoices issued in Quebec must generally be in French unless the parties otherwise agree. Registered persons must file periodic returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's secure transmission portal; no digital fiscal seal is required.

If export zero-rating is disallowed, CRA and Revenu Québec will reassess the supply at the standard GST and QST rates, add statutory interest, and claw back input tax credits claimed on costs attributable to that supply. Gross negligence or false statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec's QST regime are generally the greater of C$100 and 25% of the tax understated; deliberate fraud may attract criminal prosecution. Failure to maintain the required non-resident and foreign-remittance documentation within the 180-day period will trigger heightened audit scrutiny and retrospective reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both capital and current accounts under the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and Commercial Companies policy. There are no exchange controls, quotas, or licensing requirements for cross-border transfers. Profit remittances for non-resident limited partners are free of Forex authority approval, subject only to corporate tax obligations (Quebec CRA) and FINTRAC reporting on international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or above.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply to export proceeds in Canada (and none apply to an EOR service provider). Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely. The only legislative relevance is the PCMLTFA, which requires reporting of cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; it does not impose any timing or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) – the central monetary authority; however, Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency. Currency policy and exchange-control administration fall under the Minister of Finance and the Department of Finance Act, with anti-money laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian law under PCMLTFA requires every money-services business (MSB) providing foreign-exchange dealing or remittance services to register with FINTRAC and, for operations in Quebec, hold a licence from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Quebec). Offshore EMIs without such registrations cannot lawfully open settlement accounts in Canada. The platform 'Wise' is registered as an MSB and holds AMF licensing (or operates under the federal registration), so it can serve as a secondary payment rail for small cross-border contractor disbursements; however, Wise is not a chartered foreign bank and cannot hold corporate deposits or provide payroll tax remittance services. A Schedule I bank account remains mandatory for statutory deductions, payroll taxes, and capital-bearing escrow functions. Legal constraints also include the prohibition on treating EMI balances as collateral.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% (non-CCPC general corporate rate) + Quebec 11.5% general provincial corporate rate, combined statutory headline 26.5%; an LP is fiscally transparent, so the headline rate tax is borne by corporate or individual partners at their own rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII (s.212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation; an LP itself is a flow-through and its profit distributions to non-resident partners are not 'dividends' — such active business income is taxable under Part I on the non-resident partner, subject to the DTA permanent-establishment rule.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Both T5013 and TP-601 are due no later than five months after the end of the LP's fiscal year (typically March 31 for a December 31 year-end LP, i.e., May 31); corporate partners must file their T2/TP-600 returns within six months of their own fiscal year-end.

Box 01 – Gross sales, commissions, fees (T5013) Total gross revenue from the EOR services rendered (payroll administration and employer-of-record fees) before deductions. Include only revenue attributable to the Canadian/Quebec permanent establishment; intercompany charges from the global EOR platform must be excluded or separately reported in the reconciliation schedules. CRA: late filing penalty under ITA s.162(7) is CA$25/day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500), plus a s.162(7.1) penalty of CA$100 per partner per month (maximum 24 months) for failing to file T5013 information; Revenu Québec: daily penalties up to CA$2,500 plus interest at the prescribed rate, and 50% gross-negligence penalties for false or misleading statements.
Box 08 – Gross cost of goods sold / cost of payroll services Direct costs of providing EOR services, including salaries of employees placed under the employer-of-record arrangement. Salaries of Canadian-resident employees are deductible only if remitted through qualified Quebec payroll accounts (QPP, QPIP, EI, HSF); no deduction is allowed for non-resident payroll unless the employee is registered with CRA as a Canadian-reportable employee. CRA: late filing penalty under ITA s.162(7) is CA$25/day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500), plus a s.162(7.1) penalty of CA$100 per partner per month (maximum 24 months) for failing to file T5013 information; Revenu Québec: daily penalties up to CA$2,500 plus interest at the prescribed rate, and 50% gross-negligence penalties for false or misleading statements.
Box 100 – Business income (loss) before allocations Net active business income from the LP's EOR operations after all deductible expenses, including management fees, platform licensing fees and technology costs. Deductibility of platform fees paid to foreign affiliates requires compliance with ITA s.247 (transfer pricing) and, for Quebec, the restriction on expenses such as management fees paid to non-residents (Quebec Taxation Act). CRA: late filing penalty under ITA s.162(7) is CA$25/day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500), plus a s.162(7.1) penalty of CA$100 per partner per month (maximum 24 months) for failing to file T5013 information; Revenu Québec: daily penalties up to CA$2,500 plus interest at the prescribed rate, and 50% gross-negligence penalties for false or misleading statements.
Box 300 – Taxable capital gains Sale of capital assets held by the LP (if any). Non-resident partners must report a capital gains allocation only where the asset is 'taxable Canadian property' subject to Part I tax. CRA: late filing penalty under ITA s.162(7) is CA$25/day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500), plus a s.162(7.1) penalty of CA$100 per partner per month (maximum 24 months) for failing to file T5013 information; Revenu Québec: daily penalties up to CA$2,500 plus interest at the prescribed rate, and 50% gross-negligence penalties for false or misleading statements.
Part III – Allocation schedule (T5013 partner allocation / TP-601 RL-15 slips) Each partner's share of income/loss, contribution schedule, and partnership percentage; Quebec RL-15 (Revenus d'une société de personnes) must be issued to each partner resident in Quebec. For each non-resident corporate partner, the LP must attach the name, address, treaty-residence identifier and the agreed allocation percentage; the LP must not withhold Part XIII on business-income allocations but must be prepared to apply ITA s.226(1) if CRA issues a demand for the non-resident partner's unpaid Part I tax. CRA: late filing penalty under ITA s.162(7) is CA$25/day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500), plus a s.162(7.1) penalty of CA$100 per partner per month (maximum 24 months) for failing to file T5013 information; Revenu Québec: daily penalties up to CA$2,500 plus interest at the prescribed rate, and 50% gross-negligence penalties for false or misleading statements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory (ITA s.247(4)) where the LP's aggregate cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CA$1,000,000 per year; CRA administrative guidance TP-2023-07 requires a Local File where the Canadian entity's gross revenue exceeds CA$10,000,000 and a Master File where the MNE group's consolidated group revenue exceeds CA$1,000,000,000. Country-by-country reporting (CbCR) applies to ultimate parents with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 (approximately CA$1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the federal GAAR (ITA s.245), the Quebec GAAR (Quebec Taxation Act ss.1079.1–1079.11), the doctrine of 'place of effective management' and the OECD BEPS Action 5 minimum standard require genuine commercial substance. A Quebec LP acting as global EOR must maintain physical premises, full-time payroll operations staff, actual client contracts, payroll remittance records (federal and Quebec payroll accounts), and substantive decision-making in Quebec; a passive 'shell LP' holding only the EOR platform license will be recharacterized, leading to denial of the LP flow-through status and 50% gross-negligence penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Unremitted Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: CRA: 10% late-remittance penalty plus daily interest; 50% gross-negligence penalty (ITA s.163(2)) on unreported amounts; criminal fines on summary conviction of 50%–200% of the evaded tax (ITA s.239) and up to 5 years' imprisonment upon indictment; Quebec TAA: additional 50% penalty and criminal fines up to CA$575,000 for directors.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 and Quebec TAA s.32 for unremitted source deductions without proof of fault; the due-diligence defence (exercising reasonable care based on delegated financial specialists) is narrow; a personal restitution order, asset freezing, exclusion from the CRA/Revenu Québec top-debtor programme, and judicial restrictions on leaving Canada for outstanding tax debts exceeding CA$7,500 are available.
Shell Company – Sham LP and Artificial Allocation of Profits

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the sham doctrine and GAAR with a 50% gross-negligence penalty on re-assessed income (ITA s.163(2)); denial of the LP's flow-through status; transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s.247(2) with a 10% adjustment penalty on the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CA$5,000,000; Revenu Québec impositions under the Quebec GAAR mirroring the federal rules.

Director personal liability: Corporate-general-partner directors face joint liability for the re-assessed partnership tax and a court-ordered civil restitution of diverted profits; criminal counselling of false statements ('fraud on CRA') opens the directors to indictment under ITA s.239 and to a travel-restriction order pending the criminal trial.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Failure – Unregistered Money Services Business

Statutory sanction: Failure to register a money-services business with FINTRAC is an offence punishable on summary conviction by a fine up to CA$500,000 and 2 years' imprisonment, or on indictment by a fine up to CA$1,000,000 and 5 years' imprisonment for the EOR entity; each failure to send an electronic funds-transfer report carries administrative monetary penalties up to CA$10,000 per violation for compliance failures and CA$500,000 for deliberate AML violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly authorize an unregistered money-transfer service are personally liable for the corporate fines, may face criminal prosecution (maximum fine CA$1,000,000 and 5 years' imprisonment), and may be made subject to a restraint order freezing personal and corporate assets for the duration of the FINTRAC/RCMP investigation.
