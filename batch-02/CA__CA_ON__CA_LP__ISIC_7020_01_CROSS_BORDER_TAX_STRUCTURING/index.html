Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: the partnership computes income/loss at the entity level under ITA section 96, but the tax liability is borne by partners. Non-resident partners of a partnership carrying on business in Canada are taxable under Part I on their share of Canadian-source income (or Part XIII on passive items), subject to relief under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980), including Article IV(6) for fiscally transparent entities. An Ontario LP is distinct from an Ontario corporation, which would be subject to entity-level federal and provincial corporate income tax plus Ontario corporate minimum tax.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; partnership transparency is mandatory for an LP. Available elections include: (i) application of Canada-US Income Tax Convention Article IV(6) to claim treaty benefits through the fiscally transparent LP; (ii) ITA subsection 216(1) election by a non-resident partner to be taxed on a net Part I basis on Canadian-source rental or timber royalty income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (iii) co-ordinated elections under the hybrid mismatch and excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules in ITA sections 12.7, 18.2, 18.21 and 95.2; (iv) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA subsection 240(1); and (v) recording agreed partner allocations in the LP Agreement and reporting them on T5013 to govern cascading cross-border tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the GST/HST small supplier threshold under ETA section 148, measured on worldwide taxable supplies made in any single calendar quarter and in the preceding four calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, while a small supplier may elect to register voluntarily under ETA subsection 240(1). For non-resident suppliers of digital services or intangibles under the 2021 simplified cross-border regime, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies but with no input tax credit entitlement; professional tax structuring services follow the general threshold rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services (ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541110) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (services supplied to a non-resident), provided the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada when performed, and is not supplied to an individual present in Canada. The 2021 place-of-supply rules (ETA sections 6 and 7) locate B2B services at the recipient's jurisdiction, preserving the 0% rate for exported professional services. Contemporaneous evidence of the client's non-resident status (foreign tax ID, certificate of residence, registered office outside Canada) and the service deliverables is required at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA B13A Export Declaration or Canada Automated Export Declaration (CAED) for any tangible goods accompanying or underlying the consultancy engagement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods where services relate to cross-border deliverables
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT wire transfer record evidencing receipt of the fee in foreign exchange from the non-resident client
  • Dated and executed engagement letter, statement of work, and GST/HST-compliant invoices identifying the non-resident recipient, the nature of the tax structuring services, and the HST rate applied (0% or 13%)
  • Certificate of residence, foreign tax identification number, or non-resident declaration obtained from the client to substantiate the zero-rating claim
  • General partner minutes or authorization records confirming the cross-border transaction and the LP's export service character

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory government pre-stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API regime exists in Ontario or at the federal level. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or the CRA's Represent a Client API. Invoices must comply with ETA section 223 (supplier name, Business Number, tax amount) to support input tax credits; CRA accepts electronic invoices in PDF, XML or EDI format. The Quebec mandatory B2B electronic invoicing regime (Revenu Quebec) does not apply to an Ontario LP. The Ontario Business Registry, rather than a tax clearance system, processes provincial LP filings.

CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year GST/HST limitation period (and three-year income tax period) where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is established (ETA subsection 298(4); ITA subsection 152(4)). Failure to substantiate the export within the documentary deadline causes the supply to be recharacterized as taxable at 13% HST, with reassessed tax, arrears interest under ETA section 280, and denial of the customer's input tax credit. Gross negligence penalties under ETA section 285 reach the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; ITA subsection 163(2) imposes analogous penalties on partners. Criminal tax evasion under ETA section 284 and ITA section 327 is punishable by fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to five years. All general partners of the Ontario LP are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST liabilities under ETA subsection 272.1(3).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is free-floating and fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account transactions, with no exchange controls in force since the repeal of the Exchange Control Act in 1951. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations, so there is no prior-approval, licensing, declaration, or surrender requirement for cross-border capital injections, fee receipts, partner distributions, dividends, interest, royalties, or service payments. Profit-remittance frictions are tax-based rather than FX-based: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the 25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced under applicable double tax agreements, applies to management fees, interest, rents, royalties, and certain dividends paid or credited to non-resident persons; (ii) a non-resident limited partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income is taxed under Part I (section 115) or by Part XIII withholding on designated distributions under subsection 212(1), with foreign tax credit relief available; (iii) Ontario HST at 13%, including the reverse-charge self-assessment under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for imported advisory services from non-resident subcontractors where the LP is a registrant lacking full input-tax-credit entitlement; and (iv) Investment Canada Act notification obligations when a non-Canadian establishes or acquires control of a Canadian business. None of these constitute currency-convertibility restrictions; remittances are executable freely through Schedule I banks without Bank of Canada authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline. Cross-border tax-structuring and advisory fee receivables denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts at Schedule I institutions, swept into interest-bearing USD deposits, or converted to CAD at the commercial discretion of the LP. The only administrative obligations are FINTRAC's international electronic funds transfer (IEFT) reporting for every wire of CAD 10,000 or more, accurate mark-to-market accounting of foreign-currency balances for financial statements, and a documented treasury hedging policy where foreign-currency exposure exceeds a materiality threshold. No Goods and Services Tax or stamp-duty cost is triggered by the conversion itself.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central bank and sole issuing authority for the Canadian dollar (CAD), responsible for monetary policy, management of the Large Value Transfer System (Lynx), oversight of retail payment service provider registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), and conduct of foreign exchange operations strictly within the External Reserves Management framework. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) is the prudential regulator of federally chartered banks, including Schedule I domestic banks and Schedule II/III foreign bank branches. FINTRAC is Canada's financial intelligence unit supervising anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the PCMLTFR Regulations. The Department of Finance (Canada) exercises residual exchange-rate and currency-policy authority under the Currency Act. Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control administration and no central-bank approval requirement for ordinary cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary execution rail; LOW as a primary banking relationship. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers (PSPs) must be registered with the Bank of Canada (full compliance enforced since September 8, 2024). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not chartered banks under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in Canada without a Schedule I bank sponsorship or a Canadian PSP registration, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. For an Ontario LP with Low_Risk_Standard AML classification, a foreign EMI is viable only for (i) executing outbound disbursements to non-resident subcontractors in their local currency and (ii) small-value card-acquirer settlement pass-through, provided the EMI is contractually designated as the LP's agent rather than a depositary, zero overnight balances are retained, each transfer above CAD 10,000 is reported to FINTRAC, and the partnership agreement expressly authorizes non-bank payment intermediaries. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident limited partners: contributions are transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the partner's home jurisdiction directly to the LP's designated operating account at a Tier 1 Schedule I bank, or via the ISO 20022 pacs.008 message using the ExternalPurposeCode 'CCAP' (Capital Contribution) where the receiving bank supports structured purpose codes; where structured codes are unavailable, the MT103 field 70 remittance narrative must state 'Capital contribution per Partnership Agreement CA_LP, not taxable income'. The beneficiary must be the exact legal name and account number of the LP; pooled or third-party intermediary accounts are prohibited. For contributions above CAD 100,000, deliver to the bank in advance a capital-call notice, the general partner's drawdown resolution, and a capital-account memorandum identifying the contributing limited partner; retain the Investment Canada Act notification acknowledgment where the contribution accompanies establishment of a new Canadian business. The bank must name the LP as the account holder, record the partner's identity in the FINTRAC beneficial-ownership register, and verify that the incoming wire is not structured to evade CAD 10,000 IEFT reporting thresholds. Under Canadian law, capital contributions are not subject to Part XIII withholding and there is no obligation to convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD; the LP may hold USD or EUR deposits in segregated foreign-currency accounts.Clearing rails for trade settlements and operational disbursements: (i) domestic CAD disbursements — Payments Canada Lynx RTGS for large-value transfers exceeding the LVTS threshold and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for ordinary supplier payments, CRA tax remittances, and payroll; (ii) cross-border non-trade remittances — SWIFT MT103 single or cover payments executed through the Schedule I bank's correspondent network, with a direct correspondent relationship preferred to minimize intermediary charges; (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700/MT720) may be used for acquisition or licensing payments above CAD 750,000 where the beneficiary requires trade-finance assurance, although for a services-based LP under ISIC 7020 an LC is typically limited to proof-of-payment and bank-guarantee purposes; (iv) card-acquirer settlement for the stated Credit_Card payment rail — card transactions are authorized and settled through Visa or Mastercard networks and cleared into the LP's operational account via Payments Canada settlement optimization; and (v) partner distributions to non-resident limited partners — distributed through MT103 wires after reconciling the T5013 allocation, with Part XIII withholding remitted to the CRA where applicable and each outbound transfer above CAD 10,000 filed with FINTRAC as an IEFT report.Plain-vanilla deliverable CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP foreign-exchange forwards executed with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank under an ISDA Master Agreement or the 1998 FX Master Agreement, fully within the LP's documented treasury authority.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic or non-convertible currencies encountered in cross-border tax-advisory fee inflows from high-inflation or restricted-currency jurisdictions, booked with a regulated bank counterparty and documented under CRA derivative-reporting rules.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) for longer-term treasury rebalancing where partnership capital is held in USD but Canadian operating expenses and partner distributions are funded in CAD, subject to CSA collateral arrangements.Exchange-traded CAD/USD and CAD/EUR futures and options listed on CME, ICE, or other recognized derivatives exchanges, executed through an IIROC-member dealer for transparent margin management and delivery-hedge efficiency.Zero-cost collar and participating-forward structures fully collateralized under CSA that preserve the hedge-accounting designation under IFRS 9 or ASPE 3856 and eliminate any speculative component from the treasury framework, consistent with the LP's low-risk professional-services profile.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary execution rail; LOW as a primary banking relationship. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers (PSPs) must be registered with the Bank of Canada (full compliance enforced since September 8, 2024). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not chartered banks under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in Canada without a Schedule I bank sponsorship or a Canadian PSP registration, and their balances are not CDIC-insured. For an Ontario LP with Low_Risk_Standard AML classification, a foreign EMI is viable only for (i) executing outbound disbursements to non-resident subcontractors in their local currency and (ii) small-value card-acquirer settlement pass-through, provided the EMI is contractually designated as the LP's agent rather than a depositary, zero overnight balances are retained, each transfer above CAD 10,000 is reported to FINTRAC, and the partnership agreement expressly authorizes non-bank payment intermediaries. Primary treasury operations must remain with a Tier 1 Schedule I bank to preserve regulatory alignment with ISIC 7020 professional-services licensing requirements.
