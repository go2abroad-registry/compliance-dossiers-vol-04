Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial corporate tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account; Revenu Québec CLIC (Clic Revenu).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must register with CRA to obtain a Business Number (BN) and applicable program account (e.g., RC for GST/HST, RM for import/export, RP for payroll). Registration may be completed online through CRA's Business Registration Online, by mail, by fax, or through a representative. The entity must supply legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, articles of incorporation/continuance, directors' information, Canadian contact/attorney for service, and expected revenue. For Quebec, separate registration with Revenu Québec is required to obtain a QST number (often integrated with the BN/QST registration through a coordinated form) when the entity carries on taxable commercial activity in Quebec.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must register with CRA to obtain a Business Number (BN) and applicable program account (e.g., RC for GST/HST, RM for import/export, RP for payroll). Registration may be completed online through CRA's Business Registration Online, by mail, by fax, or through a representative. The entity must supply legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, articles of incorporation/continuance, directors' information, Canadian contact/attorney for service, and expected revenue. For Quebec, separate registration with Revenu Québec is required to obtain a QST number (often integrated with the BN/QST registration through a coordinated form) when the entity carries on taxable commercial activity in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. Tax classification follows the legal form under corporate law. Specific elective provisions exist such as Income Tax Act s.85 (rollover of assets to a corporation) and s.216 (election for non-resident rental income to be taxed on a net basis). Anti-hybrid rules (ss. 18.2/18.4 and 57.1, 956–958) constrain deduction/non-inclusion mismatches and do not offer entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies per single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are required to register once taxable supplies exceed this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under GST/HST and QST applies to supplies of goods that are exported from Canada, or supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada, and to services supplied to non-residents where the place of supply is outside Canada and the service is not supplied in connection with Canadian real estate or goods. The supplier must obtain and retain evidence of export/foreign supply within prescribed timelines (normally immediately or as soon as practical).

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance regime is currently in force federally or in Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec require invoices to contain the supplier's business number / QST registration number and to be retained as electronic records. Revenu Québec has announced a future mandatory e-invoicing framework, but it is not yet operative.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years from the date of assessment, or ten years in cases of misrepresentation/negligence. False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax amount, plus fines and interest. Criminal prosecution is possible for willful evasion, and improperly claimed credits may be clawed back retroactively with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both capital and current accounts under IMF Article VIII, with a freely floating exchange rate and no prior approval requirements for cross-border capital flows. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are subject to withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, reduced to 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty, subject to applicable limitations) and to standard anti-money laundering transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligations apply to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may hold, convert, or allocate foreign-currency accounts freely. Reporting obligations apply only for wire transfers crossing CAD 10,000 and tax filing requirements for worldwide income.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance acting as the statutory authority for exchange control measures under the Currency Act and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) responsible for transaction-level AML surveillance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Payment-centric EMIs such as Stripe and PayPal are viable as front-end collection and payment gateways for consumer transactions in musical instruments and audio gear. They must register as Money Services Businesses in Canada under the PCMLTFA and comply with FINTRAC reporting. However, they are not OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions, cannot provide treasury services such as corporate credit facilities, foreign-exchange hedging, or multi-currency liquidity pools, and should not be used as the primary corporate treasury account.

Defense evidence: REQ corporate extract, federal Certificate of Existence, complete shareholder register, ultimate beneficial owner declaration with passport copies, formal organisational chart showing direct and indirect interest, and a written explanation of the capital source.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Economic capital injection is performed via SWIFT MT103 with a precise purpose field: 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – CA_CORP – NEQ [9-digit Quebec Registration Number]'. The receiving institution must obtain a corporate resolution, any applicable pre-emptive rights waiver, and an updated Quebec registry extract. If the inbound transfer exceeds CAD 100,000, the bank may request source-of-wealth evidence including remitter bank statements, the share subscription agreement, and a valuation memo. No foreign-exchange conversion is mandated; funds may be booked as CAD or foreign-currency balance based on the account structure.Trade settlements to overseas manufacturers and suppliers are cleared through SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for direct transfers. For high-value documentary transactions, irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are issued by the Canadian bank, entailing post-shipment documentation and negotiation with the foreign advising bank. CAD-denominated supplier payments to domestic partners are cleared through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day high-value settlement, and USD payments are cleared via Fedwire/CHIPS.
