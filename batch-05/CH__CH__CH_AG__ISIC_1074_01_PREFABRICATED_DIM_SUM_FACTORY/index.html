Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA) — portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration via Swiss Federal Tax Administration's electronic portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register for Swiss VAT once turnover from Swiss taxable supplies exceeds CHF 100,000 (or at any threshold if initially registering voluntarily). Application is made to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration; the Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) is assigned via the federal register, and the VAT number is the UID followed by the 'MWST' suffix. Proof of foreign legal existence, Swiss place of business/agency, and anticipated turnover documentation are required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported to a recipient abroad qualify for 0% VAT provided transport to a third country is properly evidenced by a customs export declaration or equivalent proof. Services physically or effectively used abroad and rendered to foreign recipients may also fall under zero-rating or export exemption rules under the Swiss VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / export customs document)
  • Bill of Lading, Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment evidence for the exported goods

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Switzerland. E-invoicing may be processed via QR-bill or PEPPOL, and VAT returns must be filed electronically through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's e-services portal. Invoices must comply with VAT invoice data requirements; no government pre-approval is required.

Swiss VAT authorities may apply a ten-year retrospective audit period. Failure to substantiate export zero-rating leads to VAT reassessment, default interest, and a fine. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to criminal penalties under the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law, including fines up to three times the evaded amount and potential imprisonment for willful tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has a fully convertible currency on both current and capital account. The Swiss franc is freely tradeable and the Swiss legal framework imposes no restrictions on cross-border capital flows, profit remittances, or distribution of dividends. Current account convertibility is unconditional, and capital account movements are broadly exempt from prior approval, with only statistical reporting obligations and prudential banking documentation requirements. Profit repatriation is legally unrestricted, subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividend distributions unless reduced by an applicable double taxation treaty or the Swiss participation exemption for qualifying corporate shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in any convertible currency. Exporters must comply with SNB balance-of-payments reporting thresholds, specifically reporting export payments exceeding CHF 100,000 per calendar year on a consolidated basis to the Swiss National Bank for statistical purposes, but no obligation exists to convert any portion into Swiss francs.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital contributions are permitted freely into Swiss bank accounts. Transfers should be made via SWIFT MT103 using the company's clearly designated account. The bank will require the remittance instruction to state the purpose as 'Share capital contribution' or 'Investment in Aktiengesellschaft' and to reference the corresponding Swiss Commercial Register entry and board resolution. The absence of exchange controls means no Swiss governmental approval is required, but banks must preserve evidence of the underlying corporate resolution for AML and statistical reporting purposes.Outbound trade settlements are executed via Swiss SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) and SIX SIC payment rails, normally instructed through SWIFT MT103 messages. For larger or documentary transactions, beneficiary banks may require a commercial letter of credit governed by UCP 600, with presentation through Swiss clearing and confirmation banks. The CH_AG entity may also use SWIFT MT202 for interbank transfers when acting through correspondent banking structures. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on after-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined Swiss federal plus cantonal/communal rate for the Canton of Zug approximately 11.85% on pre-tax profit; combined national range 11.9% to 21.6% depending on canton of residence.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Federal Anticipatory Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends paid by a Swiss AG; the 35% charge is fully refundable to Swiss resident shareholders (with restitution procedures) and partially recoverable by foreign shareholders holding a valid DTA entitlement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is 30 June following the fiscal year-end (for a calendar-year company, 30 June 2025 for FY 2024); cantons typically grant an automatic extension to 30 September and, upon justified request, to 31 December; a discretionary assessment is issued if the return remains unfiled after formal reminder.

Late filing: procedural violation fine up to CHF 10,000 under Art. 238 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax; the tax authority may issue a discretionary assessment (taxable income estimated at the authority's discretion) appealable within 30 days; default interest of 3.5% to 4.5% per annum runs on any unpaid tax; an intentional incomplete or false return triggers an evasion surcharge of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland imposes no statutory Local File or Master File requirement; however, the taxpayer bears the burden of proof that intercompany transactions comply with the arm's length principle under Art. 58 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected for all material related-party transactions (no de minimis threshold). Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for Swiss ultimate parent entities of multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CHF 900 million in the preceding fiscal year; secondary local filing applies to Swiss constituent entities of foreign groups meeting the same threshold.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council regimes; nevertheless, the Swiss tax law and treaty practice require genuine economic substance - physical premises, qualified staff, actual management and value-adding functions in Switzerland - to support the arm's length allocation of profit, to obtain Swiss PE attribution, and to prevent denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test. A letterbox company engaged only in passive holding of invoices or financing is exposed to recharacterization, the denial of DTA relief, and the requalification of its income as a taxable constructive dividend.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge of at least 100% and up to 300% of the tax evaded (Art. 175 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax) plus penal interest; in cases of tax fraud committed by falsification or forgery of documents (Art. 186 DBG), criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years or fines; repeat offences exclude amnesty and leniency.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or order accounting manipulations are jointly and severally liable for the penalty surcharge and the evaded tax; the corporate veil is pierced under Art. 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations for wilful or negligent conduct; the criminal court may impose preventive travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and a German-Swiss style exit ban during the pendency of proceedings.
Withholding Tax Violation (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the full amount of unpaid anticipatory tax plus compound default interest of 4.5% per annum (Art. 14 of the Federal Withholding Tax Act); criminal fines up to CHF 30,000; the tax authority additionally claims 35% of any disguised distribution as an unequivocal tax debt.

Director personal liability: Every member of the board of directors is personally and jointly liable for the uncollected anticipatory tax; the tax authority may issue a personal penalty order against directors without prior notice; enforcement includes seizure of personal bank accounts and a travel ban where enforcement is frustrated.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The tax authority makes an upward adjustment of the Swiss taxable income under the arm's length principle; the adjustment is treated as a constructive dividend subject to 35% Federal Anticipatory Tax; a penalty of 100% to 300% of the additional tax assessment applies where the mispricing is due to intent or gross negligence; interest at default rates is levied from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's length transaction are liable for the resulting corporate damage under Art. 754 of the Code of Obligations, including the tax shortfall and the withholding tax charge; if false documentation is created to substantiate the pricing, criminal liability for tax fraud or forgery arises, carrying imprisonment and civil restitution.
AML / Shell Company / Abusive Domiciliation

Statutory sanction: Violation of due diligence obligations under Art. 305ter of the Swiss Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment up to one year or a fine up to CHF 500,000; money laundering under Art. 305bis is punishable by imprisonment up to three years or a fine; the assets concerned are confiscated; DTA benefits are denied and the Swiss tax authority may reassess undisclosed hidden reserves.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal conviction, immediate travel bans, freezing of personal assets and debarment from board positions; civil liability for restitution of misappropriated funds under Art. 754 CO; in cases of intentional money laundering, the court may order imprisonment and a subsequent entry ban obstructing the director's future travel to Switzerland and the EU.
