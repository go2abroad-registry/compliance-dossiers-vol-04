Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Ontario limited partnerships; capital contributions are determined solely by the partnership agreement and may be made in cash, property, or services. No escrow account or deposit is required under Ontario law.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain fire safety clearance from the local fire department under the Ontario Fire Code, including adequate egress, fire suppression, and storage of silicone materials; secure a municipal building permit with structural load inspection for compression presses and ventilation equipment; comply with the Occupational Health and Safety Act by completing workplace hazard assessment, machine guarding, noise control, silicone dust management, and employee WHMIS training before operations commence.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Ontario limited partnerships; capital contributions are determined solely by the partnership agreement and may be made in cash, property, or services. No escrow account or deposit is required under Ontario law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration, International and Non-Resident Tax Division; portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario Ministry of Finance — Ontario provincial tax administration; portal: ONT-TAXS / Ontario Business Registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario limited partnership must register as follows. (1) Obtain a statutory 9-digit Business Number from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (or RC348 for non-residents) and select the RT (GST/HST) account; this BN is the de facto Canadian TIN for indirect tax purposes. (2) Register when annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or, if below it, file an early voluntary registration election. (3) Register applicable Ontario provincial accounts with the Ontario Ministry of Finance when thresholds (e.g., payroll for Employer Health Tax) are met. (4) File the annual partnership information return and issue T5013 slips allocating Ontario-source income to resident and non-resident limited partners. No minimum capital or statutory license is imposed; NAICS 339930 / ISIC 3240 is the goods classification for export documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: the limited partnership computes income at the entity level for information purposes but is not itself subject to Canadian income tax; each limited partner, including a non-resident, is taxable on its allocated Canadian-source share, and the Part XIII withholding tax regime may apply to non-resident partners.

Available election forms

There is no domestic check-the-box election for an Ontario LP. Relevant elections include: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration to claim HST input tax credits and charge HST to Canadian buyers; (b) the closely related persons and partnerships elections under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act to deem certain taxable supplies to be made for nil consideration; (c) the subsection 97(2.1) Income Tax Act rollover election for property transferred to a partnership on a tax-deferred basis; and (d) a U.S. partner may independently elect on IRS Form 8832 to classify the Canadian LP as an association taxable as a corporation for U.S. federal purposes, an election over foreign classification that does not alter Canadian pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the annual taxable supply threshold for small supplier status under ETA section 148. A non-resident manufacturer selling goods into or from Ontario is required to register for GST/HST once the worldwide taxable supplies exceed this threshold; however, a non-resident seller of goods shipped to consumers in Canada may still be required to register under deemed supply rules or the imported taxable supplies regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of silicone stress-relief fidget goods exported by the vendor or the vendor's agent qualifies for zero-rating under Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where: (a) the property is shipped directly from Canada to a destination outside Canada; (b) possession and title shift to the non-resident buyer before export or at an arm's-length point; and (c) the vendor holds proof of export evidencing delivery outside Canada. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the service is performed wholly outside Canada or the recipient is outside Canada for GST/HST purposes. The zero-rating claim also requires the vendor to retain documentation demonstrating that HST was not separately charged on the export sale.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A or approved CBSA electronic export reporting)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or waybill/carrier cargo manifest showing consignee and destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance/wire confirmation evidencing payment to a Canadian financial institution
  • CBSA export report and customs broker records; optionally CRA Form E-16 adjustment schedule

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API stamping or digital signature mandate exists in Canada. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns and export declarations is compulsory for large registrants (annual taxable supplies in excess of CAD 1.5 million), and all registrants must preserve electronic source documents for CRA e-audit systems. The CRA may request export transaction evidence through customs single-window data matching between the Canada Border Services Agency and the CRA.

The CRA matches export declarations, invoices, payment flows and freight documents during an export audit. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is reassessed at 13% HST with statutory interest; a gross negligence penalty under ETA section 285 of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understatement may be imposed. In evasion cases, Excise Tax Act sections 327-328 can trigger penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for officers or partners, while any refunded input tax credits or rebates are clawed back in full. No amnesty or de minimis cut-off applies to an unsubstantiated export claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no foreign exchange control legislation, no repatriation restrictions, and no balance-of-payments registration regime. Partnership distributions, profit remittances, dividends, royalties, interest, and loan repayments may be freely remitted to non-residents, subject only to general income tax withholding and sanctions compliance. Documentary evidence of underlying trade or investment may be requested by banks for Customer Due Diligence (CDD) purposes but no government clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters may retain proceeds in any foreign currency, deposit them in offshore accounts, or convert them freely. Statutory obligations are limited to FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reports for remittances of CAD 10,000 or more, and to record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank. Banking supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing administration is delegated to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No dedicated foreign exchange administration body exists; currency and cross-border capital transactions are not subject to prior approval or exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for offshore digital EMI as primary operating bank; High as secondary collection/payout rail. Legal constraints include: (i) non-Canadian EMIs are not authorized to hold CAD clearing accounts at the Bank of Canada or access the Canadian payments system directly; (ii) the EMI must either obtain a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration under PCMLTFA or partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank to provide CAD settlement; (iii) funds held with an unlicensed EMI are not eligible for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) deposit insurance; (iv) client funds protection and creditor priority are governed by the EMI's home-country regime, which may not recognize the Ontario LP as a protected beneficiary. For treasury certainty, a Schedule I bank account should remain the primary liquidity anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident capital injections into the Ontario LP is via international wire transfer using SWIFT MT103 (or domestic wire via ACSS or Lynx if funding is intra-Canada). Canada has no mandatory wire purpose code regime, but banks require field 70 (Remittance Information) to describe 'Capital Contribution – Limited Partnership' and field 71F to show any applicable transfer charges. For injections exceeding CAD 100,000, FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting applies to the sending and receiving financial institutions. The bank may also require the partnership's CRA business number, a signed capital contribution schedule, and beneficial ownership declarations. For funds sourced from a foreign investor, the remitting bank must comply with its home-country exchange control (if any); the Canadian receiving bank will accept the credit without BoC approval.Trade settlements for raw silicone materials and supplier payments are executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 / MT202 for direct telegraphic transfers; (ii) Commercial letters of credit (LC) subject to UCP600 for larger or open-account-supplier transactions; (iii) CAD-denominated interbank clearing via Lynx or Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for domestic counterparties; (iv) platform pay rails (Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer) for B2C refunds and micro-disbursements, with overlay bank sweeps to the primary operational account. Documentary compliance for LC requires the Ontario LP to maintain a commercial invoice, bill of lading / air waybill, packing list, and third-party inspection certificate for food-grade silicone. FX conversion for outbound supplier payments in USD or CNY may be executed as spot conversions or via pre-agreed forward rates with the scheduling bank.Plain-vanilla OTC FX forwards (delivery) with Canadian Schedule I banks for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/CNY; documented under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex. These are permissible for corporate hedging of firm commercial commitments.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies where CAD delivery is infeasible or for currencies with de facto non-convertibility, executed with global bank counterparties and cash-settled in CAD; must pass internal derivative non-dealer business purpose test.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) to swap principal/interest into CAD, permissible for long-term liquidity conversion and multi-year capex funding, executed under ISDA or Canadian Master Hedging Agreement.
