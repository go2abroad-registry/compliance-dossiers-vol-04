Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement. The corporation may issue no-par-value common shares; the board must determine the adequacy of the consideration received before issuing certificates, and the paid-up capital account is credited accordingly. Non-cash consideration requires board approval and, in cases of potential shareholder prejudice, an independent valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the municipal Service de securite incendie covering the enameling ovens, electrical installation, and storage of flammable enamels; structural load inspection by a professional engineer for the die-stamping presses and kilns; workplace clearance obtained from CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'equite, de la sante et de la securite du travail) for ventilation, exposure to volatile organic compounds, noise and manual handling, with a first-aid and evacuation protocol in place.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement. The corporation may issue no-par-value common shares; the board must determine the adequacy of the consideration received before issuing certificates, and the paid-up capital account is credited accordingly. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal administration of GST/HST, Income Tax Act, and Business Number registrations via My Business Account (portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency); Revenu Québec (RQ) — provincial administration of Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), provincial corporate income tax, and employer contributions (portal: revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada/Quebec must obtain (1) a federal Business Number (BN) with GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll program accounts from the CRA (via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, RC4601 for non-residents); and (2) a Quebec establishment number (NEQ) from Registraire des entreprises du Québec and a QST account from Revenu Québec (via Form LM-2-V / online 'Mon dossier d'entreprise'). Non-resident applicants must appoint a Canadian resident representative/agent for service, provide proof of foreign incorporation, and may be registered even below the threshold if deemed to be carrying on business in Canada (s. 143(2)(b) ETA) by soliciting orders through the internet, mail order, or employees/agents in Canada. The Etsy Direct Pay/Stripe storefront constitutes solicitation nexus; the BN/GST number must be displayed on invoices and the 'registrant status' must be declared for B2B customers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An Inc./Corp. business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act: federal corporate income tax at 15% general rate (9% for manufacturing/processing for CCPCs, plus federal abatement; small business deduction 12.2%; Quebec provincial rate 11.5%; combined top ~27%; all taxable income computed entity-level, no US check-the-box pass-through).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through entity classification for corporations; available statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method and streamlined accounting elections under ETA; (ii) section 85 rollover election on transfer of qualifying property to the corporation at tax-deferred amounts; (iii) CCPC small business deduction and association/related-death elections under ITA s. 125; (iv) QST simplified method elections for small businesses with Revenu Québec; (v) Group relief elections for financial institutions; and (vi) hybrid mismatch/instrument design cautions under GAAR (s. 245 ITA) and Mandatory Disclosure Rules (DAC6/Reportable Transactions, s. 237.1 ITA).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 — GST/HST: the 'small supplier' threshold is breached when total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports, capital property, and financial services) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148). QST: identical CAD 30,000 threshold under RQ (LTVQ s. 64, Reg. 54). Non-residents soliciting orders in Canada may acquire GST/HST registration status without the threshold; voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (hard enamel lapel pins) are zero-rated for GST/HST and QST when the supplier exports the goods to a non-resident customer removed from Canada "as soon as reasonably possible" after delivery, provided the supplier obtains and retains evidence of export (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20; LTVQ s. 130 et seq.). The exporter may claim input tax credits (ITC) for inputs related to export sales; domestic supplies in Quebec remain taxable at 14.975%. Services rendered to non-residents (e.g., management graphics design) may qualify under Part V, s. 7 if performed outside Canada or related to real/immovable property located abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Export Declaration, or CBSA ACI/CAED e-manifest record; CBSA's Canada Export Reporting System records are required for postal/courier exports above CAD 2,500)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List showing exporter, consignee, HS code 7117.19/8306.29, description "hard enamel lapel pins", quantity, unit price, and total value
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill issued to a non-resident consignee
  • Proof of delivery outside Canada (signed delivery confirmation, courier tracking, or foreign customs entry evidence such as CBP entry documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or payment processor settlement record (Etsy Direct Pay / Stripe payout) evidencing foreign exchange repatriation to a Canadian bank account within the FX deadline

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance / continuous transaction control regime exists in Canada. CRA does not require digital stamping or fiscal API approval of invoices; however, registrants must charge GST/HST on taxable supplies, issue tax invoices/credit notes containing prescribed ETA Reg. 242 information, keep six-year records, and file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE / RQ electronic filing). Quebec has published an e-invoicing roadmap (PEPPOL/EDIFACT based) for mandatory B2B e-invoicing in coming years, but it is not yet in force. Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe transaction records must be retained in machine-readable form as source documents for CRA/RQ audits.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit e-commerce export vendors. Unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers reassessment of the supply at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST retroactively, denial of ITCs, gross-interest charges, and penalties: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated/refund overstated (ETA s. 280(1)); 50% penalty for false statements made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence (s. 280(1)(c)); GST evasion prosecutions up to CAD 200,000 fine and two years' imprisonment (s. 327-328 ETA); RQ equivalents under LTVQ s. 1. VAT clawback exposure includes all past filings within a six-year assessment window (unlimited for fraud).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; the Canadian dollar operates under a free-floating exchange rate regime; no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest payments, or capital repatriation; no prior regulatory approval is required for cross-border fund transfers

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or forced conversion requirements exist; export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts indefinitely; cross-border cash or monetary instrument movements exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC; no mandatory conversion timeline applies

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange monitoring and compliance reporting enforced by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility; offshore digital EMIs cannot directly hold settlement accounts at Canadian clearing institutions unless licensed under the Bank Act; they may operate via agent bank arrangements; practical utility is limited to payment facilitation and secondary disbursement, not core treasury account replacement
