Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), acting within the Swiss Commercial Register network accessible via Zefix (Central Business Name Index of Switzerland).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The statutory minimum registered share capital is CHF 100,000 (Art. 621(1) OR), of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up before entry in the Commercial Register (Art. 632(1) OR). The paid-up amount must be deposited into a blocked escrow account held in the name of the company in formation at a SIX-member Swiss bank; the bank issues an irrevocable payment confirmation (Eingabebescheinigung) attesting that the funds are at the company's free disposal and not subject to pledge, repayment agreement, or offset; the funds are released to the company upon definitive registration; non-cash contributions require an additional statutory audit report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (SFTA) - Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Administration fédérale des contributions (AFC) / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni (AFC); Headquarters: Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern, Switzerland. Official e-services portal: https://www.esty.admin.ch; Business Identifier registry: https://www.uid.admin.ch; VAT e-filing is performed through the SFTA's E-Steuererklärung MWST online platform. Cantonal tax administrations administer cantonal and communal corporate taxes, while the SFTA administers federal corporate tax, VAT, withholding tax and stamp duties.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign-owned Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) Execute notarial deed of incorporation under Art. 629 ff. Swiss Code of Obligations (OR) with minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 and deposit of at least 20% (minimum CHF 50,000) with a Swiss bank; (2) File the application for entry with the Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt); upon entry, the Central Business Name Index (ZEFIX) automatically issues the Swiss Business Identifier (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer UID / IDE / IDI) in the format CHE-116.412.235, which serves as the statutory corporate TIN for all federal and cantonal tax purposes; (3) Where the AG's worldwide annual turnover from taxable supplies reaches or is expected to exceed CHF 100,000, or where voluntary VAT registration is sought, submit the VAT registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur MWST-Registrierung) electronically to the SFTA within 30 days of the commencement of VAT liability (Art. 68(1) MWSTG); the SFTA issues the VAT number by appending the suffix 'MWST' to the UID (e.g., CHE-116.412.235 MWST); (4) VAT registration is generally non-retroactive; foreign shareholders may own 100% of the AG without a Swiss fiscal representative, but a Swiss registered office and a Swiss bank confirmation of the capital deposit are mandatory; (5) For export operations, complete the customs declarant registration in the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (BAZG/OFDF) electronic declaration system (e-dec / Passar-NCTS) using the UID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque Aktiengesellschaft under Art. 620-763 OR); no pass-through or tax-transparent status is available; the Swiss tax profile combines Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope elements, since foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate and their related income are exempt from Swiss corporate income tax with progression (Art. 52(1) DBG / Art. 21(3) StHG); the AG is subject to unlimited Swiss tax liability on its worldwide income otherwise, with participation relief and treaty-based residence benefits as applicable.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box or hybrid entity election; legal-form classification follows the Code of Obligations and is mandatory for an AG. Available elections and rulings: (i) Tax-privileged holding company and mixed/administrative company status per Art. 69-75 StHG and Art. 28-29 DBG, elected through the annual tax return and verified by SFTA/cantonal qualification certificates, providing participation exemption and cantonal profit-tax relief on non-Swiss source income; (ii) Principal-company ruling in cantons such as Zug, Zurich and Geneva for foreign-group centralization of cross-border procurement and charter-brokerage functions; (iii) Federal and cantonal patent box and additional R&D deduction elections under the Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF/USTAF); (iv) Participation deduction (Beteiligungsabzug, Art. 69 DBG / Art. 28 StHG) applied by election in the tax assessment subject to the 10% minimum participation and qualifying-dividend thresholds; (v) VAT group registration election (Art. 11 MWSTG) for Swiss entities that are financially integrated at the 100% direct or indirect ownership level; (vi) OECD BEPS hybrid-mismatch alignment is achieved through treaty rules and domestic substance requirements rather than through any domestic transparent-entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 per calendar year of worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10(1)(a) MWSTG) for persons established in Switzerland. A resident Swiss AG must register when this threshold is reached; the turnover of structurally related enterprises is aggregated for threshold purposes (Art. 10(3) MWSTG). Non-established suppliers supplying services taxable in Switzerland are liable regardless of turnover, subject to the reverse-charge relief in Art. 10(2)(c) MWSTG where the Swiss recipient is itself a VAT-liable business. Voluntary registration is permitted from the start of the business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Swiss VAT law exempts exports of goods and qualifying cross-border services with full input VAT deduction, producing an effective 0% rate. Eligibility for the ISIC 5229/02 air-cargo charter broker rests on three statutory pillars: (i) Export of goods under Art. 23(1)(1) MWSTG, where the foreign recipient obtains the power of disposal and the goods are transported out of Swiss customs territory, evidenced by a Swiss customs export declaration; (ii) Services related to export goods supplied to recipients abroad under Art. 23(2)(2) MWSTG; and (iii) Transport services and related services in cross-border traffic, including international air freight carriage and the agency/brokerage commission of an air-cargo charter broker, zero-rated under Art. 23(2)(4) MWSTG, provided the underlying carriage crosses the Swiss customs frontier and the air waybill proves the international routing. The commission for arranging an international cargo aircraft charter therefore qualifies for 0% VAT where CH_AG contracts as broker or as principal for an international charter movement; any purely domestic Swiss carriage or brokerage related only to domestic transport remains taxable at the standard 8.1% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / e-dec declaration in the Swiss customs system) including the certified export certificate (Ausfuhrschein / formulaire d'exportation) evidencing legal export from Swiss customs territory.
  • Outward Air Waybill (Master and House AWB) transmitted via CASS or directly by the carrier, showing the Swiss origin airport (ZRH/GVA/BSL), the foreign destination, cargo description, and the consignee abroad.
  • Commercial invoice (original) issued by CH_AG with the explicit annotation '0% MWST - Ausfuhr (Art. 23 Abs. 1 MWSTG)' or 'International air cargo charter brokerage - Art. 23 Abs. 2 Ziff. 4 MWSTG', including the UID-MWST number of CH_AG and the customer's tax identification.
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement (Kontoauszug / Beleg) evidencing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign customer or charterer, aligned with the 180-day cash-settlement benchmark.
  • Air cargo charter brokerage agreement / GSA (General Sales Agency) or booking confirmation together with the underlying charter party or freight contract, documenting that CH_AG acted as broker or as contracting principal, the basis of the commission, and the international route.
  • Cargo manifest and proof of delivery (POD) / cargo arrival notice, providing supplemental evidence of the physical departure from Switzerland and the international destination of the goods.

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no digital stamping, no pre-clearance API and no government authorization for invoice issuance. Invoices may be issued on paper or electronically. Electronic invoices qualify as input-VAT vouchers only if they comply with the SESTA (Swiss Standard for Electronic Invoicing for Tax Purposes) XML structure, containing the mandatory SFTA data fields: supplier and recipient UID-MWST numbers, invoice number and date, description of the supply, quantity, net amount, VAT rate and VAT amount (Art. 26(2) MWSTG). Recognized formats include the SESTA-compliant Swiss QR-bill / e-bill with embedded UID-MWST metadata. All VAT-relevant documents must be retained for 10 years (Art. 87 MWSTG). VAT returns are filed electronically with the SFTA via the E-Steuererklärung MWST portal, generally on a quarterly basis, and are due within 60 days after the end of the reporting period.

The SFTA and the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (BAZG/OFDF) conduct cross-system reconciliation between export declarations, outbound AWB data, VAT returns and bank-inflow records under the export-refund review procedure (Art. 34 and Art. 83 MWSTG). Swiss law imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, as the Swiss capital and foreign-exchange markets are fully liberalized (Art. 27 BV); the 180-day marker represents the conservative documentary-settlement benchmark used by the SFTA, customs auditors and banks to corroborate the receipt of genuine export consideration, and longer payment terms require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. If the 0% export exemption is disallowed, the SFTA retroactively assesses VAT at the standard rate of 8.1% on the invoiced commission or charter fee, adds statutory default interest (Verzugszins as published annually by the SFTA), and claws back the corresponding input VAT deduction; the assessment limitation period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of tax evasion (Art. 42(4) MWSTG). Penalties: failure to keep vouchers, refusal to provide information and procedural violations attract administrative fines of up to CHF 10,000 per offence (Art. 98 MWSTG); deliberate VAT evasion attracts a fine of up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 96 MWSTG); qualified evasion or intentional false certification attracts imprisonment of up to one year or a monetary penalty (Art. 97 MWSTG), with individual liability of the directors and officers of CH_AG for intent or negligence. Adverse findings may additionally trigger administrative criminal proceedings under the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR), revocation of the customs declarant authorization, and exclusion from future export zero-rating privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible, and there are no general exchange control restrictions on cross-border capital movements, dividend distributions, or profit remittances. However, all cross-border fund transfers are subject to Swiss Anti-Money Laundering (AMLA) due diligence, and cash or high-value transfers may be scrutinized for tax and sanctions compliance. For an air cargo charter broker with a Swiss AG, no prior central bank approval is required for profit remittances, dividends, or intercompany loans, provided the transactions are commercially justified and properly documented. The only hurdles are statutory reporting thresholds (e.g., cross-border payments exceeding CHF 100,000 require a declaration to the Swiss Customs or reporting in line with SNB statistical reporting if the company is part of the financial statistics cohort).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland does not impose any compulsory surrender, mandatory conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline on export proceeds. An exempt taxpayer or a regular Swiss AG air cargo charter broker may retain export receivables in any currency, maintain foreign currency accounts, and convert them at will. The SNB requires statistical reporting for resident companies receiving payments from abroad only when the threshold materiality criteria apply (e.g., receiving or paying in excess of CHF 50 million per year in cross-border transactions). No export proceeds need to be converted into CHF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and securities supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is rated Medium. Swiss-regulated FinTech companies holding a FINMA FinTech licence (Article 1b Banking Act) may accept public deposits up to CHF 100 million but cannot perform full commercial banking activities. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) offering e-money accounts for Swiss companies are generally not permitted to provide Swiss IBANs or operate as banks without a Swiss banking licence or passporting arrangement (Switzerland is not an EEA member). For inbound international wire settlements and FX execution, engagement with an unlicensed offshore EMI is high-risk and may create OFAC/FINMA sanctions compliance gaps. Recommended structure: use a tier-1 Swiss bank as the primary settlement account and, if necessary, a licensed EU EMI (e.g., with Malta or Lithuania banking passport) only for non-CHF collections from non-UK/EU clients, with all client SARs processed through the Swiss bank.

Root cause: Inbound wire transfers from third countries with unverified origin or purpose code

Defense evidence: Maintain a transaction ledger with SWIFT message copies, invoices, charter agreements, proof of aircraft operating permits, certificate of non-availability of exchange controls, and, where applicable, the underlying charter contract showing delivery points, freight invoices, and termination clauses. Include a written confirmation that no party qualifies as a politically exposed person (PEP) or a member of a restricted list.
High-risk financial actions by proxy signatories (authorized representatives) without UBO presence

Root cause: High-risk financial actions by proxy signatories (authorized representatives) without UBO presence

Defense evidence: Provide a certified copy of the board resolution authorizing restricted powers, a corporate signature card signed in front of the bank officer, proof that the UBO participated in the onboarding due diligence call or video conference, and a corporate AML policy requiring dual authorization for payments above CHF 50,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a shareholder or related party are best routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (for interbank cover payments) to the Swiss bank account, using purpose code 'Equity Capital Injection' or 'Shareholder Loan'. Under Switzerland's bilateral tax treaty network and the EU-Swiss Savings Tax Agreement, the sending bank may require an International Bank Account Number (IBAN) and the Swiss IBAN starting with 'CH'. The inbound transfer should be accompanied by an executed loan agreement or subscription/contribution resolution, a statement of capital under notarial deed, and, in the case of third-party loans, a confirmation that the transaction complies with Swiss Ordinance on Financial Services (FinSA) and does not constitute a lending business requiring a license. For non-EU-USD transfers, the correspondent routing must be provided with the receiving bank's SWIFT BIC; the bank will verify the origin of funds and confirm the purpose code, which must match the corporate object of the AG (air freight charter brokering) to avoid rejection.Outbound trade disbursements for aircraft charter operations, fuel, ground handling, landing fees, insurance premia, and crew cost settlements should be executed through the following clearing rails: (1) AP.13/SWIFT MT103 for genuine commercial money transfers, always including the beneficiary bank's BIC, IBAN/USA ABA or account number, and a purpose code consistent with the invoice; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) issued via the Swiss bank's trade finance desk when the counterparty demands a documentary guarantee or when the air cargo broker is acting as a principal in aircraft charter transactions; (3) Fedwire for USD-denominated disbursements to US-based cargo operators, with the payment notice clearly identifying the underlying charter agreement; (4) SEPA CT (SEPA Credit Transfer) for euro settlements within Switzerland and the EEA, using the deep SEPA scheme to transfer funds directly to EU freight forwarders. For recurring payroll and contractor payments, execution via SWIFT Plus or the bank's file-transmission command (e.g., EBICS) is recommended. All outbound disbursements must maintain a contemporaneous audit trail comprising purchase orders, serverless invoices, certificate of flight operations, and the issuance of an authorization notification to the legal, compliance, and treasury mailbox.Vanilla currency forwards on CHF/EUR/USD up to 12-month tenor, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement or IFEMACurrency options (European style) on CHF/USD and CHF/EUR for ticket price stabilization in cargo charter contractsCross-currency swaps for converting long-term USD receivables into CHF funding flows, governed by an ISDA and CSAFinancing-style FX swaps with the treasury bank for short-term liquidity alignment

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is rated Medium. Swiss-regulated FinTech companies holding a FINMA FinTech licence (Article 1b Banking Act) may accept public deposits up to CHF 100 million but cannot perform full commercial banking activities. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) offering e-money accounts for Swiss companies are generally not permitted to provide Swiss IBANs or operate as banks without a Swiss banking licence or passporting arrangement (Switzerland is not an EEA member). For inbound international wire settlements and FX execution, engagement with an unlicensed offshore EMI is high-risk and may create OFAC/FINMA sanctions compliance gaps. Recommended structure: use a tier-1 Swiss bank as the primary settlement account and, if necessary, a licensed EU EMI (e.g., with Malta or Lithuania banking passport) only for non-CHF collections from non-UK/EU clients, with all client SARs processed through the Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal, cantonal and communal headline rate of approximately 11.5%–21.2% depending on canton; the Swiss federal component is a flat 8.5% on post-tax profit (approximately 7.83% pre-tax), with the Zug ordinary full-tax regime yielding approximately 11.5% effective combined liability for an AG engaged in air cargo brokerage.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions from a Swiss AG to shareholders, including non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the tax return must generally be filed by 31 March of the year following the tax period; Swiss cantonal authorities routinely grant extensions to 30 June upon request. Under Art. 124-125 of the Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act (DBG), the taxpayer is under a duty to file once the cantonal forms are issued, and failure to respect the final deadline triggers default assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss CbCR is mandatory for group revenue of at least CHF 900 million (approximately EUR 750 million); Switzerland does not impose statutory Local File or Master File thresholds in the form of fixed amounts, but under Art. 57 DBG and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, adequate contemporaneous documentation must be available and supplied on Swiss tax audit request for all cross-border related-party transactions, including air-cargo charter fees, fuel surcharges, and handling commissions.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, Swiss corporate and tax law impose an economic-substance benchmark through Art. 50 DBG (effective seat and actual administration in Switzerland), the Swiss Criminal Code prohibition of sham transactions, and the tax treaty beneficial-ownership and LOB principles. For the air-cargo charter broker AG, the Swiss tax administration expects physical office presence, meaningful managerial decision-making in Switzerland, sufficient operational staff, genuine commercial activity in charter brokering, and locally maintained books and records; a pure letter-box company with no operational substance is exposed to retroactive denial of DTA relief and anti-avoidance reassessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud / Forged Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 176 DBG (Steuerbetrug), conviction carries imprisonment of up to 6 months and a fine; under Art. 175 DBG, simple tax evasion is fined up to 3 times the evaded tax, plus the evaded tax itself and 4.5% annual interest; supplementary Federal Act penalties may apply for customs-duty or VAT-related fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted personally, face pre-trial detention and international travel bans in mutual legal assistance proceedings, be held civilly liable under Art. 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations for restitution of the company's tax losses, and be disqualified from board service; where forged corporate certifications are involved, directors may face imprisonment of up to 5 years under Art. 251 StGB.
DTA Shopping / Shell Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding tax relief; retroactive imposition of 35% dividend/interest or royalty Swiss levy where applicable; reassessment of taxable Swiss profits with penalties; Swiss anti-avoidance principles permit annulment of abusive structures; use of the conduit may also trigger the Estonian/VAT base harmonization, where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be regarded as day-to-day executive officers and assessed personally for penalties; civil restitution claims may be brought by the Swiss tax administration against trustees and board members who advanced the sham; criminal proceedings under StGB Art. 251 and 305bis may lead to travel bans and asset seizure.
Money Laundering / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis StGB imposes up to 3 years imprisonment or a monetary penalty for intentional money laundering; negligent conduct is punishable by a fine of up to CHF 500,000 under Art. 305ter StGB; assets derived from the offense are confiscated under Art. 70 StGB; FINMA or the Swiss AML authority may impose cease-and-desist orders and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally exposed as principals or accomplices, may be subject to immediate provisional detention and travel bans, personal bank accounts may be frozen, and private assets may be confiscated; the financial supervisory sanction may include disqualification from managing any Swiss licensed entity.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Artificial Distributions

Statutory sanction: The Swiss Verrechnungssteuergesetz (VStG) requires the AG to pay the 35% WHT to ESTV; non-withheld amounts are collected retroactively with 4.5% annual interest; criminal sanctions include fines and imprisonment under Art. 44-46 VStG for willful dangerous actions, plus an administrative fine of up to three times the unpaid WHT in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers who order or implement artificial distributions are jointly and severally liable to ESTV for uncleared WHT and interest; the corporate veil is pierced where the distribution was made in the personal interest of the directors or UBO, leading to civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment in aggravated fraudulent cases.
