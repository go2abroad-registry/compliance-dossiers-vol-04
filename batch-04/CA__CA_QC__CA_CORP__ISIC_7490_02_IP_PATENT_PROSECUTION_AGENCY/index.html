Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account are mandated under the QBCA. A corporation may issue shares for nominal consideration, e.g. CAD 1.00 per share; however, the full consideration must be received by the corporation before share certificates are issued, and directors may be jointly liable for shares issued without adequate consideration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety certificate from the local fire prevention service, a certificate of structural and electrical compliance from the municipal building authority, and an employer registration with CNESST. For workplaces, implement an emergency evacuation plan, maintain adequate first-aid supplies, appoint a first-aid provider where required, and ensure that all premises, fixtures and furnishings meet the applicable occupational health and safety standards prescribed by the CNESST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the corporation is generally a taxable Canadian corporation if incorporated in Canada; it is taxed as a separate legal person and is subject to federal Part I tax on worldwide income and Quebec provincial income tax on the portion of income allocated to a permanent establishment in Quebec. No pass-through treatment is available for this limited-liability corporation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available; entity classification follows corporate law and tax treaty context. Available elections include: allocation of the small business deduction limit among associated corporations using CRA T2 Schedule 8; election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 to be taxed on net Canadian rental income; election under ITA s. 217 in respect of certain non-resident employment income; and foreign affiliate elections under ITA s. 93 for deemed dividend treatment. Canada has enacted hybrid mismatch rules consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2; those rules operate automatically and do not confer an elective opt-in or opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 for both GST/HST (worldwide taxable supplies) and QST (taxable supplies made in Quebec) over any four consecutive calendar quarters. Foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Canada or Quebec must register when the applicable threshold is exceeded, even in the absence of a permanent establishment, and may use the simplified non-resident registration procedures if eligible.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of patent prosecution services may be zero-rated for GST/HST purposes under Part V, item 7 of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. The QST regime contains an analogous zero-rating for exported services supplied in Quebec. The agency must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is resident outside Canada, the supply does not relate to Canadian-situated property under the statutory exception, and the service is genuinely consumed outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B3-3 / B3ECO) for any tangible deliverables, prototypes, or exhibits accompanying the service
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill for any physical cross-border shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank confirmation (e.g., SWIFT MT103) evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident client
  • Executed service agreement, commercial invoice, and engagement letter describing the patent prosecution services supplied to the non-resident
  • Non-resident status evidence of the recipient, such as a foreign corporate registry excerpt, declaration of non-residency, or relevant tax treaty residency certificate

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API transaction reporting, or a digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenues greater than C$1 million must file returns electronically. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns for registrants meeting defined turnover thresholds and encourages use of their secure online portal; standard XML formats are accepted for mass filing. No statutory nationwide continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing obligation exists.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rating claim and claw back input tax credits or refunds where documentary substantiation is deficient. Gross negligence penalties generally reach 40% of the tax avoided, with interest running from the date the amount was originally refunded or unreported. Misrepresentation may extend the normal reassessment period by up to 10 years. Fraudulent claims attract civil penalties and can lead to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act (s. 327) and Quebec sales tax legislation, with fines and imprisonment available. Exporters should retain the full substantiation file for a minimum of six years, and longer in cases of prior misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payments, and repatriation of invested capital by non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange-control approvals, subject to corporate law solvency restrictions under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and to statutory withholding taxes. Cross-border funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. There are no restrictions on maintaining foreign-currency accounts with Canadian banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export receipts. A patent prosecution agency may retain export-derived fees in the original foreign currency, convert them at commercial rates, or remit them abroad without central bank approval. Export receipts and cross-border service payments must be reported in the corporate income tax return, in balance-of-payments surveys when selected by the Bank of Canada, and through FINTRAC large-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reports as applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority and currency issuer); banking prudential supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision and wire reporting by FINTRAC. Canada does not maintain a separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs are not Canadian chartered banks and may not accept deposits or provide bank-guaranteed escrow in Canada. They may operate as money service businesses if registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, applicable money-services-business licensing may be required. Wise and similar EMIs are viable only for low-value disbursement and currency conversion rails; they are not acceptable as the sole clearing institution for patent office fee disbursements, client escrow, or capital injection accounts. A Canadian Schedule I bank account remains the mandatory primary treasury anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity capital injection from a foreign parent, the compliant rail is an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire to the corporation's Canadian operational account. The ordering bank must identify the remitter as the shareholder, and the beneficiary account is the corporate account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. Remittance field 70 should contain a clear reference such as 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION SHARE CAPITAL INV-YYYYMMDD'. Although Canada does not assign standardized payment purpose codes, banks may require classification under the BPM6 category 'Direct investment: equity capital' for regulatory reporting. The corporation must maintain the signed share subscription resolution, directors' resolution, updated share register, and Quebec business records. If the wire equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, the financial institution will file a large transaction report with FINTRAC; the client should be prepared to provide source-of-funds documentation.Outbound disbursements for patent prosecution and trademark registration should be executed by SWIFT MT103 payment orders for international settlements. For payments to CIPO and WIPO, the agency may use official electronic payment portals or bank-drafted wires. SWIFT MT103 is the appropriate instrument for fee settlements to overseas law firms, patent associates, and official offices; SWIFT MT202 is used only for interbank coverage. Commercial letters of credit are generally unsuitable for service-based IP fee payments and are reserved for trade in goods. Domestic CAD clearing should use the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the newer Real-Time Rail (RTR) when necessary; low-value client refunds may be sent through Interac e-Transfer if below applicable limits and permitted by bank policy. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined general rate of 26.5% (federal 15% Part I general corporate rate plus Quebec provincial general corporate rate of 11.5%); a disputable federal 2.5% additional? No - headquarters federal rate is 15% net; combined headline rate as stated.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII of the Income Tax Act applies a 25% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; Quebec does not separately withhold provincial tax on such dividends under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Both the T2 and the CO-17 are due six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; balance-due payments are due two months after year-end (three months for a qualifying CCPC with an SBD claim), with quarterly instalments required when prior-year federal plus Quebec tax obligations exceed prescribed thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 disclosure is triggered when total non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 (good transactions) or CAD 100,000 (services, rents, royalties, and other transfers); a Country-by-Country Report must be filed when the consolidated MNE group revenue is at least CAD 2,300,000,000; all other Canadian entities must contemporaneously prepare OECD-compliant Local/Master Files within three months of an audit demand and retain them for at least six years.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies GAAR — the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s. 245 of the Income Tax Act (and s. 1075.2 of the Quebec Taxation Act) — which disallows tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions absent a genuine business purpose; no formal 'Economic Substance Regulations' exist in Canada, but CRA audit positions require real substance, including employees, premises, IP budgeting, decision-making functions and commercial foreseeability for a patent prosecution agency; shell holdcos holding patent prosecution intangibles without operational substance face reassessment and a 50% gross negligence penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) I.T.A. equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction under s. 239 I.T.A. carries a fine of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 2 years; Quebec Taxation Act s. 44 imposes provincial fines and impoundment of property connected to evasion; CRA may additionally impose 100% of the tax sought as part of disgorgement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 I.T.A. for any unremitted Part XIII withholdings, GST/QST amounts, and payroll deductions whether or not collected; during evasion prosecutions, the court may issue travel bans, seize personal bank accounts, and sign restraint orders over residential real estate.
Shell Company / GAAR (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment cancels the claimed tax benefits and restores full taxable income, with arrears interest from the date each reassessed return was due; a 50% gross negligence penalty can stack where the sham arrangement was knowingly induced; the DTA benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test embedded in Canada's tax conventions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be pursued under s. 160 I.T.A. for value taken out through an artificial pipeline; directors who personally orchestrated the GAAR scheme may lose the indemnification privilege of the corporation's insurance and be assessed personally where the shell is struck off or becomes bankrupt.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation and revocation of the money services business licence; criminal charges, upon conviction, may yield imprisonment up to 2 years where laundering and financing is proven; FINTRAC also publishes non-compliance assessments in the public registry.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who directed or authorized the breach are personally guilty of the offence and liable to the same penalties; in indictable proceedings, personal forfeiture orders, travel bans, and freezing of corporate bank accounts are used routinely by the Enforcement Bureau against responsible executives.
