Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration Fédérale des Contributions AFC), Federal Department of Finance; federal VAT and corporate income tax are administered via the ESTV e-Service VAT portal (eservices.estv.admin.ch); the Swiss company and UID register is accessible via Zefix (zefix.ch).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss AG is a resident corporation taxed as an independent taxpayer on worldwide profits; no pass-through or transparent treatment; qualifying dividend/participation capital gains benefit from the Swiss participation relief; foreign permanent establishment income sourced in treaty jurisdictions is exempt with progression under most DTTs, while Swiss-source profits remain fully taxable in Switzerland.

Available election forms

Swiss law has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) voluntary VAT registration even below the CHF 100,000 threshold under Art. 11 VAT Act; (ii) elective notional interest deduction on qualifying equity capital with the approval of the tax administration; (iii) elective patent/IP box relief in most cantons (profit allocation election, up to 90% income relief); (iv) elective VAT group registration under Art. 11(3) VAT Act for closely related persons; (v) hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily under Swiss implementation of BEPS Action 2, with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide taxable turnover for businesses established in Switzerland (Art. 10(2)(a) VAT Act); non-established businesses performing taxable supplies in Switzerland must register without a threshold benefit unless all supplies are subject to the recipient's reverse-charge liability; for the ISIC 5210 kitting/assembly/fulfilment scope, services relating to movable tangible property are deemed supplied where the goods are located (Art. 8(1) VAT Act), so Swiss VAT exposure arises on all Swiss-based fulfilment services regardless of the foreign status of the principal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% (exempt with input VAT credit) applies to: (i) export of goods physically removed from Swiss customs territory, evidenced by the customs export declaration (Art. 23(1)(a) VAT Act); (ii) services directly related to export or import of goods where the consideration is included in the taxable customs value under Art. 23(1)(b) VAT Act, which is the only permissible zero-rating route for kitting/assembly services performed on export goods in a Swiss fulfilment centre; (iii) services to recipients domiciled abroad whose benefit is wholly utilized abroad (Art. 23(2) VAT Act), which for pure storage/warehousing in Switzerland is not available because the place of supply remains the goods' Swiss location; and (iv) international transport services. Mere invoicing of a foreign principal does not satisfy the zero-rating condition; the kitting consideration must either be included in the custom's value of the exported goods or the service must otherwise meet the statutory export-service nexus test.

  • Customs export declaration (e-dec/NCTS or T1/T2 transit movement with Swiss customs exit confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, explicitly stating that the kitting/assembly consideration is included in the customs value where Art. 23(1)(b) VAT Act zero-rating is claimed
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit of the goods from Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement record evidencing payment of the consideration within the documentary window
  • Export contract/purchase order and the foreign principal's VAT or EORI identification details
  • Declarations of origin (EUR.1, A.TR, REX statement) where preferential origin is claimed (not a VAT zero-rating requirement)
  • Intrastat declaration for goods movements to the EU, where applicable
  • Warehouse inventory, picking/packing, and assembly work orders connecting the specific supplied goods to the export shipment

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API submission, and no digital stamping regime. Invoices must satisfy Art. 46 VAT Act content requirements (supplier and recipient names with UID-MWST/TVA/IVA, date, nature and quantity of supply, consideration excluding VAT, applicable rate, and VAT amount stated separately). The QR-bill payment slip standard is mandatory on payment slips issued by Swiss payees but is not a fiscal clearance document. VAT returns are filed periodically via the e-Service VAT portal with post-filing documentary audits conducted by the ESTV; business and invoice records must be retained for 10 years.

The ESTV conducts post-filing documentary audits of export zero-rating claims and retains a 10-year assessment limitation period. If zero-rating is claimed without the mandatory export evidence or with false statements, the tax authority retrospectively assesses the supplies at 8.1% plus default interest (currently 4.5% p.a.) and claims clawback back to the first infringing tax period. Under Art. 96 VAT Act, deliberate tax evasion attracts fines of up to four times the evaded VAT; negligent procedural violations attract fines of up to CHF 10,000 under Art. 97 VAT Act, without prejudice to criminal proceedings. Swiss federal law contains no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day repatriation window is a documentary and bank-KYC compliance standard intended to preserve the payment evidence that substantiates the zero-rated export transaction.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible with no exchange controls, no capital flow licensing, and no prior approval gate for foreign direct investment into a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. Profit remittance via dividend distribution is free from a currency perspective but carries a standard 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which may be reduced to 0–5% under an applicable double tax treaty or refunded under the participation relief if the foreign shareholder holds at least a 10% stake for the relevant holding period. Interest on shareholder loans and fees for cross-border services are not subject to withholding tax. The SNB operates a negative interest policy on sight deposits only in exceptional situations; no capital adequacy or foreign-currency asset ratio applies to non-bank corporates. Balance-of-payments statistical reporting may be required if the direct investment related cross-border flows exceed CHF 10 million in a calendar year; this is informational, not approval-based.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation time limit apply to export proceeds for a Swiss AG. The CH_AG entity may lawfully retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currencies (EUR, USD, CNY, GBP) in any Swiss or foreign banking account, provided the economic substance of the transaction is supported by trade documents (invoices, logistics contracts, customs declarations). The only residual obligation is proper accounting under the Swiss Code of Obligations and, where the entity is VAT registered, accurate MWST (Swiss VAT) declaration; there is no SNB convertibility or forced-sale rule affecting the kitting/assembly export cash flow.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank (Banque Nationale Suisse)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a payment overlay, Medium as a standalone treasury platform. Airwallex (licensed/authorised as an Electronic Money Institution in the EEA/UK and as a Major Payment Institution in Singapore, with FINMA-recognised banking partners for SIC settlement) can support EUR/USD/CNH receipts and disbursements, multi-currency virtual IBANs, and wholesale FX pricing. The core legal constraint is that a non-Swiss EMI cannot settle CHF-denominated payments directly on the Swiss SIC rail; CHF clearing must pass through a Swiss-domiciled bank, meaning the CH_AG must retain at least one primary Swiss banking relationship. The EMI may be used as the front-end collection/disbursement engine, but statutory segregation of funds and Swiss AML obligations for a financial intermediary must be demonstrated, and material operating liquidity must not be held exclusively offshore.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a cash equity injection: (a) send the funds via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the CH_AG's Swiss account, (b) state in the remittance information field the words 'Kapitaleinlage / Capital Increase', and (c) ensure the bank can map the flow to the SNB balance-of-payments statistics as an equity capital inflow. If the contribution is a shareholder loan, route with reference to a signed intercompany loan agreement and apply at least arm's length interest; Swiss withholding tax is not triggered on interest, but the debt-equity ratio must remain commercially defensible. For a contribution in kind (e.g., contribution of receivables or inventory), a notarial report and a specific valuation statement under the Swiss Code of Obligations (Sacheinlage rules) must be recorded; the capital escrow (Sperrkonto) only satisfies the paid-up capital verification if held at a Swiss bank and released against the notarial certification of incorporation.Clearing rails: (a) SIC/Swiss Interbank Clearing for same-day CHF domestic settlements, including payroll and local supplier payments; (b) SEPA Instant for euro-denominated disbursements across the EEA without correspondent bank friction; (c) SWIFT MT103 for high-value cross-border supplier payments above CHF 50,000 where validation of the beneficiary bank is required; (d) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for bulk inventory purchases where the counterparty requires documentary credit security; and (e) Airwallex virtual accounts for low-ticket recurring producer payments in China, the United States, and Southeast Asia, with transactions subordinated to the primary Swiss account for cash pooling. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a payment overlay, Medium as a standalone treasury platform. The core legal constraint is that a non-Swiss EMI cannot settle CHF-denominated payments directly on the Swiss SIC rail; CHF clearing must pass through a Swiss-domiciled bank, meaning the CH_AG must retain at least one primary Swiss banking relationship. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on post-tax profit (approximately 7.8% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal, and communal effective rate generally between 12% and 24% depending on the canton, e.g., Zug approximately 14.5%, Zurich approximately 19.7%, Geneva approximately 24.2%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends paid by the Swiss AG to both Swiss and non-Swiss shareholders; the withholding is a tax retention remitted to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA) and constitutes a refundable or creditable charge depending on the recipient's residence.

Six months after the statutory fiscal year-end (Art. 126 DBG); cantonal authorities routinely grant extension upon reasoned request up to 30 June for calendar-year taxpayers, and in exceptional cases up to 30 September or later; late filing interest and procedural penalties apply after the extended deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory per-transaction threshold is codified, but the Swiss Tax Administration requires contemporaneous Local File documentation where intragroup transactions are material, commonly applied to transactions exceeding CHF 500,000 per category or CHF 5 million in aggregate cross-border related-party turnover; Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual turnover of at least CHF 900 million, while Master File documentation is expected for significant cross-border intercompany operations regardless of turnover.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate an explicit Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the form used by UAE or Cayman Islands; nonetheless, Swiss tax residence, treaty access, and ordinary commercial status require: (i) statutory seat and effective place of management within Switzerland, (ii) real premises, personnel, and board governance with genuine decision-making, (iii) substantive operational capacity proportionate to the AG's 'kitting, assembly, and fulfillment' activities, and (iv) no treaty-shopping, mailbox structure, or nominee-management abuse; the Swiss GAAR and the OECD Beneficial Ownership/Principal Purpose Test may deny DTA benefits to a purely formal Swiss AG, and a foreign place of effective management triggers deemed migration with full taxation of worldwide income and potential exit taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Federal and cantonal fines up to three times the evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG; administrative surcharge of at least 25% of the evaded amount in gross negligence cases; mandatory default interest of 4.5% per annum; criminal prosecution under Art. 186 DBG and Swiss Criminal Code for particularly serious cases, with custodial sentences possible in conjunction with money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for damages caused by unlawful acts (Art. 754 Swiss Code of Obligations), face personal fines and disqualification, civil restitution orders to the tax authority, travel bans during tax execution proceedings, and imprisonment for aggravated fiscal fraud or associated money laundering.
Non-Remittance of Swiss Anticipatory Tax (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: Statutory claim against the company for the full 35% gross amount plus penalty interest; administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 under the Federal Withholding Tax Act; the paid item remains non-deductible at the corporate level; in cases of willful omission criminal fines and publication of the decision may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorized signatories are jointly and severally liable for the withheld amount under Art. 12 VStG; execution proceedings may include seizure of personal assets, asset-freezing orders, travel bans, and criminal monetary penalties for complicity.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation or Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income to the commercial arm's-length level; disallowance of excess payments; recharacterization of the excess as a taxable dividend triggering 35% anticipatory tax at the company level; addition of penalty interest of 4.5% p.a.; in repeated cases administrative fines under Art. 175 DBG and professional negligence charges against the responsible corporate officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed non-arm's-length contracts bear civil liability for the resulting tax deficiency, may be ordered to make restitution to the company, and face personal enforcement against Swiss and foreign assets; where the company becomes insolvent, criminal exposure for fraudulent bankruptcy or mismanagement under Art. 165 Swiss Criminal Code may lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company Abuse and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as tax-resident at its place of effective management abroad, triggering reassessment of tax exemption or full taxation in the foreign jurisdiction; denial of DTA benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test and Swiss abuse rules; retroactive tax assessments with penalty interest; potential deregistration or compulsory liquidation through the commercial register where the corporate purpose is untrue.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for false declarations in the commercial registry and for unlawful management; exposure includes civil restitution for unpaid corporate taxes, personal travel bans issued under Swiss debt-execution procedures, and imprisonment for criminal mismanagement, fraudulent bankruptcy, or falsification of corporate documents.
