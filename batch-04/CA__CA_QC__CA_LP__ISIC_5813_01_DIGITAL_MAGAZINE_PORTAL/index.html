Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST administration through the Business Number (BN) portal 'My Business Account'; Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) administration and registration via the 'Entreprises Québec' portal using the Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ), with joint GST/HST-QST filing through a single combined return administered by Revenu Québec.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1, or written application, selecting the GST/HST account; a non-resident general partner or a resident partner must act as the designated representative, and the LP must appoint a Canadian agent for service of process if it carries on business in Quebec without a resident partner. Step 3: Complete QST registration with Revenu Québec (Form LM-1-V thereafter integrated into the BN/NEQ linkage) to obtain a QST number and the 'CléNuméro d'accès' for the Revenu Québec online portal. Step 4: For non-resident partners, file Form RC371 (Non-Resident Partnership Information Return) and ensure each partner's Canadian residency status is documented, as the LP itself is not an income tax payer but must file an information return (Form T5013) and allocate income to partners. Effective registration dates follow the small-supplier threshold rules; voluntary registration is permitted once a Canadian permanent establishment or taxable supply activity exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA) and the Taxation Act (Quebec). For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' required to register, collect, and remit tax in its own name. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA) and the Taxation Act (Quebec). For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' required to register, collect, and remit tax in its own name. A limited partner's deductible loss is restricted to the adjusted cost base of the partnership interest (at-risk rules), and the partnership must use a calendar-year fiscal period unless an election is made to use a different business fiscal period.

Available election forms

No IRS-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada; entity classification is determined by statute (LP = transparent). Available elections include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under subsection 249.1(4) ITA permitting a non-calendar-year business period where all partners are non-residents or, in narrowly defined cases, for Canadian resident partners; (ii) GST/HST joint election under section 273 ETA to treat closely related corporations/partnerships as a single person for registration and compliance; (iii) GST/HST quick method election and annual remittance election to simplify filing; (iv) election under section 216 ITA for non-resident partners earning Canadian rental income from the partnership; (v) Quebec parallel elections under the Taxation Act and the QST Act, including the election to align QST registration with GST registration; and (vi) the voluntary disclosure election to the CRA and Revenu Québec to correct past filing positions before a compliance action begins.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST (Excise Tax Act, section 240) and QST (Quebec Sales Tax Act, section 27), measured on the worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports and certain financial services) of the partnership together with associates, over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory the moment the threshold is exceeded, with an effective date at that time or earlier upon request.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of digital magazine content, subscriptions, and sponsored advertising placements are eligible for the 0% export rate when supplied to a non-resident person (not an individual who is present in Canada, and not a registrant carrying on business in Canada) under sections 5 and 7 of Part V, Schedule VI ETA (services to non-residents and advertising services, respectively), and under the mirror provisions of the QST Act (Schedule IV, sections 5 and 7). The portal must demonstrably establish that: (i) the recipient is a non-resident, (ii) the digital property is delivered electronically to a foreign IP address or foreign-based account, (iii) the contract is negotiated and executed abroad, and (iv) payment is received from a foreign financial institution. For corporate subscribers, a completed and signed form of the CRA prescribed 'Exports — Non-Resident Status Certificate' (or equivalent documentary evidence, such as a certificate of incorporation, tax residency certificate, or foreign VAT number) should be retained. Advertising services supplied to a non-resident advertiser are zero-rated even if the advertisement is intended to be directed at a Canadian audience, provided the 'directly in connection with real property in Canada or a service rendered in respect of the property' exceptions do not apply; periodic legal review of the supply chain is required to confirm that no supply of a Canadian advertising agency's services in Canada or any supply made to a Canadian resident is bundled with the zero-rated supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED) — required only for physical shipments of printed magazines or promotional materials crossing the Canadian border; electronically delivered digital content falls outside the customs declaration requirement but must be evidenced by log files and delivery records
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — mandatory for any physical export shipment to substantiate the goods leaving Canada and to match with the customs declaration and the commercial invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — a bank-issued certificate, SWIFT MT103, or foreign currency credit advice evidencing receipt of payment in Canadian dollars or convertible foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty, to be matched to each zero-rated export invoice

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance regime, government digital stamping, or QR-code clearance for e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted without prior fiscal authorization, provided the invoice contains all prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (sections 3, 169, 223) and the QST Act (sections 26–30), including vendor name and BN, recipient name, date of supply, consideration, GST/QST rates and amounts, and a description of the digital magazine portal services. Taxpayers must retain electronic records in a readable, auditable format accessible to the CRA and Revenu Québec on request; there is no API-based fiscal clearance, but payment data from Stripe and AdSense must be reconciled against invoice-level transaction logs to support zero-rating claims and input tax credit claims, and any large-volume e-invoicing system should maintain a tamper-evident audit trail to withstand audit scrutiny.

CRA and Revenu Québec aggressively audit digital exporters; where zero-rating substantiation is incomplete, both authorities may reassess the entire revenue stream as taxable at 14.975% (GST/QST), deny corresponding input tax credits, and re-characterize the amounts for income tax, resulting in compound statutory interest on the unpaid balances from the original filing due date. Penalties include: (i) late-filing and late-remittance penalties of 5% of the amount owing plus 1% per full month up to 12 months (ETA sections 280 and 284; QTA parallel rules); (ii) gross negligence penalties of up to 25% of the net tax misstated for GST/HST and up to 50% for income tax false statements; (iii) false statements or omissions penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per person under ETA section 285.1; (iv) Québec administrative monetary penalties under the Tax Administration Act; and (v) personal liability of directors of the corporate general partner for unremitted GST/QST and payroll amounts under ETA section 323 and QTA section 31, without the due-diligence defence applying where the director knew of the deficiency. Retrospective clawbacks generally extend four years from the date of the original assessment (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or fraud), and the Voluntary Disclosures Program (CRA) and the Québec Voluntary Disclosure Program may mitigate penalties, but not interest, if a qualifying voluntary disclosure is filed before any audit notification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no foreign exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance. Non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax on allocated Canadian-source income under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), with treaty relief available under the Canada–US Tax Treaty and other applicable treaties. No prior governmental approval is required for dividend/distribution remittances, only tax compliance through Form NR4 and Form NR424.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export or advertising revenue proceeds in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in any currency in a domestic or foreign bank account, and no repatriation deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers money laundering reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury clearing rail. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada). They may operate only as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, but they cannot hold wrapped Canadian deposits or provide CDIC insurance. The LP may use an EMI for collection (e.g., Stripe, AdSense payouts), but funds must be swept into a Canadian tier-1 bank account for statutory and tax compliance.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Provide full legal name, residential address, source of wealth, source of funds, and tax identification number for each limited partner; notarized passport pages; proprietary beneficial ownership attestation; organizational chart showing every owner up to the ultimate natural person; evidence of the Canadian general partner's substantive management functions.
High-volume, low-ticket digital advertising receipts (AdSense/ Stripe) creating fragmented transaction patterns.

Root cause: High-volume, low-ticket digital advertising receipts (AdSense/ Stripe) creating fragmented transaction patterns.

Defense evidence: Submit a signed business plan confirming digital magazine operations, sample executed advertising contracts, processing history from Stripe/AdSense dashboard, and a wire-level audit trail for all cross-border settlement receipts; maintain a reconciliation ledger linking each deposit to a specific publication or sponsorship invoice.
Inward capital contributions from non-bank financial intermediaries or EMI corporate credit cards.

Root cause: Inward capital contributions from non-bank financial intermediaries or EMI corporate credit cards.

Defense evidence: Provide a capital contribution certificate, the bank statement of the sending institution (where available), an anti-money laundering (AML) source-of-funds declaration certified by a qualified Canadian accountant, and confirmation that no third-party lending or currency exchange is embedded in the contribution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Quebec limited partnership must route via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers. Use SWIFT purpose code 'BEQU' (capital payment) or 'PCAP' (capital contribution) for non-resident inbound; for distributions from digital advertising, use the commercial purpose code 'ADV' (advertising revenue). Funds must reference the LP's Business Number (BN) and the bank's internal customer ID. Verification requires a signed contribution agreement, a board or general partner resolution approving the injection, and a source-of-funds report from the remitting institution. For amounts exceeding CAD 100,000, the bank will file a Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) equivalent under FINTRAC Program Requirements and may request an enhanced due diligence form.Trade settlements for digital magazine royalties, content licensing, and freelance contributors shall be executed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for cross-border wire payments, or through Canadian EFT/ACH for domestic payments. For high-value transactions above CAD 50,000, use the Real-Time Rail (RTR) through Payments Canada where applicable. International content provider contracts above CAD 250,000 may optionally use a commercial letter of credit (LC), but given the low physical goods exposure of ISIC 5813, a telegraphic transfer (T/T) with invoice matching is the standard rail. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal and provincial corporate rate; partnership is pass-through)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on distributions to non-resident partners (Part XIII withholding tax)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 5 months after fiscal year-end (e.g., if year-end Dec 31, due May 31)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total value of transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles; partnerships must demonstrate real business activity and commercial rationale

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239); fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may apply
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR; denial of treaty benefits; penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraudulent schemes

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be jointly and severally liable for corporate taxes and penalties if they authorized or participated in the sham arrangement; travel bans and extradition possible
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs; travel bans and seizure of assets may occur
