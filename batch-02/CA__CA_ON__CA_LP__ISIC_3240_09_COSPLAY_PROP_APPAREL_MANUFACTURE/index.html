Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

PARTIALLY PERMISSIBLE. Under LP Act s. 2(2) and BNA s. 3, the LP must maintain a registered office in Ontario at a physical address where service of process may be effected; a post-office box or mail-forwarding address alone is invalid. A commercial virtual-office service (licensed Ontario registered agent) satisfies the 'registered office' requirement only if: (a) the service holds itself out as accepting process on behalf of the LP; (b) the registered office is not also used as the 'principal place of business' if it is only a virtual address; and (c) the LP maintains a separate actual operational premises in Ontario where manufacturing, storage, or administrative work is performed. For ISIC 3240 cosplay prop/apparel manufacturing, a purely virtual presence would cause: (i) bank account rejection (chartered banks require a physical operating address for a manufacturing business); (ii) CRA audit risk on GST/HST input tax credits and commercial substance; (iii) municipal zoning and licensing exposure under the Planning Act, RSO 1990, c P.13 where garment/prop manufacturing requires light-industrial zoning; and (iv) Shopify Payments/Stripe underwriting refusal, because both payment processors require a verifiable physical business location for a manufacturing merchant. Practical recommendation: register the LP at a virtual office for service-of-process only, and lease a small light-industrial or shared-maker-space unit in the GTA (e.g., Toronto, Mississauga, Markham) as the principal place of business.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) under the Excise Tax Act; primary portal is CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/register-business-number.html. Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) coordinates Ontario provincial tax matters, while ServiceOntario handles registration of Ontario Limited Partnerships and extra-provincial filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic limited partnership must first register with ServiceOntario through an Ontario limited partnership declaration or, if formed outside Ontario, as an extra-provincial limited partnership. The partnership must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online or, for non-residents filing without a Social Insurance Number, the appropriate CRA account application process including Form RC348 where required. Program accounts to activate include the GST/HST account (RT), payroll and import/export accounts as applicable; the BN acts as the universal identifier for both income tax information production and GST/HST administration. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the partnership itself is flow-through for income tax but is treated as a separate registrant person for GST/HST purposes, so registration and tax returns must be in the partnership name with its own BN, not merely in the names of individual partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. An Ontario Limited Partnership is automatically fiscally transparent for income tax, with income or loss allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level under section 96 of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes the partnership may elect the Quick Method using Form GST74 if eligibility is met, may elect simplified filing or representative arrangements for non-resident registrants, and must account for Ontario HST at the combined rate rather than charging federal GST alone. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act apply automatically and cannot be overridden by an elective check-the-box classification. Partnership information returns and partner allocation schedules (T5013 family) remain mandatory even though the LP has no entity-level federal income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person is a small supplier until its worldwide taxable supplies, excluding financial services and sales of capital property, exceed CAD 30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration becomes mandatory at that threshold for persons carrying on business in Canada; a non-resident carrying on business in Ontario must register once the threshold is met, and registration may also be elected earlier to recover input tax credits. Ontario HST applies at 13% (5% federal GST plus 8% Ontario provincial component) on taxable supplies made in Ontario.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada may be zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, including goods delivered by the supplier to a common carrier, courier, or mailing service for export, or goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier with adequate proof. Services supplied to non-resident persons may also be zero-rated if they are not performed primarily in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For a cosplay costume and prop apparel manufacturer, export sales of physical goods to U.S. or offshore retailers are normally zero-rated only if the goods leave Canada and the exporter retains contemporaneous documentary evidence. The registrant must also exclude export revenues from HST collection and may claim input tax credits attributable to export activity; if the exporter is not a registrant, no input tax credit recovery is available for HST embedded in production inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration or Canada Border Services Agency export reporting records, including carrier manifest or Canada Export Declaration transmission evidencing departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and carrier or freight forwarder certificate confirming physical export and destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT payment advice, or bank/payment-processor statement evidencing payment received from the non-resident purchaser

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, government invoice stamping, or continuous transaction control. A GST/HST registrant must issue and retain invoices containing prescribed ETA information, including the vendor's BN and the amount of GST/HST charged or indicating zero-rated supply. Electronic invoicing is permitted and CRA recognizes digital records if they are complete, unaltered, and retained for six years. CRA accepts GST/HST return filing electronically through NETFILE and related APIs, and the federal government has adopted PEPPOL e-invoicing for its own procurement; no provincial API clearance or digital stamping obligation applies to cosplay apparel manufacturing sales.

The CRA can reassess export zero-rating claims within the ordinary four-year reassessment window, and without time limit in cases of misrepresentation, gross negligence, or fraud. If the required customs and payment documentation is not produced, the CRA will treat the sale as taxable in Canada, assess HST at 13% on the proceeds, deny or claw back associated input tax credits, and add statutory interest. False export claims may also trigger false-statement penalties, gross-negligence penalties, and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Export records, including all customs documentation, shipping contracts, payment receipts, and correspondence with buyers, must be retained for at least six years to support the zero-rated treatment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime. No statutory distinction is drawn between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Non-resident limited partners may freely convert CAD into foreign currency, repatriate capital, and remit partnership profits abroad without central bank exchange control approval. Remittance friction arises only from tax compliance, principally Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on partnership income or guaranteed payments and applicable tax treaty reductions; no exchange licence or central bank permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio. The Ontario limited partnership may retain export earnings in foreign currency bank accounts and convert at institutional commercial rates. FINTRAC record-keeping and OSFI-oriented bank compliance may require supporting invoices for large export receipts, but there is no sovereign exchange control requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as federal prudential regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisory authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary collection and disbursement channel; Low as a standalone treasury or capital-escrow rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex are not licensed banks under the Bank Act and cannot replace a Schedule I Canadian bank for segregated capital, LC issuance, or LVTS clearing. Such EMIs may support outgoing supplier payments and incoming consumer receivables but must be subordinated to a primary Canadian bank account. A Canadian-resident money services business relationship would trigger FINTRAC registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; a pure offshore receivable collection model is permissible but carries enhanced compliance friction.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% combined federal + Ontario headline for active manufacturing income (15% federal + 10% Ontario M&P); 0% at the Limited Partnership entity level due to fiscally transparent pass-through classification

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. Reduced to 15% generally, and 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% voting shares, under applicable tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate controlled cross-border transactions in a fiscal year triggers expectation of contemporaneous Local File-style transfer pricing documentation. Country-by-Country Report and Master File obligations apply to multinational groups with EUR 750 million (approx. CAD 1.1 billion) consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to the UAE. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in ITA s.245, the sham doctrine, and CRA's substance-over-form approach apply. A passive Ontario limited partnership with no physical presence, no employees, and no genuine manufacturing activity for cosplay props or apparel is vulnerable to recharacterization as a shell and reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. On summary conviction, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day. A civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax may also apply under ITA s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be jointly and severally liable for corporate or partnership tax penalties where they directed or acquiesced in the fraud. CRA can enforce civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and pursue imprisonment.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under ITA s.245 will disallow the tax benefit, restore taxable income, and impose interest and penalties. The CRA may also apply Part XIII withholding tax on deemed dividends or distributions to non-residents, and deny partnership loss allocations.

Director personal liability: General partners and controlling directors may be personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and penalties. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, ordering civil restitution, director bans, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for deliberate structuring.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the consideration to the arm's-length amount under ITA s.247(2), deny deductions, and impose a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment. If the documentation is absent or grossly incomplete, the gross negligence penalty of 50% may also be asserted.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally responsible for knowingly consenting to false documentation or failing to remit resulting Canadian taxes. Remedies include civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal prosecution in egregious cases.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 250,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for entities per violation. Criminal money laundering includes imprisonment up to 14 years and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who fail to implement a compliance program or who knowingly facilitate laundering face separate criminal liability, personal fines, civil forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment.
