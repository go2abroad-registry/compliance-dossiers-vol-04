Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a resident taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations. The entity is taxed on worldwide income; the combined general corporate income tax rate in Ontario is 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%). Because the parent is non-resident, the subsidiary is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), therefore the small business deduction and its reduced rates (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario) are unavailable; active-business income is taxed at the general 26.5% rate, and dividends paid to a foreign parent are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, reducible under the applicable tax treaty.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists; legal form is conclusive. Available elections: (i) GST/HST group registration election under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act to consolidate returns of closely related Canadian corporations and their non-resident group members; (ii) Income Tax Act tax-deferred rollover elections (ss. 85, 85.1, 86, 88) for transferring assets or shares into the Ontario corporation; (iii) Advance Pricing Arrangement with CRA for transfer-pricing certainty on colocation fees; (iv) treaty-based permanent establishment and branch attribution elections; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (BEPS Action 2) operate as mandatory disallowances or inclusions without an opt-in election; and (vi) Ontario does not provide the ULC cross-border disregarded-entity vehicle available in Alberta, British Columbia, or Nova Scotia.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII (status accepted as of 1951); no exchange-control authorization is required for capital inflows, dividend remittances, interest payments, or loan repatriations; profit remittances to non-residents are subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% on dividends under the Canada-US treaty for substantial shareholdings) and are not subject to any FX approval mechanism

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement applies in Canada; export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely, converted at the bank's posted rate at the exporter's discretion; there is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision delegated to OSFI; Department of Finance Canada administers residual foreign-exchange policy and the Currency Act framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-moderate viability; Canada has no dedicated EMI licence regime, so an offshore digital EMI cannot conduct deposit-taking and lacks access to LVTS settlement and CDIC deposit insurance; it may operate only as a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) and will need to register as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with Bank of Canada oversight once fully commenced; it is suitable as a payment-rail overlay, not as the primary treasury account

Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid amounts at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur a separate penalty of $1,000.
Line 600 (T2) Net tax payable or refund due. Ensure all installments and withholding remittances are reconciled; late payments accrue interest at prescribed rates. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid amounts at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur a separate penalty of $1,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services). Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 50 million and cross-border related-party transactions ≥ CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to UAE, but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act. Transactions lacking economic substance and solely for tax avoidance may be recharacterized. For data center colocation, substance is evidenced by physical assets, employees, and operational control in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax (s. 227.1) if they failed to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Penalty (s. 247(3))

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment to income (if adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000), plus interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable if they knowingly participated in the mispricing; CRA can pursue personal assets for gross negligence.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful non-compliance.
Unpaid Withholding Tax (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus interest; CRA may assess the payer for the full amount.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unremitted withholding tax under s. 227.1; CRA can issue director liability assessments and freeze personal bank accounts.
