Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Ontario law. At least one general partner and one limited partner are required. Limited partners contribute capital as agreed in the partnership agreement (cash, property, or services); a limited partner who withdraws contributed capital becomes personally liable for partnership obligations up to the withdrawn amount (s. 12). Non-resident ownership is permitted without Canadian residency restrictions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Ontario limited partnership registration administered by ServiceOntario; Ontario Ministry of Finance handles provincial revenue programs such as withheld employer remittances separate from the CRA-administered HST.

E-government filing portal

Register the Ontario limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario) by filing Form 1 Declaration at ServiceOntario. Next, obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online; where a non-resident partner has no Canadian Social Insurance Number or prior BN, submit Form RC348 by mail or fax together with the registered declaration and partnership agreement. Once the BN is issued, activate the required CRA program accounts by appending two-letter suffixes to the 9-digit BN: RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export. The BN/TIN must be quoted on all GST/HST returns, export declarations, T5013 partnership information returns, customs accounts, and payroll or Ontario employer remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; an Ontario LP is a mandatory fiscally transparent partnership under Income Tax Act s.96 and cannot elect to be taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections include the ITA s.216 election enabling a non-resident partner to report Canadian-source rental or timber royalty income on a net basis rather than Part XIII gross withholding, and the prescribed partnership fiscal-period elections under ITA s.249.1. Hybrid mismatch adjustments, where applicable, operate as mandatory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters; exceeding that threshold requires GST/HST registration. Even below the threshold, an exporter of zero-rated goods should register voluntarily to substantiate input tax credits; non-resident sellers carrying on business in Canada, traders using Canadian fulfillment centres, and suppliers distributing through Canadian online platforms may have registration obligations independent of the small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when goods (e.g., FPV drone frames, brushless motors, and assembled racing drones) are sold for export from Canada, are transferred to a destination outside Canada before use or consumption in Canada, and the exporter retains contemporaneous documentary proof of delivery abroad. Services are zero-rated as exported services when supplied to a non-resident client, the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property remaining in Canada, and no benefit or consumption occurs in Canada; repair and configuration services performed on drones to be exported may qualify where the goods are returned to the non-resident purchaser's jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A/EDI filing under the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing goods, value, exporter, and foreign buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing shipment to the foreign destination
  • Foreign customs entry or destination-country import evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Stripe/PayPal transaction log evidencing receipt of proceeds from the foreign buyer

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-connected invoice clearance, or digital stamping regime at the federal or Ontario level. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records; registered registrants must issue an invoice or sales receipt for every taxable sale above CAD 30 when a customer requests it under Excise Tax Act s.223, and must retain books and records sufficient to substantiate net tax and zero-rated exports under ETA s.286. Digital marketplace platform operators that facilitate taxable sales to Canadian consumers may themselves be deemed to make the supply and must comply with CRA simplified registration and reporting obligations.

CRA will deny zero-rating if export cannot be substantiated. Reassessment will collect unpaid GST/HST, interest, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including the net-tax misstatement penalty for false statements or omissions, typically the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated net tax in gross-negligence cases; wilful evasion may lead to criminal fines and imprisonment under ETA s.239. The general partner of the Ontario LP is jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST, and limited partners may face joint liability where they participate in management. Refunds previously paid on fraudulent or unsupported zero-rated exports are recoverable with interest without the normal four-year limitation period where the filing involves misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. There are no exchange control laws, no prior approval for majority or non-resident ownership of an Ontario limited partnership, and no statutory restriction on repatriating profits, dividends, partnership distributions, interest, royalties, or service fees. Remittances are freely executable in CAD or foreign currency through any Canadian authorized bank, subject only to income tax withholding and tax treaty requirements. The normal non-resident limited partner contribution and subsequent profit distribution structure for the FPV racing drone retail business is permitted without Canadian regulatory clearance, provided the underlying activity is non-regulated and no large non-Canadian asset acquisition under the Investment Canada Act is triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. An Ontario LP exporting carbon-fiber FPV drone components or completed racing drones may retain export revenue in foreign currency bank accounts, invoice in USD, and convert to CAD at its discretion. The only applicable obligations are customs export declarations, transaction reporting under FATCA/CRS for accounts held at Canadian banks, and internal application of the Bank of Canada's CAD/USD settlement through large-value payment systems; no statutory rule requires repatriation timing or forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), together with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally chartered bank regulation.
