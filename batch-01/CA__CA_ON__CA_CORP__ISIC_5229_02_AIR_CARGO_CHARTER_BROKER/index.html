Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA); Ontario corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Register online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or electronically; a non-resident corporation must provide certified incorporation documents, foreign registration particulars, and Canadian contact/agent details. CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RT (GST/HST) and RC (corporate income tax). Ontario payroll source deductions and Employer Health Tax use the same BN; no separate Ontario income tax registration is required because the CRA collects Ontario corporate income tax under the collection agreement. Processing takes 2–10 business days. A non-resident may apply for optional GST/HST registration even if below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in order to claim input tax credits. A non-resident with no permanent establishment but carrying on business in Canada must still register for GST/HST (s.240 Excise Tax Act) and file T2 corporate returns for Canadian-source business income.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: an Ontario/federal Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source business income and gains from taxable Canadian property. Shareholders are not taxed on corporate income until dividends are paid, and Canada applies a partially integrated corporate-shareholder tax system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Ontario/federal Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source business income and gains from taxable Canadian property. Shareholders are not taxed on corporate income until dividends are paid, and Canada applies a partially integrated corporate-shareholder tax system. Canada does not permit check-the-box flow-through classification for corporations; the statutory definition of 'corporation' in s.248(1) ITA always yields entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

Available elections: (a) s.125(5.1) associated-CCPC allocation of the CAD 500,000 small business deduction (SBD) limit among associated Canadian-controlled private corporations; (b) s.256(3) designated-member election for associated corporations; (c) GST/HST s.211(1) branch/division separate supply election; (d) s.219(2) non-resident branch tax election (Part I vs. Part XIV); (e) s.216 election for rental income of non-residents; (f) hybrid mismatch disallowance rules (ss.18.2/18.4 ITA, effective 1 July 2022) — no proactive check-the-box election exists; the rules automatically deny deductions for hybrid mismatch outcomes; (g) s.150(5) short-year election and capital cost allowance elections. Canada does not offer U.S.-style check-the-box elections (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3) for common-law corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold for supplies made in Canada (s.240 ETA); non-residents making only zero-rated exports may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The air-cargo charter broker may zero-rate: (1) exported goods (s.1, Part V, Sch. VI ETA) — possession and title must pass to the customer and the goods must be exported within 60 days of the supply or as soon as reasonably possible; (2) international air transportation of goods from a Canadian point to a destination outside Canada (s.20, Sch. VI), including air cargo charter fees; (3) freight-forwarding, brokerage, and customs-arrangement services supplied in respect of zero-rated international air transport (s.2, Sch. VI); and (4) services supplied to a non-resident person for use wholly outside Canada (s.7, Sch. VI). Domestic air-cargo or brokerage services that do not meet these tests are taxable at 13% HST. Zero-rating requires contemporaneous export proof: CBSA export record, master/house air waybill, commercial invoice with HS classification, charter agreement, and remittance evidence; missing or inadequate proof revives HST liability.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA G7 e-Export or CAED declaration), including AES filing for US-bound air cargo
  • Commercial invoice with supplier/exporter/consignee details, HS tariff classification, quantity, value, and aircraft origin-destination information
  • Bill of lading or master/house air waybill evidencing carriage by air from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Air cargo manifest or certificate of insurance evidencing the international carriage
  • Charter party agreement or broker service agreement with non-resident charterer/consignor for air cargo charter services
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103/Fedwire record demonstrating receipt of export proceeds (documentary practice evidence; the CRA does not statutorily require a specific certificate for zero-rating)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime applies to GST/HST. Canada launched a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework on the Peppol standard (from Q3 2024) through the Digital Governance Council; adoption is not a precondition for GST/HST returns. Registered corporations with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5M must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE). Invoices and supporting records must be retained for six years (s.286 ETA). Export zero-rating does not require a government-issued tax clearance certificate, only documentary evidence of export (CBSA export declaration, air waybills, commercial invoices, and payment records).

No Canadian statute imposes a 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day field is a due-diligence audit benchmark — prolonged non-receipt beyond 180 days raises sham-transaction and artificial-invoice risk. The CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years (no limitation period for fraud or misrepresentation) and income tax within four years (normal), seven years (neglect), or unlimited (fraud). If zero-rating is denied on audit, the exporter is liable for uncollected HST plus interest under s.280 ETA and penalties under s.285(1) ETA of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated net tax; first-time voluntary disclosure under the CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program may reduce penalties if filed before audit contact. Criminal tax evasion under ss.326/327 ETA carries fines up to CAD 25,000 (summary conviction) or CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years (indictment); the general anti-avoidance rule (s.274 ETA / s.245 ITA) may be invoked for artificial export arrangements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and payment-systems authority. Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration: the Exchange Control Board and its statute were abolished in 1951, so there is no central-bank approval desk, FX licensing, or exchange permit regime. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods transactions and CAD 500,000 for services transactions with non-residents (aggregate per year) trigger mandatory Local File / Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like OECD jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies; transactions must have genuine economic substance to avoid reassessment and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and apply GAAR; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; criminal charges for fraud; corporate veil may be pierced.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate debts if they allowed the company to operate without substance; potential imprisonment for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they failed to ensure compliance; may face criminal charges and imprisonment.
