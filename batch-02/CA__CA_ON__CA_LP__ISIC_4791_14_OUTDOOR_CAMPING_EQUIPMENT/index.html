Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA)

E-government filing portal

The Ontario limited partnership must obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or registering online via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The BN is established in the name of the limited partnership, not the individual partners. The partnership then creates a GST/HST program account (RT) under the same BN by filing Form RC1 or through BRO. Non-resident partners with taxable activity in Canada must separately obtain a BN and GST/HST account where they are personally making taxable supplies; the CRA requires a Canadian contact address or authorized representative (e.g., tax agent) for service of documents. Required supporting documents include the limited partnership declaration or statement of registration filed with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services, partnership agreement, and identification of all general and limited partners. CRA typically processes registration within 5-10 business days; the GST/HST account is effective from the date the partnership ceases to be a small supplier or begins commercial activity, whichever is earlier. For foreign partners, CRA Form RC459 (GST/HST Registration for Non-Resident Persons) is used where the partnership itself is non-resident; however, for a Canadian-formed Ontario LP, registration is obtained directly through BRO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership formed in Ontario is fiscally transparent and is not subject to a check-the-box regime; partners are directly liable on their distributive share of income. Elections relevant to the structure include: (i) subsection 216(1) ITA election for non-resident partners with Canadian rental or resource income to file a net-basis return; (ii) joint election under section 179 of the Excise Tax Act to treat the partnership and its members as a single GST/HST registrant in respect of certain partnerships; (iii) alternative fiscal period election under section 249.1(5) ITA; (iv) transfer pricing documentation relief election under subsection 247(4) ITA for transactions with non-resident partners; and (v) GST/HST section 156 election for supplies between closely related registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the ITA apply to disallow deductions in respect of foreign entity or branch mismatches involving a Canadian LP with non-resident partners; no separate hybrid election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada that exceeds this threshold must register; an Ontario LP engaged in internet retail of camping equipment must register as soon as taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in either test period)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (section 1) when the LP obtains documentary evidence satisfactory to CRA that the goods were exported by the supplier or a related party. For online retail of outdoor camping equipment, the LP must ship the goods to a customer's foreign address, with title transferring outside Canada and shipment via a common carrier, to qualify for the 0% rate. Gathering, shipment, and contemporaneous export documentation must be maintained; supply of goods delivered to a Canadian address prior to exportation does not qualify. Services rendered to a non-resident that are exclusively for use outside Canada may qualify for zero-rating under section 7 of Part V, Schedule VI, but pure retail e-commerce of tangible goods is the principal export category here.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice with incoterms (FOB / CIP) and destination country
  • Packing List and carrier certificate of departure
  • SWIFT Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange settlement statement paid from the non-resident customer
  • Cargo Control Document or Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export report acknowledgement

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices in standard formats (PDF, EDI, XML) provided they contain prescribed disclosure fields under the Excise Tax Act (supplier name, BN, date of invoice, description, tax amounts, and registration number). There is no mandatory API connectivity to CRA for invoice clearance; e-invoicing adoption is voluntary and governed by CRA Information Circular IC 70-6R4 (electronic records). Vendors may use the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing infrastructure on an opt-in basis; however, maintenance of source books and records in readable electronic or paper format is compulsory. The GST/HST net-file system is used for periodic returns; assessment and audit are conducted through MyBA.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rated export claims by reconciling sales invoices with customs declarations, freight documents, and foreign-currency remittances. Where export substantiation is inadequate or fraudulent, CRA may reassess the supply at 13% HST, deny input tax credits, and impose penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (interest and penalty on net tax owing), section 285 (false statements - greater of CAD 250 and 5% of net tax), and section 285.1 (gross negligence - 25% of the tax amount for misrepresentations made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence). Criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Clawback of refunds applies with interest from the date the refund was paid; partnership liabilities under the ETA flow to the general partner and, in certain circumstances, to all partners on a joint and several basis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Canada maintains no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, capital repatriation, and partnership distributions may be transmitted freely without central bank approval or mandatory conversion. The practical hurdles are tax compliance rather than FX restriction: non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on certain amounts, treaty relief requires appropriate forms, and all transfers over CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC-side reporting by the financial institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or repatriation timeline exists under Canadian law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, converted selectively, or held in multicurrency accounts at commercial rates. Mandatory conversion ratio: not applicable. Export records must still be maintained for CRA income verification and GST/HST obligations, and cash-equivalent proceeds above CAD 10,000 will trigger FINTRAC reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and retail payment oversight body. Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CFT surveillance and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary payment collection channel and LOW as a primary treasury depository. Offshore digital EMIs are not Canadian authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act. Any entity habitually providing retail payment services in Canada must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC money services business registration, if applicable. EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or PayPal can be used to collect e-commerce settlements, but balances should be swept daily to a CDIC-insured Canadian bank account and should never serve as the primary corporate treasury location.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound limited-partner capital should be routed by SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency wires into the LP's Canadian bank account. For CAD-denominated internal transfers or Canadian-source subscriptions, use Payments Canada's Lynx large-value transfer system for real-time final settlement. The remittance must name the true originating partner, include the LP's Ontario limited partnership registry number, and state the payment reference as 'Capital contribution under LP agreement' or similar. The receiving bank will apply sanctions and PEP screening to the counterparties. Canada does not impose a mandatory statutory purpose-code regime, but a clear ISO 20022 narrative code or SWIFT field 70 purpose should be supplied to avoid correspondent-bank rejection. Verification documents should include the signed subscription/escrow agreement, the capital call, partner passport/certificate of incumbency, bank statement evidence of source of funds, and beneficial-ownership declarations.For settlement of trade payables to equipment manufacturers and overseas suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 via SWIFT gpi for international wires, with the Canadian chartered bank's USD/EUR correspondent network. For CAD vendor payments, Payments Canada's Lynx or standard wire transfer can be used. For high-value purchase orders, a commercial letter of credit issued as SWIFT MT700 and amended through MT707 provides the safest clearing rail, supported by commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and certificate of origin. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties (or CAD 50 million gross revenue) triggers mandatory Local File / Master File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies. Partnerships must demonstrate real economic substance (e.g., actual business operations, employees, assets) to avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, fines up to CAD 100,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties and interest; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations, CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly participated; travel bans and imprisonment possible.
