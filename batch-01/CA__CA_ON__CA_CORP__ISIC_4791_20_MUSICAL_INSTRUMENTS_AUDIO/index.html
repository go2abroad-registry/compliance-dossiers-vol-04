Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities carrying on business in Canada apply to CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO), by filing Form RC1, or by mailing completed registration forms to the applicable CRA tax centre. The application must state the entity's legal name, country of incorporation, foreign business address, Canadian representative/agent (if any), and description of activities. CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts (RT for corporate income tax, RZ for GST/HST, RP for payroll, etc.). A foreign corporation may need to file an extra-provincial registration or provide proof of good standing before certain program accounts are activated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election applies under Canadian law; corporation classification is statutory. Elections of relevance include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election under ETA s. 228; (ii) wash election for supplies between closely related GST/HST registrants under ETA s. 156; (iii) non-resident election under Income Tax Act s. 216 to compute Canadian tax on net rental/income basis instead of Part XIII withholding; (iv) fiscal period elections under ITA s. 249.1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies threshold; a Canadian corporation must register if its total taxable supplies in a calendar quarter exceed CAD 30,000 or exceed CAD 30,000 over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, tangible personal property (musical instruments and audio gear) sold by a registrant may be zero-rated where the registrant exports the property by way of sale and the goods are shipped directly outside Canada, or are delivered to a carrier for direct export, with evidence retained. For intangible products (digital downloads), zero-rating applies when the supply is made to a non-resident and the recipient's use or enjoyment occurs wholly outside Canada, supported by records indicating the non-resident status and primary place of use.

  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying buyer, Incoterms, and description of goods.
  • Transportation document (B/L, air waybill, courier waybill) and, where applicable under the Customs Act, a CBSA export declaration (B13A) proving physical export from Canada.
  • Bank transfer/wire records evidencing receipt of export proceeds in alignment with the export invoice (an inward remittance certificate is not a prescribed CRA form, but preserved remittance evidence is recommended for audit support).

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime exists in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and records provided they meet standard record-retention requirements. Canada's pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard is voluntary and in development; no API clearance with CRA is mandated before issuing invoices or claiming input tax credits.

The CRA may reassess unsupported zero-rated supplies to taxable supplies at the prevailing HST rate (13% in Ontario) plus arrears interest. Penalties can include ETA s. 280/285 compliance penalties (5% late-filing penalty and up to 50% gross-negligence penalties on the portion attributable to wilful misstatement), section 327 criminal sanctions for evasion, and CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) for false or omitted export information. Any refund or input tax credit claimed in connection with the export transaction may be clawed back retroactively together with compound interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a liberalized capital account. The Canadian dollar is freely tradable; no advance approvals, licensing, or FX allocation are required for direct investment, current-account trade, or profit repatriation. Dividends paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (normally 25% statutory, frequently reduced by treaty), but remittance is not blocked, rationed, or deferred. Corporate treasuries may hold and settle in CAD, USD, or other major currencies without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit for holding export proceeds offshore or in domestic foreign-currency accounts. Export receipts must still be recorded for income tax under the Income Tax Act and disclosed in relevant returns (including T1134 for controlled foreign affiliates and T1135 for specified foreign property).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration exists. FX and AML supervision are distributed among OSFI, FINTRAC, and the Canada Border Services Agency for reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: High for supplemental execution, Low for primary treasury nesting. Canadian provincially incorporated entities may use FINTRAC-registered money service businesses (MSBs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or Stripe for virtual CAD/USD account workflows, provided the EMI has a Canadian MSB registration or operates through a regulated Canadian partner bank. Legal constraints: non-Canadian EMIs without FINTRAC registration and no provincial money-transmission license cannot lawfully hold or transmit Canadian-dollar balances for Ontario residents. CAD clearing must remain on Canadian Payments Association rails, and any payment institution acting as an intermediary must observe PCMLTFA recordkeeping and large-transaction reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity or intercompany loan injections are wired through SWIFT MT103/TT to the Canadian operating account. The remitting investor must reference the Ontario corporation's legal name, registration number, and a clear remittance notice (e.g., 'capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan') in field 70. Canadian banks do not require a centralized FX-control approval; however, the receiving bank will record a Balance-of-Payments (BOP) reason for Statistics Canada. For USD inflows, the instructing bank must route through the Canadian bank's US correspondent with 'for further credit to [bank/account]' instructions. Inbound amounts equal to or above CAD 10,000, or any amount that arouses suspicion, trigger FINTRAC large-transaction reporting; internal capital injection records must therefore match corporate resolutions and subscribed share capital.Use SWIFT MT103 for international supplier wires and CAD high-value clearing via Lynx (Bank of Canada RTGS); USD payments clear through the bank's US correspondent channel.
