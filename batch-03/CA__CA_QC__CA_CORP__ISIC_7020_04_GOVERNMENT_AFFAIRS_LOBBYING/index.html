Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Quebec must be a physical address within Quebec where service of process can be effected; a post-office box alone is prohibited. A virtual office or corporate-service-provider address is acceptable for the statutory registered office where the provider maintains a physical Quebec street address and mail receipt arrangement. For this government-affairs and lobbying advisory sector, a virtual office is sufficient for registry compliance but is not an adequate substitute for demonstrable Quebec commercial substance if employees carry on lobbying activities from Quebec; the Quebec Lobbyists Commissioner and Canada Revenue Agency may attribute permanent establishment risk where there is no physical place of operations.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA) and register with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec to obtain the NEQ (Numero d'entreprise du Quebec); 2) apply to CRA for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via the Business Registration Online portal or Form RC1; the BN with program suffix 'RC' is the T2 corporate income tax account and with suffix 'RT' is the GST/HST account; 3) register with Revenu Quebec for GST/HST and QST accounts (combined registration) through Clic Revenu; RQ issues the QST registration number (TVQ number) and the GST registration number; 4) file the combined GST/HST and QST return (form FPZ-500) with Revenu Quebec, not CRA; 5) as a precondition of lawful fee earning, obtain and maintain registration with the federal Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada and the Quebec Lobbyists Commissioner under the Lobbying Transparency and Ethics Act, quoting the registration identifiers in commercial contracts and fee ledgers; 6) for foreign shareholders, appoint a resident agent under CBCA/QBCA and register for Part XIII withholding (NR4/NR6) or treaty-based relief filings with CRA.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility in accordance with IMF Article VIII. The former Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952; Canadian dollars are freely exchangeable without prior central bank approval. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest payments, and repatriation of paid-up capital by non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject to (i) corporate insolvency and solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (Québec) and (ii) statutory withholding taxes under the Canada–US Income Tax Convention (generally 25% dividend rate reduced to 5%–15% for qualified US corporate shareholders). Lobbying registration requirements impose reputational and disclosure obligations, not currency restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds or service-related retainer income. Export receipts and foreign-currency consulting fees may be retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies in Schedule I bank foreign-currency accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to (i) FINTRAC large cash transaction reports (net $10,000 CAD or more within 24 hours), (ii) FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reports ($10,000 CAD or more per transaction), and (iii) CBSA cross-border currency reporting on physical movement of $10,000 CAD or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; banking and prudential supervision administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision and transaction reporting governed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Quebec deposit-taking cooperatives (e.g., Desjardins) are also supervised by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury rail. Wise Business, Payoneer, and Revolut Business can serve only as ancillary USD collection channels, because Canadian legal payment-service providers require registration as a foreign money services business (FMSB) under the PCMLTFA, and most international EMIs do not hold a Canadian Schedule I banking licence or CDIC deposit insurance. A Quebec-incorporated lobbying advisory with non-resident UBOs should not rely on EMIs for CAD-denominated retainer settlement or government-facing disbursements; any EMI balance must be swept to the domestic anchor bank account. Medium-risk classification (Enhanced Due Diligence) makes the fintech-only model operationally fragile.
