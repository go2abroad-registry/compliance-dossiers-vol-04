Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST via Business Registration Online (available at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/register-your-business.html); Revenu Québec administers the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) via its business registration portal at https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/.

E-government filing portal

A limited partnership (or its non-resident representative) must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA, with a GST/HST program account, using online registration or Form RC1. Mandatory registration is required once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold if the partnership otherwise makes taxable supplies in Canada. The partnership must also register separately with Revenu Québec for a QST account using Form SEP-1 (or online via ClicSÉQUR), supplying the partnership agreement, proof of legal existence, and, where necessary, a Canadian agent authorized for service. Non-resident partners of the Canadian limited partnership may additionally require an individual non-resident tax account number and compliance profile with the CRA for reporting Canadian-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or elective entity-classification system under the Income Tax Act. The limited partnership is inherently tax-transparent: it files an information return but computes and pays tax at the partner level. Available statutory elections include subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act for non-resident partners to file a Canadian income tax return on a net-basis; elections relating to hybrid mismatch arrangements may require disclosure via Form RC4646 or equivalent; and certain provincial family-farm or fishing partnership elections may be relevant. No general corporate-style check-the-box election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including remote and e-commerce sales); a zero threshold applies to certain non-resident registrants carrying on business in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated where they are exported by the registrant within 60 days of the supply (or such longer period as permitted) and proof of export is retained. Services may be zero-rated when performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident arm's-length person who is not registered for GST/HST. For social-commerce retail sales, the seller must substantiate the physical movement of goods out of Canada, typically through carrier documentation and customs export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of payment received from outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing Slip

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime exists for GST/HST or QST. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically via CRA NETFILE (if sales exceed CAD 1.5 million) and QST returns via Revenu Québec's ClicSÉQUR portal. Invoices must contain prescribed information; records may be kept electronically, but no government-authorised e-invoice pre-clearance is required.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and claw back the zero-rating where export documentation is deficient, leaving GST/HST at 5% and QST at 9.975% chargeable on the full value. Knowingly submitting false or misleading information triggers a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated net tax, arrears interest, and, in criminal cases, fines of up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act. Revenu Québec imposes analogous penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains an open investment regime under the Investment Canada Act, with no general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident partners in a Quebec limited partnership. However, cross-border transfers of CAD or foreign currency are subject to FINTRAC reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000 or equivalent, and a limited partnership that is a reporting entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) must implement a compliance program. Profit remittance is structurally unimpeded, but withholding tax obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) may apply to amounts paid or credited to non-residents, subject to treaty relief under the relevant tax treaty or the Canada-Québec intergovernmental coordination. No exchange control approval is required for ordinary current-account payments, export proceeds, or capital movements, provided the transaction does not involve a sanctioned jurisdiction under the United Nations Act or the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. The Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance do not impose any surrender requirement on export proceeds, and a Canadian exporter, including a Québec limited partnership engaged in cross-border TikTok Shop retail, may hold export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency in a domestic or foreign bank account without obligation to convert them into CAD. The only applicable obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or above, (ii) customs reporting for currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or above under the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations, and (iii) ordinary corporate and tax accounting requirements under the Income Tax Act and the Taxation Act (Québec). Since the entity is a pass-through limited partnership, the attribution of foreign exchange gains or losses to partners is governed by the partnership agreement and applicable provincial and federal tax rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for licensed non-bank payment service providers and fintech EMIs, subject to compliance with Canada's retail payment oversight framework administered by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration as a payment service provider. Offshore digital EMIs that are not licensed in Canada can support a Québec LP on a cross-border basis only as auxiliary payment channels, not as full banking substitutes; they may not accept deposits in Canada and must not hold settlement funds without registration. Wise B2B and similar fintech rails are viable for TikTok Shop payouts and supplier disbursements, but the primary treasury account must be held at a Canadian Schedule I bank or a registered foreign bank branch in Canada to satisfy banking and tax substance requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners into the Québec LP must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank account using SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent wire with explicit purpose code. For a limited partnership, the recommended code is 'Investment in a Canadian partnership / equity contribution' and the beneficiary must be identified as an unincorporated business. The sending partner should use the partnership's registered name, partnership registration number (Québec enterprise registrar), and account number. A source-of-funds statement and a partnership resolution authorizing the contribution must accompany the inward remittance to satisfy the bank's EDD. CAD 10,000 or above triggers FINTRAC reporting. The capital contribution is not subject to federal or provincial capital tax at the time of injection, but the LP must record the contribution as partners' capital and not as partnership income. For exchange control purposes no permit is required.Outbound trade disbursements for inventory, logistics, and supplier payments should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, documentary collections, or commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value imports. Given the TikTok Shop social commerce model, routine supplier settlements will typically use MT103 via the bank's online treasury platform or a payment service provider such as Wise B2B with underlying FX conversion. For LCs, the Québec LP should use a standard irrevocable documentary credit with incoterms stated, issued by the Canadian bank and advised through a correspondent bank in the seller's jurisdiction. Disbursements above CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting if routed as international electronic funds transfers. Dual-use concerns are not present; the primary compliance obligations are valuation, recordkeeping, and documentary support for each payment.
