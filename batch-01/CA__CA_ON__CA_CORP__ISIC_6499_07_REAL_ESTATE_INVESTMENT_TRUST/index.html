Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) portals accessible through canada.ca. The Ontario provincial corporate income tax file is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement; a separate Ontario TIN is not issued.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident REIT must: (1) file Form RC4104 (Request for a Non-Resident Business Number) with the CRA International and Large Business Directorate, or use BRO through an authorized Canadian representative, to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN); (2) open a GST/HST account using Form RC1 or BRO, quoting NAICS 525930 as the principal commercial activity; (3) open a payroll account if the REIT employs property/custodial staff in Canada; (4) register as a payer under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for withholding on distributions to non-resident unitholders; and (5) file the ITA s. 216 election (Form NR6) to report Canadian rental income on a net basis in lieu of gross Part XIII withholding.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian REIT qualifying under ITA s. 122.1 is a taxable trust that deducts distributions to unitholders, effectively eliminating entity-level tax on distributed qualifying rental income; non-resident unitholders are subject to Part XIII withholding. — A non-resident REIT must: (1) file Form RC4104 (Request for a Non-Resident Business Number) with the CRA International and Large Business Directorate, or use BRO through an authorized Canadian representative, to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN); (2) open a GST/HST account using Form RC1 or BRO, quoting NAICS 525930 as the principal commercial activity; (3) open a payroll account if the REIT employs property/custodial staff in Canada; (4) register as a payer under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for withholding on distributions to non-resident unitholders; and (5) file the ITA s. 216 election (Form NR6) to report Canadian rental income on a net basis in lieu of gross Part XIII withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian REIT qualifying under ITA s. 122.1 is a taxable trust that deducts distributions to unitholders, effectively eliminating entity-level tax on distributed qualifying rental income; non-resident unitholders are subject to Part XIII withholding.

Available election forms

(1) REIT designation election under ITA s. 122.1(10), conditional on the 90% qualifying REIT asset test, the minimum holding period, and the 95%/(75%/85%) income distribution tests; (2) closely related election under ETA s. 156(2) (Form RC4616) to treat intercompany supplies within the REIT property-owning group as made for nil consideration; (3) non-resident section 216 election (Form NR6) for net-basis taxation of Canadian rental income; and (4) ETA election to treat certain rental activities as commercial for input tax credit recovery purposes, converting exempt residential supplies into taxable supplies where permitted, subject to tax neutrality rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148); non-residents making taxable supplies of real property situated in Canada are excluded from the small supplier relief and must register from the first dollar of such supplies (ETA ss. 240(1)-(1.1)).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated if exported by the supplier and delivered outside Canada as soon as is reasonable after the supply (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6). Services are generally zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7), but services relating to real property situated in Canada, including rental, leasing, management, and brokerage services, are deemed supplied in Canada and are taxable at 13% HST (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23). A Canadian REIT earning income from Canadian buildings cannot claim zero-rating on those supplies; zero-rating is limited to services relating to foreign real property supplied to non-residents and to exported tangible personal property (e.g., replaced FF&E).

  • B13A Exports Report / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) transmission evidencing the CBSA export declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying lease or service agreement evidencing the supply to the non-resident recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statements evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds into a Canadian financial institution
  • Certificate of non-resident status of the recipient (e.g., NR301 declaration or equivalent documentation) substantiating zero-rating eligibility

No real-time fiscal clearance, API digital stamping, or pre-clearance mandate exists in Canada. The CRA requires all GST/HST registrants to retain books and records, electronic or paper, in readable format for six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230). Canada has adopted a voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoice exchange for B2G transactions (Canada e-Invoice pilot), but supplier participation is optional; no mandatory e-invoice clearance for REIT rental or export transactions.

The CRA may reassess within four years (extended to seven years for misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud) and retroactively claw back zero-rating and any input tax credits claimed (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). False or omitted export documentation triggers: (a) ETA s. 289 penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus the greater of $100 and 5% of the tax, with daily compounding interest; (b) criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 for tax evasion, with fines of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; and (c) ETA s. 323 joint and several liability of REIT trustees and directors for unremitted GST/HST. Export and FX reproceed substantiation records must be retained for six years, with a minimum 180-day reprocedation window operationalized in the REIT's transfer-pricing and treasury policy.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar is fully convertible with no exchange controls. Capital and current account transfers are unrestricted. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, reduced by treaty to 15% or lower) but are not subject to any FX approval, quota, or repatriation restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency denomination in accounts and settle freely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Foreign exchange oversight via Finance Canada and OSFI; no standalone exchange control authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, with legal constraints. Foreign or offshore digital EMIs do not hold a Canadian bank charter and cannot directly operate as primary clearing institutions. They may provide payment facilitation if registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act and supervised by OSFI, but they lack direct access to the Canadian core settlement system (Large Value Transfer System). For treasury-grade liquidity, a Canadian Schedule I bank account is mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation when the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with each non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million; a Master File is required when the consolidated group gross revenue exceeds CAD 100 million and any non-arm's length cross-border transactions occur.

Economic substance scope: Canada enforces economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act. Transactions lacking economic substance and undertaken primarily to avoid tax may have benefits denied. For real estate investment entities, CRA assesses whether the REIT designation conditions are met, including asset composition, income distribution, and the condition that distributions be funded out of real estate income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - deliberate misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, section 163.2 imposes a 50% gross-negligence penalty of the additional tax sought; if evasion is proven, criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act or section 380 of the Criminal Code carries fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid source deductions and unremitted GST/HST under section 227.1; a criminal evasion conviction can lead to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for the individual director, trustee, or beneficial owner.
Shell Company - artificial vehicle for tax avoidance

Statutory sanction: The CRA can apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to ignore the corporation, reallocate income, and assess tax plus interest; penalties for misrepresentation include a 50% gross-negligence penalty; share capital taxes or excise taxes may also apply.

Director personal liability: In extreme cases, courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a sham or used for fraudulent purposes. Directors can be held personally liable for misappropriation of assets, unjust enrichment, and any resulting withholding tax liabilities.
Transfer Pricing - non-arm's length mispricing

Statutory sanction: Under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, the CRA may adjust the transaction to an arm's length price, recalculate income, and levy a penalty of 10% on the net adjustment amount if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine an arm's length price; interest accrues on the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused such mispricing and did not exercise due diligence are subject to the statutory joint liability regime; in fraud cases, personal criminal liability may arise, and travel bans or asset forfeiture can be ordered.
AML/ATF Compliance Failure in Real Estate Transactions

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA of up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 10,000,000 for entities; money laundering can attract criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can face personal administrative monetary penalties for non-supervision; criminal liability attaches for those who intentionally participate in laundering funds, and courts may issue civil asset seizure orders and travel bans.
