Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including its Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals, administers federal income tax and GST/HST registration. Revenu Québec administers Québec income tax and the Québec Sales Tax (QST), using its online services, including clicSÉQUR. Québec-based businesses generally file GST returns with Revenu Québec under the federal-Québec tax administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

Obtain the required Québec partnership registration and Québec enterprise identifiers from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and Revenu Québec. Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts, including GST/HST (RT) and, where relevant, payroll (RP) or information returns (RZ). A non-resident without a Canadian business number may need to apply using the CRA's prescribed registration process rather than BRO; provide formation documents, partnership details, Canadian business and contact information, and evidence of authority for the representative. Register for QST with Revenu Québec when required. Registration thresholds and obligations depend on the nature and place of supply, whether the partnership carries on business in Canada or Québec, and any applicable small-supplier or special non-resident rules. A BN is not itself a GST/HST or QST registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent: it computes income under Canadian tax rules and allocates its tax attributes to its partners, who generally report their respective shares. It does not have a US-style check-the-box election to elect corporate classification. Partner-level consequences, including non-resident withholding, Canadian-source income, permanent establishment, and treaty relief, must be assessed separately. Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or require income inclusions in specified mismatch arrangements; they do not provide a general classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters for the GST/HST small-supplier test, subject to statutory exceptions and special rules. QST registration obligations are separately determined under Québec law; the general small-supplier threshold is also generally CAD 30,000. The threshold is not a blanket exemption for every non-resident or activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is determined by the statutory rules for the particular supply, not solely by the customer's foreign residence or payment in foreign currency. Qualifying exported goods may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence of export. Certain international freight transportation services, including qualifying transportation of goods to or from a place outside Canada, may be zero-rated under the applicable GST/HST rules; the route, service, contractual arrangements, and statutory definition must be verified. Domestic trucking within Canada is not zero-rated merely because it forms part of an export chain. Québec QST treatment must be analyzed under its corresponding rules. Registered suppliers may generally claim input tax credits or input tax refunds for eligible business inputs, subject to documentation and statutory restrictions.

  • Customs export declaration or other applicable export evidence, where required for the goods and transaction
  • Bill of lading, waybill, freight contract, and proof of delivery or border crossing, as applicable
  • Commercial invoice, customer and destination records, and evidence linking the transportation service to the qualifying international movement
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a general Canadian GST/HST or QST statutory requirement; retain payment records where relevant to substantiate the transaction

Canada and Québec do not generally require real-time tax clearance, digital tax stamping, or mandatory API pre-clearance of ordinary commercial invoices. Registered suppliers must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information and retain adequate books and records. Electronic invoicing may be used subject to applicable tax-document and record-retention requirements. Québec's mandatory billing measures for specified sectors do not create a general clearance mandate for long-haul trucking.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. CRA and Revenu Québec may audit the zero-rating claim and require contemporaneous records establishing the supply, recipient, route, export or international-freight conditions, and consideration. Unsupported or ineligible zero-rating can result in assessed tax, interest, penalties, denial or recovery of input tax credits or refunds, and reassessment within the applicable statutory limitation period; gross-negligence penalties and prosecution may apply in qualifying cases. TIR carnet or customs evidence supports transit or customs compliance but does not, by itself, establish GST/HST or QST zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility of the Canadian dollar. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, and the former Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952, leaving no approval, registration, repatriation, or conversion permits applicable to capital contributions, intercompany loans, or partnership profit remittances. The CAD floats freely; the Bank of Canada does not administer exchange controls and does not target or systematically intervene in the exchange rate. For the CA_LP, the only practical compliance frictions are bank-level KYC and sanctions screening, Statistics Canada monthly international-settlement reporting at CAD 25,000 or above, FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting at CAD 10,000 or above, and potential Part XIII non-resident withholding taxation on certain distributions or fees; none of these constitutes a convertibility or capital-control barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, minimum proportion, or mandatory conversion obligation for export proceeds. The LP may retain all USD freight receivables in a Canadian USD clearing account or in an offshore account without surrender to the Bank of Canada or to any federal authority. Foreign-currency holdings remain subject to Statistics Canada international-transaction reporting when individual settlements equal or exceed CAD 25,000, to the Income Tax Act section 261 functional-currency election if the LP adopts USD reporting for tax purposes, and to the LP's internal treasury policy of matching USD revenues to USD fuel-card and subcontractor settlements in order to minimize conversion friction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with functional foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments reporting administration delegated to Statistics Canada (Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Program) and AML/CTF transaction oversight to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers does not exercise banking-prudential or FX-jurisdiction over the federal-bank accounts of the Quebec Limited Partnership.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary rail for fuel-card program settlement, small vendor disbursements, and marginal FX conversion; Low as a primary operating or capital account. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the federal Bank Act and may operate in Canada only as money-services businesses registered with FINTRAC. Schedule I banks generally refuse to route the LP's TIR freight receivables or customs-bond flows through an unlicensed third-party EMI, and the Low_Risk_Standard AML classification of the business does not reduce that regulatory constraint. The core clearing and lending mandate must therefore remain with a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control approval is required. The compliant rail is a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign Limited Partner's bank to the LP's Capital Escrow Account at a Montreal Schedule I bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and bears 0% entity-level corporate income tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Income Tax Act Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; not applicable to LP distributions, which are characterized as return of allocated income or capital rather than dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Full OECD Master File and Local File documentation is generally required for MNE groups with Canadian gross revenue above CAD 50,000,000 or intercompany transactions above CAD 5,000,000. For the LP, these thresholds apply to the partnership and its partners in respect of transactions with foreign related parties.

Economic substance scope: Quebec and Canada do not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the Quebec anti-avoidance rule, combined with substance-over-form and the Commercial Corporations Act jurisprudence, require the LP to maintain real business assets, qualified drivers, equipment, revenue flows, and commercial risk. A shell LP without economic activities is disregarded for Canadian income tax purposes and may attract reassessments, penalties, and revocation of transport licenses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years. Indictable conviction: fine of 100% to 200% of tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Revenu Québec may impose equivalent penalties under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner of the LP who authorized the evasion can be prosecuted personally. Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Quebec civil law where the LP or its general partner is used as a shield for fraud, resulting in civil restitution, seizure of assets, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of the tax benefit under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, reassessment of income, interest, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty; Quebec may impose parallel penalties. The TIR guarantee may also be suspended by customs authorities for abuse of the transit procedure.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be disregarded when the general partner is a mere facade or agent of the principals. Directors and promoters who structured the abusive arrangement face personal liability for unpaid taxes, civil restitution, and in egregious cases criminal prosecution for organizing tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: CRA is authorized to adjust the transfer price and recharacterize the transaction under section 247 of the Income Tax Act. A penalty of 10% of the gross adjustment may apply where the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to establish arm's-length terms. Thin capitalization rules under section 212 disallow interest deductions on excessive debt. Revenu Québec applies equivalent provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepared false transfer pricing documentation may be subject to gross-negligence penalties and prosecution. If the corporate veil is pierced, they can be ordered to pay the full amount of the reassessment plus interest and penalties, and may be subject to travel restrictions pending criminal proceedings.
AML / Unlicensed Cross-Border Trucking

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act can reach CAD 25,000 per violation, plus cancellation of the TIR carnet license. Operating without a transport license under Quebec's Act respecting road freight transportation may result in fines up to CAD 15,000 for individuals and CAD 50,000 for corporations. Serious AML violations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can attract fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the general partner can be prosecuted personally for customs fraud, money laundering, and unlawful transport operations. Convictions may lead to civil restitution, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment; the LP's limited liability shield is void where the act constitutes fraud, and the corporate veil is pierced to reach personal assets.
