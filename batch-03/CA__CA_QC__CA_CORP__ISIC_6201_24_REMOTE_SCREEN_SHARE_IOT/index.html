Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec business corporation. The issued share capital is fixed by the incorporator/board; no escrow account, government deposit, or proof-of-funds requirement applies at formation. Shares may be issued with or without par value and consideration may be paid in money, property, or past services. Corporate existence begins on the certificate date (QBCA s. 23).

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety certificate issued by the local fire prevention service under the Fire Safety Act (C.Q.L.R. c. S-2.3.1), confirming means of egress, alarms, and extinguishers; CNESST employer registration and occupational first-aid compliance under the Act respecting occupational health and safety; office structural load adequacy confirmed against the Québec Construction Code; no industrial clearance or special environmental authorization is required for pure software or remote-support operations.

No dedicated industrial licensing board applies to software publishing and computer programming (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal income tax administration, operating through the 'My Business Account' portal; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), Quebec corporation tax, and Quebec payroll remittances, operating through the Revenu Québec online portal with CléNuméro/NEO.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation with Quebec nexus must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account by filing CRA Form RC3486 (Application for Registration for the GST/HST) via CRA's non-resident registration channel or My Business Account. Supporting documents include articles of incorporation, foreign registry extract, proof of director identity, and a completed RC59 (Authorization or Cancellation of Authorization of a Representative) where a Canadian representative is used. For Quebec, the same entity registers with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial corporate accounts using Form LM-1 (Application for Registration for the GST/HST and QST) or the online Business Registration service. The federal TIN is the 15-character Business Number program account (e.g., 123456789 RC0001); Quebec assigns a distinct QST registration file number. Registration must be completed before the first taxable supply is made in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canada/Quebec business corporation is a separate taxable person on its worldwide income; no pass-through or check-the-box eligibility for corporations; non-resident shareholders are additionally subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations. Available statutory elections include ITA s. 85 rollover on incorporation or asset transfers, ITA s. 86 share-forexchange elections, ITA s. 216 net-income election for non-resident rental suppliers, ITA s. 219 branch tax elections, capital dividend account elections under ITA s. 83(2), and fiscal period elections under ITA s. 249.1. Hybrid mismatch integrity rules in ITA s. 18.6, effective 1 July 2022, apply deduction/non-inclusion and imported-mismatch adjustments without elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The GST/HST small supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of worldwide gross taxable supplies (current calendar quarter plus the preceding four quarters) under ETA s. 228(3) and Schedule IX; the QST threshold is likewise CAD 30,000 under the Quebec Taxation Act. For non-resident suppliers of remote desktop access and IoT telemetry software, the 1 July 2021 place-of-supply rules apply: a non-resident must register for GST/HST and QST once the threshold is exceeded, and registration is required earlier where the non-resident fails to establish small-supplier status or supplies services to consumers located in Quebec through certain digital platform arrangements. B2B supplies of software access to GST/HST registrants for their commercial activities are generally zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to (i) supplies of goods exported from Canada where the goods are exported by the supplier in accordance with ETA s. 170(2), generally within 60 days of delivery; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not for use by the recipient in Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7); and (iii) intangible property, including software access, remote desktop session connectivity, and IoT telemetry data feeds, supplied to non-residents for use exclusively outside Canada. Quebec QST zero-rating rules mirror the GST framework. For remote screen-share and unattended-machine telemetry services, the contract must evidence the non-resident recipient and the foreign destination of the benefit; the supplier must retain proof of the recipient's non-resident status and documentation that the service was not consumed in Canada.

  • Customs export declaration (B13A and Canadian Export Reporting System CERS transmission) for any exported hardware or goods; for pure software services, the service agreement governs
  • Bill of lading or airway waybill evidencing physical export of goods, when applicable
  • Commercial invoice and master service agreement identifying the foreign recipient and the remote screen-share/IoT telemetry supply
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement proving payment received from outside Canada
  • Non-resident status evidence (certificate of corporate residence, foreign tax residency certificate, or comparable due-diligence record)
  • Destination and use statement from the customer declaring that the software service is consumed exclusively outside Canada

Canada does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, government API validation, or digital stamping for invoices. Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain the prescribed GST/HST/QST information under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec's parallel rules. Revenu Québec has announced a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0, with voluntary adoption in 2025, phased mandatory deployment for large businesses expected from 2026, and broader SME rollout thereafter; the CRA has no equivalent federal mandate. Taxpayers must retain machine-readable sales, export, and remittance documentation for at least six years for audit purposes.

CRA and Revenu Québec rigorously audit export zero-rating claims for documentary completeness; missing export, non-resident, or remittance evidence can cause the supply to be reclassified as taxable, triggering unremitted tax, arrears interest, and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, false or omitted statements on returns attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax at issue (ETA s. 280), with additional penalties for failure to register or remit (ETA s. 285). The normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful neglect is established. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion is available under Criminal Code s. 380, with repayment, fines, and imprisonment. Revenu Québec has parallel audit, penalty, interest, and security-demand powers under the Quebec Tax Administration Act; transfer pricing review under ITA s. 247 may additionally adjust revenue allocations where export invoicing and foreign remittance do not reflect arm's length economics. While the Excise Tax Act does not prescribe a fixed foreign-exchange repatriation date, the 180-day benchmark is adopted in this compliance framework for documentary continuity and audit defensibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange controls in Canada; the Foreign Exchange Control Orders have been repealed. Dividends, royalties, and branch remittances are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, possibly reduced under treaty), but no prior administrative approval for profit repatriation is required. Cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC, and all clearing banks are required to conduct AML/ATF due diligence on value chains.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no mandatory surrender or conversion regime for export proceeds. Export receipts may be freely retained in Canadian dollars or any foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. There are no statutory deadlines or conversion ratios. Companies may use export proceeds directly for payments, reinvestment, or repatriation, subject only to ordinary reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange control and investment regulation are administered by the Department of Finance Canada and the Investment Review Division of Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada under the Investment Canada Act; banking and payment-system supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Bank of Canada under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized under the Bank Act to accept deposits in Canada or to act as principal treasury custodians. They may only provide auxiliary payment facilities after registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, with full PCMLTFA compliance. They cannot serve as the primary corporate settlement bank or clearing hub for a Quebec-incorporated entity.

Turnover: No revenue-based statutory audit threshold applies to a private Quebec business corporation. An independent auditor is mandatory only if the corporation is a reporting issuer (public) or has not obtained the unanimous written consent of all shareholders to waive the audit (CBCA s. 163; QBCA s. 224). Any shareholder or group holding at least 5% of the issued voting shares may requisition an audit or re-audit without shareholder approval, and waivers must be renewed annually. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax rate (Federal Part I 15% + Quebec general rate 11.5%). After the 10% federal provincial abatement on income allocated to a Quebec permanent establishment, the effective combined rate on active business income is approximately 16.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 and CO-17 must be filed no later than 6 months after the end of the taxation year (e.g., a December 31, 2024 year-end must be filed by June 30, 2025). The final balance of federal and Quebec income tax is due no later than 2 months after the fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months after for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed with the T2 return where the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident party exceeds CA$1,000,000 during the taxation year. OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation should be prepared contemporaneously for any material inter-affiliate arrangement; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a general Economic Substance Regulation framework (unlike UAE/offshore regimes), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), Quebec anti-avoidance rules, and treaty Limitation-on-Benefits/Principal Purpose Test provisions require real economic substance, actual headquarters, decision-making personnel, and meaningful ownership of IP and IoT software infrastructure. A 'shell' with a letterbox director and no Canadian activity will be disregarded for attribution and treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Corporate Income Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalties from 25% to 50% of the understated federal/Quebec tax, plus late-filing penalties and interest; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries fines of up to CA$5,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Revenu Québec may impose separate fines under the Taxation Act and additional administrative penalties for assisted non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approved false filings are jointly and severally liable for unremitted corporate income taxes, withheld payroll remittances, QST/GST, and withholdings on non-resident payments. The CRA and Revenu Québec can issue tax evasion penalties personally and, on criminal conviction, a court may order restitution, travel bans, and terms of imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization under GAAR, denial of dividend and royalty treaty benefits, 10%-20% GAAR penalties, reassessment of under-reported income, and a directive that the corporation disclose all ultimate beneficial ownership. CRA sections 207.4 and 247 can add penalties for failure to file prescribed information; treaty relief can be withdrawn, causing the 25% statutory withholding to apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who tolerate the shell structure may be personally liable for unremitted taxes, considered to have participated in 'culpable negligence', and can be named in comparable penalties plus disgorgement of funds paid to shell shareholders; time served in jail for deliberate tax facilitation may apply.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Deficiency

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties of up to CA$500,000 per administrative violation for registered reporting entities; criminal charges under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds can result in fines of up to CA$2,000,000 and imprisonment for 10 years. A compliance failure may also trigger CRA audit and seizure of books and records.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for AML penalties solely for directorship, but knowingly directing or encouraging an ultimate-designated money-laundering scheme leads to criminal liability, personal fines, travel restrictions, confiscation orders, and imprisonment up to 10 years. Promotion offences and false statements expose directors directly.
