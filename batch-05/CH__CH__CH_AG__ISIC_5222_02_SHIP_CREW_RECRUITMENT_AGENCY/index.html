Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV / AFC) – portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax authorities for cantonal/communal taxes; VAT registration via FTA.

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign entities must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative if no Swiss registered office. Submit Form for VAT registration (e.g., Form 101) to the FTA, providing constitutional documents, proof of business purpose, UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) application through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (ZHRE/Zefix). TIN (UID) is issued automatically upon commercial registry entry; VAT number (MWST-Nr.) is assigned separately upon VAT registration. Non-resident entities providing taxable services in Switzerland or performing distance selling must register once taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per annum globally, unless entitled to exemption for services to Swiss recipients with reversed charge.

Submit Form for VAT registration (e.g., Form 101) to the FTA, providing constitutional documents, proof of business purpose, UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) application through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (ZHRE/Zefix). TIN (UID) is issued automatically upon commercial registry entry; VAT number (MWST-Nr.) is assigned separately upon VAT registration. Non-resident entities providing taxable services in Switzerland or performing distance selling must register once taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per annum globally, unless entitled to exemption for services to Swiss recipients with reversed charge.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box election in Swiss law. Corporation (AG) is per se taxable at entity level for federal, cantonal, and communal profit taxes (effective rate approx. 11.9-21% depending on canton). For VAT, standard rules apply; no hybrid mismatch election. Foreign entity may elect effective taxation under a Swiss permanent establishment only if treaty provisions apply. No partnership fiscal transparency for AG.

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies to export of goods (if delivered abroad and proven by customs export documentation) and to certain services directly related to exported goods or to non-resident entrepreneurs under specific conditions. Crew recruitment and management services provided to shipowners operating vessels for international traffic may be zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident entrepreneur with no place of business in Switzerland and the service is effectively used outside Switzerland. The exporter must substantiate the export with transport documents, customs declaration, and proof of the foreign tax status of the customer.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for standard B2B/B2C invoices. Invoices must contain specific VAT data. Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Swiss franc on both the current and capital accounts. There are no foreign exchange controls, no investment restrictions on non-residents, no approval requirements for inbound capital contributions, dividend distributions, profit remittances, intercompany loans, royalties, or trade-related payments. Cross-border capital flows are subject only to statistical balance-of-payments reporting duties, AML/sanctions compliance, and general civil law requirements. Profit remittance in CHF or any freely convertible currency is lawful without exchange control authorization; the only practical hurdles are tax withholding, AML source-of-funds verification, and financial intermediary due diligence.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Swiss law does not impose export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timelines for export receipts. A ship crew recruitment agency may retain and use foreign currency revenue indefinitely without converting to CHF. There is no statutory conversion ratio and no requirement that receipts be deposited in a specially designated account. Exporters and service providers must, however, maintain accurate books and comply with SNB balance-of-payments statistical reporting and with FINMA-sanctioned AML obligations when converting or transmitting large foreign-currency amounts.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera), with auxiliary supervisory and reporting functions exercised by the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low-to-Medium. An offshore digital EMI without a Swiss FINMA license cannot lawfully hold a Swiss CH-IBAN or provide payment services as a domestically regulated institution. EEA EMI passporting is not automatically recognized in Switzerland. Such EMIs may only be used as ancillary virtual-IBAN conduits, but they are generally rejected by Swiss payroll and vessel disbursement counterparties and present material AML, sanctions, and legal enforceability risk. A licensed Swiss securities firm or bank with a cloud-based onboarding platform is the preferred equivalent to a neobank experience.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident corporate shareholders in opaque or high-risk jurisdictions without visible economic substance.
        
Root cause: Non-resident corporate shareholders in opaque or high-risk jurisdictions without visible economic substance.

        
Defense evidence: Notarised shareholder register, corporate registry extracts, UBO declarations, audited financial statements or bank references for each shareholder, explanation of group structure, and evidence of the business rationale for the Swiss holding.

      

      
      

        Large inbound wires for capital injection categorized as personal savings or without contractual documentation.
        
Root cause: Large inbound wires for capital injection categorized as personal savings or without contractual documentation.

        
Defense evidence: Board resolution approving the capital increase, notarised certificate of subscription, bank account statements of the remitting shareholder, source-of-wealth report, and a signed declaration of tax and AML compliance.

      

      
      

        Crew payroll disbursement to many individual crew members in multiple low- and high-risk jurisdictions creating a perceived money-laundering or sanctions risk.
        
Root cause: Crew payroll disbursement to many individual crew members in multiple low- and high-risk jurisdictions creating a perceived money-laundering or sanctions risk.

        
Defense evidence: Complete seafarer employment agreements, manning agency contracts, vessel owner/client invoices, crew lists, statutory manning license, detailed payroll schedule with SWIFT beneficiary identifiers, and confirmation that each beneficiary is sanctioned-screened.

      

      
      

        Rapid movement of funds through a newly opened corporate account with low operational history and minimal Swiss substance.
        
Root cause: Rapid movement of funds through a newly opened corporate account with low operational history and minimal Swiss substance.

        
Defense evidence: Organizational chart demonstrating Swiss substance (office, directors, staff), cash-flow forecast, forecast of crew payroll cycles, signed lease, CH employment records, and an explanatory business plan for Swiss-based crewing management.

      

      
      

        Use of personal accounts or unregulated payment intermediaries for crew wage advances and vessel disbursements.
        
Root cause: Use of personal accounts or unregulated payment intermediaries for crew wage advances and vessel disbursements.

        
Defense evidence: Bank-approved payroll solution using dedicated cleared funds, signed master service agreement with a FINMA-regulated payment or banking provider, reconciliation protocols, and full audit trail of each beneficiary's identity and entitlement.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202/COVER to a Swiss bank account held by the Swiss AG, with the bank pre-approving the purpose and documentation. The remittance must include the corporate legal name, LEI if available, Swiss IBAN/BIC of the beneficiary, and a clear purpose of payment description, which the bank will map to the appropriate SIX Swiss payment purpose and SNB statistical balance-of-payments classification. Typical codes and narrative labels accepted include 'capital contribution', 'shareholder loan', or 'paid-in capital under Swiss Code of Obligations Art. 629-633'. Verification is not an exchange-control step but a bank-level AML/economic-background check: the client must provide the notarised shareholders' resolution, subscription confirmation, UBO declarations, and source-of-funds evidence. Swiss issuance stamp duty of 1% applies to share capital increases exceeding CHF 1 million, so the wire amount and legal documentation must be reconciled with the notional capital account and tax filing position.Outbound disbursements use: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border crew salary, supplier, and settlement payments; (2) SWIFT MT202 COVER for correspondent/aggregator funding; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR via ISO 20022 pain.001 messages; (4) Swiss domestic CHF clearing via SIC5 using pain.001; and (5) commercial letters of credit only where vessel operations or equipment procurement require documentary trade settlement. For crew payroll mass payments, Swiss banks require transmission of signed bulk payment files through EBICS, API, or secure host-to-host channels, with per-beneficiary sanctioned-name screening, BIC/IP-validated accounts, and a monthly payroll reconciliation report. 'Commercial LC' is not the standard rail for crew wages; it is reserved for shipowner/trade settlement and must be structured under UCP 600 with SWIFT MT700 messages and compliance certificates.Deliverable foreign exchange forwards on CHF/EUR, CHF/USD, CHF/GBP, and other freely convertible currency pairs through a Swiss bank or prime brokerNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to non-convertible or restricted emerging-market currencies, never for CHF, because CHF is fully deliverableCross-currency swaps and basis swaps to hedge long-term foreign-currency payroll, receivables, or vessel finance liabilitiesFX swaps and rollover swaps for short-term liquidity bridging between net settlement datesExchange-traded currency futures on major CHF pairs, executed through a Swiss-regulated brokerPlain vanilla currency options (FX puts/calls) for premium-protection on large crew payroll currency exposures

EEA EMI passporting is not automatically recognized in Switzerland. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
8.5% Swiss federal direct profit tax (flat on taxable net profit) plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; combined aggregate headline rate in the reference canton of Zug is approximately 11.5% to 14.8% depending on profit level and commune.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed by the Swiss AG; no additional cantonal or communal withholding tax.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
The ordinary filing deadline is six months after the close of the business year; for a calendar-year AG this is normally no later than 30 June of the following year. The tax administration may grant a discretionary extension upon duly reasoned request. Where no return is filed, the authority estimates the taxable base and issues an assessment with penalty interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law does not prescribe a single numeric threshold for every taxpayer. In practice, a contemporaneous OECD Local File is expected when any individual related-party transaction exceeds CHF 1 million or aggregate related-party transactions exceed CHF 5 million in a fiscal year. A Master File is expected when the Swiss entity is a member of a multinational group with consolidated group revenue above CHF 900 million, the Swiss CbCR threshold. Below these levels, the taxpayer should still retain contracts, invoices, and functional analysis to satisfy the Swiss tax authority's burden-of-proof practice.

      
Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but substance is a judicially enforceable anti-abuse doctrine. A Swiss crew recruitment AG must have a genuine registered office, local directors capable of independent management, payroll administration, bank accounts, contracts with shipowners, operational records, and adequate staff in Switzerland. If the AG is a letterbox company or merely invoices foreign crew management income through Switzerland without substantive activity, the tax authority may disregard the entity, reattribute the income to the economic owner abroad or in Switzerland, deny double-tax treaty benefits and withholding tax refunds, and impose penalties. The doctrine is reinforced under the Multilateral Instrument's principal purpose test and Swiss practice requiring the beneficial owner to exercise economic functions.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax False Declarations
        
Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income and abandoned tax advantages; default penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded for simple evasion; in fraudulent cases the criminal tax sanction may reach three times the evaded tax; confiscation and arrears interest are mandatory; imprisonment of up to three years is possible under federal and cantonal criminal tax statutes.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or order the creation of false books are civilly liable for the resulting damage under Swiss Code of Obligations Article 754. Criminal courts may impose personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; the authority may also pierce the corporate veil if the AG is undercapitalised or used as a pure instrument of fraud.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Letterbox Substance Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: The tax administration can disregard the Swiss corporate shell, allocate profits to the real foreign or domestic taxpayer, deny treaty benefits, reclassify accumulated profits as constructive dividends subject to 35% withholding tax, and order commercial registry dissolution if the registered office no longer corresponds to the actual administration; administrative fines and default tax assessments are imposed.

        
Director personal liability: Nominee directors face civil liability for acting without genuine instruction if they sign false minutes or engage in sham governance; they may be ordered to restore company assets, receive personal fines for violation of company law, and face a travel ban in criminal or regulatory investigations.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Constructive Dividend
        
Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment adding the differential to taxable profit, plus interest on the additional tax; the amount is usually recharacterised as a constructive dividend subject to Swiss federal withholding tax at 35%; penalties for incomplete disclosure can be added up to 100% of the adjusted tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who personally approve non-arm's-length intra-group contracts are jointly liable for the tax adjustment and withholding tax not collected; if the arrangement is used to strip profits from the Swiss company, the veil can be pierced and directors must compensate creditors and the tax authority from personal assets.

      

      
      

        Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Concealment
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act and Swiss Criminal Code, penalties include imprisonment of up to three years and fines of up to CHF 500,000 for aggravated money laundering, confiscation of funds, withdrawal of any required Swiss maritime staffing licence, and inclusion in AML sanction databases; company-level fines are possible under Article 102 of the Swiss Criminal Code.

        
Director personal liability: Officers are personally liable for intentional failure to submit anti-money laundering reports to the Swiss Money Laundering Reporting Office and are exposed to criminal prosecution, restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment; regulatory authorities may additionally prohibit them from acting as directors in the maritime sector.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax and Social Security Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: The Swiss Federal Tax Administration issues a collection demand for the full withholding tax not withheld, plus late-payment interest; criminal penalties and fines are imposed for culpable non-remittance, and social security institutions impose contribution arrears, penalties, and denial of deduction. In serious cases, imprisonment of up to three years may apply.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to pay dividends while insolvent and without withholding are jointly liable to the tax authority for the uncollected 35% tax and any damage to creditors. Directors may personally be named in collection rulings, subjected to travel bans, and barred from cross-border travel during tax and social security enforcement proceedings.
