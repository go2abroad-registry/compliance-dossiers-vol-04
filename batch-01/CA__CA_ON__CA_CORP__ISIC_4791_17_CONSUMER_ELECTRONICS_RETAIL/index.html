Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) administers federal corporate income tax and the GST/HST; operational portal is My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting through CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) and CAED (Canadian Automated Export Declarations). The Ontario Ministry of Finance is the provincial counterpart under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA), under which the 8% Ontario provincial portion of the 13% Ontario HST is collected and administered by the CRA alongside the 5% federal GST.

E-government filing portal

Canadian resident Business Corporation: the CRA automatically issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon federal incorporation notification from Corporations Canada or Ontario provincial incorporation from the Companies Office. The BN must be activated with program accounts: RP0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RC0001 (payroll), and RM0001 (import/export, via CBSA). Step-by-step: (1) file articles of incorporation; (2) obtain BN through CRA Business Registration Online, My Business Account, or by phone at 1-800-959-5525; (3) register RT0001 online or by submitting Form RC1; the GST/HST effective registration date is the earliest of the first taxable supply date, the date registration is requested, or a specified later date; (4) open an RM import/export account via CBSA CARM. Non-resident foreign entities not incorporated in Canada: complete Form RC1 plus the applicable Non-Resident schedule, provide a certified copy of the foreign certificate of incorporation, list of directors, home-jurisdiction tax identification number (TIN), and appoint a Canadian representative/agent with written authorization (Form RC273); the CRA then assigns a BN and RT0001 account. Foreign e-commerce vendors have access to a simplified GST/HST non-resident registration (no Canadian address or agent required) effective July 1, 2021. Registration is free, no minimum capital is required, and foreign ownership is permitted; separate Ontario extra-provincial registration may be required under the Ontario Business Corporations Act before carrying on business in Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian Business Corporation (federal/CA_ON) is a separate legal taxpayer resident in Canada, subject to Part I Income Tax Act on its worldwide income; no check-the-box or pass-through election is available for a Canadian corporation. Dividends from Canadian corporations are generally deductible to Canadian corporate shareholders under ITR s. 112; controlled foreign affiliate and foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules apply to non-resident subsidiaries under ITA ss. 91-95; foreign branch income is included on a worldwide basis with foreign tax credits; foreign affiliate dividends may be exempt through the s. 113 deduction. Ontario corporate income tax under the Corporations Tax Act is levied at the general rate of 10% on taxable income allocated to Ontario and is administered by the CRA under CITCA.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections for a Canadian corporation: (i) ETA s. 156(2) joint election by closely related registrants to treat inter-corporate taxable supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 167(1) joint election to waive self-assessment on certain supplies; (iii) ITA s. 18.2(1)(b) joint election to reduce the deduction limit where hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.6, effective July 1, 2022) would otherwise defer or deny a deduction on a cross-border payment; (iv) ITA s. 89(11) paid-up capital election in tax-deferred corporate reorganizations; (v) ETA s. 273 joint election for unincorporated joint ventures so one participant is treated as the operator/registrant. Elections under ITA ss. 216 and 217 are available to non-residents only. Ontario HST elections mirror ETA Part IX; no separate provincial filing is required beyond the CRA GST/HST return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(1): a person (including an Ontario-resident corporation) must register once its worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold; a non-resident not carrying on business in Canada has no mandatory registration unless making taxable supplies in Canada. Effective July 1, 2021, non-resident electronic-commerce platform operators and marketplace sellers of digital and tangible goods are deemed suppliers under ETA s. 149.2 and must register, collect, and remit GST/HST on Canadian sales regardless of their own supply volume. Voluntary registration below the threshold allows input tax credit recovery but imposes collection obligations. Ontario has no separate VAT registration threshold; the HST is collected through the federal registrant system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods (physical consumer electronics): zero-rated under ETA s. 16 where the supplier is the exporter and the goods are exported from Canada as part of the supply; the supplier must obtain and retain documentary proof of export, including the customs export declaration, carrier waybills showing the foreign destination and delivery, and foreign payment evidence, per CRA Memorandum 17-4-1. (2) Services (e.g., installation, extended warranty, software/support services): zero-rated under ETA s. 9 where supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA, unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. (3) Intangible personal property and digital goods (downloadable software, digital warranties, e-books, apps): zero-rated under ETA s. 21.2 where supplied to a non-resident person for use or consumption wholly outside Canada. (4) For online retail drop-shipment, export zero-rating requires the goods to physically leave Canada; a foreign recipient address on the order plus carrier proof of export and payment in foreign currency is required. If export substantiation fails, the supply is re-characterised as taxable at 13% HST.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (EED/B13A) filed through CBSA CARM or CAED for shipments with a fair market value exceeding CAD 2,000, including export declaration number and customs release data
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or certified copy) naming the exporter and foreign consignee, showing the foreign destination and signed proof of delivery (POD)
  • Commercial invoice and sales/purchase order documenting the transaction, sales price, currency, Incoterms, and non-resident customer identity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or PayPal/Stripe payout record evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident customer
  • Packing list, shipping manifest, and carrier tracking logs (Canada Post, USPS, FedEx, UPS, DHL scan events) demonstrating the goods left Canada and were delivered outside Canada
  • Foreign customs entry/import declaration of the consignee (where available) and any inspection certificates; all documentation must be retained for six years under ETA s. 286(1)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control framework exists in Canada. The CRA does not pre-authorize invoices. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M, and all T2 corporate income tax returns must be filed electronically. E-invoicing is voluntary in Ontario; a Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework is being adopted in phases, and Quebec has legislated a B2B e-invoicing mandate with staggered implementation from late 2026, which does not apply to Ontario-based suppliers. Registrants must retain electronic records and source documents in readable and auditable format for six years under ETA s. 286. For e-commerce, platform operators must maintain platform-level transaction data for deemed-supplier compliance, and may remit through CRA's online portal; there is no API-based clearance requirement or digital invoice stamping mandate at the federal or Ontario level.

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching CBSA CARM customs export data, carrier tracking logs, and banking or payment-rail records. Where export substantiation is inadequate, the supply is re-characterised as taxable at 13% HST and assessed for unremitted tax, denied input tax credits, and interest within the normal four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298), with no time limit where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established (ETA s. 298(4)). Civil penalties include: ETA s. 280(1) - greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax deficiency for making false statements or omissions knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; ETA s. 285(1) - 50% gross-negligence penalty of the additional tax. Criminal liability under ETA s. 327 for evasion: on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to five years; on summary conviction, a fine up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years. Refunds and input tax credits previously claimed on zero-rated exports are clawed back with compound daily interest. Separately, ITA s. 17(1) deems interest income into the Canadian exporters income where any receivable from a non-resident remains outstanding for more than 180 days at fiscal year-end, unless repaid during the year or excluded as an ordinary-course trade receivable; practical mitigation requires documented 180-day FX repatriation and intercompany settlement procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account purposes. There are no exchange-control approvals, notional limits, or special regimes applicable to importers, exporters, e-commerce merchants, non-resident shareholders, or intercompany financing. Profit remittances (dividends, management fees, royalties, and repayment of intercompany debt) are freely executable through any Canadian Schedule I bank. The only structural interface is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, generally reduced under an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., Article 10 of the Canada-U.S. Convention). Retail settlement currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, CNH) may be converted into CAD without licence and without any mandated conversion scheme.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender percentage for foreign-currency export proceeds. An Ontario e-commerce exporter may retain USD or CNH settlement balances abroad or in a Canadian foreign-currency account indefinitely. FINTRAC reporting arises only for cash or wire transfers at or above CAD 10,000, constituting a transparency obligation rather than a control. Export Development Canada provides trade-credit and political-risk insurance but administers no FX surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as fiscal agent for the Department of Finance Canada. Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control administration; freedom of foreign-exchange dealings is recognized under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), the Bank of Canada Act, and Canada's International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations. Funds-flow integrity and sanctions enforcement are administered under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, supervised by FINTRAC, and the Special Economic Measures Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, with legal constraints. Canada does not operate an EU-style EMI passport. A non-resident digital payment provider (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury or similar) that performs FX, remittance, or payments services in Canada must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. An unregistered foreign EMI cannot obtain a domestic settlement channel and may trigger de-risking by the primary bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate (federal 15% after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction plus Ontario 10%). Qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) are subject to a combined 12.2% rate (federal 9% via the federal Small Business Deduction plus Ontario 3.2%) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income annually.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on the gross amount of dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Treaty-reduced rates apply, for example, 5% for a shareholder holding at least 10% of voting control under the Canada–US DTA, 15% for portfolio dividends; similar 5%/15% structures apply under most Canadian DTAs with OECD member states.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — the aggregate fair market value of all reportable transactions with each non-resident related party in a taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and the obligation to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation acceptable under s.247(4) and CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R. CRA will impose the s.247(3) 10% transfer pricing penalty where an adjustment exceeds the arm's length range and the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts; Master File/Local File depth is expected for multijurisdictional groups and Country-by-Country reporting (CbCR) applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.0–1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 of the Income Tax Act) recharacterizes avoidance transactions that lack genuine economic substance; the 'central management and control' test determines de facto corporate residency; Ontario corporations must maintain a registered office, corporate minute books, an Ontario Annual Return, and a physical presence where business is carried on. CRA applies beneficial-ownership and substance-over-form principles in e-commerce audits, and a non-resident-controlled Ontario shell company that merely holds title while management decisions occur offshore will be vulnerable to GAAR recharacterization and PE attribution. Ratified Canadian DTAs include Limitation-on-Benefits and Principal-Purpose Test provisions denying treaty benefits to entities without substantive business activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Unreported E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of the Income Tax Act (minimum CAD 500); 25% failure-to-report penalty on unreported revenue; daily compound interest; CRA judicial compulsion of payment-processor records; criminal prosecution under s.239(1) with imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded; seizure of corporate and personal assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated are assessed personally for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under s.227.1(1) of the ITA and s.323 of the Excise Tax Act; criminal charges against directors and beneficial owners; travel bans and departure restrictions are common during prosecution; courts will pierce the corporate veil and order personal restitution where the corporation is a mere sham.
Transfer Pricing Fraud — Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: 10% transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3)/(3.1) on the gross or net adjustment; full reassessment plus compounded interest; T106 non-filing penalties up to CAD 25,000 plus daily penalties; GAAR application to recharacterize the scheme; Ontario reassessment through the integrated T2 mechanism.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false or incomplete T106 returns face criminal liability under s.380 of the Criminal Code (fraud over CAD 5,000 — imprisonment up to 14 years); shareholders can be assessed personally if the corporate adjustment causes insolvency and funds were diverted; international travel restrictions and asset-freezing orders are routinely sought.
Shell Company / Nominee Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA denial of treaty benefits under Limitation-on-Benefits and Principal-Purpose Test; GAAR recharacterization; 50% gross-negligence penalty on the adjusted income; Ontario Registrar may administratively dissolve the corporation for failure to file annual returns; foreign parent registrations under the Extra-Provincial Companies Act; Part XIII withholding assessed at 25% on all distributions.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face civil fraud liability and regulatory prosecution for enabling tax evasion; beneficial owners residing abroad are personally pursued through competent-authority mutual assistance; travel bans and international arrest warrants may issue upon criminal indictment; the CRA can pierce the veil when the corporation is a mere agent or facade.
AML Non-Compliance — Unregistered Parallel Payment Systems

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for a corporation; cease-and-desist orders; criminal money-laundering prosecution under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years; forfeiture of linked property; concurrent CRA reassessment and gross-negligence penalties for unreported flows.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who authorize or direct the AML violation are criminally liable and subject to personal imprisonment, travel bans, asset freezing, and extradition orders; civil restitution to victims applies; the corporate veil is set aside where the company acts as an instrumentality of the principals.
GST/HST and Payroll Remittance Defalcation

Statutory sanction: GST/HST failure-to-remit penalty of 3% of the net tax amount, increasing to 6% when default exceeds 3 months; 10% payroll source-remittance penalty under s.227(2) of the ITA; false-bookkeeping penalty of 20% under s.163(4); CRA garnishment of Stripe/PayPal settlement funds and freezing of merchant accounts; daily compound interest on all amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and trustees are personally, jointly, and severally liable for all unremitted GST/HST (s.323 ETA) and unremitted source deductions (s.227.1 ITA) without proof of fraud; the CRA issues directors' liability certificates registrable as judgments against personal assets; executives face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and personal bankruptcy exposure.
