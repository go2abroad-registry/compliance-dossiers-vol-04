Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), operating the integrated online enterprise register 'Registre des entreprises' at registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

Governing statute

Primary: Quebec Business Corporations Act (Loi sur les sociétés par actions), RLRQ c S-31.1, ss. 3, 9, 23, 106, 112 and 137-142 (incorporation by articles, registered office, directors, director residence, officers). Federal alternative for a Canada-wide presence: Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c C-44, ss. 5-8. For a single-province Quebec retail supermarket, provincial incorporation is the standard efficient route and is selected here.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts de constitution / Articles of incorporation filed electronically under s. 9 of the Quebec Business Corporations Act; completion of filing results in issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation (certificat de constitution) and the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) - federal GST/HST administration and corporate income tax; portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca). Revenu Quebec (RQ) - Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax; portal: Mon dossier d'entreprise (revenuquebec.ca).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A federal/provincial business corporation is an autonomous taxable Canadian corporation subject to federal Part I tax and Quebec corporate income tax; distributions are taxed again at the shareholder level as dividends. Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status may be available only where the corporation is not controlled directly or indirectly by non-residents, a public corporation, or a corporation with public or listed share ownership, and such status affects the small business deduction and refundable dividend tax on investment income. There is no domestic pass-through or check-the-box classification available for a CA_CORP.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; corporate classification is statutory and per se. Available elections include: Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 and the mirror Quebec Sales Tax Act (QSTA) provision for closely related corporations, which deem qualifying intercompany taxable supplies to be made for no consideration; Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 85 and s. 85.1 rollover elections on qualifying incorporation and asset-transfer transactions; ITA s. 256 associated-corporation elections for the small business deduction; GST/HST and QST input tax credit allocation options; and hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.2-18.3, which deny deductions for deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes in cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: GST/HST registration under the ETA becomes mandatory when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or, on a cumulative four-consecutive-quarter basis; the QST registration threshold under the QSTA is aligned at CAD 30,000 using the same small-supplier concept. The supermarket, with physical store turnover well above the threshold, must collect GST/HST and QST on taxable supplies, file monthly or quarterly returns electronically, and remit net tax to CRA and Revenu Quebec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible goods, including imported Asian grocery stock, is zero-rated where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada or transfers possession to a common carrier or consignee for export, and the goods are actually exported within 60 days of the transfer (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1; mirror QSTA zero-rating). Services supplied to a non-resident may also be zero-rated if performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to an unregistered non-resident under the statutory exceptions, including services in respect of goods that are exported. Retail over-the-counter sales to travelers or tourists remain taxable unless the supplier ships the goods out of Canada. Domestic sales of basic groceries are separately zero-rated under Schedule VI Part III and require no export documentation. To claim export zero-rating, the supermarket must retain contemporaneous satisfactory evidence of export as described in CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-5, including customs reports, transport documents, freight forwarder certificates, and proof of payment. GST/HST and QST paid at customs on imported commercial inventory is recoverable as input tax credits in the same reporting period by the registrant.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or paper B13A Export Declaration where export reporting is required
  • Bill of Lading, waybill, or air waybill issued by the carrier evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods, quantities, origin, and sale consideration
  • Freight forwarder or customs broker certificate of export meeting CRA Memorandum 17-5 evidence requirements
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, foreign-exchange receivables advice, or bank confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or mandatory API-based clearance regime currently applies in Canada. CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing for most corporate registrants and for registrants meeting prescribed thresholds; Revenu Quebec requires electronic QST return filing for corporate registrants. E-invoicing using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework; Revenu Quebec has announced, but not yet brought into force, a modernization proposing standard electronic invoicing and real-time sales data reporting for the QST. Retail POS receipts do not require transmission to the tax authority, but books and records must support the tax charged on every taxable sale.

CRA and Revenu Quebec apply heightened scrutiny to zero-rated export claims and require documentary proof for every export supply. Failure to substantiate export within the statutory window, generally 60 days for goods, re-characterizes the supply as taxable domestic supply and triggers reassessment, interest, and penalties. Civil penalties include the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax for false statements or omissions attributable to negligence; gross negligence and wilful evasion attract the penalty framework on the entire tax avoided. Retrospective clawbacks are permitted beyond the normal four-year reassessment period whenever a return contains a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Directors of the corporation are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and QST, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act or the Criminal Code may result in significant fines and imprisonment in export zero-rating fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, freely convertible currency regime under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. No prior regulatory approval is required for foreign direct investment except for net-benefit review under the Investment Canada Act for control-related thresholds. Profit remittance (dividends, royalties, management fees, interest) is permitted without FX restriction, subject to Canadian withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaty relief, plus Quebec-based provincial requirements where relevant. CAD settlement is effected through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), now transitioning to the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for retail and high-value payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain, invest, or pay out foreign currency earnings freely. Resident exporters only need to comply with Statistics Act balance-of-payments reporting if they are designated reporting dealers, and with FINTRAC reporting thresholds for international electronic funds transfers (CAD $10,000 or more within 24 consecutive hours).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec securities and financial products regulation by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited but conditional. Offshore digital EMIs without a physical Canadian presence are not viable as primary clearing banks because they cannot take deposits in Canada. A fintech/EMI structure in Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and may also be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Such entities can perform ancillary cross-border payment routing, currency conversion, and IBAN/account issuance in alternative currencies, but every CAD-denominated treasury flow must ultimately settle in a domestic chartered bank or Quebecrecognized credit union account to access LVTS and CDIC deposit insurance. Viability rating: Medium for auxiliary liquidity rails; Low as substitute for a Tier-1 corporate bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the company's CAD-denominated operational account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The remittance information (field 70/72) must state the purpose distinctly, e.g., 'Shareholder equity injection' or 'Subordinated shareholder loan'. Although Canada does not mandate a formal purpose code, the receiving bank may require a board resolution, updated share register or loan agreement, and a source-of-funds questionnaire to differentiate a true capital contribution from a non-arm's length inflow for tax and FINTRAC reporting. Incoming international electronic funds transfers of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC. Funds settle in CAD via LVTS and are available for trading inventory, lease payments, and payroll once the compliance hold is satisfied. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined general rate 26.5% (federal 15% = 38% statutory minus 10% provincial abatement minus 13% general rate reduction; Quebec 11.5%). Small business rate 12.2% combined (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%) on the first C$500,000 of Canadian active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid to non-residents of Canada (ITA s. 212(2)), regardless of the corporate profits; no withholding when the shareholder is resident in Canada; deemed dividends (ITA s. 84) on share redemptions or excessive paid-up capital are equally subject to 25%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due 6 months after the taxation year-end (June 30 for a December 31 year-end). Instalments of federal tax are due monthly or quarterly under ITA s. 157 when the balance of tax payable exceeds C$3,000; balance payment due on the unextended return deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 due 6 months after fiscal year-end; final Quebec balance due at the same date; late-instalment interest compounded daily at 9% (base 5% plus 4%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) must be prepared, signed and kept where the aggregate value of transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year; CRA may prescribe a higher threshold for services-only transactions; the s. 247(3) penalty is 10% of the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment and is triggered only when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue or C$5,000,000; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report (Forms T5209 and T5208) become mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least C$850,000,000 for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2023.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and the 'bona fide business purpose' test rather than a standalone Economic Substance Regulation; the optimal structural posture is a registered Quebec permanent establishment with leased premises, a valid NEQ and municipal licence, physical inventory, real employees, POS Interac and credit card rails, and independent suppliers; interposing an offshore shell (e.g., a Hong Kong or Singapore procurement vehicle with no personnel, no premises and no risks) solely to recharacterize profits will be disregarded under the sham doctrine, GAAR recharacterization, and anti-treaty-shopping principles (beneficial ownership clauses).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Cash Suppression

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on summary conviction - fine equal to 50%-100% of the evaded tax (minimum C$2,550) and up to 2 years' imprisonment; on indictment - fine equal to 100%-200% of the evaded tax and up to 5 years' imprisonment; civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; Revenu Québec imposes mirror penalties under the QTA (fine equal to 50%-100% and imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and bookkeepers who sign false T2/CO-17 returns are individually prosecutable; the corporate veil is pierced under s. 239(1)(a) for false or deceptive entries; conviction leads to restitution orders, forfeiture of assets, securities on bail restricting travel, and in serious cases, a passport seizure and travel ban pending trial.
Unremitted Withholdings and Payroll Fraud (Director Liability)

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(9)-(10): penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus 10% for each repeated failure, compounded daily interest at the prescribed rate; Quebec QTA ss. 33.13 and 178 mirror the regime; CRA may freeze corporate bank accounts and seize inventory.

Director personal liability: ITA s. 227.1 and QTA s. 33.13 impose automatic civil liability on directors for all unremitted source deductions and QST without any showing of fraud; enforcement continues against personal assets and joint bank accounts; Quebec may suspend a director's driver's licence, passport and liquor/grocery permit for non-remittance, and travel bans apply pending recovery.
Sham Transactions and General Anti-Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 245(2) GAAR: full disallowance of the avoidance benefit, recharacterization of loans as deemed dividends, reassessment of Part XIII withholding at 25% plus the s. 163(2) 50% penalty for misrepresentation; anti-treaty-shopping and beneficial-ownership tests reject the treaty rate; penalties and compound interest accrue from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute the sham are jointly liable under s. 239(1)(a) for false entries; the court may pierce the corporate veil to impose restitution; where the arrangement is part of an international profit-stripping scheme, criminal tax-fraud charges (indictable, up to 5 years) and asset freezing orders against the directors are obtainable.
