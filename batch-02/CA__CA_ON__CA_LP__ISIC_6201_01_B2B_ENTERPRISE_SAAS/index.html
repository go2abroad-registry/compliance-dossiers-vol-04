Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement for an Ontario limited partnership. Limited partners must make a bona fide contribution of money or property as set out in the Limited Partnership Agreement; withdrawal of that contribution while the partnership is insolvent or where such withdrawal causes insolvency renders the limited partner liable to creditors (s. 16 LP Act). A limited partner who participates in control of the business risks liability as a general partner (s. 14).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07) — building fire safety plan, fire alarm and extinguisher certification, and municipal fire inspection clearance for office occupancy. Structural clearance: Ontario Building Code (O. Reg. 350/06) Certificate of Occupancy confirming Group D office live loads and accessibility under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005. Occupational clearance: Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1) s. 25 and Reg. 851 workplace inspections, first-aid kit, ergonomic assessments, and supervisor health-and-safety training; municipal occupancy inspection grants the final open-for-business status.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration for the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) and the Income Tax Act. Portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html). Ontario-specific portal: Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/business-registry) for provincial limited partnership registration; Ontario's 8% provincial HST component is fully administered and collected by the CRA, with no separate Ontario tax registration portal required.

E-government filing portal

An Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) formed by non-resident partners and carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Procedure: (1) register the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through the Ontario Business Registry (name search, LP declaration filing, and Ontario registry number issuance); (2) apply to CRA for the BN using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or online via My Business Account, completing the GST/HST (RT) program account application; (3) answer the 'small supplier' determination question to assess the CAD $30,000 mandatory registration threshold; (4) simultaneously open the RM (import/export) program account and RP (payroll) program account if employees are hired or commercial goods are imported — for a pure cloud SaaS delivery, only RT is required; (5) provide the CRA with the LP agreement, the partners' non-resident status declarations (Form NR3010 for non-resident partners), and a Canadian agent for service acceptance; (6) the LP files an annual T5013 partnership information return if it has Canadian operations or income from Canadian sources, regardless of its zero-rated export profile. Voluntary GST/HST registration takes effect immediately and is recommended to recover input tax credits on Canadian startup inputs.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax: the partnership is not a taxpayer; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to each partner under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA). The LP is, however, a separate 'person' for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and must account for HST as a registrant in its own name. Territorial scope: a non-resident LP is treated as a non-resident partnership; Canadian-source business income of the partnership is allocated to non-resident partners and is subject to Canadian Part I income tax (with foreign tax credit relief in the partner's residence country); passive distributions of rental, royalty, or interest to non-resident partners may attract Part XIII withholding tax. Ontario territorial allocation: for a partnership with Ontario permanent establishment activity, business income is allocated to Ontario under the Ontario allocation rules, and the partners (including non-residents) file Ontario T1/T2 returns with respect to the Ontario-sourced allocation. — An Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) formed by non-resident partners and carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Procedure: (1) register the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through the Ontario Business Registry (name search, LP declaration filing, and Ontario registry number issuance); (2) apply to CRA for the BN using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or online via My Business Account, completing the GST/HST (RT) program account application; (3) answer the 'small supplier' determination question to assess the CAD $30,000 mandatory registration threshold; (4) simultaneously open the RM (import/export) program account and RP (payroll) program account if employees are hired or commercial goods are imported — for a pure cloud SaaS delivery, only RT is required; (5) provide the CRA with the LP agreement, the partners' non-resident status declarations (Form NR3010 for non-resident partners), and a Canadian agent for service acceptance; (6) the LP files an annual T5013 partnership information return if it has Canadian operations or income from Canadian sources, regardless of its zero-rated export profile. Voluntary GST/HST registration takes effect immediately and is recommended to recover input tax credits on Canadian startup inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax: the partnership is not a taxpayer; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to each partner under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA). The LP is, however, a separate 'person' for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and must account for HST as a registrant in its own name. Territorial scope: a non-resident LP is treated as a non-resident partnership; Canadian-source business income of the partnership is allocated to non-resident partners and is subject to Canadian Part I income tax (with foreign tax credit relief in the partner's residence country); passive distributions of rental, royalty, or interest to non-resident partners may attract Part XIII withholding tax. Ontario territorial allocation: for a partnership with Ontario permanent establishment activity, business income is allocated to Ontario under the Ontario allocation rules, and the partners (including non-residents) file Ontario T1/T2 returns with respect to the Ontario-sourced allocation.

Available election forms

Canada does not administer a 'check-the-box' election; entity classification is statutory under the ITA and ETA. Available elections and designations for the CA_LP structure include: (1) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) and s. 96 to select a non-calendar fiscal period for the LP; (2) election under ITA s. 21(1) to capitalize foreign exchange gains/losses on partnership debt; (3) ETA s. 156 joint election for corporate partners to treat certain inter-entity supplies as having no consideration (applicable to incorporated SaaS group members); (4) ETA s. 211.1 election for a non-resident supplier to be deemed to have a Canadian permanent establishment for GST/HST purposes; (5) ITA Part XIII and regulation 105/102 withholding elections allowing non-resident partners to reduce or defer Canadian withholding where the partner files a protection election; (6) hybrid mismatch disclosure on Form T1134 and the ITA s. 94.1 're-characterization' election/attribution rules for offshore partnerships; (7) the CRA optional 'one-time election' under ETA s. 240.1(3) permitting a partnership to be treated as a registrant separately from its partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold. A partnership (including a non-resident LP) is exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration until its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. For a non-resident with no Canadian physical presence, mandatory registration is required if the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada above the threshold; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits (ITCs). SaaS revenue from Canadian B2B customers counts toward the threshold and is subject to 13% HST unless the supply is zero-rated (export) or exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 7 of the Excise Tax Act and section 9 of Schedule VI, a supply of a service or intangible personal property (including enterprise B2B cloud software / SaaS) made to a non-resident person is zero-rated (0%) if all the following conditions are met: (a) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST at the time of the supply, or, if registered, did not acquire the supply for consumption, use, or supply in Canada; (b) the supply is not related to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (c) the service is performed substantially outside Canada (for digital services, the place of performance test is substituted by the 'ordinary course of use' and 'delivery outside Canada' analysis). For SaaS exports to B2B customers, CRA generally zero-rates the supply if the customer's billing and operational addresses are outside Canada, the contract is signed offshore, and the service is consumed at non-Canadian locations; data processing performed on Canadian servers does not, by itself, break zero-rating where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not consumed in Canada. Claiming zero-rating permits the LP to claim ITCs for GST/HST paid on Canadian-cost inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED) — required only when tangible media, hardware, or server equipment crosses Canada's border; for pure cloud SaaS, a service export documentation package including the executed SaaS agreement, statement of work, and offshore delivery evidence
  • Commercial invoice / CRA-compliant tax invoice bearing the LP's BN, supplier identification, invoice date, service description, consideration, and an explicit 'zero-rated export — section 9 of Schedule VI' notation
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill — applicable only for physical goods shipments; for SaaS, a server-access or delivery log demonstrating that the software platform was made available to the non-resident customer outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT wire confirmation or bank transfer record evidencing payment from the non-resident customer's bank account to the LP's Canadian or foreign account
  • Certificate of foreign non-residency — tax residence certificate or equivalent foreign business registration document, and a signed declaration confirming the customer is not registered under Part IX of the ETA for GST/HST at the time of supply

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax authority integration, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls exist in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE service (Form GST34) on an annual, quarterly, or monthly basis; payments are made through CRA online banking or wire transfer. Record retention requirements mandate that invoices, contracts, and payment records be kept in machine-readable format for six years. The Government of Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework on a voluntary basis for business-to-government transactions; Ontario has not imposed mandatory e-invoicing or clearance. Standard CRA tax invoice requirements for HST: supplier's BN, invoice date, recipient name, description of the SaaS services, total consideration, HST amount charged or an 'HST included' statement, and the rate applied.

CRA GST/HST auditors apply heightened scrutiny to export zero-rating claims, testing for: (i) genuineness of the non-resident recipient; (ii) the recipient's GST/HST registration status; (iii) place-of-supply analysis; and (iv) the commercial substance of the offshore payment flow. Legal consequences for false zero-rating claims include: (a) penalty under ETA s. 285.1 equal to 50% of the net tax overstated on a false statement; (b) gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285(1) up to 50% of the net understated tax; (c) daily compound interest at the prescribed rate on the reassessed HST; (d) retrospective reassessments extending the normal four-year limitation period indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or willful default is established; (e) denial of input tax credits with cascading reassessment of the LP's T5013 and the partners' income tax returns; (f) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and s. 329 for evasion or false statements, with fines up to CAD $25,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; and (g) disqualification of the non-resident LP from the GST/HST registration and compulsory revocation of its BN. The 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline is statutorily reinforced by the ITA's reserve rules under s. 20(11), which deny a seller's deduction for accounts receivable if settlement from the non-resident customer has not been received within the fiscal period; failure to repatriate within 180 days may also trigger the CRA's 'transaction not at arm's length' review and transfer-pricing adjustment under ITA s. 247(2).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts and has been an IMF Article VIII member since 1952. No statutory approval is required for an Ontario Limited Partnership to open foreign-currency accounts, execute spot/forward FX, or transfer funds cross-border. Repatriation and profit remittance to non-resident partners are unrestricted under FX law; however, distributions are subject to Canadian withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), generally 25% via s. 227(8.4), with treaty-reduced rates available (e.g., Canada–US Tax Convention) via NR301/NR302 forms. The Canadian dollar floats freely; no earmarking, net-open-position, or approval rules apply to a non-financial SaaS operating entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Foreign Exchange Control Act (1939) was repealed in 1951; Canada imposes no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no holding-period requirement for export or service proceeds. SaaS subscription receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or other foreign currencies. The only compliance triggers are FINTRAC cross-border electronic-fund-transfer reporting for wires of CAD 10,000 or more and currency/monetary-instrument reporting at the border, both automatically administered by the clearing bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2). Foreign exchange policy authority resides with the Minister of Finance; prudential supervision of clearing banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, S.C. 2000, c. 17.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability as a secondary disbursement layer, not as the primary settlement bank. Wise Business (FINTRAC-registered MSB) and Airwallex/Payoneer can service contractor payouts and small-value FX; Stripe Billing (via Stripe Canada Ltd.) remains the optimal merchant-of-record for recurring B2B SaaS collections. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must hold a FINTRAC MSB registration and, if operating in Ontario, a Money Service Business Act, 2020 licence from the Ontario Ministry of Finance; (ii) client funds at EMIs are uninsured pooled balances, so the LP should cap daily EMI balances; (iii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) registration with the Bank of Canada should be verified for the PSP; (iv) statutory payroll remittances (CRA source deductions) and audited financial flows must be settled through a Schedule I D-SIB account.

Airwallex Fit: High

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million; Master File required if total transactions exceed CAD 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to transactions lacking economic substance. The CRA may also challenge arrangements that are primarily tax-motivated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset seizure possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-characterize transactions, impose penalties up to 100% of tax avoided, and revoke tax treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for civil restitution and face criminal charges for fraud; travel bans and imprisonment up to 14 years.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly fail to comply; travel bans and prohibition from serving as directors.
