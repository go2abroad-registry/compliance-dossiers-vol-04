Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for the Québec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec provincial corporate income tax; Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec Clic Revenu / Mon Dossier d'Entreprise

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation not resident in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a non-resident GST/HST account via CRA (Form RC391 or RC1, granting a 9-digit BN with an RT prefix). For Quebec nexus, the entity must obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) by registering extra-provincially with the Registraire d'entreprises (REQ) (Form AR-1/AR-1A), then register with Revenu Québec for the QST using the QST registration form attached to the NEQ (QST registrant number sequenced as NR prefix or TQ preceded by the NEQ). Registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the small-supplier threshold or engaging in a taxable activity requiring a permit/licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation is a separate taxable legal person subject to Part I federal corporate income tax and Quebec Part IV corporate income tax; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available for corporate shareholders, and worldwide income of a resident corporation is subject to Canadian taxation with foreign tax credits.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime; corporate status is determined by the incorporating statutes. Relevant statutory elections include: GST/HST closely related corporation election (Section 156, Excise Tax Act), Quick Method of accounting election for GST/HST and QST, Section 216 election for non-resident rental income, and Section 219 corporate branch returns election; hybrid mismatch rules adopted under BEPS Action 2 (2021-2023) deny deduction/non-inclusion benefits but do not create entity classification choices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made during any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold) for both GST/HST and QST; a non-resident is also required to register if its worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 and supplies are made in Canada, subject to the same small-supplier exceptions.

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime currently applies in Canada or Quebec; the CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices if the EDI/electronic format contains all prescribed information and supports complete audit trails. Quebec's Act respecting the modernization of invoicing provisions will make standardized electronic invoicing (B2B and B2C) and electronic reporting to Revenu Québec mandatory on a phased schedule: from September 2026 for enterprises with annual turnover above C$15 million and from September 2027 for all other taxable suppliers; the regime mandates structured-format issuance and transmission via the Boomerang/ government-recognized components rather than a single-state fiscal clearance API.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible and freely floating currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, prior authorizations, or surrender requirements. Capital injection, dividend remittance, management fees, and intercompany loan repayments are subject to normal corporate law solvency tests and Quebec Civil Code restrictions on unlawful dividends, and to non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Part XIII) and applicable treaty provisions. Profit remittance is structurally free but may be subject to a 25% domestic withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, reduced to 5% or 15% under the Canada-US treaty, and to anti-money laundering beneficial ownership disclosure. Medical/dental aligner revenues from export sales are freely convertible into CAD or any other currency; no forced conversion or X-code licensing applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to deposit export receipts in a designated domestic account. Exporters may contract, invoice, and hold proceeds in any convertible currency. However, for receipts of CAD 10,000 or more in a single direction, financial institutions and the reporting entity must complete an Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) report to FINTRAC; the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs export documentation (via the Canadian Border Services Agency, or under the U.S.-Canada periodic reporting mechanism) must be retained for the six-year record-keeping period set out in the PCMLTFA Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada, with transaction-level monitoring and reporting enforcement under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with significant legal constraints. A purely offshore digital EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised e-money institution) may not directly maintain a Canadian-dollar operating account or provide cross-border payments for a Quebec entity unless it is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, if operating in Quebec, holds a valid licence under the Quebec Money-Services Businesses Act (chapter S-6.01) or partners with a chartered bank. An unregistered offshore EMI is legally prohibited from providing its services to persons resident in Canada and can trigger both FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties and an inability to establish clearing through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). As a practical matter, using an EMI as the primary treasury rail is not advisable for a Quebec-domiciled aligner producer; the robust route is a domestically incorporated credit union, Schedule I bank, or a Quebec trust company that offers API-based banking and correspondent USD/CAD clearing.

Large intercompany shareholders-loan contribution routed from a foreign parent into a fresh Alberta/Quebec subsidiary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investment capital is best wired by SWIFT MT103 from a bank of the parent/holding company to the Quebec corporation's CAD-denominated account at a Schedule I bank. The wire must carry a remittance information field identifying the sender, the beneficiary as CA_CORP, and a clear purpose code such as 'Equity injection for subsidiary / shareholder loan — direct investment in Canada'. The receiving Canadian financial institution will perform a source-of-funds and purpose-of-payment analysis in line with PCMLTFA; the company should proactively provide the corporate resolution, the shareholder loan note, and the Registraire des entreprises certificate to avoid a regulatory hold. Cross-border wire values in excess of CAD 10,000 trigger the FINTRAC EFT report from the bank, and the corporation must keep a corresponding compliance file. No currency conversion approval is needed, but if the inbound wire is denominated in a non-CAD currency, the bank will use its own mark or a spot transaction to credit the account in CAD, with an FX confirmation document created for the corporate treasury records.For clearance of inventory/sheet materials from external suppliers and sales settlements, the company will use: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for trade invoices, especially for USD/CAD commercial payments to Korean/European sheet producers, (2) commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 by a Quebec branch, advised through beneficiary banks, for bulk multi-layer aligner sheet purchases, and (3) domestic clearing via the Canadian ACSS and interbank Large-Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD-denominated supplier disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: annually aggregated related-party transactions of this size or greater are expected to be supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation acceptable to the CRA under IC87-2R; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbC) applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000, requiring a Master File and Local File structure consistent with OECD guidance

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute comparable to zero-tax jurisdictions; economic substance is nevertheless enforced through the corporate residency test of central management and control, the general anti-avoidance rule (subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act), Quebec's artificial-transaction rule under the Taxation Act, treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions, and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) effective for taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2023, which denies interest deductions where the group does not evidence sufficient taxable presence and financing substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% under subsection 163.2; Quebec parallel fines and seizures under the Tax Administration Act; automatic reassessment and compound accruing interest

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and accountants are personally prosecuteable; the corporate veil is lifted on evidence of personal direction or fraud; courts may impose restitution, disgorgement of tax savings, asset freezing, travel bans, and extradition or Interpol red notices for cross-border profit concealment
Unremitted Payroll Source Deductions and GST/QST

Statutory sanction: The corporation is liable for 100% of unremitted amounts plus accrued interest; additional penalties apply of 10% of the unpaid source-deduction amount and 10% for QST discrepancies; CRA and Revenu Québec may seize bank accounts, garnish receivables, and register judgments against corporate assets

Director personal liability: Directors incur joint and several personal liability under subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and section 323 of the Excise Tax Act for all unremitted source deductions and GST/QST regardless of corporate solvency; the due-diligence defense is strictly construed and requires proactive supervision; personal travel bans, asset seizures, and denial of export clearances are available enforcement measures
Sham Arrangement / Shell Corporation / Artificial Transaction

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the sham doctrine to recharacterize the arrangement, disregard intercompany agreements, deny expense deductions, reassess profits to the Canadian entity, impose transfer pricing penalties of 10%, and apply the general anti-avoidance rule to disallow the full tax benefit; Quebec reassessment may be adjusted under anti-artificial transaction provisions; treaty benefits may be denied on appeal

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham transactions face personal tax liability for unpaid corporate taxes through section 160 transfer provisions, which reassess against the beneficiary; the corporate veil is pierced where the shell is a conduit; civil restitution orders, director travel restrictions, and criminal liability for conspiracy to defraud the revenue authority are enforceable against directing minds
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Subsection 247(3) of the Income Tax Act imposes a transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to use arm's length pricing; documentation deficiencies trigger immediate penalty exposure; EIFEL rules deny excessive interest deductions; CbC non-filing attracts severe daily administrative monetary penalties and ongoing audit penalties; interest compounds on all reassessed balances

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certifications or direct artificial intercompany pricing expose themselves to gross-negligence penalties and personal assessments for unpaid tax where section 160 applies to transferred assets; persistent cross-border price manipulation can support criminal tax evasion charges, economic sanctions, and director reprimands with travel restrictions
