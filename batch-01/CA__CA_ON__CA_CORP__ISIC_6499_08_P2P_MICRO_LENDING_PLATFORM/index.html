E-government filing portal

Non-resident or domestically incorporated Ontario business corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or using BRO. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Ontario Part III corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no pass-through treatment, no partnership look-through, and no check-the-box classification. The P2P micro-lending platform is treated as a resident corporation if incorporated in Canada (legal residence per common law) with unlimited liability of shareholders limited to equity; income from global lending operations is subject to Canadian tax with foreign tax credits available. Provincial territorial allocation follows Ontario's permanent establishment rules; Ontario's 2024 general corporate income tax rate is 10% (federal 15% general + 38% abatement reduction) subject to rates current at the filing period.

Available election forms

Check-the-box is unavailable in Canadian law. Key statutory elections: (i) S. 256(9) Income Tax Act (ITA) election for loss consolidation between related Canadian corporations; (ii) S. 256(12) ITA designation for associated-control group corporate attribution; (iii) S. 142.6(2) ITA mark-to-market election for financial institutions' securities pools; (iv) S. 216(4) ITA election for non-residents earning rental income; (v) GST/HST joint election under s. 273 ETA for nil consideration between closely related corporations; (vi) s. 141.02 ETA step-by-step recapture election and ITC proration methods under s. 141.01 for financial institutions with exempt financial services; (vii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under s. 240(2.1) ETA for non-resident persons making supplies in Canada; (viii) foreign affiliate surplus accounts and hybrid mismatch elections structured under the OECD MLI/EU ATAD framework only as contractual arrangements, not statutory check-the-box filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable revenue threshold (small-supplier limit) under s. 240 ETA; a person whose global taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 within a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive quarters must register. Non-resident platform providers making taxable supplies to Canadian recipients (e.g., sale of access to the automated underwriting software without 'arranging for' financial services exemption) are required to register for GST/HST even if they have no permanent establishment, under the simplified non-resident registration regime. A platform supplying only exempt financial services is NOT required to register, but may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits; proration restricts ITCs where exempt supplies exceed 10% of total supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under s. 24 ETA where physical possession transfers to a non-resident buyer and exportation occurs as soon as reasonably possible. Services and intellectual property exported to non-residents (software licensing, automated underwriting, data analytics) are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI ETA (s. 7 for services performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to non-residents not present in Canada; s. 10 for IP assignments), provided the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not made for consumption in Canada. Critically, financial services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under s. 23 ETA only where the supply is made exclusively for the non-resident's benefit, the non-resident is the recipient, and the service is not rendered in connection with a Canadian-resident's business. A domestic exempt financial service (interest income from Canadian borrowers) remains exempt; zero-rating applies only to separately invoiced export-oriented service fees, cross-border wallet settlement services, and offshore underwriting contracts. The platform must support eligibility with legal contracts identifying the non-resident debtor/borrower, Canadian complaint of non-resident status (Form GST/HST 109 or written declaration), and transactional documentation segregating exempt and zero-rated revenue streams.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic B13A filed via CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for regulated/high-value exports (≥ CAD 2,000), including the platform's BN/RM export account number, ECI (Export Control Identification Number), value, and HS classification; low-value shipments retain commercial invoice with B13A exemption noted
  • Bill of Lading — original ocean B/L, air waybill, truck manifest or courier waybill evidencing the transport, port of exit, consignee (non-resident borrower/platform counterparty), and matching exporter identity and commodity description
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or licensed payment-processor statement proving settlement of foreign currency proceeds into the Canadian corporation's bank account, with FX conversion record and traceable P2P loan disbursement schedule

Canada imposes NO real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice authorization mandate (unlike Mexico CFDI or Brazil NF-e). Mandatory invoice content is prescribed by s. 223 ETA (supplier BN, invoice date, HST amount, and prescribed information). E-invoicing is voluntary and standardized on the Peppol network through the Canadian Digital Service via GS1 Canada. Registered businesses must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE or GST/HST NETFILE) and remit electronically if above the CAD 1 million threshold; large businesses (taxable supplies over CAD 10 million) file monthly. Export clearance data is transmitted electronically to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); no post-audit digital stamping requirement applies, but all export records must be retained for six years under s. 286 ETA.

CRA conducts export substantiation audits including transaction-level review of transport documents, repayment flows, cross-border settlement chains, and reciprocity testing of non-resident status. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers reassessment at 13% HST plus: (i) s. 280 ETA compound interest on net tax; (ii) s. 285(1) ETA false-statement penalty equal to 25% of the net tax avoidable where knowingly made; (iii) s. 327.1 ETA gross-negligence penalty of CAD 250 plus 5%–10% of the debatable amount (25% for deliberate conduct); (iv) criminal prosecution under s. 327(2) ETA and s. 239 ITA for wilful evasion — imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 200% of the amount evaded; (v) CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties up to CAD 25,000 per false export declaration and seizure of proceeds; (vi) s. 296 ETA refund/credit clawback and recovery of all input tax credits previously allowed; (vii) s. 323(1) ETA director and officer personal liability for unremitted GST/HST, extending to shareholders who exercise de facto control of the P2P lending platform. Retrospective reassessment period extends 4 years for ordinary filings and indefinitely for fraud-based misrepresentations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is floating, freely convertible, CLS-eligible and a top-5 global payments currency. Canada abolished general exchange controls in the early 1950s and maintains a fully open capital account: no surrender or conversion mandates, no prior central-bank approval for current-account or capital-account transfers, no FPI/FDI currency licensing, and no restriction on dividend, interest, royalty, loan, or profit remittance by non-residents. The only practical gates are (1) Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; 15% non-treaty? Actually the Canada-US treaty: 15% portfolio, 5% direct with 10% ownership - I'll write: treaty reduced, e.g., 5% under the Canada-USA Convention on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a 10% US corporate shareholder; 25% if no treaty), (2) FINTRAC monitoring of large EFTs (CAD 10,000), (3) Investment Canada Act notification for new business establishment by a non-Canadian (s.11.1, 30 days), and (4) sectoral licensing - FSRA MBLAA/Consumer Protection Act/Payday Loans Act and OSC securities-registration analysis, plus RPAA registration with the Bank of Canada once the retail payments regime becomes operative. None of these impair fundamental convertibility or profit remittance; the CA-Corp P2P treasury therefore faces zero sovereign FX convertibility risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable - Canada imposes no statutory timeline, minimum holding period, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, cross-border loan repayments, or any inbound FX receipts. Exporters, lenders and PSPs may lawfully keep all proceeds in any currency in any OSFI-regulated account. Reporting obligations are informational/tax-driven only: FATCA/CRS account disclosures, Part XIII information returns (NR4/T5) and FINTRAC threshold reporting. No levy, surrender premium or export-retention quota exists under federal or Ontario law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - sovereign central bank and monetary authority; full legal name: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada. Canada has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration; the Bank of Canada manages the Exchange Fund Account (under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act) and unilaterally issues the Canadian dollar. Supporting competent authorities: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally incorporated deposit-taking institutions (Schedule I/II banks), Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), Payments Canada operates/oversees ACSS, Lynx and Interac rails, and Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions under SEMA/UN Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-provincial general tax rate (15% federal Part I general rate plus 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate). A reduced combined rate of 12.2% applies to Canadian-controlled private corporations on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (9% federal small business rate plus 3.2% Ontario small business rate). The P2P micro-lending platform must scrutinize the specified investment business rule: interest income derived principally from property cannot access the small business deduction unless the corporation employs more than five full-time employees throughout the year, a critical classification question for an algorithm-driven lender.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to all dividends paid or credited by a resident Canadian corporation to a non-resident recipient, including deemed dividends on shareholder distributions and redemptions, collected by the platform as withholding agent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 reporting is mandatory where reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate in the taxation year. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation satisfying the reasonable-efforts standard of subsection 247(4) should be maintained at any material transaction value; a complete OECD-aligned Local File is commercially and defensibly required where cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000, and a Master File is expected where the multinational group's worldwide revenue exceeds the BEPS Action 13 threshold of EUR 750,000,000. The Ontario P2P platform's related-party lending, algorithm-licensing and servicing-fee flows are high audit-priority categories for the Canada Revenue Agency.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime like the United Arab Emirates or Guernsey. Substance is instead enforced through: (i) the specified investment business definition in ITA subsection 125(7), which denies the small business deduction to lending corporations without five or more full-time employees; (ii) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245, applicable where the platform's structure lacks genuine commercial purpose and substance; (iii) the OECD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation and section 247 arm's-length standard; and (iv) the Ontario Financial Services Regulatory Authority licensing rules, which require a real head office, operating officers, capital, and systems for marketplace lenders. A foreign-managed platform whose board and central management operate from Ontario may also be deemed resident under the central-management-and-control test notwithstanding incorporation elsewhere.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — ITA subsection 239(1)

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years; on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 25,000 plus imprisonment up to six months. The Canada Revenue Agency may reassess beyond the normal statute-barred period under subsection 152(4).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, participate in or acquiesce in the evasion are co-conspirators and face criminal conviction, civil restitution for the tax evaded, court-imposed bench warrants, recognizance conditions restricting international travel, and disqualification from acting as directors of any Ontario corporation.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Tax — ITA sections 212, 215 and 227

Statutory sanction: The platform is assessed as withholding agent for the full unremitted amount under subsection 215(1), plus a 10% penalty under subsection 227(8), interest at the prescribed daily compounded rate under subsections 227(8.1) and (9), and potential gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding amounts under subsection 227.1(1); the due-diligence defence requires proof that each director exercised reasonable care and supervision over the treasury and withholding functions. Personal bankruptcy does not discharge this statutory liability.
Shell Corporation / Artificial Avoidance — GAAR section 245 and Ontario dissolution

Statutory sanction: The GAAR recharacterizes the offending transaction, cancels the tax benefit, imposes a 25% penalty under subsection 245(5) plus interest, and permits reassessment beyond the normal limitation period. Under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, the Director may dissolve a defunct or inoperative corporation without prejudice, and the federal regulator may strike the corporation from the register for persistent inactivity.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal assessments under section 160 for property transferred to non-arm's-length persons at less than fair market value, including the transfer of funds to the foreign shell. Courts have granted anti-stripping orders, asset freezes, and travel restraints where directors dissipate corporate assets to frustrate CRA tax collection.
Anti-Money-Laundering Non-Compliance — Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Ontario FSRA licensing

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation imposed by FINTRAC; criminal liability for laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code section 462.31 with imprisonment up to five years; Ontario FSRA may suspend or revoke the marketplace-lending licence, issue a cease-and-desist order, and freeze the platform's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly fail to maintain AML controls or who approve transactions through unregistered conduits are personally liable for FINTRAC penalties, are subject to prohibition orders under FSRA enforcement practice, and may face criminal money-laundering charges with attendant bail conditions restricting international travel.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement — ITA sections 247 and 163

Statutory sanction: The CRA substitutes an arm's-length price under subsection 247(2), imposing tax and interest, plus a transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the mispricing adjustment that exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000 and 10% of the reported transaction value. Negligent misstatement in the T106 attracts the section 163(2) 50% penalty.

Director personal liability: The chief financial officer and directors responsible for preparation of the T106 and transfer-pricing records face personal gross-negligence penalties where the documentation is knowingly defective; a deliberate fabricated transfer-pricing study elevates the matter to a criminal tax-evasion prosecution under subsection 239(1) with imprisonment exposure and travel bans.
