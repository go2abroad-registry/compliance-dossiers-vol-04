Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up or escrow capital is required for a Quebec limited partnership. The general partner(s) must declare their contributions; limited partners must contribute the amounts agreed in the partnership agreement. Non-resident persons may be limited partners and no Canadian residency condition is imposed.

Funding & unlock path: A fire safety certificate for the office occupancy must be obtained from the local fire prevention service under the Fire Safety Act. For any server rooms or raised computer floors, a structural load inspection certificate or written assurance from a Quebec-registered engineer is required to confirm the floor can support the installed equipment. A workplace occupational clearance must be issued by the municipal permit office and, where employees work, a CNESST prevention program must be established.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up or escrow capital is required for a Quebec limited partnership. The general partner(s) must declare their contributions; limited partners must contribute the amounts agreed in the partnership agreement. Non-resident persons may be limited partners and no Canadian residency condition is imposed.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, as the general partner of the Limited Partnership (the 'LP'), that: (1) the proposed partnership agreement and declaration be approved for registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises; (2) the registered office be fixed at the initial business address in Quebec, Province of Quebec, as stated in the declaration; (3) the general partner and each limited partner subscribe to the LP's interests according to the agreed capitalized contributions, which are recorded in the LP register; (4) the named representative be appointed as authorized manager for administrative, regulatory, taxation, and banking matters; and (5) this resolution remain in force until amended by a subsequent written resolution.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Revenu Québec — Quebec income tax and QST administration. Digital portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec Online Services (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec: (1) determine whether GST/HST registration is mandatory by testing the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold on taxable supplies made in Canada; (2) apply to CRA for a general Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or the My Business Account portal, submitting constituent partnership documents and non-resident status information; (3) activate the GST/HST program account under program account code RM (or the simplified non-resident GST/HST account for pure digital suppliers); (4) register with Revenu Québec for the QST account, obtain the Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ), and link the QST number to the BN; (5) use the BN and QST/NEQ as the sole statutory tax identifiers. No separate VAT ID is issued by Canada.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (income tax transparency under ITA s. 96; for GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' required to register, collect, and remit, while partners remain jointly and severally liable). — For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec: (1) determine whether GST/HST registration is mandatory by testing the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold on taxable supplies made in Canada; (2) apply to CRA for a general Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or the My Business Account portal, submitting constituent partnership documents and non-resident status information; (3) activate the GST/HST program account under program account code RM (or the simplified non-resident GST/HST account for pure digital suppliers); (4) register with Revenu Québec for the QST account, obtain the Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ), and link the QST number to the BN; (5) use the BN and QST/NEQ as the sole statutory tax identifiers. No separate VAT ID is issued by Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (income tax transparency under ITA s. 96; for GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' required to register, collect, and remit, while partners remain jointly and severally liable).

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian federal or Quebec tax law; the limited partnership's pass-through character is statutory. Available partnership-specific elections include: (i) a fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) for certain non-calendar-year partnership fiscal periods; (ii) ITA s. 98(3) election on a partnership termination to effect a deemed sale or continuation of Canadian property; (iii) elections under the Excise Tax Act for related registrants to self-assess on supplies between members (s. 156 election), and (iv) Quebec elections under the Taxation Act mirroring federal rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.4–18.6 are mandatory and no elective opt-out is available; Quebec adopts a provincial hybrid mismatch regime integrated with CRA administration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the small-supplier threshold for GST/HST registration applies to total taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding zero-rated supplies) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident digital-service suppliers of AI video-generation software, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to Canadian consumer/destination market revenue, triggering mandatory simplified non-resident GST/HST registration. QST registration threshold is aligned with GST/HST at CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Quebec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% GST/HST and 0% QST when the supplier exports them from Canada in accordance with the Excise Tax Act and QST regulations (shipment to an address outside Canada within a commercially reasonable time, with physical export documentation). Services such as AI video-generation SaaS qualify for zero-rating when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Canada, is not registered for GST/QST, and where the supply does not fall within exclusions such as services rendered to tangible personal property or real estate situated in Canada; the non-resident's Canadian use of the service must be incidental or the supply must meet the place-of-use rule.

  • CBSA Electronic Export Declaration (e-manifest/EDI export report or legacy Form B13A)
  • Commercial Invoice and Sales Contract clearly stating the 0-rated supply and service description
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Electronic Cargo Manifest proving shipment from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank FX settlement record showing repatriation to the Canadian partnership bank account

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, or digital invoice stamping is mandatory under Canadian GST/HST or Quebec QST. Tax invoices need only contain prescribed data (BN/QST registration number, date, nature of supply, tax rate, amount). Electronic invoices are accepted without pre-clearance. Quebec imposes mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST/QST returns and certain data reports for large businesses, but not a transactional clearance mandate.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit export zero-rating claims by matching export documents, transportation records, non-resident recipient identity, and FX inflows. Disallowed zero-rating results in retrospective reassessment of net GST/HST and QST, plus statutory interest. Civil penalties apply under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec tax legislation, including 5–50% of net tax for false statements and a 25% gross negligence penalty under the Income Tax Act for knowing misrepresentations; criminal exposure for willful evasion exists under ETA s. 354 and Criminal Code s. 380. The normal four-year reassessment limitation period is removed where fraud or misrepresentation is proven, permitting unlimited retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada maintains a free-floating Canadian dollar with no central-bank exchange licensing, no prior approval for remittances, no requirement to route payments through a designated bank, and no restriction on foreign-currency bank accounts. Profit-remittance and capital-flow hurdles are tax-driven only: the Quebec limited partnership must compute and remit Canadian income tax and provincial levy exposures attributable to non-resident partners under Income Tax Act s.135 and Part X III (for interest, royalties, or management-fee-type flows), and treaty resident participants may claim reduced rates only after delivering valid residency certificates (e.g., NR301 form in Quebec or IRS Form 6166 in the United States). Partners whose aggregate specified foreign property exceeds C$100,000 must file Form T1135. No currency convertibility licence or investment-registration condition applies to AIGC software exports or SaaS subscriptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender ratio, no mandatory conversion timeline, no statutory repatriation period, and no requirement to convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. The LP may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or any convertible currency and may retain export proceeds offshore or in foreign-currency accounts at its Canadian bank. Tax guardrails apply on realization of foreign-exchange gains and losses, and the partnership must maintain an FX translation ledger, support GST/QST input tax credits on foreign-currency acquisitions, and either compute taxable income in CAD under the CRA's functional-currency rules or elect to report in the partnership's functional currency where permitted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange-control policy formally resting with the Department of Finance Canada, prudential bank supervision under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border funds-flow compliance under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — legally viable as collection-level rails (Stripe, Paddle, Wise, Payoneer) but not acceptable as the primary treasury or capital-escrow bank. Canada has no separate 'EMI' licence class; non-bank financial technology providers operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA. An offshore digital EMI providing services to a Canadian LP must be FINTRAC-registered as an MSB, identify beneficial owners of the LP, and report international electronic fund transfers of C$10,000 or more. Quebec does not offer a provincial EMI charter; provincial consumer-protection and data-residency rules (Law 25) apply to platform functionality. Recommended split: use Stripe and Paddle for SaaS collection, allow an MSB for payout concentration, and preserve the Schedule I bank account for capital calls, tax remittances, ISDA-documented hedging, and Lynx/CHIPS clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all partner capital contributions as SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.009 after bank migration) wires to the LP's tier-1 Canadian bank, settling in CAD on the Lynx system and in USD via CHIPS/Fedwire through the bank's New York correspondent. Each wire must identify the LP by its exact registered legal name in Quebec, the account number, the funding partner as the ordering customer, and a purpose narrative 'LP Capital Contribution — investment in AIGC video synthesis software'. The receiving bank will request the source-of-funds pack: the LP agreement's capital-call clause, the contributing partner's recent account statement evidencing fiat origin, and a declaration by the general partner that no crypto-asset conversion occurred before the transfer. Verification is mandatory for every international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more under FINTRAC rules, so the LP should pre-submit its large-value wire questionnaire and beneficial-ownership documentation. (ii) Domestic Canadian supplier payments: Canadian EFT via the Real-Time Rail or Lynx for high-value same-day credits. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec provincial corporate income tax rate for general active business income (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident corporate partner, reduced to 5% under the Canada-US treaty and 15% under many other DTA treaties if the beneficial owner is a company that holds at least 10% of the voting stock; for limited partnership distributions, the characterization follows the underlying corporate partner status and withholding applies at the corporate level only when the partnership is a specified investment flow-through (SIFT) or when the distribution is from a Canadian-resident corporate payer

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 or more in gross revenue per fiscal year and transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons exceeding CAD 250,000 (gross) trigger mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation; transactions below CAD 250,000 are not exempt, they are only excluded from the formal documentation penalty regime

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Canadian federal and Quebec law do not apply the OECD-style ESR with prescriptive substance thresholds applicable in zero-tax jurisdictions; but general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec's statutory anti-avoidance rule (SAAR) require genuine business purpose and economic substance; for limited partnerships engaged in AI avatar video generation, the partnership must maintain local substance: physical office (Quebec), Canadian-resident employees, board meetings, management decision-making, and bank accounts; a partnership without substantive Canadian activities may be vulnerable to treaty shopping allegations and denial of DTA benefits under the OECD base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) framework

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Federal and Quebec)

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, sanction of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded as a civil penalty; criminal prosecution on indictment carries a fine of CAD 10,000 to CAD 200,000 (or 50% of evaded tax if greater) plus imprisonment up to 5 years; Quebec under Tax Administration Act sections 52 and 53: fine of 50% of hidden income and penal prosecution up to CAD 20,000 and imprisonment up to 18 months for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner of the limited partnership are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, GST/HST, payroll deductions, and penalties under subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, if they failed to exercise due diligence in ensuring remittances; civil restitution via fraudulent conveyance actions; travel bans under criminal court orders; imprisonment up to 5 years; directors cannot hide behind the corporate veil for tax evasion where they participated in the deception
SAAR/GAAR (Avoidance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallows the tax benefit and reassesses tax payable plus interest and penalties (up to 25%); Quebec SAAR applies the same denial; no criminal penalty if no fraudulent intent, but additional tax plus interest applies

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies if they signed tax returns knowing the avoidance transaction lacked substance; no imprisonment absent criminal intent, but asset freeze and personal damages under oppression remedies if undue tax advantage diverted partnership funds
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA treaty relief under the limitation-of-benefits clause and general substance-over-form doctrine; reassessment with full 25% withholding tax on all distributions plus interest and late-payment penalties; in Quebec, registration as a shell corporation may be cancelled; no separate tax statute specifically penalizes shell status but penalties for false certification apply

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally exposed for unpaid withholding taxes under Canadian excise tax and income tax statutes; may face personal criminal liability for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act if shell was used to mask beneficial ownership; travel bans and asset seizure possible
AML/Compliance Red Flag (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for each violation for corporations, up to CAD 500,000 for directors; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for knowingly contravening FINTRAC requirements

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable for AML penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the non-compliance; court may impose restitution, freezing of personal assets, and travel restrictions pending criminal investigation
Transfer Pricing Penalties

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 247(3) of the Income Tax Act, transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the amount of net adjustment on the understated income (or overstated loss) that exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million and 10% of gross revenue; also interest and possible criminal fines if fraudulent intent; Quebec penalty similar (10% of adjustment)

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for transfer pricing penalties only if they caused the partnership to be reckless in documentation; no direct statutory personal liability for the corporate partner's own tax, but if the partner is a foreign entity, the general partner's directors may be personally liable under Quebec civil code for negligence causing partnership damages
Failure to Withhold and Remit on Non-Resident Distributions

Statutory sanction: Withholding failure penalty under section 227 of the Income Tax Act: the withholding agent (the partnership or general partner) is liable for the full amount plus interest; penalty 10% of the amount not withheld, up to CAD 10,000; if knowingly fails, penalty of 20% plus imprisonment up to 5 years under tax evasion provisions

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner are personally liable for all withheld amounts and penalties under due diligence tests; they cannot shield behind the partnership veil because the Act imposes direct joint and several liability on the general partner's directors
