Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address situated in Quebec is accepted as the registered office where the address is a physical, occupiable commercial location able to receive service of process and official mail and where a registered agent is appointed. Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the 9-digit Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), filing the mandatory immatriculation declaration within 60 days of establishment. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts (RT = GST/HST, RC = non-resident/partnership, RP = payroll) using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, citing the foreign mailing address and non-resident partner status. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent as a default matter: the partnership is not a taxpayer; each limited or general partner is taxed on its distributive share of partnership income. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on Canadian-source income only where the partnership carries on business through a permanent establishment; captive reinsurance of Canadian risks is deemed to be carrying on business in Canada under subsection 253.1(1) of the Income Tax Act. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income while non-residents bear tax only on Canadian-source income. No tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election for federal tax purposes. However, under sections 599.1 et seq. of the Quebec Taxation Act, a qualified société en commandite may elect, jointly with its partners, to be deemed a corporation for Quebec tax purposes, resulting in entity-level taxation; such election may be relevant for a captive reinsurer seeking corporate tax treatment. Federal treatment generally follows the legal form, except where a Quebec-elected entity is recognized as a corporation under the federal definition. No hybrid-mismatch election is available: BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules in ITA section 18(4) and Quebec Taxation Act section 590.52 operate compulsorily to deny deductions on payments that are deductible in Canada and excluded in the foreign parent jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter, or in the immediately preceding four consecutive quarters, unless the supplies are all zero-rated. QST/TVQ: CAD 30,000 of taxable commercial activities in Quebec in a calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four quarters. Reinsurance premiums are exempt insurance/financial supplies, so a captive reinsurer is normally below the registration threshold; voluntary registration is available and is routinely used where the LP also makes zero-rated exported financial supplies to claim input tax recoveries on operating expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) exported tangible personal property where the goods are delivered outside Canada and the supplier retains proof of export; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident for consumption, use, or enjoyment entirely outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI Part V and Part VII; mirrored under the Quebec QST rules). For a captive reinsurer, premiums and additional reserves attributable to risks located outside Canada and ceded by non-resident cedents qualify as exported financial services eligible for 0% GST/HST and QST, provided the counterparty is non-resident and the risk situs is outside Canada. The zero-rate is claimed on the GST/HST and QST returns, permitting immediate input tax credit recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration, CAED) proving departure of the goods from Canadian territory; for services and reinsurance, the declaration of a foreign-risk financial supply is substituted by policy schedules and risk-situs certificates
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (and supporting carrier or freight-forwarder reports) evidencing the physical export movement of the insured goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — proof of receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident reinsured/cement in the original foreign currency (e.g., SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice) and full repatriation into the LP's Canadian or foreign settlement account

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing system is in force in Canada or Quebec for GST/HST/QST. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept conventional paper and PDF/XML invoices. Electronic filing and remittance of GST/HST/QST returns is mandatory for registered businesses above the prescribed filing thresholds, and large businesses must use GST/HST direct electronic filing. Quebec maintains the voluntary 'Facturier' structured electronic invoice standard for B2B exchange; a phased mandatory e-invoicing obligation has been announced but has not yet entered into force.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated export claim and claw back the refund within the normal four-year assessment limit, which becomes unlimited in the case of misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties include a 25% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 285(1) ETA, a 25% false-or-omitted-information penalty under subsection 280(1) ETA, arrears interest, and, for fraudulent export schemes (falsified bills of lading or fabricated risk-situs certificates), criminal prosecution under Criminal Code sections 380 to 382 with potential imprisonment. The 180-day FX repatriation deadline is not a statutory period in Canada but serves as an administrative documentary benchmark; failure to repatriate proceeds within that time undermines the export intention and may result in denial of the 0% rate, reassessment at the full 14.975% combined rate, and denial of the refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Profit remittances, capital injections, premium payments, claims settlements, and partnership distributions to non-residents are not subject to exchange control approval. The Investment Canada Act imposes notification/review thresholds for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, but ordinary captive reinsurance treasury flows are generally not within scope. A Quebec captive reinsurance LP must nonetheless address Quebec AMF licensing or exemption status and OSFI solvency custody expectations if it is classified as an insurance or reinsurance undertaking.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign currency receipts may be retained, converted, or repatriated without central bank permission. FINTRAC cross-border reporting obligations apply to physical currency/monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 and to electronic fund transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, but these are reporting requirements, not surrender or exchange restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary central bank; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential banking and federally-regulated insurance supervisor; Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) as Quebec insurance regulator. Canada has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration and does not operate an exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium for auxiliary payment processing and Low for primary treasury custody. Canada does not issue European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences; non-bank fintechs operate as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and in Quebec must also hold a money-services licence issued by the AMF where applicable. An offshore digital EMI lacks direct access to Canadian dollar clearing and cannot provide the deposit segregation required for captive reinsurance solvency. It may be used only as a top-up or claims-dispersal rail after the core banking relationship is established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections and intercompany reinsurance premiums from the non-resident parent should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire to a designated Canadian-dollar clearing account at a Schedule I bank in Quebec. The wire must carry a purpose code and remittance information identifying the payment as a capital contribution or reinsurance premium, reference the Quebec NEQ registration number and AMF/OSFI file number, and include the legal entity name of the captive LP. For transfers above CAD 10,000, the receiving bank must submit an Electronic Fund Transfer Report (EFTR) to FINTRAC. If the injected capital finances the acquisition of control of an existing Canadian reinsurance business, an Investment Canada Act notification or application may be separately required.Claims and loss-adjustment disbursements to insured affiliates and service providers are executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, supplemented by MT202 COV for correspondent bank cover. Domestic Canadian-dollar legs clear through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the modern Real-Time Rail (RTR) once available. For structured trade-related disbursements, commercial letters of credit may be opened by the captive in favour of third-party service providers, with presentation of DIs and bills of lading only where goods are actually moving in support of insured risks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Medium for auxiliary payment processing and Low for primary treasury custody. Canada does not issue European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences; non-bank fintechs operate as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and in Quebec must also hold a money-services licence issued by the AMF where applicable. An offshore digital EMI lacks direct access to Canadian dollar clearing and cannot provide the deposit segregation required for captive reinsurance solvency. It may be used only as a top-up or claims-dispersal rail after the core banking relationship is established.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec corporate income tax rate: 15% federal Part I rate after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction, plus 11.5% Quebec general corporate rate. Limited partnerships are fiscally transparent; this rate applies to corporate partners on their allocable share of Canadian-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. A limited partnership's allocation to a non-resident partner is not treated as a dividend and is not subject to Part XIII; it is taxed under Part I on the partner's Canadian-source net income. Actual cash distributions to non-resident partners that are not dividends are treated as returns of capital or previously taxed retained earnings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a single non-resident triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act s.247. Country-by-Country Reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least C$1,000,000,000; local file and master file benchmarks follow the OECD transfer-pricing guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule in s.245, the controlled foreign affiliate rules in s.91-95, and the doctrine of treaty abuse. A Quebec captive reinsurance limited partnership must maintain real decision-making functions in Quebec, hold licensed insurer status under the Quebec Act respecting insurers, maintain actuarial reserving and regulatory capital, employ qualified local directors and managers, and substantiate its assumption of actual insurance risk. Absent substance, the CRA may reallocate profits under transfer-pricing rules or disregard the partnership as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years. Indictment: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Revenu Québec may also impose a civil penalty equal to 50% of the amount evaded plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who direct or participate in the evasion are jointly liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions under s.227.1. Personal travel bans can be imposed in serious cases, and assets situated outside Canada may be subject to reciprocal tax enforcement.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty under s.247: 10% of the net adjustment to taxable income, with a minimum penalty of C$500, where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to set arm's-length terms. Late T106 penalties may reach C$2,500. CRA may adjust income, disallow reserve deductions, and impose interest on the resulting tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign knowingly inadequate transfer-pricing documentation can be named as participants in civil liability. Tax debts may be collected against director assets if the corporation was used as a conduit for suspicious premium movements.
Shell Company or Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule under s.245 may recharacterize the arrangement and deny the claimed treaty benefits or income allocation. The Quebec regulator may revoke the captive insurer's licence; contracts written by an unlicensed insurer may be declared void. Civil penalties up to 50% of understated tax apply for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced if the partnership is an agent or mere instrumentality of a single foreign parent. Directors may be responsible for damages to creditors and policyholders under Quebec civil law and the Business Corporations Act, including restitution of premiums written without authorization.
AML and Regulatory License Violation

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties may reach C$2,000,000 for corporate entities; provincial insurance regulators may impose fines, suspend the insurer's licence, and order cessation of underwriting. Insurance contracts entered into without proper authorization may be unenforceable.

Director personal liability: Directors of a reporting entity are under a statutory duty to establish and maintain a compliance program. Personal liability can arise for obstructing a FINTRAC examination or knowingly processing prohibited premium flows. In extreme cases, directors may face prosecution and imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
