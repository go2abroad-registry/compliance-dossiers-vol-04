Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration portal 'My Business Account' (canada.ca/cra-my-business-account); corporate income tax and GST/HST are administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial collection agreement with Ontario; Ontario corporate tax registration is integrated into the CRA Business Number (BN) system; incorporation registrations are filed with Corporations Canada (ISED) and the Ontario Business Registry.

E-government filing portal

A resident Ontario Business Corporation obtains its statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically at incorporation via Corporations Canada or the Ontario Business Registry; the CRA then activates required program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RN (non-resident withholding) and RA (import/export). Additional program accounts are opened through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. A non-resident foreign entity without Canadian incorporation must file Form RC1 with the CRA to obtain a BN, and must register for GST/HST when it carries on a commercial activity in Canada (including making taxable supplies in Canada) or when its taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada under ITA s. 253, including soliciting orders, is also required to file Canadian corporate income tax returns, and cannot use a U.S.-style disregarded pass-through vehicle to avoid Canadian entity-level taxation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canada does not recognize pass-through or check-the-box classification for corporations; a Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA Part I, subject to federal and Ontario corporate income tax on worldwide income if resident and on Canadian-source business income if non-resident; no territorial or tax-exempt-zone classification is available in Ontario for this commercial activity.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; the corporation is per se an entity-level CRA taxpayer. Relevant statutory elections for this structure include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely-related corporations to treat qualifying intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA s. 227 Quick Method election for registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (iii) the ITA s. 125 associated-corporation business-limit allocation agreement among associated Canadian-controlled private corporations; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 operate mandatorily — no election is available to preserve deduction/non-inclusion benefits in cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in the prior four consecutive calendar quarters, with worldwide taxable supplies counted for small-supplier status; GST/HST registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) goods physically exported from Canada under Schedule VI, Part I ETA, where the supplier exports the goods directly or through a commercial carrier and retains prescribed proof of export; and (ii) services and intellectual property supplied to a non-resident under Schedule VI, Part V s. 7 and related IP provisions, where the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the supply does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The anti-cheat engine licence, SaaS/middleware and integrity-audit services delivered to offshore gaming operators qualify as zero-rated exports where the software is not made available to a person inside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident; the supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the offshore recipient and the export character of the supply.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or automated Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier certification evidencing delivery outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice, licence or SaaS agreement, and scope-of-work evidencing a supply to a non-resident
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or merchant settlement report evidencing receipt of foreign or Canadian funds
  • CRA proof-of-export and record-retention file (ETA ss. 89.1 and 286 records, normally retained for 6 years)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing, or digital stamping as of 2025. Electronic invoices are acceptable under ETA s. 169 if they contain all prescribed information and records are retained. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/EDI) and remit electronically above threshold amounts. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing framework is adopting Peppol interoperability for public procurement, but private B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary. Cross-border exporters must transmit electronic export declarations through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (B13A / EDI Export Reporting) for controlled or commercial goods.

The CRA may reverse export zero-rating on audit when the exporter fails to produce the mandatory proof-of-export bundle, retroactively clawing back uncollected GST/HST plus statutory interest; the normal 4-year GST/HST reassessment window extends to 8 years where a waiver is filed, and no limitation period applies for fraudulent misrepresentation. Civil penalties include ETA s. 283 false-statement penalties (greater of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax at stake), ITA s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax, and criminal sanctions under ETA and ITA evasion provisions with fines between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment. Absence of corroborating FX receipt or repatriation documentation within the 180-day pipeline standard materially increases the risk of zero-rating denial and reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime with no capital or current account exchange controls. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject only to standard Canadian tax filing and FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required under Canadian law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts and converted at the prevailing market rate. All transactions remain subject to anti-money laundering reporting requirements to FINTRAC, with large cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 mandatorily reported.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability for offshore EMIs due to strict Canadian payment service provider regulations under the Payment Services Act. Cross-border digital EMIs are constrained by licensing requirements and AML obligations; local banking rails are preferred for compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 threshold: aggregate intercompany transactions with each non-arm’s length non-resident exceeding CAD 1,000,000 per year; Transfer Pricing Local File (RC4645): intercompany transactions exceeding CAD 5,000,000 per non-arm’s length non-resident in a taxation year; Master File (RC4644): MNE group consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000 (or EUR 750,000,000 equivalent)

Economic substance scope: Canada enforces the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, section 245 ITA) and the Supreme Court of Canada doctrines in Canada Trustco and Copthorne Holdings requiring genuine economic substance, durable business purpose and commercial rationality independent of tax effects. Ontario has no separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA will apply GAAR, sham doctrine, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument’s Principal Purpose Test to disregard arrangements lacking real functions, personnel, premises or risk in Canada; anti-hybrid provisions (section 18.2) and foreign affiliate rules remain active

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded under subsection 239(1) ITA; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2); unlimited reassessment period for fraud; criminal fines up to CAD 200,000 on conviction

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and employees who participated or acquiesced in the evasion face imprisonment up to 5 years (indictable) or 2 years (summary), restitution orders, and joint liability for corporate tax under subsection 227.1; the CRA may apply to court for a departure prohibition order (travel ban) under section 226
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; reassessment of 25% Part XIII WHT on the full royalty flow, plus interest and penalties; transfer pricing adjustment imposing foreign-based entity penalties

Director personal liability: Directors who approved distributions while Part XIII amounts were unremitted are personally liable for the remittance under subsection 227.1; a court order may impose restitution of the full dividend amount, travel restrictions, and seizure of personal assets for corporate debt
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation per corporation; separate penalties for failure to register, report suspicious transactions, or maintain records; dissemination of customer data to law enforcement upon non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are culpable for offences committed with their consent, knowledge or wilful blindness; imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines up to CAD 500,000; travel bans may be sought where the corporation is under money-laundering investigation
Withholding Tax Failures (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: The payer is assessable under subsection 215(1) for the full amount that should have been withheld; penalty under subsection 227(8) of 10% of the amount (or 20% where deliberately not deducted) plus daily compounding interest; penalty for failure to produce NR301/NR302 documentation up to CAD 25,000

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit non-deduction or non-remittance of Part XIII tax are personally liable under subsection 227.1 for the unremitted trust amounts; the crown is a preferred creditor in any insolvency; judicial travel bans and asset freezes issue where the unpaid amounts exceed CAD 25,000
