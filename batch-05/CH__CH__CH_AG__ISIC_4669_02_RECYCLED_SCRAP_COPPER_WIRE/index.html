Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), portal: www.estv.admin.ch; direct corporate income tax is administered jointly by the ESTV and the cantonal tax authorities (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung, e.g., Steuerverwaltung Kanton Zug) and the municipal tax offices; VAT is exclusively administered by the ESTV Main Division Value Added Tax (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer) through its e-VAT portal (eMWST). Customs export procedures are handled by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS / Oberzolldirektion BAZG).

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) obtains its statutory business identification number UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, format CHE-116.XXX.XXX) automatically upon entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the relevant cantonal registry. For Swiss VAT, separate registration with the ESTV is required: registration is mandatory when the net turnover from taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000 within a calendar year (Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act); voluntary registration is available below this threshold (Art. 11 VAT Act). The UID simultaneously constitutes the VAT number (shown with the suffix 'MWST' / 'TVA'), superseding the former separate MWST-Nr. A foreign entity without Swiss Commercial Register entry must apply to the ESTV for a UID and VAT registration, submitting a written application (formular 030e), power of attorney, board resolution, certified certificate of incorporation and excerpt from the foreign commercial register, proof of Swiss business address or Swiss representative, and a declaration of turnover. Direct tax registration follows automatically with the competent cantonal tax authority after commercial registration; within 60 days the tax administration issues a file number and provides the annual corporate tax return package.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the AG is a body corporate subject to unlimited Swiss corporate income tax (CIT) on its worldwide income at cantonal, communal and federal level, with two statutory territorial carve-outs: (i) permanent establishments situated abroad and (ii) foreign immovable property are exempt in Switzerland but taken into account for the progression factor (Art. 52 Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act, DBG). Core rates: federal CIT 8.5% on post-tax profit (approximately 7.83% effective on a pre-tax basis); combined cantonal/communal rates vary between approximately 11.9% in Zug and 21.0% in Geneva/Zurich for ordinary income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the AG is a body corporate subject to unlimited Swiss corporate income tax (CIT) on its worldwide income at cantonal, communal and federal level, with two statutory territorial carve-outs: (i) permanent establishments situated abroad and (ii) foreign immovable property are exempt in Switzerland but taken into account for the progression factor (Art. 52 Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act, DBG). Core rates: federal CIT 8.5% on post-tax profit (approximately 7.83% effective on a pre-tax basis); combined cantonal/communal rates vary between approximately 11.9% in Zug and 21.0% in Geneva/Zurich for ordinary income. No pass-through regime is available for an AG.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for an AG. Available statutory elections and reliefs: (1) VAT group taxation election (Art. 13 VAT Act) for Swiss group entities, treating the group as one taxable person; (2) corporate income tax group taxation (Steuergruppe, Art. 61-67 DBG) enabling profit and loss pooling among Swiss group members; (3) participation exemption election (Art. 69-70 DBG) for qualifying dividends and capital gains on participations held at minimum 10% or with market value of at least CHF 1 million; (4) cantonal notional interest deduction (NID) on qualifying equity capital; (5) tax ruling/advance tax ruling (Vorbescheid/Tax Ruling) with the competent cantonal tax authority and ESTV for certainty on transfer pricing, substance, and financing structures; (6) partial taxation of dividend income under Art. 18b DBG for substantial individual shareholders. Hybrid mismatch elections do not exist in Swiss federal law; abusive hybrid financing arrangements are subject to recharacterization under Swiss tax avoidance doctrine and the Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual net turnover derived from taxable supplies in Switzerland (excluding VAT and exempt supplies; Art. 10 VAT Act). For a foreign company, the threshold is measured from Swiss-sourced taxable supplies with a place of supply in Switzerland; once CHF 100,000 is exceeded in a calendar year, compulsory registration applies with effect from the following year. Below the threshold, voluntary registration is possible, conferring the right to claim input VAT deduction. For the ISIC 4669 'cross-border physical consignment of non-ferrous scrap' business, physical export consignments are outside the scope/zero-rated; only the Swiss portion of turnover (e.g., domestic handling, commissions, storage in Switzerland) counts against the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for the 0% zero rate under Art. 23 VAT Act when: (i) the goods are physically transported from Swiss customs territory to a foreign country by the supplier, the buyer, or a third party acting for either; (ii) the supply is made for consideration and the recipient obtains the power to dispose of the goods; (iii) the export is established in a credible manner by the exporter holding the prescribed documentary evidence. For the subject business of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire (HS 7404), the Swiss AG must evidence that the goods have irrevocably left Swiss customs territory; transit through the EU must be documented as a transit procedure (NCTS/ATLAS). Transport to Liechtenstein is subject to special rules (not automatically zero-ratable). Because the output is taxed at 0%, the exporter retains the right to deduct input VAT on purchases and obtains periodic refunds from the ESTV insofar as input VAT exceeds output VAT; this is the primary VAT recovery mechanism for the exporter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / NCTS export declaration) and validated exit certificate issued by FOCBS evidencing the goods' departure from Swiss customs territory
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) stating the exporter's UID/MWST-identification number, commodity description 'shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire', weight in tonnes, HS tariff code 7404, price and the express reference to zero-rating under Art. 23 VAT Act
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill or CMR/CIM transport document proving physical exit from Switzerland and the foreign destination
  • Contract or sales confirmation (order/purchase agreement) with the foreign counterparty defining delivery terms (Incoterms) and transfer of title/risk
  • Customs import declaration or other destination-country customs evidence (e.g., EU summary declaration ATLAS) corroborating arrival in the foreign customs territory
  • Bank or payment documents: SWIFT/TT confirmation, documentary letter of credit and compliant negotiation documents, or inward remittance certificate, evidencing receipt of the foreign consideration

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory government-controlled e-invoicing platform, no digital stamping and no SAF-T filing mandate (as of 2025). Electronic and PDF invoices are fully valid if they satisfy the substantive legal requirements of Art. 26 VAT Act (name and address of the supplier and recipient, itemized description, quantity, date of supply, consideration, applicable rate). Common Swiss e-invoicing formats accepted for VAT purposes include Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung), SIX eBill, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X and XML-based formats; paper invoices remain permissible. Tax-relevant records, including invoices, customs declarations, and export evidence, must be archived in a tamper-proof manner for 10 years (Art. 81 VAT Act and Art. 958f Swiss Code of Obligations).

The ESTV and the FOCBS conduct risk-based audits on export dealers in scrap metals (high-risk sector: precious and non-ferrous metals). If the export documentation is found to be false, incomplete or inconsistent, the zero-rating is retrospectively overturned and the unconditional tax is claimed at the standard rate (8.1%) plus arrears interest published periodically by the ESTV (approximately 4.5% per annum in the current notification cycle). Input VAT deductions are disallowed retroactively. Under the Swiss VAT Act, tax evasion (Art. 197) is punishable by a fine up to three times the tax evaded; intentional diminution of VAT revenue (Art. 196) carries a fine of CHF 1,000 to CHF 200,000 (or to CHF 800,000 under aggravating circumstances); negligent conduct attracts fines and administrative penalties. Presenting forged or false documents to customs constitutes evasion of customs duties under Art. 96 ff. of the Customs Act (ZG), with imprisonment up to two years or substantial fines. Violations may also expose directors to criminal liability under Swiss criminal law. Statute of limitation for recovery of VAT is generally five years, and up to ten years in case of evasion; clawback claims for incorrectly credited export refunds follow the same limitation periods. In addition, false export documentation triggers reputational risk with financing banks under Swiss anti-money laundering obligations (GwG), as scrap metal trading is considered a medium-risk sector requiring enhanced documentary verification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under the Swiss Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments and the Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely floating with no surrender, approval, or restriction on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of invested capital. No distinction is drawn between resident and non-resident accounts for conversion purposes. Foreign exchange transactions are not rationed and no prior authorization is required for cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Swiss law imposes no obligation to convert foreign currency proceeds into CHF, nor a timeline for repatriation of export earnings. Companies may retain funds in any freely convertible currency and settle invoicing in any agreed currency, provided the transaction is documented under Swiss commercial and banking regulations.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) in coordination with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking conduct and anti-money laundering supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Swiss law does not provide for European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing under FINMA. Entities from EEA/UK cannot passport an EMI authorization into Switzerland. Foreign digital EMIs may only provide payment services to Swiss clients through a supervised Swiss banking or FinTech licensee (Art. 1b Banking Act). For cross-border physical metals trading, a traditional Swiss bank account or a fully licensed Swiss FinTech institution is required; unlicensed offshore EMIs are not a viable alternative for holding corporate funds or conducting SWIFT transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans to the Swiss AG must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with clear remittance information. Recommended purpose codes: 'KAPITALEINLAGE' (capital contribution) or 'DARLEHEN' (shareholder loan) for capital injections; for ordinary trade advances, 'HANDELSGESCHAEFT' (trade transaction). The receiving Swiss bank will require the remitter to be identified on the company's approved payer list and will verify the economic substance of the transaction against the Commercial Register and the company's charter. No exchange control declaration is necessary. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal direct corporate income tax of 8.5% assessed on net profit after tax (effective 7.83% on pre-tax profit). The Canton of Zug imposes a combined cantonal/communal profit tax of approximately 11.9% on pre-tax profit for the ordinary trading company; the aggregate Swiss federal-plus-cantonal-plus-communal statutory burden for a Zug Aktiengesellschaft is thereforeapproximately 14.5% for tax year 2024.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax ('Verrechnungssteuer') on all distributions of profit by the AG, including cash dividends, constructive dividends, partial liquidations, and benefits-in-kind. The tax must be withheld from the gross distribution and remitted to the ESTV within 30 days of the value date; non-remittance creates a strict personal liability of the payment agent.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed within six months after the close of the fiscal year. For a company with a 31 December balance-sheet date, the filing deadline is 30 June of the following calendar year. The Zug tax authority routinely grants an extension until 31 January of the second following year upon a written reasoned request; unauthorized late filing does not suspend the accrual of default interest and penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The ESTV transfer-pricing documentation guidance requires a Local File where annual cross-border related-party turnover reaches CHF 8,000,000 or more, or where intra-group financing balances exceed CHF 8,000,000. A Swiss Master File is expected for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CHF 50,000,000 or when the foreign parent jurisdiction requires one. Below these thresholds, the Zug tax authority expects an arm's length statement and adequate contemporaneous evidence on the intercompany scrap procurement, processing, and sales margins.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute as found in certain offshore jurisdictions. However, economic substance is a core Swiss tax principle: the AG must demonstrate actual management and control in Switzerland, physical office premises, qualified staff, genuine decision-making, and real business risk absorption. A letterbox company is denied treaty benefits, withholding-tax refunds, and cantonal capital-tax reduction, and may be requalified as transparent for tax purposes. The OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test and the Swiss Federal Supreme Court jurisprudence on 'Scheingeschäfte' apply. The recycled scrap copper wire trading AG must therefore keep substance metrics: HR payroll, minutes of Swiss board meetings, warehouse/QC procedures, and transactional risk management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Federal and Cantonal Direct Tax)

Statutory sanction: Reassessed tax plus default interest at 3.5-4.5%; administrative fine up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); qualified tax fraud 'Steuerbetrug' (Art. 186 DBG) carries imprisonment up to three years; withdrawal of the commercial licence for repeated non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign an incomplete return are jointly and severally liable under Art. 754 Swiss Code of Obligations for resulting tax damage. Criminal conviction triggers restitution of evaded tax, confiscation of assets, travel restrictions and freezing orders (Art. 177 SchKG), disqualification from board office, and custodial sentences in severe fraud cases.
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax recharacterisation of the AG as tax-transparent with profit allocated to the foreign shareholder; denial of the 35% withholding-tax refund; 35% withholding on deemed constructive dividends; extra federal and cantonal tax plus 30-50% reassessment penalties; possible deregistration under Art. 913h CO.

Director personal liability: De facto and nominal directors cannot invoke the corporate veil: they are personally liable for unpaid taxes, social security contributions, and VAT under Art. 52/53 DBG and SchKG 52; travel bans and the obligation to open the company books are imposed in insolvency proceedings; criminal sanctions for aiding a sham entity under Art. 146 CP apply.
AML and Cash Transaction Non-Compliance (Scrap Commodity Sector)

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) exposes the trader to fines up to CHF 500,000 (Art. 14 GwG); money laundering under Art. 305bis CP carries imprisonment of one to five years; the entire cargo and trading proceeds are subject to confiscation under Art. 70 CP; VAT fraud under Art. 146 CP imposes separate custodial penalties.

Director personal liability: Managing directors approving unregistered cash settlements or omitting the UBO identification face criminal prosecution and personal confiscation; the AG's charter and insurance do not indemnify criminal fines; responsible officers may be banned from entering the EU/Schengen area while criminal proceedings are pending.
Federal Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Defaults

Statutory sanction: The debtor company is required to pay the unwithheld tax itself plus interest of 3.5-4.5%; fines up to three times the tax amount under Art. 70 VStG; the AG may be liquidated by the ESTV if the tax remains unpaid after seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as paying agents are personally liable for the 35% remittance irrespective of corporate fault; in bankruptcy, the responsible officer receives a travel ban and the obligation to reconcile the tax ledgers under SchKG 170; repeated deliberate omission triggers criminal tax prosecution for evasion of the withholding tax (Art. 65 VStG) with imprisonment up to two years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Full income adjustment with 3.5-4.5% interest; penalty of up to 50% of the reassessed tax under Art. 175 DBG; reclassification of the excessive payment as a constructive dividend triggering 35% WHT and refund denial; treaty relief under MAP is only available with compliant documentation.

Director personal liability: Signing officers who knowingly approve non-arm's length transfer prices are criminally liable for qualified tax fraud (Art. 186 DBG) and civilly liable under Art. 754 CO for the shortfall; in insolvency, the Swiss tax administration pursues directors and de facto officers for restitution; foreign travel may be prohibited while the criminal investigation is active.
