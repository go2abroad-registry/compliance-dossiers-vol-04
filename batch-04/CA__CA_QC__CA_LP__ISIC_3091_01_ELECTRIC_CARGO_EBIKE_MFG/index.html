Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Quebec), a limited partnership is a fiscal intermediary that computes income but is not itself a taxpayer; profits, losses, and tax attributes flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. For GST/HST/QST purposes, however, the partnership is a distinct 'person' registered in its own name and must charge, collect, and remit GST/QST independently. Non-resident partners are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding and Part XIII/Part XIV rules on allocations; the partnership must complete annual T5013 (federal) and RL-15 (Quebec) information returns. Territorial scope: partners are taxed on worldwide income, with Quebec-source allocation rules applying to resident and non-resident partners.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime. Available elections include: (a) partnership fiscal period election under section 249.1 of the ITA; (b) GST/HST closely related non-resident election (Form GST25) allowing nil-valued taxable supplies between closely related registrants; (c) ETA section 273 election for nil consideration among closely related parties; (d) QST equivalent election under the Act respecting the Québec sales tax and its regulations; (e) capital cost allowance allocation elections (ITA sections 13/20) made by the partnership and partners; (f) joint elections for non-arm's length transactions and section 216/217 elections for non-resident partners receiving rental or employment-type amounts; (g) documentation and self-assessment elections under the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.4–18.6, 100.1) to mitigate BEPS Action 2 outcomes where non-resident partners structure through hybrid entities. The LP must formalize all elections in writing and retain partner-level consents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (value of consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada/Quebec) over any consecutive four calendar quarters — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA section 240 and QSTA equivalent. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada that exceeds this threshold must register for GST/HST and QST, and becomes a registrant no later than the day the threshold is exceeded; no registration threshold applies to non-resident performers entering Canada or on certain permanent-establishment triggering activities. For the LP's export-oriented sales of electric cargo e-bikes, registration is recommended even below the threshold to claim ITC/ITR on production inputs; QST threshold mirrors the federal C$30,000 rule over four consecutive calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI: section 22 for goods exported by the supplier (including electric cargo e-bikes delivered to overseas buyers), and section 7 for services supplied to non-resident recipients who are not registered and not in Canada. QST zero-rating mirrors these rules under ARQST sections 129.1 et seq. The LP must satisfy four cumulative criteria: (1) the goods are delivered outside Canada by the supplier or exported 'as soon as practicable' after the last taxable supply (ETA s.22(1)); (2) the supplier retains contemporaneous 'evidence of export' acceptable to the CRA (customs export declaration, cargo control document, bill of lading, freight forwarder certificate, customs broker statement); (3) title and risk pass outside Canada (Incoterms EXW/FOB/CIF with non-resident purchaser); and (4) the sale is not a supply of property located in Canada at the time of supply to a registered recipient in Canada. The 0% rate applies at the time of export; the LP claims input tax credits/refunds for the GST/QST embedded in raw materials, batteries, components, and manufacturing overhead, creating an export refund position payable by CRA/Revenu Québec.

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance regime exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0), supported by the CRA and Revenu Québec through certified service providers; this framework enables machine-readable exchange of invoices but does not mandate government pre-approval. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns and QST returns where annual taxable supplies exceed C$1M, and Revenu Québec requires electronic remittance for large businesses. Record retention must follow specified electronic format requirements under the ETA and Quebec Tax Administration Act; the LP must keep export transaction records, sales ledgers, and ITC substantiation in a retrievable digital format for six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rating claims within four years of the taxation year, with an unlimited reassessment period where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Substantiated clawback consequences include: reversal of the 0% rate and reinstatement of GST/QST at 14.975% on the full export consideration; disallowance of corresponding ITC/ITR on manufacturing inputs; interest at CRA's prescribed rates (currently 9%–10% compounded daily) plus late-filing penalties of 5% plus 1% per month up to 12 months; a negligent false statement penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 25% of the understated tax; a gross negligence penalty equal to at least C$100 and up to 50% of the understated tax under ETA s.285.1 and QSTA equivalents. Criminal exposure arises under ETA s.327.2, ITA s.239, and Québec Tax Administration Act provisions: fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; customs offences under the Customs Act carry parallel penalties including seizure of goods or proceeds. The CRA and CBSA operate joint Export Verification and post-clearance audit programs targeting manufacturers claiming export zero-rating, with satellite data cross-checks on shipment weights, e-bike battery content (UN3480/UN3481 dangerous goods), and Canada Border Services Agency exit records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Article VIII. No central bank exchange controls restrict profit remittances, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-resident limited partners. Profit distributions from a Quebec LP to non-residents are subject only to standard Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25%, subject to treaty reduction) and must be reported via Form NR-4 or equivalent. All FX transactions settle freely in CAD or USD with no prior approval requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely, held in offshore accounts, or converted at any licensed financial intermediary. Reporting obligations apply solely under the Income Tax Act (foreign income inclusion) and FINTRAC for cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium with regulatory constraints. Canada permits Payment Service Providers (PSPs) to operate under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective 2024, requiring registration with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) may open CAD multi-currency accounts for a Canadian LP, but must be licensed under provincial money-services laws and comply with FINTRAC registration. Direct settlement in CAD via a Canadian clearing institution is enhanced if the EMI partners with a Schedule I bank. Full 'bank-grade' LC issuance is generally unavailable from EMIs, limiting their use to operational remittances and small-value supplier payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners inject capital via SWIFT MT103 wires to the LP's Canadian bank account in CAD or major convertible currencies. Wire must include a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Equity Capital' / 'Contribution to Partnership Capital') and reference the LP's tax identification number. For contributions above CAD 10,000, the receiving bank must file an FTFR (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) report. To avoid reclassification as a loan, the partnership agreement must evidence that the capital contribution is at-risk, subordinated, and not redeemable at the partner's unrestricted demand. A Canadian counsel-authored contribution side-letter should confirm non-debt characterization.Trade settlement for electric cargo e-bike component purchases shall use SWIFT MT103 for letters of credit payable at sight, or MT700 confirmed irrevocable documentary LCs when supplier financing is needed. Direct TT wires (MT103) are appropriate for prepayment below CAD 50,000 or with bank-issued advance-payment guarantees. For workflows requiring CAD/USD dual-currency settlement, banks will pre-agree a spot conversion rate; CAD-denominated payments from the LP account can be settled through the automated Clearing House (ACSS) on domestic legs. High-value concentrated payments above USD 1 million each should be routed through Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the global SWIFT gpi network.Outright FX Forwards (up to 12 months) with Schedule I Canadian banks for USD/CAD and EUR/CAD exposures, requiring ISDA Master Agreement or a clean FX addendumNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permitted over-the-counter with foreign counterparties but not offered by Canadian domestic banks; may be executed via a Cayman or UK dealer subject to OSFI prudential approval and Canadian ancillary-business rulesCross-Currency Swaps — permissible only for matched commercial debt or procurement obligations, requiring board-resolution approval and hedging-eligibility assessment under IFRS 9 hedge accounting rules

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium with regulatory constraints. Canada permits Payment Service Providers (PSPs) to operate under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) effective 2024, requiring registration with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) may open CAD multi-currency accounts for a Canadian LP, but must be licensed under provincial money-services laws and comply with FINTRAC registration. Direct settlement in CAD via a Canadian clearing institution is enhanced if the EMI partners with a Schedule I bank. Full 'bank-grade' LC issuance is generally unavailable from EMIs, limiting their use to operational remittances and small-value supplier payments.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Limited Partnership (LP) level because the entity is fiscally transparent (pass-through) for both Federal and Quebec income tax purposes; no entity-level corporate income tax is imposed on the LP. Instead, each partner pays tax on its allocable share of LP income: a corporate partner is subject to the combined Federal-Quebec general rate of 26.5% (Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%), reduced to 12.2% combined on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income if the corporate partner qualifies as a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) under the Small Business Deduction (Federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%); an individual partner is taxed at graduated rates up to a top combined marginal rate of 53.31%.
