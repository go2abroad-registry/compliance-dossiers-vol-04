Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST via My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html); Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) via clicSÉEQ Affaires (cliceqaffaires.revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation not resident in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number from CRA and a GST/HST program account before making taxable supplies in Canada, using CRA online registration or Form RC1. If the foreign corporation carries on business in Quebec, it must separately register with Revenu Québec for a Quebec Business Number (NEQ) and a QST account; registration is completed through Revenu Québec's online business portal and requires evidence of foreign corporate existence, Canadian address for service, and contact person or representative if no place of business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person; a resident corporation is subject to federal and Quebec income tax on worldwide income; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are subject to tax on Canadian-source income, with no general tax-exempt zone regime.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election for business corporations; incorporation creates a per se taxable entity. Available elections relevant to non-residents include the option under Income Tax Act section 216 to file a return for Canadian rental or timber income at a flat rate and section 217 for certain Canadian employment or scholarship income, with corresponding Quebec provisions. Hybrid mismatch adjustments apply automatically under ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6 and Quebec's Tax Administration Act, not by elective check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; Revenu Québec applies the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated for GST/HST/QST if the supplier exports the goods as part of the supply and retains contemporaneous documentary proof of physical export; unroasted green coffee beans physically exported from Canada are zero-rated. Offshore services are zero-rated when rendered to a non-resident and not performed in connection with Canadian real property or goods to be consumed in Canada. The exporter must demonstrate, through shipping and banking records, that the goods left Canada and the consideration was received from outside Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / B13A declaration, where required
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill, together with shipping instructions and carrier certificate of export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the green coffee beans, quantities, marks, numbers, and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/foreign-exchange evidence of receipt of funds from the foreign buyer
  • Exporter's internal zero-rating support worksheet evidencing the date of export and linkage to the invoice
  • Export permits, certificates of origin, or phytosanitary certificates if the destination jurisdiction or controlled-goods rules require them

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction controls exist at the federal or Quebec level for general distributions. Registered persons must issue invoices containing prescribed GST/HST and QST details, retain electronic records, and file returns electronically; Revenu Québec imposes sales-recording and certified-bookkeeping obligations for specified businesses above turnover thresholds, but universal e-invoicing clearance is not mandated.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated claim, deny the zero-rating benefit, and charge uncollected or ineligible GST/HST and QST together with interest from the date the return was filed. Penalties include failure-to-file and gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the net tax understated, as well as possible criminal prosecution and cancellation of registration for fraudulent export zero-rating claims. The exporter must retain export and foreign-currency evidence for at least six years and must demonstrate that the goods were actually exported and sold to an arm's-length non-resident; absent this, the invoice may be treated as taxable and the entire tax becomes collectible.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; there are no surrender requirements, exchange allocation queues, or central-bank FX approval for importers. Profit remittances by a Quebec business corporation are unrestricted at the exchange-control level, but a non-resident parent's dividend, interest, royalty, or management-fee remittance may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory rates, reduced by applicable tax treaty) and must satisfy Quebec corporate solvency requirements before declaring dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for exporting entities' hard-currency receipts; export proceeds may remain in any currency and no surrender obligation exists. Mandatory compliance is limited to CBSA export declarations (e.g., B13A process for controlled or specially listed goods) and FINTRAC's electronic funds transfer reporting threshold (CAD 10,000) plus financial sanctions screening under Global Affairs Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's central monetary authority but not a foreign exchange control administrator; FX/AML reporting is administered by FINTRAC, prudential bank conduct by OSFI, and trade documentation by the Canada Border Services Agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for stand-alone use by a Quebec-incorporated importer. An offshore digital EMI that accepts deposits or provides money transfer services in Canada must typically register with FINTRAC as a money services business and, if operating in Quebec, obtain a Quebec money-services business registration; an unregistered offshore EMI cannot access Canadian domestic clearing rails (Payment Canada ACSS/Lynx) and will be treated by Canadian banks as a non-account counterparty. Use only as a supplemental, pre-funded remittance channel with traceable SWIFT traces and full audit trails.
