Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 nominal; at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up contribution must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) held with a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank, and a bank confirmation of capital availability (Kapitaleinzahlungsbestätigung) must be submitted to the notary and the Commercial Register. Contributions in kind require an audit confirmatory report (Sachgründungsbericht).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the cantonal fire prevention authority (AICAA / Gebäudeversicherung): fire alarm and extinguisher systems, emergency exits, and storage separation distances per VKF/AEAI norms. Structural load inspection for any racking and cathode storage floors per SIA 260 and SIA 263 standards, with load-bearing capacity report. Occupational safety clearance under the Labour Act (ArG, SR 822.11) and SUVA guidelines: first-aid equipment, lifting/manual-handling protocols, operator instruction, and an internal occupational safety and health plan; employees working with heavy cathode bundles require edge protection and PPE compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 nominal; at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up contribution must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) held with a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank, and a bank confirmation of capital availability (Kapitaleinzahlungsbestätigung) must be submitted to the notary and the Commercial Register. Contributions in kind require an audit confirmatory report (Sachgründungsbericht).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Bundesgasse 32, 3003 Bern; e-filing portal: www.estv.admin.ch; UID register portal: www.uid.admin.ch. Cantonal and communal tax authorities assess corporate income and capital taxes jointly with the Confederation, while the ESTV has sole competence for VAT.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss-incorporated Aktiengesellschaft is automatically entered in the national UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) register upon Commercial Register enrollment; the issued UID concurrently functions as the VAT identification number. For VAT-specific activation, the AG or its Swiss attorney submits the ESTV Form 001 (registration sheet for taxable persons) with a current Commercial Register extract to the ESTV VAT Division; VAT registration becomes legally effective upon ESTV written confirmation. Registration is mandatory for Swiss-resident entities once worldwide turnover from taxable supplies exceeds CHF 100,000 per calendar year, and voluntary registration below the threshold is available under Art. 11 of the VAT Act. A foreign-owned trading AG must appoint a Swiss tax representative with domestic service address for VAT procedural purposes, but no statutory license is required for wholesale base-metals trading.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A Swiss AG is a separate legal taxpayer, subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability on worldwide profits with carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and immovable property; no pass-through or look-through treatment applies. Federal corporate income tax is levied at the effective statutory rate of 8.5% after federal surtax mechanics, plus cantonal/municipal profit tax and capital tax; profit allocation follows arm's length substance principles for commodity traders.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide check-the-box or pass-through classification elections. Relevant statutory elections for the AG: (i) VAT group taxation under Art. 62 VAT Act for closely connected entities; (ii) voluntary VAT registration below CHF 100,000 turnover under Art. 11 VAT Act; (iii) election of monthly VAT filing instead of quarterly filing; (iv) participation exemption relief for dividend income under Art. 70 of the Federal Direct Tax Act (not an elective regime); and (v) advance tax rulings with the cantonal tax administration confirming profit attribution, warehouse permanent establishment treatment, or margin recognition for offshore copper trades. Swiss domestic rules implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 neutralize hybrid mismatches without elective deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 para. 2 lit. a VAT Act). Swiss-resident AGs with turnover below CHF 100,000 are exempt unless they opt in under Art. 11. For supplies of goods physically located in Switzerland, including consignment stock of non-ferrous copper cathodes, the supplier must register once turnover exceeds the threshold; the import of copper cathodes into Swiss customs territory by the AG also triggers VAT at the border. Purely offshore back-to-back trades where the goods never enter Swiss territory are outside the Swiss VAT territorial scope and confer no Swiss creditable input tax on VAT-bearing costs unless tied to taxable Swiss supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act, the delivery of movable goods is zero-rated when the supplier or a commissioned carrier dispatches or transports the goods from Switzerland to a foreign destination and the buyer or a third party receives them abroad. This applies to Grade A copper cathodes physically shipped from a Swiss warehouse or Swiss customs-bonded depot, including sales consummated via LME warrant transfer with physical uplift in Switzerland. For goods that never enter Switzerland, the Swiss AG's margin from pure merchanting/transit trade is outside Swiss VAT; the trader must not issue a Swiss VAT invoice or claim export zero-rating for such flows. To preserve 0% VAT and the corresponding input VAT recovery on export-related Swiss costs, the AG must demonstrate the complete logistics chain, the transfer of title/risk abroad, and the receipt of foreign exchange consideration within the statutory substantiation window.

  • Customs Export Declaration – electronic export declaration through the Swiss customs processing system (e-dec / NADIM-ES) with the export accompanying document, evidencing physical departure of the copper cathodes from Swiss customs territory.
  • Bill of Lading – original or certified true copy of the ocean Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill naming the Swiss AG as consignor and the foreign counterparty as consignee, confirming the foreign discharge port or place of delivery.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – SWIFT MT103 or bank certificate evidencing settlement of the invoice by the non-resident buyer; the Swiss exporter must prove receipt of the foreign currency consideration as part of the substantive export proof chain.
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Swiss AG referencing Art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act, the 0% rate, and the Swiss UID, accompanied by packing list, insurance certificate, warehouse inventory movement report, and LME warrant handover documentation.

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no approved-invoice software mandate, no digital fiscal stamp, and no mandatory clearance API. VAT accounting is executed through periodic returns submitted on the official ESTV VAT portal (quarterly by default; semi-annual below CHF 5,000,000 turnover; monthly optional), integrating tax due and deductible input tax. The Swiss eBill standard, Peppol-compliant EDI flows, and scanned paper invoices are accepted as documentary records; invoices must comply with Art. 26 VAT Act (supplier UID, recipient, date, nature of supply, consideration, rate, and separately stated tax). Books and records must be retained for ten years, and the ESTV may conduct remote digital audits without prior notice.

The ESTV commodity-sector risk program subjects export zero-rating claims to full documentary verification; insufficient substantiation at audit triggers retrospective reclassification at 8.1% VAT plus default interest and denial of related input VAT deductions. Intentional VAT evasion is punishable by a criminal fine of up to four times the evaded tax (Art. 88 VAT Act) with forfeiture of deduction rights; negligent violations carry fines up to CHF 225,000 (Art. 87 VAT Act). Falsified Bills of Lading, phantom exports, and zero-rating claims on goods that never transited Switzerland constitute tax fraud before the Penal Tax Division, leading to damage- and penalty-based clawbacks. The ten-year statutory retention obligation (Art. 96 VAT Act) extends audit exposure; once the 180-day repatriation substantiation period lapses, the ESTV applies evidentiary weight in favor of taxable treatment, and late-produced documentation does not cure the assessment absent force majeure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Swiss franc and foreign currencies are freely exchangeable in the interbank and retail markets, with no restriction, approval, registration, or surrender requirement for capital injections, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, trade settlements, or portfolio flows. Profit remittance from a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is available without exchange-control approval, subject to Swiss corporate law solvency tests, statutory audit of annual financial statements, and a 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends, which may be reduced or refunded under an applicable double tax treaty. Inward and outward foreign direct investments, including share capital increases and shareholder loans, are not subject to SNB licensing. Residents are only required to report selected cross-border asset and liability positions to the SNB for statistical purposes when statutory reporting thresholds are met. Copper cathode trading is treated as a genuine current-account transaction and is freely convertible and transferable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A Swiss exporter may retain proceeds in any invoicing currency, may convert such proceeds at freely negotiated market rates, and may use them directly for trade disbursements. There is no mandatory surrender of foreign currency to the SNB or any other authority. The only relevant obligation is statistical: resident companies with material foreign receivables, payables, loans, or deposits must report quarterly or annually to the SNB balance-of-payments survey when the SNB notification thresholds are exceeded. Documentary export evidence is nevertheless expected by banks for AML and trade-finance due diligence, including commercial invoices, bills of lading, warehouse receipts, and customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) is the central bank and monetary authority. There is no dedicated foreign exchange administration or exchange-control authority in Switzerland. Foreign exchange statistics and balance-of-payments monitoring are managed by the SNB; market conduct and banking licensing are supervised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA); export controls and sanctions implementation are administered by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to FINMA licensing constraints. Switzerland does not recognize an EU e-money or payment-institution passport; a foreign EMI cannot operate autonomously in Switzerland without FINMA authorization. A non-Swiss fintech can offer cross-border clearing to the Swiss entity only through a licensed Swiss bank partner or by obtaining a Swiss fintech banking license or payment services license under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA). The practical route is to use a FINMA-licensed bank as the settlement anchor, with the fintech providing FX and payment-orchestration rails rather than holding client funds independently.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Swiss AG must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or SIC Instant Payment wires from the shareholder bank to the Swiss corporate account using the bank identifier code, the Swiss IBAN, and a clear remittance reference such as 'Kapitalerhöhung', 'Contribution to share capital', or 'Shareholder equity injection'. Switzerland imposes no exchange-control permit and no SNB approval for such flows. For a share capital increase, the notarized shareholders' resolution must be filed in the Swiss commercial register before or contemporaneously with the credited funds. SNB statistical reporting applies only when prescribed balance-of-payments thresholds are exceeded. The receiving Swiss bank must apply standard KYC and may request the corporate resolution, a UBO declaration, and source-of-wealth evidence. For shareholder loans, a documented intercompany loan agreement, arm's-length interest terms, and proper accounting treatment are required; equity must not be disguised as debt for thin-capitalization purposes.Clearing rails for trade settlements consist primarily of SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account payments, documentary letters of credit issued through SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707, standby letters of credit issued via MT760, and SWIFT gpi for end-to-end payment tracking. Swiss and European EUR/CHF settling parties can clear through SIC, euroSIC, or SEPA-equivalent channels. Banks also support trade payment undertakings and bank payment obligations in commodity trade finance.
