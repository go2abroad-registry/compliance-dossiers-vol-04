Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible in Quebec for a business corporation incorporated solely for aircraft engine dry leasing, provided the service includes a physical street address located within Quebec, a reception/mail handling capacity, and unrestricted access for service of process and regulatory correspondence. The registered office cannot be a post-office box or an exclusively electronic address. A commercial virtual office that provides a dedicated meeting room for directors' meetings, corporate records storage, and acceptance of registered process may satisfy the commercial substance requirements. However, if the operation develops a genuine fixed place of business in Quebec with employees or physical assets, a true physical lease will be required. For a pure leasing entity with no Québec-based employees, assets, or management activities, Quebec tax authorities and lenders may seek evidence that the registered office is not a mere shell address and that the directors exercise real oversight, which can be shown through corporate minute books, service contracts, and bank account mandates held at the virtual office location.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)/Agence du revenu du Canada, GST/HST; portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial: Revenu Québec, Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial corporate taxes; portal 'CléSécuriQ' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). Quebec-based registrants file combined GST/HST and QST returns with Revenu Québec under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a Business Corporation (federal or Quebec) engaging in aircraft-engine dry leasing: (1) incorporate federally or provincially and obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through the Register for a Business Number service; online issuance is immediate, mail/fax (Form RC1) takes 4–8 weeks. (2) Register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec within 60 days of beginning activity in Quebec to obtain a 10-digit NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) and the Quebec establishment file. (3) Open GST/HST program account (code 'RT') with CRA; because the business has a permanent establishment in Quebec, register for QST with Revenu Québec (single combined registration; Form LM-1 or CléSécuriQ). (4) Submit certified constitutive documents (articles/letters patent, by-laws, directors' list, registered office address in Quebec, power of attorney) and, if a non-resident director/signatory, a CRA Form RC59 and the statutory representation mandate. (5) A mandatory licence is not required for dry engine leasing (requires_statutory_license=false); however, the engine must be cleared through CBSA on import/export, and Export Control List (EPC) permits must be obtained if the engine/technical data is listed. (6) Obtain a Canadian banking account and, for ITC purposes, preserve the registration certificate and QST/GST numbers on all invoices.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free currency regime. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject only to standard tax reporting and anti-money laundering compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement for mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Canadian residents must report all foreign exchange transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 to the Bank of Canada under the Bank of Canada Act. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted to CAD at prevailing market rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
