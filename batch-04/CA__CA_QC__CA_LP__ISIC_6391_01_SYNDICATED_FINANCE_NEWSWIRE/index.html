Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), federal income tax and GST/HST — www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec, QST, provincial income tax, and administration of GST/HST for Quebec-established businesses — www.revenuquebec.ca; entity-level registration through Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for the Numéro d'entreprise du Québec (NEQ) — www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine nexus — a non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Quebec must register with REQ via the electronic declaration (form A-1) and obtain the NEQ, supported by a certified translation or notarized copy of the partnership agreement, proof of general partner residence, and a designated attorney for service in Quebec if no physical establishment. Step 2: After NEQ issuance, register for GST/HST and QST with Revenu Québec (form LM-101-V) through 'Mon dossier d'entreprise'; non-residents may alternatively file form RC1 with CRA under the federal GST/HST regime. Step 3: CRA will issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN); Revenu Québec assigns a QST registration number and a reporting code. Step 4: Open the partnership information return program account with suffix RP to support T5013/TP-600 filings, and add payroll (RP) and import/export (RM) accounts as needed. Step 5: Registration takes effect before the first taxable supply; the small supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (worldwide for residents; Canada-only for non-residents), and voluntary registration is available for non-residents to recover input tax credits. Electronic issuance of the NEQ occurs same-day; GST/HST/QST registration takes 1–3 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal and Quebec income tax purposes, the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; income, losses, and credits flow through to partners in proportion to their interests, and the LP files only information returns (T5013 federal / TP-600 Quebec). Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source business income attributable to the partnership (Income Tax Act s. 2(3) and s. 115; Quebec Taxation Act equivalent). For GST/HST/QST, the LP is a distinct 'person' and mandatory registrant separate from its partners, and must collect and remit tax on taxable supplies. Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed on Canadian-source income only. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable — no free-trade zone or tax holiday regime applies to this service business in Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime, but the following elections are available: (i) Income Tax Act subsection 102(1) — a publicly traded partnership may elect to be taxed as a corporation if its units are listed on a prescribed stock exchange and its gross income is predominantly from Canadian-source business; (ii) Excise Tax Act section 150 — GST/HST election to treat closely related corporations as a single person, relevant only where the corporate general partner and the LP are deemed related for inter-supplies; (iii) Excise Tax Act section 156 — quick method accounting for small suppliers (vulnerable to misapplication for export-heavy businesses); (iv) QST Act section 12.3 — corresponding Quebec election to section 150; (v) for US partners, the Canadian LP qualifies as an eligible entity for US check-the-box (IRS Form 8832), permitting classification as a partnership (default), corporation, or disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes; and (vi) anti-hybrid protective elections under Income Tax Act ss. 12.2(2.1)–(2.2) to neutralize BEPS Action 2 deduction/non-inclusion mismatches on outbound interest, royalties, and service payments from the LP to related foreign entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable supplies for resident registrants; taxable supplies made in Canada for non-residents; applies identically to GST/HST and QST under the small supplier exemption (Excise Tax Act s. 148; QST Act s. 407).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of financial newswire/API syndication services is zero-rated for GST/HST under Paragraph 9, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act, and for QST under the mirroring rule in Schedule VII, Section 9, QST Act, where the recipient is a non-resident and the service: (a) is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (b) is not a personal service; and (c) is not supplied to a non-resident who is registered for GST/HST in respect of that supply. Intangible digital data feeds also qualify for zero-rating under Paragraph 25, Schedule VI ('data') when supplied to a non-resident. The LP must contemporaneously establish the recipient's non-resident status and the off-shore destination of the service. Zero-rated supplies are reported at 0% on GST/HST and QST returns, allowing full recovery of input tax credits (ITCs) and Quebec input tax refunds (ITRs) attributable to the export activity. A non-resident recipient without a Canadian establishment is not liable for reverse-charge tax where the supply is received and used entirely outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / CAED) — required only if physical data storage media or hardware is exported; not applicable to pure API digital transmission
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — physical export only; for digital services substitute an authenticated electronic service delivery record (API access logs, server handoff dates, bandwidth metadata)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 or payment gateway confirmation evidencing the export consideration received from the non-resident subscriber
  • Executed Master Subscription Agreement — identifying the non-resident subscriber, destination territory, service scope, and licensing fee structure
  • Non-Resident Status Certificate — certificate of tax residency issued by the recipient's competent authority or a completed NR301 treaty self-declaration
  • Zero-Rating Support Invoice — issued within 30 days with a statement of export zero-rating, retained in the LP's books and records for the six-year retention period

No statutory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec for the financial newswire sector. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS) is voluntary and currently piloted by CRA and industry participants. Registrants must issue invoices within the periods set by Excise Tax Act ss. 223 and QST Act ss. 209 and retain invoices and books for six years. Revenu Québec mandates electronic return filing (EDI/XML) for GST/HST-QST returns of large businesses, but this is data transmission rather than clearance.

CRA and Revenu Québec actively audit zero-rated export claims, and the burden of proof rests on the LP (Excise Tax Act s. 296). If documentation is incomplete or the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Canada, the full GST/HST/QST becomes assessable with compound daily arrears interest at prescribed rates. Statutory penalties: (i) late or insufficient remittance penalty of 7%, or 10% on repeat occurrences (Excise Tax Act s. 280); (ii) gross negligence penalty up to 50% of the tax (Excise Tax Act s. 285(1); QST Act s. 1045); (iii) criminal prosecution for wilful evasion — fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years (Excise Tax Act s. 327; QST Act s. 1062); (iv) reassessment window of four years for ordinary errors, unlimited for fraud or misrepresentation; (v) direct reassessment of non-resident partners under the partnership attribution rules (Income Tax Act ss. 152(1.4)–(1.5)); and (vi) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s. 245) may deny artificial zero-rating structures, with a penalty of up to 25% of the tax benefit (s. 245(5)). Refund claims for over-remitted GST/HST/QST expire two years after the end of the claim period, and improperly claimed ITC/ITRs are clawed back with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no surrender, licensing, or prior-approval requirements for inbound equity contributions, profit remittances, subscription-fee repatriation, royalties, or intercompany service payments by a Quebec limited partnership. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely without central-bank rationing or dual-rate mechanisms. The only material frictions are fiscal: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on qualifying outbound payments (25% statutory, treaty-reducible under the Canada-US and Canada-EU treaties) and Quebec's provincial withholding and reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed its Exchange Control Act in 1951, and no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds, including cross-border newswire subscription and syndicated-finance content receipts. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency indefinitely in authorized foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks. Passive reporting obligations remain: Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys apply to cross-border transactions above C$0 in designated survey strata, and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reports (EFTRs) are mandatory for wire transfers of C$10,000 or more in a 24-hour window.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and currency authority, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as federal bank prudential regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the anti-money-laundering supervisor. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control commission; the Minister of Finance manages the Exchange Fund Account through the Bank of Canada, while Quebec's Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) supervises provincial co-operative banking (Desjardins).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium, national style: trend. In clip, only the magic rays wrap; they matter. In practice: Medium, ancillary-only. Non-Canadian digital EMIs such as Wise Platform, Payoneer, and Revolut Business are not Schedule I banks; they may operate in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) and cannot provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts or Lynx direct clearing. A Quebec LP may lawfully hold an EMI wallet for Stripe-style merchant settlement or small-value vendor payouts, but the EMI cannot serve as the capital-escrow or primary treasury rail; funds should be swept daily to a Schedule I bank account to avoid uninsured counterparty concentration and to preserve auditable trail for FINTRAC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident limited-partner capital injections must arrive as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers to the LP's CAD operational account at a Schedule I bank, with field 70 structured as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / APPORT DE CAPITAL', field 72 referencing the capital-call notice number, and the aggregate routed through SWIFT gpi for real-time traceability. USD contributions are cleared via the bank's New York correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) into a USD sub-ledger; the bank will automatically generate a FINTRAC EFTR when any 24-hour aggregate reaches C$10,000. Documentary verification is limited to the bank's KYC package, the Quebec REQ attestation, the executed capital-call notice, and apostilled partner resolutions, as no exchange-control approval is needed. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Limited Partnership level (pass-through entity under ITA s. 96 and Quebec Taxation Act s. 606.1); combined federal + Quebec headline general corporate rate applied by a corporate partner on allocated business income: 26.5% (15% federal general + 11.5% Quebec general); combined CCPC small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income: 12.2% (9% federal + 3.2% Quebec); individual partners taxed at progressive marginal rates on allocated income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Federal Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s. 212(2) applied to deemed dividends on distributions of Canadian-source income from a partnership to non-resident partners; distributions to resident partners and to a resident corporate partner are exempt from Part XIII (inter-corporate dividend exemption under ITA s. 112); Quebec does not impose a separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value of reportable cross-border non-arm's length transactions triggers the mandatory filing of CRA Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents); Country-by-Country reporting (Form RC4649) applies where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CAD 750 million equivalent; no separate Quebec transfer-pricing return exists, but Revenu Quebec may request the T106, Local File, and Master File on audit; contemporaneous documentation (OECD TP documentation package) is mandatory regardless of transaction size where tax planning relies on transfer prices.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation code comparable to the UAE ESR; instead, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the 2024 codified economic-substance recharacterization provisions, and the Quebec GAAR (Taxation Act s. 1079.1.2). A Quebec LP engaged in syndicated finance newswire must demonstrate real operational substance in Quebec: qualified employees, actual decision-making, independent data infrastructure, and genuine revenue contracts; a nominal LP with a shell corporate general partner used to route treaty-favored payments to non-resident partners is rebuttable as a conduit and subject to full reassessment, treaty denial, and GAAR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Part XIII Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Federal: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 2 years (ITA s. 239); gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated amount (ITA s. 163(2)); daily compound interest on unpaid amounts; CRA asset freeze and seizure powers (ITA s. 226.1). Quebec: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 18 months (TaA s. 56); in addition, Revenu Quebec may revoke licences and issue certificate judgments for seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings and director penalties under ITA s. 227.1 (including interest and penalties) and are jointly and severally liable with Revenu Quebec under the TaA; civil restitution orders may be granted; criminal convictions trigger travel bans and passport surrender; foreign directors are subject to arrest warrants and extradition in egregious kickback or concealment schemes.
Recurring Failure to File Information Returns

Statutory sanction: CRA penalties under ITA s. 162: CAD 25 per day (minimum CAD 100; maximum CAD 2,500 per return); slip penalties increasing to CAD 250 per slip; CRA may issue an information demand, then prosecute the failure as an offence (maximum CAD 25,000 fine and 12 months' imprisonment); Revenu Quebec imposes parallel penalties under TaA ss. 56.1-58 and may expose the LP's tax attributes to a complete denial of loss deductions.

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors are personally assessed when penalties remain unpaid after the GP is dissolved or insolvent; personal fines and imprisonment apply to the controlling mind responsible for the omissions; CRA can obtain a court order prohibiting the director from leaving Canada and can register director liability certificates against personal real and moveable property.
Shell Company / GAAR Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under ITA s. 245 and the 2024 economic-substance recharacterization rule with a 50% penalty applied to the tax benefit; denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; retroactive disallowance of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustments up to 10 years, Part XII.2 tax on designated investment income; Quebec GAAR reassessment with interest compounding daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the artificial structure face civil restitution claims from the LP's creditors and the tax authorities; courts will pierce the veil where the LP is a mere agent or sham; directors may be assessed for the full tax benefit personally; in criminal GAAR-fraud cases, imprisonment is available and travel prohibitions are imposed during investigation.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalties under the PCMLTFA up to CAD 2,000,000 for a corporate entity per violation; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money laundering is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC compliance agreement and public naming; Quebec's AMF may impose additional regulatory fines and revoke registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners act as 'responsible persons' and are personally prosecutable for money laundering offences; criminal forfeiture of personal assets; courts may issue travel bans and take passports where flight risk is established; director bans from managing any registered Canadian enterprise for up to 5 years after a conviction; Quebec civil liability is extended to a GP's directors for fines the LP cannot pay.
