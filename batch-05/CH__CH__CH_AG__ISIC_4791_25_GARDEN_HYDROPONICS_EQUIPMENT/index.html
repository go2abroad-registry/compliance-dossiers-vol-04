Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (kantonales Handelsregisteramt) in the registered office canton, e.g., Handelsregisteramt Zürich; central publication through Zefix (Central Business Name Index) and the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC/SHAB).

(contents of articles of association), Art. 629 (election of board), Art. 640 (paid-up capital); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV) Art. 38 et seq.; Federal Act on Occupational Pensions (minimum mandatory employee benefits, applied upon first hire).

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of Incorporation certified by a Swiss notary public (öffentliche Beurkundung), accompanied by the Articles of Association (Statuten) and the application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Handelsregistereintragung).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 44 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital for an Aktiengesellschaft is CHF 100,000; at least 20% of each nominal share (minimum aggregate CHF 50,000) must be paid up before registration. Paid-up funds must be deposited into a Swiss bank or PostFinance account with a blocking note in favour of the company until registration, and the bank must issue a capital payment confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung). The funds become freely disposable only after the Commercial Register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance for the rented office/warehouse is confirmed by the local fire inspection authority (Gebäudeversicherung / Feuerpolizei) through a land-use and occupancy confirmation. Structural load inspections are not required for non-industrial commercial units. Workplace hygiene and occupational safety for handling of packaging and computer environments follow the Swiss Labour Act (Arbeitsgesetz) and SUVA directives for office workplaces; electrical equipment must bear CE markings and satisfy the Swiss Technical Inspection Association (Swiss TS) conformity requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for an Aktiengesellschaft is CHF 100,000; at least 20% of each nominal share (minimum aggregate CHF 50,000) must be paid up before registration. Paid-up funds must be deposited into a Swiss bank or PostFinance account with a blocking note in favour of the company until registration, and the bank must issue a capital payment confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung). The funds become freely disposable only after the Commercial Register entry.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
Resolved by the Board of Directors on this day: (1) The issued share capital of CHF 100,000 is accepted, and the founders' subscriptions identified in the deed of incorporation are ratified; the required CHF 50,000 paid-up contribution has been credited to a blocked Swiss bank account and the bank confirmation is approved. (2) The registered office is adopted at the address stated in the Articles of Association, and management authority is granted to the listed corporate officers for all ordinary administrative acts. (3) The Managing Director and the Chairperson of the Board are appointed as authorised officers with sole or collective signature. All filings are delegated to the notary.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not required for pure e-commerce distribution; if warehousing or packing is carried out, the relevant authority is the Cantonal Trade Office (Kantonales Gewerbeamt) and the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) for import of equipment. No factory or industrial manufacturing permit is necessary.

Category: No statutory operating license (Bewilligung) is required for online retail of hydroponic gardening equipment under Swiss law, provided the business is carried out as a registered commercial enterprise and complies with product safety and consumer information rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland applies full current and capital account convertibility under the Swiss Federal Constitution and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments. Profits, dividends, royalties, and capital proceeds are freely transferable without exchange-control approval. The Swiss franc is a freely floating, fully convertible hard currency with no investor registration, repatriation licensing, or residual non-resident restrictions. Commercial banks execute cross-border payments subject to Swiss anti-money-laundering and economic-sanctions screening only; there are no convertibility barriers or profit-remittance taxes beyond ordinary withholding taxes where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, obligatory conversion ratio, or mandated repatriation timeline for export receipts in Switzerland. Swiss exporters retain full discretion over currency denomination, timing, and holding of foreign currency. SNB reporting obligations apply only to banks for statistical balance-of-payments purposes on cross-border payment transactions (threshold-based, not approval-based); enterprises are not required to convert export proceeds into CHF or to hold them within Switzerland.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse, exercising monetary and statistical oversight; the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF) supervise financial market policy but Switzerland maintains no discretionary foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Swiss law does not recognize passported EU e-money institutions (EMIs) as banks; a non-bank EMI may execute payments solely as a payment service provider under the Swiss Payment Services Act (PSA), but such providers require FINMA licensing or a licensing-exemption structure and cannot hold client deposits in the same manner as a bank. Offshore digital EMIs without a Swiss license may operate only via commercial agency arrangements with a Swiss licensed financial intermediary for Swiss-sourced payments; however, their viability for long-term treasury clearing and capital escrow is low due to heightened AML scrutiny on virtual-currency farms and non-bank fintech platforms. A Swiss AG is therefore advised to use a regulated Tier-1 bank as the primary liquidity account and to use EMIs solely as downstream collection channels for low-value online card payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Swiss AG’s operational or capital escrow account must comply with the Swiss Code of Obligations (share capital deposit requirements) and bank-internal AML circulars. Wires should be executed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT with full Originator and Beneficiary information. Swiss banks do not require a proprietary 'purpose code', but for statistical and clearing purposes you must include the commercial reference (e.g., 'Share Capital Subscription', 'Convertible Loan', 'Trade Receivable Payment') and remit using the Beneficiary's IBAN (CH + national clearings via SIC). Verification at the receiving bank will demand the underlying capital subscription documentation and a completed Form A (disclosure of beneficial owner) if not already held. The recommended compliant routing is: overseas payer bank – correspondent Swiss bank (typically UBS/ZKB) – SIC/PostFinance clearing – Swiss AG’s IBAN. All funds must settle in CHF or the nominated foreign-currency account and be booked under the correct economic purpose account to avoid automatic blockage by money-laundering filters.For trade disbursements, the Swiss AG uses standard clearing methods: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent-bank transfers, with mandatory structured remittance information (e.g., invoice number, ISIC order reference); (ii) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for bank-to-bank interbank settlements underlying an MT103; (iii) commercial letters of credit opened via MT700 with the confirming bank, ensuring documentary compliance under UCP600; (iv) SEPA credit transfers for EEA-based creditors, with full EUR transfer via STEP2/EACHA inter-bank infrastructure. For online retail payments incoming from Stripe/PayPal, treasury settlements are made by these PSPs to the Swiss bank account via domestic ACH or international SWIFT; outbound supplier payments above EUR 10,000 should be sent by MT103 rather than card to preserve audit trails and avoid merchant-of-record liability.
