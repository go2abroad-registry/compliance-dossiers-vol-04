For an Aktiengesellschaft engaged in wholesale brokerage of SSDs and server processors (ISIC 4651), a pure postal-only virtual office with mail forwarding and no physical access is NOT permissible as the sole registered seat. E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply for a Swiss VAT number (MWST/TVA) via the FTA’s online VAT registration service. Requirements include proof of legal existence (e.g., extract from commercial register or equivalent foreign registration), a Swiss business address, and a declaration of actual or anticipated Swiss taxable turnover. An Aktiengesellschaft (AG) receives a nine-digit UID plus MWST/TVA suffix upon successful registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss tax law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; all Aktiengesellschaft (AG) entities are mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Available elections are limited to VAT grouping under Art. 17 of the Swiss VAT Act (eligible related entities may file as a single taxable person) and specific hybrid mismatch elections implemented to align with BEPS Action 2, such as the election to apply the transparency rule for in-scope hybrid instruments in accordance with the Swiss Dispossession Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual taxable turnover from supplies in Switzerland (Art. 10 VATA), inclusive of reductions; small businesses under this threshold are exempt but may opt for and remain liable to VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of physical goods (including SSD and CPU chips) qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the supplier is the exporter or the goods are exported by a third party on the supplier’s behalf, and the goods are physically removed from the Swiss customs territory to a foreign destination. The recipient must be located outside Switzerland. For offshore services that are directly related to exported goods, zero-rating may apply provided the service is supplied to an overseas recipient and the connection to the exported goods is established.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec)
  • International Transport Document (Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Foreign Customs Entry (if available)

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous digital stamping is required under Swiss VAT law. However, all invoices (electronic or paper) must satisfy Art. 37 VATA content requirements. Export transactions require an electronic export declaration through the Swiss Customs Administration’s 'e-dec' system, which transmits customs data in near-real-time. Approved Exporter status permits simplified customs procedures but does not eliminate the obligation to file electronic customs declarations.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) retains the right to reassess zero-rated supplies and retroactively claw back the corresponding input VAT deduction if export evidence is insufficient or fraudulent. The standard civil penalty for VAT evasion equals the evaded tax amount (Art. 66 VATA); aggravated cases are subject to criminal prosecution under Art. 72 VATA and the Swiss Penal Code, including custodial sentences, fines, and an extended five-year limitation period for reassessment. FTA audits scrutinise export documentation integrity, transport contracts, and payment cycles to detect fictitious exports or circular trading.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the broad freedom of the Code of Obligations and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA). No general approval is required for foreign exchange operations, profit remittances, or cross-border capital flows. However, Swiss-domiciled entities must comply with balance-of-payments reporting to the SNB, and inbound/outbound transfers exceeding CHF 1 million are subject to monthly statistical disclosure. Dividends and profit repatriations are not subjected to exchange controls, but may trigger Swiss withholding tax at a statutory rate of 35%, with relief under applicable double taxation treaties. The Swiss franc is freely floating, and the SNB does not impose convertibility restrictions, surrender rules, or minimum holding periods, preserving high liquidity and capital mobility for the CH_AG wholesale semiconductor trading entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in Switzerland. An entity is not required to surrender hard-currency export proceeds to the SNB, nor is there any obligation to convert export income into Swiss francs. Receipts from global SSD and CPU chip wholesale settlement may be held in foreign currency or repatriated freely. The only compliance duty is the SNB's Balance of Payments statistics reporting, which applies to each payment transaction individually or cumulatively exceeding CHF 1 million per month, requiring submission of the payment code and counterpart information in accordance with Annex 4 of the SNB reporting directives.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) acting as the central bank, and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) overseeing financial market policy and cross-border capital movement reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Unregulated offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully accept Swiss client deposits because deposit-taking requires a banking license under the Swiss Banking Act. A non-bank fintech may only act as a payment initiator or technical service provider if fully segregated from funds, and must hold a FinTech license from FINMA if obtaining deposits above CHF 1 million only for short periods, or operate under the Swiss Payment Services Act if not taking deposits. For cross-border settlement of semiconductor commodity trades, the only reliable payment service is a supervised licensed EMI or bank in Switzerland, or a European EMI passporting into Switzerland under the cross-border provision, but account opening with such EMIs is not equivalent to a domestic bank account for escrow or trade-dispute purposes. Therefore, for the target wholesale SSD/CPU chip trading entity, the recommended approach is a domestic Tier-1 bank account for all escrow-related transactions and a secondary licensed EMI for minor disbursements, subject to full KYC remittance checks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from parent shareholders in Switzerland or foreign jurisdictions are performed via SWIFT MT103, using the Swiss purpose code 'D.III.1 – Capital contribution' (Einlage) in accordance with SNB Balance-of-Payments reporting. The bank will require an updated excerpt from the Commercial Register confirming the paid-in capital, a board resolution authorising the capital increase, and for injections originating from non-OECD jurisdictions, the bank requires a source-of-funds statement and a legal disclosure of the formal transfer trail to major shareholders. Transaction monitoring applies to the amount versus the bank-registered authorised share capital; for security-relevant goods trading, the capital account will be segregated into an escrow sub-ledger. All inbound capital flows should be booked with a clear payment reference quoting the share registry ID and the SNB payment code 'PL 103' if annual total exceeds CHF 1 million.Trade settlements for SSD/CPU purchases and sales are executed predominantly over the SWIFT MT103 standard for direct telegraphic transfers and via SWIFT MT700 for documentary Letters of Credit covering consignment shipments. The treasury blueprint recommends: (1) domestic Taiwanese or Korean suppliers and customers concerned with Open Account terms mitigate the transaction by using a SWIFT MT103 with a matching inspect-and-pay condition embedded in the bank's collection service, (2) high-value shipments above CHF 500,000 are arranged under an Irrevocable Unconfirmed Commercial LC (sight) with a Swiss bank as issuing/advising bank, ensuring title transfer and compliance with the Incoterms 2020 shipment terms (FCA / CIF). Settlement currency matching is recommended to be USD for global memory/chip trades and CHF or EUR for European counterparties, but SWIFT supports any freely convertible currency. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

The statutory federal headline rate is 8.5% of taxable profit after federal corporate income tax. Cantonal and communal profit taxes vary by jurisdiction; for the Zug benchmark, the combined federal, cantonal, and communal effective ordinary rate is approximately 14.5% to 15.0% for a trading company.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Swiss withholding tax on dividends is 35% of the gross distribution, levied on ordinary, bonus, and constructive dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate tax return is due within six months after the close of the financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the deadline is 30 June of the following year. Extension requests may be granted by the competent cantonal authority, typically up to an additional three to six months, provided the request is timely filed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File) is required for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding CHF 500,000 in intercompany turnover or where the Swiss entity's annual turnover exceeds CHF 10 million. For a high-volume wholesale SSD/CPU trading entity, these thresholds are generally exceeded; therefore, contemporaneous TP documentation must be maintained and filed upon request. A Swiss Taxpayer Accuracy Spec sheet (Annex TP-4) may be required by the Zug tax administration.

Economic substance scope: Swiss law does not apply offshore ESR regimes, but domestic substance requirements derive from the legal-economic approach: a corporation must have independent substance to be treated as the taxpayer for the assets and income it reports. The company must have an actual seat and place of management, bank accounts, qualified personnel, administrative facilities, books and records, minutes of shareholder and board meetings held in Switzerland, and actual decision-making in Switzerland. For a Zug AG engaged in wholesale SSD/CPU trading, substance should cover: local directors, a physical office, sufficient commercial activity, bookkeeping, foreign bank accounts, and negotiation/logistics roles. If substance is deficient, the tax authority may reclassify the entity as a mere agent or conduit, attribute income to the effective entrepreneur, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (intentional under-declaration)

Statutory sanction: Art. 174 DBG: fine of 40% to 100% of the evaded tax. Art. 175 DBG: tax fraud by using false documents or falsified accounts carries a fine of up to 2.5 times the evaded tax, and if committed through criminal means, imprisonment under Art. 146 StGB up to 5 years. The tax re-assessment is payable with interest at 3% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately file false returns may be personally criminally liable. They face a fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Under civil law, they owe the company restitution for the tax loss under Art. 754 CO if their conduct was unlawful and blameworthy. In severe cases, travel bans and extradition may apply.
Sham / Shell company without economic substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority disregards the substanceless entity for tax purposes and reattributes the income to the effective management jurisdiction. Additional assessments are raised with interest. A hidden distribution of profits may be deemed, triggering 35% Swiss withholding tax. Fines up to 2.5 times the tax evaded apply. Treaty benefits are denied. The company may be struck off by the commercial register if it fails to meet substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for intentional establishment and operation of a sham entity under Art. 754 CO. They may be sued by the tax authority in bankruptcy for tax deficiencies if they acted grossly negligently. Prison sentences of up to 5 years can be imposed if they personally participated in fraudulent documentation. Travel bans under stock exchange or tax investigation proceedings are possible.
Anti-Money Laundering / sanction violations

Statutory sanction: Violation of Swiss AML (GwSG) due diligence may result in a fine of up to CHF 500,000. Money laundering under Art. 305bis StGB carries imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine. Breach of Swiss embargo and sanctions regulations (e.g., goods destined for Iran, Syria, Russia) is punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years and a fine. Confiscation of proceeds and criminal liability for the beneficial owners.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sanction-affected trades are personally liable. They face criminal imprisonment, personal fines, and extradition risk. Asset freezes and travel bans are commonly imposed. The director's personal assets can be seized to cover restitution for non-financial loss or fines. The corporate veil will not shield deliberate criminal conduct.
Transfer Pricing documentation failure and transfer mispricing

Statutory sanction: Art. 58 DBG gives the FTA power to adjust profits to arm's length. Implicit penalty: burden of proof shifts to taxpayer if documentation missing. Deficiency assessments plus interest (3%) apply. Intentional under-taxation may produce a fine of 40%–100% of the evaded tax. Non-procedural failure can be fined up to CHF 10,000. Foreign exchange adjustments and recalculation of deemed dividends may trigger withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP documentation or knowingly approve artificial pricing can be criminally prosecuted for tax fraud under Art. 175 DBG. They are personally and jointly liable for the company's tax damage under Art. 754 CO. In cross-border cases, Swiss authorities may cooperate via mutual assistance leading to personal seizure and extradition. Imprisonment up to 5 years possible if the misconduct constitutes ordinary criminal fraud.
