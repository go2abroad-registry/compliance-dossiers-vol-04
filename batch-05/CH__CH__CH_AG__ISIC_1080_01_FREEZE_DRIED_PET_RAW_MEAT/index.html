Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC / Swiss Federal Tax Administration – FTA), VAT Division (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer / Division principale de la TVA); cantonal and municipal tax administrations for direct taxes. Official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; online services via www.eav.admin.ch and the Swiss UID register portal (www.uid.admin.ch).

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss AG: (1) notarise the articles of incorporation and statutory audit appointment; (2) register in the cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister) – the Federal Statistical Office/FTA automatically allocates the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX); (3) apply separately for VAT registration with the FTA if annual worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 or if voluntary registration is desired; the VAT number is the UID followed by 'MWST/TVA/IVA'. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office must appoint a Swiss VAT representative (domiciliary agent with Swiss residence) and file the FTA's simplified registration form for non-established taxable persons; the representative holds joint liability for VAT. The AG's UID is obtained electronically within days of commercial register publication; VAT activation typically requires an additional FTA application and confirmation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; Swiss entity classification is statutory, and an AG is automatically a corporation subject to Swiss corporate income tax (federal, cantonal, municipal). Available elections and reliefs relevant to a manufacturing AG: Swiss VAT group taxation election (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) under Art. 13 VAT Act; option to tax real estate for VAT; participation deduction (Beteiligungsabzug) on qualifying dividend and capital-gain income; federal/cantonal licence box (patent box) and super-deduction for R&D under the Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF 2020); notional interest deduction on qualifying equity in certain cantons; deemed foreign permanent establishment relief to avoid double taxation; and Swiss-implemented hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS. The freeze-dried pet food business may also opt for a special application to treat products as animal feed (reduced VAT rate) if substantiated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding VAT and excluding exempt supplies); threshold applies to Swiss-resident entities and, with modifications, to non-established suppliers. Non-established suppliers of digital/electronic services or certain services to Swiss final consumers may be registrable below the threshold; reverse-charge applies for Swiss recipient of services from abroad. Pet food/kibble supplies qualify alternatively for the reduced rate of 2.6% (animal feed), while freeze-dried raw meat destined for export is 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods (including freeze-dried pet raw meat and kibble) qualifies for 0% Swiss VAT under Art. 23(1) VAT Act provided: (a) the supplier is registered for VAT, (b) the goods physically leave Swiss customs territory, and (c) the supplier substantiates export by means of customs documentation (e-dec / SAD equivalent) and transport/remittance proof. Services related to exported movable goods and certain services performed on goods subsequently exported are likewise zero-rated under Art. 23(2). The reduced 2.6% rate applies only to domestic supplies of animal feed; export supplies therefore shift to 0% with full input VAT deduction. The supplier must hold proof of export at the time the VAT period is submitted; economic risk of retrospective assessment arises if documentation is incomplete.

  • Swiss Customs export declaration (e-dec data record / export manifest / Swiss SAD equivalent) with customs office reference and date of export
  • Bill of lading, CMR waybill, air waybill, or postal/courier consignment note evidencing physical departure from Switzerland
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Bank credit advice / SWIFT inward remittance certificate showing actual receipt of payment (FX repatriation evidence)
  • Veterinary health certificate / Swiss Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (FSVO) export certificate for pet food and raw meat products, where applicable

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate for B2B/B2C transactions. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) has been the mandatory standard payment slip structure for most domestic invoicing since 1 January 2020 (fully replacing IS payment slips); however, QR-bill is not a tax clearance mechanism. Federal procurement e-invoicing (B2G) applies via eBill/XML formats in phases. No continuous transaction controls, no government API clearance, and no digital stamping requirement exist for Swiss VAT. Invoices must retain statutory content (Art. 26 VAT Act), and e-invoices must be archived for 10 years.

The FTA conducts targeted export audits comparing e-dec customs data against VAT returns and bank statements; missing or late documentation triggers reclassification of the supply at 8.1% (or 2.6%) with interest and possible 10-year retroactive assessment. False export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Art. 59 VAT Act: penalty up to three times the tax evaded (intentional), administrative fines up to CHF 300,000, and criminal prosecution under Swiss administrative criminal law; responsible directors and the Swiss VAT representative may be held personally liable. Additionally, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger presumption of fictional consideration, VAT correction, and clawback of input VAT, plus penal interest of 4.5%–5% per annum.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Swiss franc (CHF) and all major currencies enjoy complete convertibility in both current and capital account transactions. No prior authorization, exchange-control licence or mandatory conversion applies to cross-border payments, profit remittances, capital contributions or foreign-direct-investment inflows. Profit repatriation by an Aktiengesellschaft is subject only to corporate formality, tax law (especially Swiss withholding tax on dividends, often reduced or refunded under double tax treaties), sanctions screening, FATCA/QI classification and commercial bank credit/KYC policy. Capital withdrawal is equally unrestricted, though SWIFT payment messages and SNB statistical reporting thresholds apply at the level of the executing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export proceeds surrender regime under Swiss law. Export receipts from freeze-dried pet-food trade may be retained in any currency in Swiss or foreign bank accounts, with no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation. Swiss balance-of-payments statistical reporting is carried out by banks (through SNB Form BNL/PZ) for cross-border payment flows above certain thresholds, but this is purely informational and imposes no conversion obligation on the exporter.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); Switzerland has no segregated foreign-exchange administration, with oversight of currency and payment instruments resting on the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) of 1996 and international sanctions enforced by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as digital currency exchanges and neobanks are treated by Swiss banks as unregulated third-party processors unless holding a FINMA license. A Swiss-registered fintech may operate under the FINMA 'FinTech' licence (Art. 1b Swiss Banking Act) with a CHF 1 million client-deposit cap and no interest-taking. Legal viability for foreign EMIs as a primary corporate treasury account is Low: they cannot hold a blocked capital escrow certificate valid for the Commercial Register and generally do not meet the substance expectations of Swiss relationship banks. Viability for auxiliary payment processing is Medium, provided the EMI passport or correspondent structure cannot evade AMLA obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the Swiss AG are routed via SWIFT MT103 for customer payments or MT202 for interbank transfers into the beneficiary account at the relationship bank. CHF-denominated credits are cleared through SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing); EUR through euroSIC; USD and other currencies through the correspondent banking network. The payment must reference the company's CHE-CR number, political purpose 'EINZAHLUNG KAPITAL' or 'SHARE CAPITAL DEPOSIT', and must originate from the shareholder's verified account. Because Switzerland has no exchange-control licence, no SNB approval is required, but banks will ask to see the escrow certificate and registration documentation before releasing funds from the capital account.Outbound supplier settlements for freeze-dried pet raw meat products and logistics use SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers, MT700 for documentary letters of credit under UCP600, and SWIFT MT798/MT707 for LC amendments. CHF and EUR payments clear through SIC and euroSIC respectively; USD via SWIFT correspondents. No strict payment instrument is mandated by law, but instruction messages should state the underlying invoice, Incoterms, contract number and customs still reference. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Swiss TP documentation practice (in line with OECD TP Guidelines), Master File preparation is expected when worldwide group revenue exceeds CHF 50 million; a Local File and TP analysis is required where Swiss related-party revenues exceed CHF 1.5 million or for all cross-border related-party transactions if the Swiss entity's annual turnover exceeds CHF 15 million. Contemporaneous documentation should be prepared and filed upon request; no statutory publication threshold is codified in the tax code.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in Art. 5 and Art. 9 DBG, requiring that the effective place of administration and actual economic activity be in Switzerland. A shell corporation cannot claim Swiss treaty residence; the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) has been adopted, and transactions involving letter-box companies with no real activity may be disregarded and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG); where qualified tax fraud is proven, imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to CHF 30,000 (Art. 186 DBG). The tax authority may also impose default interest at 4.5% per annum and deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorised officers may be prosecuted criminally, incurring individual fines and imprisonment; civil liability for the additional tax and interest applies if the corporate veil is pierced; a departure ban (travel ban) may be ordered during the criminal investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Swiss anti-abuse doctrine and Art. 9 DBG, the company may be re-characterised as non-resident for tax treaty purposes; its income may be reallocated to the ultimate beneficial owners; Swiss treaty residence certificate refused; cantonal tax privileges not granted.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations of substance and benefit from undocumented distributions may be personally liable for the cumulative additional tax and subject to debarment; charges of forgery and procuring false attestation may trigger imprisonment, travel ban, and extradition under administrative mutual assistance.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA, SR 955.0), a supervisory fine of up to CHF 500,000, revocation of business licenses for severe violations; aggravated money laundering (Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code) may be punished by imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can incur criminal responsibility if they executed or facilitated the transaction after notice of the suspect source; personal passports may be cancelled and assets frozen, and global travel bans issued pending proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Arm's Length Violation

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax authority may upwardly re-assess taxable profit and impose an assessment surcharge of up to two-thirds of the tax; if documentation is absent due to wilful concealment, that constitutes fraudulent tax evasion (Art. 186 DBG) and is subject to criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who control the related-party pricing may be held personally liable for the additional tax under joint and several liability; they may also face prosecution for falsification of documents and be subjected to a 5-year travel ban.
Withholding Tax Evasion / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The distributing AG remains liable for the unpaid withholding tax plus interest; attempting to evade withholding tax constitutes criminal tax fraud under Art. 59 of the Federal Withholding Tax Act, with a fine of up to CHF 500,000 and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the withheld tax amounts falsified; they may also face criminal proceedings for disobedience to official orders; travel bans and passport seizure are standard pre-emptive measures.
