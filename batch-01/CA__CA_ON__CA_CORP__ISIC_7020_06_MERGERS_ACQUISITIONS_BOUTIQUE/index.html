Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online portal, My Business Account, and Represent a Client. Ontario corporate income tax administration is integrated with the CRA through the federal T2 Corporation Income Tax Return. The Ontario Ministry of Finance administers separate provincial tax programs where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian corporation obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA by incorporating federally or in Ontario and registering through Business Registration Online, telephone registration, or the applicable incorporation interface. The corporation must then open the required CRA program accounts, including the RC corporate income tax account and, where applicable, the RT GST/HST account, RP payroll account, and RM import-export account. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada may apply for a BN and relevant program accounts by filing CRA Form RC1 or using the CRA non-resident registration process. The applicant must provide legal name, jurisdiction and date of formation, registered and operating addresses, directors, authorized representative information, business activities, fiscal period, expected Canadian activities, and supporting incorporation or registration documents. A non-resident may be required to appoint an authorized representative and provide proof of identity and legal existence. GST/HST registration is generally required when the entity exceeds the applicable small-supplier threshold, unless voluntary registration is elected. Ontario corporations file federal T2 returns with applicable Ontario schedules; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Ontario may also have Ontario filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate legal entities and do not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election or an S corporation election. A corporation may qualify for different statutory categories, including Canadian-controlled private corporation, public corporation, private corporation, or other corporation, depending on ownership, control, residency, and listing facts. These classifications affect rates, credits, refundable taxes, and eligibility for the small business deduction. A corporation may elect or file under specific provisions for items such as functional currency, amalgamation, rollover transactions, capital dividend account treatment, loss utilization, and tax-deferred transfers of eligible property. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act may deny deductions, include amounts in income, or otherwise neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes; they are not elective check-the-box classifications. Treaty residence and competent-authority relief may be relevant where dual residence or permanent-establishment issues arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, before taxes, measured under the small-supplier rules. The threshold generally applies over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration is required when the entity ceases to be a small supplier, although voluntary registration is permitted. Certain non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register without relying on the ordinary threshold rules, subject to the applicable place-of-supply and registration provisions. Taxable M&A advisory services supplied in Ontario generally attract 13% HST unless a specific zero-rating, exemption, or place-of-supply rule applies. Certain services that constitute exempt financial services are not subject to GST/HST and generally do not generate input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the supplier retains evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated only where the place-of-supply and specific zero-rating provisions are satisfied, including the requirement that the service generally not be performed in Canada on tangible personal property, real property, or an individual present in Canada, and that no exclusion apply. M&A advisory services require transaction-specific analysis. Services relating to financial instruments, arranging or effecting specified financial transactions, or other exempt financial services may be exempt rather than zero-rated, while general strategic, valuation, diligence, or management consulting services supplied to an eligible non-resident may qualify for zero-rating if the statutory conditions are met. Zero-rating normally requires obtaining and retaining evidence of the customer's non-resident status, contractual scope, recipient and payment records, location of performance, and the absence of Canadian-use or exclusionary facts. Exported services do not automatically qualify merely because the customer pays from outside Canada or payment is received in foreign currency.

  • For exported goods, applicable documentation may include the Canadian export declaration or applicable Canada Border Services Agency export reporting record, commercial invoice, packing list, carrier waybill or bill of lading, proof of delivery outside Canada, and customs or transport records.
  • For exported services, customs documentation is generally not applicable. The recommended substantiation file includes the engagement agreement, customer certification and evidence of non-resident status, invoice identifying zero-rated treatment, proof of payment, correspondence showing the recipient and use of the service, work-product and delivery records, and records supporting that the service was not an excluded financial, real-property, tangible-personal-property, or Canada-performed service.
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general Canadian GST/HST statutory requirement. Foreign-exchange receipts, bank statements, SWIFT confirmations, and payment records should nevertheless be retained where they support the commercial and non-resident status evidence.

Canada and Ontario do not generally impose a real-time electronic invoice clearance, mandatory government e-invoicing, or universal digital tax-stamping regime for ordinary B2B services. Registrants must issue invoices or other supporting documentation containing prescribed GST/HST information, maintain reliable electronic books and records, and retain records generally for six years after the end of the relevant year, subject to specific exceptions. Electronic invoices and electronic records are acceptable if they preserve integrity, accessibility, readability, and auditability. CRA electronic filing obligations apply to specified returns and registrants, including GST/HST returns in circumstances prescribed by law. Large corporations may have mandatory electronic filing obligations for certain returns. Tax invoices should identify the supplier's legal name and BN/GST/HST number, invoice date, recipient, description, consideration, applicable rate, tax amount, and relevant tax treatment. There is no general API-based pre-clearance requirement for this M&A advisory business.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or mandatory deadline for bringing export proceeds into Canada. CRA may reassess GST/HST where zero-rating was unsupported, the recipient was resident in Canada, the service was used in Canada, an exclusion applied, or records were inadequate. The reassessment may include the uncollected GST/HST, applicable interest, and denial or adjustment of related input tax credits or refunds. Net tax charged or collected but not remitted may be assessed as tax owing. Administrative penalties may apply for false statements, gross negligence, failure to file, late filing, failure to remit, repeated non-compliance, or failure to maintain adequate books and records; gross-negligence penalties can be substantial and may be calculated by reference to the understated tax or overstated refund. Knowingly false or deceptive GST/HST returns or claims may result in civil penalties and, in serious cases, prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, including fines and imprisonment. Refunds or input tax credits obtained through false documentation may be recovered with interest and penalties. CRA may audit prior reporting periods within the statutory reassessment period and may reassess beyond the ordinary period in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful blindness, fraud, or failure to file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating currency regime under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; no prior administrative authorization, central-bank licensing, or exchange-control approval is required for equity injections, dividend remittances, loan repayments, escrow releases, or cross-border success fees. Profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders is subject to Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5-15% under an applicable treaty, e.g., Canada-U.S. Tax Convention Article X), but not to any FX rationing, queuing, or convertibility restriction. The sole compliance gates are PCMLTFA anti-money-laundering measures, OSFI-mandated AML/KYC policies of the clearing bank, and economic sanctions screening administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds or cross-border service fees. Advisory fee receivables may be retained in the currency of the underlying engagement or converted freely. For Canadian tax purposes, foreign-currency income must be translated into Canadian dollars under ITA s. 261 (functional-currency reporting) or the CRA's quarterly exchange-rate methodology; any foreign-currency gain or loss on disposition of funds is taxed as a capital gain or allowable capital loss under ITA s. 39(1.1). International electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting by the clearing financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of authorized deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); and securities-related client-fund custody oversight by the Ontario Securities Commission (OSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary banking rail. A Canadian corporation may hold supplemental operating balances at regulated offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise PLC) for small-volume FX conversions, but Canadian Schedule I banks will not accept an unregulated EMI as settlement counterparty, and the Canadian Payments Association's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) is accessible only to regulated financial institutions. Primary treasury, escrow, and SWIFT clearing rails must be maintained at a Canadian Schedule I bank to satisfy OSC client-fund custody obligations and FINTRAC reporting integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for foreign share-capital injections: SWIFT MT103 message transmitted from the external remitting bank to the Canadian Schedule I clearing bank, field 50K stating full remitter identity, field 59 stating the beneficiary Canadian corporation and account number, and field 70 remittance information carrying the Statistics Canada Balance of Payments purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (BOP item 1000). The clearing bank will verify the supporting share-subscription resolution and subscription agreement and will report the EFT to FINTRAC if the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. For shareholder or intercompany loans, the wire must reference BOP item 8000 (Intercompany Loan) and be accompanied by an executed loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate to avoid ITA s. 17 imputed-interest adjustments.Primary clearing rail for cross-border outbound payments is SWIFT MT103. Success-fee payments to foreign advisors must carry purpose code 'Business Services – Management, Investment and Consulting Services' (BOP item 268). Escrow releases on closing of a target acquisition must carry purpose code 'Sale of Equity Securities' (BOP item 3200). CAD-denominated domestic clearing runs through the CPA Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); cross-border settlement is effected via the beneficiary bank's correspondent network. Commercial letters of credit are available for collateralized retainer obligations but are not market-standard for M&A advisory fee disbursements; the escrow account, governed by a tripartite escrow agreement with the Canadian bank as escrow agent, is the customary and preferred clearing mechanism for deal consideration and deferred purchase price.Deliverable FX forwards (CAD notional against USD, EUR, GBP) with tenors up to 24 months; a hedge-designation memorandum documenting underlying deal exposure suffices; no mandatory collateral is required for line-backed facilities.Cross-currency swaps for multi-jurisdictional earn-out and deferred-purchase-price exposures.Vanilla FX options (calls/puts) where an explicit hedge-designation memo and board-approved risk policy exist.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic-currency exposures (e.g., BRL, INR, CNY, KRW); CAD/USD NDFs are not offered in the market because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible and all CAD trades are physically deliverable.
