Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration and CRA My Business Account; Ontario HST is administered federally by the CRA under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement, with the Ontario Ministry of Finance retaining ancillary provincial tax programs such as Employer Health Tax where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario limited partnership must be registered as a separate GST/HST registrant by its general partner. Procedure: (1) file the extra-provincial limited partnership declaration under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990, c. L.16) to create local legal nexus; (2) have the general partner apply online via CRA Business Registration Online (or file Form RC1) for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (suffix RT), naming the partnership as the registered entity; (3) if the general partner is non-resident and has no Canadian address, appoint an authorized representative using Form RC59 and provide a Canadian service address; (4) CRA typically issues the BN and GST/HST account within 5-10 business days, with near real-time issuance for most online applications and effectiveness as of the requested or activity-commencement date; (5) post-registration, the partnership files GST/HST returns (Form GST34 or GST50) on its assigned monthly/quarterly/annual cycle, files the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually, issues T5013 allocation slips to resident and non-resident limited partners, and uses the same BN for CBSA customs exporter/importer reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; an Ontario limited partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96(1), with income and loss computed at the partnership level and allocated to general and limited partners under the partnership agreement. Available statutory mechanisms and elections: (a) transparent partnership allocation regime with annual T5013 filing, subject to limited-partner at-risk rules in Income Tax Act ss. 96.1 and 143.2 that cap deductible losses for non-recourse financed contributions; (b) partnership fiscal-period designation under Income Tax Act s. 96(3) requiring a common business period no longer than twelve months; (c) hybrid mismatch neutralization rules under Income Tax Act s. 18.4, effective for payments made or transactions occurring after June 30, 2021, which deny deductions or include amounts where a foreign member is subject to divergent tax classification, subject to a transitional election for qualifying pre-existing financing arrangements; (d) under the Excise Tax Act the partnership is itself a registrable person for GST/HST, so the partnership (rather than individual partners) holds the GST/HST account and claims input tax credits for manufacturing inputs; (e) export zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V requires no election but mandates collection of conclusive proof-of-export documentation within the statutory two-year period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a partnership is not required to register for GST/HST if the value of consideration for its taxable supplies made in Canada (including those of associated persons, excluding financial services) is CAD 30,000 or less in the current calendar quarter and in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available immediately, and mandatory registration and output tax collection commence once the threshold is exceeded (Excise Tax Act ss. 148 and 240).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sterile examination nitrile gloves (Health Canada Class I/II medical devices under the Medical Devices Regulations SOR/98-282; HS 4015.12/4015.19) qualify for the 0% export rate under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V where the partnership (a) transfers ownership to a non-resident, (b) exports the goods or delivers them to a carrier for export within the 60-day statutory window following delivery, and (c) obtains documentary proof of export satisfactory to the CRA within two years of the original supply (Excise Tax Act s. 23(2.1)). Services directly related to exported goods, including contract sterilization, freight, and export insurance supplied to the non-resident, are also zero-rated. No separate refund application is required for exports because the 0% rate eliminates output HST; the partnership instead claims full input tax credits (ITCs) for Canadian production inputs on its GST/HST return, and any resulting credit balance is paid as a cash refund by the CRA.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime for GST/HST; however, GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or CRA My Business Account) for periods ending after 2013, retain complete electronic books and records for six years (Excise Tax Act s. 286), and substantiate zero-rated exports with CRA-acceptable documentary evidence under CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.7. On the customs side, exports of goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more, and all controlled or regulated goods, must be declared electronically in near real-time through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS/G7) with ACI eManifest pre-load and pre-arrival data; no digital stamp is applied, but the CERS B13A electronic record constitutes the operative fiscal proof of export.

CRA audit scrutiny is heightened for medical PPE export zero-rating claims. Where the proof-of-export bundle is deficient or goods are diverted back into the Canadian market, the CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 13% HST, claw back input tax credits claimed on related production inputs, and charge arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate compounded daily. Substantive penalties include: (a) the 25% false-statement or omission penalty under Excise Tax Act s. 281.1, applied to the net tax understated or refund overstated; (b) the Income Tax Act s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the misstated partnership income allocated to limited partners; (c) criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment for evasion or knowing misrepresentation under Excise Tax Act ss. 280.1-283 and the Customs Act, including CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) fines up to CAD 25,000 per contravention for export-reporting failures; and (d) joint and several liability of the general partner for the partnership's HST debts, with retrospective reassessment authority of up to four years, unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the former Currency and Exchange Control Act, now repealed. No registration or approval is required for profit remittances, capital injections, or repatriation. Dividends and partnership distributions to non-residents are subject to withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, but the related wire transfer raises no convertibility hurdle and may be processed freely in CAD or a convertible foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists in Canada. Export receipts may be retained, converted, or disbursed in any currency at the reporting entity's discretion. Banks apply reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for international electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but this is a record-keeping obligation, not a surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can provide rapid multi-currency disbursement rails and lower transaction friction. However, they are not licensed as banks in Canada; they operate as money service businesses and must be registered with FINTRAC and, to the extent they solicit Canadian clients, comply with provincial MSB licensing regimes. A Canadian LP may open accounts with such EMIs for operational flows, but a devoted corporate bank account with a Schedule I bank remains the prudent primary treasury vehicle for ISO 13485-goods-related settlement and LC operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada does not tax the Limited Partnership at entity level; for a corporate partner allocated Canadian business income, the combined federal/Ontario general corporate income tax rate is 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); 25% combined rate applies to qualified Canadian manufacturing and processing profits (15% federal + 10% Ontario M&P rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; ordinary profit distributions from a Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding. Instead, the non-resident partner's allocated Canadian-source business income is taxed under Part I, and if a non-resident corporate partner operates through a Canadian PE, a 25% branch tax under Part XIV applies to after-tax profits repatriated abroad, subject to DTA reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than March 31 following the calendar year in which the partnership's fiscal period ends; for a fiscal period not ending on December 31, the due date is the last day of the sixth month after the fiscal period end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the taxation year; the final balance of tax is due two months after the taxation year-end for most Canadian-controlled private corporations claiming the small business deduction, and three months after year-end for other corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory when the aggregate value of specified non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000 in the taxation year, or when the CRA makes a formal request; contemporaneous Local File / Master File-equivalent documentation is expected for transactions above this threshold and for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or greater than CAD 750,000,000 triggering Country-by-Country Reporting. For the glove LP, related-party purchases of nitrile latex, dipping chemicals, technology licences, and sales commissions must be covered by full TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA vigorously applies the common-law substance-over-form doctrine, the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), and treaty anti-abuse principles to deny tax or treaty benefits where an Ontario LP is a shell. The LP must have local payroll for glove production and sterilisation, physical warehouse facilities in Ontario, real procurement and sales decisions, board meetings with meaningful management, and arm's length risk allocation; failure to demonstrate these facts will cause the CRA to reassign income to the entity with actual decision-making and to deny treaty benefits under beneficial ownership and Limitation-on-Benefits clauses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion may result in a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to five years' imprisonment on indictment; on summary conviction, fine of 50% to 200% and up to two years' imprisonment; civil reassessment at 50% gross-negligence penalty plus compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent conduct; directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings under section 227.1, face oppression- remedy personal damages, may be named in criminal indictments, can be placed on travel restriction lists, and are exposed to extradition and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under section 247(2) to the arm's length price, apply a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the gross adjustment amount where reasonable documentation is absent, add a failure-to-provide-records penalty of up to CAD 50,000, and impose daily compound interest plus the 50% gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly ignore non-arm's length pricing can be held personally liable for taxes assessed as a result of the mispricing; CRA may invoke section 160 to assess shareholders and directors for assets moved to a shell; civil fraud claims expose directors to personal restitution, Mareva injunctions, and travel bans during CRA/RCMP investigations.
Artificial Residence / Treaty Shopping / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule nullifies the tax benefit and may result in a 50% GAAR penalty; treaty benefits are denied under beneficial ownership and Limitation-on-Benefits analysis; CRA may allocate the business income to the entity truly carrying on the glove manufacturing activity, with interest and penalties applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for distributions paid to the shell while the LP is insolvent; courts can pierce the veil on equitable fraud grounds, issue worldwide freezing orders, impose personal restitution to CRA and creditors, and enforce travel bans under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act for non-citizen directors.
AML / Economic Sanctions in Medical Export Trade

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can reach CAD 1,000,000 for regulated entities; criminal money-laundering charges under Criminal Code section 462.31 carry imprisonment up to five years and asset forfeiture; export permits may be suspended by Global Affairs Canada.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly ignore AML red flags can be charged as parties to the money-laundering offence; personal liability for civil fraud damages, possible ICAO travel bans for extradition risk, and imprisonment; corporate veil-piercing is available when the LP is used as a laundering conduit.
Unlicensed Medical Device Establishment and False Tax Claims

Statutory sanction: Health Canada may assess penalties up to CAD 5,000 per day, seize product, and suspend or revoke the establishment licence; CRA will deny SR&ED investment tax credits and may apply a 50% penalty for false or wilfully misleading claims, with interest accruing from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face regulatory strict-liability exposure for medical-device violations, personal financial liability for the denied credits and penalties, and possible disqualification from serving as officers under provincial corporate law; in serious cases involving patient-harm, criminal negligence charges and travel bans may follow.
