Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A corporation must maintain a registered office address in the relevant jurisdiction. A Quebec registered office must be a real physical address in Quebec capable of receiving legal notices and official correspondence; a post-office box alone is insufficient. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine physical address, reliable receipt and forwarding of service, and access to corporate records where required. For a cybersecurity SaaS business, a virtual office is generally compatible with incorporation because no ordinary premises-based operating licence is required. It does not replace tax, employment, privacy, cybersecurity, customer-contract, or substance requirements. The company should maintain documented management, technical operations, records, banking controls, and decision-making functions appropriate to its activities and transfer-pricing profile.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board, subject to the Business Corporations Act. Quebec does not require a statutory escrow account for incorporation capital. The corporation must maintain adequate corporate records and evidence of share issuance and consideration received.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, occupancy, zoning, and fire-safety approvals where required by the premises and scope of work. Fire protection systems and emergency plans must comply with the applicable Quebec building and fire-safety framework and municipal requirements. Structural load inspection is required for material alterations, heavy equipment, server rooms, or construction work. Workplace health and safety obligations, including CNESST registration where applicable, must be satisfied. Ordinary leased office SaaS operations generally require no separate industrial clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board, subject to the Business Corporations Act. Quebec does not require a statutory escrow account for incorporation capital. The corporation must maintain adequate corporate records and evidence of share issuance and consideration received.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sector-specific industrial licensing board applies to ordinary cybersecurity SaaS, cloud vulnerability scanning, or penetration-testing services. Category: No dedicated manufacturing or industrial operating licence is ordinarily required for cybersecurity SaaS. Separate authorization may be required for regulated clients, controlled technologies, telecommunications activities, or physical data-centre operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; a resident Business Corporation is taxed at entity level on worldwide income. Cross-border elections of practical relevance include: (i) a U.S. parent's Form 8832 election to classify the Canadian subsidiary as disregarded or partnership for U.S. federal tax, creating a hybrid entity that must be documented under Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5); (ii) the GST/HST closely-related corporation election under ETA s. 156 (with parallel QST election in Québec) to treat intra-group supplies as made for nil consideration; (iii) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-free transfers of property to the corporation and the capital dividend account (CDA) election (Form T2054); and (iv) mandatory transfer-pricing and FAPI compliance for non-resident parent groups, which cannot be avoided by election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the International Monetary Fund Article VIII. No restrictions or approvals are required for cross-border capital transfers, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or trade-related payments. Quebec corporations may freely pay dividends and repatriate profits through the Canadian banking system or authorized financial institutions, subject only to general AML/KYC due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion timeline for export receipts. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and FINTRAC may require documentation for export declarations and anti-money-laundering reviews, but retained foreign-currencies may be held indefinitely in active Canadian or offshore bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Foreign exchange transactions are subject to the Currency and Foreign Transactions Reporting Act (Canada) and FINTRAC mandated suspicious transaction reporting, but no exchange control licensing is required.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility as an auxiliary funding/foreign-exchange-hedging route but low suitability as the sole corporate treasury vehicle. Canadian-licensed fintechs (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho) do not offer full commercial banking. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are generally acceptable for receiving foreign-currency revenue and making vendor payments, provided (a) the EMI holds funds through a partner bank licensed in a reputable jurisdiction, (b) the account is used for legitimate operational disbursements, and (c) all Canadian-source funds are ultimately held in a Canadian Schedule I bank or Canadian credit union account. Legal constraints: EMIs are not member of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation; funds are custodial, not deposits; Quebec's Act respecting the distribution of financial products and services does not apply to purely banking-type operations, but consumer-fraud and data-residency rules may require specific contractual protections.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border wire transfers to or from high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., FATF grey-listed states) or unknown treasury-service providers

Root cause: Cross-border wire transfers to or from high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., FATF grey-listed states) or unknown treasury-service providers

Defense evidence: Policies and procedures evidencing a legitimate cybersecurity SaaS business, including: (a) notarized certificate of incorporation and Articles of Amalgamation; (b) Quebec enterprise registration extract; (c) audited or management-prepared financial statements; (d) customer agreements and platform terms-of-service; (e) invoices and payment-receipt records for distinct end customers; (f) UBO/control-person declarations with source-of-wealth attestations; (g) explanation of the purpose-code category for each wire (e.g., 'Intra-company payment' or 'Payment for SaaS services'); (h) sub-ledger trail supporting the commercial counterparty.
Disproportionate inbound subscription revenue volume (SaaS monthly recurring) relative to entity salary and operating expenses, suggesting a payment-clearing-shell risk

Root cause: Disproportionate inbound subscription revenue volume (SaaS monthly recurring) relative to entity salary and operating expenses, suggesting a payment-clearing-shell risk

Defense evidence: A consolidated treasury forecast showing monthly MRR, gross margin, payroll (Revenu Québec + Canada Revenue Agency deductions), GST/QST remittance, AWS/Hosting invoices, and intercompany services agreements. A dedicated logical separation of client subscription funds in the operational account, plus quarterly board financial reporting, satisfies bank underwriters that the account is not an unregulated money-service business.
Blocked designation due to beneficial ownership complexity or offshore nominee directors

Root cause: Blocked designation due to beneficial ownership complexity or offshore nominee directors

Defense evidence: Updated schedule of all directors, officers, and UBOs; legal opinions confirming no beneficial-ownership obfuscation; a copy of the trust agreement (if applicable); evidence that no politically exposed persons are present; and a notarized resignation letter for any nominee directors. Final defense includes a sworn undertaking to notify the bank of any UBO change within 30 days.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax for 2024/2025 (15% federal Part I rate + 11.5% Quebec provincial rate for a general business corporation; CCPC small business deductions are outside this base case because the shareholder may be non-resident).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on gross Canadian-source dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. No provincial dividend withholding tax is imposed; the 25% rate is reduced when an applicable income tax treaty provides lower domestic withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; the federal tax balance-due day for a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) is generally three months after the fiscal year-end, while CCPCs using the eligible grace period may have a two-month balance-due date. The six-month filing deadline is for all corporations.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The CO-990 is due within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; the provincial balance-due day is generally three months after the fiscal year-end for a general corporation, or two months for a Canadian-controlled private corporation that qualifies. The six-month filing deadline applies to all corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair-market-value threshold for all reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in the taxation year, which triggers mandatory contemporaneous Form T106 / Schedule G transfer-pricing documentation. A Master File and Local File should also be prepared, although CRA's administrative benchmark for the simple Local File threshold is consistent with this CAD 1,000,000 standard.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime of the type found in UAE/Caribbean jurisdictions. However, Canadian tax law recognizes an economic-substance standard through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the treaty limitation-of-benefits and beneficial-owner provisions. CRA may disregard the corporate form where a Quebec-registered cybersecurity SaaS corporation lacks real employees, premises, business purpose, and Canadian active decision-making. In such cases, CRA may recharacterize the company as not resident in Canada for taxation or allocate its revenue to a foreign principal under the central-management-and-control test, exposing the in-plane profit to Canadian Part I tax and potential Part XIII branch withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine within the same range but increased multiplier (up to 200% or 300% under judicial discretion) and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA will also reassess beyond the normal 3-year limitation period because fraud vitiates the limitation; Quebec's Taxation Act provides a similar provincial penalty of 50% to 200% plus imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated, accepted full legal and factual control, or knowingly facilitated the false filing are jointly and severally liable to CRA for the corporation's assessed tax, interest, and penalties. The court may pierce the corporate veil in Quebec under the Civil Code for fraud or abusive conduct; personal bank accounts and assets can be seized; travel bans and probation orders may attach upon imprisonment. Director liability insurance does not indemnify fraudulent conduct.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement and T106 Omission

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the Canadian corporation using the arm's-length principle, add the entire intercompany adjustment to taxable income, and apply the transfer-pricing misstatement penalty under ITA s.247(3): 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment when a reasonable effort standard is breached. Failure to file Form T106 automatically triggers a penalty of CAD 100 per month per return, up to CAD 2,500 per return type, plus interest; reporting a false transaction can escalate to a gross-negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Transfer-pricing adjustments alone generally do not pierce the corporate veil, but directors who sign false T106 returns and knowingly misrepresent the intercompany agreement are exposed to director penalties for false information; a director may also be personally liable for consolidated corporate tax debt if the corporation was a sham or alter-ego vehicle managed solely for fraud. In serious cases, the director may face criminal tax-evasion proceedings and a personal restitution order.
Shell Corporation / Lack of Economic Substance / Resident Misdirection

Statutory sanction: CRA may deem the corporation not to be a resident of Canada for tax purposes, reassess all Canadian-source income as domiciled in the foreign principal, deny treaty relief where the residence-beneficial-owner test fails, and apply GAAR to cancel the claimed tax effects. A deemed Canadian PE may cause full 26.5% combined corporate tax on the attributable profits, plus Part XIII branch tax of 25% on the after-tax non-remitted earnings. Quebec's Registrar of Enterprises may also dissolve the entity for false address declarations.

Director personal liability: The Quebec courts (and the Federal Court in tax matters) can pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a mere façade or is used for a fraudulent or abusive purpose. The controlling foreign director or beneficial owner may be personally assessed as a principal for the corporate tax liabilities; criminal prosecution can include travel bans pending trial; imprisonment of up to 5 years may follow conviction for tax evasion connected to the shell structure.
Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime and Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Criminal Code of Canada: administrative monetary penalties for regulated entities can reach CAD 2.5 million; criminal money-laundering conviction is punishable by a fine not exceeding CAD 1 million and imprisonment for up to 10 years. FINTRAC may suspend online payment processing services, and asset freezes under the United Nations Act can be applied; Revenu Québec may suspend GST/QST registrations and freeze supplier portals.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors who know or were wilfully blind to laundering proceeds are indictable as party principals under s.21 of the Criminal Code, exposing them to the same maximum imprisonment of 10 years; personal fines, seizure of cryptocurrency and fiat holdings, passport cancellation, travel bans, and interim asset-impound orders are common. The corporate veil does not protect directors from direct criminal liability, and restitution orders may be made personally against them for the full amount laundered, not merely the corporate tax exposure.
Unpaid Statutory Source Deductions and Quebec Payroll Liabilities

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec can assess the entire unpaid employee source-deduction amounts against the corporation, impose a 10% failure-to-remit penalty plus daily interest, deny expense deductions for unremitted withholdings, and issue an assessment for the employer's share of unpaid Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance; Quebec law provides parallel penalties under the Taxation Act and Act respecting fiscal malpractice.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporation are jointly and severally liable for unremitted employee source deductions, CPP/EI, Quebec health-services fund, QST/QPIP and other withheld amounts under ITA s.227.1, ETA s.323 and corresponding Quebec legislation. CRA and Revenu Québec routinely issue director liability certificates, freeze personal bank accounts, register liens against personal real estate, and prohibit directors from leaving Canada until the debt is settled; imprisonment is possible for fraudulent omission or knowing non-remittance.
