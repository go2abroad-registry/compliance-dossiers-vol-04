Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax administration; online portal: My Business Account (MyBA) via the CRA website; provincial registration through ServiceOntario for an Ontario Business Identification Number (BIN).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-controlled Ontario-eligible business corporation must first complete either federal incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincial incorporation under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, then obtain an Ontario BIN from ServiceOntario. After incorporation, the corporation applies to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1A (or RC1 for one-step registration), specifying the required accounts: GST/HST, corporate income tax, and mandatory RG/RT payroll accounts if applicable. Non-resident corporations must also provide a Canadian representative or address for service and file Form RC4564, Non-Resident Corporation Information Return, within specified filing deadlines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide check-the-box entity classification elections under the federal Income Tax Act; resident corporations are automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Eligible corporation transactions may elect under section 85 to roll over assets at adjusted cost base, and related GST/HST registrants may elect joint elections for streamlined self-supply treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework are implemented through sections 18.7 and 18.8 of the Income Tax Act, restricting deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) over any period of 12 consecutive months; exporters may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits on expenses attributable to zero-rated export sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (including baijiu spirits) from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when possession is transferred to a non-resident person and the goods are shipped directly from Canada by the supplier, an agent, or a common carrier. The zero-rating applies only where the goods are substantially exported and not consumed or retained in Canada; supporting evidence must demonstrate physical export, delivery to a non-resident, and payment of proceeds in acceptable foreign currency or CAD.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A / Canada Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or API-based digital stamping regime in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (ETA), including supplier's BN, date of supply, description, tax amount, and total consideration. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA's Netfile or GST/HST E-services; businesses exceeding the annual threshold are required to file electronically.

CRA may deny export zero-rating where documentation fails to establish the goods were exported, resulting in reassessment at 13% HST plus interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a 10% penalty on any net tax understated, increased to 50% interest for false statements and tax evasion. Gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act may apply in cases of wilful participation in fraudulent export schemes, and the normal four-year reassessment period is extended in cases of misrepresentation or fraud for retroactive clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current and capital account convertibility under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act. There are no FX approval requirements for the import of Chinese Baijiu export proceeds into Ontario, nor for onward repatriation or dividend remittance to a Chinese parent shareholder. Profit remittance by the Canadian operating subsidiary is subject solely to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to 10% for dividends under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement where the beneficial owner holds at least 10% of voting shares) and to transfer pricing arm's length requirements under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act. No exchange control license or central bank approval is required for capital injections, shareholder loans, or trade settlement in CAD, CNY, or USD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists under Canadian federal or Ontario provincial law. The entity may retain, convert, or offshore foreign currency receipts at its discretion; however, all cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 (including successive transactions designed to circumvent the threshold) must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For export-specific proceeds, the Canadian entity must also retain underlying commercial documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs export declaration) for audit by the Canada Border Services Agency and the Ontario Alcohol and Gaming Commission (AGCO).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with policy authority under the Department of Finance Canada and transaction monitoring/reporting administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (complementary rail only). Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and may only operate in Canada as money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC; they cannot serve as the primary operating or escrow account for a licensed liquor wholesale entity under Ontario's Liquor Control Act. AGCO compliance mandates that settlement funds trace to a Canadian-chartered financial institution account; bank reconciliation audited under Canadian generally accepted auditing standards further requires a Schedule I or II bank account. EMIs may be authorized solely for small-amount collections or foreign-currency disbursements subject to FINTRAC reporting and the bank's positive consent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital from the Chinese shareholder to the Ontario liquidity pool (CAD) is via SWIFT MT103 from a Chinese commercial bank to a Royal Bank of Canada or Bank of Nova Scotia corporate account with ISO 20022 purpose code 'ISB' (Investment in a Canadian enterprise) for equity, or 'DSH' (Disbursement of shareholder loan) for intercompany debt. Each wire must include: (i) the Canadian entity's full legal name and Ontario incorporation registration number, (ii) the Chinese remitter's legal name and unified social credit code, and (iii) a clear narrative referencing the shareholder resolution or loan agreement. The receiving Canadian bank verifies the beneficial ownership declaration under the Canada Business Corporations Act, obtains FINTRAC reporting where the amount exceeds CAD 10,000, and validates the arm's length interest rate on shareholder loans to satisfy Section 247 transfer pricing rules. Inward remittances in CNY are accepted by the Canadian bank's treasury desk, converted to CAD at the prevailing market rate, and documented with a FX contract.Trade settlement outflows to Chinese baijiu producers are executed via SWIFT MT103 (single and irrevocable wire transfer) for prepayments and open-account settlements, and via Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600-issued by Scotiabank or RBC Trade Finance, confirmed by a Chinese advising bank) for documentary shipments exceeding CAD 100,000. The MT103 message must map to purpose code 'GDS' (import of goods) and include the commercial invoice number, HS code, bill of lading reference, and the baijiu supplier's Chinese bank details. For LC payments, the Canadian issuing bank requires the full documentary set (commercial invoice, packed list, original bill of lading, certificate of origin, health certificate), and the negotiation bank in China is settled through correspondent banking; CAD/CNY convertibility is automatic, and the Canadian entity may utilize the bank's demand deposit sweep to minimize idle balances.Deliverable FX Forwards (CAD/CNY and CAD/USD) with tenors up to 24 months, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or Canadian Bankers Association FX Master AgreementCross-Currency Swaps (CAD/CNY) for matched cash-flow hedging of shareholder loans and long-term trade payablesVanilla FX Options (CAD/CNY) for asymmetric downside protection on high-volume quarterly paymentsFX Swaps (CAD/USD) for temporary liquidity covering bridge financings and trade settlement gapsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for CAD/CNY because both currencies are fully convertible and deliverable; Canadian bank dealers will only quote NDFs where the CNY leg is subject to channel restrictions, and usage must be separately justified to internal treasury risk committees
