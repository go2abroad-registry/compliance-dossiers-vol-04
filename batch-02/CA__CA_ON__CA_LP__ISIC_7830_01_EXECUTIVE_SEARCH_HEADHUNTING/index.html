Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) via the 'My Business Account' portal and Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial counterpart for Ontario is the Ontario Ministry of Finance via 'ONT-TAXS' e-Services and Ontario Business Central, covering employer health tax (EHT) and provincial tax attributes.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident Limited Partnership procedure: (1) apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) for the partnership through Business Registration Online or Form RC460-1, opening program accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM); (2) register for GST/HST if annual taxable supplies in Canada exceed C$30,000 in any single quarter or over the four preceding calendar quarters, or elect voluntary registration to claim input tax credits on Canadian establishment costs; (3) file Form T5013 partnership information return and T5013 slips annually, allocating income/loss to general and limited partners; each non-resident partner must hold a valid SIN/ITN or BN and file Canadian tax returns (Part I or ITA s. 216 election); (4) register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for EHT once Ontario payroll exceeds C$1,000,000 (small-employer exemption up to that limit); (5) appoint a Canadian resident agent for service of process and maintain a provincial extra-provincial registration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; Ontario LPs are per se fiscally transparent. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96(3) election to align the partnership fiscal period with the partners' taxation year; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners to pay Part I net-basis tax on specified Canadian-source income; (iii) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 81 Quick Method election for GST/HST registrants with taxable supplies below C$400,000; (iv) ETA s. 156(1)/(2) closely related election allowing zero-rated inter-party supplies between the partnership and affiliated entities; (v) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 12.6–12.8 apply automatically and cannot be elected around, denying double deductions arising from partnership-hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148(1)): mandatory GST/HST registration arises once a person's total consideration for taxable supplies in Canada exceeds C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding calendar quarters. A non-resident LP supplying executive search services to Canadian-based clients carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same threshold; an LP exporting zero-rated services only is not required to register but may elect to do so to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Executive search and headhunting services qualify for 0% GST/HST as exported services under ETA Schedule VI, s. 23 when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST, provided the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not rendered to an individual in Canada. Candidate identification and advisory services supplied to a foreign corporate employer are zero-rated even where the candidates are Canadian residents, because the recipient is the non-resident business; placement of executives into a Canadian operating entity for a non-resident group must satisfy a benefit-to-Canada substantive test and CRA will scrutinize whether the service is truly 'in respect of' a foreign recipient's business outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13 / Canadian Automated Export Declaration) — applicable to exported goods; not required for pure offshore service exports but retained where contract deliverables cross the border
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — carrier-issued proof of export for physical deliverables and marketing materials; for services replaced by the signed engagement agreement and delivery acceptance record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B, or correspondent-bank payment confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement from the non-resident client
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice — invoice expressly stating 'Zero-rated exported service — ETA Schedule VI, s. 23' with client's foreign address and non-resident declaration
  • Signed executive search retainer agreement stating place of performance, recipient's non-resident status, and confirmation of non-application to Canadian real property or Canadian-situated tangible property

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, invoice stamping, or national e-invoicing mandate. CRA accepts electronic invoices in PDF, EDI, and API-transmitted formats; registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars under ETA s. 223 and retain all electronic records and supporting documentation for six years. Optional CRA GST/HST NETFILE and XML-based reporting feeds exist; Ontario has no separate provincial e-invoicing clearance system.

CRA may retroactively disallow export zero-rating treatment, reassess the uncollected 13% HST, deny associated input tax credits, and impose interest and penalties: (i) 50% gross-negligence/false-statement penalty under ETA s. 285(1) or ITA s. 163(2); (ii) unlimited reassessment period where misrepresentation, negligence, or carry-on default exists (ETA s. 298(6), ITA s. 152(4)); (iii) criminal prosecution for evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment; (iv) clawback of already-refunded GST/HST with compound interest and suspension or revocation of GST/HST registration; (v) general and limited partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations, and non-resident partners may be assessed personally.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no foreign-exchange controls restricting conversion, remittance, or profit repatriation. For an Ontario limited partnership with non-resident partners, deemed or distributed earnings allocated to non-residents may be subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, but this is a tax compliance matter rather than a currency control. The partnership should maintain a CRA business number, file partnership information returns, and document the tax treaty position to ensure efficient cross-border distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada: there is no statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export and service-provider receipts may be retained in any permitted currency, converted freely through a Canadian Schedule I bank, and are reportable only for balance-of-payments statistical purposes through Statistics Canada if prescribed thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; Canada does not operate a centralized foreign exchange control agency, and exchange-control-related oversight is shared by FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering reporting and OSFI for prudential bank supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium. Offshore digital EMIs or B2B payment platforms such as Wise Business may be used as auxiliary cross-border collection and disbursement rails, but they cannot replace a Canadian Schedule I bank account. The EMI must be authorized, AML-compliant, and, if carrying on business in Canada, registered as a money service business with FINTRAC. The LP must maintain a primary Canadian banking relationship to provide regulatory clarity, preserve the audit trail, and ensure settlement-finality for retainer receipts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (15% Federal Part I general corporate rate + 11.5% Ontario general corporate income tax rate). For a limited partnership, the partnership itself is fiscally transparent; income is taxed at the partner level, not at the LP level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividend payments to non-residents. For limited partnerships, distributions of partnership income are treated as business income taxable under Part I at the non-resident partner level, not as dividends; no further Part XIII withholding applies to actual flow-through distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required where non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1 million in aggregate in a taxation year. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for Canadian multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750 million or more. For limited partnerships, each corporate partner that executes intercompany documentation must maintain accurate records substantiating arm's length pricing for retainer fees, management service charges, and IP royalties.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the Canada Revenue Agency can reject transactions lacking real commercial substance under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), the common law sham doctrine, and transfer pricing reasonableness provisions. A Canadian limited partnership providing executive search and headhunting services must demonstrate genuine business activity, physical presence, active employees, real clients, and arm's length compensation in Ontario.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act section 239: fines from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 2 years. Civil penalties under section 163 include 50% gross negligence penalties on the misreported amount; section 227.8 applies 10% negligence penalties on unremitted withholdings plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and withholding taxes under section 227.1. General partners of the LP face unlimited personal liability for civil tax penalties. Personal travel bans and seizure of personal assets occur following criminal tax evasion convictions; fraudulent concealment can result in imprisonment of the directors and general partners.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), denial of expenses and loss claims, transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, and potential 50% gross negligence penalty. The partnership may be declared a sham, rendering every transaction void for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for all partnership obligations and reassessed taxes; directors of a corporate partner who signed false declarations may be jointly liable for resulting penalties and interest, and can be subject to civil asset freezes. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion may result in imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment reallocating income to Canada, plus a 10% penalty on the amount of the net adjustment if documentation is deficient; interest accrues daily on the additional tax. CRA can also issue a 50% gross negligence penalty in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly design and sign intercompany pricing agreements are subject to personal liability if they cause the corporation to fail to file required returns or make false statements. Individual convictions may impose fines equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per severity level; criminal penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for intentional non-compliance. Directors and compliance officers can be personally subjected to penalty orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally named in compliance orders for AML breaches if they authorized or acquiesced in the violation. Individuals involved in money laundering through the firm risk asset forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years (or life if the proceeds are from illegal drug trafficking).
Unremitted Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount, plus 20% if the failure is made knowingly; the Crown may assess 100% of the unremitted amount against the employer through a reassessment. Director liability certificates are registered and enforceable against personal assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and the LP's general partner are personally liable for unpaid source deductions plus interest and penalties under subsection 227.1(1). CRA can seize personal bank accounts, freeze assets, and restrict travel against directors who participate in the failure.
