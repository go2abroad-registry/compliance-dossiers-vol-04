Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax, and Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec income tax. Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account at canada.ca; Revenu Québec's Entreprises Québec portal and CléNumérique at revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent and does not itself pay income tax, but it must establish a federal Business Number (BN) with CRA using Form RC1 (Partnership Registration) or online Business Registration Online, and must open a GST/HST account if it exceeds the small supplier threshold. The LP must also register with Revenu Québec for QST using Form LM-1 or online registration, providing the partnership declaration, list of general and limited partners, and Quebec place of business details. Each partner must supply its own tax identifier: Canadian partners use SIN/BN; non-resident partners must supply a tax identification number from their country of residence and, if carrying on a taxable activity in Canada, obtain a CRA non-resident account number. A foreign partner with no Canadian presence may need to appoint a representative for service of process and file prescribed withholding elections under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box or entity-classification election; the Quebec LP is classified as fiscally transparent by operation of ITA sections 96-103 and the Quebec Taxation Act. There is no election to convert the LP into an entity taxed at partnership level. The principal elections relevant to an LP are: (i) an election under ITA subsection 34(1) for an alternative taxation year of the partnership; (ii) partner-level elections affecting allocation and at-risk loss limitations under ITA subsection 96(2.1) and related provisions; and (iii) GST/HST partnership registration elections under the Excise Tax Act where the partnership is treated as a registrant for the purposes of the joint venture. If a non-Canadian member is itself a hybrid entity, Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 et seq.) may automatically disallow or adjust mismatching tax outcomes; such rules do not provide a unilateral statutory election to alter the LP's Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies, other than supplies of capital assets, measured in the current calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; a similar CAD 30,000 threshold applies under the QST regime for taxable supplies made in Québec, with mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded or the registrant is a non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Québec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act for: (i) tangible personal property exported by the supplier, delivered to a carrier for export, or exported in the course of a continuous journey; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is performed outside Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iii) intangible personal property, including streaming licences and digital intellectual-property rights, supplied to a non-resident person where the property is not used in Canada and is not a prescribed supply made through a Canadian establishment. For a vertical micro-drama distribution business through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the LP's supplies to the app-store operator or to non-resident end users may be zero-rated when the records demonstrate that the digital content is delivered outside Canada, the contracting recipient is non-resident, and the supply is not part of the seller's Canadian business establishment. The seller must retain contemporaneous evidence of non-residency and place of consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A) or authorized e-manifest data for physical export shipments
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract identifying the non-resident purchaser or app-store operator
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier manifest evidencing physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank credit advice evidencing payment from outside Canada
  • Streaming royalty reports, user-geolocation logs, and platform settlement reports substantiating digital delivery to non-resident consumers

Canada and Québec do not impose real-time fiscal clearance, pre-invoice digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction controls. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or a CRA-approved API/EDI channel when the registrant exceeds prescribed electronic filing thresholds. QST returns must be filed electronically through Revenu Québec's e-services. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and may be exchanged through Peppol-compatible networks, but no government validation, serialization, or clearance is required before issuance.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, and may claw back zero-rating together with unremitted GST/HST and QST, interest, and penalties. Penalties include gross-negligence penalties under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 50% of the misreported net tax), failure-to-remit penalties and daily interest, and Quebec penalties under the Taxation Act and the Act respecting the Québec Sales Tax. Deliberate fraudulent export claims can lead to criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible under both the current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls, no registration or approval requirements for cross-border fund transfers, and no limits on profit or dividend remittances to non-resident partners. Quebec is subject to the federal Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act for the LP's general partner, but the LP's capital account and partnership income allocations may be repatriated freely. Horizontal taxation issues do not apply; remittance is a cash distribution from the LP, subject to the LP's partnership agreement and Quebec's Civil Code obligations. Non-resident partners may receive distributions without Bank of Canada approval; the only applicable compliance is Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Part XIII withholding tax on certain amounts, with treaty relief available under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for U.S. partners, and under other applicable treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratio for export receipts. A Quebec limited partnership earning revenue from global vertical micro-drama distribution (via Apple IAP, Google Play, Stripe) may retain ad revenues in foreign currency (USD/EUR) in offshore or deemed-foreign settlement accounts; however, for CRA reporting, all income must be reported annually in CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or an acceptable average rate. No statutory timeline exists for converting export proceeds into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no separate exchange-control authority or Foreign Exchange Administration mandate exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium. Canada does not grant a distinct 'electronic money institution' (EMI) license comparable to EU EMI passports. Payment service providers in Quebec require either a bank license under the Bank Act, a provincial money-services licence (in Quebec, registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers for money services businesses under the Money-Services Businesses Act), or an agent/white-label arrangement with a Schedule I bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK/FCA EMIs) cannot isolate the LP from Canadian AML compliance; they may provide USD settlement rails for Stripe or platform proceeds, but funds flowing into a Canadian LP bank account must be routed through a regulated Canadian financial institution. Effective practical rating: Medium for multi-currency treasury holding, Low for accepting regulated settlement without a Canadian banking partner.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity inherent in a limited partnership structure (multiple limited partners, nominee agreements, or foreign-held LP interests).
