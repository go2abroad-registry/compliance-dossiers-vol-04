Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery via the Ontario Business Registry for the limited partnership registration

E-government filing portal

A foreign-headed Ontario Limited Partnership first files an Initial Declaration under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario) with ServiceOntario. After the Ontario registration is obtained, the LP must apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT) through Business Registration Online, by submitting Form RC165 (GST/HST Application for Registration), or by calling the CRA Business Registration line. Non-resident LPs making taxable supplies in Canada must register even without a Canadian permanent establishment; the CRA will issue the BN/RT account for the non-resident partnership. The LP should also open the RT account before first taxable supply if the small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-headed Ontario Limited Partnership first files an Initial Declaration under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario) with ServiceOntario. After the Ontario registration is obtained, the LP must apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT) through Business Registration Online, by submitting Form RC165 (GST/HST Application for Registration), or by calling the CRA Business Registration line. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No federal 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian law. A Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA); no election exists to convert the LP to an entity-level taxpayer. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership may be registered as a separate registrant and may elect a representative member under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) administrative rules. Hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically under ITA ss. 94.2-94.92 and related ETA provisions; there is no elective 'opt-in' hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter and CAD 30,000 in the four preceding calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident LP engaged in online retail of auto aftermarket parts in Ontario must register for GST/HST once this threshold is exceeded, including where sales are made through digital commerce channels.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property (automotive parts and accessories) is zero-rated for GST/HST under Schedule VI Part V of the ETA when the vendor supplies the goods for delivery outside Canada, including by shipping via common carrier, freight, or courier to a foreign address, or by delivering to an export warehouse for export by the purchaser. The vendor must export the goods within the prescribed time frames and retain full export documentation. Services that are directly in respect of exported goods may also be zero-rated where the requirements of Schedule VI Part V are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CBSA Automated Export Declaration or equivalent Canadian export reporting record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outbound shipment and destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX record evidencing receipt of export proceeds within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, and third-party tracking showing the ultimate foreign destination and date of export

Canada has no national mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. The CRA does not require digital stamping or pre-clearance of invoices. Registered persons must issue GST/HST-compliant invoices containing prescribed information; electronic invoices in PDF, EDI, or XML are accepted. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically when amounts are owed or when required by CRA based on revenue threshold; CRA also uses API-based e-filing for certain returns, but no continuous transaction control or real-time tax authority clearance is required.

CRA may audit export proofs retrospectively; missing or inconsistent documentation will cause the zero-rating to be denied, resulting in reassessment of the full 13% Ontario HST on the export supply, plus arrears interest and penalties under the Excise Tax Act. A late-filing penalty of 1% of the net tax plus 25% of that amount per late month (maximum 12 months) may apply. False statements, omissions, or gross negligence attract severe penalties, including penalty equal to a substantial percentage of the net tax misstatement, daily compound interest, and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion, with retroactive clawback of input tax credits and refunds that were claimed on the strength of the invalid export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with fully convertible current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar may be freely bought, sold, and held in CAD/USD and other major currencies without central-bank approval. Profit remittance and repatriation of capital or partnership distributions from an Ontario Limited Partnership are not subject to exchange-control limits, quotas, or licensing. The only practical hurdles are Canadian tax compliance, treaty-eligible withholding obligations where applicable, and AML/KYC documentation. No conversion ratio, no repatriation licence, and no current-account restrictions apply to e-commerce trading proceeds under ISIC 4791.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement applies to export proceeds in Canada. An Ontario LP may retain export receipts in any fully convertible foreign currency, use them directly to pay foreign suppliers, or convert them at commercial bank rates at any time. Exporters should use the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for accounting and tax valuation. FINTRAC requires electronic funds transfer reporting for cross-border EFTs of C$10,000 or more, but this is a reporting obligation only and not an exchange-control or surrender mechanism.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank; Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange administration body. Exchange-control policy is liberalized, cross-border fund reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and economic sanctions are administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability as an ancillary payment rail only; not acceptable as the primary treasury banking relationship for the Ontario LP. An offshore digital EMI is not licensed under the Bank Act (Canada) as a deposit-taking institution, cannot hold Canadian Lynx settlement accounts, and cannot legally accept retail or corporate deposits in Canada. If the EMI operates into or within Ontario, it will generally require FINTRAC money-services business registration under the PCMLTFA, Ontario Money-Services Business Act licensing through the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA), and may face registration and safeguarding obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act. The EMI can support supplier payouts or currency conversion in selected corridors, but all capital injections, CAD clearing, trade settlements, and escrow balances should be held at a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all partner capital injections through regulated banking channels only: domestic CAD contributions should use Payments Canada Lynx same-day high-value settlement, and cross-border USD/FX contributions should use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers. Canada does not require a statutory SWIFT purpose code, but banks expect a clear remittance narrative. Use field 50K for the contributing partner's full legal name, field 59 for the Ontario LP legal name and account number, and field 72 with the text 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO ONTARIO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP - NO EXCHANGE CONTROL RESTRICTION'. Institutions must report cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC. To minimize friction, prepare an executed capital drawdown notice, source-of-funds file, and confirmation that the remitter is exactly the contributing partner or the authorized corporate general partner. Reject transfers originating from crypto exchanges, unregulated money-service businesses, or third-party accounts unless fully pre-cleared by the onboarding bank.Supplier payments for auto parts purchases should use SWIFT MT103 for global open-account telegraphic transfers, Lynx for high-value CAD domestic settlements, ACSS batch EFT for ordinary Canadian-dollar supplier payments, and USD correspondent clearing for US suppliers. For large or first-time purchases, issue an irrevocable confirmed commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700/MT710 through the Schedule I bank's trade finance desk. Every payment must be referenced by invoice and purchase-order numbers in SWIFT field 70/72 to support audit. Stripe and PayPal should be used only for inbound merchant card settlement and daily sweeps into the operating account; they should not be used as outbound supplier payment rails. Treasury should pre-fund USD payables using spot or forward FX contracts to avoid unhedged settlement risk.Deliverable CAD/USD foreign exchange forward contracts, plain vanilla, with tenors typically from one to twelve months under an ISDA FX/Currency Definitions agreement.CAD/USD cross-currency swaps, including mark-to-market and reset forms, for hedging long-term USD commercial payables or committed purchase obligations.CAD/USD FX options, European plain-vanilla calls and puts, to hedge contingent USD disbursements or uncertain timing in supplier purchase orders.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging exposures in non-fully-convertible secondary currencies where no deliverable forward market exists; NDFs are legally permissible in Canada but are not the market standard for CAD, because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible.
