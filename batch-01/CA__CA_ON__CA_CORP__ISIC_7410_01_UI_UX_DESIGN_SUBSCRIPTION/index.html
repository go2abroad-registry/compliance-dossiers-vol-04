Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals; Ontario corporate income tax and HST are administered by CRA on behalf of Ontario under a joint federal-provincial tax administration agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated UI/UX design agency registers for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) with the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre, or online through BRO. No minimum capital or Canadian office is required; the applicant must provide its foreign charter, principal business activity (NAICS 541430), worldwide and Canadian turnover, and bank details. CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT (GST/HST) program account suffix; an income tax account and Ontario corporate tax account are established through the same BN once the entity carries on business or establishes a permanent establishment in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a resident and separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, taxable on worldwide income; no pass-through regime is available, and a Canadian corporation is a per se corporation for U.S. check-the-box purposes under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or equivalent pass-through election for corporations. Available statutory elections and safeguards include: (i) the ETA s. 156 nil-consideration election between closely related corporations; (ii) the GST/HST Quick Method remittance election for small suppliers; (iii) the non-resident small supplier threshold application; (iv) Canada-U.S. Treaty Art. IV(6) fiscally transparent entity relief preserving U.S. look-through treatment of hybrid LLCs without altering Canadian entity-level taxation; and (v) the hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 212.4, which operate without a domestic elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or in the previous four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers, registration under the 2021 simplified foreign-supplier framework becomes mandatory once Canada-destined specified supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in four consecutive quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where (i) tangible goods are exported as soon as practicable to a non-resident recipient; or (ii) a service is supplied to a non-resident person and the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply, and not a service performed while the recipient is an individual present in Canada. UI/UX design-subscription services supplied to non-resident B2B clients therefore qualify for zero-rating where the subscription agreement designates the non-resident recipient and foreign place of supply; no statutory FX repatriation deadline exists under Canadian law, but receipt of foreign currency within 180 days is the documentary benchmark for audit substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13/CUS-303 for goods; CBSA G7/G8 electronic export reporting where applicable),
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of tangible deliverables from Canada, where any physical goods are shipped,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: CRA does not issue a formal certificate; equivalent evidence is a bank remittance record, SWIFT MT103, foreign exchange conversion slip, or AR invoice settlement showing receipt of consideration from the non-resident within the 180-day repatriation benchmark,
  • Commercial invoice and executed subscription agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, place of supply, and the zero-rated status, retained with books and records for six years pursuant to ETA s. 286.

Canada and Ontario impose no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), mandatory API reporting, digital stamping, or pre-clearance certification regime for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are permitted and must be retained in accordance with CRA Information Circular IC 87-2; taxable invoices must show the supplier's BN/RT account number, invoice date, and total consideration. Non-resident digital registrants must file and remit electronically through CRA My Business Account.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims against the documentary bundle and place-of-supply rules; reversal of the 0% rate for Canadian specified supplies triggers HST reassessment with statutory interest at the prescribed rate plus 4% for corporate taxpayers, and the normal four-year reassessment period may extend to ten years for misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). Penalties include ETA s. 285.1 at 50% of falsely claimed credits or refunds, ss. 280-283 failure-to-register, failure-to-file, and failure-to-remit penalties of C$250 plus 6% per annum, and criminal fines up to C$25,000 and/or five years' imprisonment for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency with no bands, approval requirements, repatriation quotas, or conversion hold-periods. Inbound equity injections, outbound profit repatriation, management fees, royalties, and dividends are not subject to exchange-control licences. The only relevant frictions are Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced typically to 5-15% for dividends and 0-15% for royalties/management fees), OBCA solvency requirements for capital-reduction distributions, and standard FINTRAC/CRA reporting thresholds. No domestic 'subscriber capital' currency conversion mandate applies to an Ontario business corporation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designated-account holding period for exported design-subscription services. The Ontario corporation may retain foreign-currency receivables indefinitely in USD or other currency accounts, convert at its treasury discretion, or maintain matched balances for natural hedging. The residual compliance floor is FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting on wires of CAD 10,000 or more (bank-reported rather than client-filed) and Statistics Canada's Report on International Transactions in Services (RITS) collection thresholds for non-financial enterprises.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; foreign-exchange administrative control functions ceased with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. Prudential bank supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border AML/EFT surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada therefore operates an open, unlicensed foreign-exchange administration regime with no discretionary exchange-control authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is an inbound SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the Ontario corporation's account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with the wire narrative strictly limited to 'shareholder equity injection / capital contribution' and no invoice or service references. CAD-denominated credits settle via CPA LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) with same-day finality; USD credits settle through the bank's U.S. correspondent clearing (Fedwire or CHIPS). The receiving bank automatically files a FINTRAC EFT report for any credit of CAD 10,000 or more; no client-side pre-approval is required. The corporate minute book must evidence the subscription resolution, share issuance, and updated register; non-resident subscribers holding 10% or more should be documented for ongoing Part XIII and T1134 compliance analysis. Legal constraints: EMI and fintech balances are not CDIC-insured deposits and do not participate in Canadian Payments Act settlement finality; therefore payroll, CRA, and Ontario statutory remittances should be settled from a Schedule I bank operating account. Use of an offshore EMI without Canadian MSB registration creates FINTRAC registration exposure, CRA foreign-reporting exposure, and GST/HST collection-location ambiguity. As a Low_Risk_Standard entity, a hybrid model - Stripe/Wise for collections and Tier-1 bank for treasury - is the legally optimal blueprint.
