Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility with no exchange control restrictions. Profits and capital distributions may be remitted freely, subject to applicable Quebec and federal withholding taxes on non-resident partnership members. CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and other major currency conversions are freely available through authorized banks. No administrative license or central bank approval is required for currency conversion or remittance of dividends or partnership distributions.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in domiciled foreign currency accounts, convert to CAD at market rates, or utilize held funds for offshore trade payments without any residual balance repatriation obligation.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking supervision; Global Affairs Canada for trade control policy; no dedicated foreign exchange administration exists under Canada's liberalized cross-border capital regime.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Moderate feasibility as a secondary liquidity channel only. Non-bank fintech EMIs and payment service providers are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; they operate under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requiring registration with the Bank of Canada. Cross-border digital EMIs may provide virtual IBANs and CAD collections, but lack direct SWIFT membership and are subject to FINTRAC money service business registration. They are not viable substitutes for primary corporate banking relationships and may be constrained by Quebec language compliance and partnership account verification requirements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident limited partners with opaque beneficial ownership chains
        
Root cause: Non-resident limited partners with opaque beneficial ownership chains

        
Defense evidence: Full organizational chart with each limited partner's owning entity and individuals; notarized LP agreement; declarations of ultimate beneficial ownership; passport copies and proof of residence; corporate registry extracts from the applicable foreign jurisdiction.

      

      
      

        High-value capital contributions without a documented source of funds
        
Root cause: High-value capital contributions without a documented source of funds

        
Defense evidence: Bank statements of contributing member for six months; audited financial statements if applicable; sale or repurchase agreements if funding derives from prior entity distributions; tax clearance certificate or confirmation of lawfully sourced funds; signed source-of-funds declaration.

      

      
      

        Exposure to high-risk jurisdictions or sanctions-listed payment counterparties
        
Root cause: Exposure to high-risk jurisdictions or sanctions-listed payment counterparties

        
Defense evidence: OFAC and Global Affairs Canada sanctioned party screening results; trade compliance questionnaires from all material suppliers and customers; jurisdiction risk matrix; SWIFT CPR and KNUCK character code verification; exclusion of all Russia, Belarus, and sanctioned Iranian trade lanes.

      

      
      

        Mixed-currency clearing with crypto-linked or third-party payment processors
        
Root cause: Mixed-currency clearing with crypto-linked or third-party payment processors

        
Defense evidence: Detailed treasury use-case mapping; Amazon Pay and Stripe settlement statements showing e-commerce revenue flows; reconciliation reports between receivables and disbursement accounts; attestation that no digital asset transfer is routed through the main operating account; payment processor compliance certificates.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
0% at the Limited Partnership level (fiscally transparent flow-through entity); an underlying corporate partner is taxed on its share at the combined general corporate rate of 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). A qualified Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) partner carrying on active business income may benefit from the small business deduction, yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 13% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (federal 9% + Quebec 4%).

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% statutory Part XIII tax (ITA s. 212(2)) on distributions by a Canadian partnership deemed to be dividends paid to a non-resident limited partner in respect of Canadian-source business profits (ITA s. 212(13.1)).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 - mandatory Form T106 and minimum contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are required when the aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border transactions with specified non-resident related parties equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year (ITA s. 247(4) and s. 237.1). A master-file / global-documentation package is expected where the worldwide group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 20,000,000. For the Quebec Montessori wooden block manufacturer, the CAD 1,000,000 threshold is typically crossed once consolidated intercompany beechwood supply, Amazon_Pay marketplace sales commissions, and North American distribution flows are aggregated.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations regime of the type found in offshore financial centres; equivalent substance-over-form discipline is imposed through the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245, with the 2023 Budget amendments creating a rebuttable presumption of misuse) and the Quebec parallel anti-avoidance provision (Taxation Act s. 1079.6.2). The Quebec LP manufacturing physical wooden blocks on owned or leased factory premises in Quebec, employing local production staff, holding inventory, purchasing beechwood locally, and making strategic operating decisions in Quebec possesses clear economic substance. A 'mailbox' limited partnership without real Quebec activity and personnel will be vulnerable to sham characterization, GAAR recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits under the Canada-US treaty's limitation-on-benefits provision, and administrative penalty exposure.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion - false income disclosure
        
Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on indictment, a fine of not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years; on summary conviction, a fine of 50%-100% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to two years. Quebec Taxation Act s. 1063 imposes additional provincial fines and penalty interest at the prescribed rate plus 10% of the unpaid tax.

        
Director personal liability: The general partner and its directing officers are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's unpaid federal and Quebec taxes under ITA s. 227.1 and Taxation Act s. 1107.0.0.1; criminal conviction triggers a criminal record, Federal Court registration of the Minister's certificate, passport surrender orders, and CBSA exit-control travel bans; extradition between Canada and the United States remains available for cross-border evasion schemes.

      

      
      

        Part XIII Non-Resident Withholding Failure
        
Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(8): penalty of 10% of the amount that should have been withheld, plus prescribed-rate interest on the unremitted balance; the 25% tax itself remains collectible from both the partnership (s. 215(1)) and the non-resident partner, with gross negligence exposing the entity to the 50% penalty under s. 163(2).

        
Director personal liability: The general partner's directors are personally liable for the full unremitted Part XIII amount under ITA s. 227.1 without the protection of the corporate veil; Revenu Quebec enforces the same liability provincially, and directors may face civil seizure of personal assets, director disqualification orders, and travel restriction measures pending criminal tax prosecution.

      

      
      

        Sham Partnership / Shell LP (GAAR Abuse and Simulation)
        
Statutory sanction: ITA s. 245 GAAR permits the CRA to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the transaction on arm's-length economic terms, and impose a 25% GAAR penalty under s. 245(5.2); where a materially false statement or misleading declaration accompanies the transaction, the 2023 Budget rule raises the penalty to 50%. Revenu Quebec's parallel GAAR produces identical recharacterization and provincial penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Civil courts in Quebec pierce the veil where the LP is a mere simulation (arts. 316 and 317 of the Civil Code of Quebec), making the general partner and its directors unlimitedly liable for the reassessed tax, interest, and penalties; directors additionally risk personal injunctions, asset freezes, and placement on Revenu Quebec's tax-debtor exit-control list.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(3): CAD 2,500 for the first month of failure to file Form T106 plus CAD 100 per additional month (maximum CAD 12,000), and CAD 250 per month (maximum CAD 25,000) where the transaction is wholly unreported; ITA s. 247(2) re-determination of the arm's-length price triggers reassessment, interest, and an additional 10% penalty for grossly negligent documentation under s. 247(3).

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign the T5013 without exercising reasonable diligence are jointly liable for the adjusted tax under the Quebec civil solidary-liability regime; CRA policy permits penalty assessments directly against the partnership's general partner, and a pattern of deficient documentation supporting criminal referral elevates the risk of personal prosecution.

      

      
      

        AML / Beneficial-Ownership Transparency (FINTRAC and Corporate Registry)
        
Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for non-compliance with registration, record-keeping, or verification duties; criminal penalties on indictment of up to CAD 2,000,000 and five years imprisonment for deliberate structuring to avoid reporting.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable where they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the contravention; Quebec courts may additionally issue ineligibility-to-serve orders and impose travel bans preventing departure during pending AML or tax-fraud proceedings.
