Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) via the official portal estv.admin.ch and the UID register (Zefix / UIDW).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying out VAT-liable activities in Switzerland must register for VAT with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration through the online VAT registration form (Form / Formulaire 155.005) or via the Suisse Tax Portal (S-TP). The FTA issues a Swiss VAT number (MWST/Ust-ID) and a company identification number (UID/BE number). The registration process requires a business address, proof of identity of legal representatives, description of the planned Swiss activities, estimated turnover, and, where applicable, a Swiss customs declaration number. Advancement of registration before commencement is recommended; VAT liability is effective from the moment the turnover threshold is exceeded for supplies in Switzerland.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) is a resident company subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income with tax residence based on registered office or place of effective management, and is jointly and severally liable for VAT as a taxable entity; non-resident companies without permanent establishment in Switzerland are only subject to Swiss tax on Swiss-source items, while VAT applies to supplies effected or deemed effected in Switzerland. — A foreign entity carrying out VAT-liable activities in Switzerland must register for VAT with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration through the online VAT registration form (Form / Formulaire 155.005) or via the Suisse Tax Portal (S-TP). The FTA issues a Swiss VAT number (MWST/Ust-ID) and a company identification number (UID/BE number). The registration process requires a business address, proof of identity of legal representatives, description of the planned Swiss activities, estimated turnover, and, where applicable, a Swiss customs declaration number. Advancement of registration before commencement is recommended; VAT liability is effective from the moment the turnover threshold is exceeded for supplies in Switzerland.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) is a resident company subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income with tax residence based on registered office or place of effective management, and is jointly and severally liable for VAT as a taxable entity; non-resident companies without permanent establishment in Switzerland are only subject to Swiss tax on Swiss-source items, while VAT applies to supplies effected or deemed effected in Switzerland.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Switzerland. Available statutory elections include the election for VAT grouping (rowsing group for VAT purposes) among related Swiss entities, the election for corporate income tax grouping (Steuerkonsolidierung) at cantonal level, the election for deemed cost-plus / margin taxation for certain service activities, and the option to waive VAT exemption on real estate supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules under Swiss law (Art. 60a-60i of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax) provide defensive measures but no elective hybrid vehicle status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (irrespective of where customers are located), except that a lower CHF 150,000 threshold applies to subsidies and certain non-profit entities; foreign businesses that exceed CHF 100,000 of worldwide turnover become VAT-liable in Switzerland to the extent they perform supplies in Switzerland, including digital services to final consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to the export of tangible goods and to services provided in Switzerland when the service recipient is located abroad (based on the place-of-recipient rule or the place-of-benefit rule for services), provided the supplies are properly evidenced and the exporter keeps adequate books and records. For MMORPG digital supplies (e.g., in-app purchases, subscriptions, downloadable content), zero rating applies when the customer is a non-Swiss business or, for B2C digital services, the effective place of use and benefit is abroad and the supplier can demonstrate that the recipient resides outside Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss Customs Export Declaration / via NCTS or e-dec) and, for physical goods, a copy of the bill of lading or CIM consignment note / air waybill, together with a proof of departure from Switzerland.
  • Commercial invoice containing seller and buyer identification, description of exported goods or services, value, quantity, VAT zero-rating clause and reference to the export customs declaration number.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or alternative banking settlement and SWIFT/IR evidence demonstrating that the export proceeds are received in accordance with the terms of the underlying contract, including proof of the customer's place of establishment for services.

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate and no compulsory government API clearance for standard B2B/B2C invoicing. E-invoicing in structured XML formats (e.g., Swiss eBill, QR-bill, EDI/XML) is recognized and encouraged; the Swiss QR-bill is mandatory for domestic invoices sent to private consumers for certain payment purposes and contains a unique QR reference. There is no nation-state digital stamping for VAT invoices; however, invoices must contain mandatory data per Art. 37 VAT Act (seller identification, UID number, invoice number, date, description, rate).

Swiss tax authorities and the Federal Customs Administration may retrospectively audit export zero-rating within five years (extendable to ten years in cases of tax evasion). If zero rating is claimed without valid export documentation, the VAT is reclaimed at the standard rate equal to the input tax credit previously claimed, plus default interest (5% per annum) and a surcharge of up to 3 to 5 times the tax evaded in cases of fraudulent claims. Criminal liability can arise under Art. 184 VAT Act for tax evasion (Versteuerungsbetrug / fraude fiscale), with fines up to CHF 500,000 or imprisonment in severe cases. Additionally, the FTA can issue a penalty of 10% to 30% of the contested VAT for negligence, and a refusal to issue export certificates for future consignments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF; the Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange-control permits, no prior SNB approval for dividend distributions, profit remittances, principal repayments, or intra-group loans. The only practical restrictions are Swiss corporate law balance-sheet maintenance and the 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends. No FX license is required for trade settlements or treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender or conversion ratio exists under Swiss law. Export receipts, including Apple App Store and Google Play payouts to the Swiss AG, may be retained in the original foreign currency (USD/EUR/GBP) in any Swiss bank account, or converted into CHF at the commercial market rate. The SNB requires statistical reporting in the aggregate by banks, but this does not impose a surrender, repatriation, or hedging obligation.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque Nationale Suisse, Banca Nazionale Svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Constrained feasibility. An offshore digital EMI cannot act as the primary clearing house for the Swiss AG unless it is duly licensed by FINMA under the Swiss Payment Services Act (PSA) and is not merely serving Swiss customers through a cross-border digital channel. An EEA EMI may be used as an ancillary treasury/business wallet for platform receipts if the Swiss entity is the customer and the EMI does not conduct a payment business in Switzerland; primary corporate banking and settlement rails must remain with a Swiss licensed bank.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax rate calculated on profit after federal tax (equivalent to 7.83% effective federal rate on pre-tax profit); combined cantonal and communal effective rates typically range from 11% to 21%, with the Canton of Zug approximate all-in effective rate of 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) applies to dividend distributions and deemed distributions to shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must generally be filed within six months after the close of the fiscal year. Administrative extensions are available on request in most cantons, including Zug, but the final filing deadline is typically set by the cantonal tax authority and may not exceed each annual assessment cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax law has no stand-alone statutory TP documentation threshold, but the ESTV requires contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation when a Swiss company is part of an internationally active group and, as an administrative benchmark, when annual gross turnover exceeds CHF 80 million or cross-border related-party transactions are material. A Master File may be required for groups with consolidated global turnover of EUR 750 million or more under OECD BEPS-compliant practice; in any event, the taxpayer must bear the burden of proof for arm's length compliance.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland operates an entity-level taxation system with no general Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE ESR for ordinary operating AGs. However, substance requirements apply through tax treaty entitlement and anti-abuse rules: an MMORPG publishing AG must demonstrate real economic presence in Switzerland, including local strategic management, payroll, banking, and actual operational decision-making. In the absence of substance, passive royalty, dividend, and intercompany income can be recharacterized, denied treaty protection, subjected to Swiss withholding tax on deemed distributions, or classified as tax evasion.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evaded is reclaimed with interest; administrative fine may reach up to three times the evaded tax; repeated or particularly serious evasion triggers criminal prosecution under the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax and cantonal tax laws.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or cause the AG to evade tax may be held personally liable for restitution of evaded tax and penalty amounts. Criminal conviction may include imprisonment for up to three years; serious cases involving forged records may lead to cross-border red notices and extradition proceedings.
Shell Company and Abuse of Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the Swiss entity as fiscally nonexistent, allocate profits to the place of effective management, deny treaty relief on dividends and royalties, impose 35% withholding tax on deemed distributions, and levy penalty interest on underpaid taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to operate without economic substance face public law liability for assessed taxes and withholding obligations. Travel bans may be imposed during international tax investigations, and personal bankruptcy proceedings may follow if the AG cannot satisfy tax debts.
Money Laundering / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Willful money laundering under Article 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment for up to three years; commercial or gang-related laundering carries imprisonment for up to five years plus fines. The Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) may order asset freezes, and the AG may lose banking access.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable if they knew or should have known the source of funds was criminal. Personal sanctions include confiscation of assets, civil damages, and imprisonment. Non-resident directors remain subject to extradition from most jurisdictions for Swiss money laundering offenses.
Constructive Dividend and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax authorities may recharacterize the excess payment as a tax-neutral but dividend-equivalent distribution subject to 35% withholding tax, add retroactive interest, reject deduction of the underlying expense, and impose penalty assessments of up to the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve materially non-arm's-length transactions may face personal liability for withholding tax not collected, and for damages suffered by the AG. In severe cases involving intentional depletion of Swiss tax base, criminal sanctions for qualified tax evasion apply.
