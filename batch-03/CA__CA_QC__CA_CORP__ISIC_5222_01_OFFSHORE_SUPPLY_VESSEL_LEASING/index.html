Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal; Revenu Québec / Gouvernement du Québec - provincial. Portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/business/online-services/).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate: federal (Corporations Canada, Online Filing Centre) or Quebec (Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ)); obtain Articles, registered office in Quebec. 2) Obtain federal Business Number (BN): register via CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) or, for non-resident incorporators, CRA Form RC4601; activate programme accounts RC (income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if employees), RM (CBSA import/export). 3) Register with Revenu Québec via the joint provincial-federal business registration service; obtain Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST account (programme TQ, standard rate 9.975%). 4) Activate maritime prerequisites: Transport Canada vessel registration, classification society certificates, P&I insurance, and CBSA export reporting account—required for zero-rated offshore exports. 5) Commence filing: T2 (corporate income tax), GST/HST returns, QST returns, and Quebec corporate tax (COT/T2) returns per statutory deadlines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope = worldwide income for a Canadian-resident corporation incorporated in Canada (ITA s.250(1)(c)); Quebec corporate income tax at entity level; Tax_Exempt_Zone = not applicable; offshore activities beyond territorial seas do not exempt the resident corporation from federal Part I tax or Quebec tax.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; Canadian federal and Quebec corporate taxpayers cannot elect pass-through. Available statutory elections relevant to Stage 3: (a) ETA s.156 / QST mirror: election by closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration (GST/HST/QST intra-group simplification); (b) ETA ss.227-228 'Quick Method' of accounting for GST/HST/QST for small suppliers (annual taxable supplies ≤ CAD 400,000; QST mirror in Quebec); (c) ITA s.85 rollover election on transfers of property to the corporation (asset incorporation); (d) ITA s.216 election for a non-resident owner of Canadian rental real property (not applicable to vessel charters; retained for completeness); (e) ITA s.115.2-type elective deeming for international shipping operators (verify treaty shipping exemption; reserved for non-resident counterparties rather than the resident chartering entity).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small supplier threshold; ETA s.123(1) 'small supplier' - worldwide taxable supplies of CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four quarters; QST small supplier threshold mirrors at CAD 30,000 under the Quebec Taxation Act. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, but voluntary registration is available to foreign/offshore operators to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI Part V s.10 for a supply (including lease/charter by way of lease, licence or similar arrangement) of an 'offshore drilling unit' or a 'support vessel', including an offshore platform supply vessel, used in offshore exploration or exploitation of petroleum minerals (within or beyond Canadian territorial waters). Parallel relief: Schedule VI Part V s.1 (tangible personal property exported by the supplier) and Part V.1 s.23 (services supplied to a non-resident, subject to exceptions). To qualify the charter must: (i) be of a vessel that meets the statutory definition of a support vessel (Canada Shipping Act registry or recognized flag-State registry); (ii) serve an operator of an offshore drilling unit engaged in offshore exploration/exploitation; (iii) not be a supply consumed in a normal taxable Canadian commercial activity (e.g., domestic coastal logistics); and (iv) be substantiated by transport, voyage and charter documentation. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, GST (5%) and QST (9.975%) apply on the charter consideration, plus penalties/interest.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED)/Automated Commercial Environment export record, including Cargo Control Number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill evidencing foreign destination or offshore installation
  • Transport Canada (Canadian Register of Vessels) or flag-State Certificate of Registry
  • Charter party agreement (Bareboat/Time/Voyage) identifying the vessel, charterer, and offshore unit operator
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 (or bank credit advice) evidencing collection of the export proceeds
  • P&I insurance certificate, ISM/ISPS compliance documents, and cesser/offshore rider evidencing statutory/regulatory fitness of the vessel

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital government clearance in Canada/Quebec for B2B maritime invoices. ETA-registered persons must retain invoices/records in Canada or provide them electronically on demand; invoices must carry prescribed information (ETA s.221(1) and regulations, including BN/GST-HST registration number, date, seller ID, recipient, consideration, and description). Electronic invoices (PDF/EDI/eXML) are accepted without pre-clearance. Quebec has announced (budget roadmap 2023-2024) a phased PEPPOL B2B e-invoicing mandate for QST purposes—expected for large enterprises from 2026-2027—but no operative mandate exists for offshore vessel-leasing supplies. GST/HST returns must be e-filed (GST/HST NETFILE/EDI) by registrants with annual taxable supplies > CAD 1.5 million; T2 corporate returns must be e-filed if gross revenue > CAD 1 million.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/HST/QST within the normal 4-year period (ETA s.298, Quebec Tax Administration Act) and without time limit where the zero-rating claim is based on misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. On clawback, the full 14.975% tax (GST 5% + QST 9.975%) is assessed on the charter consideration, added to daily compound arrears interest at the prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec quarterly rates, and is subject to gross-negligence penalties (ETA s.285; ITA s.163(2)) of 50% of the net tax understated. Criminal tax evasion (ETA s.327; ITA s.239; Quebec Taxation Act mirror) is punishable by fines of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment (up to 5 years on indictment / 2 years less a day summarily). Further clawback exposure: director's liability under ETA s.323 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act for unremitted GST/QST of the corporation; loss of export zero-rating on audit triggers reopening of previous returns within the 4-year window (or longer for negligence); transfer-pricing adjustments may be imposed under ITA Part XVI.1/s.247 where export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day commercial benchmark or are retained as non-arm's-length balances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose prior approval, quota, compulsory repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements for lawful foreign investment, intercompany funding, dividends, interest, royalties, vessel-charter receipts, or loan repayments. Outbound remittances remain subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing support, sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, lender covenants, and bank transaction-monitoring review. Quebec entities must maintain adequate books and records and comply with federal and provincial tax filings. Payments involving designated persons, sanctioned jurisdictions, blocked property, or unusual maritime counterparties may be delayed or refused under Canadian sanctions and financial-crime controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion ratio. Export receipts may generally be retained in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, lender, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Offshore supply-vessel chartering and related maritime services should maintain invoices, charter-party agreements, voyage or service records, proof of performance, customs documentation where applicable, and beneficial-owner information. Foreign-currency balances should be recorded under applicable Canadian accounting standards, with foreign-exchange gains and losses recognized for tax and financial-reporting purposes. Export reporting, customs declarations, and trade controls may apply depending on the goods, equipment, technology, destination, and transaction structure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration and generally has no exchange controls or mandatory currency-conversion authority. FINTRAC administers federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing obligations, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions and insurers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and disbursement; low as a substitute for a Canadian bank account, escrow account, or regulated deposit account. A foreign electronic-money institution does not automatically obtain Canadian authorization merely by serving a Quebec corporation. Payment activities connected to Canada may fall within the Retail Payment Activities Act framework administered by the Bank of Canada, subject to statutory scope and transition rules. A provider may also require authorization or registration in its home jurisdiction and must satisfy sanctions, AML, safeguarding, data, consumer-protection, and correspondent-bank requirements. Offshore EMI balances are not Canadian deposits and may not receive Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation protection. Use of an offshore EMI should be limited to documented payment flows after legal review, with a Canadian bank retained for payroll, tax, escrow, reserve liquidity, and core treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity, shareholder loans, and other funding through an account held in the corporation's legal name at a Canadian commercial bank. Use a regulated correspondent-bank wire, normally SWIFT MT103 or the applicable modern ISO 20022 equivalent, with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, and payment-purpose information. Identify the transaction as equity subscription, paid-in capital, shareholder loan, or other documented funding; do not describe a loan as equity or use generic personal-remittance wording. Before settlement, provide the bank with the subscription or loan agreement, board approval, capitalization table, source-of-funds evidence, originating-bank confirmation, beneficial-owner information, and any foreign-exchange or investment filings required in the sending jurisdiction. Record the receipt in the general ledger and reconcile it to the bank statement and corporate resolutions. There is no universal Canadian statutory FX purpose-code regime; bank-specific purpose fields and any sending-country purpose codes must be used accurately. Verify withholding-tax, thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, debt-equity, and corporate-law consequences before remitting interest, dividends, or principal.Use Canadian bank operating accounts for charter fees, vessel operating expenses, insurance, port charges, suppliers, crew-related costs, taxes, and approved intercompany payments. For ordinary settlements, use a traceable SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 customer-credit-transfer message with invoice number, charter-party reference, vessel or IMO number where relevant, service description, beneficiary details, and accurate sanctions-screening data. For higher-value or performance-dependent transactions, use a commercial documentary letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or bank-controlled escrow, subject to the bank's trade-finance approval and applicable ICC rules, such as UCP 600 or ISP98 where incorporated. Do not use escrow terminology unless the bank has established a legally documented escrow or trust arrangement. Retain invoices, contracts, proof of delivery or service, customs records where applicable, tax support, approval evidence, and payment reconciliations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% general Canadian dividend withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Quebec does not impose a separate dividend tax or ancillary withholding. Canada-Quebec combined effect is expressed federally. Under certain tax treaties, this rate is reduced (commonly 15% for portfolio dividends, 5% for substantial shareholding dividends under the Canada-US DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not statutorily prescribe Local File and Master File in exactly the same OECD three-tier format, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and Form T106 are mandatory when a reporting taxpayer engages in non-arm's length cross-border transactions above CA$1 million in aggregate (for goods) or CA$500,000 in aggregate (for services, interest, royalties, rentals and other transactions, including offshore vessel charter hire) with a single non-resident counterparty. Large CCSF/comprehensive returns require Schedule A when gross revenue exceeds CA$50 million. The documentation threshold applies separately for each non-resident related counterparty and must be prepared before the annual return filing date.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime like the UAE or similar offshore financial centers. Instead, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and Quebec's anti-avoidance provisions (Tax Administration Act art. 1039) apply: any corporate structure or related-party arrangement formed to obtain a tax benefit while lacking genuine business purpose, business risk, or real operation in Quebec may be recharacterized, contractual substance disregarded, and the tax benefit denied. For offshore supply vessel leasing, CRA and Revenu Québec analyze vessel employment, crew management, charter negotiation, invoicing, funding, and executive decision-making; a Canadian-registered/Quebec-registered entity with bare title but no operational command is exposed to sham or conduit treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Misstatement of Income

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239, conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years on a first offence and up to 5 years on a subsequent offence. Civil exposure includes full reassessment, compound interest, and a gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated federal tax under s.163(2), with Quebec penalties imposed under ss.157 to 163 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns or directed concealment may be prosecuted as participants and, upon conviction, face imprisonment, substantial criminal fines, restitution orders, and prohibitions against acting as a director. CRA may also assert director's liability under ITA s.227.1 for unpaid source deductions and unremitted amounts within the corporation.
General Anti-Avoidance Rule / Shell Company or Artificial Entity Abuse

Statutory sanction: The benefit is denied under federal ITA s.245 and Quebec Tax Administration Act art. 1039; reassessments add tax, interest, and, for a notifiable transaction, a 25% GAAR penalty based on the tax benefit denied. The failure to disclose a notifiable transaction also triggers party penalties and documentation sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who derived the benefit may be jointly and severally liable for the tax avoided if they were active parties in the scheme; no corporate-veil protection attaches to a sham. Severe cases can lead to criminal veil-piercing under fraudulent conveyance statutes and removal of limited-liability protection.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA will recharacterize or adjust the transaction under ITA s.247. Where documents were not provided, the taxpayer is liable to a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the total net adjustment, subject to statutory minimums, plus daily compound interest. Separate penalties apply for missing T106 ($50 per day to a $2,500 maximum) and for incomplete/misleading information, plus Quebec equivalents where provincial adjustments are made.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the T2 and T106 while knowing that documentation was false or incomplete may be held personally accountable in a civil penalty proceeding and exposed to a gross negligence penalty where the director prepared or participated in fraudulent records; the director is not protected by reliance solely on a grossly negligent advisor.
AML / Ultimate Beneficial Ownership / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC administrative penalties can reach CA$1 million for a corporation and CA$500,000 for an individual; under Canada's economic sanctions statutes, sanctions evasion can yield fines up to CA$25 million and imprisonment up to 10 years in serious cases. False UBO declarations can lead to deregistration of the corporation, fine, and criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for administrative sanctions if they caused the corporation's compliance system to fail; knowing participation in sanctions evasion or money laundering exposes directors to prosecution, judicial freezing of assets, travel bans, and imprisonment. Directors of a licensed maritime operator also owe a non-delegable duty to know the client's identity, vessel use, and funding source.
