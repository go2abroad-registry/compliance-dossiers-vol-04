Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and Income Tax Act compliance; Revenu Québec for the Québec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec provincial corporate and partnership income tax, and payroll source deductions. Under the Canada-Québec tax administration agreement, Revenu Québec administers GST/HST for businesses established in Quebec. Portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Revenu Québec Clic Revenu and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ registry).

E-government filing portal

(1) Apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC4022 for non-residents; the BN is the federal TIN and anchors Program Accounts RT (GST/HST) and RC (income tax). (2) Register the limited partnership (société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ (Quebec enterprise number), which is mandatory for carrying on business in Quebec. (3) Open the QST account through Revenu Québec Clic Revenu using the NEQ; the QST registration number is the NEQ with a program identifier such as TQ. (4) A non-resident LP without a Canadian establishment must appoint a resident mandatary (fiscal representative) for service of process and may be required to post security under ETA s. 211 and the QST Act. (5) File annual federal T5013 and Quebec TP-61 partnership information returns; each non-resident partner must disclose its home-jurisdiction TIN and is taxable on its Quebec-source partnership share, and distributions to non-resident partners may trigger Part XIII withholding and Quebec equivalents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada or Quebec; classification is statutory. The CA_LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (partners are taxed; the LP files information returns only) but is a separate registrant person for GST/HST/QST. Statutory elections include: ITA s. 97(2) contribution rollover, ITA s. 96(3) partnership continuation and fiscal-period elections, ITA s. 98(1)/98(2) partnership cessation and disposition rollovers, ITA s. 22 receivables transfer election, and ETA ss. 156-157 and s. 273 elections for closely related persons and joint ventures. Hybrid mismatch rules (BEPS Action 2) are not yet in force in Canada; the current framework counters mismatch outcomes through CFC and foreign-affiliate rules, transfer-pricing rules, and the proposed excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold measured over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA ss. 123(1) and 240, and the equivalent QST registration threshold). Once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, mandatory registration applies; a non-resident LP carrying on business in Quebec through an establishment, inventory or agent must register, and voluntary registration is available to recover input tax refunds. No special threshold applies to medical device exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property, including polyethersulfone hollow-fiber dialyzers and hemodialysis hemofilter kits, is zero-rated under ETA s. 22 and the corresponding QST provision where (a) the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada, or (b) possession or ownership is transferred to a non-resident person who does not acquire the goods for consumption, use or supply in Canada, and in either case the goods are exported as soon as a reasonable time after the supply without further processing, fabrication or transformation in Canada. Services supplied in respect of exported goods (ETA s. 23) and certain services to non-residents (ETA s. 9) are also zero-rated. The manufacturer reports the 0% rate on its GST/HST and QST returns and thereby recovers embedded GST/QST through input tax credits and refunds. A Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under the Medical Devices Regulations (SOR/98-282) is a statutory precondition to lawful manufacture and export, and the goods must be declared through the Canadian Export Reporting System with the correct HS classification for dialysis equipment.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent SWIFT MT103 / TT wire confirmation from the receiving bank) evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds from the named buyer

Invoices must satisfy ETA ss. 221-223 and corresponding QST rules, including separate disclosure of GST/HST and QST, the supplier's BN and QST registration number (NEQ), date, description of goods, consideration, and tax amounts. Voluntary interoperability is available under the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework; Revenu Québec expects records and invoices to be available in French where the supply is made in Quebec.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If export documentation is false, incomplete or not retained for the statutory six-year period, the 0% rate is denied: GST/HST and QST are reassessed at the combined 14.975% rate, any input tax credits or refunds already paid are clawed back with statutory interest, and the normal four-year reassessment period is suspended for misrepresentation or fraud. The gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280.1 is the greater of CAD 2,500 and 25% of the excess refund or credit claimed; intentional evasion under ETA s. 327 carries fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. General partners and directors are jointly and severally liable for remittance defaults, and Revenu Québec may impose equivalent penalties, interest and recovery measures under the QST Act and the Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions; there are no general surrender requirements, exchange controls, or capital transfer restrictions. Profit remittance is subject only to statutory withholding tax rates under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII 25% or treaty-reduced rates), and no special central bank authorization paraphernalia exists for dividends, repurchase of partnership units, or liquidation distributions. Beneficial ownership transparency filings under the Canada Business Corporations Act and provincial equivalent in Quebec are required, but these are not currency restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Canada. Canadian exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely. However, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, reporting thresholds apply for large international wire transfers (e.g., $10,000 CAD ETNs), and the Canada Border Services Agency has record-keeping obligations, but no forced conversion to CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) acting as monetary authority; exchange controls are administered under the Currency Act, the Canadian Payments Act, and FINTRAC supervision, with OSFI licensed banking institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are permissible as treasury middleware but cannot serve as a sole banking sanctuary in Quebec for a medical-device manufacturer. They are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; they typically hold customer funds in pooled segregation with Canadian schedule I banks. High feasibility for FX conversion, fee-efficient trade disbursements, and virtual IBAN reception, but legal constraints include reduced client protection, no CDIC deposit insurance, KYC/AML obligations that may not satisfy Canadian source-of-funds audits, and the inability to issue commercial LCs or provide credit lines. For LPs in Quebec, reliance on unregulated EMIs may raise substance and control queries with Revenu Québec; prudent use is limited to pass-through payroll or disbursement rails, while core corporate banking and capital escrow must reside with a bank licensed in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (LP capital calls, partner contributions, or group treasury transfer) must be made via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire/CHIPS transfer to the Canadian depository. The purpose code must precisely describe the underlying transaction: e.g., 'BQ-Banking Direct Investment (Equity or LP Contribution)' or 'BR-Business Trade' for purchase of medical raw materials; for capital contributions, use code 112 (Equity capital) or 114 (Capital in LP units) depending on the bank's format. Include full legal name and Quebec registration number of the LP as the beneficiary, and have the remitter provide its LEI (if applicable). Revenu Québec may require a capital contribution journal entry and a CR-1 or corporate tax schedule; but from a clearing perspective, banks will verify source-of-funds narratives and may request a copy of the capital call letter, partnership agreement, and board resolution. Incoming wires denominated in USD or EUR will be converted upon booking to CAD or held in a USD clearing account if a currency account package is chosen.Trade disbursements for dialysis filter raw materials (e.g., hollow-fiber polyethersulfone, PU potting resin, medical-grade packaging) should be cleared by SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments and by documentary commercial letters of credit (irrevocable at sight) for high-volume import contracts. For MT103, the ordering customer must use the bank's required purpose code (e.g., 'ST-International Trade / Medical Supplies') and attach the underlying proforma invoice. The LC should include a 60- to 90-day tenor with a confirmed payable clause for Taiwanese or European resin suppliers; compliance of documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bills of lading, certificate of analysis) must be monitored via the bank's trade services department. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are permissible as treasury middleware but cannot serve as a sole banking sanctuary in Quebec for a medical-device manufacturer. They are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; they typically hold customer funds in pooled segregation with Canadian schedule I banks. High feasibility for FX conversion, fee-efficient trade disbursements, and virtual IBAN reception, but legal constraints include reduced client protection, no CDIC deposit insurance, KYC/AML obligations that may not satisfy Canadian source-of-funds audits, and the inability to issue commercial LCs or provide credit lines. For LPs in Quebec, reliance on unregulated EMIs may raise substance and control queries with Revenu Québec; prudent use is limited to pass-through payroll or disbursement rails, while core corporate banking and capital escrow must reside with a bank licensed in Canada.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined maximum (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%) for a corporate partner of the limited partnership; the LP itself is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96(1) and s. 107/115.1 of the QTA.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII WHT on Canadian dividends paid to a non-resident (ITA s. 212(2)); LP profit allocations are not 'dividends' and are not subject to Part XIII, but the non-resident corporate partner's Canadian business income may be exposed to the 25% Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings (treaty-reduced); distributions in kind of inventory or IP could be deemed to be a disposition at FMV.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (CRA) must be filed for reportable transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate during the taxation year; Quebec requires parallel disclosure on CO-17 for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions above the same threshold; Master File (Form RC4646) applies to groups with global gross revenue over CAD 1.1 billion; contemporaneous Local-File-style documentation (org charts, comparables, profit-level indicators) must be produced within 3 months of the CRA's written request; otherwise a 10% transfer pricing penalty under ITA s. 247(4) applies to the net adjustment.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated ESR statute in Canada, but the GAAR (ITA s. 245) and judicial compelling business-purpose doctrine require the LP to have genuine commercial activity: Irish-style hollow-fiber polyethersulfone dialyzer production, premises, equipment, staff, and operating risk must be present in Quebec; absence of substance permits the CRA to (i) ignore the LP under the 'bare trust/agency' doctrine, (ii) reallocate profit to the true economic owner, and (iii) impose a 25% GAAR penalty; tax treaties further require the limiting of benefits to partners that are resident and have real business activity (PE threshold).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty: 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2) and QTA s. 1059); repeat misrepresentation penalty: 50%; criminal conviction: fine of 50%-200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years (ITA s. 239(1)); the CRA also denies deductions for fines and penalties paid (ITA s. 67.6).

Director personal liability: Directors and signatory partners are jointly and severally assessed for the corporation/partnership's unpaid taxes, penalties and interest under ITA s. 227.1(1) and director liability under QTA ss. 24 and 1049; CRA can order seizure of personal assets, a travel ban through a judicial order, and a restitution order for Quebec's Minister of Revenue.
