Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario provincial component of HST, administered by the CRA under the harmonized tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Ontario Limited Partnership does not receive a separate corporate TIN; the Canadian business tax identifier is the 9-digit Business Number (BN). The non-resident general partner or authorized representative must complete CRA registration through Business Registration Online (BRO) or submit Form RC1, and may open a GST/HST account, payroll account, and import/export account under the same BN. The Ontario LP must also complete any applicable extra-provincial limited partnership registration with the Government of Ontario's Companies and Personal Property Security Branch. Written evidence of the LP agreement, confirmation of Ontario extra-provincial registration, and partner identification are required. CRA will issue a BN/GST/HST registration confirmation letter; there is no separate VAT-ID document.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. For income tax, the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; the partnership files information return T5013 and partners report their allocated income/loss. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is generally a separate registrable person. Available elections include the joint GST/HST election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for qualifying closely related registrants (nil-consideration treatment of intercompany supplies), and partnership allocations may be made by the limited partnership agreement. Hybrid entity mismatch provisions under the enacted hybrid mismatch rules and the Income Tax Application Rules may deny benefits where jurisdictionally inconsistent tax treatment exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies made in Canada per calendar quarter or over twelve consecutive months, whichever determination makes the person no longer a small supplier; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods may be zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act where the goods are physically exported in accordance with the conditions in Schedule VI, Part V, generally requiring export to occur within a prescribed period and proof of export. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 or related provisions when the supply is for use wholly outside Canada, does not relate to real property located in Canada, and the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST. For audited supply-chain compliance services, zero-rating requires the contractual service to be genuinely performed for a non-resident beneficiary and not consumed in Canada.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A and electronic CADEX/ACI e-manifest data)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document evidencing export out of Canada
  • Commercial invoice describing goods or services and showing zero-rated export treatment
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT credit statement or bank transfer confirmation; Canada does not issue a formal inward remittance certificate)
  • Destination and foreign buyer documentation, including HS codes and consignee details
  • Export accounting records and reconciliation to the GST/HST return and ITC claims

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction controls. Electronic invoicing is voluntary; CRA publishes the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and supports the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. Registrants above mandatory electronic filing thresholds must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through an EDI/software service. Books and records, including export documentation and electronic invoices, must be retained in a readable and accessible format and provided to CRA upon audit. No API-based clearance stamp is required.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and claw back related input tax credits if documentary evidence is insufficient, untimely, or inconsistent. False or negligent export claims attract interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, civil penalties for false statements or omissions under section 285, gross negligence penalties under section 327(6), revocation of registration, and criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. CBSA may also issue non-compliance penalties and assess forfeitures under customs legislation. Retroactive reassessment periods are generally four years from the date of assessment, but may be unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with no separate United States-style exchange control statutes. Both current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, royalties) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio capital, repatriation) are freely permitted without prior Bank of Canada approval. Profit remittance for a non-resident partner in an Ontario Limited Partnership is unrestricted upon satisfaction of anti-money-laundering verification, tax withholding obligations under the Canada-Ontario taxation framework, and the completion of T106 information returns for non-resident partners. Any amount may be converted to CAD or foreign currency on the open interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency, held offshore, or repatriated at the discretion of the entity, provided that the related transactions are not subject to suspicion-based reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Standard recordkeeping obligations require retention of all underlying customs, invoice, and wire transfer documents for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction monitoring and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs-adjacent trade controls; no dedicated foreign exchange administration body exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict boundaries. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating without any Canadian regulatory footprint generally cannot clear CAD-denominated payments into the domestic Canadian payment ecosystem without using an intermediary Canadian bank partner. Legitimate EMIs that hold a money-services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC (or are a registered foreign MSB with nominal presence) and are also licensed in a CPC-0 equivalent jurisdiction may support inbound/outbound wires and FX settlement for the audit firm's supply-chain payment flows. Regardless of EMI suitability, all Canadian-dollar funding and disbursement rails must ultimately touch a Canadian-chartered bank or a registered Canadian clearing agent for the purpose of Beneficial Ownership reporting under PCMLTFA. Therefore, the LP should maintain one primary Canadian bank account as the anchor rail, with an EMI used only for low-value subcontractor remittances and non-resident supplier reimbursements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Entity classification as a Limited Partnership with non-resident limited partners

Defense evidence: Provide: (1) notarized certificate of limited partnership from Ontario registry; (2) the full partnership agreement with capital contribution schedule; (3) a Limited Partner register identifying each partner's name, jurisdiction, and control status; (4) declaration that all partners are compliant with FINTRAC recordkeeping; (5) T106 Information Return for Non-Resident Partners or approved exemption rationale; (6) ultimate beneficial ownership table for any corporate partners.
Customs AEO / supply-chain audit scope may be misclassified as high-risk trade sector requiring trade sanction screening

Root cause: Customs AEO / supply-chain audit scope may be misclassified as high-risk trade sector requiring trade sanction screening

Defense evidence: Submit: (1) dated client engagement letters showing audit-only advisory scope, with no freight forwarding, customs brokerage, or physical goods movement; (2) CBSA AEO self-assessment certificate or proof of active application; (3) cybersecurity and Data Processing Agreement (DPA) confirming audited client data is held and destroyed in compliance with PIPEDA; (4) a negative sanctions match report from authoritative screening lists (OSFI, FINTRAC, UN, global PEP lists).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For non-resident partner capital contributions, the routing should use SWIFT MT103 to the partnership's Tier-1 account with the following clearing and reference protocol: SWIFT Instruction Code: CRED (credit reporting) with Consolidated FB (statement narrative) containing the Ontario Registration Number and entity legal name; CAD remittance purpose codes per the Canadian Financial Transactions Classification should reference T545 (wire transfer for partnership capital account), T001 (trade in services), or T502 (investment income redistribution), though Canada does not mandate a sector-specific mandatory purpose code; FINTRAC requires that the originating bank's details and complete Beneficial Ownership information be captured for any transfer exceeding CAD $3,000. To mitigate cross-border friction, first route the contribution via a Canadian clearing bank's foreign correspondent, then execute internal domestic clearing through the Canadian High Value Clearing and Settlement System (HVCS). Continuous Linked Settlement is not available for CAD in this flow; use traditional correspondent banking.For trade settlements and supply-chain audit supplier payments, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 via the commercial bank's cross-border payment channel. For CAD domestic payments to enrolled vendors, use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) via Payment Canada, or Real-Time Rail (RTR) if instant final payment is required. For USD-denominated settlement to overseas suppliers, the recommended methodology is either: (1) SWIFT MT103 through the bank's USD correspondent network (potentially via an intermediary like The Bank of New York Mellon or JPMorgan); or (2) a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued under UCP600 for payments above CAD $50,000 that are contingent on presentation of Canadian customs documents, security audit fulfilment certificates, and bills of lading. Strike-off trigger

Turnover: No universal statutory audit is imposed on an Ontario limited partnership merely because it is an LP.
