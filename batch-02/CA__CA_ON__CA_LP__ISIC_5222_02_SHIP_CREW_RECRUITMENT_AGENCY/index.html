Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), Ottawa; registration and filing portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario-specific authorities: Ontario Business Registry (extra-provincial LP registration), Ontario Ministry of Finance (Employer Health Tax and Ontario reinvestment rules).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership must first register the LP under Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16) through the Ontario Business Registry at www.ontario.ca/business-registry. Subsequently, the LP obtains a 15-character CRA Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or by registering online through BRO. For GST/HST, the program account code 'RT' is issued; for payroll, 'RP'; for excise, 'RE'. Non-resident partners are not issued separate BN accounts; the LP files NR4 statements of amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners, and the non-resident partner must file CRA Form T1040-M or T2121-M for Canadian-source business income allocated through the fiscally transparent LP. Where the LP expects to charge HST, the GST/HST registration must be effective before the first taxable supply; otherwise the small supplier threshold (CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, including each partner's share) applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). The LP itself is not a taxpayer; income, gains, credits, and liabilities flow through to partners. A non-resident partner is taxable in Canada only on the LP's Canadian-source business income computed as if the LP were a separate person, subject to permanent establishment rules under the Canada-US Tax Treaty and Part XIV S.115 attribution mechanics. For Ontario purposes, partners resident in Ontario are subject to Ontario income tax on their distributive share; the LP is not subject to entity-level Ontario income tax except the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act annual registration fee and potential 'specified partner' EHT exposure.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Federal Canadian tax law; entity classification is determined by statutory fact and partnership agreement. The LP may elect under Section 102(2) of the Income Tax Act to be excluded from Canadian partnership status for specific federal purposes where all partners are not resident in Canada. For GST/HST, the LP may file an election under Section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to designate one partner as operator of a GST/HST joint venture if the crew-agency cash flows qualify. Canada has not enacted comprehensive OECD Action-2 hybrid mismatch matching rules; however, CRA may apply the foreign investment entity provisions (Sections 94.1-94.3) and non-resident trust rules to prevent inorganic deferral, plus treaty Limitation on Benefits clauses in the Canada-US context.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, Sections 148-149 of the Excise Tax Act). The LP's threshold is aggregated with all partners' revenue for the same fiscal period, including non-resident partners' offshore supplies, unless the non-resident supply is zero-rated. Once the threshold is exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies, with an effective date retroactive to the date of the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated HST at 0% applies (a) to a supply of goods exported from Canada pursuant to Section 6 of Schedule IX, Excise Tax Act; (b) to a supply of services made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and where the service is not in connection with (i) Canadian real property, (ii) tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply, or (iii) a service rendered to a Canadian carrier (ss.7(1)(a)); and (c) to a supply of a transportation service by sea that begins or ends in Canada to a non-resident shipper if the destination is outside Canada. For maritime crew recruitment (ISIC 5222), the crew-agency fee will be zero-rated if the contracting shipowner is a non-resident, the crewed vessel is registered outside Canada, and no element of the service relates to Canadian real property or goods. The agency must retain evidence of the non-resident status of the customer (foreign corporate registration, vessel flag certificate, IMO number, Master's power of attorney) to sustain the zero-rate.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED export report) only if the supply includes physical goods such as spare equipment; otherwise, export declaration N/A for pure offshore crew services
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Vessel Manifest documenting the vessel's international voyage and the non-resident consignee
  • Crew List (Form FAL-5, IMO Standard) signed by the Master, evidencing onboard placement of seafarers on the foreign-flag vessel
  • Crew agency service agreement and Articles of Agreement / Sea Service Book detailing the foreign shipowner as recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 proof of wire receipt of gross crew-agency fees in CAD, USD, or EUR from the non-resident shipowner, corroborated by the foreign exchange record of the Ontario LP
  • GST/HST zero-rating declaration signed by the agency and Form GST189 (General Application for GST/HST Rebate) if input credits are later claimed in relation to the zero-rated supply

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API stamping, or digital stamp mandate; GST/HST compliance is return-based. Registrants with gross annual revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or API. Voluntary continuous transaction controls exist only for PEPPOL-compliant B2B e-invoicing (interoperability with CRA's Network of Providers); CRA accepts original invoices in PDF/XML/EDI format. All invoicing must support the mandatory information requirements of Section 169 and Section 176 (invoice contents, tax number, HST amount, customer identity), but there is no pre-authorization by the fiscal authority for each invoice.

CRA may reassess zero-rated input tax credits for up to four years from the original return date (up to 10 years where misrepresentation or negligence is shown). Fraudulent or grossly negligent zero-rating substantiation attracts a 50% gross-negligence penalty under Section 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act, plus interest under Section 280 (50% of net tax owed). Retrospective clawback of GST/HST input tax credits previously claimed is made under Section 232.01, and unremitted HST on wrongly-zero-rated fees is assessable at 13% with daily compounding interest. Criminal prosecution under Sections 327 and 328 of the Excise Tax Act applies to wilful evasion, including false documentation, forging crew lists, or creating fictitious deep-sea vessel charters; the CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program does not shield an LP after formal audit notification, and tax shelter penalties under Section 237.1 may apply to marketed crew-crewing tax-aggregation schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no administrative approval regime for inward or outward remittances. The Canadian dollar floats freely; the Bank of Canada does not restrict settlement or conversion. Profit distributions to non-resident limited partners of an Ontario LP are permissible, subject to Part I tax on the non-resident's Canadian-source business income share and, where allocations are passive or guaranteed (e.g., interest or management-fee type), Part XIII withholding at 25%, reduced to 15%, 5%, or 0% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980), as amended, or other applicable treaties. Cross-border crew recruitment services supplied to non-resident shipowners are generally zero-rated for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act, which materially simplifies cash-flow mismatch and refund mechanics. There are no exchange-control licenses, conversion caps, or documentation requirements for foreign investors injecting capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds or offshore service receipts. A licensed crew recruitment agency may freely retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. Statutory reporting obligations nonetheless apply: (i) cross-border currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and are reportable to FINTRAC; (ii) international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; (iii) enterprises with annual international services transactions exceeding CAD 500,000 must file Statistics Canada's Quarterly Survey of International Transactions in Services; and (iv) foreign currency balances and other specified foreign property held outside Canada in excess of CAD 100,000 must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) on Form T1135.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), is the sole issuer of Bank of Canada notes and manager of the national payment and settlement infrastructure (Lynx, ACSS, Real-Time Rail). The Minister of Finance, Department of Finance Canada, retains statutory authority over the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). No separate foreign exchange control administration exists; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces cross-border movement reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - A foreign digital EMI operating without a Canadian entity cannot legally open an account for an Ontario LP. A FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) licensed in Canada (e.g., Wise Payments Canada Inc., Convera Canada) may onboard the LP for supplementary multi-currency payout rails, provided client funds are held in a trust account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Legal constraints: EMIs are not CDIC-insured, lack direct Lynx/LVTS settlement access, and must conduct independent KYC with FINTRAC; their use is permitted only as a secondary disbursement channel for high-volume, low-value seafarer payroll, never as the primary treasury concentration or FX execution venue.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: non-resident limited partners send SWIFT MT103 wires (or FEDWIRE converted to SWIFT) to the LP's account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Field 50K identifies the remitting partner; Field 57A specifies the Canadian beneficiary bank's correspondent; Field 59 names the LP with full account number and Ontario address; Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - AGREEMENT DATED [YYYY-MM-DD]'; Field 71A should be 'OUR' to credit the full amount. CAD-denominated inflows settle through Lynx (Payments Canada large-value system) into the bank's master account at the Bank of Canada; USD-denominated inflows settle via nostro accounts and are converted with the bank's standing FX instruction. For inflows equal to or above CAD 250,000, the bank's compliance desk will require the signed contribution agreement, partner resolutions, and evidence of source of funds. Canada does not mandate a statutory SWIFT purpose code; the bank will internally classify the flow as 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital - Inward' for Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting. FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to wires of CAD 10,000 or more. Loans from non-resident partners must bear arm's-length interest to avoid deemed-interest rules under section 17 of the Income Tax Act; loans with no interest will trigger imputed income to the LP.Clearing rails: (i) Cross-border vendor and seafarer payroll payments via SWIFT MT103, settled through nostro accounts (CAD via Lynx; USD via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's U.S. correspondent; EUR via TARGET2), with full beneficiary name, IBAN/account, and beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC; (ii) Domestic Canadian crew payroll via Payments Canada ACSS batch EFT credit (direct deposit) using zero-value-date settlement, or same-day intraday value via Lynx; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600, SWIFT MT700/MT707) and standby letters of credit (ISP98) for high-value crew-provision procurement, bunker fuel purchases, and vessel agency services where bank recourse is required; (iv) Documentary collections (URC522) for low-risk invoicing to established shipowners; (v) Multi-currency payroll disbursement to seafarer beneficiaries in the Philippines, India, Ukraine, and the EU routed through the bank's MT103 cross-border service or a licensed FINTRAC-registered fintech payout rail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general federal (15%) and Ontario (11.5%) corporate income tax rate; a Limited Partnership is a fiscally transparent pass-through entity, so no entity-level income tax is assessed and each partner is taxed on its allocable share at its own marginal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII rate under ITA s. 212(2). Because the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent, no Part XIII 'dividend' arises on ordinary profit distributions by the LP; however, allocations of Canadian-source profits to non-resident limited partners attract a statutory 25% withholding under ITA s. 194(1), treatable as a proxy for the Part XIII rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / contemporaneous documentation is mandatory where the total value of controlled transactions of the LP with non-resident non-arm's-length persons equals or exceeds C$5,000,000 for controlled transactions involving goods or C$1,000,000 for controlled transactions involving services, intangibles, royalties, or interest (per CRA TP documentation regime for taxation years beginning on or after 1 January 2022). A Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated group revenue in the preceding year is C$1,000,000,000 or more. Documentation must be completed and available at the filing due date to obtain penalty relief.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; economic substance is nevertheless enforced through: (i) ITA s. 245 GAAR — an Ontario LP formed for the dominant purpose of obtaining a tax benefit, without a bona fide business purpose or economic substance (personnel, office, client contracts and bank accounts), may be recharacterized; (ii) common-law partnership tests from Continental Bank (Leasing) Corp. requiring a genuine business carried on 'in common'; (iii) PE-attribution doctrine — the LP's Ontario office crew-management activities are attributed to every partner, and each non-resident partner claiming treaty relief must prove its share of the Canadian operation contains real economic substance (staff, cost, risk and assets) to retain Canadian PE profits and avoid artificial allocation of profit to offshore entities; (iv) Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act registration and annual maintenance impose ongoing obligations, and the CRA will disregard an LP's separate identity where it is merely a shell/facade.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation (ITA s. 239)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment: a fine of 100%-200% and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA will assess unreported income plus statutory interest, gross-negligence penalties under s. 163(2) (up to 50% of understated tax), and may freeze and garnish the LP's bank accounts without prior notice.

Director personal liability: The general partner (individual or entity) and every director/officer of a corporate general partner who authorized the evasion are joint and severally liable for the tax, penalties and interest under ITA s. 227.1 and can be restrained from leaving Canada (bail condition) where criminal charges are laid; conviction triggers criminal records and disqualification from holding directorships.
