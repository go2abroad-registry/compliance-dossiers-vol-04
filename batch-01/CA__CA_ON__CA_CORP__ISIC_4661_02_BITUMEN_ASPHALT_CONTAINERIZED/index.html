Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial-only taxes. Federal and Ontario corporate income tax administration is centralized with the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign business corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT0001 suffix) by applying through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1A, 'Request for a Business Number from a Non-Resident,' together with the corporation's constitutive documents and a designated agent or representative in Canada if no branch records are maintained. The BN is also used for Ontario corporate tax assessments; no separate Ontario corporation tax ID is required.

The BN is also used for Ontario corporate tax assessments; no separate Ontario corporation tax ID is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. A Canadian business corporation is automatically a separate taxpayer. Statutory elections affect only taxable income or GST/HST mechanics, including Income Tax Act s.85 rollovers, s.216 non-resident rental income net-basis election, s.217 non-resident employment income election, and Excise Tax Act s.156 election for closely related registrants to disregard supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically under Canada's integrity provisions in accordance with BEPS Action 2 and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or a single calendar quarter); non-resident small suppliers are subject to the same threshold unless they are carrying on a qualified commercial activity and have voluntarily registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA s.143 and Schedule VI, Part V when tangible personal property is supplied by a registrant to a non-resident and is exported from Canada as soon as is reasonably practicable after the supply (e.g., containerized drummed or bagged bitumen). The exporter must retain commercial and transport documents proving that the goods left Canada and that title transferred to a non-resident. Services supplied to non-residents for offshore logistics may be zero-rated unless the service is performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada and not subsequently exported in compliance with the ETA's timing requirements.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A, ACI eManifest, or equivalent EDI export conveyance) for containerized cargo
  • Original bill of lading or multimodal waybill evidencing Canadian port of loading and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the exact HS/product code and container numbers
  • Non-resident purchaser identity evidence (tax registration certificate, attestation, or treaty certificate)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/TT wire advice evidencing cross-border payment and foreign currency repatriation
  • CRA or customs export permits/cover notes as required by the Export Control List (not applicable to bitumen/wholesale asphalt)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or CRA pre-approval system for e-invoices. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically in XML, EDI, or PDF provided the statutory content required by ETA s.3 of Schedule VI is present. Ontario does not impose a separate e-invoicing mandate; Quebec's phased B2B e-invoicing regulations do not apply to Ontario.

The CRA may reassess and claw back the 13% HST if zero-rating is claimed without sufficient export documentation, and such clawback carries statutory interest from the original assessment. Knowingly or grossly negligent false statements may attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded under ETA s.285. Wilful evasion is a criminal offence under ETA s.327 punishable by fines from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also cancel input tax credit refunds and revoke the registrant's zero-rating capacity for serial deficiencies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible across both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control approval regimes, no profit-remittance licensing requirements, and no prior central-bank authorization for inbound equity investment or outbound dividend flows. Canadian banks routinely process CAD, USD, EUR, CNY, and commodity-linked currencies. Profit remittances are subject only to CRA Part XIII dividend withholding tax of 25% by statute, typically reduced to 15% (general) or 5% (substantial corporate shareholding, generally at least 10% voting power) under an applicable tax treaty; capital contributions and loan principal repayments are not taxable and face no remittance impediment. Material inbound direct investments are separately reportable to Statistics Canada under the Statistics Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or repatriation requirement on export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign-currency balances offshore or onshore without conversion restrictions, and no minimum conversion ratio is prescribed. Obligations are limited to: (i) a CBSA B13A Export Declaration for goods departing Canada above the CAD 2,000 threshold; (ii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer reporting for every international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) ongoing export trade statistical reporting to Statistics Canada through the monthly survey. No central-bank approval or export credit collateral requirement applies to drummed or bagged bitumen exports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); cross-border banking supervision is exercised under the Bank Act by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML registration and transaction reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Part XIII withholding on outbound dividends is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury or escrow rail; Medium for niche cross-border payment arbitrage. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business) are not authorized banks under the Bank Act (Canada), cannot accept deposits for foreign clients in the manner of a Schedule I bank, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot underwrite FX forwards, NDFs, or commercial letters of credit. They must be FINTRAC-registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and, where applicable, registered or exempted under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Because this entity must receive capital escrow funds and issue SWIFT-grade documentary credits, a tier-1 Schedule I bank is mandatory; EMIs are permissible only as a supplementary collection conduit with a capped monthly throughput.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing uses SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV (cover payment) sent to the receiving Schedule I bank. The remittance information (field 70) must contain a clear purpose, e.g., 'Equity Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan - Unpaid Share Capital', and the interbank settlement leg must be denominated in CAD or USD per the bank's merchant relationship. The receiving Canadian bank performs the PCMLTFA source-of-funds check and confirms whether a Statistics Canada direct investment report is required. Once received, funds are cleared domestically through LVTS/Lynx or internal settlement into the Operational Account. If capital is contributed as paid-in capital, no withholding tax is levied; if structured as a shareholder loan, any interest flow back will attract CRA Part XIII withholding. A CRA account registration (BN/RP) and a directors' resolution authorizing the account and borrowings must be submitted at account opening.Primary clearing rails for trade settlement: SWIFT MT103 T/T payments executed against supplier invoices, and commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) issued by the Canadian bank for bulk bitumen and containerized cargo purchases in excess of CAD 100,000. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined statutory rate (Federal Part I general rate 15% + Ontario general corporate income tax rate 11.5%); reduced to 12.2% combined (Federal 9% + Ontario 3.2%) for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income per associated group.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada, absent treaty relief; illustrative treaty rates: Canada–United States DTA – 5% for beneficial owner corporations holding at least 10% voting power, otherwise 15%; Canada–China DTA – 10% generally, 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10%; Canada–United Kingdom DTA – 5% for qualifying corporate holdings, otherwise 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A T106 information return is mandatory when the aggregate annual value of reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation consistent with OECD Chapters I–V and CRA Information Circular 87-2R is required for penalty protection regardless of revenue size; the ITA s. 247(3) transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies only where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4649) and notification (Form RC4651) are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 853,000,000 (EUR 750,000,000 equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE; however, substance is tested through the GAAR under ITA s. 245, the rejected-transaction doctrine, corporate residence based on central management and control, treaty beneficial-ownership and limitation-on-benefits clauses (including the Canada–US LOB), and the OECD MLI principal purpose test. For a bitumen wholesale trading entity, CRA will expect a physical office, locally employed management and traders, genuine procurement and logistics decision-making in Canada, and bank accounts through which commercial substance flows; a low-substance booking vehicle will be recharacterized and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to two years less a day; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment without limitation period, plus a s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or participate in the evasion are personally charged as parties to the offence; CRA may obtain civil judgments, freeze bank accounts, and pursue personal assets; criminal conviction results in imprisonment, a criminal record, and prohibition from acting as a director.
