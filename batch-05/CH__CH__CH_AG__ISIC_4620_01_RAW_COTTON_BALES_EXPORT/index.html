Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration ESTV/AFC (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, Administration fédérale des contributions); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch and taxpayer portal: www.estv.admin.ch/kundenportal

E-government filing portal

An Aktiengesellschaft is first entered in the Commercial Register, which automatically issues the Swiss Federal Statistical Office operating company number UID/IDE/IDI; the UID also serves as the VAT identification number for Swiss VAT purposes. For indirect tax registration, the AG must submit the VAT registration application to the Federal Tax Administration through the E-Services VAT portal, stating the commencement date of taxable activity in Switzerland. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office or permanent establishment that becomes liable for Swiss VAT must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative and file a special registration form with the ESTV VAT division. The VAT registration certificate is issued after validation of the UID and business address.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; AG is a separate legal taxpayer, subject to full unlimited tax liability on worldwide income for Swiss tax purposes, with deviations for foreign permanent establishments and foreign land; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions; no pass-through treatment is available for AG entities — An Aktiengesellschaft is first entered in the Commercial Register, which automatically issues the Swiss Federal Statistical Office operating company number UID/IDE/IDI; the UID also serves as the VAT identification number for Swiss VAT purposes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; AG is a separate legal taxpayer, subject to full unlimited tax liability on worldwide income for Swiss tax purposes, with deviations for foreign permanent establishments and foreign land; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions; no pass-through treatment is available for AG entities

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Swiss law; an AG cannot elect pass-through taxation. Available statutory elections include: (i) Swiss group taxation election under the federal Tax Harmonization Act permitting domestic and foreign group companies to be taxed as a single tax unit, (ii) voluntary Swiss VAT registration for small businesses below the CHF 100,000 threshold, and (iii) VAT aggregation of related Swiss entities during a five-year binding period. Hybrid mismatch rules apply in accordance with the Swiss implementation of the EU anti-tax-avoidance directive; there is no elective disclosure regime for hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual global turnover from taxable supplies (excluding Swiss VAT and after deducting items excluded from taxable consideration) received or accrued during a business year; voluntary VAT registration is permitted below this threshold. A reduced threshold regime exists for persons whose only sufficient taxable activities are covered services or goods under the reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate if the goods are physically removed from Swiss customs territory and delivered to a foreign destination; the supplier must obtain the customs export declaration and transport evidence proving that the goods left Switzerland within 30 days of the supply. For services, zero rating applies primarily where the place of supply is outside Switzerland, in particular B2B services to non-resident recipients, transport services related to exported goods, and ancillary services to eligible export transactions; all outward supplies must be supported by contracts, correspondence, and custodian or banking records proving the cross-border nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and official export certificate
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR road transport waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export payment or Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation

Switzerland does not operate a national real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API stamping, or a pre-clearance electronic invoice system. VAT administration is self-assessment: taxpayers issue invoices without government pre-authorization. However, invoices must contain statutory particulars, including supplier and recipient UID, type of supply, tax rate, amount of tax, and the date of supply. Electronic invoices are accepted, including PEPPOL and Swiss QR-invoice formats, provided the integrity and legibility of the VAT invoice metadata are preserved for the statutory retention period. Software-based cash register systems must comply with the Federal Act on VAT fiscalization requirements, but there is no central government stamping.

The Federal Tax Administration routinely audits export zero-rating claims and requires the complete retention of all export documentation for at least ten years. If documentary proof of export is missing, insufficient, or contradicted, the VAT deemed exempt must be retroactively reclaimed at the standard rate, together with default interest (currently 3.5% per annum). Intentional falsification or presentation of forged export evidence constitutes tax evasion under the Swiss VAT Act, punishable with a fine of up to CHF 250,000 or imprisonment for serious cases involving fraud, and the VAT debt may be collected from the recipient if the supplier is insolvent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc with no exchange-control regime on current-account or capital-account transactions. There are no restrictions on inward or outward transfers, dividend remittance, capital repayment, intercompany financing, or forward hedging. Practical hurdles are limited to bank counterparty due diligence under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act, sanctions screening, and the 35% Swiss withholding tax on qualifying dividend distributions unless reduced under a tax treaty or relieved via notification procedure. Profit remittance via dividends or branch repatriation is freely permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no statutory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in the currency of receipt, used directly to settle import payables, or converted freely. Statistical reporting obligations apply under the SNB Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Ordinance: resident non-bank enterprises with either consolidated assets above CHF 1 million or annual foreign receipts/payments above CHF 1 million must report cross-border payments and receivables/payables to the Swiss National Bank on a monthly or quarterly basis through the SNB's electronic reporting portal. The reports are for statistics and balance-of-payments compilation, not exchange-control authorisation.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB – Schweizerische Nationalbank; Banque nationale suisse; Banca nazionale svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury or clearing rail; MEDIUM as a supplementary convenience for small-volume AP/AR payments. Legal constraints: under the Swiss Financial Market Infrastructure Act (FMIA), Financial Institutions Act (FinIA), and Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), only FINMA-licensed or authorised payment institutions and e-money issuers may provide payment services to Swiss-domiciled clients. An offshore digital EMI holding an account in a third country is not a substitute for a Swiss-based transaction account and may create unlicensed payment-service exposure, weak audit evidence, and counterparty risk. A Swiss or FINMA-passported fintech (e.g., Swissquote or licensed neobank) may be acceptable for low-volume disbursements, but corporate raw-cotton trade flows requiring documentary letters of credit, guarantees, and interbank clearing must be executed through a systemically relevant commercial bank.

Raw cotton shipments from or to sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions, or transit through embargoed ports

Defense evidence: Country-of-origin certificates from competent agriculture or export authorities; full shipping evidence (bills of lading, warehouse receipts, ginnery weighbridge tickets); commercial invoices cross-referenced to independent price benchmarks (Cotlook A or New York cotton futures); irrevocable documentary L/C confirmation; ocean carrier and logistics provider receipts; denial or export-license verification from the Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) if applicable.

Defense evidence: Formal PEP declaration with source-of-wealth and source-of-funds documentation; independent references from a Swiss-regulated fiduciary or auditor; bank certificate confirming no adverse historical defaults; explanation of the commercial rationale for the cotton export business; consent to enhanced monitoring, periodic renewal of KYC, and near-real-time transaction screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for EUR) to the Swiss AG's CHF or foreign-currency account held with a FINMA-licensed bank. The remittance information must state 'Share Capital AG / Commercial Register UID' and, if the bank's system requires an ISO 20022 purpose code, use 'EQUI' (equity) or 'CFIN' (corporate finance). No SNB exchange-control approval is required, but the receiving bank must perform AMLA source-of-funds checks. Supporting documentation must include a board or shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, notarised subscription statements or a share-subscription agreement, a bank confirmation that the capital amount is at the free disposal of the company (as required for commercial-register registration of an AG), and the company's commercial register extract. For capital above CHF 1 million or any non-cash contribution, the bank and a licensed audit firm will require a sworn statement by the auditor and evidence that the amount was actually credited to an escrow or blocked account in the company's name.Outbound trade settlements for raw cotton exports will use SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments and documentary collection remittances, and a documentary commercial letter of credit (L/C) opened via SWIFT MT700/MT707, with settlement cover transmitted by MT103/MT202. For the CHF-domestic leg, clearing is via SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) operated by SIX Interbank Clearing; EUR legs via TARGET2/SEPA; USD via Fedwire/CHIPS through bank nostro accounts. All payment messages must quote the invoice number, bill of lading number, country-of-origin certificate reference, and Swiss UID; banks run each message through sanctions and prohibition filters. For an L/C, the exporter must present a clean-on-board bill of lading, signed commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, cotton classification certificate (grade, staple, micronaire), weight certificate, and a phytosanitary certificate where required; all documents must be strictly compliant with UCP600. Documentary collections are processed under URC522, with the presenting bank remitting proceeds only against acceptance or payment. Where open-account terms are used, credit risk may be covered by Swiss Export Risk Insurance (SERV) or private credit insurance.Plain-vanilla FX forwards traded under a bank-approved ISDA 2002 Master Agreement / FX CSA, with maturities of up to 24 months, used to lock conversion rates on forecast export receivables or import payables in USD, EUR, CNY, INR, PKR, and other major currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or restricted currencies (Chinese yuan offshore CNH or onshore CNY, Indian rupee, Pakistani rupee, Nigerian naira, Ethiopian birr) with cash settlement in CHF or USD against an agreed fix, to hedge trade receivables or payables without requiring physical settlement in the restricted currency.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS / long-form FX swaps) matching the currency denomination of trade-finance facilities, ECAs, or working-capital debt, executed under standard ISDA/CSA documentation with a FINMA-authorised bank or broker-dealer.Tom-next and spot conversion transactions for operational treasury purposes, expressly excluded from speculative dealing and governed by an internal treasury policy with pre-trade counterparty limits.

Companies that exceed neither the ordinary nor the simplified audit thresholds may validly opt out of an audit by unanimous shareholder resolution, provided they employ fewer than 10 full-time employees on average and no statutory exception applies. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct tax on after-tax profit, equivalent to 7.83% pre-tax; combined federal, cantonal and communal rate for a Zug trading AG approximately 14.6%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal advance tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on Swiss-source dividends, refundable or reduced under applicable double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No codified CHF threshold for Local/Master File; CbCR mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CHF 900m; prudent threshold for temporary Swiss TP documentation is related-party transactions ≥ CHF 1m annually or intercompany financing ≥ CHF 500,000; ESTV may request OECD-aligned TP documentation on audit regardless of de minimis levels.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) do not apply directly; however, OECD/MLI and Art. 210 DBG anti-abuse rules require effective substance for treaty benefits, participation exemption and PE attribution: own physical office, resident managing director, local accounting and strategic decision-making in Switzerland; Zug trading AG should employ operational staff for brokerage if annual commission income exceeds CHF 5m; artificial interposition without substance is retroactively recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Qualified Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of 1–3× the evaded tax plus penalty interest at 4.5% p.a.; criminal fine up to CHF 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; publication of judgment; asset seizure under Art. 70–72 Swiss Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for restitution under Art. 754 CO; travel bans and exit restrictions; extradition within EFTA/EU frameworks; disqualification from Swiss board offices up to 5 years; personal asset seizure.
Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: AG liable for 35% WHT plus 5% late-remittance interest and penalty surcharge up to 100% of unremitted amount; ESTV enforcement via bank account attachment and bankruptcy proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors personally solidarily liable where WHT was collected or utilizable but not remitted; criminal liability for debt fraud under Art. 166 Criminal Code; personal bankruptcy initiated by ESTV.
Shell Company Defeat

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization and retroactive taxation of undistributed profits with penalty interest; denial of participation exemption and DTA benefits; dissolution proceedings; fines up to CHF 30,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners subject to governance liability under Art. 754 CO; international judicial assistance and travel bans; personal tax assessments in residence country under AEOI data exchange; restitution orders with personal asset seizure.
AML and Trade Mis-invoicing

Statutory sanction: Administrative AML fines up to CHF 500,000; criminal money laundering penalties under Art. 305bis/305ter Swiss Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 3–5 years; confiscation of instrumentalities and proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorised signatories exposed to negligence-based liability under Swiss AML Act; travel bans and passport seizure; cross-border cooperation under CARF/AEOI exposing personal and corporate accounts; disqualification from financial intermediation.
