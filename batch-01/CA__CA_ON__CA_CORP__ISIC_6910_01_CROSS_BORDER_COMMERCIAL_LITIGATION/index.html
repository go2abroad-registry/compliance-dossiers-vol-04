Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA) for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income and import/export accounts; Ontario corporate income tax and the Ontario HST component are administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement; Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) is administered by the Ontario Ministry of Finance via Ontario's ONE-ID / Business Registration portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign entity carries on business in Canada or has a permanent establishment (PE) in Ontario. Step 2: Submit a Request for a Business Number (Form RC1) through Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the nine-digit Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN for federal and Ontario tax purposes. Step 3: Open required CRA program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RC (corporate income) where the corporation is resident in Canada, and RM/RE (import/export) if bringing goods into Canada. Step 4: A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must provide a Canadian address for service and may be asked to appoint a representative acceptable to the CRA; registration is mandatory before making taxable supplies in Canada unless the small supplier exception applies. Step 5: Ontario registration is automatically coordinated with the CRA for HST and Ontario corporate tax; a separate EHT account is required once Ontario payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000, with small employer exemption rules. Step 6: Foreign counsel/lawyers must also register under the Law Society of Ontario's licensing and trust account rules; the statutory tax ID remains the CRA BN for all federal/Ontario indirect tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. There is no partnership or flow-through treatment for a corporation. A Canadian-incorporated corporation controlled by non-residents is a non-CCPC (not a Canadian-controlled private corporation) and is taxed at the general corporate rate without the small business deduction. Territorial scope: a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income, Canadian-source employment income, certain Canadian-source property income, and taxable capital gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property. A Canadian branch of a non-resident corporation is also subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% on after-tax earnings, subject to treaty relief; the Ontario branch is additionally liable to Ontario corporate tax on Canadian-source income allocated to Ontario.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections: (i) Election under s. 216 of the Income Tax Act to file a Part I return for rental income or royalty income from Canada instead of the Part XIII withholding tax; (ii) Election under s. 219 of the Income Tax Act by a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada to file a Part I return and to treat the Canadian branch as a distinct taxpayer for branch tax purposes; (iii) GST/HST election under s. 273 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations to simplify intercorporate supplies; (iv) GST/HST annual filing election under s. 227(1), periodic return election, and the quick method election (lawyers are statutorily excluded from the quick method); (v) Transfer-pricing documentation elections are not available, but taxpayers may file a voluntary disclosure under the CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program to avoid penalties for prior misclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold) for residents; a non-resident supplier is ineligible for the small supplier exception if it has a permanent establishment in Canada or solicits orders in Canada, and must register for GST/HST before making taxable supplies in Canada. Legal services supplied in Canada to Canadian clients by an Ontario litigator are taxable at 13% regardless of the service provider's residence; a non-resident law firm providing services to Canadian residents will require registration if the services are 'supplied in Canada' under s. 143 of the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Legal services supplied by an Ontario GST/HST registrant to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under ss. 7 and 9 of Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and does not constitute a service of acting as an agent/trustee. Cross-border commercial litigation, international arbitration and asset-tracing work performed from an Ontario office for a foreign client, even where court filings occur in Ontario, generally qualifies for 0% where the non-resident recipient test is satisfied and the service is not directly connected to Canadian real or tangible property. If the foreign client has a Canadian permanent establishment or is itself a GST/HST registrant claiming input tax credits, the supply may be taxable at 13% HST. Contingency and success fees are treated as consideration for zero-rated legal services under the same conditions; disbursements billed to a non-resident client follow the character of the underlying supply (most litigation disbursements are zero-rated incidental to the exempt/zero-rated legal service).

None at the federal or Ontario level: Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no digital stamping and no federal e-invoicing mandate. Registrants must issue GST/HST invoices containing the prescribed particulars under s. 3 of the GST/HST Regulations (seller's name or BN, buyer's name, date, description of supply, consideration, and tax rate/amount or a clear indication that GST/HST is included). Quebec is progressively mandating electronic invoicing and platform-based clearance (Revenu Québec), but that does not apply to Ontario-based litigation chambers. The Law Society of Ontario's record-keeping and trust accounting rules require contemporaneous IOLTA trust ledger entries, bank reconciliation and retention of source documents for six years, which effectively functions as a separate documentary integrity regime.

CRA audit practice prioritizes zero-rated exports; all substantiation must be retained for six years (s. 286 Income Tax Act; s. 359 Excise Tax Act). Penalties and clawback risks: (i) 50% penalty on falsely claimed zero-rating/net tax under s. 285(1) ETA; (ii) failure to provide documentation penalty of CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 under s. 280(1) ETA; (iii) false statement or omission penalty under s. 327(1) ETA; (iv) tax evasion under s. 239(1) ITA / s. 327(2) ETA: fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years; (v) systemic clawback of input tax credits claimed on related disbursements, with interest compounded daily and arrears interest at the prescribed rate; (vi) the normal four-year assessment limitation period is extended to seven years for neglect, and indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default is established (s. 298(4) and (6) ETA; s. 152(4) ITA). Professional consequence: breach of Law Society of Ontario trust rules for improper IOLTA handling may trigger additional regulatory proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no statutory requirement for Central Bank approval, registration, or licensing of foreign exchange operations, and no quantitative restrictions apply to inbound capital injections, outbound loan repayments, or profit remittances by a federally or provincially incorporated business corporation. Dividends, interest, and management fees remitted to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% for dividends under Canada–US/UK/France treaties and to 15% for interest in most cases), but no exchange-control clearance is required. Directors may freely maintain multi-currency accounts, convert CAD at market rates, and repatriate litigation proceeds without limitation; applicable hurdles are taxation, sanctions screening, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds rather than convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds or any foreign-currency receipts. Receipts from cross-border legal services, arbitration awards, settlement recoveries, and cost awards may be retained indefinitely in CAD, USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency accounts at Canadian deposit-taking institutions or remitted abroad without awaiting a statutory window. The only relevant compliance obligations are FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reports and Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for transactions at or above C$10,000, plus income tax disclosure of foreign-source income and controlled foreign company implications, if applicable. No Central Bank directive mandates conversion of litigation settlement proceeds into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and financial stability authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential banking supervisor; FINTRAC as the national AML/ATF regulator; Global Affairs Canada as the sanctions administration authority. Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Control Administration and has not operated exchange controls since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Digital electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise and equivalent non-bank EMIs) are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot legally hold IOLTA client trust funds because Law Society of Ontario (LSO) By-Law 9 requires trust money to be deposited only with a bank, trust company, or credit union authorized to carry on business in Ontario. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) may be used on a limited basis for operational petty cash, urgent currency conversion, or small outgoing disbursements, but their correspondent-banking constraints, sub-account structures, and KYC interoperability make them unsuitable for arbitration award collection or settlement clearing. Chartered Tier-1 bank rails remain mandatory for trust integrity, security-for-costs escrow, and high-value wire traffic.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions or shareholder loans from non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through a correspondent bank, using a narrative purpose description such as 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan for Litigation Working Capital' together with the ISIC 6910 / NAICS 541110 services classification. The destination account should be a dedicated Operational CAD or USD account at a Tier-1 Canadian commercial bank, and bank charges must be pre-agreed (OUR/BEN/SHA) to avoid net settlement ambiguity. Compliance steps include: (i) Statistics Canada Foreign Direct Investment reporting where the transaction meets the C$1,000,000 or greater threshold; (ii) CRA thin-capitalization and Part XIII interest-stopping rules for shareholder debt; (iii) FINTRAC EFT Report filing for each transfer above C$10,000; (iv) sanctions and PEP screening of the remitter and beneficial owner; and (v) documented board resolution approving the capital injection. FX conversion from the remitter's currency into CAD is unrestricted and should be executed at a bank-negotiated interbank spread with a same-day value date.Outbound trade and litigation disbursements are cleared through the following verified rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers for foreign counsel fees, court filing fees, expert-witness retainers, and asset-tracing payments to overseas banks; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian relationship bank for security-for-costs undertakings and cross-border escrow guarantees in institutional arbitration; (iii) domestic Interac e-Transfer for immaterial Canadian disbursements; and (iv) bank drafts/certified cheques for Ontario Superior Court filings. Each outbound wire must be supported by a legal invoice, dispute-resolution budget, sanctions re-screening of the beneficiary, and FINTRAC EFT Report where the amount is C$10,000 or more. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility. Digital electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise and equivalent non-bank EMIs) are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot legally hold IOLTA client trust funds because Law Society of Ontario (LSO) By-Law 9 requires trust money to be deposited only with a bank, trust company, or credit union authorized to carry on business in Ontario. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) may be used on a limited basis for operational petty cash, urgent currency conversion, or small outgoing disbursements, but their correspondent-banking constraints, sub-account structures, and KYC interoperability make them unsuitable for arbitration award collection or settlement clearing. Chartered Tier-1 bank rails remain mandatory for trust integrity, security-for-costs escrow, and high-value wire traffic.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (Local File); CAD 50,000,000 (Master File)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, economic substance is required under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules to prevent artificial tax avoidance. Transactions lacking commercial substance may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest and penalties; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239)

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (s. 227.1); criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company / Artificial Transactions

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions under GAAR (s. 245); denial of tax benefits; penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the artificial arrangement; civil restitution and potential director disqualification
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering (s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code)

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation
