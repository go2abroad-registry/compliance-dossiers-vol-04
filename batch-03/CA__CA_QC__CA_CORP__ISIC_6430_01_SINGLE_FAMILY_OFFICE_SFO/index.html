Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national federal tax authority for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Revenu Québec (RQ) — provincial tax authority administering the Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), Quebec corporate income tax, and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec business registry); portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada or under the Business Corporations Act (Québec) via the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), obtaining a federal Certificate of Incorporation and/or a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); a foreign-incorporated SFO must complete extra-provincial registration in Quebec and designate an attorney (mandataire) for service of process. Step 2 — Register with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN); the BN is the federal TIN and anchors program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll). Step 3 — Register with Revenu Québec through 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' using Form LM-1-V for the QST (TVQ) account, the Quebec corporate income tax account, and employer accounts (QPIP, CNESST). Step 4 — A non-resident corporation must open its CRA/Revenu Québec accounts with supporting documentation: certificate of incorporation, registered office address, directors list, Canadian agent details, proof of Canadian activity, and expected taxable supply volume; the CAD 30,000 small-supplier test applies federally and provincially, but non-residents carrying on a Canadian commercial activity or holding Canadian real property may be registrable below the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian/Quebec business corporation is a taxable entity subject to Part I of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1) on worldwide income if resident in Canada; non-resident-controlled corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income with a Part XIV branch tax on profits repatriated; no pass-through eligibility for corporations (pass-through status is limited to partnerships and trusts); the SFO may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if controlled by Canadian residents, otherwise it is classified as a non-resident-controlled or public/other corporation ineligible for the small business deduction; Quebec-resident corporations file Quebec corporate income tax (Part IV, Quebec) with Revenu Québec.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; entity classification follows legal form. Key elections for an SFO: (i) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of capital property/assets into the corporation; (ii) ITA s. 83(2) capital dividend election (Form T2054) to distribute tax-free capital dividends from the capital dividend account; (iii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident earning Canadian rental income to file a Part I return instead of Part XIII withholding; (iv) ITA s. 219 branch tax election for a non-resident corporation; (v) GST/HST closely related corporation election (ETA s. 156) and QST mirror election (QST Act s. 378) to treat intercompany supplies as nil-value; (vi) hybrid mismatch neutralizing rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.5 for cross-border hybrid arrangements (no elective opt-out for aggressive arrangements); (vii) ITA s. 256.1(4) election to disregard prescribed share-attribute arrangements when determining foreign control status — relevant for a foreign-family-owned SFO seeking CCPC status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold for GST/HST (ETA s. 123(1) definition) and QST (QST Act) measured on total worldwide taxable supplies, excluding financial services, sales of capital property, and certain zero-rated exports, over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; an SFO charging management, administrative, or investment-advisory fees to resident family entities must register for GST/QST once fees exceed CAD 30,000; non-resident persons making taxable supplies in Canada or holding Canadian real property are outside the small-supplier relief in certain cases and must register regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An SFO registrant may claim 0% GST/QST on: (1) goods — a supply of tangible personal property made to a non-resident person where the goods are exported from Canada by commercial carrier or mail as part of the supply, with export occurring no later than the earliest of the day consideration becomes due, the day it is paid, or the day the goods are delivered to the carrier/mailer (ETA s. 20(1) and Sch. VI, Part V; QST mirror rules); (2) services — a supply to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant where the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or relates to real property or tangible personal property situated outside Canada, or relates to intangible personal property for use wholly outside Canada (ETA s. 9 and Sch. VI, Part II); (3) financial services supplied by an SFO are exempt, not zero-rated, and carry no input tax credits; (4) the burden of proof rests on the registrant — contemporaneous documentary evidence must establish the export and payment flow; the CRA and Revenu Québec require export to occur within a reasonable time, with a practical evidentiary benchmark of 180 days for payment settlement.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A64/A8 or automated eManifest export report for highway, rail, air, and marine modes)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical movement of goods outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice, international sale contract or purchase order with the non-resident purchaser
  • Freight forwarder's or carrier's certificate of export or proof of foreign customs entry/import declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank wire confirmation, or foreign exchange transaction record evidencing repatriation of sale proceeds
  • For exported services: written service agreement, detailed statement of work, performance records proving services performed outside Canada, and payment evidence from the non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory Peppol/API e-invoicing clearance regime exists in Canada or Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices and records provided integrity is assured; every invoice must contain the prescribed particulars (ETA s. 223 and QST Act — seller name, GST/QST registration number, date, description, consideration). Quebec mandates electronic QST return filing, remittance, and data transmission (EIS/EDI) for large registrants and requires electronic remittance of QST amounts exceeding CAD 10,000; there is no pre-clearance or government stamping of invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period (unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file); denial of zero-rating triggers reassessment of GST/QST plus daily-compounded arrears interest (prescribed rate under ETA s. 144 / Tax Administration Act) and reversal of claimed input tax credits. Civil penalties: false statements or omissions under ETA s. 285.1 (10% of the tax evaded, or 5% where understatement exceeds CAD 1,000,000; 20% for repeat violations); gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) and ETA s. 285 (up to 50% of the evaded amount). Criminal exposure: ETA s. 282 and ITA s. 239 fraud — fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; Criminal Code s. 380 tax evasion; Quebec penalty provisions: QST Act s. 59, Tax Administration Act (CQLR c. A-6.003) ss. 51–58 and penal provisions — penalties of 50% to 100% plus prosecutorial fines. Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/QST of the corporation (ETA s. 323 and QST Act s. 422). For SFO structures, CRA scrutinizes cross-border related-party fee arrangements for sham, agency, and transfer-pricing recharacterization, and may retrospectively claw back zero-rating where the services in fact benefited Canadian resident beneficiaries.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the current and capital accounts. There are no Canadian exchange controls, no foreign currency rationing, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or the remittance of profits. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends and capital returns freely, subject to applicable domestic withholding tax on dividends under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% statutory rate, reducible by tax treaty) and to securities registration or CRS filing requirements where relevant. Family office holding structures must also assess Quebec minimum tax and prescribed disclosure rules for foreign assets under section 233.3.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation under Canadian federal or Quebec provincial law to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or to any designated bank. Exporters may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts, choose their preferred settlement currency, and do not face mandatory conversion ratios or minimum holding periods for export receipts. All export transactions are, however, subject to FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting and to sanctions screening under Canadian laws and international obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control authority; the Bank of Canada regulates monetary policy and OSFI supervises federally regulated financial institutions. Foreign exchange activities are administered by commercial banks under the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC AML/CPF regulation, but no pre-approval from the central bank is required for cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with significant legal constraints. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) that do not hold a Canadian money service business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC cannot legally open domestic accounts or process CAD-denominated payments in the regulated banking perimeter. Foreign-owned EMIs may obtain a provisional MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, but the practical ability to serve a single family office as a primary treasury rail is limited by correspondent banking de-risking and the requirement to maintain a physical compliance presence in Canada. Direct integration with the domestic Canadian clearing system (real-time rail Lynx, now operated by Payments Canada) is generally unavailable to unlicensed EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

C e complinant wire transfers for capital injection should use the beneficiary's legal name and full address, the Family Office corporation's Canadian bank account and SWIFT/BIC code, and a purpose description that includes 'Equity contribution for corporate treasury and investment' or 'Capital subscription for Québec business corporation'. Canada does not prescribe a specific government purpose code for capital transfers; banks accept a narrative payment to satisfy the FINTRAC transaction monitoring requirement. The remitting bank should use MT103 (SWIFT) or the equivalent international wire message, and the Canadian call or client will be subject to the normal Canadian regulatory verification for incoming wire transfers (OSFI Guideline B-8). For any transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, a FINTRAC cross-border currency report may be triggered; large corporate transfers are exempted if the client is a reporting entity holding an accurate and current beneficial ownership statement. Best practice is to pre-notify the relationship manager and provide a board resolution and source-of-funds document before the wire is dispatched to avoid investigation hold.For trade settlement, the preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct documentary and open-account payments and irrevocable commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by a Canadian chartered bank for trade flows requiring security. The correspondent network is typically routed through the Canadian bank's United States or London correspondent for foreign currency, or through the local Canadian clearing system (Lynx) for CAD. All outbound payments must be screened against Canadian sanctions (Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act), the Terrorist Financing Suspicious Transaction reporting regime, and OFAC-derived lists. For real-time payments inside Canada, the use of Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail is starting, but MT103 remains the standard for cross-border. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total value of transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year triggers Local File requirement; Master File required if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine. For investment holding companies, CRA may challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance, especially if they are used to defer or avoid Canadian tax. A single family office must demonstrate real business purpose, active management, and adequate staffing to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they failed to withhold or remit (s. 227.1). Criminal liability extends to officers who participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment: denial of tax benefits, interest, and penalties up to 50% of the benefit. CRA may also impose a 10% penalty for failure to file TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; personal net worth assessments and injunctions may be issued.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code (up to 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income, impose a 10% penalty on the net adjustment (if documentation absent), and charge interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they directed or acquiesced to the non-compliance; CRA can pursue personal assets for unpaid corporate tax liabilities.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full amount of unwithheld tax, plus interest and penalties (up to 20% of the amount).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and enforce via garnishment or seizure of personal assets.
