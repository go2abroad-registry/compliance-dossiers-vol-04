Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — GST/HST registration, federal income tax and partnership accounts; Ontario Ministry of Finance — Ontario provincial tax administration; Ontario Business Registry — limited partnership registration. Portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Ontario registration via https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry

E-government filing portal

Register the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through the Ontario Business Registry, then obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' either online via Business Registration Online or by mailed/faxed application. The BN application must identify the limited partnership and any relevant program accounts, including a GST/HST program account (suffix RT) where the LP carries on commercial activity in Canada or is obliged to collect tax. A foreign-owned LP may appoint a Canadian agent for service, but the BN is issued in the name of the LP itself. For foreign partners, individual tax identification numbers (SIN/ITN) are separately required where partners file Canadian returns; the LP itself is not assigned a separate corporate income tax number beyond its BN and may be required to file a partnership information return (T5013).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: income, losses, credits and liabilities flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is itself treated as a distinct person/registrant separate from its partners, and input tax credits are claimed by the partnership. The LP is not taxed at entity level on business income; each partner includes the share in their own return, subject to Canadian non-resident withholding and Part XIII tax rules where applicable.

Available election forms

Canada does not implement a U.S.-style check-the-box election. The limited partnership's transparent classification is statutory and cannot be re-elected as corporate. Available mechanisms include: (i) a partnership fiscal year election under the Income Tax Act to synchronize fiscal year-end with majority partners; (ii) a joint election for partnership status for GST/HST under Excise Tax Act s. 177(2), deeming supplies made by a partner in the course of the partnership's commercial activities to be made by the partnership; (iii) under subsection 96(1), a jointly filed partnership allocation schedule (T5013) for income/loss allocations; and (iv) under hybrid mismatches provisions, CRA may react to foreign elections by recharacterizing the LP's transparent status for cross-border payments, but no standalone domestic election creates or revokes pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded. The LP may voluntarily register below the threshold to recover input tax credits connected to commercial activities. The 13% HST in Ontario applies to taxable supplies made in the province; exports of services to non-residents are generally zero-rated at 0%, subject to substantiation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, supplies of services made to a non-resident person are zero-rated at 0% where the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, is not registered under GST/HST, and the supply is not an excluded supply such as services rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of the supply. For outsourced AAA game asset sculpting and character rigging, the supplier must evidence that the deliverable (3D models, rigs, metadata files) is transferred to a foreign studio in digital form, used wholly outside Canada, and the client is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST. A signed written declaration from the client confirming non-resident status must be obtained no later than the time the zero-rated supply is reported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (mandatory only where physical media is exported; not applicable to purely digital delivery)
  • Bill of Lading (required only for physical shipments; not applicable to digitally transmitted game assets)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire confirmation evidencing receipt of funds from the foreign contracting party
  • Signed written non-residency declaration or equivalent tax identification evidence from the foreign studio
  • Digital service invoice stating the supply is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule IX to the Excise Tax Act, with the LP's Business Number

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-clearance obligation exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and business records, but requires that all invoices contain the supplier's Business Number, date, description of supply, consideration, and the GST/HST rate applied or an indication of zero-rating. Registrants must retain books and records (including electronic source data) for six years. Remittance and return filing are generally electronic through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; however, there is no continuous transaction control regime.

CRA may audit zero-rated export claims and assess unremitted GST/HST plus interest where the LP fails to maintain adequate proof of the non-resident recipient, the digital nature of the supply, or the actual receipt of foreign currency. Under the Excise Tax Act s. 285, a false or misleading statement or omission in a return, including a fraudulent zero-rating claim, attracts a penalty of 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded. CRA may reassess within four years, with the period extended by the same number of years in cases of misrepresentation, fraud or neglect. Criminal tax evasion under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act s. 326 can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Refunds or input tax credits traceable to disallowed zero-rating are clawed back through reverse assessment and any exporter who has knowingly issued false export invoices may be jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no general exchange control regime or capital repatriation permits. Profit remittances and capital distributions by an Ontario limited partnership to non-resident partners are permitted without prior regulatory approval, subject to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, possible partnership return filings, and bank-level know-your-client verification. Current-account transactions, including payments for exported 3D art and animation services, are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. An exporter may retain foreign currency in any bank account in Canada for any period. Transaction-level reporting occurs only where a transfer exceeds CAD 10,000 (FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report) or for statistical balance-of-payments purposes under the Statistics Act. No central bank approval is required before using export receipts for working capital or treasury hedging.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money laundering and transaction reporting for cross-border payments

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with significant legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the European 'electronic money institution' licence as a domestic authorisation. Any offshore EMI offering payment services to Canadian customers must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act and register with the Bank of Canada, and must also satisfy PCMLTFA AML obligations. As a practical matter, an overseas digital EMI without Canadian payment-service registration cannot act as the sole clearing rail for an Ontario LP; it is viable only as a supplementary collection tool after establishing a commercial account with a Schedule I bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Fiscally transparent Ontario Limited Partnership (LP): no entity-level income tax. Income is allocated to partners under ITA s. 96. Corporate partners are subject to the combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate of 25.0% (15% federal + 10% Ontario general). Ontario CCPC small business rate: 12.2% (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, where eligibility is tested at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
