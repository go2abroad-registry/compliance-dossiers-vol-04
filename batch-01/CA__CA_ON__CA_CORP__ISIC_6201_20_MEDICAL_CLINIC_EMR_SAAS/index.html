Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — administered via My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO); provincial excise matters for Ontario HST are administered by the CRA under the Ontario HST framework.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts for GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC), and import/export (RM). Registration is made through CRA Form RC1A or the Business Registration Online service. A foreign corporation must also assess whether it is 'carrying on business' in Canada under section 174 of the Excise Tax Act; if so, GST/HST registration is mandatory unless the small supplier threshold is met. Ontario-specific provincial registration (e.g., Ontario Corporation Tax or Employer Health Tax) may apply separately through the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts for GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC), and import/export (RM). Registration is made through CRA Form RC1A or the Business Registration Online service. A foreign corporation must also assess whether it is 'carrying on business' in Canada under section 174 of the Excise Tax Act; if so, GST/HST registration is mandatory unless the small supplier threshold is met. Ontario-specific provincial registration (e.g., Ontario Corporation Tax or Employer Health Tax) may apply separately through the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Canadian federal tax law; corporations are automatically separate taxable entities. Relevant elective mechanisms include: (i) CRA non-resident GST/HST registration election under section 241.1 of the Excise Tax Act; (ii) election to treat a non-resident corporation as resident under subsection 250(1)? VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies made globally by the entity over any four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada may be required to register below this threshold if they are engaged in a commercial activity in Canada).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 5 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada, and to services supplied to non-residents where the service is performed entirely outside Canada or is in respect of exported goods. In the context of EMR SaaS, sale of software services to foreign medical clinics, hospitals, or health data processors qualifies as zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident and is not for use in Canada; the supplier must maintain contemporaneous documentation satisfying CRA guidelines.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA Export Reporting via Canada Export Reporting System (CAERS)
  • Commercial invoice with clear reference to export or foreign customer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for goods) or service delivery logs (for SaaS)
  • Inward remittance certificate / payments evidence proving non-resident consideration
  • Signed service agreement or sales contract identifying foreign recipient and Canadian service provider

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) for most registrants, but invoice-level clearance/API transmission is not required. E-invoicing is voluntary; Canadian suppliers may use PEPPOL-compliant networks or EDI formats for commercial efficiency only.

CRA may reassess, impose arrears interest, and apply penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act for gross negligence: a civil penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax avoided. Criminal prosecution for fraud under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act may yield fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective post-export audit verification, and clawback of claimed refunds plus statutory interest if documentary evidence is insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada assumes IMF Article VIII obligations with a free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD). There are no exchange control restrictions on either current account or capital account transactions, and no requirement to obtain central bank approval for dividend, interest, or royalty remittances. The only structural hurdle is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, commonly reduced under the Canada–US treaty to 5% for dividends with 10% voting ownership holdings; interest generally 0%; software royalty/EMR licence fees treated as business profits or services and not subject to Part XIII if no permanent establishment). Cross-border equity injections are permitted, subject only to Investment Canada Act notifiability thresholds, and foreign currency accounts can be held freely. The CAD/USD rate is determined by markets; no central bank conversion mandate or gate exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable – Canada does not impose surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratios on export proceeds. The EMR SaaS exporter may retain foreign currency (USD) receipts in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely. The only obligations are tax-driven: (i) under s.39 of the Income Tax Act, realized foreign exchange gains/losses are recognized for income tax; (ii) under s.95, certain foreign affiliates’ active business income may produce FAPI implications with a 5% passive income taint, though the Canadian corporate entity's own export receivables are not subject to FAPI; and (iii) FINTRAC requires EFT reporting for incoming/outgoing international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but this is an AML reporting rule, not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); the Minister of Finance is responsible for foreign exchange reserves and Investment Canada Act administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility with FINTRAC registration condition. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury) may provide CAD account onboarding only if it is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and fulfills the MSB registration, compliance program and CDD requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. There is no Canadian 'EMI passport' regime; direct non-bank clearing access to Payments Canada LYNX is not available, so EMIs rely on sponsor bank rails and can be used for payment collection, but are not recommended as the primary treasury account for holding material liquidity. Legal blockers: OSFI unauthorized 'bank' liability, FINTRAC MSB license, and enhanced proof for health-tech (PHIPA) that payment intermediaries do not access clinical information.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for equity injection: the foreign investor transfers funds via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/SEPA depending on jurisdiction) to the corporate CAD operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank with SWIFT field 70 clearly stating 'Investment – Share Subscription CA-ON entity', attaching the board resolution, subscription agreement and a completed FINTRAC EFT Report (for transfers > CAD 10,000) if processed through an MSB; alternatively the bank's prior-approved foreign currency (USD) account can receive the USD wire, then execute a bank spot/forward conversion. Because there are no Canadian FX controls, obtaining OSFI clearance is unnecessary; the barriers are (a) the bank's KYC source-of-funds proof, (b) Investment Canada Act notifiability determination when non-Canadian control in fact crosses the economic thresholds (CAD 1.3M/5.3M for WTO investors; notifiable then if enterprise value/profit levels trigger), and (c) practical confirmation of the beneficial ownership amount and the 5% withholding bracket for subsequent dividend outward remittances.Clearing rails: (1) Domestic CAD payment settlements via Payments Canada LYNX RTGS on the SWIFT network (minimum CAD 25,000) for same-day high-value payments, or EFT/Interac e-Transfer for small amounts; (2) Cross-border USD payments via SWIFT MT103 from the Canadian bank to US correspondent accounts (e.g., RBC US, TD Bank US); SWIFT GPI tracking advisable; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are technically available for hardware resale/clinical device procurement within the EMR ecosystem, but are not the standard for SaaS and only if the counterparty demands trade finance; (4) For cloud/software supplier payments (e.g., AWS, Microsoft Azure), use virtual card (Stripe Issuing) or rapid wire, attaching invoice and a compliance record in the ERP. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for tangible goods; CAD 1 million for loans; Master File required if total revenue > CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions > CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles; treaty benefits require beneficial ownership and economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 50% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they fail to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for penalties and interest; potential civil restitution and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations per violation; administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; travel bans and asset seizure.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of CAD 1,000 per month for each month documentation is missing, maximum CAD 2,000; tax adjustments with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in non-compliance; potential criminal charges for gross negligence.
