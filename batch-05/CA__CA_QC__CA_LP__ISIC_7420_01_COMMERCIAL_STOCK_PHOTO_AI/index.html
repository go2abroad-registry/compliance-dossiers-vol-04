Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Québec Enterprise Registrar), 255 boulevard Crémazie Est, Montréal, Québec H2M 1L5; online via the Mon entreprise portal at www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), arts. 2236-2265, in particular arts. 2250-2265 governing the société en commandite (limited partnership); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1), arts. 16-39 and 85-89 (declaration of registration, registration of partnerships, and annual update obligations). The Quebec Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1) does not apply to a limited partnership; governance is contractual under the Civil Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation (Declaration of Registration) of the société en commandite / limited partnership, followed by the issuance of the certificate of registration (attestation) and the enterprise number (NEQ).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — cra-arc.gc.ca; Revenu Québec (RQ) — revenuquebec.ca (Quebec-specific GST/HST and QST administration)

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership: (1) obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) via the Registraire des entreprises (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca), which is a precondition for tax accounts; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account with CRA using Form RC1 or the My Business Account portal (non-resident applicants may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process under ETA s. 241.1); (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec after the NEQ is active, using the CléT or CLIC secure online service; (4) if the LP has no establishment in Quebec but derives taxable supplies in Canada, maintain records of the non-resident status of each partner and obtain a Canadian 'treaty' or 'waiver' tax identification number for non-resident partners as required for filing partnership information returns; (5) register each non-resident partner for a CRA individual/corporation tax account upon receipt of Canadian-source allocable income.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian income-tax law treats a limited partnership as fiscally transparent; the Quebec LP is not an entity-level income taxpayer; however, for GST/HST and QST, the partnership itself is a registrant person and may collect/remit taxes separately from its partners) — For a Quebec limited partnership: (1) obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) via the Registraire des entreprises (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca), which is a precondition for tax accounts; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account with CRA using Form RC1 or the My Business Account portal (non-resident applicants may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process under ETA s. 241.1); (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec after the NEQ is active, using the CléT or CLIC secure online service; (4) if the LP has no establishment in Quebec but derives taxable supplies in Canada, maintain records of the non-resident status of each partner and obtain a Canadian 'treaty' or 'waiver' tax identification number for non-resident partners as required for filing partnership information returns; (5) register each non-resident partner for a CRA individual/corporation tax account upon receipt of Canadian-source allocable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian income-tax law treats a limited partnership as fiscally transparent; the Quebec LP is not an entity-level income taxpayer; however, for GST/HST and QST, the partnership itself is a registrant person and may collect/remit taxes separately from its partners)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. The partnership's classification is governed by the Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96 et seq. and Quebec civil law (Civil Code of Quebec arts. 2236–2245). Available elections: (a) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4); (b) optional net-base taxation for non-resident partners on Canadian rental/investment income under ITA s. 216(1); (c) GST/HST joint election under ETA s. 156 for closely related persons, if applicable; (d) hybrid-mismatch documentation requirements under ITA ss. 12.6–12.8 must be satisfied where payments are made to or through hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.14975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold for non-resident suppliers of taxable digital products/services supplied to Canadian/Quebec recipients (Excise Tax Act s. 123(1) 'small supplier'; QST Act s. 1 equivalent). Exceeding this threshold on supplies made in Canada/Quebec triggers mandatory registration. For online marketplace selling, the platform may be deemed the supplier under 2021 rules, but the seller remains primarily liable unless the platform fully assumes collection and remittance responsibilities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for stock-photo and AI-generative visual-asset sales applies when the supply of intangible personal property (copyright/license) is made to a non-resident of Canada (and, for QST purposes, to a person outside Quebec) who is not registered for GST/HST/QST, provided the supply is not for use in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply (Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 7 and 10 of ETA; QST Schedule II, s. 5). The supplier must obtain documentary evidence demonstrating the recipient's non-resident status, the exported nature of the supply, and the non-Canadian place of use. Supplies to Canadian registered persons or to residents are taxable at 14.975%.

  • Not applicable to pure digital delivery; for physical media: Customs Export Declaration (B13A), Bill of Lading/Airway Bill, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • For digital exports, retain: (1) export supply contract / license agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and IP rights; (2) commercial invoice with zero-rated declaration; (3) payment evidence (SWIFT or Stripe/PayPal settlement showing non-Canadian source currency or foreign bank); (4) recipient jurisdiction evidence (IP geolocation, billing address, corporate registration, or non-resident self-certification); (5) if applicable, proof of the recipient's registration in a foreign tax system.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API mandate, or digital stamping applies in Canada/Quebec. Invoices must contain: supplier name, GST/HST and QST registration numbers, invoice date, description, quantity, price, tax rates, and tax payable. Electronic invoicing is permitted and must preserve consent, integrity, and readability; public-sector e-invoicing (Peppol Canada) is optional.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess any zero-rated supply if documentary evidence is insufficient. In that event, the tax is collectible (14.975% of the invoice) plus interest and late-payment penalties. For negligent misstatements, ETA s. 280 imposes a 50% penalty of the tax sought; s. 279 establishes an absolute liability offence with fines up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment. Revenu Québec has equivalent authority under QSTA s. 1025. The normal 4-year reassessment period may be extended to 7 years for negligence and is open-ended for fraud or misrepresentation. Third-party liability and director/partner liability may attach when a partnership fails to remit collected GST/HST or QST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; there are no exchange-control permits, no restrictions on inbound equity contributions, and no licensing requirement for profit remittances by a Québec LP. A non-resident limited partner is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its share of Canadian partnership business income, while Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) applies only to separate royalty, interest, and management-fee payments. Image-licensing payments made to non-resident creators attract Part XIII royalty withholding unless a tax treaty reduces or exempts the rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds; the LP may freely retain USD/EUR receipts in Canadian foreign-currency accounts. The only compliance triggers are statistical and AML-based: exporters of services exceeding the Statistics Canada Trade in Services reporting threshold must file mandatory returns, and financial institutions must report to FINTRAC every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more crossing the Canadian border. No Québec rule imposes a minimum holding period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and bank-note issuer; the Minister of Finance administers the Exchange Fund Account, OSFI supervises Schedule I banks for prudential soundness, FINTRAC administers AML/ATF and large cross-border transfer reporting, and in Québec the Autorité des marchés financiers regulates provincial payment-service and financial-product arrangements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Pass-through entity: no entity-level Canadian income tax; net partnership income allocated to partners. A taxable non-resident/corporate partner's combined federal+Quebec general headline rate is 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; reduced to 15% under the Canada-US DTA (5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation controlling at least 10% of voting shares). For the LP, routine distributions of earnings are typically returns of capital or allocated income (not subject to Part XIII), but a non-resident partner's share of LP income may attract Part I tax or, for non-resident corporate partners, Part XIV branch tax of 25% (5% under Canada-US treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013/CO-1017 due on the earlier of (i) March 31 of the year following the fiscal period end and (ii) five months after the fiscal period end (i.e., March 31 for a December 31 year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2/CO-17 due six months after the corporate partner's fiscal year-end (e.g., June 30 for a December 31 year-end); balance-due payment due two months after year-end (three months for eligible CCPCs), with quarterly instalments.

Late T5013/CO-1017: federal penalty of CAD 25/day for each partner (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500), plus Quebec penal provisions under the Tax Administration Act (fines up to CAD 25,000 for a corporation and legal costs); a corporate partner's late T2/CO-17 attracts 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months, and Quebec penalties mirroring the federal structure.
Part II / Schedule for allocation + Box 845/846 Allocation of net business income and capital cost allowance among partners by class of partner (general vs limited), percentage interests, and partner TINs/BNs. Non-resident corporate partners receive a T5013 slip; their Canadian-source share of income is computed under s. 2(3) and the Canada-US DTA 'business profits' article; treaty protection requires no PE in Canada and an arm's length allocation. Late T5013/CO-1017: federal penalty of CAD 25/day for each partner (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500), plus Quebec penal provisions under the Tax Administration Act (fines up to CAD 25,000 for a corporation and legal costs); a corporate partner's late T2/CO-17 attracts 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months, and Quebec penalties mirroring the federal structure.
T2 Line 1 (Revenue) Total revenue, including the partner's share of LP business income and income from stock-photo licensing services. A non-resident corporation with no permanent establishment should still file T2/CO-17 only for treaty-treatable allocable income; claim Part I exemption under Article VII if the LP does not constitute a PE. Late-filed/non-filed T2/CO-17 for the corporate partner: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months; repeated failure: additional 10% penalty; Quebec's CO-17 penalty regime is parallel; total fines may exceed CAD 25,000 per calendar day through civil assessments and ordinary interest.
T2 Line 60 (Taxable Income) Taxable income after claimed deductions: partnership allocable income minus non-capital loss carryforwards and allowable business investment losses. Deduct no Quebec SBD at the non-resident corporate level; apply section 111(1)(a) 20-year loss carryforward subject to continuity rules. Late-filed/non-filed T2/CO-17 for the corporate partner: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months; repeated failure: additional 10% penalty; Quebec's CO-17 penalty regime is parallel; total fines may exceed CAD 25,000 per calendar day through civil assessments and ordinary interest.
T2 Schedule 1 (Small Business Deduction) SBD calculation for qualifying CCPC partners on active business income up to CAD 500,000 federal/Quebec combined threshold. Non-resident-owned investment corporations (NRO) or foreign corporations are ineligible for SBD; ensure Canadian partner (e.g., a Canadian holding LP) files Schedule 1 correctly. Late-filed/non-filed T2/CO-17 for the corporate partner: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months; repeated failure: additional 10% penalty; Quebec's CO-17 penalty regime is parallel; total fines may exceed CAD 25,000 per calendar day through civil assessments and ordinary interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File (Form to be produced contemporaneously) mandatory where the MNE group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CAD 50 million in the preceding fiscal year; Local File required where the total value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions (services, royalties, asset licences, and cost recharges) equals or exceeds CAD 10 million annually; contemporaneous documentation must be completed before the tax return filing date.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, ITA s. 245 GAAR (and Quebec's Tax Administration Act anti-avoidance provisions, e.g., ss. 375-379 TAA and the Quebec GAAR in the Taxation Act s. 389.0.1.4) deny 'avoidance transactions' lacking economic/business substance; courts apply purposive interpretation (Canadian Trustco, Shell Canada) and CRA may recharacterize artificial IP holding structures; the LP and its corporate partners must demonstrate real premises, employees, board-level decision-making, and revenue-generating functions in Quebec to substantiate substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239: summary conviction fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax plus interest; Quebec comparable penal regime under TAA arts. 31-44 with fines of 50%-200% of avoided tax and prison terms.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and QST (ITA s. 227.1; TAA ss. 30-33) without a defence of due diligence; participation in management by a limited partner nullifies limited liability under Civil Code of Québec art. 2242 — such partner becomes personally liable for all partnership debts; culpable directors risk travel restrictions in Quebec for outstanding tax debts, civil restitution, revocation of e-filing credentials, and penal imprisonment.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million and 10% of gross revenue; s. 247(8) documentation penalty of CAD 500/month up to CAD 5,000 (per CRA; higher for large corporations up to CAD 25,000 for egregious failures); compound interest and reassessment windows extended under s. 152(4)(a) to 6+ years for non-arm's-length transactions and 10 years for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length pricing can be assessed for the corporate general partner's unremitted taxes to the extent of assets distributed (s. 160 transfers) and, through Quebec civil law, for complicity in a fault causing damages to the Crown (Civil Code of Québec art. 1457); criminal exposure under s. 239 if the pricing scheme is concealment-driven; CRA 'name and shame' non-compliance campaigns can freeze bank accounts and garnish receivables.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR and Foreign Base Erosion)

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the arrangement; Quebec GAAR may impose QST/income tax recalculations; an assessment can be issued to a non-resident partner directly for Canadian source income under s. 212(1)(d) and s. 2(3); penalties/interest compound; fraud-level omissions extend the limitation period to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the LP has no separate identity (Civil Code of Québec arts. 317 and 2242): the court may order personal civil restitution against the controlling director and general partner; in Quebec, an order prohibiting the individual from leaving the province can be issued under the TAA when a tax debt exceeds CAD 1,000 and the Minister proves risk of evasion; directors also risk being named as third parties liable under s. 160 for transferred assets.
AML/ATF — Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (FINTRAC): up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for money-services entities and up to CAD 500,000 for other regulated entities (higher under the 2023 PCMLTFA amendments for repeat offenders — up to CAD 2,000,000); criminal conviction fine of up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years for knowingly failing to report; seizure of CDN assets worth of illicit proceeds.

Director personal liability: Failure to cause the LP/corporate general partner to maintain AML compliance exposes directors and officers to personal criminal-liability prosecution under the PCMLTFA (s. 74-75) including imprisonment for up to 10 years for laundering proceeds; directors may be removed in FINTRAC enforcement actions; reputational and disqualification orders can be issued jointly with the AMF (Quebec financial markets regulator).
