Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office is not permissible as the sole registered seat of a Swiss AG because the statutory seat must be the place of effective administration (CO Article 626). Commercial domiciliation services in Zug may provide a correspondence address only if central administration and decision-making are demonstrably located in Switzerland. For the bonded warehouse licence, the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS/BAZG) mandates physical, secure and access-controlled warehouse premises within a customs free zone or bonded depot; a virtual office will cause licence refusal and trigger substance scrutiny by the tax administration and the commercial register.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum authorized share capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash at incorporation and deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank, evidenced by an unqualified bank capital confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) attached to the registration filing. Alternatively, the articles may provide for CHF 50,000 authorized capital fully paid in. Released only after Commercial Register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the cantonal fire insurance authority under VKF model guidelines, comprising fire-resistant construction, sprinkler coverage and hazardous-goods segregation; structural load and static inspection by the cantonal building office confirming the floor capacity for high-density racked storage; SUVA occupational safety confirmation under the Accident Insurance Act (UVG) and Occupational Safety Ordinance (ArGV 3) covering workplace layout, mechanical handling equipment and employee training; civil defense (Zivilschutz) building obligations, including any required shelter areas and access routes, verified during the building permit procedure.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum authorized share capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash at incorporation and deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank, evidenced by an unqualified bank capital confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) attached to the registration filing. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV; Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC), official multi-lingual VAT portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch (VAT e-fiscal platform, 'Mehrwertsteuer/TVA' online filing). Complementary registries: Central Business Name Index (Zefix) and the Swiss Business Identifier (UID) registry at https://www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

A newly formed Swiss AG is filed with the cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister), which generates the Swiss UID (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) as the universal business identification. VAT registration must then be submitted electronically to the ESTV via the official VAT registration form (Form 15 'Registrierung zur Mehrwertsteuer / Inscription à la TVA'); the UID becomes the VAT number supplemented by the MWST/TVA/IVA suffix. A foreign entity with no Swiss seat must appoint a fiscal representative (Steuervertretung/représentant fiscal) domiciled in Switzerland and file the simplified non-resident registration form with the ESTV; the same UID-VAT number procedure applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A newly formed Swiss AG is filed with the cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister), which generates the Swiss UID (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) as the universal business identification. VAT registration must then be submitted electronically to the ESTV via the official VAT registration form (Form 15 'Registrierung zur Mehrwertsteuer / Inscription à la TVA'); the UID becomes the VAT number supplemented by the MWST/TVA/IVA suffix. A foreign entity with no Swiss seat must appoint a fiscal representative (Steuervertretung/représentant fiscal) domiciled in Switzerland and file the simplified non-resident registration form with the ESTV; the same UID-VAT number procedure applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box election; an Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily subject to entity-level federal direct tax and cantonal/communal corporate tax, with shareholders taxed only on distributions. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) small-business exemption election under art. 25 VAT Act for turnover below CHF 100,000; (ii) optional VAT taxation (Option) on real estate and certain inter-affiliate supplies; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) and patent-box elections on qualifying income; (iv) hybrid-financing instruments are decoupled per Swiss domestic anti-hybrid provisions, but no elective entity-status classification exists for treaty purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover (excluding exempt supplies) per arts. 10–11 of the Swiss VAT Act; businesses below the threshold may register voluntarily unless they cross-border supply inbound services subject to the acquirer-reverse-charge mechanism. Non-resident suppliers of digital and electronically supplied services to Swiss private consumers (B2C) must register from the first franc (single registration threshold of CHF 100,000 is not applied to them).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under art. 53 VAT Act where durable/supplied movable goods are physically exported from Switzerland and the foreign recipient is established abroad; international transport services and ancillary services under art. 23 VAT Act are also 0% rated when used abroad. For the Free Trade Zone / Bonded Depot undertaking (customs-bonded storage), supplies of goods remain outside the VAT charge (suspended) until removal for export, which then qualifies for export zero rating provided the goods leave Swiss customs territory under the customs supervision of the Federal Customs Administration and the exporter retains conclusive evidence of the actual export movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) processed electronically via the Swiss customs clearance system 'e-dec GV' with confirmed export release
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Transport evidence (CMR/air waybill or container track-and-trace record)
  • Bonded warehouse inventory register confirming physical removal
  • Depot operating licence of the Free Trade Zone (bewilligte Zolllager/zona franca licence)

Switzerland does not impose government-run real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction reporting or digital stamping. VAT-liable documentation follows art. 26 VAT Act with mandatory invoice elements and 10-year retention. The legal payment instrument is the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung; fully applicable since 2022) and E-Bill (Peppol/CH-specific network) is contractually recognised; VAT returns are submitted electronically to the ESTV on a periodic basis (quarterly or semi-annually) through the e-fiscal portal with aggregate data, not invoice-by-invoice clearance.

Under arts. 104–106 VAT Act, a groundless zero-rating claim triggers reassessment of statutory VAT with interest from the filing date and an administrative fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; wilful fraud additionally carries criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years under art. 105 VAT Act plus publication of the penalty decision. Customs-related deficiencies (false or incomplete export declarations) are sanctioned under arts. 120–123 Customs Act with retrospective clawback of suspended duties, loss of the bonded warehouse licence, and disqualification from privileged VAT procedures; Swiss tax authorities may apply a 10-year limitation period for misuse cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, with no exchange control restrictions under the Swiss Codification of Civil and Commercial Law or the Currency and Payment Instruments Act (CPIA). Dividend and profit remittance are not blocked, but a statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax applies to dividend distributions and similar payments; reduced treaty rates are available under Switzerland's DTA network. There are no licensing requirements for opening foreign currency accounts, entering into cross-border payments, or repatriating investment proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no conversion requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to hold export earnings in Swiss francs. Bonded warehouse operators may freely retain and reinvest export receipts in any currency. Only normal corporate income tax and VAT/reporting obligations apply, including the requirement to substantiate transactions with proper documentation and invoices.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, charged with monetary policy and national payment system oversight. Foreign exchange administration is not centralized; trade and capital flows fall under the general supervision of the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Foreign-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital banks that lack a FINMA license generally cannot open operational corporate accounts with Swiss IBAN facilities for a Swiss-domiciled company. Switzerland permits a FINMA FinTech license for deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million without a full banking licence, and these entities can provide payment accounts for Swiss companies, but their cross-border clearing access is often limited. Fully offshore digital EMIs are generally treated as non-regulated intermediaries and cannot serve as the primary treasury account for a Swiss free zone bonded warehouse operator; they may be used only for auxiliary prepaid or virtual accounts, subject to the Swiss bank's AML aggregation rules.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax assessed on post-tax profit (effective rate approximately 7.83% of pre-tax profit); combined cantonal and communal municipal charge in Zug brings the aggregate ordinary statutory burden to approximately 11.9–12.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Verrechnungssteuer (Swiss withholding tax) on AG dividends, levied at source on distribution; refundable to Swiss-resident shareholders and reduced or refunded under a double taxation agreement to non-residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after fiscal year-end (30 June for a 31 December balance sheet date); automatic extension to 31 December upon request, and further extension is regularly granted for professionally prepared audited filings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Swiss Local File or Master File thresholds exist; the ESTV and cantonal tax administrations apply OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and request documentation when related-party transactions exceed commercial materiality (in practice after related-party turnover above approximately CHF 500,000–1,000,000); a mandatory Country-by-Country Report notification and filing arises for Swiss MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a centre-of-administration residency test and an economic-substance review for treaty entitlements and withholding-tax refunds; a letterbox vehicle lacks substance. A free-trade-zone bonded depot operated as an AG with permanent local management, warehouse staff, customs-bonded full records, office premises, and locally documented pricing decisions satisfies the administrative practice; the Swiss treaty network embeds OECD PPT/LOB clauses, so the depot's actual warehousing function is decisive.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion — under-declared income / false returns

Statutory sanction: Imposition of the evaded tax plus interest and a fine from one to three times the evaded amount under Art. 175 DBG; repeated, wilful, and concealed evasion is prosecuted as tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG with custodial penalties up to three years and forfeiture of unjust gains.

Director personal liability: Board members and the managing director who participated in or tolerated the evasion are personally liable under Art. 754 CO for resulting company losses; individuals face criminal fines, imprisonment, and the use of Swiss mutual legal assistance — passport confiscation and travel restrictions may follow upon formal extradition proceedings outside Switzerland.
Disguised profit distributions / shareholder advantage

Statutory sanction: Tax authority recasts the advantage as a constructive dividend: additional corporate income tax, 35% withholding tax exposure at source, default interest, and potential evasion fines; the AG loses deductibility of the underlying payment.

Director personal liability: The shareholder is personally taxable on the deemed dividend; directors who approved the transaction cannot rely on the corporate veil and may be jointly liable for the 35% withheld tax and for civil restitution of diverted assets.
Shell / letterbox company and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, refund disallowed, and 35% Swiss withholding tax imposed; civil interest and fines for misuse; in international structures the authorities may reallocate profits entirely outside the Swiss entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintained the conduit structure face accessory liability for Swiss withholding taxes not withheld and enhanced fines; insolvency proceedings could subsequently expose the founders through liability rules for undercapitalised corporations.
AML / customs and VAT abuse in the bonded depot

Statutory sanction: Customs authorities assess unpaid duties and 8.1% VAT plus customs-evasion fines of up to the duty or tax evaded in aggravated cases; violations of the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act carry administrative fines of up to CHF 500,000, and criminal money laundering under Art. 305bis StGB carries imprisonment up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to install an effective AML framework at the depot are criminally exposed; confiscation of corporate and personal assets, director disqualification measures, and, upon criminal conviction, enforceable imprisonment or extradition affecting the right to travel internationally.
