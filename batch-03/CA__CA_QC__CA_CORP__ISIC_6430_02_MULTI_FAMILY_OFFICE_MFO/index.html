Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal corporate income tax (T2) and GST/HST, administered via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (canada.ca/revenue-agency); Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec corporate income tax (CO-17) and Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), administered via the 'clic Revenu' secure portal (revenuquebec.ca). Corporate existence and provincial identity are issued by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), which coordinates registrations with CRA and RQ through the integrated 'Concierge' service. Financial-services licensing is separate: the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates portfolio managers/advisers in Quebec.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or Quebec Business Corporations Act, filing articles with Corporations Canada or REQ; receive the 11-digit Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), which serves as the provincial master identifier used by RQ. Step 2 — Register the corporation with CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts: RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RP0001 (payroll). For foreign-owned entities, an appointed Canadian representative (agent for service) is required; non-resident directors must provide contact and residency declarations. Step 3 — Register with Revenu Québec (through the Concierge service or form LM-1) to obtain the QST account (RM0001) and Quebec income-tax account (RZ0001); registration is mandatory once taxable supplies in Quebec exceed CAD 30,000, and is required immediately upon commencing a taxable activity even below threshold if the business is carried on in Quebec. Step 4 — File annual returns (T2 federal, CO-17 Quebec) and periodic GST/HST and QST returns (monthly, quarterly, or annual depending on revenue and sector); maintain CRA/RQ records in Canada for at least six years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Federal and Quebec law do not provide a 'check-the-box' pass-through election for corporations; Canadian corporations are per se taxpayers under ITA Part I and Quebec Taxation Act Part I. Available elections relevant to a multi-family office: (i) functional currency election under ITA s. 261 for financial reporting (CAD or USD); (ii) s. 85(1) rollover election to transfer assets into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (iii) s. 83(2) capital-dividend account election to distribute tax-free capital-dividend reserves; (iv) specified financial institution (SFI) status elections for GST/HST input-tax-credit computation; (v) GST/HST s. 156 election for closely related corporations (delayed remittance); and (vi) hybrid-mismatch structural elections must avoid ITA s. 18.2–18.4 (deduction denial) respecting deductible payments to foreign related entities. For QST, parallel elections under the QST Act mirror GST elections; separate returns are filed because Quebec no longer harmonizes QST with HST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST): taxable supplies made worldwide (GST) or in Quebec (QST) over the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, excluding zero-rated supplies and financial services, determine mandatory registration. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies exceeding this threshold must register; below it, voluntary registration is permitted to recover input credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 where the supplier exports the goods (or e) or delivers them to a commercial carrier for export, provided the goods are not delivered in Canada; documentary proof of export (carrier documentation, freight waybill, export declaration) must be retained. Services: zero-rated under section 7 of Part V where the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not registered under GST/HST and the service is not for consumption in Canada — applies to advisory, consulting, or professional services, including discretionary portfolio-management and family-office advisory fees charged to foreign private-client families, provided the services are not rendered to Canadian-resident beneficiaries or in respect of Canadian real property. Financial management fees paid by non-resident clients may therefore qualify for zero-rating if the asset mandates and execution locations are outside Canada; fees from Canadian resident clients remain exempt (not zero-rated). QST mirrors the GST zero-rating provisions under the QST Act (sections 44 et seq.) with a corresponding QST rebate mechanism for prescribed non-resident services; exporters must obtain from the non-resident client a signed declaration of non-residency and non-registration.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or subject to export controls; commercial invoice stating HS codes and value)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (ocean or air carrier contract evidencing physical export, consignee name, port of lading and destination; must be dated and matched to the sale contract)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT Credit Advice (bank documentation showing the foreign-currency settlement received from the non-resident counterparty, together with the executed service agreement and non-resident declaration)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices and records if they are complete, legible, and retained in an auditable format (CRA 'Electronic Record Requirements' and RQ digital-records policy). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 model is voluntary and available through accredited service providers. GST/HST returns are filed via GST/HST NETFILE and QST returns via RQ CLIC on a periodic basis (monthly/quarterly/annual), not transaction-by-transaction. A supplier must issue invoices containing the BN, GST/HST registration number, or QST registration number; no pre-approval of invoice sequences is required.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for service exports; the 180-day benchmark is retained as a prudent bank/counterparty documentary-consistency standard for proving export proceeds. CRA and Revenu Québec audits zero-rating claims on substance-over-form, testing: (i) actual export shipment and delivery outside Canada; (ii) non-resident status and absence of Canadian GST/QST registration; (iii) place-of-supply rules; and (iv) contemporaneous documentation. Failure to substantiate triggers reassessment clawing back zero-rated treatment, plus interest (prescribed daily compound rate) and penalties: GST/HST false-statement penalty (ETA s. 285.1) of 25% of the net tax understated, escalating to 50% for gross negligence; criminal prosecution for willful evasion (ETA offence provisions and ITA s. 239) with fines and imprisonment; ITA s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalties for income-tax misstatements; and an extended reassessment period beyond the standard four-year window where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Records substantiating exports must be retained for at least six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no exchange control authorization is required for inbound equity injection, dividend, interest, or management fee repatriation; the Canadian dollar is freely floating and position-taking is unlimited; only residual compliance exists: (i) cross-border currency/negotiable instrument movements ≥ CAD 10,000 must be disclosed to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); (ii) resident corporations with specified foreign property > CAD 100,000 must file CRA Form T1135; (iii) hedging and financing of MFO treasury are unrestricted but bank counterparty credit limits under OSFI Guideline B-2 apply

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export or advisory fee receipts; foreign currency denominated proceeds may be held offshore indefinitely, subject only to bank CDD/SWD controls, FINTRAC record keeping, and CBSA threshold declarations; MFO cross-border fee settlement in USD/EUR is permitted in any correspondent account without exchange rationing

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial system authority; foreign exchange policy administered by the Department of Finance Canada (Federal-Provincial Fiscal Arrangements Act) and OSFI; AML/CFT reporting on FX transactions overseen by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for ancillary FX and disbursement rails; Low for escrow/custody. Canada does not issue an E-Money Institution (EMI) license; foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Interchange, Airwallex) can only operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, licensed under the Quebec Act respecting money-services businesses (Autorité des marchés financiers). Offshore EMIs may not hold discretionary client funds, act as qualified custodian, or provide portfolio-level custody for an AMF-registered MFO; their permissible role is restricted to low-value operational disbursements, liquidity velocity, and hard-currency spot conversion where the MFO maintains a one-for-one bank pre-funding arrangement and issues no client money float

Offshore EMIs may not hold discretionary client funds, act as qualified custodian, or provide portfolio-level custody for an AMF-registered MFO; their permissible role is restricted to low-value operational disbursements, liquidity velocity, and hard-currency spot conversion where the MFO maintains a one-for-one bank pre-funding arrangement and issues no client money float

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate; 14% combined effective on the first C$500,000 of qualifying active income (federal 9% + Quebec 5% SME rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under federal Part XIII (s.212(2) ITA). Quebec imposes no additional provincial dividend WHT; the federal rate covers the whole withholding obligation on dividends paid by a Quebec-resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Both the federal T2 and the Quebec CO-17 must be filed no later than six (6) months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. A CCPC's balance-due date is three (3) months after year-end; a non-CCPC must pay within three (3) months. Estimated instalments are due quarterly (monthly for large corporations); interest runs from the balance-due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 (CAD) in aggregate related-party cross-border transactions in the taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation under s.247(4) ITA and s.1090.0.1 of the Québec Taxation Act; the CRA applies a relaxed 'reasonable efforts' standard for SMEs with ≤C$15M gross revenue, but the formal Local File/Master File suite is prepared whenever cross-border related-party transactions exceed the C$1M threshold. The OECD CbC report is required only for groups with consolidated revenue ≥€750M.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a Caribbean-style ESR code; economic substance is enforced through the federal GAAR (s.245 ITA), the Quebec GAAR (s.1079.1 TQ), the sham doctrine, and the specified-investment-business provisions (s.125(7) ITA). For the MFO, the AMF requires full discretionary portfolio-management functions (investment committee, order generation, record-keeping, client due diligence, compliance officer) to be performed in Quebec with employees, premises and board meetings located there; a shell continuation risks (i) GAAR reassessment of fees, (ii) denial of the SBD, (iii) deemed residency where effective management is outside Canada, and (iv) AMF licence revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement (federal & Quebec)

Statutory sanction: Federal: s.239(1) ITA — on indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to two years (summary conviction up to $25,000 and/or 12 months), plus gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s.163(2)). Quebec: s.55 TAA — fine of 100% to 250% of the avoided tax and imprisonment; additional administrative penalties at Revenu Québec.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's taxes withheld or collectible (s.227.1 ITA / s.30.3 TAA) without a due-diligence defence; criminal convictions for conspiracy (Criminal Code) — imprisonment up to five years; assets abroad frozen; court-ordered masseI travel restraints in relation to the offence; director disqualification under the CBCA/QBCA.
Unregistered/Unlicensed Portfolio Management and Discretionary Trading

Statutory sanction: Quebec Securities Act (L.R.Q., c. V-1.1), s.208 et seq.: for a legal person, an administrative fine up to C$5,000,000 plus cease-trade orders; AMF may impose administrative monetary penalties up to C$2,000,000 per contravention and require full restitution to clients; criminal penalties include imprisonment up to five years less a day for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly engage in the unlicensed activity are criminally liable; AMF may issue director-occupation bans and disgorgement orders; civil liability for client losses, punitive damages, and possible extradition proceedings for cross-border activity.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Failure (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Since October 1, 2024, FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties reach up to C$4,000,000 per contravention for an entity (C$2,000,000 for individuals); non-registration is a separate offence: fines up to C$2,000,000 and/or six months' imprisonment (summary) or five years (indictment) on criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners of a private family office that deliberately structures transfers to avoid STR reporting face money-laundering charges under the Criminal Code (s.462.31) — imprisonment up to ten years; FINTRAC penalty amounts are due personally only where the conviction is obtained; practical exposure: extended personal asset-tracing, seizures under the Seized Property Management Act and Canadian/foreign MLAT assistance, plus travel bans as bail/gaol conditions.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Tax on Repatriations

Statutory sanction: s.215(1) and s.227(8) ITA: the payer is personally liable for the full amount of the unwithheld tax (25% of the gross) plus daily interest and late-withholding penalties; the gross amount may be recharacterised under s.15(1)/(2); Quebec s.1015 TQ imposes parallel re-assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutorily liable for the unremitted Part XIII amounts (s.227.1 ITA, no due-diligence defence for wilful default); criminal conviction for deliberately failing to withhold (s.239) carries imprisonment up to two years; solvent non-resident shareholders remain liable in collection actions abroad as CRA proceedings are enforced through the mutual assistance provisions of each DTA.
Sham / Shell Corporation with No Economic Substance (GAAR and SBD Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Federal GAAR (s.245 ITA) and Quebec GAAR (s.1079.1 TQ): the avoidance transaction is voided, tax plus interest re-assessed immediately; the C$500,000 SBD limit is denied entirely (associated-corporation attribution); gross-negligence penalty of 50% under s.163(2) for false statements; the entire MFO income may be recharacterised as property income (specified investment business) at 26.5% combined.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors who sign the false allocation or who orchestrate the shell have personal liability for the reassessed amounts through the directors' liability provisions; criminal fraud (Criminal Code s.380) where the avoidance exceeds C$5,000 — imprisonment up to 14 years; the CRA can apply for a court order freezing personal and corporate assets and prohibiting departure from Canada pending the audit.
