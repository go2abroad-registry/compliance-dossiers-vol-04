Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Québec Enterprise Registrar) – online portal www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca with counters in Québec City and Montréal.

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), arts. 2236–2245 (société en commandite / limited partnership); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1), ss. 22, 31 and 34; Regulation respecting fees payable under the Act (CQLR c P-44.1, r. 1).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account rule under Québec law (C.C.Q. arts. 2236–2245). The declaration must state the amount or value of each limited partner's contribution; a limited partner who participates in management forfeits limited-liability protection (art. 2238). Non-resident partners may contribute in any currency; the partnership issues units recorded in the partnership register rather than share capital. Low capitalization is acceptable for software publishing.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety or structural certification for a virtual head office. If physical premises are leased, obtain a municipal fire-safety certificate under the Québec Safety Code, comply with the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1) and CNESST workplace first-aid rules, and meet building and accessibility codes for the leased occupancy class.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST Registration Portal and Business Number (BN) account; Revenu Québec - Québec Sales Tax (QST) registration and Registre des entreprises (NEQ) portal for the Province of Quebec.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign-owned limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec must first obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) through Revenu Québec's Registre des entreprises, then register for a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account using Form RC348 or the CRA's Business Registration Online service, followed by a separate QST registration through Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V. The general partner (or its authorized representative) must hold legal authority to bind the partnership. The partnership itself is the registrant for GST/HST and QST; the general partner is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's obligations. Proof of foreign existence, registered address in Quebec, and a Quebec agent for service may be required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime comparable to the U.S. IRS § 7701. A Canadian limited partnership is by default a fiscally transparent partnership for Canadian income tax purposes, with income, gains, and losses allocated to partners. No elective entity-level taxation is available to a Quebec limited partnership. However, for GST/HST and QST, a partnership is treated as a person and may elect under the Excise Tax Act and QST legislation to have certain closely related corporations or partnership members report and remit tax; selected listed financial institution rules and partnership anti-avoidance allocations must be reviewed. A hybrid mismatch election is not statutorily available; taxpayers must rely on Canadian domestic law and the Canada-U.S. treaty applicable provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: A person or partnership becomes a registrant for GST/HST and QST once its worldwide taxable revenues from taxable supplies made in Canada and Quebec, including through permanent establishments, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. For a non-resident partnership whose only taxable supplies are zero-rated exports, registration is generally not compulsory but may be elected voluntarily to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada (including Quebec) by the registrant and to certain services performed for non-residents. For digital hyper-casual mobile gaming services, the supply is zero-rated as a supply made to a non-resident person where the recipient is outside Canada and no Canadian payment/collection nexus triggers taxation. Ad-mediation and platform payouts from AdMob and AppLovin do not themselves create a deemed supply in Canada; the app developer must evidence that the end-user downloading and gameplay occurs outside Quebec/Canada. Where a non-resident platform sells the game to Canadian consumers, the game publisher may be deemed to be carrying on business through a Quebec permanent establishment if the place of management or road-warrior activities are in Canada. Zero-rating of exported goods additionally requires proof of export within a reasonable time (generally 60 days) and possession of prescribed export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13 or equivalent electronic export reporting)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other carrier transport document evidencing delivery to a foreign destination
  • Cargo Control / Freight Waybill documentation demonstrating the goods left Canada in the same or substantially unchanged state
  • Foreign customs entry or proof of importation in the destination country, when available
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale showing the foreign buyer and sale price
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice confirming foreign currency payment (/foreign exchange repatriation ledger)

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscally cleared electronic invoicing or a central government clearance platform for GST/HST or QST. Electronic invoices are acceptable if the invoicing system produces a complete, legible, and verifiable record of supplier and customer information, tax amounts, dates, and tax numbers. Quebec however imposes a regulated electronic billing and payment infrastructure for certain business-to-business transactions through Revenu Québec, but this does not require cryptographic fiscal stamps. Taxpayers using software must ensure audit trail integrity and meet record-keeping obligations under the Excise Tax Act and the QST Act. For hyper-casual gaming revenue, the supply is generally an electronically supplied service; no customs-style clearance applies, but books and records must demonstrate the place of supply.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated returns and claw back input tax credits together with interest and penalties if the exporter fails to substantiate that the supply was exported or supplied to a non-resident. Penalties for knowingly making a false statement in a GST/HST or QST return are the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax amount, plus provincial penalties under the QST regime. Habitual disregard or fraudulent zero-rating claims may lead to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, criminal interest, and disgorgement of refunds. For digital services, the CRA uses taxpayer portal data, payment rails (AdMob/AppLovin payout records), and platform geographic metadata to challenge unfounded zero ratings. The exporter must retain electronic evidence of IP address geolocation by installed user base, platform reporting, bank wire/remittance records, and contracts with advertising networks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII and imposes no exchange controls or capital account restrictions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully convertible for both current and capital transactions. A Quebec limited partnership may remit profits and returns of capital to non-resident partners without exchange-control approval; the only fiscal impediment is the non-resident Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, typically 25% unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty. US-dollar receipts from AdMob, AppLovin and other ad networks may be retained in USD bank accounts in Canada or transferred abroad without mandatory conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory obligation requiring exporters to surrender, convert or repatriate export proceeds, and there is no prescribed timeline or conversion ratio for holding foreign currency receipts. The only FIAR-relevant requirement is that physical cross-border movement of monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); administered with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF enforcement, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises all federally incorporated banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex may be used as payment facilitators for low-value operational flows, but they cannot replace a Canadian Schedule I bank for capital escrow, profit distribution, or statutory corporate treasury accounts. Any EMI operating in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, obtain a money-services business permit from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Non-registered offshore EMIs will be rejected by Canadian compliance as non-compliant deposit takers. If properly registered, EMIs are acceptable only for outgoing micro-payouts and developer settlements, not for capital injections or partner distributions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal + Quebec general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). The Quebec Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent; no entity-level income tax is imposed and the rate applies at the partner level when a corporate partner is taxed on its share of LP income. For active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) partner up to CAD 500,000, the combined small business rate is 12.2% (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%).

The T5013 is due on or before the day that is 5 months after the end of the LP's fiscal period (e.g., 31 May following a 31 December year-end). Each corporate partner files its own T2 return within 2 months (non-CCPC) or 3 months (CCPC) of the partner's year-end; individual partners file T1 by 30 April.
