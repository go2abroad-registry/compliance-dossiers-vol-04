Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under the QBCA. The corporation may commence filing with zero issued capital, and shares may be issued for money, property, promissory note, or past services. No government escrow account or capital contribution is mandated; if a shareholder subscribes but does not fully pay, the unpaid balance is collectible by the corporation on the terms of the subscription and stated capital is recorded in accordance with QBCA accounting rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal income tax and GST/HST; portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online); Revenu Québec (Quebec income tax and QST; portal: CliCréQ Affaires / Revenu Québec business account); Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec enterprise registry, NEQ issuance).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online. The applicant selects the corporate income tax program account (RC) and the GST/HST program account (RT). If the corporation has an establishment in Quebec or carries on taxable activity in Quebec, it must also obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and register with Revenu Québec for a QST account (NEQ plus TQ program identifier). Registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. A foreign corporation may need to designate an agent or attorney for service in Quebec; no minimum paid-up capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act or the Quebec Taxation Act; a business corporation is always taxed as a separate taxable entity and is never eligible for pass-through classification. Certain limited elections are available for foreign-source surplus, non-resident rental income, and non-resident payroll; however, none can recharacterize the entity as a disregarded hybrid for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; QST registration is required for any person who is not a small supplier and carries on a taxable supply business in Quebec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods exported directly outside Canada may be zero-rated for GST/HST/QST. Data-labeling, annotation, and RLHF AI-training services supplied to a non-resident client are generally zero-rated as services supplied to a non-resident person, provided the services do not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and are not rendered to an individual in Canada. The non-resident client must not be registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply unless specific exceptions apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for physical export of goods, where applicable
  • Commercial invoice or service contract clearly identifying the zero-rated supply
  • Bill of Lading or other transportation document evidencing physical exportation of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation showing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Evidence of the client's non-resident status (e.g., foreign tax residency certificate or overseas business registration)
  • Internal reconciliation ledger matching invoices, payments, and GST/HST/QST returns

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose real-time tax clearance, API fiscal stamping, or mandatory digital clearance. Electronic invoices are permitted if they contain the prescribed information: supplier name, BN/QST registration number, date, nature, and tax amounts. Pan-Canadian PEPPOL voluntary e-invoicing standards may be used; Quebec public-sector contracts may impose electronic invoice exchange requirements, but there is no centralized real-time CRA or Revenu Québec validation/approval mechanism.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated supplies and claw back previously claimed input tax credits where the exporter fails to retain documentary substantiation for the statutory record-keeping period of at least six years. False or grossly negligent statements in returns can trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including 50% of the understated tax for misrepresentation, compounded interest, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. Where misrepresentation is proven, the normal four-year limitation period may be extended retroactively; Quebec imposes similar penalty exposure and may hold directors jointly liable for unremitted QST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. No exchange control permit or Bank of Canada authorization is required for foreign equity injections, dividend remittances, or service payments. Only general AML and tax compliance obligations remain, including residency, beneficial ownership identification, and Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-residents. Quebec's tax seasonality does not bear on convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not prescribe any statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold funds in foreign currency accounts and repatriate at their discretion. Any international electronic fund transfer equal to or greater than CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC by the banking institution, and the client may be required to complete a 'Large Cash or International Wire Verification' form.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border fund transparency; OSFI supervises the banking sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - viable for small international disbursements but not for escrow capital injection. Offshore EMIs (including Wise Platform and Payoneer) must register with FINTRAC as foreign money services businesses (MSBs) when servicing Canadian clients, and they are not regulated as banks under the Bank Act; they cannot hold deposits in Canada without a federal banking licence. For BPO payroll and contractor payments, permissible as a distribution rail under Fintrac MSB registration, but must not be the sole treasury rail for statutory capital or tax accrual.
