Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial: Revenu Quebec (RQ) - Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). Corporate registries: Corporations Canada (federal) and Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ), which issues the 10-digit Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). Revenu Quebec administers provincial QST and, under federal-provincial agreement, also collects federal GST/HST for Quebec-based registrants.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the CA_CORP entity under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; obtain articles of incorporation; register further with REQ to obtain the NEQ (Quebec enterprise number). Non-resident ownership is fully permitted; under CBCA at least 25% of directors must be Canadian residents, while QBCA requires a majority of directors to be resident Canadians. Step 2 - Apply to CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO using Form RC1 (domestic) or RC101 (non-resident corporation); request the following program accounts: RT (corporate income tax), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll source deductions) if hiring, and RM (import/export) only for goods. For a Quebec helpdesk SaaS corporation, the RT and RC accounts are mandatory; the RP account is required once employees are engaged. Step 3 - Register with Revenu Quebec for the QST account under the NEQ (program account SI or TR for resident; NR for non-resident), source deductions (AS), employer health-services fund (CN), and CNESST coverage if applicable. Step 4 - Foreign or non-resident SaaS suppliers making taxable supplies to Canadian or Quebec consumers must, where the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded, register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240(1.2) and for QST under QSTA s. 415.1 using a simplified regime; this is supplementary to, and does not replace, the domestic registration of a Canadian-resident CA_CORP. Step 5 - Enrol for CRA My Business Account and RQ CléSigne digital credentials; file all registrations electronically; retain CRA confirmation letters and Revenu Quebec notices as proof of statutory registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. CA_CORP, being incorporated in Canada, is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and is the sole taxpayer for federal Part I tax and Quebec Taxation Act tax on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits for foreign-source income. No pass-through treatment applies; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends, and non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding. If the corporation is Canadian-controlled (CCPC), the small business deduction applies to the first C$500,000 of active business income, yielding a combined federal and Quebec rate of approximately 12.2%; the general combined rate is approximately 26.5%. The foreign affiliate exempt-surplus rules provide a territorial carve-out only for qualifying treaty-country affiliates.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; classification is statutory. Available elections include ITA s. 85(1)/(2) rollover of property into a corporation, ITA s. 86 share reorganization, ITA s. 256.1(1) deemed acquisition of control, ITA s. 216(1) election for non-resident rental income, ETA s. 156 election for supplies between closely related corporations (nil consideration), and ETA s. 273 joint election for taxable supplies. Hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 and Quebec Taxation Act ss. 1032.0.1+ (effective 1 July 2022) are mandatory de-duplication and re-determination rules with limited carve-outs for treaty-resident entities and certain transparent 'flow-through' structures; they provide no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000; GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148 measured over any four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies (for residents) or taxable supplies to Canadian consumers (for non-resident digital suppliers); QST threshold mirrors the federal threshold under QSTA s. 19. For non-resident suppliers of digital services to Quebec consumers, the first taxable supply may trigger immediate registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Helpdesk SaaS and conversational-bot cloud services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (services supplied to a non-resident) where the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; supplies of software licences/intangible personal property to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23. QSTA ss. 200-207 mirror these rules for services exported from Quebec. Where the non-resident recipient is registered under GST/HST, zero-rating continues to apply only if the service is acquired primarily for the recipient's commercial activity outside Canada. The supplier must demonstrate, through contracts and business records, that the place of supply is outside Canada; supply to Canadian consumers remains taxable at 14.975%.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (ITA s. 152(4)(b); ETA s. 298(1); QSTA s. 436); the period is unlimited where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud, and may be extended to eight years where a waiver is filed. If export zero-rating is denied, the supplier is personally liable for GST/HST and QST omitted, plus daily compound interest, with no right to recover the tax from the customer. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains de facto full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and there are no general exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees by non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard non-resident withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Quebec provincial tax rules, as well as country-specific tax treaty requirements. Cross-border equity injections are subject only to Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds and standard FINTRAC reporting for large transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion or repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters and software-as-a-service providers may retain foreign currency earnings in domestic or offshore foreign-currency bank accounts and deploy them freely for settlement, reinvestment, or hedging, subject only to ordinary income recognition in Canadian tax filings and FINTRAC reporting for transactions of C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign exchange administration is not separately consolidated, with policy implementation under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Account managed by the Minister of Finance and operationalized by the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized to operate as banks in Canada. They may engage in limited payment functions after registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada. However, for a Quebec business corporation handling helpdesk SaaS subscription revenues, a Canadian chartered bank account is the primary treasury rail; offshore EMIs are acceptable only as ancillary settlement collection channels, not as principal corporate operating or escrow accounts. Legal constraints include conformance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC registration obligations, and the requirement to avoid using the term 'bank' in any marketing or contract language.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian chartered bank account under the exact corporate legal name and Quebec registre number. The remitter must classify the transfer using a purpose code corresponding to direct equity investment, such as SWIFT category 'EQUI' (Equity Investment), with supporting board resolution and subscription agreement attached in the message field 72 if the bank requires. For shareholder loans, use the SWIFT purpose code 'GADP' (General Administration) or 'CORP' (Corporate Credit) with a notarized loan agreement. The receiving Canadian bank will verify the beneficial owner on the wire, and any transfer exceeding C$10,000 triggers an electronic funds transfer report to FINTRAC. Crediting is immediate through Payments Canada's Lynx system, and the funds must be designated in the bank's internal records as paid-in capital or shareholder loan on the corporate ledger.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 for low-value recurring helpdesk licensing fees and through commercial letters of credit (documentary credits) issued by a Canadian chartered bank for high-value hardware procurement or vendor contracts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined: Federal Part I 15% + Quebec provincial 11.5% (2024); non-CCPC / foreign-controlled corporations pay the full 26.5% rate on all active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident (Quebec) corporation to a non-resident shareholder; no separate Quebec provincial withholding on cross-border dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months of the fiscal year-end. Balance of Part I tax due within 2 months after year-end (general corporation) or 3 months (CCPC eligible for the small business deduction); quarterly instalments where Part I tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 must be filed within 6 months of the fiscal year-end (matching T2); provincial balance due within 2–3 months of year-end; quarterly instalments for corporations with provincial tax payable above CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold under ITA s.247; CRA applies risk-based documentation reviews. Country-by-country reporting is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 750,000,000 (EUR 750M). Penalty threshold: 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245; Quebec Taxation Act s.1079.1); (ii) the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test applicable to Canadian tax treaties; (iii) the common-law corporate-residence test of 'central mind and management'; and (iv) Quebec civil law art. 317 CCQ, which disregards juridical personality where it is used to defeat the interests of the tax administration. A Quebec helpdesk corporation receiving Canadian business income must maintain real presence — Quebec-based directors' meetings, employees, contracts, books and records — to preserve treaty residence and avoid being recharacterized as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / unreported revenue

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded (ITA s.163(2); Quebec Taxation Act s.1049); criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (indictable) or 2 years (summary); daily compound interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII tax under ITA s.227.1 and Quebec Tax Administration Act s.24.0.1; travel restrictions via CRA departure certificates (ITA s.226); criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for consenting/acquiescing.
