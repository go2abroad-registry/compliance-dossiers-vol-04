Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible as an address for administrative correspondence and mail forwarding, but it must be a physical place in Québec capable of receiving service of process and located in the municipal territory of the declared establishment. Pure mailbox or non-physical virtual addresses are not compliant with the REQ's requirement to indicate an actual establishment (établissement) in Québec. For a cross-border B2B marketplace, Revenu Québec may disregard a merely virtual office when assessing permanent establishment income and Quebec value-added tax (TVQ); the partnership should maintain an operational presence through remote management or leased commercial space if substance is to survive audit.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account; no exchange-control permits, no capital-export restrictions, no mandatory CAD conversion, and no restrictions on holding foreign currency bank accounts in Canada. Profit remittance by a Quebec Limited Partnership is unrestricted from a treasury perspective, with the only relevant hurdle being tax compliance: non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25% on dividends, interest, rents, royalties and certain partnership distributions, reduced to treaty rates of 5–15% where applicable), and Quebec may impose its own provincial withholding obligations on payments to non-residents. CAD, USD, EUR and offshore CNH accounts may be opened and maintained freely, and funds may be remitted offshore without central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into CAD. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely; the only compliance obligations are standard corporate tax recognition of foreign income and FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting where transactions exceed C$10,000 individually or in aggregate within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central bank and supervisor of payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act, together with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for prudential bank supervision and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) for AML/CFT and monetary reporting. Canada maintains no foreign exchange control authority and no exchange control statute; the Exchange Control Act was terminated in 1951, and the Canadian dollar is fully subject to IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for FINTRAC-registered international payment service providers and digital PSPs; Low for unlicensed offshore EU/UK EMIs. A foreign EMI may not lawfully open settlement or operating accounts in Canada without registration as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and, for Quebec operations, without complying with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) money-services-business regime on insurance and money-services licensing. Schedule I Canadian banks generally refuse to inter-operate with, or settle for, unlicensed offshore EMIs, and the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act framework (Royal Assent 2024, phased implementation) now requires registration of payment service providers engaged in Canadian payment activities. The recommended structure is to maintain primary CAD/USD settlement accounts at Schedule I banks and to use a FINTRAC-registered PSP strictly as a collection rail for Alibaba.com Pay pay-ins from China and other Asian markets.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque non-resident ownership chain above the Quebec LP; inability to document natural-person ultimate beneficial owners at or above the 25% threshold.

Root cause: Opaque non-resident ownership chain above the Quebec LP; inability to document natural-person ultimate beneficial owners at or above the 25% threshold.

Defense evidence: Notarized organizational chart tracing every intermediate vehicle to natural persons; certified partnership agreement and Registraire des entreprises registration certificate; UBO register identifying all 25%-plus owners; passport copies; recent residential proof; constitutive documents and corporate resolutions of each holding entity; audited financial statements; signed declaration of no nominee or trustee arrangements covering the LP.
Rapid inbound capital injection from Asia or other offshore jurisdictions via SWIFT wires, with size or velocity inconsistent with the declared ISIC 4791 B2B marketplace business plan.

Root cause: Rapid inbound capital injection from Asia or other offshore jurisdictions via SWIFT wires, with size or velocity inconsistent with the declared ISIC 4791 B2B marketplace business plan.

Defense evidence: Executed capital contribution and subscription agreements; escrow instruction letters; source-of-wealth declarations from each investing partner; bank reference letters from top-tier originating banks; board or limited-partner resolutions approving the capital raise; audited or management financials of contributors; milestone-based business plan with projected transaction volume.
Cross-border B2B marketplace disbursement pattern characterized by high-frequency payments to third-country merchants (US, EU, China) and potential sanctions/PEP screening hits.

Root cause: Cross-border B2B marketplace disbursement pattern characterized by high-frequency payments to third-country merchants (US, EU, China) and potential sanctions/PEP screening hits.

Pass-through tax classification and non-resident partner participation triggering enhanced due diligence on the LP's legal substance, including any nominal general partner or agency-like wording.

Root cause: Pass-through tax classification and non-resident partner participation triggering enhanced due diligence on the LP's legal substance, including any nominal general partner or agency-like wording.

Defense evidence: Legal opinion from a Quebec lawyer confirming the limited partnership is validly constituted under the Civil Code of Quebec and is tax-transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; evidence of economic substance including Canadian office, records and operational oversight; CRA filings and Part XIII remitter registration where relevant; fund-flow description differentiating capital contributions, distributions and management fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital is SWIFT MT103 (for CAD and EUR) and Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent leg for USD, credited to the LP's CAD/USD operational account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remitter must pass full KYC with passport-level identification, tag 70 should state the clear economic purpose (e.g. Capital contribution under Limited Partnership Agreement, ISIC 4791 platform operations), and the originating bank must provide traceable intermediary chain. CAD-final clearing occurs via Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS); USD clears through New York CHIPS/Fedwire. FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting applies only where the movement exceeds C$10,000 in a single transaction or within a 24-hour aggregate. No Canadian exchange-control permit or official purpose-code registration is required.Primary outbound rails are SWIFT MT103 for commercial payments to B2B suppliers and Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700, typically sight LC) for high-value wholesale procurement requiring document-based settlement, with MT760 bank guarantees where foreign counterparties demand security. CAD disbursements clear via Lynx, USD via CHIPS/Fedwire, and EUR via TARGET2 through corresponding banking relationships. Marketplace payouts to Chinese merchants on the Alibaba.com Pay side are consolidated through a FINTRAC-registered PSP and reconciled in the treasury ledger before final reimbursement from the LP's Schedule I settlement account; digital CAD payouts to domestic merchants may use Payments Canada Real-Time Rail (RTR) where commercially available, with EFT fallback.Deliverable FX Forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR and offshore CNH deliverable forwards) with authorized dealer banks of Schedule I standing and tenors generally up to 12 monthsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in CNY for hedging onshore Chinese exporter receivables where deliverable CNH is not availableCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) between CAD, USD and RMB/CNH for interest-bearing capital injections and long-dated treasury exposureVanilla FX Options and Zero-Cost Collars on CAD/USD and USD/CNH for asymmetric hedging of marketplace pricing riskExchange-Traded FX Futures on CME (CAD/USD futures and CNH/USD futures) as a transparent listed hedge overlayShort-dated FX Swaps, Tom-Next and Forward-Forward roll structures for daily liquidity and clearing inventory management under ISDA/CSA collateral arrangements
