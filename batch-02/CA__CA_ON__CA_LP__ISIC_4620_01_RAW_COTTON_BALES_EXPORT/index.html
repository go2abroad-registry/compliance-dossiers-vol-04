Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal competent authority responsible for Business Number (BN) issuance, GST/HST registration, income tax and partnership information returns (T5013), with the primary online portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html via Business Registration Online (BRO). In Ontario, the provincial component of the HST is administered by the Ontario Ministry of Finance through the same CRA filing infrastructure; customs export declarations are cleared through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Single Window initiative.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and non-resident investors establishing an Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) must obtain Canadian statutory tax identifiers through the following CRA procedure: (1) File the LP declaration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act to obtain the provincial certificate of limited partnership; (2) apply for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and program accounts by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts) or by using the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, selecting 'Limited Partnership' as the legal entity and providing the LP's full legal name, the general partner's details and a Canadian address of record or Canadian agent for service; (3) for a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada, attach the limited partnership agreement, foreign jurisdiction certificates of good standing for each general partner, and a completed CRA non-resident questionnaire, and specifically request the GST/HST (RT) account; (4) where the LP has no permanent establishment in Canada, CRA may impose a security deposit (typically up to 50% of estimated annual net GST/HST) and require appointment of a Canadian representative for service of process; (5) after approval, CRA issues the BN plus separate program accounts (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax and RP for payroll if applicable); (6) for export trade, the LP must also register with the CBSA as an exporter to obtain a customs exporter identifier for the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) and, if triggered under Ontario transparency rules, file the significant-control-person registry information. The T5013 Partnership Information Return must then be filed annually within five months of the LP's fiscal year-end.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada operates no US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. For federal income tax purposes, the Ontario LP is mandatorily transparent (pass-through) under ITA ss. 96-103: the partnership files an information return but all income, gains, deductions and credits are allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is statutorily treated as a separate person under ETA s. 272 but may jointly elect under ETA s. 273 with all registrant partners to have the LP disregarded as a separate supplier, in which case each partner is deemed to make supplies in proportion to its interest. Additional available elections include partnership fiscal-period alignment elections permitting a common non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period, and the hybrid mismatch neutrality elections under ITA ss. 18.2-18.3 (in force for payments made after 30 June 2022), which determine whether deduction denial applies where the LP is treated as opaque in a foreign jurisdiction such as the United States. A US entity classification election (Form 8832) is a domestic tax-only election and does not override Canadian statutory transparency or exempt the LP from Part XIII withholding on distributions to non-resident partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange-control regime under the Currency Act; both current-account and capital-account transactions, including profit remittances and equity repatriation, are generally unrestricted. Cross-border payments may be subject to reporting thresholds under FINTRAC and to withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act, with treaty relief available under the Canada-US Tax Convention and other applicable treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion timelines apply to Canadian export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in foreign currency accounts, although for income tax and Controlled Foreign Corporation attribution purposes proper books and records must be maintained.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada is the monetary authority; foreign exchange and banking conduct regulation is administered through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing surveillance is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. A non-resident electronic money institution (EMI) may not directly provide payment services in Canada unless registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, in Ontario, licensed as a money dealer or other payment-services licensee under provincial anti-money-laundering and consumer-protection rules. Corporate treasury can use FINTRAC-registered Canadian fintechs for cross-border payment settlement, but the fintech must maintain a domestic compliance presence and cannot substitute for licensed deposit-taking bank infrastructure for high-value trade settlements.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is imposed on an Ontario limited partnership solely because it is an LP. A mandatory independent audit arises only when: (i) the LP or its general partner is a reporting issuer or investment fund subject to securities legislation, requiring audited annual financial statements under NI 81-106; (ii) the partnership agreement, an LC-issuing bank, or an export finance facility contractually requires audited statements; (iii) the corporate general partner is itself a distributing corporation under the CBCA or OBCA; or (iv) the CRA formally requires audited statements because the LP's books prove inadequate. For a private cotton-brokerage LP, the customary standard is a compilation or review engagement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Ontario provincial 11.5% general corporate rate; note: Limited Partnership is a pass-through entity, not subject to corporate income tax at the entity level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for non-resident shareholders under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. However, for a Limited Partnership, distributions to partners are not dividends; the LP is a pass-through entity. Non-resident partners are taxed on their share of partnership income under Part I, and no withholding tax is imposed on the distribution itself. If the partnership were to pay a dividend (which is not typical), the 25% rate would apply, subject to treaty reduction.

The partnership must also indicate the proportion of income allocated to each non-resident partner. Late filing penalty for T5013 is CAD 25 per day, up to a maximum of CAD 2,500. Additional penalties may apply if the partnership fails to provide information to partners, and partners may face penalties for failing to report income. Interest on unpaid taxes accrues at the prescribed rate.
Box 2 (Income Allocation) Allocation of partnership income or loss to each partner based on the partnership agreement, including business income, capital gains, and other amounts. For non-resident partners, the allocation must be reported in Canadian dollars. The partnership must also indicate whether the partner is a resident of a treaty country and provide the treaty article under which relief is claimed. Late filing penalty for T5013 is CAD 25 per day, up to a maximum of CAD 2,500. Additional penalties may apply if the partnership fails to provide information to partners, and partners may face penalties for failing to report income. Interest on unpaid taxes accrues at the prescribed rate.
Box 3 (Expenses and Deductions) Deductible expenses incurred by the partnership, such as cost of goods sold, operating expenses, and capital cost allowance. Expenses must be allocated to partners in the same proportion as income. Non-resident partners must ensure that expenses are reasonable and supported by documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total value of all transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year). If the total value of cross-border transactions exceeds this threshold, the partnership must prepare and maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including a Local File and Master File (if applicable).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act, which can recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance. Additionally, treaty benefits may be denied under the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) provisions in certain tax treaties. The CRA may challenge arrangements that are primarily tax-motivated and lack commercial substance. For partnerships, the CRA may scrutinize the allocation of income to ensure it reflects actual economic activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Section 239). Penalties include fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil penalties may also apply, including gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes withheld or collected by the partnership under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. For tax evasion, directors can face criminal charges and personal fines. Travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR to deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (Section 245). Additionally, treaty benefits may be denied under LOB provisions, leading to full withholding tax exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner may be held personally liable for taxes arising from GAAR reassessments if they authorized or participated in the scheme. They may also face civil penalties and, in severe cases, criminal charges for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The CRA can adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000 and documentation is inadequate), and charge interest on the additional tax. For gross negligence, penalties can be 50% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the mispricing. They can be assessed personally for the penalty amount and may face criminal charges for tax evasion if intent is proven.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties include fines up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1,000,000 for entities, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they authorized or acquiesced to the non-compliance. They may also face criminal charges and travel bans if the violation is linked to money laundering activities.
