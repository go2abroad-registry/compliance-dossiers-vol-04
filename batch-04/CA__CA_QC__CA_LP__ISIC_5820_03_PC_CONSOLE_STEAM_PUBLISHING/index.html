Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada and, in Quebec, Revenu Québec; combined GST/HST and QST registration is processed through the single-window portal of Revenu Québec for any enterprise with a Quebec establishment, with the federal Business Number (BN) being issued through the CRA Business Registration Online portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Form the société en commandite (Limited Partnership) under the Civil Code of Québec and file the declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) and the partnership's juridical personality. Step 2: Open the partnership's GST/HST (RT) and QST (RQ) accounts with Revenu Québec, which, under the Quebec tax collection agreement, also collects the federal GST on behalf of the CRA; the 9-digit NEQ becomes the single BN-linked identifier used by both tax administrations. Step 3: A non-resident partner without physical presence in Canada may appoint a representative for service and file the non-resident declaration; registration is compulsory once the worldwide taxable-supply threshold of CAD 30,000 is exceeded, but voluntary registration and final-tax returns may be filed immediately to recover input tax credits during the pre-launch build phase of a game studio.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; an LP is fiscally transparent as a matter of statute (Income Tax Act subsection 96(1)). The Quebec LP is simultaneously a separate legal person under the Civil Code of Québec and fiscally transparent at the federal level, producing hybrid-entity characteristics relevant under BEPS Action 2. Available elections affecting tax identity include: (i) Excise Tax Act subsection 177(1) election permitting the partnership to register and account for GST/HST as a distinct person from its members; (ii) Excise Tax Act subsection 273(1) election for a qualifying partner to be the registrant for the partnership's supplies; (iii) Income Tax Act subsection 103(1) election to substantiate a non-pro-rata allocation of partnership income under a reasonable business-purpose basis; and (iv) ETA section 273.1 election for the deemed separate status of the partnership to be disregarded for simplified GST/HST recovery by non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA section 148 and the parallel QST rule in the Quebec Taxation Act; digital-store sales to Canadian consumers by the LP count toward the threshold, and marketplace facilitation rules deem Valve/PlayStation to collect tax on specified non-resident principal sales, which does not eliminate the LP's own registration duty for direct Canadian revenue)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: the supply of intangible personal property (including Steam/PlayStation PC and console game digital licences and publishing rights) to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated where the property is not used in Canada and no service is performed in Canada, and the supply is not made to a Canadian consumer via a Canadian retail presence; corresponding QST zero-rating mirrors the GST rule. For digital-store export sales the LP must establish that the underlying consumer-purchaser is located outside Canada, evidenced by IP/location data on the platform transaction, non-Canadian payment instrument, and contractual licence territory terms.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A export declaration for physical media/collector editions and for controlled digital-asset shipments; no customs declaration applies to pure electronic deliveries via Steam or PlayStation Store)
  • Bill of Lading (for tangible exports; for digital deliveries the platform licence agreement, digital delivery confirmation, and store settlement reports serve as the transport-substitute document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Canadian financial institutions do not issue an official IRC; a SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank account statement showing receipt of USD/CAD payout from Valve or PlayStation Store constitutes the functional equivalent)

No federal or provincial real-time fiscal clearance, SII-style digital stamping, or mandatory B2B API clearance is currently in force for GST/HST/QST in Canada; invoice substantiation follows ETA section 169(4) and QST counterpart provisions requiring supplier name, registration number, and tax amount. Quebec has announced a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing roadmap aligned with the PEPPOL framework for 2027-2030, but it remains proposed rather than enacted; optional PEPPOL and EDI exchange is already accepted as documentary evidence by Revenu Québec.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; denial of zero-rating converts the supply to taxable status and retroactively claws back the GST/QST refund with arrears interest and net-tax deficiency. ETA section 285.1 imposes a minimum penalty of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax for knowingly making false statements; ETA section 280.1 adds a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought; fraudulent refund claims attract criminal liability under ETA section 327.1 and comparable Quebec Taxation Act provisions, with imprisonment on indictment and full return of refunded input tax credits, plus prohibition from continued use of the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no exchange control laws, no quantitative limits on inward or outward remittances, and no prior authorization requirements for foreign direct investment into a Quebec limited partnership. Profit and capital remittances to non-resident partners are freely permitted, subject only to domestic source withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for distributions that are characterized as interest, dividends, rents, royalties, or certain management fees. For partnership allocations, a careful review of the Canadian partnership rules is required because the deemed disposition of a partnership interest or the receipt of a partnership distribution by a non-resident partner may trigger Part XIII withholding unless relief is available under an income tax treaty. In practice, no Bank of Canada approval is needed to convert CAD into USD, EUR, or any other currency for trade or capital purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. A Quebec limited partnership engaged in PC and console game publishing via Steam and PlayStation may retain 100% of its export receivables in the original currency of settlement (typically USD or CAD at the storefront payout level). The only operational compliance requirements are: (i) reporting of large international electronic funds transfers (EFT) of CAD $10,000 or more to FINTRAC; (ii) cross-border currency declaration (Form E677 / Canada Border Services Agency) for physical currency or monetary instruments over CAD $10,000; and (iii) standard tax filing of foreign income and deemed taxable Canadian establishments. No deposit of export earnings into a designated government-controlled account is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority with exclusive authority over monetary policy and currency issuance. Foreign exchange administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and measures/control instruments applied under the Financial Administration Act. The day-to-day supervision of banking entities is executed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces anti-money laundering and terrorist financing obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign digital banking platforms and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and Mercury are lawfully accessible to a Canadian limited partnership, but they do not hold a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II banking license and are not members of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). Their Canadian-facing operations must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC if they provide transfer services to or from Canada. For a game publisher whose receivables originate from Steam (Valve) and PlayStation Store (Sony), these platforms are useful for low-value, high-frequency pipeline collections, multi-currency virtual accounts, and fast settlement. Legal constraints include: no CDIC deposit insurance above the platform's own banking partner, no formal escrow warehousing capability, no letters of credit or discounting services, and heightened compliance scrutiny if the EMI operates as an unregistered MSB. The cautious blueprint is to maintain a tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account as the primary treasury vault and use a FINTRAC-registered fintech corridor solely as a concentration and conversion gateway for individual storefront payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant structure: contributions by non-resident limited partners should be sent to the Quebec LP's Canadian operating account via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'CUPC - Capital Contribution to a Limited Partnership' or the equivalent bank-specific beneficiary narrative. For CAD settlement, the rail is the Bank of Canada's Lynx RTGS system through the receiving Canadian bank; for USD, the rail is either the U.S. Fedwire via a Canadian bank's correspondent or an internal book transfer if the bank operates a U.S. clearing desk. Verification requirements include: a capital call memorandum signed by the general partner, the partnership agreement provision governing additional contributions, original bank confirmation evidencing the source account in the remitting jurisdiction, and a certificate of non-resident status (Form NR301 or NR302 where applicable). If inflow exceeds CAD $10,000, FINTRAC reporting applies to the Canadian receiving bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign digital banking platforms and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and Mercury are lawfully accessible to a Canadian limited partnership, but they do not hold a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II banking license and are not members of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). Their Canadian-facing operations must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC if they provide transfer services to or from Canada. For a game publisher whose receivables originate from Steam (Valve) and PlayStation Store (Sony), these platforms are useful for low-value, high-frequency pipeline collections, multi-currency virtual accounts, and fast settlement. Legal constraints include: no CDIC deposit insurance above the platform's own banking partner, no formal escrow warehousing capability, no letters of credit or discounting services, and heightened compliance scrutiny if the EMI operates as an unregistered MSB. The cautious blueprint is to maintain a tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account as the primary treasury vault and use a FINTRAC-registered fintech corridor solely as a concentration and conversion gateway for individual storefront payouts.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable to the LP itself; the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent. For corporate partners carrying on business in Quebec, the combined federal + Quebec corporate headline rate is 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners that are distributed from Canadian-source income of the LP. This is the default rate absent a treaty reduction; it is relevant to the LP because a non-resident partner's distributive share is treated as a Canadian-source distribution, not as a dividend from a corporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Report on Transactions with Non-Arm's Length Non-Residents) must be filed for any taxation year in which the aggregate value of non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Comprehensive contemporaneous documentation supporting arm's length prices (i.e., a Local File and, at the multinational group level, a Master File) is required when the Canadian taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000, or upon request by the CRA under the 'reasonable efforts' standard.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and the substance-over-form doctrine; a limited partnership used to route digital game publishing income without actual employees, offices, decision-making, or commercial risk can be recharacterized upon audit, especially if treaty benefits are claimed. The LP must have genuine business substance in Quebec (e.g., contractual transfer pricing, local operating staff, and active management) to maintain its status as a fiscally transparent partnership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Income

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the understatement of tax under ITA ss. 163(2); accrued interest from the due date; criminal prosecution under ITA ss. 239: on summary conviction a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the penalties where they directed, consented to, or acquiesced in the evasion; the court may order civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans (by order seeking a cross-border movement permit), and imprisonment for fraud-related offences.
Failure to File or Incomplete Partnership Returns

Statutory sanction: For T5013: CAD 25 per partner per day, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500; for the Quebec CO-15: an equivalent Revenu Québec penalty; interest is compounded daily; if the failure is made knowingly, a further penalty of up to 50% of the tax referred to in the return.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable to the CRA and Revenu Québec for unremitted source deductions and related penalties under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; failure to enforce filing compliance can result in director liability assessment and civil collection against personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the LP's income to the arm's length amount and impose a 10% penalty on the net transfer pricing adjustment under ITA ss. 247(3); the underlying corporate partners bear the resulting Part I tax deficiency plus interest; in egregious cases a 50% gross negligence penalty may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing are jointly and severally liable for the adjustment penalty; upon failure to pay, civil enforcement includes asset garnishment, director restitution orders, and a travel ban where the director attempts to flee the jurisdiction; corporate criminal liability can arise under ss. 247(7).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA can invoke GAAR (section 245) to deny the tax benefits and recharacterize the income; a penalty of 50% of the denied tax benefit is imposed; in cross-border situations, CRA may initiate a Mutual Agreement Procedure making the treaty benefit impermissible and voiding the LP's tax treaty claim.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are exposed to personal liability under the Canada Business Corporations Act for making false representations during audit; civil restoration, prohibition from acting as a director, travel restrictions, and up to 5 years imprisonment for inducing the creation of a sham company have been judicially upheld in egregious cases.
