Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible and floating currency. Canada imposes no exchange controls under the Currency Act or the Exchange Control regulations; both capital and current account transactions, including direct investment, dividends, interest, and management fees, are fully remittable. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax (typically 25%, often reduced by treaty) and must be reported through prescribed Forms NR4 and Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to cross-border transfers of $10,000 CAD or more, but prior regulatory approval for conversion or remittance is not required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters and logistics entities may retain, convert, or hedge export earnings freely. Compliance obligations are limited to transaction-level reporting to FINTRAC (for transfers over $10,000 CAD) and filing of B13A Export Declaration with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where required for statistical customs purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for Canadian-licensed fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs), such as Airwallex (Canadian MSB registration), Wise, and Knightsbridge FX, subject to FINTRAC registration and provincial money-services licensing requirements in Quebec. Offshore, non-Canadian EMIs are generally not permitted to service Canadian-domiciled businesses unless they hold a restricted Canadian money-services licence or are otherwise exempt under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Any target payment rail, including Airwallex Local Clearing, must route disbursements through a Canadian-regulated clearing partner to remain compliant. Direct injection into the Canadian banking system is best achieved through a schedule I bank account with an API-connected treasury layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity or shareholder loans must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the corporate operating account in CAD, using the payment descriptor 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' corresponding to the corporate minute book and share registry. For amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC receives the transfer report automatically via the financial institution. The wiring party should include the company's CRA Business Number (BN) and the Quebec enterprise registrar number (NEQ) in the remittance advice to accelerate credit. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions in goods, services, interest, rents, or royalties with each non-resident triggers mandatory Form T106 'Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents' filing and requires contemporaneous Local File documentation in CRA format (OECD-aligned chapters I-IV TPG). Master File is mandatory where total annual consolidated revenue of the multinational group exceeds CAD 50,000,000. Penalties for non-filing/insufficient documentation: CAD 100/day up to CAD 2,500 per T106; also, if TP documentation is inadequate, CRA can reassess at arm's length without the benefit of documentary support.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain sectoral Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to the UAE; however, economic substance is enforced through: (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) and Quebec GAAR (s.1079.28.1 Taxation Act), which deny a tax benefit where a transaction is an avoidance transaction lacking genuine economic substance/real business purpose; (2) judicial doctrines of substance over form and bare trusts; (3) the OECD BEPS 12 mandatory disclosure rules (optional CDRs in force for dais 2023+); (4) anti-treaty-shopping rules in DTAs and the MLI. A Quebec parcel consolidation hub must demonstrate substantive business operations (Canadian warehouse, sorting lines, employees, contracts, actual linehaul movement of parcels, invoice issuance) to sustain transfer pricing positions and the availability of treaty-protected income characterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Corporate Fraud

Statutory sanction: Federal: criminal tax evasion under s.239 ITA – fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evadedand imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of understated tax plus interest; Quebec: parallel offence under the Tax Administration Act with fine equal to double the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized, acquiesced to, or participated in the evasion are personally prosecuted; indemnification is unavailable under corporate law. Penalties include personal fines, imprisonment, seizure of personal assets, CRA/Revenu Québec liens, and court-ordered travel prohibition under s.28 TAA for tax debts exceeding CAD 10,000.
Shell Company Profit Shifting / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization (s.245 ITA) eliminating the tax benefit; penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the denied benefit; Quebec GAAR (s.1079.28.1 Taxation Act) equivalent reassessment; reassessments can lift the full 3-year normal reassessment period to 6 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, or indefinitely for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false transfer pricing certificates or structured sham transactions are jointly assessable for tax, interest, and penalties; can face criminal charges; travel bans and asset freezes enforced under the TAA; personal bank accounts and real estate subject to certificate filing at the Quebec land registry.
Non-Filing of Cross-Border Information Returns

Statutory sanction: T106: CAD 100 per day, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per return; T1134: CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500 per year; T1135: CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500 per year, increased to CAD 100 per day up to CAD 12,000 for gross negligence; provincial penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act for failure to disclose; also, the statutory 3-year reassessment window is extended to 6 years.

Director personal liability: CRA and Revenu Québec assess directors personally for penalties where the director was responsible for accounting and knowingly allowed the non-filing; amounts are non-dischargeable in bankruptcy; personal liability extends to the unpaid penalties and interest.
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 10,000,000 for entities; money laundering offence under Criminal Code s.462.31 punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment; proceeds of crime subject to civil forfeiture; FINTRAC compliance orders can suspend access to payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or facilitate laundering face personal criminal liability and imprisonment; court-ordered restitution; permanent debarment from payment system onboarding; travel restrictions under court orders; personal assets seized under the Seized Property Management Act.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding / Director Liability

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold: penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus 20% on a subsequent failure (s.227(8) ITA); interest compounded daily at prescribed rates; Quebec parallel penalties under s.17 TAA; the payer remains liable for the full 25% withholding even if not collected from the beneficiary.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 ITA for the full unremitted withholding taxes, interest, and penalties; the liability survives resignation and cannot be extinguished by bankruptcy for source/tax withholding obligations; Revenu Québec issues a certificate of lien and can prohibit the director from leaving Quebec when the debt exceeds CAD 10,000.
