Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration, via the 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) portals (canada.ca/revenue-agency); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial income-tax-adjacent levies and Employer Health Tax (EHT), with Ontario HST administered federally by CRA under the Ontario HST agreement.

E-government filing portal

Foreign LP procedure: (1) Confirm Canadian nexus — a non-resident LP holding FBA inventory in Ontario is generally carrying on business in Canada and should hold a Business Number (BN) before importing goods or collecting GST/HST. (2) Obtain the BN by filing Form RC1 via CRA Business Registration Online, identifying the LP, Ontario agent for service, partner schedule and ISIC 4791 internet-retail activity; open the BN-RT GST/HST program account and, for imports, the BN-RM import/export program account. (3) Where goods are imported under the LP's own name, register the RM account with CBSA and appoint a licensed customs broker under power of attorney; evaluate CRA's Deferred Sales Tax Program (DSTP) and Duties Relief Program to defer GST/HST payable at importation. (4) File GST/HST returns through GST/HST NETFILE (non-resident registrants may elect annual filing while below threshold) and file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually, allocating Canadian-source income to partners. (5) If Ontario employees are hired, add the RP payroll account and register for Ontario EHT and WSIB.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax; partners (resident and non-resident) bear tax on their shares of Canadian-source partnership business income, so the LP itself pays no entity-level income tax. For GST/HST purposes the LP is itself a 'person' and holds a separate registrant identity from its partners; no check-the-box or other elective entity-classification regime exists in Canadian law.

Available election forms

(a) ITA s. 249.1 election to use a non-calendar fiscal period for the partnership, affecting partner inclusion timing; (b) GST/HST reporting-period election (annual/quarterly/monthly) and eligibility elections for the Quick Method or Simplified Accounting Method; (c) ITA s. 216 election available to non-resident partners to file a net-income return for Canadian rental-type income (limited application to an FBA trading model); (d) DSTP election for deferral of GST/HST at importation; (e) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6) are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions that deny double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes and are not elective, although reliance on the Canada-US treaty fiscally-transparent-entity rule (Article IV(6)) is an available planning position for US partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory registration arises when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident vendors of 'specified supplies' (digital products and services) fall under the simplified non-resident registration regime; for a physical-goods FBA seller, Canadian FBA inventory establishes a carrying-on-business nexus and the importer-of-record role, so the LP should register before reaching the threshold and before any importation; Amazon Canada as marketplace operator is the deemed collector/remitter for facilitated sales, but the LP retains importer-of-record and income-tax filing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act to goods exported from Canada. The LP may charge 0% HST where goods are delivered from a Canadian FBA warehouse to a foreign buyer address and the LP ships or arranges shipment of the goods directly to the non-resident customer, or where the goods are placed with a common carrier for export under a through bill of lading. Offshore services performed for a non-resident are zero-rated where the service is supplied to a non-resident and relates to exported goods or is performed wholly outside Canada. The LP must substantiate each export with contemporaneous commercial, customs and FX settlement documentation; supplies of FBA inventory sold to Canadian consumers within Canada are taxable at 13% HST and are collected by Amazon as marketplace operator (or by the LP if the supply is not an Amazon-facilitated sale).

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • B13A Export Declaration (CBSA) or carrier/courier manifest export confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada to the non-resident consignee
  • Foreign customer purchase order or Amazon marketplace order record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or Amazon payout statement evidencing receipt of foreign consideration
  • Carrier delivery confirmation at the foreign destination warehouse or port

No real-time electronic invoicing clearance mandate exists as of 2025 (no fiscal stamping, no continuous transaction controls, no tax-authority API pre-approval for invoices). B2B e-invoicing through PEPPOL or EDI is voluntary; CRA requires registrants to retain supporting records (including electronic invoices and export documents) for six years and to present them on demand. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via NETFILE, and cross-border shipment data flows through CBSA eManifest and the B13A declaration — not through a tax-clearance stamping system.

Export zero-rating is a high-scrutiny CRA audit area. If the LP cannot produce a complete export and FX remittance paper trail (including payment evidence within 180 days of supply), CRA will recharacterize the supply as domestic taxable at 13% HST, reassess the net tax with daily compound prescribed arrears interest, and may impose: (i) the ETA s. 280 gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the net tax understated (minimum CAD 100); (ii) the ETA s. 285 false-statements penalty of up to 25% of the tax; (iii) prosecution under ETA s. 327 for wilful evasion, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; (iv) the normal four-year reassessment limit is lifted where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default is established; and (v) input tax credits claimed on imported FBA inventory and Canadian warehousing costs are clawed back when the underlying export zero-rating is overturned.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange rate regime with no central bank approval requirement for current account or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: a non-resident Limited Partner's share of Canadian-source income from a Limited Partnership may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25% under the Income Tax Act, reduced by treaty, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty), and the remittance itself is unrestricted. Banking transactions are subject to anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-client (KYC) compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). A clearance from the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) may be required before a non-resident partner's share of income can be remitted without withholding, typically via a waiver or reduced-rate certificate under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement for export proceeds surrender. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no timeline requiring export receipts to be repatriated or converted into CAD. Amazon FBA proceeds earned by a Canadian Limited Partnership (CA_LP) may be held in foreign currency (e.g., USD) in offshore or domestic accounts at the taxpayer's discretion, subject to Canadian income tax reporting of foreign accrual and currency translation rules (CRA functional currency reporting under Section 261 of the Income Tax Act is available only for qualifying Canadian businesses).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability with legal constraints. Non-resident Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Stripe, and Payoneer may offer Canadian-dollar or multi-currency settlement accounts. However, under Canadian payment services regulation, an entity providing payment services to Canadian residents may need to register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, may require a retail payment activity license. A foreign EMI without Canadian registration can service the Amazon FBA entity only through its licensed Canadian partner banks or as an unregulated payment facilitator, creating correspondent banking risk. The recommended structure is dual rail: a Tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account as the primary settlement account and a FINTRAC-registered MSB or RPAA-licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex Canada) for cross-border FX and bulk disbursements.

Root cause: Dual use of operating and escrow accounts and commingling of partner capital versus operating revenues may produce account freeze/suspension risk.

Defense evidence: Implement and document a corporate treasury policy that clearly separates: (i) capital escrow account for partner capital contributions, (ii) operational collection account for Amazon settlements, (iii) FX/clearing account for disbursements, and (iv) tax reserve account for HST/federal/provincial filings. Provide the policy and flow-of-funds matrix to the bank at onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection to CA_LP from non-resident partners: (a) Use SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) from the partner's home jurisdiction bank to the LP's CAD operational or capital escrow account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. Each remittance must reference the underlying partnership capital contribution purpose and include a structured payment reference matching the LP's name and Ontario registration number. For tax purposes, characterize any funds advanced by a non-resident partner to the LP as either capital contribution or shareholder/partner loan; ensure a written capital contribution agreement or loan agreement is maintained. Canadian exchange control approval is not required. For funds from a US partner, use cross-border wire via Federal Reserve/CHIPS/ACH, with a Form 8300 equivalent not applicable; CAD receiving account will clear via Lynx (Canada's high-value payment system) for same-day settlement if over CAD 100,000. Include purpose code 'CAPT' or 'CABI' per SWIFT category, or the bank's internal purpose classification; for Canada, no central bank approval or purpose code registration is enforced, but the bank may require a 'purpose of payment' declaration for AML/CFT screening. Payments exceeding CAD 10,000 may trigger FINTRAC reporting (large cash transaction reporting is for cash; international electronic funds transfers are reportable only in certain circumstances — in practice, banks report under suspicious transaction monitoring). Provide source-of-funds documentation (bank statements, signed capital subscription schedules) to the receiving bank upon request. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability with legal constraints. Non-resident Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Stripe, and Payoneer may offer Canadian-dollar or multi-currency settlement accounts. However, under Canadian payment services regulation, an entity providing payment services to Canadian residents may need to register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, may require a retail payment activity license. A foreign EMI without Canadian registration can service the Amazon FBA entity only through its licensed Canadian partner banks or as an unregulated payment facilitator, creating correspondent banking risk. The recommended structure is dual rail: a Tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account as the primary settlement account and a FINTRAC-registered MSB or RPAA-licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex Canada) for cross-border FX and bulk disbursements.
