E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Swiss AG is an opaque resident company subject to unlimited worldwide income tax liability (Art. 50 DBG), with territorial exemption for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate that are actually taxed abroad (Art. 52 DBG). No Swiss tax-exempt zones or export-processing zones exist; effective federal+cantonal+communal headline CIT for an AG ranges approximately 14.0%-21.0% depending on canton and profit level.

Available election forms

Swiss law has no US check-the-box regime; the AG is per se opaque. Available elections: (1) Finance-branch election (Art. 51a DBG) allowing a Swiss branch of a foreign group with at least 51% foreign shareholding and at least CHF 10 million branch equity to be taxed as an independent Swiss subsidiary and claim participation relief; (2) Notional interest deduction on excess equity (Art. 25a VStG) with annually published rates; (3) Patent box election (Art. 24b VStG) granting a 90% exemption on qualifying IP and software-related income; (4) R&D super-deduction up to 150%; (5) VAT group election (Art. 13 LTVA) consolidating related Swiss entities into one VAT account; (6) Participation relief on qualifying dividends and capital gains from holdings of at least 10% or CHF 1 million market value.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (Art. 10(2) LTVA as amended with effect from 1 January 2025; previously CHF 150,000); CHF 150,000 for non-profit and charitable organisations; voluntary registration below the threshold is available for businesses conducting an economic activity (Art. 11 LTVA), and foreign suppliers of e-services or B2B services to Swiss recipients must register once Swiss-sourced turnover exceeds the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the 0% export rate under Art. 23(1) LTVA applies when goods leave Swiss customs territory to a foreign destination; the export must occur in the month of supply or within the following five months and be evidenced by the customs export record. Services: 0% applies under Art. 23(2) LTVA for services directly related to exported or imported goods and international transport, and under Art. 23(3) LTVA for services supplied to a foreign recipient for the recipient's direct use or benefit outside Switzerland; in practice, cross-border B2B EMR SaaS supplies are outside the scope of Swiss VAT under the place-of-supply rule (customer's place of business, Arts. 20-21 LTVA), preserving full input VAT deduction, whereas B2C supplies to Swiss-resident consumers attract 8.1% Swiss VAT.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR freight document according to transport mode
  • Sales or SaaS contract including Incoterms and delivery terms

  • Proof of offshore use: licensee deployment records, server access IP logs, and foreign VAT registration numbers of customers

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance engine, no digital stamping requirement, and no mandatory API pre-clearance. Statutory invoice content is governed by Arts. 26-27 LTVA (supplier name/address/MWST number, supply date, description, consideration, tax rate and tax amount; simplified invoices below CHF 400). E-invoicing is admissible where data integrity, legibility and retention are guaranteed (Art. 26a LTVA); B2G e-invoicing with the Federal Administration uses Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 and Swiss eBill; no Continuous Transaction Control regime exists.

No Swiss statute imposes an FX repatriation deadline; however, ESTV VAT audits (Art. 79 LTVA) verify export substantiation and the 10-year document retention obligation (Art. 88 LTVA). Where zero-rating is not proven, the supply is retroactively taxed at 8.1% with default interest of 4.5% per annum (Art. 75 LTVA); assessment limitation is 5 years relative and 10 years absolute, and VAT claims arising from tax evasion are not time-barred. Deliberate evasion triggers a penalty of up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 96 LTVA) and possible criminal fiscal-fraud prosecution (Art. 14 VStrR); procedural omissions are fined up to CHF 8,000 (Art. 87 LTVA). Voluntary disclosure before audit notification can reduce or waive penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc for both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no licensing requirements for foreign direct investment, and no restrictions on profit remittances or dividend distributions. However, distributions to shareholders are subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35%, which may be reduced or eliminated under applicable double taxation treaties if the recipient provides the required tax residence certificate. Foreign exchange transactions in CHF are completely free, but commercial banks may require accurate purpose codes and standard anti-money laundering declarations for high-value or cross-border transfers. No exchange rate margin or surrender obligation applies to any CHF conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or minimum conversion ratios for export receipts in Switzerland. Export proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held in foreign currency accounts with Swiss banks, or converted into CHF at market rates without approval from the Swiss National Bank or any other governmental authority.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) that do not hold a Swiss banking license or FINMA fintech license cannot legally provide core payment or e-money services to Swiss-domiciled companies on a standalone basis. Such entities may provide support services through a white-label arrangement with a fully licensed Swiss bank or a licensed EU EMI that has passporting rights recognized in Switzerland. A Swiss FINMA-licensed fintech license (public deposits up to CHF 100 million without interest) is the most direct path for a regulated EMI vehicle, but this requires substantial substance, local audit, and compliance infrastructure. For the target SaaS entity, reliance on an unlicensed offshore EMI is not recommended in a regulated medical-data environment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Swiss AG must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA instant wire to the designated capital escrow account at a Swiss tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must include the corporate purpose code (e.g., '10' for capital contribution under the Swiss National Bank's external statistics nomenclature, or the corresponding bank's purpose code for direct investment), the beneficiary legal entity name exactly matching the shareholders' register, and the reference to the notarized board resolution authorizing the share capital payment. For transfers exceeding CHF 100,000, the receiving Swiss bank will require proof of the remitter's identity, source of funds, and a fully completed form 'K – Konversion' only if FX conversion is requested. No exchange approval is required, but the bank must verify the UBO declaration under FINMA Circular 2016/1. Capital contributions made in foreign currency are credited at the current market rate; the bank's FX confirmation (deal ticket) must be retained as the official conversion evidence for subsequent tax audits.Outbound operational disbursements for software licenses, sub-processors, and international trade settlements should clear through SWIFT MT103 for high-value vendor payments and SEPA instant credit transfers for EU-zone operational payments. For procurement of hardware or licensed medical device components requiring documentary evidence, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the Swiss bank is permissible, with standard UCP 600 terms. Disbursement instructions require the corporate account Austrian/ISIC purpose code (including '10' for services and '02' for goods), plus supporting invoices and, where applicable, EU customs declarations. All outbound MT103 messages must reference the underlying commercial contract number and be accompanied by an anti-money-laundering tax-relevant declaration when the beneficiary is in a non-EU jurisdiction. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on post-tax profit (7.83% effective on pre-tax profit), plus cantonal/communal profit taxes; ordinary aggregate headline rate generally 11.9%-21.0% depending on Canton and municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends and distributions from a Swiss AG; no separate cantonal withholding tax. Domestic qualifying shareholders may obtain a full refund; foreign shareholders must invoke a DTA or the Swiss parent-subsidiary directive to reduce or exempt the 35% rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is generally within 6 months of the fiscal year-end; extensions are routinely granted up to 12 months after the year-end (for a calendar-year AG, frequently 31 December of the following year). If the taxpayer is in default after a final reminder, a 10-day grace period applies before official assessment.

Deadline: · Frequency:

TP documentation must exist no later than the filing date of the corporate tax return. CbCR notification must be filed within 12 months of each group year-end; CbCR itself must be filed within 12 months via the automatic exchange of information mechanism.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss TP documentation/local file is mandatory when aggregate controlled transactions with foreign related parties exceed CHF 10,000,000 per fiscal year; a Master File is required when consolidated group revenue exceeds CHF 900,000,000; CbCR is required above the same group-revenue threshold. Practical tax authorities may demand lower-de minimis documentation for financial transactions, IP flows, or management fees.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not codify a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the tax-avoidance doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirement, Art. 6 of the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Tax, and OECD MLI standards require genuine economic activity: operational premises, qualified personnel, decision-making powers, risk management, and actual corporate substance. An AG used merely as a conduit for EMR/IP licensing, clinic software revenue, or treaty-shopping royalties may be recharacterized as a shell, with DTA benefits denied and income attributed to the real beneficiary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Procedural fine up to CHF 10,000 for obstructing authorities; evasion fine up to three times the evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG; qualified tax fraud with forged documents attracts imprisonment up to 3 years and monetary fine under Art. 186 DBG, plus default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or order the omission are personally liable for tax penalties and fines; criminal convictions can include up to 3 years imprisonment, travel bans, and personal asset seizure; in insolvency, directors are jointly and severally liable for tax damages arising from negligence.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority disregards the corporate entity, reallocates the income to the ultimate beneficial owner, refuses DTA relief, denies participation exemption and patent box benefits, and imposes full tax adjustment plus interest and procedural penalties up to CHF 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the empty structure to operate may be personally liable for unpaid taxes, restitution to creditors, prohibition from serving as corporate board members, and criminal fines or imprisonment in aggravated tax fraud cases.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code imposes imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine for standard money laundering; serious cases carry 1 to 5 years imprisonment; administrative fines under GwG can reach CHF 500,000; confiscation of criminal proceeds and forced winding-up of the legal entity can be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or authorized the AML breach face penal liability, asset freezes, bail restrictions, travel bans, and civil liability for damages suffered by creditors, investors, or the affected clinic network.
Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 40 VStG, intentional violation is punishable by a fine up to CHF 30,000 plus the outstanding WHT and default interest; aggravated cases carry imprisonment up to 2 years and a monetary sentence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the distribution are personally and jointly liable with the company for the unpaid withholding tax; criminal conviction may lead to fines, travel restrictions, disqualification as director, and personal asset execution.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing and Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: The tax authority can re-determine the taxable base using CUP, TNMM or Profit Split; disallow the deduction of the entire controlled payment, impose default interest of 3.5%, procedural fines up to CHF 10,000, and apply criminal tax-fraud penalties up to 3 years imprisonment if the documentation contains materially false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement non-arm's length intercompany pricing are liable for damages in bankruptcy for breach of fiduciary duty; criminal liability attaches for deliberate false documentation, and Swiss courts may impose personal liability for tax penalties, restitution, and board disqualification.
