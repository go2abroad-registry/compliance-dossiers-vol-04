Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/). Ontario coordination: Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Business Registry for provincial LP registration; GST/HST is a single federal-administered account, no separate Ontario VAT ID.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming an Ontario Limited Partnership must first register the LP with the Ontario Business Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990). The LP then obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 filed online through My Business Account, activating the RT (GST/HST) program account, which serves as the Canadian VAT ID. Registration is compulsory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is permitted immediately to recover input tax credits (ITC) on start-up costs. Non-resident partners do not require a separate Canadian TIN solely by holding LP interests, but must file Canadian tax returns on Canadian-source partnership income.

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; fiscal transparency of LPs is automatic under s. 96(1) Income Tax Act (ITA). Relevant elections for a CA_ON ERP/CRM cloud LP: (i) s. 97(2) ITA partnership rollover election on property contribution; (ii) s. 85(1) ITA elective rollover on taxable transfers of property to the partnership; (iii) s. 249.1 ITA election for a fiscal period different from the calendar year; (iv) s. 22 ITA election for disposition of accounts receivable; (v) GST/HST Quick Method or Net-Tax Calculation elections where eligible; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules (OECD BEPS Action 2, in force for taxation years beginning after 30 June 2022) operate as mandatory self-adjustment rules with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding GST/HST) over a single calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four consecutive quarters; non-resident suppliers of digital cloud/ERP/CRM services to Canadian recipients are subject to the same threshold once the supply is deemed made in Canada under GST/HST place-of-supply rules (ETA s. 240; s. 123 definition of 'small supplier'; Memorandum 16-2).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) applies under the Excise Tax Act where ERP/CRM cloud software is supplied to a non-resident recipient taking delivery offshore, where intangible personal property (software licences) is supplied to a non-resident under the place-of-supply rules, and where services are performed for a non-resident recipient for consumption wholly outside Canada. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence of export, foreign recipient status, and non-resident destination; export ITCs remain claimable, and refund/rebate is available where ITCs exceed output tax.

  • B13A Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CBSA) or carrier proof of export for physical deliverables
  • Commercial invoice and signed master service agreement evidencing offshore supply of ERP/CRM cloud services to a named non-resident recipient
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic carrier record for tangible goods components
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX receipt demonstrating receipt of export proceeds from abroad
  • Non-resident status evidence (foreign tax residency certificate, W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E, or signed non-resident declaration)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API stamping, or digital clearance mandate. Electronic invoicing is lawful where records are complete; the CRA requires invoices to show the registrant's BN/RT program account, date, consideration, and GST/HST treatment. Canadian public-sector e-invoicing uses the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework and the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework; CRA e-services APIs are available for account administration but are not mandatory invoice clearance infrastructure.

CRA may reject zero-rating where export substantiation is deficient, retroactively reassess full 13% HST, claw back related input tax credits, and charge compound statutory interest. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, extended indefinitely for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or fraud. Civil gross-negligence penalty: 25% of the net assessed tax (minimum CAD 250). Criminal tax evasion liability: fines of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to two years; partnership and partners may be held jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible with no exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions. Canada has long since accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittances are constrained by Canadian tax compliance rather than FX licensing: cross-border dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid by an Ontario limited partnership are generally subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, relief being available under an applicable tax treaty. There are no prohibitions on foreign ownership of partnership interests, and no exchange-control permit is required for inward capital contributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory surrender obligation, mandated conversion percentage, or deadline for export receipts under Canadian law. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts and settle at negotiated commercial rates through chartered banks or authorized payment service providers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is otherwise integrated into the OSFI-supervised financial system and the Currency Act framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore electronic money institutions and e-money tokens are not recognised as banks for Canadian clearing purposes. Unlicensed EMIs cannot directly access Payments Canada settlement rails or CAD liquidity. They may operate on a white-label basis through a Canadian chartered bank or federally regulated payment service provider, but must avoid accepting deposits or issuing stored-value products that trigger CDIC deposit-taking prohibitions and OSFI/Retail Payment Activities Act registration obligations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

The Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; no entity-level Canadian income tax. A corporate limited partner resident in Ontario is subject to combined federal and Ontario rates: general 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) active business income 12.2% (federal 9% + Ontario 3.2%) on the first CAD 500,000 of combined taxable business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident: 25%. For a limited partnership, ordinary distributions to a non-resident limited partner are generally a return of capital and not subject to Part XIII; however, allocations of Canadian-source partnership profits are taxed under Part I, and payments of amounts characterized as interest, royalties, management fees, or rents are subject to Part XIII at a 25% statutory rate. Under the Canada-US treaty, treaty-resident corporate partners owning at least 10% of voting shares enjoy a 5% dividend rate; portfolio dividends qualify for 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons in the taxation year (other than services); CAD 1,000,000 for cross-border services; when thresholds are exceeded, the taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation sufficient to demonstrate 'reasonable efforts' under ITA s. 247(3) and CRA IC 87-2R; otherwise a 10% transfer pricing adjustment penalty applies, in addition to interest and the reassessment itself.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the Canada Revenue Agency applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) requiring an economic or commercial substance test; transactions entered with a primary purpose of obtaining a tax benefit are re-characterized, and a 25% GAAR penalty applies to transactions occurring on or after June 13, 2024; the sham and substance-over-form doctrines also apply; the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) regime limits interest deductibility to 30% EBITDA for large corporations with net interest expense exceeding CAD 250,000 in fiscal years beginning after October 1, 2023; limited partner loss deductibility is restricted to the partner's 'at-risk' amount under ITA s. 96(2.1)-(2.2).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Criminal Fraud

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment for up to 5 years, or both; gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; CRA may obtain judicial enforcement orders including injunctions and orders preventing departure from Canada (writ of ne exeat regno) in aggravated collection cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner who authorize or acquiesce in the offence are personally liable for fines and restitution; criminal conviction exposes individuals to imprisonment and professional disqualification; civil liability attaches under provincial fraudulent conveyance and oppression remedies.
Non-Filing and Non-Remittance of Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 162(1): 5% plus 1% monthly late-filing penalty (12-month cap); s. 162(7): CAD 25 per day for T5013 returns with CAD 2,500 cap; s. 227(9): 10% penalty on unremitted withholding, 20% if the omission was deliberate; statutory interest accrues daily at the CRA prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for failure to remit withheld amounts if the partnership or corporate partner is insolvent; due-diligence defence is narrow and requires documented oversight of payroll and withholding functions.
