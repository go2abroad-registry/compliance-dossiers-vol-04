Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued for consideration authorized by the directors, subject to the articles, corporate law, solvency rules, and proper recording in the securities register. A bank account should be opened after incorporation, and share subscription funds should be paid into the corporation and documented by subscription resolutions and accounting records.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax. Primary portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec 'ClicSÉQUR Affaires' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/services-en-ligne/cles/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA, then add program account identifiers: GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and an import/export (RM) account. A non-resident may apply directly without Canadian incorporation; a Canadian representative or tax agent may be authorized. After federal registration, the entity must separately register with Revenu Québec if it carries on taxable activities in Quebec; Revenu Québec assigns a QST number and an enterprise number, using the federal BN where applicable. Registration as a non-resident registrant requires proof of foreign legal existence, registered address in Canada and/or a designated representative for service, and details of Canadian business operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. A Quebec business corporation is always a separate taxpayer at both federal and provincial levels. Limited elections potentially applicable in cross-border scenarios include: the Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident income tax filing with respect to Canadian-source rents/royalties, and treaty-based tie-breaker determinations regarding residence for a corporation incorporated in Canada but managed outside Canada. No hybrid mismatch election is available the exporter can use to alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of cumulative taxable supplies (before expenses) for both GST/HST and QST small-supplier thresholds; once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 30 days for GST/HST and within the prescribed period for QST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST and QST registrant may zero-rate export supplies of tangible personal property, including shredded copper scrap and non-ferrous metal waste, under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST rules, provided the property is shipped or delivered by the supplier to a recipient outside Canada, or to a non-resident recipient, and is exported as soon as is reasonably possible after the supply is made. The supplier must retain proof of export and must not use the goods in Canada before export. The zero-rating applies regardless of whether the foreign purchaser is registered for GST/HST or QST.

  • CBSA export declaration, Canada Customs Export Declaration (CCECD), or equivalent CARM/EDI export manifest record
  • Bill of lading / multimodal transport document showing Canadian exporter and overseas consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Weight ticket, scale report, and assay or grade certificate for shredded copper scrap
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for B2B invoices. Registered suppliers must retain invoices in an audit-trail format acceptable to CRA and Revenu Québec. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE once required, and QST returns through Services en ligne Revenu Québec. B2B e-invoicing may be transacted through EDI/PEPPOL but is not mandatory. For exports, customs declarations must be transmitted electronically to the CBSA through CARM-compatible EDI/API channels or its portal; no revenue-authority 'digital stamp' is affixed to invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating where the exporter fails to provide contemporaneous documentary proof of export. If zero-rating is denied, the exporter is liable for unreported GST/HST and QST on the deemed taxable supply, plus interest and late-filing penalties, with a retrospective reassessment normally limited to four years, extended to six or more years in cases of negligence or misrepresentation. Additional civil penalties apply under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec fiscal statutes for false statements, omissions, or wilful evasion, including penalties of up to 25% of the tax evaded and, for indictable offences, fines and imprisonment. Exporters involved in fraud may also lose refunds, be added to non-compliant registrant lists, and face customs penalties under CBSA legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control statute; the former Exchange Control Act has been repealed and the Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Trade payments, service payments, dividends, interest, royalties, equity injections and intercompany loans may flow without central-bank approval or licence. The only practical remittance hurdles are: (i) non-resident withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), normally 25% reduced by treaty, with parallel Quebec withholding under the Taxation Act (Quebec); (ii) FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) enhanced source-of-funds verification imposed by Schedule I banks on non-resident remitters; and (iv) the requirement that profit remittances be supported by board resolutions, audited financial statements and proof of arm's-length pricing, particularly where a Quebec recycled-scrap exporter distributes to a foreign parent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation deadline. Exporters may freely retain, hedge and disburse foreign currency receipts. Compliance anchors are instead documentary and statistical: (i) each export shipment over CAD 2,000 must be reported to CBSA through the CARM portal / B13A export declaration, and the associated transaction number should be retained as underlying-trade evidence; (ii) international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; and (iii) an export certificate, commercial invoice and bills of lading must evidence the zero-rated GST/HST and QST treatment of qualifying exported copper scrap, which in turn forms part of the banking audit trail for outbound trade settlements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no single foreign exchange administration; policy responsibility rests with the Bank of Canada, prudential bank supervision with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF supervision with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and export documentation with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury usage. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence; an entity accepting retail deposits is either a regulated bank (Schedule I/II) or a money-service business registered with FINTRAC, and payment-service providers must additionally register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, do not provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts, and commonly prohibit scrap-metal trade flows in their terms. They are acceptable only as a secondary collection gateway for small pre-shipment deposits, never as the exclusive settlement rail for documentary LC-backed copper wire exports.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital from a non-resident shareholder should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' for equity injection, 'LOAN' for shareholder debt, or 'TRAA' for goods pre-payment, into the Quebec corporation's CAD/USD operating account at the chosen Schedule I bank. No Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control form is required. Prior to the transfer, the Canadian receiving bank should receive a funds pre-advice package: NEQ registration, directors' resolution authorising the injection, UBO and source-of-funds evidence. The wire must state the remitter's full legal name, the corporation's full legal name and NEQ number, and the trip's economic purpose to avoid sanctions-policy false positives. FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. For inbound direct investment, Statistics Canada inward investment reporting thresholds apply; the injection must be documented in a CBCA/Quebec share register and, where applicable, registered with the Registraire des entreprises within the statutory period. Capital should be parked in a dedicated capital escrow sub-account for at least 90 days before application to trade disbursements.Outbound settlement to overseas scrap suppliers and tolling partners uses three primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for telegraphic payments, using purpose codes 'TRAA' or 'GDSO', matched against commercial invoices, weight lists and bills of lading retained as a transaction audit file; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700, typically confirmed by a top-tier foreign bank, with the document schedule comprising commercial invoice, full set of clean-on-board ocean bills of lading, weight/assay certificate, certificate of origin and pre-shipment inspection certificate; and (iii) domestic CAD settlement through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system or the Automated Clearing Settlement System, with USD cleared through the Canadian bank's correspondent account and Fedwire.
