Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national tax administration, registration portal (Business Registration Online / canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax policy, with Ontario corporate and HST components administered federally by the CRA under the Ontario Taxation Act, 2007 and the Ontario Harmonized Sales Tax Act, 2010; Ontario Business Registry for non-tax statutory registration of the extra-provincial limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Ontario limited partnership with the Ontario Business Registry (extra-provincial LP registration) as a condition of carrying on business. Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) for the partnership via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory tax identifier (TIN) for GST/HST program account 'RT'. Step 3: Each non-resident partner without a Social Insurance Number applies for an Individual Tax Number (Form T1261) or, for corporate partners, obtains a BN and registers as carrying on business in Canada if required. Step 4: The partnership registers for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold (or voluntarily to claim input tax credits on inventory acquisitions); an 'RT' account is opened. Step 5: The partnership files an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return with the CRA; no separate partnership income tax return is required because the entity is fiscally transparent. Step 6: Ontario tax registration is effected through the same federal T5013 filing and the Ontario partnership schedules; no separate Ontario tax number is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — federally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada): income, gains, losses and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners in each fiscal period; the ordinary limited partnership is not an entity-level taxpayer. Ontario departure: a 'large limited partnership' with any corporate limited partner and Canadian gross revenues above C$10 million and Canadian gross assets above C$5 million is statutorily deemed to be a corporation for Ontario corporate income tax purposes, imposing Ontario filing, instalment and permanent-establishment attribution rules on the partnership (Taxation Act, 2007, s. 50.1 et seq.).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or elective entity classification regime; legal form is conclusive. Available elections relevant to this LP: (i) subsection 97(2) election for tax-deferred transfer of property to the partnership on formation; (ii) subsection 98(3) election on partnership winding-up; (iii) joint elections under the Excise Tax Act to treat certain supplies between registrants and non-residents as zero-rated where statutory conditions are met; (iv) elections under income tax hybrid-mismatch rules (sections 18.2–18.4) and the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) framework to grandfather or recharacterize affected arrangements; (v) Ontario election related to the deemed fiscal period of a 'large limited partnership'. No election can override the federal pass-through status of a valid limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) of the partnership and associated persons over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration once exceeded for any person carrying on business in Canada; voluntary registration permitted for exporters to recover input tax credits on imported inventory and warehousing costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when (a) the contract for shipment requires the supplier to ship the goods to a destination outside Canada, (b) the supplier delivers the goods to a common carrier for export, or (c) the goods are exported by a non-resident buyer under prescribed conditions; shipment must occur within a reasonable time and the supplier must maintain documented proof of export. Services: zero-rated as a supply made to a non-resident where the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada and not performed in respect of goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; accordingly, warehousing and inventory handling services performed on goods located in Canada remain subject to 13% HST notwithstanding the non-resident customer. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline for zero-rating; payment need only be received in the ordinary course of business for the supply to be supported.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System / carrier e-manifest) evidencing departure from Canada
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or air waybill showing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire confirmation evidencing settlement from the non-resident buyer in foreign currency

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. The CRA accepts paper, PDF, and EDI invoices provided they satisfy the information content requirements of the Excise Tax Act and are retained for six years. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing framework (Peppol/BUSDOX network) is voluntary only; no CRA system clearance is required before issuing a GST/HST invoice or export zero-rated invoice.

The CRA cross-matches GST/HST returns with customs export data, freight forwarder records, and foreign-currency payment flows. If export evidence is insufficient or documents are inconsistent, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Ontario and the LP is assessed 13% HST on the full invoice value, plus backdated interest. Gross-negligence penalties equal to the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax amount apply (Excise Tax Act s. 285), and criminal evasion penalties may reach 200% of tax evaded with imprisonment. Under the Income Tax Act, a false statement or omission in a T5013 can trigger a 50% gross-negligence penalty (s. 163) and unlimited reassessment periods where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established; the ordinary four-year federal reassessment window does not apply in those cases. Partners may be jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net GST/HST liability in the year they were partners (Excise Tax Act s. 272.1), and a 180-day benchmark for repatriation is retained for cross-border benchmarking only — Canadian law does not impose a fixed 180-day FX repatriation condition for export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under both current and capital account headings. There are no exchange controls, no approval requirements for current-account payments, and no restrictions on inward equity contributions or outward profit remittance. Dividends, interest, and royalties paid by a Canadian payer to a non-resident limited partner are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 25%, reducible by treaty); no separate repatriation licensing or convertibility hurdle applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory conversion ratio. Exporters are freely permitted to hold, settle, and repatriate export receipts in foreign currency. The only compliance obligation is accurate reporting of export transactions in the importer/exporter database maintained by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), which does not affect treasury liquidity or conversion timing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF enforcement. Canada maintains no discretionary foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legally constrained. A Canadian limited partnership cannot rely on offshore digital EMI accounts as its primary banking treasury layer for clearing and settlement, because such EMIs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and their deposit-taking or clearing activities in Canada would trigger Retail Payment Activities Act registration and, if holding deposits, violate banking exclusivity. Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore EMIs are viable only as ancillary pre-funding/payment-rail gateways for low-value disbursements, subject to registration with FINTRAC as a money services business and compliance with the Cross-border Payment Services Framework. The core CAD liquidity must be held at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfers to the Ontario LP's Schedule I bank operating or capital escrow account, denominated in CAD or USD. The bank will require a clearly stated purpose of contribution, the source of funds narrative, and the reason code on the wire either as 'Equity Contribution' or 'Paid-in Capital' (no government approval required). For amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC Monetary Services Reporting triggers only on cross-border electronic funds transfer if the instruction is received from abroad; the LP must provide a CPF or information update to the receiving bank, including the contributor's full name, address, and date of birth. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (federal Part I 15% + Ontario 11.5%). The Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under s. 96 ITA: no entity-level income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of income. A non-resident corporate partner's allocated share of Canadian business income is taxable under Part I at 26.5% (plus Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings).
