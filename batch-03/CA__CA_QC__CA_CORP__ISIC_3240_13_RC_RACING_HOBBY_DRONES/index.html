Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal corporate income tax, portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec corporate income tax, portal: Revenu Québec CLIC (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign or domestic corporation must first obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) either via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the GST/HST program account (RC0001) and the corporate income tax program account. Step 2: For Quebec, the QST registration (Numéro de TVQ) is obtained from Revenu Québec through the integrated registration mechanism on Form RC1, or, where the entity is not eligible for simultaneous registration, through CLIC using Form LM-1-V ('Demande d'inscription au fichier de la TVQ'). Step 3: Before tax registration is finalized and any local bank account is opened, a non-Quebec or foreign corporation must obtain extra-provincial registration under the Business Corporations Act (Québec) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, filing the initial declaration and registering the head office in Quebec. Step 4: A non-resident corporation without a Canadian permanent establishment must file CRA Form RC372 ('GST/HST and QST Registration for Non-Resident Businesses') and designate a Canadian agent/representative for service and remittance purposes; Revenu Québec may additionally require Form LM-1-V with a mandatary schedule. Step 5: Upon issuance, the BN and QST number must be printed on all commercial invoices and import/export documentation; the entity is then enrolled for electronic filing (GST34 for federal, FP-2000-V for Quebec) through CRA My Business Account and RQ CLIC respectively.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable person under ITA s.2(1), liable to Canadian federal corporate income tax on worldwide income; a resident corporation in Quebec is also liable to Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Québec); there is no territorial exemption for active foreign business income, and no U.S.-style check-the-box pass-through election is available; no tax-exempt zone applies to manufacturing in Quebec; foreign tax relief is provided via ITA s.126 and QSTA s.780 credits.

Available election forms

Canada does not administer an IRS-style check-the-box classification regime. Available elections relevant to the QC corporate tax identity include: (a) fiscal year-end election under ITA s.249.1(1) allowing a non-calendar taxation year on the first corporate return; (b) associated-corporation allocation election under ITA s.256(1) to apportion the federal small business deduction (and the Quebec equivalent under QSTA s.20.11); (c) GST/QST election under ETA s.156 and QSTA s.220 for nil-consideration supplies between closely related corporations; (d) ETA s.150 annual filing election for small suppliers; (e) ETA s.211.1 Quick Method election for GST in Quebec (not available for QST ITR under RQ rules); (f) hybrid mismatch elections under ITA s.18.2(3) and s.20.2(3) where cross-border hybrid financing arrangements are deployed, subject to the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendation framework; (g) QST input tax refund (ITR) attribution election under QSTA s.217 for entities conducting exclusively commercial activities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — combined worldwide taxable supplies threshold for the small-supplier exemption under ETA s.148(1)(d) and QSTA s.16(1)(b); registration becomes compulsory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated export supplies of FPV drones count toward this threshold, but voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits/ITRs on manufacturing inputs even below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of FPV racing drones and components qualifies for 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 (tangible personal property exported by the supplier or delivered outside Canada to a non-resident recipient) and for 0% QST under QSTA s.47(1), provided the goods are exported 'as soon as is reasonably possible' after delivery. Related services, such as carbon-fiber milling design and brushless-motor integration engineering performed for a non-resident customer, are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 (services supplied to a non-resident, not wholly performed in Canada) with the corresponding QST zero-rating under QSTA s.48. For B2B export sales, the supplier must retain a written or electronic export statement per the Zero-Rated Supplies (Exports) Regulations; the small-supplier threshold is not a bar to zero-rating where registration has been completed; ITC and ITR claims for inputs consumed in export production are available in full.

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API pre-clearance at federal or provincial level. The CRA launched the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol framework) effective December 2023; federal B2G e-invoicing is being phased in for federal procurement suppliers for contracts above CAD 25,000 (expected 2024–2026). In Quebec, invoices must comply with ETA s.223 and QSTA s.31 content requirements, mandating the supplier's BN and QST number, invoice date, supplier/customer identity, description, consideration excluding tax, GST and QST rates and amounts, or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated; electronic invoices are accepted on an equivalent legal basis with document-retention obligations of six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims for ISIC 3240 manufacturers. If the exporter fails to produce the required documentary bundle, the 0% rate is retroactively reversed and 5% GST plus 9.975% QST are assessed on the full invoice consideration, with daily compound interest under ETA s.296 and QSTA ss.1101–1103. Civil penalties apply: 5% of the assessed tax for a false statement on a first occurrence and 10% for subsequent occurrences (ETA s.285 and QSTA equivalent); a 50% gross-negligence penalty is imposed under ETA s.285.1 for knowingly or grossly negligent false claims; credit for claim of export is also fully clawed back. Criminal liability for tax evasion under ETA s.327 (and QSTA s.854) may reach fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Although Canada imposes no statutory foreign-currency repatriation timeline, non-repatriation of proceeds within the 180-day administrative benchmark is a red flag for phantom-export audits, and CRA may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245) or reassess on a substance-over-form basis. All export evidence must be retained for six years under ITA s.230.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized and freely floating currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD, numeric code 124) is fully convertible without any exchange permit, approval, or mandatory central bank deposit requirement. All profit remittance channels (dividends, interest, royalties, branch profits) are legally unrestricted; the only material friction is the Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (standard 25%, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty and other applicable DTTs) and any reporting requirement under the Income Tax Act and CRS/FATCA. Escrow-free capital injections and dividends payable in any major currency are permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines exist in Canada. Exporters are free to hold, invest, or disburse foreign currency proceeds in offshore accounts indefinitely. Compliance obligations are limited to customs/statistical reporting: a B13A export declaration must be filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value or when the goods are subject to export controls. FPV drone components (carbon-fiber milling, brushless motors, telemetry modules) must be screened against the Export Control List and the Canadian Controlled Goods Program, with a technical end-use certificate required for any listed dual-use item.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Bank of Canada Act, in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange control policy and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of banks. Transaction reporting and AML supervision are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. There is no separate foreign exchange control administration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined headline corporate income tax rate, composed of the 15% federal Part I general corporate rate and the 11.5% Quebec general corporate rate, before tax credits. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may reduce federal tax to 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income and Quebec tax to 3.2% on the same eligible tranche, yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 12.2% for that portion, subject to passive income clawback rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under federal Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. This rate applies to the gross dividend before any treaty reduction. Quebec does not impose a separate statutory withholding tax on dividends as such, but treaty benefits must be claimed consistently with both federal and Quebec assessments because Canada's tax treaties cover taxes of general application in all Canadian provinces.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The CRA requires a Form T106 disclosure for any controlled cross-border transaction or series of transactions where the aggregate annual value exceeds CAD 1 million. Contemporaneous Local File-style documentation must be available at the time of filing the T2, and the CRA expects a detailed transfer-pricing study where controlled transactions exceed CAD 1 million or where complex intangible or intercompany financing structures exist. A Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 50 million or where the CRA formally requests one after risk assessment.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the CRA and Revenu Quebec rely on the general anti-avoidance rule, the sham doctrine, the corporate veil, and the transfer-pricing rules in section 247 of the Income Tax Act to recharacterize arrangements lacking commercial substance. A Quebec FPV drone corporation must maintain a real registered office, substantive business functions, physical inventory or assembly capability, employee decision-makers, bank accounts in the corporation's name, and accounting records demonstrating that the business is not a conduit or shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess beyond the normal reassessment period, impose a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated federal tax under s. 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, and refer the matter for prosecution. Criminal penalties under s. 239(1) include a fine between 50% and 200% of the amount sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to two years on summary conviction or up to five years on indictment. Quebec imposes equivalent penalties under its Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and can be personally assessed under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. The corporate veil will be pierced in cases of fraud, and a named director may face criminal prosecution, an arrest warrant, a border alert or travel interruption, substantial civil restitution orders, and a term of imprisonment.
Unremitted Payroll Deductions and GST/QST

Statutory sanction: The CRA may assess unremitted source deductions as trust funds, impose a 10% failure-to-remit penalty, and charge compound daily interest. Revenu Quebec may charge a separate penalty for failure to remit QST. These amounts are not discharged by corporate insolvency.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutorily liable for unremitted employee source deductions and GST/QST regardless of corporate status. The prosecutor can obtain civil judgment against directors personally, and a director who knowingly failed to remit can be criminally prosecuted. Travel restrictions or arrest warrants may be issued where non-remittance is part of a deliberate evasion scheme.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Non-Arm's-Length Payments

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length price under s. 247(2), deny the deduction for intercompany payments, impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, add gross-negligence penalties, and charge interest from the original filing date. Revenu Quebec similarly adjusts the provincial allocation and may impose penalties.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false T106 or knowingly creates fictitious intercompany invoices can be personally assessed for penalties where the corporation is unable to pay and where the statutory conditions are met. Criminal charges can follow if the intercompany structure is used to evade tax, and a court may order restitution and impose imprisonment.
