Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – My Business Account portal (cra.mybusinessaccount.ca); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial components administered through HST

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities register for a mandatory Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through CRA’s ‘Register my business’ online service, using Form RC1 or the Non-Resident Registration procedures (CRA Guide RC4024). The BN serves as the statutory TIN for tax accounts; a corporation must also provide corporate charter details, director/shareholder information, a Canadian service address, and, if no physical presence, a Canadian agent for service and representative under the Income Tax Act. A separate CBSA customs registration and bonded warehouse licence are obtained from the Canada Border Services Agency, requiring facility plans, security bonds, and compliance with the Customs Bonded Warehouse Regulations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective flow-through classification exists for Canadian federal corporations. A business corporation is taxed at the entity level, with available elections limited to specific statutory provisions: eligible dividend election (s. 89 ITA), deemed dividend on winding-up (s. 84 ITA), capital dividend account election (s. 83(2) ITA), and group filing for parent/subsidiary under s. 186. Hybrid mismatch restrictions under s. 18.14–18.17 ITA operate compulsorily rather than as elections, disallowing deductions arising from hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) made in Canada during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-residents, registration is also required if carrying on business in Canada and this threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Canada qualify for zero-rated GST/HST (0%) when the goods are physically delivered outside Canada by the exporter or the recipient, provided the exporter retains documentary proof of export and exports occur within a reasonable time after sale. Services exported to non-residents may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VII to the Excise Tax Act where the supply is not for Canadian consumption or enjoyment, with conditions addressing services to a non-resident who is not in Canada, personal property, and Canadian real property. Bonded warehouse removals to export status qualify for zero-rating where the goods are formally declared for export under CBSA customs control.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or eManifest export declaration/receipt)
  • Bill of Lading (or other transport contract such as waybill, cargo manifest, or air cargo flight manifest)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange proof of receipt of export proceeds

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. GST/HST invoices must contain the information prescribed by s. 224 of the Excise Tax Act. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework is voluntary and PEPPOL-based via the Digital Business Network. For customs and bonded warehouse operations, CBSA mandates electronic reporting through the CARM platform and eManifest for imports/exports, with bonded warehouse admissions, transactions, and removals recorded in the CBSA accounting/control system.

CRA may reassess GST/HST net tax, recover input tax credits, and assess interest and penalties within six years of the reporting period (with no time bar if misrepresentation or fraud is shown). Penalties for false or misleading statements include 25% of the understated net tax under s. 285 of the Excise Tax Act. Exporters failing to substantiate zero-rating with the prescribed documentation are subject to clawback of zero-rated treatment, recapture of ITCs, and interest from the original due date. Wilful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution with fines of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. CBSA may separately impose administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act and require forfeiture or additional duties where export declarations are false or goods are diverted from customs control.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account items under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control approval requirement for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. Cross-border payments are subject only to standard tax withholding obligations, anti-money laundering (AML), and financial sanctions screening. Dividend distributions to non-residents attract Canadian withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, subject to treaty relief. No material FX hurdles exist for a bonded warehouse operator repatriating earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists for export proceeds to be surrendered to the central bank or converted into Canadian dollars. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts either domestically or offshore. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no specified timeline. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC requirements for any transfer exceeding CAD 10,000 and standard tax disclosure for cross-border transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border digital fintech EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are operationally viable as auxiliary disbursement channels for low-value repetitive payments, but are not recommended as primary clearing rails for bonded warehouse treasury functions. Feasibility rating: LOW as a substitute for a regulated domestic bank; MEDIUM as a supplementary payment service. Legal constraints require foreign EMIs to either register as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC if soliciting Canadian clients or rely on a Canadian partner bank for CAD clearing. They cannot provide key trade finance instruments such as commercial letters of credit, bankers' acceptance, or official capital escrow services, and their transaction limits often preclude high-value customs disbursements and LC-backed settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File) required for transactions with non-arm's length non-residents where annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 20 million or the value of specified transactions exceeds CAD 5 million; Ontario-specific filings incorporate the federal T106 information return requirement for reportable transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the economic substance doctrine through the 'reasonable transaction or series' test; bonded warehouse operators in customs free trade zones must demonstrate real economic substance, including physical premises, personnel, operational control, and business purpose, to avoid GAAR reassessment and anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act; provincial and municipal CFTA licensing in Ontario requires proof of a genuine warehouse operation, inventory custody duty, and customs compliance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to two years; civil reassessment with gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; interest compounded daily; loss of bonded warehouse license by the Canada Border Services Agency

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and agents can be jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unremitted withholdings and certain corporate tax debts; courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or sham transactions; travel bans and seizure of personal assets or proceeds of crime, and imprisonment up to two years upon conviction
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment to deny the tax benefit under section 245 of the Income Tax Act; anti-hybrid mismatch provisions under the Income Tax Act; general anti-avoidance penalty and interest; denial of treaty benefits under treaty abuse rules; reputational and regulatory consequences under Ontario's Business Corporations Act

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed personally for the proportion of tax deemed unpaid due to avoidance where the corporation is insolvent; court-ordered restitution, travel bans, limited-liability veil piercing in cases of sham or fraud, and potential criminal liability for counselling or participating in an abusive tax arrangement
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for administrative violations and CAD 2,000,000 per criminal offence; FINTRAC enforcement; customs smuggling penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per count or the value of the goods, forfeiture of goods and conveyances; seizure of the bonded warehouse license; corporate criminal liability under the Criminal Code

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, bans from serving as directors under the Canada Business Corporations Act, asset freezes, travel bans, criminal imprisonment for money laundering offences, and civil forfeiture of personal assets
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty of 10% of net transfer-pricing adjustment for large corporations where documentation requirements are missed; upward adjustment of Canadian income; secondary adjustments deemed dividends subject to withholding tax; loss of treaty relief; interest and penalties on reassessments

Director personal liability: Directors can be liable as shadow directors where they directed deficient transfer pricing arrangements; personal liability for failure to withhold on deemed dividends resulting from transfer pricing adjustments; travel bans and prohibition from leaving Canada where significant outstanding tax liabilities exist
Failure to Withhold and Remit

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax liability assessed against the payor; 10% penalty for failure to remit or pay amounts representing withholdings for more than two days late, plus 10% additional penalty for repeated failures; interest on unremitted amounts; prosecution for failure to remit leading to fines and possible imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted withholding taxes under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, provided the CRA has made reasonable collection efforts against the corporation; personal civil liability extends to interest and penalties; directors may be subject to travel bans and asset execution
