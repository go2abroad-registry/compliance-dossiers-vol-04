Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital; an Ontario business corporation may be incorporated with nominal or no paid-up capital. Consideration for share issuance is to be fixed by the board under s. 22; no escrow account is required for a non-reporting Ontario business corporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); national registration portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under Ontario's federal-provincial collection agreement, and the Ontario Ministry of Finance does not operate a separate corporate TIN registration pipeline.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity, determine first whether it carries on business in Canada for GST/HST and income tax purposes. If yes, apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts (RT = GST/HST, RP = payroll, RZ = import/export) through CRA Business Registration Online, by written application to the CRA Non-Resident Unit, or by filing Form RC1. A federal corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act receives its BN automatically upon CRA registration and must also complete Ontario corporate tax registration through the CRA. Payroll account registration is mandatory if the corporation has employees in Ontario; there is no separate Ontario TIN. The 9-digit CRA BN is the single tax identifier for federal, Ontario, GST/HST, payroll and import/export compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident foreign entity, determine first whether it carries on business in Canada for GST/HST and income tax purposes. If yes, apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts (RT = GST/HST, RP = payroll, RZ = import/export) through CRA Business Registration Online, by written application to the CRA Non-Resident Unit, or by filing Form RC1. A federal corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act receives its BN automatically upon CRA registration and must also complete Ontario corporate tax registration through the CRA. Payroll account registration is mandatory if the corporation has employees in Ontario; there is no separate Ontario TIN. The 9-digit CRA BN is the single tax identifier for federal, Ontario, GST/HST, payroll and import/export compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; every Business Corporation is a taxable corporation required to file a T2 corporate income tax return. A non-resident corporation may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act to file a Canadian tax return on gross rental income to reduce Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, or make a section 215(6) undertaking on Form NR6 to withhold only on the taxable portion of payments. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA section 18.3 apply mandatorily and are not elective; they deny deductions for amounts arising from deduction/non-inclusion mismatches with foreign entities. Tax treaties do not create entity classification elections but may affect sourcing and entitlement to treaty benefits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies, including digital supplies, in any calendar quarter or trailing four-quarter period; exceeding this threshold mandates GST/HST registration for suppliers carrying on commercial activity in Canada. Non-residents below the threshold may still register voluntarily, and mandatory registration applies if the non-resident makes supplies in Canada during a subscription period unless the small supplier exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when the supplier makes a supply of goods by way of sale and exports them from Canada, usually by delivering them to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada before they are sold or otherwise used in Canada. Services are zero-rated only when specifically enumerated in Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act; examples include services performed for a non-resident not registered for GST/HST in respect of exports or property situated outside Canada, and certain advisory or software services supplied to non-residents where the service is not made in Canada or performed by an employee/agent in Canada. Payroll SaaS supplied to a foreign enterprise must satisfy the applicable statutory zero-rating test; the mere fact that the recipient is non-resident is not sufficient if the supply is considered a Canadian domestic supply through CRA's place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian export report, B13A or approved eCAT filing)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, continuous transaction control, or fiscal stamping. CRA requires every GST/HST registrant to issue invoices containing prescribed particulars: the registrant's name, BN and GST/HST registration number, date of invoice, total consideration, and tax payable. Electronic invoices and EDI are permitted. GST/HST returns and payments must generally be e-filed through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE; there is no API-based real-time clearance requirement, but CRA requires retention of electronic records for six years. Canada is aligning with PEPPOL e-invoicing standards but has not enacted mandatory private-sector invoice clearance.

CRA treats zero-rated export refunds as a high-risk area. Claimants must maintain a complete documentation bundle proving export, delivery and receipt by the non-resident; failure to do so permits CRA to reassess, deny or claw back the zero-rating and refund, with statutory interest. Penalties for knowing or wilful false statements include civil penalties up to 50% of the net tax or refund misstated, the potential cancellation of input tax credits, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment on conviction. Retroactive clawback may also extend the normal reassessment period if misrepresentation is shown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full Article VIII status under the IMF Articles of Agreement and imposes no quantitative restrictions on either current-account or capital-account convertibility. Profit and capital repatriation are unrestricted; non-resident dividend and interest remittances are subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) at the 25% statutory rate or a lower treaty rate, with CRA Form NR4/Summary reporting. Cross-border shareholder loans into a Canadian corporation must also respect the thin-capitalization rules in subsection 18(4) if the non-resident lender holds 25% or more of the shares and the debt-to-equity ratio exceeds 1.5:1. No exchange-control approval is required for any inbound capital injection or outbound trade payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export-proceeds surrender rules, statutory repatriation timelines, or mandatory conversion ratios in Canadian law. Exporters and SaaS platforms may retain and reinvest foreign currency without restriction. The only compliance triggers are FINTRAC-reportable international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (reported by the financial institution), CRA record-keeping under section 230, and, where applicable, Statistics Canada direct-investment mandatory surveys for cross-border equity and loan flows above prescribed thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential supervision of regulated financial institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF and international EFT transaction surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17). Canada maintains a freely floating exchange-rate regime with no foreign-exchange control board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High as a limited supplementary rail, but not as a primary deposit-taking institution. The Canadian legal system has no direct equivalent of the EU/UK Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; offshore digital EMIs must either register as a foreign money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC or partner with a Schedule I bank to access Canadian payment rails. The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) now requires payment-service providers operating in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada, covering platforms that execute payroll direct debits, remittances, and digital wallets. EMIs are legally prohibited from accepting deposits or issuing stored-value instruments that function as bank deposits in Canada without a federal or provincial bank licence.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-velocity payroll SaaS disbursements to numerous third-party beneficiary employees/contractors across multiple jurisdictions, intersecting with FINTRAC international EFT reporting and sanctions screening thresholds.

Root cause: High-velocity payroll SaaS disbursements to numerous third-party beneficiary employees/contractors across multiple jurisdictions, intersecting with FINTRAC international EFT reporting and sanctions screening thresholds.

Defense evidence: Provide (i) executed payroll processing agreements with each client employer; (ii) a consolidated payroll mandate register listing beneficiary tax IDs, jurisdictions, and remittance schedules; (iii) an internal sanctions-screening policy covering UN, OSFI, OFAC, EU, and UK lists; (iv) FINTRAC EFT-report control procedures; (v) transaction-monitoring algorithms for velocity anomalies; and (vi) an independent AML audit report conducted in the last 24 months.
Non-resident directors or majority foreign beneficial ownership triggering enhanced CDD and source-of-wealth (SOW) investigation.

Root cause: Non-resident directors or majority foreign beneficial ownership triggering enhanced CDD and source-of-wealth (SOW) investigation.

Defense evidence: Provide notarized certificate of incumbency/corporate good standing, corporate register (KBIS or equivalent), 24-month investor bank statements, executed share-subscription agreements, the shareholder register, Canadian tax-residency declarations for each 25%+ beneficial owner, and a legal opinion from a licensed Ontario counsel confirming non-PEP status or PEP risk mitigation.
Frequent cross-border FX forwards and imported payroll remittances that may resemble remittance-house typologies or unlicensed money-laundering operations.

Root cause: Frequent cross-border FX forwards and imported payroll remittances that may resemble remittance-house typologies or unlicensed money-laundering operations.

Defense evidence: Submit a board-approved treasury/FX hedging policy, an approved counterparty dealer list, MT300/MT103 audit trails evidencing real payroll liabilities, a payroll register with per-beneficiary pseudonymized identifiers, registered MSB/RPAA status where applicable, and an AML/ATF program certified by the designated compliance officer registered under FINTRAC.
Co-mingling of injected share capital (escrow) with operational payroll float, creating an opaque flow-of-funds profile and increased chargeback risk.

Root cause: Co-mingling of injected share capital (escrow) with operational payroll float, creating an opaque flow-of-funds profile and increased chargeback risk.

Defense evidence: Deliver a segregation-of-funds attestation letter from the banking institution, a formal escrow agreement annexed to the shareholders' resolution, monthly account reconciliation schedules showing movements between the escrow account and operating account, audited financial statements (or review engagement) from a licensed CPA firm, and a legal opinion confirming the escrow structure complies with the Ontario Business Corporations Act and federal AML regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder capital is remitted via SWIFT MT103 Corporate Credit to the Ontario corporation's CAD account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire instruction should include the invoice/reference field (field 70) citing 'Shareholder Capital Injection' or 'Shareholder Loan', the company name, and the underlying board resolution number. There is no official Canadian 'purpose code', but banks apply internal credit-clearance procedures, and any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more triggers automatic FINTRAC reporting by the receiving institution. The corporation must record the allotment of shares in its register; CRA thin-capitalization thresholds must be assessed for the loan-versus-equity mix. Foreign direct investment should be flagged to Statistics Canada for survey inclusion where total assets, revenue, or debt to the non-resident parent exceed prescribed thresholds.For payroll SaaS platforms, the primary outbound rails are (i) Payments Canada ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) for recurring CAD payroll direct deposits and pre-authorized debits; (ii) Lynx, the Bank of Canada–settled RTGS system, for high-value CAD transactions above the ACSS limit; and (iii) SWIFT MT103/MT103 STP messages via SWIFT gpi for cross-border payments to offshore contractors, infrastructure vendors, and international payroll beneficiaries. USD disbursements are routed through the Canadian bank's US branch or US correspondent (typically via Fedwire or CHIPS). Canada has no foreign-exchange surrender or approval step for trade payments. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) are generally unnecessary for services-based recurring disbursements but may be requested by enterprise procurement clients; standby letters of credit are supported by the bank when contractual performance guarantees are required.
