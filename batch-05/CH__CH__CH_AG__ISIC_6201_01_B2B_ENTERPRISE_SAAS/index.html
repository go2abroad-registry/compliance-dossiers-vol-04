Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

An AG requires minimum total share capital of CHF 100,000 under Art. 621 OR. At incorporation, shares must be paid in cash to at least 20% of nominal value and in all events at least CHF 50,000 must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) held by a Swiss bank or PostFinance; disposal of the escrow is authorized only after Commercial Register entry, and the remaining balance must be fully paid within three months of release under Art. 632 (1)-(2) OR.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC). Official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT eServices via ePortal (www.eportal.estv.admin.ch); public UID register via www.uid.admin.ch and company register via www.zefix.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss AG: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the Cantonal Commercial Register automatically generate the Swiss Business Identifier (UID, format CHE-123.456.789) under the UID Act; (2) VAT registration is filed electronically through the ESTV ePortal using the UID; after approval the UID is extended with the code 'MWST' (TVA/IVA) and constitutes the Swiss VAT number, effective retroactively from the commencement of taxable activity. For a foreign enterprise without a Swiss seat or permanent establishment: (a) appoint a Swiss tax representative (art. 67 VAT Act) who is jointly and severally liable; (b) file the simplified VAT registration application via the ESTV ePortal; (c) obtain a foreign-entity VAT registration number for Swiss-source supplies. No income-tax registration is required for a non-resident shareholder of the CH AG unless a Swiss permanent establishment or Swiss real estate is held; the AG itself must be registered for corporate income tax with the cantonal tax administration of its seat after commercial-register entry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Swiss corporation income tax on worldwide profit, with relief for qualifying participations and foreign permanent-establishment income (territorial exemption). Federal direct tax is 8.5% of post-tax profit (i.e. 7.83% pre-tax); cantonal and communal taxes vary and aggregate effective rates typically range from 11.9% (Zug) to 21.0% (Geneva). No pass-through treatment or federal check-the-box election exists for a Swiss AG; the AG is always taxed at entity level and distributions are additionally subject to Swiss withholding tax (35%). No corporate tax-exempt zones apply to SaaS operations in Switzerland; customs free ports exist only for tangible goods warehousing.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box election. Available elections and regimes for a CH AG: (1) VAT group taxation (art. 21 VAT Act) for closely linked Swiss entities, treated as a single taxable person; (2) partial taxation procedure (Teilbesteuerungsverfahren) for dividend income, with 50% federal reduction and cantonal reductions ranging from 30% to 60%; (3) participation relief (Beteiligungsabzug) for dividend and capital-gain income from qualifying participations of at least 10%; (4) cantonal STAF incentives: patent-box regime ring-fencing qualified patent and similar IP income at a 90% reduction, R&D super-deduction up to 150%, and notional interest deduction on excess equity where the canton has enacted these measures; (5) hybrid mismatch rules are implemented through the Multilateral Instrument preference provisions and treaty-override mechanisms rather than a domestic hybrid-election statute.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 net annual worldwide turnover from taxable activities for enterprises established in Switzerland (art. 10(1) VAT Act). Foreign-established enterprises have no turnover threshold and are taxable from the first franc of Swiss-source turnover unless a simplified registration procedure applies (art. 67 VAT Act). Voluntary registration for Swiss-established SMEs is available from CHF 100,000; sector-specific relief applies to agriculture, non-profit entities and small businesses with turnover below CHF 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating with full input-VAT deduction applies under art. 23 VAT Act to: (1) physical goods exported by the supplier from Switzerland with customs proof; (2) services, including B2B Enterprise SaaS, whose place of supply is deemed to be outside Switzerland because the recipient is established abroad (art. 18(2)(a) VAT Act, B2B rule); the supplier must demonstrate the recipient's foreign establishment through documentary evidence such as the recipient's foreign VAT/TIN number, official company-register extract, executed contract, invoicing data, payment routing via SWIFT/BIC/IBAN, and IP-geolocation of cloud access; (3) ancillary services directly connected to exported goods. SaaS supplied to a Swiss-established customer is subject to Swiss VAT at 8.1% (8.6% from 1 January 2026) at the recipient's place of establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) filed in the Swiss customs e-dec system for physical goods; not applicable to purely digital SaaS deliveries, as no physical movement of goods occurs
  • Commercial invoice stating the supplier, foreign recipient, service description, payment currency, and the zero-rating legal basis under art. 23 VAT Act
  • Proof of foreign establishment of the recipient: foreign VAT/TIN number, company-register extract, or a signed recipient declaration under art. 23(3) VAT Act
  • Contractual agreement evidencing the B2B SaaS subscription, license scope and cross-border delivery terms
  • Bank payment trail (SWIFT/IBAN statement or inward remittance credit) evidencing cross-border receipt of consideration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note when tangible storage media or on-premise appliances are exported as part of the software transaction

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction-control regime analogous to the Italian Sistema di Interscambio (SdI). Electronic invoicing in B2B flows is voluntarily exchanged under the Swiss SWE-B / eBill XML standard; the QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the mandatory presentation standard for Swiss payment slips and is widely embedded in SaaS billing platforms; suppliers to the Federal Administration are required to submit e-invoices in the SWE-B standard. For audit purposes the ESTV accepts structured electronic evidence of export transactions, provided the data is complete, authentic and retained for the statutory 10-year record-keeping period.

Switzerland imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline and maintains no exchange-control regime; the Swiss National Bank only requires statistical reporting of foreign receivables, and export zero-rating is not conditioned on foreign-currency inflow. If the ESTV determines that zero-rating was claimed without valid recipient-establishment proof, the tax deficiency is assessed retroactively for up to 5 years (10 years in cases of intentional evasion), with statutory default interest and full clawback of the unpaid VAT. Intentional tax evasion attracts a fine of up to three times the evaded tax (art. 97(3) VAT Act); gross negligence attracts a fine of up to once the evaded tax (art. 97(2) VAT Act). Where the taxpayer secures an undue tax advantage through forged documents, false statements or deceit, the criminal offence of Abgabebetrug (art. 96 VAT Act) applies and is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a monetary penalty of up to CHF 300,000, in addition to the full retrospective clawback and interest. The ESTV's internal audit unit conducts risk-based documentary audits of SaaS exporters, applying heightened scrutiny to recurring cross-border invoices lacking contemporaneous recipient-establishment evidence.
