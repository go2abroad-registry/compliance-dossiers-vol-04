Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Federal authority for GST/HST, income tax and excise; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario component: Ontario Ministry of Finance administers provincial corporate minimum tax and Ontario HST component, but GST/HST registration is exclusively through CRA.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Form RC1 or online through Business Registration Online (BRO). The LP itself is a fiscally transparent partnership; the BN is used for GST/HST account registration, payroll, and importer/exporter accounts. A non-resident LP that is not a 'small supplier' (annual worldwide taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000) must register for GST/HST. Registration requires: (i) prescribed information about the LP's general partner and each partner; (ii) posting of security in prescribed circumstances (under ETA s.299), and (iii) designation of a representative resident in Canada. In Ontario, the LP should also obtain an Ontario Master Business Licence / register in the Ontario Business Registry if it has an establishment, but no separate provincial TIN is required for income tax. Foreign entity must provide proof of legal existence, partnership agreement, names of partners, and expected Canadian revenue.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No ‘check-the-box’ regime exists in Canadian federal income tax law. The limited partnership is statutorily treated as a pass-through (fiscally transparent) entity under the Income Tax Act (Canada) s.96; the partners compute their shares of Canadian-source income. The partnership must file an information return (T5013) but pays no income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules (BEPS Action 2) apply where a partnership is treated as opaque in a foreign jurisdiction; however, no election is available to change Canadian classification. For non-resident partners with Canadian-source film licensing revenue, a net-basis election under s.216 Income Tax Act is available for rental or royalty income from Canadian property, allowing the partner to file Part I tax returns on a net basis rather than withholding tax under Part XIII. Additionally, a partnership can elect under s.97(2) to defer recognition on property transfers to the partnership; no entity-level corporate election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — computed on the worldwide taxable supply turnover (excluding zero-rated supplies) of the limited partnership, aggregated with associated persons, over the preceding four calendar quarters and any reasonable expectation for the current quarter. A non-resident with no permanent establishment in Canada is still required to register if it solicits orders in Canada or delivers goods in Canada unless the CA30,000 threshold is not exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A licensed film copyright supplied by an Ontario limited partnership to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act when the supply is of an intangible personal property (intellectual property rights) and the supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, or where the supply is acquired for consumption outside Canada. Where the film licensing constitutes a supply of 'intellectual property services' or a licence of rights that is not used in Canada, 0% GST/HST applies. The zero-rating is available only if the rights are not 'used otherwise than exclusively in the course of commercial activities' — standard export zero-rating. A direct shipping or export of physical film masters or promotional media is zero-rated as the export of tangible personal property. For distribution rights exploited solely abroad (edit, broadcast, and licensing), the export zero-rating is available; however, if the licence permits use in Canada or is granted to a Canadian resident, the full 13% HST applies unless the recipient is a GST/HST registrant. The partnership must obtain documentary evidence of non-residency and the foreign use of the rights within the required record retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) — mandatory for physical shipments of film prints or masters leaving Canada; electronic authorization through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System is required for goods exceeding CAD 2,000
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill — proof of overseas delivery of physical media
  • Signed Film Licence Agreement / Master Distribution Agreement — evidencing non-resident recipient and foreign territory scope
  • Certificate of Non-Residency — corporate registry record or residency certificate of the licensee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank remittance receipt matching the licensing fee under the exported rights

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime currently exists in Canada. CRA does not require pre-approval of invoices or live API postings. Voluntary e-invoicing via interoperable networks (e.g., Peppol / PostgreSQL) is encouraged but not legally mandatory. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information: supplier's name/BN, buyer's name, date, description, HST rate and amount. A Simplified Invoice may be issued for taxable supplies under CAD 150. Non-residents registered under the GST/HST regime may use the CRA My Business Portal to file returns electronically and make payments; no statutory digital clearance step is imposed.

CRA may require substantiation of zero-rating at audit; a failure to maintain sufficient documentary evidence may result in reassessment applying 13% HST to the export, retroactive interest under the Excise Tax Act, and penalties equal to 20% of the net tax understated (ETA s.280 plus gross negligence penalty s.285.1). Knowingly claiming zero-rating for supplies made in Canada carries a civil penalty of up to CAD 25,000 in addition to the understated tax and interest. Misrepresentations attributable to fraud or intentional omission are subject to reassessment outside the normal four-year limitation period (unlimited for misrepresentation due to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default), potential summary conviction for evasion (ETA s.327.1), and for senior partner, conditional imprisonment up to two years or a fine up to 200% of the amount sought to be evaded. Further, clawback of input tax credits based on fraudulent export documentation may be applied on a joint and several liability basis against the general partner of the limited partnership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility as an IMF Article VIII member. No exchange control regime, no prior approval for cross-border transfers, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency, and no restriction on profit or capital repatriation. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating with deep two-way interbank liquidity. The only substantive frictions for a film-licensing limited partnership are (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on royalties remitted to foreign sub-licensors or foreign partners at the statutory 25% rate, reduced to 0–10% under treaty (notably Canada–US); and (ii) GST/HST registration and self-assessment obligations on imported licensing services. Neither constitutes a convertibility impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion, or minimum retention period applies to export or licensing proceeds. A Canadian LP may hold USD or other foreign-currency balances indefinitely in domestic or nostro accounts. Mandatory reporting obligations are limited to (i) Statistics Canada international transactions surveys for license-fee receipts above the reporting threshold; (ii) CRA Form T1135 disclosure of specified foreign property exceeding CAD 100,000; and (iii) FINTRAC cross-border funds-transfer reports of CAD 10,000 or more, which are filed by the financial intermediary, not the LP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and issuer of the Canadian dollar; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential supervisor of federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CTF supervisor and reporting hub; Payments Canada as operator of the LYNX (large-value) and ACSS (retail) clearing systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low–Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (UK/EU/EMI-licensed) are not passportable into Canada and cannot hold direct membership in Payments Canada settlement systems; therefore they cannot provide domestic CAD clearing or a formal Ontario deposit-taking relationship. A foreign EMI soliciting Canadian clients must register as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA and may require provincial money-transfer licensing in Ontario. Viable supplementary use exists for low-value USD receipts from international film-platform sales, foreign-currency pooling, and expense reimbursements, but the EMI must not be the sole or primary treasury rail. All final CAD settlement must remain in a Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are received via SWIFT MT103 wires from each limited partner to the LP's dedicated Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account at the designated Schedule I bank. Remitters must populate field 70 (Remittance Information) with the LP's full legal name and a unique contribution reference tied to a signed capital-call notice. The LP must pre-notify the relationship manager and provide, per tranche, the executed capital-call notice, a General Partner resolution, and a source-of-funds declaration. Wires of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC EFT record-keeping by the bank; no government approval is required. If the partner's jurisdiction imposes FX controls, the remitting partner converts into USD or CAD in its home market and wires through a correspondent bank; Canada imposes no requirement that flows be routed through a specific domestic bank.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct payment of sub-licensing royalties, producer fees, and profit participations; (ii) MT103/MT202 COV where a correspondent-bank cover payment is required for high-value USD transactions; (iii) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 for minimum-guarantee or advance-against-delivery structures, advised through the LP's Canadian bank; and (iv) SWIFTNet documentary collections for title-delivery milestones. Domestic CAD disbursements settle via LYNX (large-value) or ACSS (retail batch); USD disbursements clear via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent network.
