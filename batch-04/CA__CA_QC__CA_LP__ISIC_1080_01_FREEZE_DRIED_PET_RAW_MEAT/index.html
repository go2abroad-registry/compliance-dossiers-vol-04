Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada – national portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); for Quebec, Revenu Québec – portal: Cliquer (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Quebec limited partnership for taxable commercial activity should: (1) register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, obtaining the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ); (2) apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) with the GST/HST, import/export, and payroll program accounts through Business Registration Online or RC1 Application Form; (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec, referencing the same BN; (4) for each non-resident partner, obtain or identify the appropriate Canadian tax identification number and file any Form NR4 reporting obligations; and (5) use the CRA BN as the statutory TIN/VAT ID for GST/QST purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Quebec société en commandite is fiscally transparent for income tax, but is treated as a separate person for GST/QST and may be required to register where it carries on taxable commercial activity.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. Under Excise Tax Act subsection 273(1), a partnership can elect with participants to treat a joint venture as solely a participant's activity for GST/HST purposes; Quebec has a mirror QST election. Hybrid mismatch classification of foreign limited liability entities follows the Income Tax Conventions Interpretation Act and CRA administrative practice, not a domestic entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST); registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of freeze-dried pet raw meat products is zero-rated for GST/QST when the goods are exported by the supplier or the supplier's agent outside Canada, the purchaser is a non-resident, and the supplier maintains contemporaneous evidence satisfactory to CRA and Revenu Québec that the goods were delivered abroad. The export must occur within a reasonable time after the supply, and the supporting documentation must establish both the foreign destination and the identity of the foreign purchaser.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory pre-clearance e-invoicing regime exists in Canada/Quebec. Tax invoices must meet Excise Tax Act and Revenu Québec information requirements, including mandatory French-language business records in Quebec. GST/HST and QST returns are filed electronically through CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec Cliquer; Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary but not currently mandated.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and recover uncollected GST/QST, together with interest and penalties, where export documentation is deficient or misleading. Penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty under the Excise Tax Act for false statements and comparable Quebec penalties, extended reassessment periods for misrepresentation, retrospective denial of input tax credits, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion. Retroactive clawback of any refund claimed under a false export zero-rating can also attract customs administrative penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The Quebec limited partnership may freely receive capital contributions from non-resident partners and remit profits abroad, subject to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Quebec) regarding withholding tax on partner distributions that are treated as Canadian-source income. No Bank of Canada approval or exchange control permit is required for profit repatriation, provided applicable tax compliance and information reporting obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Freeze-dried pet food manufacturers may retain USD or other foreign currency proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts. However, FINTRAC reporting applies to cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires export declarations for controlled or regulated goods. Foreign exchange gains and losses on export receivables are recognized in the Quebec LP's financial statements in accordance with IFRS or ASPE and are taxable or deductible under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority responsible for monetary policy and financial stability; foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising federally regulated financial institutions and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforcing cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) plus Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate for benchmarking; the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so no corporate income tax is paid at the LP level and all income is allocated to partners

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII statutory dividend withholding applies to actual dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident; LP distributions are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but the non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on the allocated Canadian-source business income, and subsequent distributions of previously taxed retained profits are free of additional Canadian withholding

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm-s-length transaction threshold with non-residents triggers mandatory filing of Form T106 and the preparation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File); groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more must additionally prepare a Master File and Country-by-Country Report

Economic substance scope: Standalone Economic Substance Regulations as applied in offshore financial centres do not formally apply in Canada; however, commercial substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245, the deemed permanent establishment rules for non-resident partners, the OECD MLI principal purpose test for treaty access, and Revenu Quebec anti-avoidance rules; a Quebec LP lacking real freeze-dried pet food production operations, tangible inventory, employees, or premises risks reclassification as a conduit with income reattributed to the partners and loss of limited liability protection

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Federal gross negligence penalty under ITA subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under ITA section 239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to two years; Revenu Quebec imposes parallel fines of 50% to 200% of the amount defrauded and imprisonment up to 18 months

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or acquiesce in the false filing are personally liable under ITA section 227.1 and comparable Quebec provisions for unremitted amounts; criminal conviction can trigger asset seizure, court-ordered travel restrictions, passport surrender, and director disqualification
Shell / Conduit Partnership Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization disallows the partnership structure and reattributes 100% of the income to the controlling partner; interest, gross negligence penalties of 50%, and administrative fines up to CAD 2,000,000 may be imposed; Revenu Quebec may void the LP agreement under sham doctrine

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its board face unlimited joint and several liability for the full reassessed tax; courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution on directors, issue travel bans, and impose imprisonment for intentional conduit arrangements
AML / Anti-Money Laundering and Unregistered Financial Intermediation

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; Criminal Code money laundering offence under section 462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years and forfeiture of proceeds

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in laundering or unregistered financial intermediation are personally liable, subject to property freezing orders, travel bans, and professional prohibition orders; criminal forfeiture reaches personal assets of culpable directors
