Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni ATC) via www.estv.admin.ch and the VAT portal at www.vat.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT with the Federal Tax Administration if it supplies services or goods in Switzerland that exceed the statutory turnover threshold. The registration is filed via the online E-Servi portal or the official Form 1040 by a domestic representative. The entity receives a UID number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Numéro d'identification des entreprises / Numero d'identificazione delle imprese), which serves as the VAT registration number. Registration must be completed no later than 30 days after the commencement of taxable activity. Non-resident entities may be subject to simplified registration and are required to name a Swiss fiscal representative for service of process and tax administration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not established in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT with the Federal Tax Administration if it supplies services or goods in Switzerland that exceed the statutory turnover threshold. The registration is filed via the online E-Servi portal or the official Form 1040 by a domestic representative. The entity receives a UID number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Numéro d'identification des entreprises / Numero d'identificazione delle imprese), which serves as the VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Swiss tax law. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is automatically subject to corporate income tax at the federal, cantonal, and communal levels. VAT group registration under Article 15 of the Swiss VAT Act (ORV/OSub/OTVA) may be elected to treat closely linked entities as a single taxable person; the election is optional and requires economic, financial, and organizational integration. No hybrid mismatch election is available for Swiss entity classification purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies, unless the entity is a non-resident supplying services or goods in Switzerland, in which case registration may be required regardless of turnover for supplies that are subject to VAT in Switzerland and not covered by a recovery procedure; voluntary registration is possible under certain conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods from Switzerland to foreign destinations is zero-rated for Swiss VAT if the supplier proves that the goods were physically dispatched or transported out of Switzerland. Services related to immovable property abroad, certain intellectual property transfers abroad, and services benefiting the recipient abroad may be zero-rated or outside the scope of Swiss VAT. A direct export must be evidenced by customs declarations and commercial documents showing delivery occurs outside Switzerland; the recipient must be a foreign entrepreneur or final consumer outside Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. VAT invoices must contain mandatory details specified in Article 26 of the Swiss VAT Act and Swiss QR-bill standards are recommended for B2B invoicing. The Swiss Federal Administration has introduced checks on e-bill and QR-bill formats for electronic invoicing but no API-based real-time clearance is required. Retention of electronic records is required for ten years.

Improper or fraudulent claims for export zero-rating subject the taxpayer to reassessment and collection of unpaid VAT, interest up to 4% per annum, and administrative fines up to three times the tax evaded under Article 57 of the Swiss VAT Act. Criminal penalties for intentional tax evasion can also apply. The Federal Tax Administration audits zero-rating documentation retrospectively and may require production of export proofs, freight contracts, payment records, and foreign importation evidence within statutory limitation periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible. Switzerland maintains no foreign exchange controls and does not differentiate between capital and current account transactions for convertibility purposes. Profit remittance by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate filing, tax return, and dividend withholding tax procedures; no central bank approval or foreign investment screening applies to ordinary dividend distributions or return of capital. Withholding tax of 35% applies to dividends but is refundable under applicable treaty or Swiss withholding tax relief procedures for eligible recipients.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or required conversion ratios for export receipts in Switzerland. Exporters may retain and freely use foreign currency earnings in domestic or offshore accounts. Compliance obligations are limited to anti-money laundering and tax reporting duties, including accurate declaration of foreign income and maintenance of underlying trade documentation such as invoices, shipping documents, and proof of payment.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMIs that lack a Swiss banking or fintech license. Switzerland allows licensed fintech companies to accept public deposits up to CHF 100 million, but only FINMA-licensed entities may provide payment services to Swiss corporate clients. Offshore EMIs operating under UK or EU e-money licences are not passported into Switzerland; they may provide ancillary payment cards or virtual IBANs but should not be relied upon as the primary treasury or clearing rail. A FINMA-regulated Swiss fintech or a licensed Swiss commercial bank is legally preferable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with a clear payment narrative referencing 'equity contribution to Swiss AG' or, where applicable, 'share capital increase'. The beneficiary account should be a Swiss-domiciled corporate bank account in the legal name of the AG. Supporting documentation must include the commercial register excerpt showing authorized share capital, the notarized board and shareholder resolutions, and evidence of the capital contribution entry. Payment purpose codes should use ISO20022 recommendation or SWIFT code categories consistent with equity capital contributions; practical purpose descriptions include 'INVR' for inward investment or 'CAPT' for capital transfer. Banks may request confirmation that the funds are not derived from criminal activity and may require the remitter to pass source of funds checks.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlements, commercial letters of credit issued by a Swiss bank for high-value or documentary trade transactions, and SEPA CT for euro-denominated payments inside SEPA. For resin statue sales to retail customers, payout services such as Stripe and PayPal are acceptable as collection rails but should be swept daily to the primary Swiss operating account. Outbound supplier payments should use SWIFT MT103 with commercial invoices, packing lists, and shipping evidence preserved in the trade file. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (after-tax equivalent 7.83%); Zug combined effective rate 11.9% (federal 7.83% + cantonal/communal 4.07%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on any dividend declared by the CH AG, regardless of the recipient's residence; domestic relief exists for qualifying 20%+ participations (Art. 5 VStG) and treaty refunds reduce the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due 6 months after the end of the business year (for calendar-year taxpayers, by 30 June following the tax year); the cantonal tax authority routinely grants extensions up to 31 October.

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory amount in Swiss law; administrative practice under OECD TP guidelines requires a Local File for controlled cross-border transactions exceeding approx. CHF 500,000 per related party per year; a Master File is expected for groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding CHF 900 million. Transactions below threshold must still be arm's length but full documentation may be waived.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland imposes no annual ESR filing; however, substance is decisive for treaty benefits and withholding tax refunds: a CH AG must maintain at least one registered office, a board meeting at its seat, real staffing, and actual business records in Switzerland. A letterbox/mailbox company claiming DTA relief without personnel or premises will be denied bona fide beneficial ownership and may face tax fraud prosecution. Patent box and R&D incentives additionally require qualifying R&D activity performed in Switzerland.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine of at least 25% and up to 100% of the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG), plus the tax in arrears and default interest; repeated offence raises the minimum to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally sign a false return are jointly and severally liable for the fine under Art. 176 DBG if the AG cannot pay; criminal court may impose travel bans and, upon tax fraud charges, imprisonment up to 6 months.
Tax fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under Art. 186 DBG and Art. 14 of the Federal Administrative Criminal Law Act: imprisonment up to 6 months or a fine up to three times the tax evaded, with simultaneous confiscation of forged records. FTA may also enforce 35% withholding on undeclared distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face personal criminal prosecution; assets can be frozen and foreign travel restricted. If the nominal shareholder is a mere nominee, the individual controlling the AG is treated as the true offender (beneficial owner) and progressively punished.
Shell company / abuse of treaty residence

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits and of refund claims for the 35% Swiss withholding tax; tax reassessment with 4% late interest; Swiss FTA additionally reports the ultimate beneficial owner to residence jurisdictions under the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance (MAAC).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid Swiss withholding tax if they knowingly allowed the AG to distribute profits without withholding or without substance; directive may be issued for provisional seizure and exit from Switzerland (Ausreise) may be prohibited during fraud investigation.
AML / money laundering (SWICO Art. 305bis)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code, imprisonment up to three years and fine up to CHF 500,000; violation of mandatory disclosure under Art. 305ter (lack of due diligence) attracts up to one year or fine. Corporate entity liability up to CHF 5 million under Art. 102 CC.

Director personal liability: Directors who ignored the AG's invoicing red flags are held as co-principals under Art. 102 para 2 CC; liable to civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans pending penal order, and in aggravated cases effective custodial sentences.
