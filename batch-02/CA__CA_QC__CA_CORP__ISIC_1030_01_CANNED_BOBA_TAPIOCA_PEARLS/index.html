Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html); in Quebec, Revenu Quebec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) administers Quebec corporate income tax, QST, and GST/HST under the federal-provincial single-administration agreement via the 'CLIC Entreprises' and 'Mon Dossier d'entreprise' portals; customs/export reporting: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - CARM system and Canadian Export Declaration (CAED/G7).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (Registraire des entreprises du Quebec) to obtain the NEQ. 2) Register through Revenu Quebec's single-window 'Entreprises' service for: Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account (RT), QST account (RP/RQ), payroll and corporate income tax accounts; federal corporate income tax account may be obtained via CRA BRO or Form RC1. 3) File all corporate, director (including non-resident director), registered-office, and activity description details; a non-resident corporation must appoint a representative for service in Quebec. 4) Registration is generally immediate online; if the business is a non-resident without a permanent establishment but carrying on business in Quebec, it must still register for GST/HST/QST if above the threshold or to claim input tax refunds. 5) Small suppliers below CAD 30,000 may voluntarily register to recover GST/QST paid on inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate tax person under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 2; taxed on worldwide income under Part I; no corporate transparency or check-through for C-Corporation-type entities. Federal general rate 15% plus Quebec general rate 11.5% (combined ~26.5%); reduced rates 9% federal and 3.2% Quebec for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. Foreign subsidiary dividends are effectively exempt under ITA s. 113/90; none of the Pass-Through_Eligible, Territorial_Scope, or Tax_Exempt_Zone characterizations apply - Canada taxes worldwide income and has no special tax-exempt zones for this activity.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Elective tools relevant to this structure: (i) GST/HST s. 156 election (and parallel QST election) for closely related corporations to value intercorporate supplies at nil; (ii) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 to use a non-calendar taxation year; (iii) associated-corporation aggregation election (ITA s. 256(2.1)) affecting the CCPC small business limit; (iv) for treaty-based hybrid entities (e.g., US LLC), the Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7) allows member-based allocation; hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 18.6) and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) apply automatically and are not elective; (v) QST specific election for refund of QST on commercial bad debts or the 'specified financial institutions' attribution election where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15.47375%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - GST/HST and QST 'small supplier' threshold using total worldwide consideration for taxable-type supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Quebec must register once this threshold is exceeded; mandatory registration takes effect on the day the threshold is crossed, with a 29-day (GST/HST) and 30-day (QST) filing window.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property from Canada/Quebec qualify for 0% GST/HST under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V s. 9 and for 0% QST under the parallel QST Schedule IV, provided the vendor exports the goods, or delivers them outside Canada to a common carrier or consignee, within a reasonable time and retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export. Offshore services supplied to non-residents (Schedule VI Part V s. 7) may also be zero-rated. Canned boba tapioca pearls are generally 'food for human consumption' and may be zero-rated domestically under Schedule VI Part III, but export zero-rating is the primary mechanism for cross-border sales; the vendor must claim input tax credits/refunds for GST/QST paid on ingredients, packaging, and aseptic-tubing machinery inputs, including the Quebec DES refund rule for large corporations.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration (CAED/G7 electronic report through CARM) for shipments above CAD 2,000 or controlled/special commodities
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing continuous carriage to a final non-resident destination with proof of delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - although Canada does not issue a statutory IRC, bank-issued proof of foreign-currency inward remittance (SWIFT MT103 / TT credit advice) is retained as audit evidence of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting the exported goods, quantity, unit price, and 0% rate
  • Foreign exchange/bank settlement records confirming receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Carrier certificates or storage/forwarder documentation (if goods pass through bonded logistics) evidencing export

No real-time transaction clearance, fiscal-digital-stamping, or mandatory API e-invoicing regime exists federally or in Quebec. Canada is adopting Peppol BIS for B2G e-invoicing in federal procurement (rolling out 2025-2026), but B2B/B2C e-invoicing remains optional. Tax invoices must show the supplier's BN/GST-HST registration number (and QST number in Quebec), tax amounts, and recipient details; simplified invoices are permitted under CAD 150. Sellers must retain digital records in machine-readable format for six years; CBSA CARM mandates electronic reporting of export data, and Revenu Quebec accepts EDI/XML invoices without pre-clearance or digital stamping.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within 4 years for normal audit (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is involved), deny the zero-rating and any input tax credits/refunds, recover net tax and interest, and convert the export to taxable status at 5% GST + 9.975% QST. Gross-negligence penalty is 25% of the amount at issue under ETA s. 285 and comparable Quebec provisions; third-party penalties up to CAD 100,000 or double the tax amount apply for aiding false export claims. Criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 239/240 and Quebec fiscal fraud provisions can lead to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment (up to 15 years in Quebec). Records supporting the export zero-rating claim must be kept six years; failure to contemporaneously substantiate the export with transport, delivery, and foreign-currency settlement documentation is a frequent audit trigger that results in retroactive clawback of refunds, penalties, and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Canada has assumed the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and does not impose exchange controls, prior-authorization gates, or compulsory conversion ratios for profit remittance, intercompany loans, royalties, dividends, or repatriation of direct-investment capital. The only material profit-remittance frictions are fiscal: Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% on payments of dividends, interest, and royalties to non-residents, reduced under the Canada-United States Tax Convention (1980), as amended, to 5% for dividends paid to a U.S. corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting shares (15% for other dividend cases), 0% for most arm's-length interest payable to U.S. residents, and 10% for trademark-type royalties; Quebec does not superimpose a separate provincial withholding charge on outward remittances. Additional limitation points are the Canadian excess interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules effective for taxation years commencing after September 30, 2023, the CBCA/QBCA insolvency and balance-sheet tests governing shareholder distributions, and CRS/FATCA reporting triggered by the receiving Canadian bank. None of these constitute a currency-convertibility or central-bank permission hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. The Quebec exporter may retain receivables in CAD or in any foreign currency, may settle via free interbank markets, and may maintain foreign-currency bank accounts without Bank of Canada authorization. The only compliance duties are (i) banks must report international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC; (ii) physical cross-border movements of currency or bearer monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the CBSA; and (iii) export merchandise must be documented through the Canadian export reporting process (OGA/B3 customs data and Statistics Canada records). These are informational and AML obligations, not surrender or conversion edicts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa. Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no dedicated governmental foreign-exchange control authority. The applicable statutory and supervisory anchors are the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Trade-account movement documentation is handled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Statistics Canada rather than by a central-bank license office.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; cross-border digital payment providers operating into Quebec must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, for the new regime, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which becomes fully operational in September 2025. Offshore EMIs can lawfully provide currency conversion, cross-border payment execution, virtual-account rails, and multicurrency wallet segmentation for the boba-tapioca manufacturer, but they are not licensed to accept Canadian deposits in the banking-law sense and cannot operate as a substitute for a Schedule I chartered bank's escrow, trade-finance, or pre-shipment credit capacities. The Quebec Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) may also apply provincial payment-provider registration requirements. Accordingly, a licensed EMI is viable as a secondary settlement and FX-allocation rail for low-value vendor payments, but all capital injections, letters of credit, and statutory liquidity accommodations must be held with a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (i) the parent shareholder transmits a SWIFT MT103 wire from its U.S. bank account to the Quebec operating company's CAD-denominated operational account at a Schedule I bank; (ii) the SWIFT beneficiary/remittance information must include a genuine Balance-of-Payments purpose code for direct investment, usually described as 'Equity injection' or 'Capital contribution to wholly-owned subsidiary', accompanied by the share-subscription reference and REQ corporate identification number, with empty or generic purpose codes strictly prohibited; (iii) the USD leg clears via Fedwire or CHIPS, and if the receiving bank executes the conversion to CAD, the CAD leg settles through Lynx, Payments Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement (RTGS) system that replaced the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); (iv) pre-credit verification requires the bank to hold the board resolution, the REQ registry extract, executed subscription agreement, and evidence that the remitting entity is a bona fide shareholder; and (v) the bank files the mandatory FINTRAC international EFT report for wires at or above CAD 10,000, while physical cross-border cash movements of CAD 10,000 or more additionally require a CBSA currency declaration. Where provisional funds must be deployed only after the share-issue conditions are satisfied, the capital is parked in a segregated Capital Escrow Account at the receiving bank until the REQ amendment and certificate issuance are complete.Outbound trade settlements are executed on three principal clearing rails: (i) direct telegraphic transfers via SWIFT MT103 for payment against invoice to Asian tapioca-starch suppliers and authorized packaging vendors, with FX conversion executed at the paying bank and the CAD settlement leg cleared through Lynx (and, for USD, through Fedwire/CHIPS); (ii) commercial letters of credit issued by the Quebec corporate's Canadian bank and advised through a correspondent in the supplier's country, governed by UCP600 and eUCP, with reimbursement handled under SWIFT MT700 and MT740/MT742 messages, thereby mitigating counterparty and documentary risk for high-value purchase contracts; and (iii) documentary collections (documents against payment or against acceptance) for open-account contracts where bill-of-lading title documents are drawn through the remitting and presenting banks. For ambient quick-cook canned boba tapioca pearls, the standard practice will favour MT103 open-account settlement for recurring volumetric purchases, with LC facilities reserved for seasonal bulk movements and new-supplier onboarding. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; cross-border digital payment providers operating into Quebec must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, for the new regime, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which becomes fully operational in September 2025. Offshore EMIs can lawfully provide currency conversion, cross-border payment execution, virtual-account rails, and multicurrency wallet segmentation for the boba-tapioca manufacturer, but they are not licensed to accept Canadian deposits in the banking-law sense and cannot operate as a substitute for a Schedule I chartered bank's escrow, trade-finance, or pre-shipment credit capacities. The Quebec Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) may also apply provincial payment-provider registration requirements. Accordingly, a licensed EMI is viable as a secondary settlement and FX-allocation rail for low-value vendor payments, but all capital injections, letters of credit, and statutory liquidity accommodations must be held with a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) when the aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gulf ESR rules do not apply in Canada; the operative substance doctrine is the federal general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the corresponding Quebec GAAR, which disregard transactions lacking a genuine commercial purpose and enforce substance-over-form analysis, assisted by CRA and Revenu Québec audit powers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act and equivalent Quebec provisions: fine of not less than CAD 50,000 and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil reassessment adds interest and a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion can face personal civil liability for unremitted source deductions under section 227.1, court-ordered restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where fraud or sham conduct is proven.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income and application of section 247(3): penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue, plus compounding interest and potential double taxation absent relief.

Director personal liability: Directors do not automatically bear transfer-pricing penalties, but directors who authorize false transfer pricing studies or fraudulent invoicing can be exposed to Canadian fraud charges, provincial securities actions, and creditor clawback liability in insolvency.
Artificial Avoidance and Shell Company Structures

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the full tax benefit, recapture of QST input tax refunds where applicable, accrual of interest, and exposure to anti-avoidance penalties and provincial assessments; sham transactions may be treated as void against the tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial transactions without business purpose can be found to have breached fiduciary duties; in bankruptcy, courts may order directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and impose director conduct orders where fraud or gross negligence is established.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering Invoicing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, reporting entities face administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation; the Customs Act imposes penalties of up to 100% of the value of the goods for false statements, and criminal money laundering charges carry imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of reporting entities can be personally named in FINTRAC administrative penalty proceedings; deliberate structuring or falsified trade documents may lead to personal criminal liability, forfeiture orders, and civil claims by receivers in fraudulent conveyance actions.
