E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election exists under Canadian income tax law; a Business Corporation is compulsorily taxed as a separate person at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c.1) and the Quebec Taxation Act (RLRQ c. I-3) on worldwide income with foreign tax credits. Available elections relevant to a Quebec MMORPG publisher: (i) Excise Tax Act (ETA) subsection 273 election to treat closely related corporations as a single person for zero-rated intercompany app-store and IP-licensing transactions; (ii) ETA section 156 zero-rated intercompany supply election (Form GST25); (iii) tax-deferred rollover elections under ITA subsections 85(1)/85(2) and Quebec Form LM-101-V for corporate restructuring and IP transfers; (iv) associated-corporation allocation election under ITA section 256 with Revenu Quebec Form CO-1027; (v) SR&ED proxy-method election for qualifying software development expenditures (ITA section 37(8)); (vi) ETA section 191.1 election to establish specified-person status for streamlined input tax credit recovery. Hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 apply but do not alter the mandatory entity-level classification of a Canadian private corporation; the federal government has not enacted US-style check-the-box regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold): compulsory registration once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted to recover input tax credits on development costs, App Store/Google Play commissions, and cloud infrastructure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act (section 10: intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada; section 23: exported services) and mirrored under Quebec QST rules. For App Store and Google Play sales, the Canadian publisher makes a zero-rated export supply to the non-resident platform operator or foreign purchaser when the digital content is not for use or enjoyment in Canada; domestic Canadian consumer B2C supplies remain taxable and are collected through the storefront. Conditions for validity: (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of supply; (ii) the intangible property is used wholly outside Canada based on location-of-use analysis; (iii) the publisher retains territorial app-store sales reports and Apple/Google platform agreements; (iv) no deemed taxable supply arises in Canada under ETA sections 142/143. Since July 1, 2021, foreign platform operators collect and remit Canadian GST/HST and Quebec QST on B2C Canadian downloads, preserving the Canadian publisher's export zero-rating position on its B2B supply to the platform.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA electronic export reporting via Canadian Export Reporting System or B13A form, as applicable; formally waived for pure digital MMORPG deliveries but retained for any physical collector's media shipments)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport evidence for tangible mixed deliverables; for digital export substitute server-side delivery logs, CDN access records, and App Store Connect/Google Play transaction manifests
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank MT103 SWIFT receipts, App Store/Google Play payout reconciliation statements, or Payment Service Provider (PSP) settlement reports evidencing foreign-currency or platform-net remittances

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls (CTC). Invoice validity is governed by prescribed elements under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec QST rules (supplier legal name, BN, QST number, date, description, GST/QST amounts or tax-included indication) and records must be retained for six years. Revenu Quebec requires electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns for large registrants; CRA and Revenu Quebec are voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL/UBL 2.1 e-invoice framework. App Store Connect and Google Play payout statements must be reconciled to the general ledger and jurisdictional sales reports (country, price tier, tax point, platform fee) to substantiate zero-rating and QST/GST collection on Canadian downloads.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period (unlimited in cases of fraud or misrepresentation), re-characterize the supply as taxable at GST 5% plus QST 9.975%, claw back input tax credits, and charge arrears interest at the prescribed rate. Civil penalties include the ETA section 280 false-statement or gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 20% of the net tax evaded, the ETA section 285.1 misinformation penalty, and mirroring QST penalties under Quebec law. Income-tax integration may trigger the ITA section 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated taxable income where export documentation is fabricated; criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and ITA section 239 applies to willful evasion, and directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/QST under ETA section 323 and Quebec equivalent, with no minimum-capital shield available. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is a prudent treasury control aligned with App Store/Google Play payout cycles (approximately 30-45 days) and CRA foreign-affiliate tracing expectations; no separate federal FX repatriation statute applies to digital service exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible without exchange-control permits or prior central-bank approval for either current or capital account transactions. There is no statutory distinction requiring separate authorization for inbound capital injections, dividend remittances, or trade-related payments. Profit repatriation from the Quebec corporation to non-resident shareholders is legally unrestricted, but is subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act at 25%, reduced where an income tax treaty applies; no further corporate or tax approval is required. The operational risk for an MMORPG treasury is settlement currency concentration in USD/CAD, rather than any regulatory convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export-proceeds surrender law. There is no statutory timeline requiring repatriation of foreign receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to sell export proceeds to an authorized dealer. Export and royalty proceeds may be retained in Canadian dollars or in a foreign-denominated bank account. Digital game publishing proceeds paid by Apple App Store and Google Play do not trigger customs export declaration requirements; however, receipts above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC record-keeping and large-transaction reporting by the financial institution, and underlying business contracts should support the commercial nature of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration authority; exchange-control functions are not centralized in the Bank of Canada. Supervisory responsibility for chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT and money-services-business registration are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Provincial-level consumer and money-transmitter licensing for Quebec is administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign fintech EMIs and cross-border payment platforms can serve as settlement-acceptance and treasury-optimization rails, but they cannot legally replace a Schedule I Canadian bank account for the Quebec corporation. A non-resident electronic-money institution seeking to provide money-transfer or payment services to Canadian clients must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, obtain Quebec AMF licensing as a money transmitter. These institutions are not banks, do not hold Canadian banking charters, and cannot provide CDIC-insured deposits or loan facilities. Digital payment providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are best used as supplementary inbound settlement wallets, with scheduled sweeps into the primary operational account at a Canadian chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control approval, registration, or purpose-code declaration is required in Canada for inbound equity capital. The recommended compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 international wire from the shareholder's foreign bank to the corporation's CAD or USD operational account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using the Canadian bank's institution number, transit/branch number, and SWIFT/BIC, with an intermediary correspondent if required. The remittance instruction should identify the ultimate beneficiary legal name, the share-capital purpose in SWIFT field 70, and reference the shareholder resolution. Canadian banks will conduct source-of-funds verification and apply FINTRAC large-transaction reporting where the amount is CAD 10,000 or more. If the bank system requests a purpose standard, the documentation should state equity injection/share subscription; there is no official Canadian 'purpose code' regime. USD payments to the United States use Fedwire/CHIPS through the Canadian bank's US correspondent. For commercial trade settlements and larger licensing agreements, a documentary letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank under UCP600 is permissible, as are standby letters of credit under ISP98. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign fintech EMIs and cross-border payment platforms can serve as settlement-acceptance and treasury-optimization rails, but they cannot legally replace a Schedule I Canadian bank account for the Quebec corporation. A non-resident electronic-money institution seeking to provide money-transfer or payment services to Canadian clients must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, obtain Quebec AMF licensing as a money transmitter. These institutions are not banks, do not hold Canadian banking charters, and cannot provide CDIC-insured deposits or loan facilities. Digital payment providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are best used as supplementary inbound settlement wallets, with scheduled sweeps into the primary operational account at a Canadian chartered bank.
