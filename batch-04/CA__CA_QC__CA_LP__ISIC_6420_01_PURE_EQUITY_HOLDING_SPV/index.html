Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal at www.canada.ca (federal GST/HST accounts and Business Number/BN issuer); Quebec provincial counterpart: Revenu Québec (RQ) — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) account and Numéro d'entreprise du Québec (NEQ) issued via the Registraire des entreprises du Québec at revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-incorporated Canadian limited partnership (or non-resident owning an LP): (1) determine whether the partnership carries on business or is required to collect GST/HST in Canada; (2) apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account, using Form RC1 or the online electronic application, providing legal name, fiscal period, business activity (ISIC 6420 / NAICS 551112 passive share holding), mailing address, and the operating business identification of each general partner; (3) register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the Quebec enterprise registrar to obtain the NEQ and QST account; (4) where the partnership has no Canadian resident partner, file Form RC59 (Business Number request for non-resident) and appoint an authorized representative under the CRA electronic services; (5) use the BN for T5013 partnership information return filings and the QST number for TP-600R-QC and provincial remittances; (6) designate a service address in Canada for records; if no taxable supplies are made, registration is not compulsory, but an optional QST holding-entity election may be filed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian domestic law; the limited partnership is statutorily transparent for income tax purposes (ITA s. 96, subdivision j). Available elections/mechanisms: (i) statutory characterization as a 'Canadian partnership' under ITA s. 102, maintained by ensuring all partners are Canadian residents, preserving Part I taxation and treaty access; (ii) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 to compute Canadian tax liabilities in US dollars; (iii) optional QST registration / holding-entity election under s. 165.1 Quebec Sales Tax Act (QSTA) for QST input-tax refund recovery; (iv) GST/HST joint election under ETA s. 273 for inter-company registrant references; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.5, 18.6, 20.26, 20.27 effective July 1, 2022) are statutorily prescribed rather than elective, with prescribed information-return documentation for importer mismatch relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (other than zero-rated) made in Canada over a calendar quarter and the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240(1)); identical CAD 30,000 threshold under the Quebec Sales Tax Act for QST registration; a pure equity holding SPV earning only dividends has no taxable supplies and is not required to register unless it elects or supplies management services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated where the supplier ships or delivers tangible property to a destination outside Canada and exports it as soon after shipment as is reasonable (ETA Sch. VI Part V ss. 1, 2, 10, 26); services: zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident not registered under Part IX and not for consumption in Canada (ETA Sch. VI s. 7); financial services supplied to non-residents may zero-rate only as 'exported financial services' under ETA Sch. VII subject to premium-consideration and foreign-account documentation tests. For ISIC 6420 pure equity holding: dividends and share dispositions are not supplies for GST/HST and therefore are not zero-rated events; intercompany management fees billed to non-resident subsidiaries can qualify for zero-rating if the contract, non-resident status, and place-of-consumption tests are documented; input tax credits are generally unavailable for exempt holding activity, but the QST holding-entity election (s. 165.1 QSTA) permits QST recovery on input costs.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Canada Customs Form B13A or CES/ITS electronic export report) for goods, when the export control or valuation thresholds apply
  • Commercial Invoice with zero-rating statement and intercompany transfer-pricing documentation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank FX wire confirmation for the export proceeds
  • Certificate of Residence of the non-resident purchaser (CRA or Revenu Québec acceptable proof, including reciprocal tax-treaty certifications) and the underlying zero-rated service contract where applicable
  • Six-year books and records retention schedule including T5013/TP-600R-QC partnership returns and QSTA filings

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing clearance is currently in force at CRA or Revenu Québec for B2B supplies. Minimum fiscal documentation obligations include keeping full, sequential, and tamper-proof records of invoices and receipts; GST/HST/QST returns are filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/EFILE; Revenu Québec online services) on periodic cycles. Canada and Quebec are aligned with the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based B2B e-invoicing framework; Quebec has announced a future transactional data reporting and B2B e-invoicing mandate but it is not yet enacted.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating, claw back uncollected GST/QST, and assess arrears interest. Reassessment periods: four years generally, seven years for negligence, and unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud (ETA s. 298, ITA s. 152(4)). False-statement penalties: greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax misstated (ETA s. 280) with parallel Quebec penalty provisions; gross-negligence penalties also apply under ETA s. 285; criminal evasion penalties under ETA s. 327 / ITA s. 239 expose the taxpayer to fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years, with equivalent prosecution powers exercised by Revenu Québec under the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. No exchange-control restrictions impede dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or equity injection by a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident partners. The residual compliance burden is documentary and tax-driven: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax or treaty relief, Revenu Québec and Canada Revenue Agency reporting, Investment Canada Act net-benefit review where applicable, and enhanced due diligence for FINTRAC and global-sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters and holding entities may retain foreign-currency balances freely; record-keeping obligations apply under the Income Tax Act and FINTRAC thresholds for currency and virtual-currency transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) for monetary policy and official published exchange rates; Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for sanctions administration, AML/PF supervision, and foreign-currency transaction reporting; no dedicated exchange-control authority exists in Canada under the Currency Act or the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. A non-bank fintech EMI, whether Canadian-registered as a money-services business or a foreign digital EMI, may provide supplementary cross-border collections, FX execution, and low-value payment routes; however, it cannot lawfully replace a Schedule 1 bank for capital escrow and treasury settlement in Canada. Any foreign EMI carrying on money-services-business activity in Canada must be registered with FINTRAC and comply with Quebec consumer-protection and money-services rules; account-opening limits, deposit-insurance absence, and enhanced-source-of-funds scrutiny materially reduce viability for passive holding-company structures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 or, for intercompany funding, SWIFT MT202 for inbound equity contributions from the foreign parent into the Quebec LP’s Schedule 1 bank CAD or USD operational account. The wire must carry a Statistics Canada-compliant purpose code consistent with direct investment/equity capital and the remitter must provide, before or concurrently with the transfer, an executed equity subscription agreement, board/general-partner resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, and expected inflow/outflow narrative. Funds may be received in CAD, USD, EUR, CNY, HKD, or other convertible currencies and converted at the bank’s execution rate; all receipts are posted to the partnership capital-account ledger and made available to Revenu Québec and CRA for information verification.Outbound disbursements should clear through traditional interbank rails: SWIFT MT103 for straightforward supplier or distribution payments; Commercial Letters of Credit with presentation of conforming shipping and invoice documents for trade-backed obligations; and, for same-day CAD disbursements, Payments Canada’s Large-Value Transfer System (LVTS). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at LP level (fiscally transparent); corporate partners are taxed at the combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate of 26.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII ITA) on distributions out of Canada; reduced to 5% under most DTAs where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% voting shares; 15% portfolio rate for other shareholders

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – the aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a particular non-resident related party requiring Form T106 (Reasonable Efforts Information Return). Master File and CbCR are mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1.1 billion (approx. EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulation; however, GAAR under s.245 ITA, treaty shopping restrictions, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test require real economic substance. A pure equity-holding LP must demonstrate commercial rationale, active management decisions, properly documented intercompany agreements, and adequate capitalization; otherwise dividends and gains may be recharacterized and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s.163(2) ITA equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal fine under s.239(1) of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; Quebec also applies administrative penalties under s.1049 TLTA.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be jointly and severally liable for taxes, penalties, and interest if they authorized the fraud; travel bans may be issued under tax treaties; criminal imprisonment applies to responsible officers.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment plus a 10% penalty on net adjustment (s.247(3)) if gross adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; documentation penalty of CAD 1,000 per month for each failure. Interest accrues from the first non-compliance date.

Director personal liability: Directors/partners who knowingly sign false documentation may face civil liability equal to the penalty portion; CRA can assess penalties against the partnership and collect from general partner assets; serious cases lead to criminal prosecution.
Withholding Tax Defaults on Non-Resident Partners

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the full unwithheld amount plus interest and penalties under s.227(8); penalty of 25% of the amount not withheld is imposed under s.227(3); repeated non-compliance can trigger prosecution under s.238(2).

Director personal liability: Trust liability under s.227(3) applies to directors of the general partner; personal recovery includes seizure of personal assets and travel restrictions; if funds are intentionally diverted, personal criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245(2) ITA denying the tax benefit and imposing interest and penalties; Quebec analog under s.1079.1 TLTA; monetary penalty of 50% of the tax benefit may be imposed if the arrangement was a planning misuse; treaty benefits may be denied under OECD BEPS PPT.

Director personal liability: While GAAR is corporation-level, officers who knowingly implement a sham arrangement face joint personal liability for reassessed taxes and severe professional consequences; travel bans are possible in extreme cases; fraud charges may be brought under the Criminal Code.
AML/CFT Compliance Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 100,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for entities per violation under the PCMLTFA; criminal money laundering conviction carries imprisonment up to 14 years under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code; asset freeze and forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct, authorize, or participate in money laundering or financing of unlawful activities are personally liable to pay penalties and face imprisonment; travel bans and extradition are applicable under international instruments.
