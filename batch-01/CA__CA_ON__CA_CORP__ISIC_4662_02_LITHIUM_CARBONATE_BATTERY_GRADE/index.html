Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); non-resident GST/HST registration matters are handled by the CRA International Tax Services Office (Ottawa); Ontario HST (8% PST component) is jointly administered by CRA under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement; Ontario-specific tax matters: Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign (non-resident) corporation must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account. Procedure: (1) File Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts') or complete Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration; (2) Provide certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation/continuance, articles), proof of the foreign entity's existence, the name and address of the Canadian agent or permanent establishment (if any), and the expected date of first taxable supply in Canada; (3) For a non-resident,bother: if operating without a permanent establishment in Canada but required to register for GST/HST, CRA issues a BN based on the international address and designates a deemed registration; (4) The BN is used for both federal corporate income tax (T2) and GST/HST; Ontario corporate income tax is now administered through the same T2 return under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement, so no separate Ontario tax ID is generally issued; (5) Foreign corporations that merely export to Canada and do not carry on business in Canada are not required to register, but a corporation carrying on business in Canada (or making taxable supplies in Canada above the threshold) must register before the supply is made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian Business Corporation taxed at corporate entity level; no US-style pass-through/check-the-box; a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income, and a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source income only; Ontario-resident corporations are subject to the Ontario corporate income tax component in the T2 return); Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable in Ontario (no provincial export-processing zone GST/HST relief, though certain 'export warehousing' activities can be relieved under duty/free-trade-zone type programs administered by CBSA).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election under the Income Tax Act (ITA), but the following elections are relevant in this context: (1) Hybrid-mismatch elections and reporting — ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.6 (excessive interest and structured/substantiative deduction mismatches, effective for arrangements entered on or after 1 July 2022; phase-in for existing arrangements) require enhanced documentation and can deny deductions for the Canadian company if the counterparty treats the same payment inconsistently; (2) GST/HST related-party election under s. 156 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) may deem supplies between closely related registrants to be for nil consideration, eliminating GST/HST on intercompany flows; (3) The 'streamlined accounting/filing' options such as the Quick Method (s. 227 ETA) are available to the Canadian operating affiliate; (4) Subsection 150(2) ITA or s. 219 branch elections generally not applicable to a lithium carbonate trading corporation; (5) For non-resident withholding on dividends/royalties, the filing of a treaty-based relief application (Form T602) can reduce Part XIII withholding but is not an entity classification election; (6) A corporate continuance or rollover under s. 85 can be elected as part of inbound structuring, but does not alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident supplier is required to register for GST/HST if it carries on business in Canada and its annual worldwide taxable supplies other than for non-resident zero-rated supplies exceed CAD 30,000, unless the supplies are zero-rated exports or made through a permanent establishment and the non-resident provides a valid 'non-resident invoice' arrangement; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits (ITCs).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI Part V (s. 1 and 2) where battery-grade lithium carbonate is (i) supplied by the Canadian corporation and exported by the supplier as part of the supply agreement to a destination outside Canada, or (ii) supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the ETA and the goods are exported as soon as is reasonably possible, understanding that none of the goods are consumed or re-supplied in Canada. Services reasonably attributable to the export (logistics, insurance, brokerage) are also zero-rated under Part V s. 7 when performed for the non-resident or performed outside Canada. The supplier must retain evidence that the goods physically left Canada within a reasonable period (CRA administrative benchmark: 'as soon as is reasonably possible', with 12 months generally accepted for commercial export corridors, and much shorter — typically 1–30 days — for standard containerized battery-metal shipments), otherwise the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 13% HST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (report generated through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CESRS) / B13A; proof of the exporter export records)
  • Commercial invoice and/or Canada Customs Invoice (CCI) identifying the lithium carbonate (HS 2836.91.00), quantity, unit price, purchase order, and terms of sale (Incoterms)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill (ocean or airway transport contract showing Canadian port of loading and foreign port of discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — for Canada, a bank-issued SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice or foreign-exchange record confirming the export sale proceeds were received in the Canadian corporation's bank account
  • Packing list, weight certificate, and terminal/warehouse receipts corroborating the physical movement and storage of the battery-grade powder
  • Proof of the foreign customer's non-resident status (EIN/tax ID and registered business address) and, where applicable, the foreign customs import declaration evidencing entry of the goods in the destination country

Canada has NO mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital-stamping regime. The CRA requires GST/HST invoices to contain the prescribed information under ETA s. 221 and Regulation 238 (supplier name/city and BN; date; description; consideration; tax amount or 'tax included'; and the words 'zero-rated' when applicable). The Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoice interoperability (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format) is voluntary and operates through certified PEPPOL access points; CRA allows electronic records (including PDF/EDIFACT/XML) if they are complete, legible, and retained for 6 years (ETA s. 286 / ITA s. 230) at an address in Canada or accessible from Canada. Exports require a retained 'satisfactory evidence' package (B13A export declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents) which CRA may request on audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime with no administrative exchange controls. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account items (trade payables, receivables, fees) and capital-account items (shareholder capital injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation, royalty flows). No Bank of Canada approval or foreign-exchange licence is required for profit remittance. The only material constraints on outbound flows are the Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally reduced to 10% for eligible dividends under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement) and the need for a Certificate of Compliance under Section 116 where taxable Canadian property is disposed of by a non-resident. All cross-border payments require the bank to attribute a Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters may hold proceeds in any currency in domestic or offshore accounts. However, Canadian authorized institutions must record the ITRS purpose code for every cross-border transaction and must file FINTRAC reports for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and for large cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; exchange-rate policy and administration of any FX measures vest in the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act; prudential supervision of Schedule I banks and their FX-risk positions rests with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); cross-border and large-value transaction reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Payments Canada regulated financial-technology entities and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Corpay) are feasible only as supplementary payment corridors for spot FX and mass payables. They are not viable as the primary treasury or clearing rail for ISIC 4662 battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement because they generally cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600, cannot hold a Lynx RTGS settlement account, and may not support CAD-denominated escrow or trade-finance facilities. If used, the EMI must hold a registered FINTRAC MSB licence or a Canadian payment-service-provider authorisation, and the parent corporation must maintain its primary operating and capital accounts at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital must be routed as a foreign direct investment (FDI) SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 telegraphic transfer using a Statistics Canada ITRS purpose code corresponding to direct investment in a Canadian enterprise (equity). The Canadian receiving bank will require, in advance of or contemporaneously with the wire: the board resolution approving the share issuance, the subscription agreement, the certified incorporation documents, a source-of-funds attestation, and the remitter's banking details to complete sanctions screening. If the wire exceeds CAD 10,000, FINTRAC EFT reporting applies. CAD funds settle in real time through Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS) and are credited to a segregated capital escrow account; the corporation must file a CO-511 report with the Bank of Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System, and the CRS/FATCA self-certifications must be updated before booking.Trade settlements for battery-grade lithium carbonate procurements will be executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers (ISO 15022) or ISO 20022 pacs.008 messages for standard supplier payments, and through documentary Commercial Letters of Credit per ICC UCP 600 for larger or structured lots. The CAD leg is cleared via Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS) with final settlement at the Bank of Canada; USD legs clear via Fedwire/CHIPS through the US correspondent network. Every instruction must contain correct field 52A (ordering institution), 57A (beneficiary bank), and 59 (beneficiary), including the Chinese supplier's SWIFT BIC, to avoid OFAC, UN, CFPOA, and Global Affairs Canada sanctioned-party false-positive blocks.
