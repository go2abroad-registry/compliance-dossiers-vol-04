Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); online GST/HST account registration and Business Number portal (Canada.ca/business-number). Ontario corporate and HST liabilities are administered by the CRA on behalf of the Ontario Minister of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by applying online through CRA My Business Account. The GST/HST program account suffix (RT) is initiated at the same time or added later to the same BN. The applicant must provide its legal name, registered office or place of business, principal business activity, and the name of any Canadian representative. No separate VAT ID exists; the BN plus RT suffix serves as the GST/HST registration number. Ontario extra-provincial corporate licensing is administered by Ontario regulatory authorities, not by the CRA, but does not delay the BN process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law. A Business Corporation incorporated federally or under Ontario law is automatically classified as a corporation for income tax and GST/HST purposes. Available statutory elections include the GST/HST quick method election and annual filing election for eligible registrants; section 85 rollover elections for intercorporate transfers of property; and elections for deemed supplies of certain internal asset transfers. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act, sections 18.2 to 18.7, apply automatically by statute and do not require an affirmative taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in any trailing four-consecutive-calendar-quarter period. Once a supplier is no longer a small supplier, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Zero-rated and exempt supplies have distinct treatment, and non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold concept unless special registration rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exports of tangible personal property where the goods are supplied to a recipient and are exported from Canada as part of the supply. Truck feeder services used for the physical movement of bonded export goods to or from an international airport may be zero-rated as a supply of transportation service if the service is rendered with respect to goods destined outside Canada, the contract or waybill reflects a foreign destination, and the export is completed. Services performed wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated. Full compliance with the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, and retention of evidence showing actual export is required.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or approved CBSA Export Reporting Service output)
  • Commercial invoice and executed trucking/feeder freight contract
  • Bill of lading / waybill showing ultimate destination outside Canada
  • CBSA ACI eManifest cargo control document (PARS or bonded carrier consent) for highway truck movements
  • Proof of bank settlement for the export sale or freight payment, including ACH or wire credit advice confirming foreign-currency remittance

Canada does not operate a national real-time e-invoicing clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Electronic filing is mandatory for registrants above the CRA revenue threshold through GST/HST NETFILE or authorized third-party software. Export reporting is separate from tax invoicing and is handled through CBSA export reporting systems, including the Export Reporting Service and ACI eManifest for highway truck carriers. There is no API-based tax authority approval of individual invoices.

CRA may disallow zero-rating, reassess uncollected HST at 13%, and charge interest where the export documentation does not prove removal from Canada or where payment repatriation is absent or unexplained. Civil penalties include the Excise Tax Act penalty for false statements or omissions, generally 25% of the unremitted tax, plus gross negligence penalties for repetitive conduct. Export zero-rating fraud can also result in prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code, with significant fines, loss of business registration, and imprisonment for responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility in accordance with IMF Article VIII obligations. Dividend distributions, branch remittances, interest payments, equity withdrawals and loan repayments require no Bank of Canada or government approval. Operational friction is limited to FINTRAC large-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000), bank-level enhanced due diligence for ultimate beneficial owners, and CBSA documentation for trade-linked flows. No repatriation ceiling, payment queue, or waiting period applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished compulsory surrender of foreign exchange in 1951. A customs bonded road-feeder operator may retain export or cross-border service proceeds indefinitely in any convertible currency, with no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no surrender obligation. The only compliance overlay is ordinary Income Tax Act translation of foreign currency income at year-end and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border EFTs at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). The Bank of Canada exercises monetary-policy authority and acts as fiscal agent for the Exchange Fund Account; the Minister of Finance holds statutory responsibility for exchange-rate intervention and foreign-currency reserve management under the Financial Administration Act. Canada operates no separate foreign-exchange commission or pre-transaction licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate as a supplementary settlement channel, Low as a primary banking rail. A FINTRAC-registered money-service business or EMI such as Wise Canada ULC can provide low-cost CAD/USD routable account numbers and expedient SWIFT conversion, but is not eligible as the principal institutional account for capital escrow, CBSA customs-bond remittances, or large trade settlements. EMI accounts do not confer direct Payments Canada membership, have no physical branch for notarized sign-offs, and expose the treasury to concentration risk. The recommended architecture is a Schedule I bank core relationship with EMI used only for micro-disbursements and FX corridor efficiency.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed within 6 months after the end of the fiscal period. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year. Line 550 / 580 – Total taxes payable and refund Combined federal and provincial tax payable, less instalments, credits, and other payments, resulting in balance payable or refund. Ensure all federal and provincial instalments and any Part XIII withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, or other Canadian-source payments to the head office are reported and reconciled. Late filing penalty under ITA section 162(1) is 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% per full month late for up to 12 months (cumulative maximum 13% of unpaid tax). Penalties increase if the CRA demands a return and one is not filed. Interest compounds daily at the prescribed rate. Repeated or wilful failure to file may lead to criminal prosecution and heightened civil penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal monetary threshold in Canadian law for preparation of contemporaneous documentation; all cross-border non-arm's length transactions must be documented. In practice, CRA and OECD-aligned guidance expect a Local File and Master File when aggregate covered transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 per related counterparty or CAD 5,000,000 in total. The documentation must be provided within 90 days of a CRA request or an adjustment may attract a penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a distinct Economic Substance Regulations code as found in offshore jurisdictions. Instead, the doctrine is applied through case law, the statutory general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, ITA s.245), and the transfer pricing rules. A customs bonded trucking entity must demonstrate real personnel, dispatch offices, drivers, equipment, insurance, operational premises, and commercial risk; a shell vehicle with contractual flows but no substantial activity will be recharacterized by CRA and may face GAAR adjustments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s.239) and Criminal Code; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment of up to 5 years. Civil penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of gross negligence misstatement amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unreported source deductions and unremitted corporate taxes if they acquiesced in wilful evasion; CRA can pursue director personal assets, freeze bank accounts, and secure court orders preventing departure (travel restrictions) and requiring personal restitution.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the intermediary under GAAR (s.245) and reallocate profits to persons actually earning them; penalties of 50% for gross negligence associated with misstatements; part XIII withholding tax may be recaptured on amounts characterized as deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false certificates, cause misrepresentations, or transfer assets out of the corporation while knowingly stripping it of equity may be personally liable for civil restitution, and foreign travel may be restricted through court-ordered asset preservation orders.
Transfer Pricing Misinvoicing / Cargo Undervaluation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under s.247(2) increasing Canadian taxable income; TP penalty under s.247(3) is the lesser of 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment or CAD 24,000; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation may lead to application of the penalty and disallowance of deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in preparing false transfer pricing documents or directing the undercharge can be considered culpable under the wilful evasion framework; civil fraud claims and director surcharge penalties could arise, and CRA may pursue them personally for withholding liabilities arising from disguised profit repatriation.
Money Laundering / Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties ranging up to CAD 100,000 per violation for a corporation; senior officers may receive penalties in their personal capacity. Conviction may lead to imprisonment of up to 10 years for laundering proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct or fail to supervise money laundering activities face criminal liability, asset forfeiture of personal holdings, passport surrender orders, and Canadian court-imposed travel bans while proceedings are pending.
