Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital CHF 100,000; at least 50% (CHF 50,000) must be paid up in cash before registration. Funds must be held in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto / compte bloqué) at a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank until the Commercial Register confirms registration; the bank's release confirmation is a mandatory filing exhibit. In-kind contributions require an audit report and certified opening balance sheet.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), portal: www.estv.admin.ch / eVAT portal for VAT filings; UID Register maintained by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office / UID registration system.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity that carries out taxable supplies in Switzerland must first obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Identificateur d'entreprise / Identificatore d'impresa). The foreign entity registers for VAT through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's eVAT portal using Form 'Registration of a taxable person', accompanied by an official excerpt of the foreign commercial register, articles of incorporation, proof of the principal place of business, and a Swiss tax representative mandate if required. Upon acceptance, the FTA assigns a UID with a separate MWST-VAT number, which serves as the VAT identification number for invoicing and refund purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident foreign entity that carries out taxable supplies in Switzerland must first obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Identificateur d'entreprise / Identificatore d'impresa). The foreign entity registers for VAT through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's eVAT portal using Form 'Registration of a taxable person', accompanied by an official excerpt of the foreign commercial register, articles of incorporation, proof of the principal place of business, and a Swiss tax representative mandate if required. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box pass-through election. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is invariably taxed at the entity level on worldwide income in Switzerland subject to territorial allocation rules and tax treaties. Available tax elections relevant to the AG include the election to form a VAT group under Article 52 of the Swiss VAT Act, the election to waive VAT exemption on voluntary supplies (e.g., option to tax real estate under Article 22 VAT Act), and election to apply simplified accounting methods for input VAT deduction in certain sectors. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available in Swiss domestic law; however, Swiss anti-hybrid rules apply to neutralise double non-taxation effects.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 net taxable worldwide turnover per calendar year for Swiss-resident taxable persons; CHF 100,000 net taxable turnover from Swiss supplies for foreign-based undertakings with no Swiss place of business, calculated on a calendar-year basis. Exempt persons may opt-in at any time. For postal and low-value imports a separate registration may be required for foreign distance-selling suppliers above CHF 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate applies to the export of goods where the supplier transfers the power of disposal to the foreign buyer and the goods demonstrably leave Swiss customs territory; the export must be evidenced by a valid customs declaration and transport documents. For services, zero-rating applies when the place of supply is outside Switzerland, particularly B2B services supplied to a foreign-based enterprise, or B2C services effectively used abroad. Vertical ERP and CRM cloud services delivered electronically to non-resident customers generally fall outside Swiss VAT scope or qualify for export zero-rating where the place of supply is located abroad; the supplier must retain evidence of the customer's foreign residence, contract, and actual payment from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss customs exit declaration / ATS or NCTS message) proving physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR waybill, or other transport document evidencingcross-border movement
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the foreign purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment statement evidencing receipt of consideration from abroad

Switzerland does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for standard B2B/B2C invoicing. Invoices must contain the mandatory data set defined in Articles 26 and 27 of the VAT Act, including the supplier's UID/VAT number, beneficiary name, invoice date, nature of supply, consideration, and applicable VAT rate. Electronic invoices (e-invoices, PDF/A with XML data, Swiss eBill, QR-bill, and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X-style formats) are accepted, but the original machine-readable file must be retained for audit purposes. Processing and filing of VAT returns is performed via the eVAT online portal, which is the technological clearance point for periodic tax returns and VAT refund applications.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration conducts risk-based post-clearance audits of zero-rated exports and may require production of all underlying documents. If the required export proof is not complete, credible, and internally consistent, the tax authority denies the 0% treatment and reassesses VAT at the applicable standard rate, with default interest from the original due date. Intentional or negligent documentary falsification or fictitious exports trigger VAT evasion proceedings under Articles 96 to 102 of the VAT Act, leading to penalties of up to three times the evaded tax, criminal fines, and publication of the sanction. The statutory limitation period for prosecution of VAT evasion is generally 10 years. Additionally, the input VAT deduction related to the failed export is clawed back retrospectively, and further penalties apply for missing or false records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Means (Bundesgesetz über die Währung und Zahlungsmittel). There is no legal distinction between capital account and current account transactions; no exchange control, no approval requirement, no repatriation or surrender obligation applies to foreign-currency receipts or investments. Profit remittances such as dividends, royalties, and service fees are freely transferable in any currency, subject only to corporate and withholding tax compliance. The Swiss National Bank does not exercise licensing discretion over individual transactions or payment flows and imposes no quantitative restrictions. Practical hurdles to profit remittance may arise solely from bank counterparty due diligence and from Swiss withholding tax at 35% on dividends and analogous distributions, which can be reduced under treaty or exemption procedures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law does not require exporters to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any statutory timeline. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; proceeds may be retained in any foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts of the exporter. However, resident enterprises with large cross-border assets or liabilities are subject to statistical reporting obligations to the SNB for the Swiss Balance of Payments and International Investment Position. In particular, companies with annual turnover exceeding CHF 500,000 must file periodic reports (K1/K2 categories) through the SNB electronic reporting facility. Failure to file accurate reports is an administrative violation, not an FX restriction.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury infrastructure; acceptable only as a complementary payment rail. The Swiss banking regime requires a licence under Art. 1 of the Banking Act 1934 for accepting deposits from the public professionally, and a FinIA licence for financial market activities. Offshor e-money institutions (EMIs) domiciled in the EU cannot passport into Switzerland because Switzerland is not an EEA member state. An overseas digital EMI may provide non-deposit payment services into Switzerland only through a Swiss-licensed correspondent or by obtaining a fintech banking licence under Art. 1b BankG (authorisation cap of CHF 100 million in deposits). For a CH_AG vertical CRM SaaS entity, the practical approach is to pair a Tier-1 Swiss bank account as the operational reserve and treasury anchor with digital EMI accounts (e.g., Stripe, Airwallex, or Currencycloud) for collection and disbursement in foreign jurisdictions, provided the EMI's own KYC/AML controls are at least FATF-equivalent and contractual terms do not create unlicensed deposit-taking in Switzerland.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan injections must be routed through SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent correspondent clearings) with explicit purpose codes and documentation. For equity contributions, the bank will require a CORPORATE ACTION reference and a narrative describing 'Share Capital Contribution – Art. 630 ff. CO / Swiss capital increase'. Recommended purpose code fields: 'C901 – Capital transfer' in the customer-to-bank instruction; SWIFT P3 category type 'CASH'; and element 72 with reference 'CAPITAL INCREASE / NOTARIAL MINUTES' to prevent erroneous classification. Shareholder loans should use code 'L901 – Intercompany loan' and be supported by a written loan agreement, maturity schedule, and group treasury policy. The verification bundle for any inbound capital injection includes: (1) notarised board minutes and shareholder resolutions approving the contribution; (2) entry in the Swiss commercial register for the paid-up capital; (3) audited or unaudited financial statement demonstrating genuine use of funds; (4) confirmation that the transaction does not trigger Swiss withholding tax (capital contributions are not subject to WHT, while shareholder loans must avoid recharacterisation as 'lending business' under Circular DWSF 2023); (5) proof of the origin of funds from the ultimate shareholder and evidence that funds are transferred solely from that shareholder's bank account. For a vertical cloud AG, the optimal route is a one-time SWIFT MT103 from the parent treasury with advance notice to the Swiss account officer.For trade and operating disbursements, the primary clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 as irrevocable single-member transfer for ordinary vendor payments, software subscription reseller commissions, and payroll; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) incidental for cross-border projects where invoicing involves large implementation milestones and customer supply chains; (3) SEPA direct debit for collection from EU-end customers (since Switzerland is part of the SEPA zone, despite not being an EU member, using a Swiss bank with SEPA BIC ensures euro settlement at competitive cost); (4) batch payments using the SIC and euroSIC clearing systems for domestic and pan-European settlement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory rate on net profit (effective federal rate ~7.83% as the federal tax is deductible); combined federal and cantonal/communal effective rates range from ~11.9% (Zug) to ~21% (Geneva), with a typical national average of ~14.7%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on the gross amount of all dividends and other profit distributions paid by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory OECD-aligned Master File and Local File required for Swiss entities forming part of a multinational group with consolidated group revenue exceeding CHF 400 million (BEPS Action 13 implementation); below this threshold documentation is not mandatory but contemporaneous TP documentation is strongly recommended to defend the arm's length result; Advance Pricing Agreements (APAs) are available from the Federal Tax Administration

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute similar to OECD offshore centres; however, substantive economic substance is enforced through the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) Principal Purpose Test (PPT), Swiss anti-abuse case law, and the TRAF 2020 abolition of privileged holding/management/domicile company regimes; a Swiss AG must demonstrate genuine local presence—physical office, operating payroll, locally exercised management and decision-making—to access DTA benefits, participation relief, and to avoid requalification as a sham or mere booking entity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 3× the amount of federal tax evaded; additional cantonal/communal fines of 100% of the evaded tax; the underlying tax remains due with default interest; no criminal conviction for purely unintentional omissions if corrected immediately in certain cantons

Director personal liability: Board members and beneficial owners can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, subjected to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 3 years in aggravated cases of tax fraud
Qualified Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to CHF 250,000; imprisonment of up to 3 years; forfeiture of unlawful profits; loss of amnesty protection; duration of penalty assessment extended

Director personal liability: Criminal conviction of the responsible directors, officers, and ultimate beneficial owners; cross-border arrest warrants and extradition; mandatory restitution of unpaid taxes with interest from personal assets
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief and refund of the 35% withholding tax (MLI PPT); disallowance of participation relief and patent-box benefits; requalification of sham transactions as constructive dividends subject to WHT; reassessment with 5-year limitation period; penalties up to 3× evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors exposed to personal liability for unpaid withholding tax; civil claims by the Swiss tax administration for restitution; revocation of Swiss residence and work permits for foreign directors; exclusion from future directorship positions
Withholding Tax and Deduction Obligations (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: The corporation is held strictly liable for the full 35% WHT on the gross amount, even if no tax was withheld from the beneficiary; default interest at approx. 4.5% p.a.; procedural fines of up to CHF 10,000; the tax claim has preferential rank over ordinary unsecured creditors in bankruptcy

Director personal liability: Statutory personal liability of the board for WHT remittance; personal assets of directors can be seized for the unpaid WHT; criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years for fraudulent concealment of distributions; shareholders may be ordered to repay the net distribution in the liquidation process
AML and Compliance Red Flags (AMLA, Sanctions, PEP)

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to CHF 500,000 for regulated entities under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA); freezing of bank accounts; mandatory reporting to the Money Laundering Reporting Office (MROS); withdrawal of business licenses; dissolution of the company for serious or repeated violations

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers personally liable for AML breaches; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for money laundering; confiscation of stolen or laundered assets; travel bans; prohibition from serving as a director in any Swiss company; inclusion on international financial sanctions lists
