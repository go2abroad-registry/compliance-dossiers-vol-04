Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions), official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; electronic services and Swiss VAT registration portal: www.estv.admin.ch/mwst-e-services

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss commercial-register presence must file the official Swiss VAT registration form for non-established businesses through the ESTV eServices portal. The application includes a completed Form 60, powers of attorney, and declaration of expected Swiss turnover. Upon approval, ESTV issues a Swiss Value Added Tax identification number in the format CHE-123.456.789 MWST and, where applicable, the legal entity UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer). A Swiss tax representative or named Swiss agent must be appointed for service of process and VAT compliance if the entity has no seat, principal office, or permanent establishment in Switzerland. The AG, as a commercial-register entity, must also complete incorporation with the relevant Cantonal Commercial Registry, obtain the UID, and then register for VAT via Form 100 or the e-service channel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not apply a United States-style check-the-box regime. The Aktiengesellschaft is taxed as a separate, non-transparent corporate person under Swiss federal, cantonal, and communal income tax law. Available elections are limited to statutory tax mechanisms: the option for VAT grouping of closely controlled entities; the election of Swiss GAAP or IFRS for corporate income tax base determination; the qualified participation deduction and capital-contribution relief elections; the group tax allocation relief for reorganizations; and the Swiss simplification regime for qualifying cross-border supply structures. Hybrid mismatch provisions under Swiss tax law disregard or recharacterize economically abusive payments but are non-elective. There is no valid election for an AG to be treated as a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Swiss-established entrepreneur, annual worldwide taxable turnover of CHF 250,000 (or a Swiss domestic turnover above CHF 250,000 where the worldwide threshold applies only to undertakings not established in Switzerland). A non-established foreign supplier must register for Swiss VAT when annual taxable supplies effected in Switzerland reach CHF 100,000; for B2B services supplied by a non-established supplier, the reverse-charge mechanism generally transfers the VAT accounting obligation to the Swiss recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Swiss 0% VAT export rate applies: (1) to movable goods physically exported from Switzerland by the supplier or by an authorized third party and delivered abroad; (2) to services directly related to exported goods or to the export transaction; and (3) to qualifying offshore services rendered for and substantially used by recipients domiciled abroad. The supplier must prove export from the Swiss customs territory through a valid customs declaration and receipt by the foreign buyer; for pharmaceutical heparin sodium export, a batch certificate, invoice, transport documents, and proof of consideration must be consistent. Zero-rating is denied for purchases or services connected to consumption or use in Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / déclaration en douane d'exportation) with customs clearance reference
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Switzerland
  • Commercial invoice in the name of the Swiss exporting AG and the foreign buyer, with Incoterms and quantity/per-unit and batch reference to heparin sodium
  • Evidence of export dispatch, such as certificates of origin, EUR.1 or A.TR movement certificates, and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment confirmation showing settlement in foreign currency through Swift Wire or documentary Letter of Credit
  • Swiss VAT export evidence form, where the tax office requires formal substantiation of the zero-rated supply
  • Pharmaceutical batch dossier and conformity certificate for heparin sodium, required by Swissmedic and the importing jurisdiction

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. Swiss VAT law requires the issuance of a compliant commercial invoice within the civil-law billing period, with the invoice retained for ten fiscal years. The QR-bill and eBill electronic invoicing formats are recognized standards, including PEPPOL-compatible transmission, but paper invoicing remains lawful. For exports, the exporter must transmit an electronic customs export declaration through the Swiss Customs procedure system and retain customs clearance messages as fiscal evidence for the zero rate.

Swiss federal and cantonal VAT authorities may retrospecively disallow the export zero rating if documents are incomplete, untimely, inconsistent, or fail to establish that title and risk passed abroad. In such a case the supplier is assessed VAT at the standard rate of 8.1%, plus default interest from the VAT reporting period. Intentional or negligent incorrect export documentation can trigger penalties under Articles 96 and 100 of the Federal Act on Value Added Tax, including monetary fines up to CHF 400,000 or, for aggravated deliberate tax evasion, imprisonment of up to three years; the tax at issue may be collected from the recipient under joint liability rules, and professional disqualification or additional cantonal penalties may be imposed. The limitation period for assessing under-declared export VAT is generally ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible at free-market rates for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals, no surrender requirements, no repatriation restrictions, and no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts for convertibility purposes. Dividend remittances and capital repatriation by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft are unrestricted at the exchange-control level, but distributions of declared dividends are subject to Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35%, refundable in full or partially under the Swiss-EU/EFTA double-taxation treaties or reclaimable by qualifying Swiss corporate shareholders through Form 85/82 procedures. Profit and principal proceeds may be freely converted into any currency and held offshore, subject only to FINMA-compliant banking provenance documentation and statutory reporting to the SNB for balance-of-payments statistics where a single cross-border payment equals or exceeds CHF 10 million.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio into CHF, and no statutory repatriation timeline. An ISIC 3250 heparin-sodium exporting AG may lawfully maintain export receivables in USD, EUR, or CNH in domestic or foreign bank accounts, sell those currencies at freely negotiated market rates, or offset them against imported heparin-crude payables, provided the foreign-exchange transactions are reflected in the statutory accounting records and any cross-border payment reaching the SNB reporting threshold is declared for statistical purposes. The absence of any surrender rule applies equally to proceeds from active pharma extraction activities and to intercompany service or royalty income.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank, French: Banque nationale suisse), headquartered in Berne and Zurich, as the sole issuer of Swiss franc banknotes and the guardian of monetary and payments stability; the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervises banking and AML conduct, while the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF) coordinates international financial-market and sanctions policy. Switzerland applies no foreign-exchange licensing regime; the SNB maintains statistical oversight of cross-border payment flows through the National Bank Ordinance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail and only marginally acceptable as a complementary collection channel. Switzerland is not an EEA member and does not recognise EU or UK electronic-money-institution passporting; a foreign EMI may serve the Swiss AG solely through genuine reverse solicitation or a FINMA-regulated agency relationship with a Swiss licensed bank. A foreign digital EMI could also obtain a FINMA 'fintech' licence under Article 1b of the Banking Act for limited deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million without interest-bearing business, but full payment-account operation for an active pharmaceutical exporter generally requires a full Swiss banking licence or a delegated banking-authorisation model. Consequently, the blueprint treats EMIs as non-viable for the capital-escrow and export-LC clearing functions, while permitting their use for low-value foreign-currency receivable sweeps where the counterparty bank is FINMA-approved.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for the share-capital injection is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's own bank account to the Swiss bank's nostro account or directly to the blocked capital-escrow account, with a purpose code identifying a direct-equity participation in a resident enterprise for SNB balance-of-payments statistics. The receiving Swiss bank performs OFAC/SECO sanctions screening, negative-media checks, and source-of-funds verification against the board resolution and subscription documents before issuing the Form A liberation confirmation required by the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). There is no central-bank approval or FX licence; CHF wires clear same-day in central-bank money through the SIC system, while USD and EUR injections clear via SWIFT correspondent networks within one-to-two banking days, after which the capital is released to the operational account upon registration of the AG.Outbound clearing rails for heparin-sodium sales and international disbursements are: (i) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700 and advised or confirmed by the Swiss banking partner, with settlement executed through SWIFT MT103/MT202; (ii) direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account customers, cleared through SIC for CHF, through SEPA for EUR, and through the US correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS) for USD; (iii) documentary collections under URC 522 with payment at sight or by acceptance for restricted-currency and high-risk markets; and (iv) buyer-financing or supply-chain finance facilities denominated in USD and EUR secured on conforming transport and inspection documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland does not statutorily impose Local File/Master File thresholds; however, CbCR is mandatory for an MNE group with consolidated group turnover of CHF 900 million or more. Contemporaneous TP documentation should be maintained for all intercompany transactions and produced on request of the tax authority; practical audit-trigger threshold is generally CHF 250,000 per transaction category, and CHF 100,000 for related-party financing, royalty or service charges.

Economic substance scope: While Switzerland has not adopted a codified 'Economic Substance Regulation' in the style of UAE/offshore regimes, Swiss tax law, treaty practice and administrative guidance impose a de facto substance test for corporate taxpayers. A Swiss AG claiming tax residence and treaty benefits must be effectively managed in Switzerland, have a real office, personnel, decision-making independence, adequate capital for its operations, and must not be a letterbox entity. In the pharmaceutical sector, the Swiss entity must be able to demonstrate operational involvement in purchasing, logistics, quality control and risk-taking. Lacking substance leads to treaty shopping denial, tax avoidance re-characterisation under GAAR principles, and revocation of cantonal tax privileges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 175/176 DBG: fine of one-third of evaded tax for simple evasion; one to two times the evaded tax for tax fraud; additional delinquency interest of 3.5% per annum; revised separate assessment; in aggravated cases, criminal imprisonment up to three years and criminal fine up to CHF 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign or cause a false return assume personal criminal liability; are exposed to civil claims for restitution by the company; may be subject to travel bans and asset freezes during the inquiry; conviction leads to prison sentence and disqualification from serving as director.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Audit finding triggers re-assessment on arm's length principles: profits are reallocated to the place of effective management; treaty benefits denied; Swiss withholding tax of 35% is imposed on deemed distributions; administrative fine up to CHF 10,000; potential mandatory liquidation if the company is deemed non-existent.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for uncovered corporate tax and withholding tax when they failed to register or report; can be prosecuted for fraudulent representation; travel bans and criminal restrictions if the structure facilitated tax evasion; civil restitution of assets distributed out of capital.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations in Pharmaceutical Distribution

Statutory sanction: Administrative enforcement by FINMA: fines up to CHF 500,000, licence withdrawal and disqualification; criminal consequences under Art. 305bis StGB: imprisonment up to three years and fine up to CHF 1,500,000 (aggregate) for qualified money laundering; aggravated cases from predicate offences.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officer and MLRO can be criminally prosecuted; personal asset confiscation; travel bans and extradition possible; directors are barred from other FINMA-regulated entities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing taxable profit; secondary adjustment classifying excess payment as constructive dividend giving rise to 35% WHT plus 5% interest; penalties: one-third to double of the additional tax under Art. 175/176 DBG; documentation penalties up to CHF 10,000 based on failed production of records.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately approved the unjustified pricing are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalties; criminal tax evasion consequences apply; travel bans and imprisonment up to three years in aggravated cases.
Disguised Profit Repatriation and Unreported Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as hidden dividend distribution; Swiss withholding tax assessed at 35%, 5% default interest from the due date, and penalty of one-third to double of the unpaid tax under VStG; criminal conviction for failure to withhold.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for withholding and remitting tax; failure to remit constitutes a criminal offence leading to fines up to CHF 250,000 and imprisonment up to three years; board members may be subject to travel bans and are civilly liable for restitution of the unpaid tax to the State.
