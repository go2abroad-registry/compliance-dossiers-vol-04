Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — principal federal tax administration for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number registration. Portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Ontario Business Corporations Act. STEP 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC1. STEP 3: Open a GST/HST account under the same BN when annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available for foreign entities expecting a Canadian tax nexus. STEP 4: Ontario corporate tax obligations are administered by the CRA and filed with the federal T2 corporation income tax return. STEP 5: Non-resident corporations must designate a Canadian representative or agent for service of process, provide a Canadian mailing address, and maintain books and records in Canada if a permanent establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)); pass-through treatment is not available.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada; corporate status is determined by incorporation and cannot be overridden. CRA classifies foreign hybrid entities based on their legal characteristics and the Income Tax Act. Limited statutory elections are available, including partnership elections under subsection 96(3), intercorporate dividend refund elections, flow-through share elections, and capital cost allowance re-allocation elections. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are constrained by general anti-avoidance rule (section 245) and specific anti-hybrid provisions, such as excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (section 18.4).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies in Canada (for residents) or from a Canadian business (for non-residents); registration becomes mandatory once the 'small supplier' threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed for tangible personal property exported outside Canada, provided possession passes to a non-resident before consumption or use in Canada and the goods are shipped to a foreign destination. Services supplied to non-residents may qualify, excluding services related to Canadian real property or goods physically delivered in Canada. For OOG heavy project cargo logistics, the exporter must hold written contracts evidencing foreign destination, title transfer, and arm's-length terms.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration submitted to the Canada Border Services Agency via the Canada Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading (out-of-gauge / heavy-lift cargo B/L showing foreign port of discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign currency settlement or letter of credit proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice and project cargo packing list (ISIC 5229 scope description and HS classification)
  • Proof of export receipt signed by the consignee or receiving terminal

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies in Ontario. GST/HST invoices may be issued in any electronic format, subject to full readability and audit trail integrity. Mandatory electronic filing applies to registrants with annual net tax of more than CAD 10,000; returns are submitted through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE, API/XML services, or direct debit arrangements. Records must be retained in Canada for a minimum of six years.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rated claims by cross-matching customs export records, B/L data, and FX flows. If the export conditions are not evidenced, the supply is re-taxed retroactively, with unpaid GST/HST assessed under the Excise Tax Act, plus compound daily interest. Civil penalties may apply at 10% of net tax, increasing to 25% in cases of serious negligence; willful evasion carries criminal fines up to CAD 25,000 and up to 12 months' imprisonment on summary conviction, with higher penalties available under formal criminal proceedings. Any previously refunded tax may be clawed back from the date of supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains no exchange-control regime under the Currency Act; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no legal restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or capital repatriation by a non-resident shareholder, subject only to withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties. No central bank approval or government license is required for ordinary profit remittance or capital returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in CAD or any permitted foreign currency account. Reporting obligations do exist: cash or negotiable instrument movements equal to or exceeding C$10,000 must be reported to CBSA/FINTRAC, and Canadian tax residents must report specified foreign property and foreign income under the Income Tax Act where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority only; no foreign exchange control mandate); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CFT transaction reporting, while the Canada Border Services Agency administers cross-border currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-Limited. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) is not authorized to take deposit liabilities in Canada and cannot operate as a Schedule I or Schedule II bank. An EMI may lawfully provide payment services in Canada only if it is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, depending on the province, licensed under provincial money-services legislation. For project-cargo escrow, milestone payments, and documentary-credit operations, a Schedule I bank account remains the recommended primary rail; a FINTRAC-registered EMI platform may be used for low-value disbursements only.
