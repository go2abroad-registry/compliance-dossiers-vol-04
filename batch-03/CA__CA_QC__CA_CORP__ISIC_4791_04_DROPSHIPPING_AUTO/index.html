E-government filing portal

For a Quebec-registered business corporation (including a foreign-owned Canadian subsidiary) and for a foreign dropshipping principal, the statutory TIN/VAT ID procurement sequence is: (1) incorporate or maintain the CBCA/QBCA corporation and obtain the articles of incorporation and Quebec NEQ (numéro d'entreprise du Québec) from the Registraire des entreprises; (2) obtain the nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, which automatically creates the RC (corporation income tax) account; (3) open the GST/HST 'RT' program account on the same BN using Form RC1 or the GST/HST account registration service, stating the effective registration date and worldwide taxable supply volumes; (4) register for QST with Revenu Québec (combined federal/provincial registration election available) using the BN-based RQ account and, where applicable, the Quebec enterprise number; (5) appoint a Canadian representative for service if the corporation is a non-resident-owned or non-resident-managed entity without a physical establishment in Canada; (6) retain CRA and RQ certificates, account confirmation numbers, and authorization history as evidence of the statutory tax identity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, and no requirement for central bank approval on inward or outward remittances. Cross-border transfers equal to or above C$10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC. For dropshipping activities, payments for goods and services fall within the current account and are freely remittable. Dividends to non-resident shareholders are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax at treaty rates, but the underlying FX transfer is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds. Dropshipping receipts in foreign currency (e.g., USD) may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts, converted at commercial rates, or repatriated without a timeline. For statistical purposes, exporters must file a B13A Export Declaration if goods are exported, but for dropshipping where goods ship directly from suppliers (often non-Canadian), the service fee component is not an export of goods. No CAD conversion ratio exists. All records must be kept for tax and AML compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally regulated financial institutions; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF supervisory authority; Revenu Québec for provincial licensing of money services businesses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate. Canadian-regulated fintechs (e.g., Stripe Treasury, Payoneer, Wise Canada) can operate as payment service providers or money services businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC and provincial regulations. Foreign offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut) may offer CAD accounts but must be registered as MSBs in Canada if serving Canadian clients. For automated dropshipping with Stripe and PayPal as target rails, such EMIs provide ancillary clearing but cannot replace a Canadian chartered bank for settlement in CAD via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR). Key legal constraints: MSB registration, beneficial ownership disclosure, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. EMIs are less viable for capital escrow accounts due to lack of OSFI deposit insurance (CDIC).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant method: international wire transfer via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or shareholder to the Canadian operational account. Purpose code: In the message field 70 (Remittance Information) use 'Capital contribution – equity subscription' or 'Shareholder loan – intercompany funding' and include the Quebec enterprise registration number. Frequencies above C$10,000 trigger FINTRAC report. For amounts greater than C$100,000, the bank may require evidence of the corporate resolution and beneficial ownership. Funds can be received in CAD, USD, or EUR and converted via the bank's spot rate. The corporate account must be opened before the transfer. Ensure that the receiving bank has a valid correspondent relationship with the remitting bank; avoid using non-bank money transmitters for escrow capital injections.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 (Single Customer Credit Transfer) for standard supplier payments, with a corporate-to-corporate instruction including the supplier's full bank details and purpose code 'Trade settlement – dropshipping inventory'. For high-value transactions (typically > C$100,000), a Documentary Letter of Credit (Commercial LC, UCP600) issued by the Canadian bank can be used to secure supplier payment, especially with Asian manufacturers. Alternatively, CAD clearing can be executed via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day domestic settlement or via the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for retail payments. International disbursements must be screened against OFAC/UN/EU sanctions. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal period end; final balance of federal tax is due within 2 months after year-end (non-CCPC) or 3 months after year-end (CCPC); quarterly instalments become mandatory when tax payable exceeds C$3,000

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; the Quebec tax balance is due within 2 months after year-end for a non-CCPC and 3 months after year-end for an eligible CCPC; Quebec instalments are required when the prior year tax payable exceeds C$3,000
