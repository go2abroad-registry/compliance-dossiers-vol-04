Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA); electronic filing: GST/HST NETFILE / GST/HST Remittance

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on a commercial activity or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT-prefixed GST/HST program account. Procedure: submit CRA Form RC1A 'Request for a Business Number - Non-Resident' electronically through the CRA Non-Resident Registration service or by mail to the Sudbury Tax Centre; attach the foreign certificate of incorporation, identify the Canadian agent or representative for service, describe the nature of Canadian commercial activity, and declare anticipated Canadian GST/HST taxable turnover; the BN is generally issued within 2-5 business days and concurrently serves as the national tax identifier and as the program identifier for GST/HST, payroll, import/export (RM), and provincial Ontario accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal/provincial Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a resident taxpayer separately liable under Part I of the Income Tax Act to federal corporate income tax and Ontario corporate income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through election available; Territorial_Scope applies only to non-resident taxpayers earning Canadian-source income; no Tax_Exempt_Zone applicable.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; available elections include: Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election for closely related corporations to exclude intercompany supplies from GST/HST; Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216(1) election for non-residents to file a Canadian tax return on rental income; ITA s. 97(2) partnership rollover election; and Part XIII non-resident withholding elections. Hybrid mismatch rules aligned to OECD BEPS Action 2 apply under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (interest and dividend mismatches) and ss. 94-95 (offshore investment and foreign affiliate hybrid structures).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual small supplier threshold: registration is mandatory as a GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)); voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits; e-commerce marketplace rules effective July 1, 2021 may deem a distribution platform (e.g., Shopify acting as marketplace facilitator) to be the supplier of goods sold by non-resident sellers, with independent GST/HST registration and collection obligations for the platform.

No continuous transaction controls (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime exists in Canada; the Pan-Canadian Electronic Invoice (PDX) standard based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is voluntary for B2B and mandatory only for federal government procurement; CRA requires all GST/HST registrants with annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million to file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST Remittance; business records, sales invoices, and electronic audit files (e.g., Canadian equivalent of GIFI) must be retained in Canada or made electronically accessible to the CRA during an audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility prevails: Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with no exchange controls on current-account transactions, capital-account movements, dividend payments, or profit remittances to foreign shareholders. There are no approval queues, no surrender ratios, and no licensing gates for repatriation of equity or liquidation proceeds. Residual practical hurdles are regulatory rather than exchange-control: (i) FINTRAC cross-border currency and electronic funds transfer reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000 or above; (ii) bank-level KYC, beneficial-ownership, and source-of-funds verification on inbound capital; (iii) Canadian withholding tax exposure under Income Tax Act s. 212 on non-resident interest, royalties, and management fees, which elevates treaty eligibility and arm's-length intercompany documentation to treasury-critical status; and (iv) intraday liquidity considerations in less-traded crosses (EUR/CAD and GBP/CAD spreads widen) that make minimisation of non-USD foreign-currency inventory a prudent treasury policy for a cosmetics DTC operator.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951, Canada has imposed zero mandatory surrender, zero conversion-ratio, and zero repatriation-timeline requirements on export receipts. An Ontario-incorporated exporter may invoice in CAD, USD, or any currency, hold export proceeds indefinitely in resident or offshore accounts, and convert on the open market at its sole discretion. The only binding compliance gateways are: (i) FINTRAC declarations for physical cross-border currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export statistical reporting on customs documentation; and (iii) GST/HST zero-rating substantiation, requiring a documentary export suite (commercial invoice, bill of lading, proof of payment, centennial customs entry) where cosmetics are exported so that the Ontario HST framework treats cross-border sales as zero-rated supply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Canada's central bank and monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act, with complementary supervisory and administrative mandates exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF surveillance and cross-border transfer reporting, the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-control policy, and Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments Division for international capital-flow data collection. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control commission; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and the Bank of Canada, OSFI, and FINTRAC collectively constitute the de facto cross-border treasury regulatory perimeter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognize 'Electronic Money Institution' as a distinct licensed category; a foreign fintech acting as a payment intermediary must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, as of the 2025 regulatory transition, as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Cross-border platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are viable as a secondary corridor for low-value vendor disbursements, cross-currency conversion where all-in spreads beat the Tier-1 bank's small-business schedule, and payouts to foreign beauty suppliers; they are not viable as the corporation's master operating bank because they do not provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts, direct ACSS/Lynx settlement for Shopify Payments payout files, commercial letter-of-credit origination, or credit facilities for FX forwards. Recommended treasury topology: a Schedule I chartered bank as the cash-management anchor, one FINTRAC- and RPAA-registered fintech account as a conversion/disbursement overlay, and no reliance on unregulated offshore neobanks for CAD-denominated operational liquidity, as the merchant's own bank remains legally bound to perform client-identification duties that unregulated intermediaries cannot satisfy.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); reduced to 12.2% for qualifying Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation active business income on the first CAD 500,000 taxable income (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (non-resident withholding tax) on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to reduction under an applicable double taxation agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal-year-end. The final balance of federal and Ontario corporate tax is due two months after the fiscal year-end for corporations, or three months for qualifying CCPCs whose taxable income for the previous year exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is required when the total value of reportable cross-border transactions involving non-resident related parties exceeds CAD 2,000,000 in the taxation year; a Master File is required when the MNE group's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000 and the aggregate value of reportable transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000. A Canadian corporation that is a member of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of CAD 900,000,000 or more must file Country-by-Country Report (Schedule 208 / CRA slip).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Gulf Cooperation Council-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, economic substance is tested under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the sham doctrine, substance-over-form jurisprudence, and the OECD BEPS substantive activity requirement in treaty limitation-of-benefits provisions. A Canadian incorporated corporation is presumed resident but must maintain real decision-making, physical offices, employees, and accounting records in Ontario; a shell entity with no substance is exposed to CRA's anti-avoidance recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and s.247 transfer-pricing re-pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under s.239 of the Income Tax Act, tax evasion is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years on indictment (or two years less a day on summary conviction). Civil penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty on unreported net income pursuant to s.163(2). Ontario imposes analogous penalties on the provincial tax portion administered under the same T2 process.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and can be assessed under s.227.1 and s.160 of the Income Tax Act. Criminal conviction may lead to immediate fines, imprisonment, departure denials, and extradition risk where funds or evidence are routed across borders.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reallocate income back to the Canadian taxpayer, apply the 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends, and impose a penalty of 50% of the tax avoided. The Ontario Minister may administratively dissolve the corporation for failure to comply with the Corporations Information Act, and the corporation may be deemed a sham at common law, causing all transactions to be disregarded.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham shell company can be held personally liable for unremitted Part XIII taxes and source deductions under s.227.1. A court can pierce the corporate veil for fraud or tax evasion, ordering civil restitution from director assets; in serious cross-border schemes, a travel ban may be imposed pending criminal trial.
AML / Money-Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 100,000 for a first violation and up to CAD 500,000 for repeated or deliberate violations. Criminal money-laundering under the Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years and fines of up to CAD 500,000. A FINTRAC non-compliance finding also publicly exposes the taxpayer to bank de-risking and audit escalation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and senior managers are personally exposed to criminal liability where they know, direct, or intentionally leave wilful blindness to laundering design. Individual directors may be fined and imprisoned; travel restrictions are available through judicial interim release conditions where a foreign flight risk is established.
