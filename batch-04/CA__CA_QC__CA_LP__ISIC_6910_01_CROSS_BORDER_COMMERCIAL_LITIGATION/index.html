Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed in the partnership agreement (art. 2237 CCQ) and may be in cash, property or services. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) for federal GST/HST and Business Number administration, with taxpayer portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/gst-hst.html); Revenu Québec for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec payroll accounts, with portal 'Revenu Québec Online' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/). The limited partnership must also obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) before completing tax registration.

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) engaged in cross-border commercial litigation services: (1) file the declaration with REQ to register the partnership and obtain an NEQ, identifying the general partner and registered office; no minimum capital is required; (2) apply to the CRA for a Business Number using Form RC1, selecting the general partner as the partnership's 'selected partner' and representative for GST/HST, so that the partnership holds its own BN and GST/HST program account; (3) register the same partnership with Revenu Québec for QST, using the combined CRA/Revenu Québec registration service or the equivalent RQ web registration, and providing the NEQ, partnership agreement, and declarations of all general and limited partners; (4) where the general partner is non-resident, designate an authorized representative in Canada and file the relevant non-resident representation election with CRA/RQ before taxable revenue reaches the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian income tax purposes the LP is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer; each partner, including each non-resident partner, includes its share of partnership income or loss under ITA s. 96. For indirect tax purposes the same LP is a distinct 'person' for GST/HST and QST, and must hold its own registration, collect output tax, and file GST/HST and QST returns. Territorial_Scope: a non-resident partner is taxable only on Canadian-source income from the partnership, and in Quebec on income from property or a business carried on in Quebec, subject to treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. The primary statutory deeming election is ITA s. 96(3), which permits the members of what would otherwise be a partnership to elect that the arrangement be treated for income tax purposes as co-ownership rather than a partnership. For indirect taxes, the 'selected partner' mechanism allows the partnership to authorize one partner to administer, file, and remit GST/HST and QST on its behalf. There are no elective carve-outs from Canada's hybrid mismatch rules, which follow the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework; taxpayers must instead align the tax classification of the LP across jurisdictions and avoid deductible/non-includible arbitrage, particularly where the foreign jurisdiction treats the LP as a corporation while Canada treats it as transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the federal small supplier threshold (ETA s. 148(1)) and the parallel QST small supplier threshold require mandatory registration once taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made by the LP in Canada or Quebec exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding three consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold, and once the threshold is exceeded the LP must register retroactively to the first taxable supply that triggered the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the sale of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports them by shipping to a destination outside Canada. Services: legal, arbitration, and asset-tracing services supplied to a non-resident client are zero-rated if the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant in Canada and the service is not 'in respect of' real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of supply; for QST the corresponding disqualifying nexus is real property or tangible personal property situated in Quebec. Cross-border commercial litigation services for a foreign party are therefore zero-rated only where the dispute, claim, or asset recovery does not touch Canadian or Quebec immovables or goods physically in the jurisdiction; where the foreign client is registered for GST/HST and acquires the service for use in a Canadian commercial activity, zero-rating is likewise unavailable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A for any shipped goods supporting the litigation file; nil declaration for pure legal services)
  • Engagement letter and itemized legal fee statement evidencing the non-resident recipient, the offshore nature of the supply, and the absence of any nexus to Canadian real property or goods located in Canada/Quebec
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement evidencing payment remitted from outside Canada into the LP's IOLTA trust account or operating account
  • Certificate of residence of the non-resident client and, where treaty relief is invoked, the applicable Canadian treaty form (e.g., NR301, NR302, or Schedule A) together with the foreign jurisdiction's tax identification number

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no invoice-level e-invoicing regime exists in Canada or Quebec. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices provided the format and content satisfy statutory record-keeping rules. Voluntary Peppol BIS e-invoicing is available for B2G transactions with the federal government, and Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns and certain information returns, but there is no transaction-level pre-clearance or tax authority API validation.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated claim beyond the normal four-year reassessment window under ETA s. 298(6) where the registrant has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. A successful clawback reverses the 0% rate and re-imposes GST at 5% plus QST at 9.5%, yielding 14.975% combined output tax, together with daily compound arrears interest and penalties. Civil penalties include the ETA false-statement penalty equal to 50% of the net tax in issue and parallel QST penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act; directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable under ETA s. 323 and the corresponding QST director's liability provision; criminal prosecution for evasion can result in fines and imprisonment. Revenu Québec conducts parallel audits and will apply its own clawback and penalty rules where the zero-rated supply is found to have nexus with Quebec.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange controls, satisfying IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible into and out of the Canadian dollar (CAD) without central-bank authorization, advance-deposit requirements, or repatriation licenses. Profit remittance from a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) to non-resident partners is lawful and requires no prior Bank of Canada approval; however, Canadian federal income tax on partnership income allocable to non-resident partners and Part XIII withholding on certain distributions (statutory 25%, potentially reduced under the Canada-China Tax Treaty of 1986, as amended) apply. Quebec levies its own tax and withholding rules under the Taxation Act (RLRQ c. I-3) and requires Form TP-64.3 or analogous filings from non-resident partners conducting business in Quebec. Litigation recoveries and fee receipts are treated as ordinary business revenue and are not subject to currency expropriation or mandatory conversion. The only practical hurdle is elevated tax-compliance documentation (NR6, NR4, T2042, treaty-residency certificates) and FINTRAC reporting on significant cross-border currency or wire transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligations, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no repatriation timelines for export proceeds or cross-border service receipts. The Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) applies only to controlled goods, technology, and sanctioned jurisdictions under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA); it does not govern currency conversion. For legal-services receivables under ISIC 6910 (cross-border commercial litigation, international arbitration and asset tracing), foreign-origin fees may be received in USD, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies via correspondent banking and converted at prevailing interbank CAD rates with zero surrender requirement. The sole compliance triggers are FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or above), tax accrual rules under fiscal-year income reporting for accrual-basis partnerships, and, where foreign partners are present, Quebec's provincial tax remittance framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52); anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Quebec-specific supervision of registered money-services businesses and securities matters by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary settlement and infrastructure. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not recognized as authorized clearing agents under the Canadian Payments Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-21) and the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c 23), which requires any payment service provider operating in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada for end-to-end retail payment clearing; furthermore, FINTRAC requires MSB registration under the PCMLTFA for receiving or transmitting funds on behalf of others. A Quebec LP cannot lawfully hold legal-client trust funds in an unlicensed offshore EMI, as counsel owes fiduciary and IOLTA-preservation duties under the Barreau du Québec regulations. For the LP's own operational accounting and treasury aggregation, select EMIs with Canadian MSB registration and a Canadian physical office may be acceptable as supplementary rails for fee collection, but the core liquidity clearing must remain with a Schedule I bank. The recommended stance is a dual-entity architecture: bank-account primary plus FINTRAC-registered EMI fallback strictly for non-client receivables and fee disbursements.

Defense evidence: Certificate of formation from REQ showing date of registration; executed LP agreement with capital-commitment schedule; proof of economic substance in Quebec (a lease of office premises, local professional staff contracts, or an affiliation agreement with a Quebec-qualified avocat firm); bank-capital-transfer receipt showing the initial escrowed contribution from the first foreign limited partner; the general partner's constitutional documents and dual identification (passport plus corporate registry excerpt) with a live, notarized signature verification; and independent legal advice certificate confirming the LP's power to contract, to hold trust funds, and to execute FX hedging instruments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners fund capital contributions through a wired chain: sender's home bank (e.g., a PRC bank conducting an outbound remittance under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) framework using purpose code for outward direct investment or partnership contribution) → intermediate global correspondent (usually via SWIFT MT103 or, for large volume, MT202 COV) → Canadian Schedule I bank's Nostro account → final credit to the Quebec LP capital escrow account. The Canadian bank requires the inbound wire to carry a clear payment reference of 'Partnership Capital Contribution — [LP Name], REQ File Number' to reconcile against the FINTRAC large-transaction regime. For CAD settlement, the receiving bank clears through Lynx (the Canadian Large Value Transfer System successor to LVTS) with real-time gross settlement; for USD, the preferred rail is via a US correspondent Fedwire transfer converted to CAD at the LP's chosen conversion rate. The three-way escrow arrangement (LP escrow + counsel trust account + funding partner escrow) is standard for multi-jurisdictional litigation chambers to prevent misapplication of capital; all inbound funds are verified against the partnership's AML/KYC counterparty blocklist and sanctions screen before final posting. Documentation package: certified copy of the LP agreement, a board/general-partner capital-call resolution, the source-of-wealth letter, and the foreign bank's remittance confirmation. Canadian banks will request the LPA's subscription-commitment table and a statement of non-lending/non-deposit-taking nature. Deposit of CAD 10,000 or more requires FINTRAC large-transaction reporting within 15 calendar days by the bank.For the operational disbursement of litigation-funding or settlement-distribution flows, primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for single high-value wire transfers (memo lines must identify the underlying litigation file and matter name to permit sanctions-pattern analysis); (ii) MT202 COV for cover payments when settling through a Canadian correspondent with a foreign beneficiary bank; (iii) commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP 600-ruled) where the litigation involves payment of court costs, expert fees, or deposit of a security-for-costs bond to an overseas court registry; and (iv) Lynx for same-day CAD vendor payments to Canadian counsel, experts, and the Quebec legal-services trust account. For currency pairs, Canadian banks conventionally process USD/CAD conversion at the session's spot price with an explicit consumer-risk disclosure; any outgoing wire in JPY, EUR, CNY, or GBP uses a correspondent chain through the issuing bank's European or Asian counterparty. Trade disbursements to Chinese counterparties must include the correct SWIFT BIC format plus an 11-digit CNAPS number for CNY, or use an MT103 with the beneficiary's bank IBAN/EUR platform for EUR-denominated payments. All outbound wires above CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC; outbound wires to a SEMA/UN-sanctioned jurisdiction must be rejected. A commercial letter of credit may be issued by the Canadian bank on behalf of the LP only where legal counsel certifies the underlying obligation, to avoid financial-institution liability concerns.Outright CAD/USD FX Forwards (vanilla over-the-counter forwards with settlement dates matched to the litigation settlement calendar and fee-equalization dates; exempt from exchange-trade mandate under Securities Act (Québec) because they are entered into by an eligible commercial counterparty for hedging, not speculation)Rolling FX Forwards (extendable foreign-exchange forwards used to bridge multi-tranche settlement proceeds across different arbitration hearing dates; require ISDA 1992/2002 master agreement and Credit Support Annex margin posting when non-bank LP counterparty)Cross-Currency Swaps (CAD/USD interest-rate-and-principal swaps used to align the LP's USD-denominated escrow settlement assets with CAD operating liabilities; permissible under Quebec's derivatives framework and subject to trade-reporting to the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) through a registered swap data repository where the notional exceeds CAD thresholds)FX Options (CAD call / USD put or USD call / CAD put structures used to cap conversion cost on large settlement receipts; available through bank treasury desks with a threshold of 90 days tenor to avoid speculative classification under the Income Tax Act hedger treatment)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary settlement and infrastructure. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not recognized as authorized clearing agents under the Canadian Payments Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-21) and the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c 23), which requires any payment service provider operating in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada for end-to-end retail payment clearing; furthermore, FINTRAC requires MSB registration under the PCMLTFA for receiving or transmitting funds on behalf of others. A Quebec LP cannot lawfully hold legal-client trust funds in an unlicensed offshore EMI, as counsel owes fiduciary and IOLTA-preservation duties under the Barreau du Québec regulations. For the LP's own operational accounting and treasury aggregation, select EMIs with Canadian MSB registration and a Canadian physical office may be acceptable as supplementary rails for fee collection, but the core liquidity clearing must remain with a Schedule I bank. The recommended stance is a dual-entity architecture: bank-account primary plus FINTRAC-registered EMI fallback strictly for non-client receivables and fee disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined effective headline rate for a resident corporate partner in Quebec (federal net rate 15% [statutory 38% less 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction] plus Quebec corporate tax rate 11.5%); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and taxed at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on resident corporations' dividends paid to non-resident individuals or unincorporated partnerships; no distinct Quebec dividend withholding tax is imposed because Canada levies federal Part XIII tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal T5013 deadline: the earlier of March 31 in the calendar year following the fiscal year-end and the day on which the first partner's income tax return is due. Quebec RL-15 deadline: March 31 following the partnership fiscal year-end, or the same earlier partner-return date. A December 31 fiscal year-end therefore requires filing by March 31 of the next calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Documentation obligations under section 247 of the Income Tax Act are triggered when the aggregate fair market value of all reportable transactions between a Canadian taxpayer and a related non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million per person in a taxation year; for a transaction with a non-resident related person involving a share of a corporation, partnership, trust, or a right to income, the CAD 1 million threshold also applies but requires contemporaneous documentation and Form T106. Concurrent preparation of a CBC Local File is expected for any controlled transaction above this threshold, and a Master File is expected for entities in multinational groups with aggregate related-party revenue exceeding CAD 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations regime for local partnerships, but the GAAR (subsection 245(2) Income Tax Act), law-sham doctrine, and CRA's administrative position under IC 87-2R require the LP to have genuine business activity and real economic substance. A Quebec LP used solely to route litigation returns through a tax-favoured jurisdiction will be challenged as a sham, a conduit, or an abuse of treaty provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statement in Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus interest and penalties; civil penalty under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated amount for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: General partners of a Quebec LP have unlimited personal liability; limited partners who take part in the control and management (Civil Code of Quebec art. 2237) lose limited liability. Directors of the corporate general partner face joint and several liability for unpaid source deductions (s.227.1 ITA), Quebec health contribution, and any unpaid partnership GST/QST, with travel bans under the Tax Administration Act and 5-year imprisonment for employer failures to remit.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterisation of the transactions and denial of treaty benefits and deductions; CRA may issue reassessments with penalties and interest; failure to maintain the LP's registration with Registraire des entreprises du Québec leads to administrative dissolution and acceleration of compliance obligations.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the partnership veil. The corporate general partner and its active directors may be held personally liable for the LP tax debt under section 462 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act (unpaid amounts owed by the partnership) if they authorised the artificial structure; persistent willful neglect supports a direct finding of accessory liability.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Vulnerabilities

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals, plus criminal sanctions for laundering the proceeds of designated offences; additional Quebec securities/legal-sector sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be subject to personal fines, restitution orders, and the 10-year FINTRAC compliance discipline; criminal prosecution can result in up to 10 years' imprisonment and travel restrictions pending trial.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's Length Misallocation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of consideration under section 247 of the Income Tax Act with double-counting protection; penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment for amounts in excess of the higher of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; also penalty for failing to file Form T106 (late-filing penalty of CAD 100 to CAD 10,000).

Director personal liability: CRA can reassess a non-resident LLP partner or the general partner; directors who approve a transfer-pricing scheme knowing it is artificial may be personally liable under Quebec partnerships law and the Tax Administration Act, with forfeiture of limited-partnership protection and possible prohibitions on leaving Canada.
Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent (the corporate general partner and the LP) becomes liable for the full unreported 25% Part XIII tax, plus daily interest and penalties (5% plus 1% per month). Section 235 failure to withhold also exposes the payer to a further penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly authorised payment free of withholding are jointly and severally liable with the partnership for the unpaid Part XIII tax and may be subject to a civil restitution order; deliberate evasion is prosecutable as a crime with imprisonment up to 5 years.
