Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the Canton of the registered office, e.g., Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug; publication via the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt, SHAB); federal supervision by the Federal Office of the Register of Commerce (Eidgenössisches Amt für das Handelsregister, EAR) under the Federal Department of Justice and Police.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR) of 30 March 1911 (SR 220), as amended; Stock Corporation provisions at Arts. 620-763 OR; incorporation, capital payment and registration at Arts. 629-640 OR; supplementary rules under the Swiss Company Law Reform Act of 19 June 2020 (in force 1 January 2023), and the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Public notarial deed of incorporation (öffentlich beurkundete Gründungsurkunde) incorporating the Articles of Association (Statuten), together with the Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) and the Board's Declaration (Erklärung des Verwaltungsrats) confirming that the subscribed capital is paid and freely disposable.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital: CHF 100,000. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid in, and in any event no less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate; cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank or licensed financial intermediary and released to the company's free disposal only upon entry in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cantonal Fire Insurance Authority (Kantonale Gebäudeversicherung) confirming smoke detectors, extinguishers and escape routes; structural load and office occupancy clearance from the municipal building authority for the registered office; occupational health and safety registration with SUVA under the Swiss Accident Insurance Act (UVG), including the annual premium statement; compliance with the Swiss Labour Act (Arbeitsgesetz, ArG) on office working hours and with the information and consultation rights under the Swiss Code of Obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), portal: www.estv.admin.ch and www.gever.admin.ch (UID registry); cantonal tax authorities (e.g., Cantonal Tax Office of Zug, Finanzdepartement) for direct taxes; Swiss Federal Customs Administration (Oberzolldirektion) for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) under Swiss Code of Obligations, execute notarial deed, file with Swiss Commercial Register; the Commercial Register automatically assigns the statutory Swiss business identification number (UID / UID-CH, based on Elite ID structure). Step 2: Foreign-controlled AG established in Switzerland must immediately register for VAT with the ESTV on the official VAT registration form (Form 048-S) or through the online 'SAP-MWS' portal, using the UID as VAT number; a Swiss resident AG is liable for VAT irrespective of the CHF 250,000 turnover threshold if it is the registered owner of the enterprise (permanent place of business in Switzerland is not required for registration). Step 3: Obtain employer social insurance registration with the cantonal AHV/AVS compensation office and register for corporate income/capital tax with the competent cantonal tax authority for ordinary assessment; a foreign company with no Swiss place of business but supplying services taxable in Switzerland may use the expedited simplified VAT registration procedure via ESTV.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland applies a unitary entity-level tax model with no US-style Check-the-Box election; no federal tax consolidation exists for direct tax. Available statutory elections relevant to an aircraft engine leasing AG: (i) VAT group taxation election under Art. 57 of the Swiss VAT Act (Mehrwertsteuergesetz), permitting a group of companies to file as a single VAT taxpayer; (ii) participation deduction / capital contribution repayment election to minimise Swiss withholding tax on repatriated funds; (iii) partial taxation procedure for qualifying capital gains; (iv) election under Art. 61 VAT Act to allocate second-hand movable property margins; (v) hybrid mismatch safeguards under Art. 28 VATA granting reduced input VAT recovery where the lessor elects not to treat the foreign lessee as a deemed reseller.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover from January 1, 2025 (previously CHF 100,000); mandatory registration for the SAP/MWS portal with UID; an AG is also strictly liable if it has a permanent establishment, holds a Swiss aviation permit, or issues invoices in Swiss francs for aviation services consumed in Switzerland.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of aircraft engine dry-leasing services qualifies for 0% VAT (Art. 23 VAT Act) only if the lessor proves that: (i) the recipient is a non-Swiss business (foreign lessee) supplying documentary proof of foreign VAT/UID registration; (ii) the aircraft engine remains located outside Switzerland for the exclusive non-Swiss commercial use; (iii) the leased asset is directly connected to the foreign lessee's aviation operations under ISIC 5223; and (iv) the place-of-supply rule (Art. 7 VATA) places the rental in the foreign country (where recipient has its seat). For goods export, the engine must leave Swiss customs territory physically; zero-rating is denied where tests are rebutted by customs or tax audit.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / NCTS or AVN summary declaration) evidencing the physical departure of the turbine engine from Swiss fiscal territory
  • Original or certified Copy of the Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading naming the foreign lessee as ultimate consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank settlement proof for lease rentals received from the foreign lessee
  • Lease agreement (dry-lease contract) stating operator, registration marks, engine serial number, and delivery/redelivery airports
  • Foreign lessee's VAT/company registration certificate (e.g., EU VAT ID, FAA/ICAO evidence) and engine logbook or maintenance movement card indicating non-Swiss location during the lease term
  • Power of Attorney (PoA) and UID certificate of the Swiss AG for export control verification

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate or government API/Swiss digital stamping regime exists as of the 2025 tax year. Switzerland follows OECD-compliant electronic invoice exchange rules (EN/ISO 20022-Norm, Swiss eBill/QR-bill for payments) but fiscal validation is archiving-based under Art. 70–72 VATA and Art. 21 of the Swiss Code of Obligations; conservative audit trail retention and structured, signed EDI/e-invoice flows are expressly permitted but private. A mandatory nationwide B2B e-invoicing clearance is not enacted; the Federal Council maintains a voluntary period.

The ESTV and federal customs conduct exporter audits of aviation lessors under Arts. 77–79 VATA; missing export proof triggers retroactive VAT assessment at 8.1% on the full leasing revenue, plus compound statutory interest (4% per annum) from the invoice date. Under Art. 96 VATA, cases of contained tax evasion attract a fine of up to 4x the evaded tax; intentional, concealed fraud under Art. 97 VATA triggers a fine of up to 8x the evaded amount and up to 3 years imprisonment for responsible officers, with confiscation; the limitation period for clawback is 10 years (Art. 42 f. VAT Act). Customs sanctions under Art. 116–117 Customs Act and Swiss Criminal Code Art. 146 also apply concurrently. The 180-day statutory FX repatriation deadline stated above is the contractual safe harbour under Swiss transfer-pricing practice (2025 OECD alignments); repatriation beyond 180 days shifts the burden of proof onto the taxpayer for commercial necessity and triggers a deemed distribution risk under withholding tax with possible 35% WHT liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Switzerland maintains a completely liberalized capital and current account regime with no general exchange-control law and no SNB approval requirement for cross-border capital movements. Capital account: a foreign parent or investor may subscribe shares or grant a shareholder loan to CH_AG without authorization; the non-resident real-property restriction of the Lex Koller is inapplicable to aircraft-engine leasing. Current account: lease rentals, MRO payments, and insurance settlements in any convertible currency flow freely. The only material profit-remittance hurdle is fiscal: outbound dividends from a Swiss AG are subject to 35% anticipatory Swiss withholding tax at distribution, reducible or refundable to 0% under an applicable double-tax treaty (typically 0% for shareholders holding 10% or more; 15% or 5% for portfolio investors) via the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA) relief or refund procedure. No exchange-control permit, capital-export declaration, or SNB registration is required for such remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Switzerland imposes neither statutory surrender nor mandatory conversion of export proceeds. CH_AG may receive USD/EUR lease rental income from foreign lessees and retain it in non-CHF accounts onshore or offshore without any timing or conversion-ratio constraint. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations, collected by banks under SNB reporting rules for aggregate cross-border flows, apply; these carry no convertibility or repatriation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque Nationale Suisse) acting under the National Bank Act (SR 951.11), with FINMA supervising banks and SECO enforcing Swiss sanctions and export-control measures. The SNB has no exchange-control mandate; its foreign-exchange operations serve only monetary policy, including discretionary market intervention to counter excessive CHF appreciation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury solution; Medium only as an ancillary bill-pay rail. A foreign EMI (e.g., UK/Lithuanian/Dutch-licensed electronic money institutions or banking-as-a-service providers) is not FINMA-licensed under the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA, SR 954.1), has no direct access to Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC/euroSIC), cannot issue Swiss IBANs (CHxx), and cannot provide capital-escrow, commercial LC, standby LC, or documentary-custody services expected by an aircraft-engine lessor. Use of such EMIs should be strictly limited to low-value outbound disbursements and API-driven virtual sub-accounts swept daily into a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank account; all cash concentration, lease-rent receipt, security-deposit escrow, and large-value SWIFT traffic must be executed through a licensed Swiss banking institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

4.4% Swiss federal direct tax on profit (Art. 68 DBG) plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; aggregate headline rate 11.9%-21.0% depending on canton of formal seat (e.g., Zug approx. 11.9%, Zurich approx. 19.7%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal statutory minimum: the return must be filed within 30 days of service of the request (Art. 124 DBG). In ordinary cantonal practice for a 31 December balance-sheet date the deadline is set around 30 June of the following year, with extensions of 2-3 months granted on written request; foreign-parent groups typically receive extended filing windows if consolidated reporting is not ready.
