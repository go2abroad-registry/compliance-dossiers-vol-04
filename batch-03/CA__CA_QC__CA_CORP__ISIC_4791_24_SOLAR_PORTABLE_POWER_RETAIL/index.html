Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the QBCA; the articles may create one or more classes of shares, with or without par value. No escrow or capital deposit account is required. For the online retail activity, the incorporators typically subscribe 1,000 no-par common shares for cash or in-kind consideration, and the paid-up capital is recorded in the shareholders register subject to the general solvency tests of the QBCA.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification regime. A Business Corporation is per se an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)) and Part I of the Quebec Taxation Act (RLRQ c. I-3). Available elections relevant to this e-commerce structure include: (1) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status election via constating documents and share registers (subject to Part XXIX of the CBCA and s. 125(7) ITA definitions); (2) small supplier threshold election — a person may elect under s. 233(2) ETA to waive small-supplier status and voluntarily register for GST/HST; (3) GST/HST reporting-period election (monthly, quarterly, annual) and net-tax calculation using the 'Quick Method' permitted for certain retailers with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000 (exclusion for online marketplace operators under proposed rules requires case analysis); (4) section 85(1) ITA / s. 36(14) Taxation Act tax-deferred rollover elections for property transfers into the corporation on incorporation; (5) QST input-tax-refund (QST ITR) rebate election for newly incorporated entities; (6) inter-provincial permanent-establishment allocation election (Form T2202 for federal, and agreement-based allocation under the Quebec Taxation Act). Hybrid mismatch elections: Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 12.7 and 18.4-18.6 ITA) do not permit elective carve-outs; mismatches are countered automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, s. 148 ETA and s. 80(1) AreVTQ); zero-registration for foreign entities with substantial presence or physical delivery into Canada; the threshold is immediately exceeded once the CAD 30,000 mark is crossed in a single calendar quarter, at which point registration becomes compulsory effective at the earlier of the date of exceeding or the first day of the following month.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (portable battery generators and solar panels) from Canada are zero-rated supplies under s. 23 ETA (for GST/HST) and s. 22 of the QST rules, provided the goods are exported by the supplier (or by a non-resident consignee, or under the verification requirements of s. 23(2) ETA) within 60 days of the sale and documentary evidence is retained. Services directly tied to exported goods (warranty, installation instructions, remote technical support for offshore customers) are zero-rated under ss. 7 and 9 Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA when the services relate to exported goods and are performed for a non-resident. Sales to non-residents who do not take delivery in Canada (online sales to US consumers) qualify for zero-rating where the seller retains proof of export. Intra-Canada online retail deliveries to consumers in Quebec attract 5% GST and 9.975% QST at the point of delivery (place of supply rule for goods: the province where the goods are received).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration submitted electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS, or G13/E14 declaration for controlled goods; contains exporter BN, HS classification, value, and destination)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/rail/road) or courier airway bill showing the non-resident consignee and port of exit
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice showing zero-rated consideration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API regime currently applies in Canada. GST/HST and QST invoicing is self-issuance based: suppliers must issue tax invoices (containing vendor name/BN, taxable amount, tax amount, and exemption/zero-rating identification) within 15 days of the supply. Electronic invoicing is permitted subject to the integrity requirements of s. 4 of the ETA and s. 36 of the QST regulations (legibility, audit trails, and retention of 6 years). The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol PINT A-NZ profile) is voluntary; the CRA and Revenu Québec do not operate a government clearance node. Marketplace platform operators (e.g., Stripe, Shopify) may be liable for GST/HST and QST on facilitated supplies under the platform vendor rules (s. 178.2 ETA; s. 473.2 AreVTQ), although the operating Canadian corporation is the primary taxpayer for direct retail sales. For business-to-consumer sales under CAD 150, no customs clearance is required for online shipments, but a fully completed 'Form GST370' is retained as part of the exporter's records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status. There are no general exchange-control approvals for trade or investment flows. Profit remittance by a Quebec business corporation to non-resident shareholders is tax-driven rather than exchange-driven: dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on the gross amount (25% statutory; reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g. 5–15% for treaty jurisdictions), and interest, royalties and management fees carry varying withholding tax rates. Branch profits of non-resident corporations are subject to Part XIV branch tax. No Quebec-specific currency restrictions exist, but the Investment Canada Act may require notification or review for foreign-controlled acquisitions of Canadian business or incorporation in sensitive sectors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio. There is no prescribed timeline for repatriating export receipts, and a Canadian exporter may lawfully retain foreign currency in offshore bank accounts or Canadian foreign-currency accounts. Substantive compliance duties remain: accurate recognition of foreign exchange gains/losses under Income Tax Act section 39 and, where elected, functional currency reporting under Part XII.1; retention of export transaction documentation under the Customs Act and Income Tax Act; and FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for any physical movement of currency or bearer negotiable instruments exceeding CAD 10,000. In practice, a registered importer under CBSA must keep books matching Export Declarations (BS14) and customs accounting documents for at least six years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); policy and regulatory oversight exercised with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC under the Currency Act, the Bank Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, but strictly conditional. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) can support inbound low-value settlements and outbound supplier disbursements in a cost-effective manner for e-commerce treasury. However, they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot serve as the principal operating account for a Canadian corporation. Legal constraints: if the EMI onboards the Canadian entity or solicates clients in Canada, the EMI or its Canadian agent must be registered as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act once fully gazetted; EMI funds are not CDIC-insured; and the EMI must not be used for capital injections or shareholder drawdowns due to the inability to confirm true legal ownership and source of funds in a Canadian regulated capacity. It is advised to maintain Tier-1 commercial bank rails as the treasury backbone and use EMI rails only for low-risk supplier payments and currency conversion breakage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: foreign shareholder instructions a SWIFT MT103 wire to the corporation's Canadian commercial bank account, with beneficiary as the Quebec entity and beneficiary bank as a Schedule-I Canadian bank. Use SWIFT purpose code 'EQUITY' for subscription of shares (or 'DEBT' for shareholder loan); in field 70 (remittance information), reference the corporate resolution, share-subscription agreement or loan agreement, plus the CRA business number of the Canadian payee. All wires are subject to FINTRAC's bank-led electronic transaction reporting and any e-cross-border report if the shareholder physically transports currency instruments above CAD 10,000 into Canada. Banks will request a source-of-funds package before first inbound remittance: foreign bank statement, shareholder wealth substantiation, constitutional documents, and a corporate resolution. Receiving bank performs spot conversion from EUR/USD to CAD unless a USD subsidiary treasury account is pre-opened. The corporation must keep a capital contribution register and, within Quebec, file an annual updated Registry of Companies / Registraire des entreprises declaration.Outbound vendor settlements are executed customarily via SWIFT MT103 T/T transfers for open-account prepayments and import disbursements, with the Canadian bank as remitting bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, but strictly conditional. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) can support inbound low-value settlements and outbound supplier disbursements in a cost-effective manner for e-commerce treasury. However, they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot serve as the principal operating account for a Canadian corporation. Legal constraints: if the EMI onboards the Canadian entity or solicates clients in Canada, the EMI or its Canadian agent must be registered as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act once fully gazetted; EMI funds are not CDIC-insured; and the EMI must not be used for capital injections or shareholder drawdowns due to the inability to confirm true legal ownership and source of funds in a Canadian regulated capacity. It is advised to maintain Tier-1 commercial bank rails as the treasury backbone and use EMI rails only for low-risk supplier payments and currency conversion breakage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions during the taxation year triggers the requirement to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File/Master File). A Canadian Master File is separately required for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue above CAD 1 billion in the preceding taxation year.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE, but the Canada Revenue Agency applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) to disregard or recharacterize transactions lacking genuine economic substance. Quebec's Fair Taxation Act similarly authorizes reassessment of sham or artificial arrangements. Additionally, treaty abuse is countered through the anti-treaty-shopping interpretation doctrine and the OECD Pillar Two GloBE rules may apply to MNE groups above the €750 million threshold.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s.239 ITA: fine of 50%–200% of tax evaded or up to CAD 10,000, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the understated tax, plus interest and compounding penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and GST/HST under s.227.1 ITA and corresponding Quebec legislation; directors who participated in, acquiesced to, or directed the evasion may be personally liable for the full corporate tax deficiency, subject to civil asset seizure and a court-ordered travel restriction or warrant as part of criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the tax benefit (s.245 ITA) plus retroactive interest and penalties; transfer pricing adjustments under s.247(2) may also apply, imposing a penalty of 10% on the net transfer pricing adjustment if documentation is inadequate; Quebec may impose analogous penalties under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for shipping or receipt of dividends while the corporation is insolvent or where the arrangement is deemed a fraudulent conveyance; courts may pierce the corporate veil, render directors liable for the corporate tax debt, and issue a prohibition against managing a corporation under provincial Corporations legislation.
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC) Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1 million per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds (Criminal Code §462.31) can result in fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly directed, authorized, or acquiesced to violations of the PCMLTFA are personally liable for monetary penalties; in cross-border money laundering investigations, directors may face seizure of personal assets, freezing orders, and international travel bans or extradition requests where treaty obligations apply.
