Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration, Business Number (BN) registration, and GST/HST program-account (RT) administration; primary portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online. Ontario Ministry of Finance — provincial tax administration for Ontario-source filings (including the Ontario partnership information return and Employer Health Tax); portal: ontario.ca/page/taxes-rebates-credits. The 8% Ontario provincial component of HST is collected and administered by the CRA; no separate Ontario indirect-tax registration is required.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign person establishing or acquiring an interest in an Ontario limited partnership engaged in performance media buying, the statutory sequence is: (i) register the LP on the Ontario Business Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16) and appoint a general partner located or served in Ontario; (ii) apply to the CRA for the LP's 9-digit Business Number (BN) and RT (GST/HST) program account, using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or the CRA Business Registration Online service, designating the Ontario head office and a representative partner for service; (iii) if the value of the LP's worldwide taxable supplies, including those of associates, exceeds CAD 30,000 in the current or any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, GST/HST registration and remittance is mandatory from the day the threshold is exceeded; where the LP makes only zero-rated Canadian supplies (e.g., qualifying offshore media-buying services to non-residents), the LP may nonetheless elect to register voluntarily to claim input tax credits; (iv) file the applicable Ontario partnership information return with the Ontario Ministry of Finance and, where the LP has Ontario-resident or non-resident partners allocated Ontario-source income, remit Ontario provincial tax through the respective partners' returns. The BN serves as the consolidated federal tax identifier (the 'TIN'); no separate federal VAT-ID certificate is issued outside the CRA program-account confirmation.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Income Tax Act (Ontario): the partnership computes income, deductions, and credits at the partnership level, but no entity-level federal or Ontario income tax is imposed; each partner (resident or non-resident) is taxed on its allocable share of Canadian-source and Ontario-source income. For GST/HST purposes the LP itself is a 'person' under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and registers, collects, and remits HST in its own BN program account; the LP is accordingly the reporting registrant for indirect tax, while the partners bear the direct income tax. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are subject to Part I tax under the Income Tax Act on their partnership share and must file Canadian tax returns; limited partners are further constrained by the at-risk amount and limited-recourse rules (ss. 96(2.1) and 96(2.2) ITA) restricting loss allocation. — For a foreign person establishing or acquiring an interest in an Ontario limited partnership engaged in performance media buying, the statutory sequence is: (i) register the LP on the Ontario Business Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and investment transactions. Current-account receipts, including advertising, affiliate commissions, and other service income, may generally be converted and remitted without a statutory conversion deadline or mandatory surrender ratio. Capital-account transactions are also generally permitted, subject to corporate-law authority, lender or investor restrictions, sanctions, beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds review, withholding-tax obligations, partnership allocation rules, transfer-pricing requirements, and accurate tax reporting. An Ontario limited partnership should document capital contributions, partner loans, distributions, intercompany charges, and related-party settlements. Canadian non-resident withholding tax may apply to specified payments, including certain interest, royalties, management fees, and other amounts deemed to arise in Canada; the applicable treaty position and statutory rate must be confirmed before payment. A limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, but classification, filing, withholding, and reporting consequences depend on the partnership agreement, partner status, income character, and applicable federal and provincial rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to repatriate or convert foreign-currency receipts within a prescribed period. Foreign-currency export and service receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at commercially agreed rates. The partnership should retain contracts, invoices, delivery or performance evidence, payment records, bank statements, and accounting support establishing the commercial purpose and tax treatment. Large international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, including aggregated transfers within the applicable 24-hour period, may trigger reporting by the financial institution or money-services business to FINTRAC. Cross-border tax reporting, related-party documentation, GST/HST treatment, and foreign-asset reporting may apply independently of any FX conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing capital controls, mandatory export-receipt surrender, or general restrictions on conversion or repatriation. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers applicable anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing obligations for reporting entities, while the Canada Revenue Agency administers tax reporting and transfer-pricing rules.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Ontario 11.5% for general corporate income; note: LP is pass-through, partners taxed at their own rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners; for partnerships, withholding applies to the partner's share of Canadian-source income, not merely distributions)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue or CAD 10 million in cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. Partnerships must demonstrate real economic activity, including substantive operations, employees, and decision-making in Canada, to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, maximum CAD 200,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they fail to withhold and remit; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the tax avoided and penalties; may face civil restitution and travel restrictions.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations, and imprisonment for individuals up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to implement AML compliance; travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
