Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec (RQ) — provincial QST, QPIP, and provincial income tax administration. Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account; RQ 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' (CLIC); corporate/partnership registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for the NEQ.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec: (1) Apply to CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with the RC (GST/HST) program account via Form RC348 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts') or through the CRA BRO portal; (2) Register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the REQ to obtain the 10-character Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (3) Register for QST with Revenu Québec (Form LM-1-V) using the NEQ, obtaining a 15-digit QST registration number; (4) After registration, file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually and remit GST/QST returns (GST34-1); non-resident partners report Canadian-source income under ITA s. 2(3)/115. Registration is mandatory once taxable supply thresholds are exceeded; the GST/HST registration effective date is the earlier of the first taxable supply and the application date.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Quebec L.P. (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under ITA s. 96; income, gains, and losses flow through to partners pro rata. For GST/HST and QST the partnership is, however, treated as a separate 'person' registrant (ETA s. 123(1); Quebec Taxation Act). Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed only on Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(3), 115) subject to treaty relief. No Quebec tax-exempt zone or special maritime zone applies to offshore towage/salvage operations.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; classification follows the Income Tax Act and ETA. Available elections include: (1) hybrid mismatch elections under ITA ss. 18.4(3) and 18.4(4) (Forms RC605 'P' and 'Q' elections) to treat certain hybrid entities as corporations or to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, relevant to cross-border LP structures; (2) ETA s. 156 election for closely related registrants (limited application to an L.P. corporate general-partner group); (3) QST elections with Revenu Québec for associated persons and simplified accounting; (4) partnership fiscal-year elections (ITA s. 249.1) and CCA allocation elections in prescribed form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15.47%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital and financial services) in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)); the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST registration. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold; registration is mandatory once exceeded and voluntary below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: (1) Part V, s. 1 — supplies of goods exported by the supplier, with prescribed evidence of exportation; (2) Part VII, s. 7(1) — supplies of transportation services (including freight towage) in respect of the movement of goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada; (3) Part V, s. 7 — supplies of services made to a non-resident client where the service is rendered wholly outside Canada or, with exceptions, where the performance is in Canada but the service is supplied to a non-resident and not in connection with taxable Canadian property. For offshore towage/salvage, services rendered on the high seas to non-resident shipowners or in support of international commercial shipping qualify for 0% GST and 0% QST; services performed within Canadian waters for Canadian-resident principals are taxable at the 5% GST + 9.975% QST rate unless a specific international-transportation exemption applies. The registrant must retain contemporaneous evidence to substantiate the non-resident destination and beneficial recipient.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CAERS/CAED) — Form B13A or electronic export reporting filed with the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing the international movement or destination of the towage/salvage operation
  • Signed towage/salvage contract (e.g., Lloyd's Open Form or BIMCO towage agreement) identifying the non-resident principal and scope
  • Commercial invoice and GST/QST invoice showing 0% tax applied and the supplier's BN/QST registration numbers
  • Foreign exchange remittance certificate or bank wire-transfer statement evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident client
  • CRA Form GST11 (Certification of Export) or equivalent documentary evidence of exportation, where goods are moved as part of the supply

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Invoicing must satisfy ETA s. 169(4) and s. 3 of the Information Returns Regulations (supplier name, BN/QST registration number, date, description, and tax particulars). Electronic invoices are accepted; the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing network is being adopted voluntarily for B2B/B2G. Quebec requires QST returns to be filed electronically through Revenu Québec's EDT system, but no pre-clearance of individual invoices is mandated.

CRA and Revenu Québec generally reassess within 4 years of the filing due date, with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or gross negligence (ETA s. 296). Clawback: zero-rating is reversed and the taxpayer is assessed for unremitted GST/QST plus prescribed daily compound interest if evidence of export or non-resident status is absent or deficient. Penalties include: ETA s. 285(1) gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of net tax understated; s. 280 failure-to-file penalties; and criminal prosecution under s. 327 — summary conviction fines up to CAD 250,000 and 2 years' imprisonment, or indictment fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and 5 years' imprisonment. Quebec mirrors these sanctions under the Quebec Taxation Act and Tax Administration Act, with material penalty exposure for false or omitted statements connected to zero-rated marine claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no exchange-control legislation; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed and no approval is required for inbound capital, outbound dividends, loan repayments, or profit remittances. For a Quebec Limited Partnership paying profits to non-resident partners, the principal constraint is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and corresponding Quebec provincial withholdings, not currency convertibility. Escrowed salvage remuneration and Lloyd's Open Form awards may be paid in USD or CAD and converted freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds in Canada. A Quebec LP may retain offshore service revenue in foreign currency, maintain foreign-currency bank accounts domestically, or convert proceeds to CAD through licensed financial institutions. The only regulatory obligations are the standard FINTRAC anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds and tax-reporting/transfer-pricing documentation obligations under the Income Tax Act for cross-border transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign exchange and banking-system integrity functions are supplemented by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec-level supervision is exercised through the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for financial intermediaries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for settlement-only payment collection and low as a primary liquidity or escrow treasury vehicle. Offshore digital EMIs such as electronic-money institutions based in the UK/EEA do not hold Canadian banking licences under the Bank Act (Canada); they cannot take deposits, originate credit, or provide a Lloyd's-standard-form escrow security that Canadian maritime counterparties will accept. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada) fully applies, as registered payment service providers with the Bank of Canada. For the Quebec LP, an offshore EMI may be used for low-value cross-border collections, but not for LOF security deposits or capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident limited partners must arrive to the Quebec LP via SWIFT MT103 from an owned-account in a recognised jurisdiction. The remittance field must use a clear purpose code, notably /UGT/ or /GIFT/ where appropriate, or the bank’s own business-purpose description, with reference to the LP’s Quebec registration number (NEQ). For example: Field 70: /PURP/TRADE; /LPIS/CA_LP; 'Capital contribution to Quebec LP for offshore towage and marine salvage operation'. The beneficiary must match the exact account name at the Canadian bank; funds may be held temporarily in a pre-funding account before transfer into the operating account. Verification includes confirming the sender’s bank is not subject to a Canadian sanctions measure and that the ultimate beneficial owners match the KYC-approved list.Trade and operational disbursements should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier, subcontractor, bunker, and port payments; SWIFT MT202 Cover is reserved for bank-to-bank interbank positioning where required.
