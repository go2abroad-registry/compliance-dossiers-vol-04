Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Quebec, administered by the Quebec Ministry of Employment and Social Solidarity

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Quebec), CQLR c. S-31.1, principally sections 3-10, 18-20, 30-35, 102-107, and 108-113; the Civil Code of Quebec also governs registered-office, mandate, contract, and recordkeeping matters. A federal alternative is the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for constitution of a Quebec business corporation and accompanying constituting act filed through the Registraire des entreprises online service or prescribed paper form. The corporation must also file its initial declaration within the statutory period.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 100 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Quebec has no statutory minimum issued or paid-up share capital and no mandatory escrow account. Shares may be issued for consideration permitted by the Business Corporations Act, including money, property, or services already performed, subject to directors' valuation and solvency requirements. The corporation must maintain adequate capital for its contracts, vessels, insurance, and maritime regulatory obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, occupancy, fire-prevention, and emergency-access approvals; complete structural, dock, lifting-equipment, electrical, fuel-storage, and load inspections as applicable. Vessel safety, firefighting, lifesaving, pollution-response, and crew certification requirements must be satisfied before commercial operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec provincial tax administration. Registration portals: CRA Business Registration Online (via CRA My Business Account) and Revenu Québec's Registre des entreprises, CLIDE (Client Identification Database).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtaining a statutory tax identifier must apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and to Revenu Québec for a Quebec Sales Tax (QST) number, unless the entity is a small supplier under the Excise Tax Act or the Quebec Taxation Act. Non-resident corporations may apply using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with supporting corporate documents; for QST, foreign entities use the Revenu Québec 'Registration for GST/HST and QST' process, designating a representative resident in Canada if required. The BN is issued for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts; the QST number is assigned concurrently for provincial tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity obtaining a statutory tax identifier must apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and to Revenu Québec for a Quebec Sales Tax (QST) number, unless the entity is a small supplier under the Excise Tax Act or the Quebec Taxation Act. Non-resident corporations may apply using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with supporting corporate documents; for QST, foreign entities use the Revenu Québec 'Registration for GST/HST and QST' process, designating a representative resident in Canada if required. The BN is issued for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts; the QST number is assigned concurrently for provincial tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a US-style 'check-the-box' election. A corporation is defaultly taxed as a separate entity. Available mechanisms include: (1) section 216 election for non-residents receiving Canadian rental or timber royalties; (2) section 219 election for non-resident corporations with taxable Canadian business income; (3) joint election for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act (e.g., closely related corporations election); (4) Quebec-specific election under the Taxation Act for amalgamation or winding-up. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Canadian Income Tax Act impose adjustments rather than elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide annual taxable supplies threshold for both GST/HST and QST; if exceeded, mandatory registration. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada or Quebec may be required to register even below this threshold based on nexus and permanent establishment criteria. Maritime towage and salvage services are generally subject to the same threshold unless zero-rated).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies charged at 0% GST/HST and generally relieved from QST when: (a) goods are exported from Canada as part of a continuous export transaction; (b) services are supplied to a non-resident and the services are not connected to real or tangible personal property situated in Canada and are not for consumption in Canada; (c) services are in respect of a conveyance or vessel used primarily in international commercial transportation (including deep-sea towage and salvage operations where the vessel is engaged in international voyages); and (d) supply is made to a non-resident in respect of a passenger or cargo journey to or from Canada. Specific zero-rating provisions for maritime salvage and towage services are set out in Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding Quebec regulation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or equivalent, with Canadian Automated Export Declaration filing)
  • Bill of Lading (including sea waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange repatriation documentation evidencing actual receipt of foreign currency
  • Towage or salvage contract detailing the international voyage, vessel registry, and points of departure/arrival

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing clearance mandate exists federally or in Quebec. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's e-services (GST/QST Internet filing) when taxable turnover exceeds CAD 1,500,000 or as requested; invoices must meet prescribed content requirements under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec's Taxation Act. No API-based fiscal stamping or government clearance of invoices is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit zero-rated export claims; if export status is false or unsupported, the supplier faces reassessment of GST/HST and QST at the applicable rates, plus interest and penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (penalty up to 25% of the amount assessed) and section 1048 of the Quebec Taxation Act. For deliberate fraud or false statements, criminal penalties may apply, including fines and imprisonment; Revenu Québec can also revoke registration and impose penal provisions under the Tax Administration Act. Retrospective clawback periods are generally four years, extended to eight years or indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with a fully free-floating Canadian dollar and no exchange-control regime on current-account or capital-account transactions. Foreign investment into a Quebec business corporation (incorporation, equity subscription, shareholder loans) is unrestricted, subject to information-and-notification thresholds under the Investment Canada Act for acquisitions of a Canadian business. Profit remittance — dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees — may be freely paid without prior Bank of Canada consent, subject only to combined federal–Quebec withholding tax (generally 25% where no treaty applies; 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for qualifying 10% corporate shareholders under the Canada–US Treaty) and to Revenu Québec/CRA filing obligations (NR4 and RL-3 returns). No sectoral FX licence, local-repatriation quota, or maritime-related convertibility restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in Canada. A Quebec towage/salvage corporation is under no statutory obligation to surrender, mandatorily repatriate, or convert export or cross-border service proceeds into Canadian dollars. Receivables may be retained in USD or EUR in Canadian bank foreign-currency accounts or in foreign accounts; however, all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting, cross-border movement of monetary instruments over CAD 10,000 must be declared, and specified foreign property (including foreign bank accounts) with an aggregate cost amount exceeding CAD 100,000 requires annual reporting on CRA Form T1135. Arm's-length documentation must accompany intercompany billing for towage and salvage services to sustain transfer-pricing scrutiny by the CRA and Revenu Québec.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) holds monetary authority and sole issuance of the Canadian dollar; prudential supervision of federally chartered Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates Quebec financial-services businesses; and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, with material legal constraints. Non-bank digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be registered as money-services businesses with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, licenced by the AMF; they offer multi-currency virtual accounts and cheaper SWIFT disbursement. However, they are not Schedule I banks, are not members of Payments Canada's Lynx high-value settlement system, cannot provide overdraft or committed credit, and are excluded from holding client deposits in the regulated sense. For an ISIC 5222 offshore towage and salvage contractor, EMIs are acceptable only for low-value operational disbursements (harbour dues, survey fees, crew amenities); all Lloyd's Open Form salvage awards, towage hire, and tug-purchase escrow funds must be cleared through a tier-1 Schedule I institution to avoid settlement and reputational risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions from a foreign parent into the Quebec business corporation are cleared as SWIFT MT103 customer transfers (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD), with the purpose-code narrative citing the share-subscription agreement; CAD transfers settle via Payments Canada's Lynx high-value RTGS (successor to LVTS), with Payments Canada Real-Time Rail for low-value instant payments where available. The receiving bank requires a board resolution authorizing the share issuance, the executed subscription agreement, a certified copy of amended federal or Quebec articles reflecting any class creation and allotment, a private-placement availability memorandum (accredited-investor exemption under National Instrument 45-106), and a source-of-funds declaration from the remitter. Funds must credit an account held strictly in the corporation's legal name; no third-party collection account or nominal account is permissible.Outbound treasury payments for offshore towage and salvage are routed through (i) SWIFT MT103 wires for towage hire, port-disbursement-account top-ups, bunker supply, crew payroll, casualty communications, and Lloyd's award payments; (ii) UCP 600 documentary letters of credit for hull repairs, dry-docking, and the purchase-acquisition escrow of tugs or salvage equipment; and (iii) Payments Canada direct-debit and wire rails for domestic counterparties. For LOF 2020 proceedings, salvage remuneration and security are settled through the Lloyd's Salvage Arbitration Branch (LSAB) guarantee/escrow mechanism, with US-dollar disbursements streamed through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent to avoid double-conversion cost. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 250,000 for intangibles (annual aggregate with each non-resident).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; no specific ESR but substance is critical for transfer pricing and treaty relief. Offshore towage and salvage operations must demonstrate real business activity, personnel, and decision-making in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, fines up to CAD 25,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate tax debts and penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to evasion; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, penalties up to 50% of additional tax, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax liabilities if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million per violation, imprisonment up to 5 years, and revocation of licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML fines and penalties if they failed to implement adequate compliance programs; travel bans and personal asset seizure possible.
