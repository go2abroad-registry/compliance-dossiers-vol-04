Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual-office mailbox arrangement is not permissible for a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The registered office (Sitz) must correspond to the place of actual administration within Switzerland (Art. 56 of the Swiss Civil Code ZGB and Art. 626 para. 2 OR). For an ISIC 5820 hyper-casual games company operating a remote digital model, a credible physical seat in Switzerland—or a licensed domiciliation provider in the target canton providing a full registered-office service (records, registered mail, corporate minutes, and board facilities)—is mandatory. Domiciliation arrangements are accepted only where actual administrative substance, board decision-making, and accounting records are demonstrably located in Switzerland; otherwise the entity risks reclassification as a mere mailbox company by the tax administration and the commercial register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized public deed of incorporation and Articles of Association (Statuten) together with the formal Application for Entry in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), filed electronically by the Swiss civil-law notary public.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Swiss AG minimum statutory share capital is CHF 100,000. At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal value per share (i.e. CHF 20,000 on a CHF 100,000 capital) must be actually paid up and deposited in a blocked/escrow Swiss bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the formation company; the bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) must be produced to the Commercial Register and the funds may be released only upon definitive registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory formation audit report; the unpaid balance is subject to a board call for payment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance certificate issued by the cantonal fire inspection authority applicable to the leased office premises; structural load and evacuation capacity confirmed by the landlord's structural certificate for the designated office use; occupational safety and workplace clearance effected through Suva (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund) or an approved insurer, including ergonomic workstations, first-aid facilities, and legally compliant emergency exits.

Paid-in capital rule: Swiss AG minimum statutory share capital is CHF 100,000. At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal value per share (i.e. CHF 20,000 on a CHF 100,000 capital) must be actually paid up and deposited in a blocked/escrow Swiss bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the formation company; the bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) must be produced to the Commercial Register and the funds may be released only upon definitive registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory formation audit report; the unpaid balance is subject to a board call for payment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Authority: Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), Schwarztorstrasse 50, Berne; VAT registration and e-services portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch / Swiss VAT e-service 'SuisseTax' (E-Services de l'AFC). Cantonal tax authorities (Zug Tax Administration for the Zug Canton setting) handle cantonal/communal corporate income and capital taxes; the VAT is a federal indirect tax administered solely by the ESTV.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft via notarial deed, registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) — the AG obtains the Swiss Business Identifier (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer UID / IDE / IDI, 9 digits), which is the statutory company tax ID for cantonal and federal income-tax purposes. Step 2: For VAT, apply online on the ESTV VAT e-portal using the UID; the ESTV issues a VAT number comprising the UID plus the MWST/IVA/TVA suffix and a validation code. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Resident Aktiengesellschaft is a separate taxable person subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax at 8.5% on net profit (effective federal rate after deduction of ordinary taxes: 7.83% due to federal tax being deductible as business expense; average combined federal/cantonal/communal effective rate in Zug about 14.5%). Modified Territorial_Scope: Swiss-resident AGs are taxed on worldwide income, but profits of foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt under the applicable double-taxation treaty and the 'object exemption' (Objektbefreiung). No pass-through or fiscally transparent treatment applies; the AG is a per-se corporation with no partnership option. Capital tax imposed at cantonal level. Not a Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the AG is per se corporate. Statutory elections relevant to this digital-publishing AG: (1) Participation relief election (capital gains/dividend exemption) for qualifying shareholdings ≥10% — reduces effective rate on investment income; (2) Patent-box election and R&D super-deduction (150%) at cantonal level (Zug) via tax ruling; (3) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election for cantonal tax purposes of qualifying finance functions; (4) VAT group taxation election under Art. 20 VAT Act for Swiss-consolidated entities; (5) Small-business exemption election: with worldwide turnover ≤CHF 250,000 and Swiss-taxable turnover ≤CHF 100,000, the AG may elect exemption from VAT even when the general threshold is exceeded solely through foreign B2B services (Art. 10 para 2 VAT Act, 2025 revision); (6) Flat-rate 'Saldo' simplified settlement procedure election under Art. 37 VAT Act (up to CHF 250,000 turnover). Hybrid mismatch elections: none available; Switzerland applies OECD BEPS Action 2-type rules and denies deductions for hybrid mismatches as of 2020 Finance and Tax Amendment Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (including services to foreign recipients) triggers mandatory VAT registration; from 1 January 2025, small businesses with world-wide turnover ≤ CHF 250,000 and Swiss-taxable turnover ≤ CHF 100,000 may remain exempt (for export-oriented service providers such as hyper-casual game publishers monetized through foreign ad networks).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods export zero-rating: the 0% rate applies if physical goods leave Switzerland and the exporter substantiates export via the Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhrnachweis, art. 53 VAT Act). Offshore services (the dominant revenue stream: ad-network payouts from Google Ireland / AppLovin US, app-store royalties): Swiss VAT does not apply because the place of supply is outside Switzerland under Art. 21 para 2 VAT Act (B2B services supplied to a recipient located abroad); the AG must not charge Swiss VAT and must substantiate the customer's foreign place of business. The AG's input VAT on Swiss purchasing (development, cloud hosting, legal, marketing) is creditable only to the extent supplies are subject to Swiss VAT; mixed foreign/Swiss turnover requires an apportionment key (direct allocation and pro-rata) in the Swiss VAT periods. Refund of foreign VAT (e.g., EU VAT on AdMob commissions) cannot be claimed in the Swiss VAT return; Swiss input VAT refunds are paid quarterly by the ESTV.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Ausfuhrzollanmeldung (only for physical exports; digital game distribution does not trigger Swiss customs clearance)
  • Place-of-Supply Evidence Bundle for digital services: (i) written contracts with each ad-network (Google AdMob/AdSense, AppLovin/IronSource) stating recipient establishment in Ireland/USA; (ii) invoices listing the AG's UID-MWST number, counterparty's foreign VAT/GST ID and the service description; (iii) bank transfer statements / SWIFT MT103 inward remittance certificates evidencing cross-border settlement; (iv) customer business-status declarations (Bescheinigung über die Unternehmereigenschaft) or official extract from Irish/US tax registers confirming corporate status; (v) per-period 'list of services supplied abroad' submission for the Swiss VAT return.
  • Bill of Lading / Airwaybill for goods export scenarios
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — mirror of bank records confirming FX repatriation from the ad-network counterparty

No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control (CTC), no digital stamping and no mandatory e-invoicing clearance API in Switzerland. Invoices may be issued on paper or as structured e-invoices (eBill / QR-bill / EPUB; XRechnung not required). Mandatory invoice content under Art. 26 VAT Act: seller identity/UID-MWST number, buyer identity, description of service, consideration and applicable tax rate. For imports and exports, electronic customs declarations via the customs platform (e-dec / CargoTIS / transit TZS) are mandatory for goods; VAT on imported services falls under the reverse-charge mechanism of the recipient (Art. 45 para 3 lit. c VAT Act) with self-assessment in the periodic VAT return, eliminating the need for fiscal representation. The AG must maintain accounting records and e-audit accessible data; retention period 10 years under the Code of Obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both the capital and current account. The CHF is freely convertible and there are no general exchange-control restrictions on cross-border remittances, profit repatriation, or foreign shareholding. However, certain financial reporting duties apply to payments to and from persons domiciled abroad, primarily for balance-of-payments statistics; these do not constitute approvals or transfers of control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export receipts in CHF or any foreign currency. Swiss exporters and holding companies may retain foreign currency on ledger, convert at freely traded market rates, or deploy funds into approved treasury instruments; only standard AML, tax-declaration, and statistical-reporting obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Direction générale de la Banque nationale suisse, administered operationally with the Swiss Federal Department of Finance; no separate foreign-exchange-control administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for principal bank-account use; MODERATE only as an auxiliary payout rail. Switzerland is not an EEA / EU member, and the EU EMI passport does not extend to Swiss territory. An offshore digital EMI, unless separately licensed by FINMA under the Swiss Banking Act (including the fintech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act) or acting through a Swiss-licensed partner bank, cannot lawfully hold client funds or operate a comprehensive Swiss clearing function. Offshore EMIs may be tolerated only for non-core transaction routing, subject to the Swiss company's own due diligence and strict segregation of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing uses SWIFT MT103 wire to the Swiss AG's CHF IBAN, preceded by a valid board resolution and shareholder capital payment mandate. For EUR inflows, use SEPA CT or TARGET2 settlement with IBAN referencing 'QR no purpose code' at some banks; for CHF, SNB payment system SIC is automatically used. The required purpose narrative must include 'Paid-in capital', 'Equity contribution', or 'Capital reserve payment' to distinguish from ordinary trade or loan funds. Verification requires the bank to confirm source-of-funds through investor's KYC file, a source-of-wealth declaration if the shareholder is an individual, and legal proof that the controlling shareholder is authorised to make the payment. No exchange-control approval is needed in Switzerland.For vendor and contractor payments, Swiss corporates predominantly use SWIFT MT103 in EUR, USD, or CHF with real-time gross settlement (RTGS) via SIX xclear for domestic CHF; SEPA CT for EU EUR payees; and correspondent-bank-cleared MT103 or MT202 COV for non-Swift periphery banks. Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 are permissible for high-value procurement of hardware, SDK licensing, and media buying, particularly where Asian or Eastern-European payee banks demand documentary protection. Trade disbursements must observe the ORDO, FATF, and Swiss tax-at-source rules on royalties; no treasury repurchase or surrender obligation applies.Outright Vanilla FX Forwards (deliverable) for EUR/USD/CHF and all fully convertible currency pairs, entered under ISDA/FXA documentation with FINMA-regulated or equivalent first-class banks.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for calendar-pegged or restricted-deliverability currencies (e.g., CNH, INR, KRW, BRL) where the underlying exposure is non-CHF revenue/street revenue from foreign app stores, with settlement in USD or EUR.Cross-Currency Swaps (basis swaps) for matching medium-term balance-sheet assets and liabilities in foreign currency.FX Options (vanilla put/call) used as stand-alone hedging instruments or within zero-cost straddles, subject to the bank's derivative-risk disclosure policy and the company's IFRS hedge-accounting election.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for principal bank-account use; MODERATE only as an auxiliary payout rail. Switzerland is not an EEA / EU member, and the EU EMI passport does not extend to Swiss territory. An offshore digital EMI, unless separately licensed by FINMA under the Swiss Banking Act (including the fintech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act) or acting through a Swiss-licensed partner bank, cannot lawfully hold client funds or operate a comprehensive Swiss clearing function. Offshore EMIs may be tolerated only for non-core transaction routing, subject to the Swiss company's own due diligence and strict segregation of funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal flat corporate income tax plus cantonal and communal income taxes; combined effective headline rate approx. 14.6% in Canton Zug and approx. 21.1% in City of Zurich for a resident AG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% final Swiss withholding tax imposed on gross Swiss-source dividends, payable regardless of beneficiary residence, with reductions only under an applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after the legal fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year AG the statutory filing date is 30 June of the following year, with extensions routinely granted to 31 December upon written request to the Zug Tax Office.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 900,000,000 group consolidated revenue triggers country-by-country reporting; no single statutory de minimis threshold for Master File/Local File is codified by ESTV, but Swiss TP documentation is required for all cross-border related-party transactions exceeding CHF 1,000,000 in services and CHF 5,000,000 in goods to satisfy contemporaneous documentation standards.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation statute, but the OECD-conformed treaty abuse doctrine and the MLI Principal Purpose Test operate as a functional ESR: a Swiss AG must show actual entrepreneurial substance, active decision-making, qualified personnel, real offices, and assumption of operating/financial IP risk. Shell holding or IP-box companies without substance are denied treaty benefits, re-assessed under CFC rules, and exposed to mandatory arbitration and anti-abuse penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax plus default interest; criminal tax fraud (Steuerbetrug) subjects the taxpayer and responsible officers to fines up to CHF 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years under Swiss Federal Direct Tax Law Art. 186 and Swiss Criminal Code forgery provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, CEOs, and CFOs can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall when responsible for false declarations; they face personal criminal fines, disqualification from board positions during proceedings, asset seizure, foreign cooperation-based travel restrictions, and actual imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Empty Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, reclassification of royalties as disguised shareholder distributions subject to 35% withholding tax, full taxation in Switzerland plus 5% late interest, and administrative penalty of up to 3 times the adjusted tax; substance reassessment may also trigger profit reallocation to the foreign beneficiary.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a shell for treaty-shopping are personally liable for the incorrectly retained dividend WHT, can be sued for restitution by the tax administration, placed under a travel ban pending criminal investigation, and sentenced to imprisonment of up to 5 years under Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis for complicity in disguised transfers.
AML / Money Laundering Through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the GwG, non-compliance can trigger supervisory fines of up to CHF 500,000 and confiscation of funds; intentional money laundering under Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis carries imprisonment of up to 5 years or a monetary penalty, with criminal forfeiture of all proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal prosecution and imprisonment up to 5 years if they knowingly participated in laundering ad-network proceeds; travel bans, pre-trial asset freezes, and civil restitution orders are routinely imposed against the board; compliance officers and finance managers are equally exposed under Art. 29(g) GwG.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / IP Under-Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax authorities may upwardly adjust taxable income to the arm's-length result, disallow excessive expense deductions, impose default interest of 4% to 8% annually, and add a penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax; complete re-qualification of payments as constructive dividends triggers 35% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the 35% withholding tax uncollected where they approved constructive dividends; criminal transfer pricing misrepresentation can lead to personal monetary fines, seizure of Swiss bank assets, mutual assistance travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 3 years under Swiss tax fraud provisions.
