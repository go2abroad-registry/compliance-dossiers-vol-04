Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal); Revenu Quebec / Agence du revenu du Quebec (provincial). Government portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (canada.ca); Revenu Quebec Clic ReQ business portal (revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

The foreign Quebec limited partnership (societe en commandite) registers in three steps: (1) obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) via the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec; (2) register with CRA for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, including import/export (RM) and payroll (RP) accounts if engaged in physical vessel trading operations; (3) register separately with Revenu Quebec for QST and provincial payroll accounts (Form LM-101-V or Clic ReQ). A non-resident LP is required to appoint a Canadian representative for service (Excise Tax Act s. 273) and may be required to post security in the amount of anticipated GST/QST before accounts are activated. The LP must file a T5013 partnership information return annually even though it is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The foreign Quebec limited partnership (societe en commandite) registers in three steps: (1) obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) via the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec; (2) register with CRA for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, including import/export (RM) and payroll (RP) accounts if engaged in physical vessel trading operations; (3) register separately with Revenu Quebec for QST and provincial payroll accounts (Form LM-101-V or Clic ReQ). A non-resident LP is required to appoint a Canadian representative for service (Excise Tax Act s. 273) and may be required to post security in the amount of anticipated GST/QST before accounts are activated. The LP must file a T5013 partnership information return annually even though it is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime for domestic partnerships: the Quebec LP is statutorily flow-through under Income Tax Act s. 96, with income/loss allocated to partners each fiscal period. Relevant elections include: (i) GST/HST section 156 election among closely related registrants to treat qualifying supplies as nil; (ii) QST mirror elections under the Quebec Taxation Act; (iii) partnership fiscal-period elections where all partners are corporations (ITA s. 249.1); and (iv) for U.S. tax classification of the Canadian LP, an IRS Form 8832 entity classification election (check-the-box), subject to the per se corporation rules and the presence of at least one unlimited-liability general partner. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by Canada's anti-hybrid and general anti-avoidance (s. 245) provisions rather than by an elective hybrid-regime mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies made in Canada (GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold). A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada, or regularly making taxable supplies in the course of a Canadian commercial activity, is nevertheless a registrant and must register regardless of the 30,000 threshold (ETA s. 240; Quebec mirror). The threshold is measured over the current and preceding four calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar by ocean vessel is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (zero-rated exports) and the corresponding QST rule where the sugar is delivered to a non-resident purchaser, loaded on an ocean vessel in Canada, and exported without consumption or use in Canada. The supplier must hold contemporaneous evidence that the sales contract (Incoterms) places export responsibility on the LP, that the vessel/B/L confirms a foreign destination, and that the sugar leaves Canada in the ordinary course of trade. Zero-rating entitles the LP to reclaim all GST/QST input tax credits on export costs (port handling, stevedoring, freight, insurance, documentation). Any goods brought temporarily into Canada under duty-deferral or bonded-warehouse arrangements and later exported retain zero-rated treatment, provided the CBSA export entries are matched to the import entries.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA export report filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System / CARM portal, including cargo control number and electronic export entry
  • Clean on-board ocean Bill of Lading - port-to-port B/L naming the non-resident buyer/consignee and the foreign discharge port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank Swift MT103, LC negotiation statement or FX advice evidencing remittance and settlement of the full export sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and signed VHP raw cane sugar commodity confirmation (ISIC 4631) with Incoterms, vessel nomination, quantity, polarization grading and price
  • Certificate of origin and laboratory certificate of analysis (polarization) for raw centrifugal cane sugar
  • Shipping attestation package - mate's receipt, cargo manifest, weight certificate and cargo insurance evidencing transshipment out of Canada

Canada does not operate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital authorization for VAT. However, the LP must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed GST/HST/QST information (ETA s. 221, Quebec mirror), keep books and records in Canada or accessible electronically for six years (ETA s. 286), and report export shipments electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System / CARM, including cargo control and e-manifest data. Optional EDI/PEPPOL formats are accepted, but no government clearance platform stamps or authorizes B2B invoices.

CRA and Revenu Quebec conduct targeted substantive audits of commodity-export zero-rating. A failure to maintain and produce the complete export documentation bundle results in: (i) retrospective reassessment of the full GST/QST (14.975%) on export sales, with daily compound interest; (ii) disallowance and clawback of all input tax credits associated with the zero-rated export chain; (iii) penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act, including false-statement penalties of CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 and culpable-conduct penalties up to 25% of the net tax avoided; (iv) an extended four-to-eight-year reassessment window in cases of negligence or misrepresentation; and (v) criminal liability (fines and/or imprisonment) for willful evasion of GST/QST. The taxpayer bears the legal burden of proving export eligibility, and the CRA may additionally invoke the general anti-avoidance rule where artificial vessel-trade financing or routing arrangements are detected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar is fully convertible with no restrictions on current or capital account transactions; profit remittance, dividend distributions, and partnership withdrawals are freely transferable subject to corporate income tax, non-resident withholding tax on allocable income, and FATCA/CRS reporting; no prior approval required for cross-border wires

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Canada; export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, and no timeline or conversion ratio applies to Canadian entities, including Quebec limited partnerships

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is decentralized under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, with no dedicated exchange control agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) providers are not chartered banks in Canada; they generally require registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and may need a provincial license, and clearing through domestic Canadian rails is restricted unless the EMI establishes a Canadian subsidiary or partners with a Schedule I bank

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a formal two-tier Local File / Master File threshold; however, CRA's contemporaneous documentation guidelines require a complete transfer-pricing file (Local File equivalent) when non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate for goods and services in a taxation year. A Master File is expected from multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 1,000,000,000 or more, and CbC reporting is mandatory where consolidated revenue exceeds the OECD €750 million threshold (CAD equivalent). In practice, CRA will accept the OECD Action 13 transfer-pricing documentation package as best practice for all audit contexts.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR). Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and Quebec's comparable anti-avoidance rule (section 1079.1.11 of the Taxation Act) disregard artificial transactions lacking genuine economic substance and business purpose. Treaty relief is also denied to shell/conduit entities under the beneficial ownership doctrine and the OECD BEPS Action 6 guidance. The LP's sugar cargo arbitrage must be supported by local commercial decisions, actual contracts, banking infrastructure, and decision-making functions to withstand audit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under subsections 239(1) and 239(1.1) of the Income Tax Act, wilful tax evasion is an indictable offence carrying a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 2 years. Under the Quebec Taxation Act, civil penalties may be imposed at 50% of the tax avoided, and criminal prosecution may result in fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for the partnership's unpaid taxes if they fail to use due diligence under subsection 227.1 (withholding amounts); travel bans can be issued under the Seized Property Act or the Income Tax Act's departure block; directors can also be held jointly and severally liable under section 160 for transfers at undervalue and may be extradited for tax fraud.
Shell Company / Artificial Transaction

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under section 245(2) removes any tax benefit from the transaction and can result in an increase of tax, plus a 50% gross-negligence penalty under section 163.2 if grossly negligent statements are furnished; Revenu Québec may impose a 50% penalty on the amount of tax avoided and order confiscation of assets acquired through the sham.

Director personal liability: The courts may pierce the partnership's corporate veil where the LP is a facade; directors then become personally civilly liable for the full amount of taxes plus interest and penalties, may have their passports cancelled or a travel ban issued under the Tax Administration Act, and face imprisonment for counselling fraudulent transactions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / FINTRAC Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 for a first violation and CAD 2,000,000 for a subsequent violation may be levied; criminal charges can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years for an individual director who knowingly engages in or launders proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are subject to unequivocal personal liability; FINTRAC may name them in enforcement notices, and a court can order payment of penalties from personal assets, impose a travel ban to prevent departure during investigation, and issue a restriction on fiduciary activity for a period not exceeding 10 years.
Misreporting of Non-Resident Withholding Obligations

Statutory sanction: Subsection 227(8) imposes a 10% penalty on the amount withheld short plus 7% annual interest (compounded daily); failure to remit is subject to a further 20% penalty on the remittance shortfall, and the CRA may recover the unpaid withholding from the partnership's assets and ultimately pursue the non-resident beneficiary directly.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are deemed trustees for withheld amounts under subsection 227.1; without due diligence, directors may be assessed for the full unremitted withholding taxes, and the CRA can seize bank accounts, freeze assets, and initiate bankruptcy proceedings against the individuals.
