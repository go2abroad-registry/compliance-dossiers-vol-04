Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — federal Business Number/GST/HST registration, filing, and collection; portal: Business Registration Online and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/account.html). Quebec-specific: Revenu Québec — QST, employer payroll contributions, and sectoral tax credits; portal: Mon Dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign-incorporated entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The BN is the statutory TIN for direct tax and is the parent number for program accounts: GST/HST (14-character number, e.g., RT000000001), payroll deductions (RP), and import/export (RM). The application requires certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign incorporation, board authorization appointing a Canadian representative or agent, and a Canadian service/mailing address. After the BN is granted, the same entity must register with Revenu Québec for QST using Form LM-1 or the online QST registration service, and must obtain a Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) establishment number whenever it maintains an establishment in Quebec. Registration obligations are mandatory before taxable activities begin unless the small-supplier threshold applies; a non-resident with a Quebec permanent establishment cannot claim the small-supplier threshold for QST if its gross revenue from that establishment exceeds CAD $30,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal/provincial business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate legal taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act; there is no pass-through or check-the-box classification in Canada. Profits, losses, assets, and liabilities are taxed at the corporate level, with integration achieved through eligible dividend rules. A US/foreign LLC or similar hybrid entity is not recognized as a Canadian corporation unless incorporated in Canada; foreign limited-liability status is assessed under CRA's foreign entity classification rules.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections include: (1) allocation of taxable income among associated corporations under ITA s.256; (2) non-resident's s.216 election to compute tax on rental/royalty income on a net basis; (3) rollover elections under ITA ss.85, 86, and 88 for incorporation, reorganization, and amalgamation; (4) GST/HST nil-consideration relief elections between closely related corporations under ETA ss.156-157 and the corresponding QST election; (5) deemed dividend and surplus elections for transactions with foreign affiliates; and (6) hybrid mismatch rules in Part XVI.1 of the ITA operate as anti-abuse disgorgements, not elective regimes. Foreign entity classification is determined by legal form and CRA administrative rules; there is no elective check-the-box mechanism for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST and QST, the general small-supplier threshold is CAD $30,000 of total worldwide taxable revenues from taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (increasing to CAD $50,000 for charities). A non-resident carrying on a taxable business in Canada, including through a Quebec permanent establishment, is required to register regardless of Canadian revenues if the permanent establishment generates taxable sales; remote sellers and digital marketplace operators must register once Canadian taxable sales exceed CAD $30,000 on a 12-month basis. In Quebec, the same CAD $30,000 threshold triggers QST enrollment for non-residents, except that a permanent establishment in Quebec may create a mandatory registration even below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated under GST/HST Schedule VI Part V and corresponding QST rules when the supplier exports the goods, transfers them to an authorized carrier/consignee for export, or exports them within prescribed time limits, and retains documentary proof of export. Regulatory consulting services for FDA 510(k) filings and CE marking supplied by a Quebec corporation to a foreign client are zero-rated as taxable supplies made to a non-resident where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not received in Canada (not in respect of real property located in Canada, not performed for goods situated in Canada, and not an exempt supply). If the consultant conducts inspections or work physically in Quebec, the portion performed inside Canada may be taxable unless the supply is otherwise zero-rated as an export service in respect of software/intellectual property located outside Canada or in respect of the client's foreign business. Services supplied entirely to a non-resident for use outside Canada and not connected to Canadian real or tangible property should be zero-rated, allowing input tax credit recovery on related expenses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A, CAED, or CBSA-compliant customs export record) for physical goods; for services-only contracts, a service export declaration/attestation setting out the offshore nature of the work
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or international carrier receipt showing the goods left Canada; for services, signed delivery/service completion certificate with the foreign client's foreign address
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/MT103 credit advice, or bank statement evidencing foreign currency proceeds received into a Canadian financial institution
  • Executed client agreement identifying the foreign purchaser, jurisdiction, regulatory scope (FDA/CE), and place of use or benefit
  • Non-resident status confirmation obtained from the client (e.g., client's foreign TIN/VAT number or certificate of registration in the destination country) and, where applicable, overseas customs entry or regulatory submission reference

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or integrated e-invoice validation exists for GST/QST in Canada. Quebec operates a self-assessment VAT system: a supplier must issue an invoice in paper or electronic format containing prescribed identifiers (supplier's name and address, CRA BN and QST number, date, description of supply, consideration, and GST/QST rates and amounts). The Pan-Canadian and Quebec public-sector e-invoicing frameworks are being implemented on a voluntary/B2G basis, not as a tax clearance precondition. CRA and Revenu Québec may require taxpayers to provide fiscal records in digital format on audit, and electronic sales suppression is prohibited. There is no API pre-clearance gateway for issuing merchant receipts.

CRA and Revenu Québec can reassess a taxpayer to recover uncollected GST/QST plus arrears interest and, in cases of misrepresentation, extend the normal four-year assessment period to seven years. Claiming zero-rating without sufficient export evidence triggers denial of refunds, reversal of input tax credits, and penalties: late or incomplete filings attract 5% plus 1% per month (possibly 10%/12% for repeated failures); false statements or omissions can draw penalties of CAD $250 to CAD $25,000 under the Excise Tax Act and comparable Quebec penalties; gross negligence penalties equal 25% of the misreported net tax; fraud or evasion can result in criminal fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Exporters are expected to create and retain a contemporaneous export evidence file for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility for CAD, USD, and EUR, without advance approvals, counterparty quotas, or conversion schedules. Profit remittance is unrestricted: a Quebec subsidiary may declare dividends at any time if the solvency test (Quebec Business Corporations Act) is met, and remittance is effected via any bank or FX provider. The only frictions are taxation: non-resident withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25% standard, reduced by treaty) and Quebec-specific income tax compliance for the paying corporation. Loans and intellectual-property royalties must be invoiced at arm's length to avoid recharacterization as deemed dividends under subsections 212(2) and 212(3) of the ITA, in addition to Part XIII royalty withholding rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, minimum retention period, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; all receipts may be held in CAD or foreign currency. The only regulatory filters are (i) FINTRAC's mandatory electronic funds transfer reporting threshold of CAD 10,000, which is executed by the clearing financial institution on the client's behalf, and (ii) CBSA/FINTRAC cross-border currency and monetary instruments declaration for physical cash or bearer instruments of CAD 10,000 or more. The Canadian parent entity may therefore deliver a USD/EUR cross-currency blend into Quebec operations without central-bank pre-clearance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary and macroprudential authority, but Canada does not operate an exchange-control administration. The policy levers for restrictions are held by the Minister of Finance under the Investment Canada Act and by Global Affairs Canada under sanctions and export-control legislation; FINTRAC supervises cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). A corporate treasury in Quebec therefore deals with the Bank of Canada only indirectly, through payments systems and settlement (Large-Value Transfer System) rather than FX licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium, structurally subordinate to a Schedule I bank account. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed to open deposit accounts in Quebec; they operate as foreign money transfer businesses and must, if soliciting Canadian clients, be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA. Wise Business's Canadian implementation is provided through a licensed MSB partner and can be used only as a tactical disbursement rail for small EUR/USD payments to European notified bodies or FDA-tool vendors. The primary operating account, credit line, and reserve balances must remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank; unrestricted foreign EMIs are deemed a compliance weakness in KYC and FX audit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Founder/shareholder capital must be remitted as a SWIFT MT103 (or MT101 in batch) to a Quebec Schedule I beneficiary, e.g., National Bank of Canada (SWIFT BNDCCAMMONT). The wire must identify the purpose code EQUI (Equity) or INTC (Intra-Company Payment); no Canadian pre-clearance is required, and no replacement of foreign exchange takes place unless a conversion is requested. The financial institution will file the mandatory FINTRAC EFT report when the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. The treasury must retain the share subscription resolution, the Quebec securities register, and an FX conversion statement if the injection is in USD/EUR. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for intangibles (per transaction or aggregate with non-arm's length non-residents)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require genuine economic substance; a shell company without real activity may be denied treaty benefits and subject to reassessment

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty on understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment (s.245) denying tax benefits; treaty shopping denial under OECD BEPS; penalties up to 10% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; travel bans and extradition risk in cross-border cases
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to implement AML policies; travel bans and seizure of assets possible
