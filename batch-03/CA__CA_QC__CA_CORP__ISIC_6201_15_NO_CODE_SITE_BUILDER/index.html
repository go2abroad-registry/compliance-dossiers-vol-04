Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Acceptable for the ISIC 6201 low-code website-builder industry, provided the office is a physical address in Quebec where legal process, REQ correspondence, and Revenu Quebec documents can be served during business hours. Under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises and the Quebec Business Corporations Act, the registered office (siege social) may be the office of a licensed corporate agent or a commercial virtual-office provider with dedicated mail reception and a named person authorized to accept service; there is no statutory requirement of a storefront, inventory, or employee occupancy for a purely digital software publisher. Practical limitation: the address cannot be a bare post-office box and must correspond to an actual civic address; CRA and Revenu Quebec generally accept a virtual-office address for a low-risk software vendor, while the company must maintain complete corporate records and, for GST/QST purposes, a books-and-records address in Canada. Recommended structure for this client: a Quebec registered office at the corporate agent's commercial address coupled with a director's home address as the business address for ordinary correspondence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or central bank approvals for inbound investment or profit remittance. Non-resident corporate shareholders face Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and certain payments, generally 25% unless reduced by treaty, but no convertibility hurdle exists. Capital repatriation and loan repayments are freely permitted provided they correspond to documented equity or debt instruments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. Exporters may hold, convert, or repatriate foreign currency proceeds freely. Cross-border monetary transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC, and records supporting export transactions must be retained for tax and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administering cross-border currency reporting and anti-money laundering supervision. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange administration or exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to High viability for using licensed Payment Service Providers and Electronic Money Institutions as ancillary collection/settlement rails, but low viability as the sole treasury platform. Offshore EMIs lacking Canadian Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC are legally restricted from maintaining domestic accounts or clearing CAD in the Large Value Transfer System. A Quebec-based MSB registration and provincial licensing under the Retail Payment Activities Act may be required, making Tier 1 banks mandatory for core corporate treasury functions.

Defense evidence: Certified certificate of incorporation, register of directors, share register with beneficial ownership schedule, corporate chart, audited financial statements, tax residence certificates, and a board resolution identifying economic substance, business purpose, and source of initial funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be structured as either share-subscription proceeds or documented shareholder loans. Routing is via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor bank to the Quebec entity's Tier 1 bank account, with a clear remittance narrative distinguishing 'share capital contribution' or 'intercompany loan disbursement'. Canada has no statutory payment-purpose code system; however, FINTRAC large-transaction reporting applies to receipts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. The receiving bank will require verification of source of funds, and non-Canadian investors may need to file under the Investment Canada Act only if thresholds are exceeded; standard verification include a board resolution, share subscription agreement, loan agreement, and evidence of the remitter's corporate existence.Outbound operational disbursements for SaaS vendors, contractors, and licensing fees are cleared using SWIFT MT103 cross-border wires, with Canadian domestic settlements executed through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). For procurement of physical goods ancillary to software business, Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) may be used. Payments to OFAC, UN, and Canadian sanctions targets are prohibited, and outgoing wires of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting. Beneficiary bank details and SWIFT BIC/IBAN equivalents must be reconciled against bank records to prevent sanctions-related delays.Vanilla deliverable FX forwardsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic currency exposuresCross-currency swapsFX options (plain vanilla calls and puts)Exchange-traded futures and options on the Canadian dollar
