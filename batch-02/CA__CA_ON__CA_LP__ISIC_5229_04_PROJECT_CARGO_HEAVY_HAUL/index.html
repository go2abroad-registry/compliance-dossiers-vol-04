Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - exclusive administrator of the federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registries, with Ontario corporate income tax and the 8% provincial HST component collected under the Canada-Ontario tax collection agreements; portals: canada.ca, cra.gc.ca, and Business Registration Online (BRO), including the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre Non-Resident Unit for non-resident LPs. The Ontario Ministry of Finance retains jurisdiction only over employer health tax and the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act declaration registry.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - File the limited partnership declaration under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (Ministry of Government and Consumer Services) to establish legal existence; this declaration must be in place before the CRA Business Number is issued. Step 2 - Apply for a CRA Business Number using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or BRO, electing the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) and, if applicable, payroll (RP) and/or corporate income tax (RC) accounts; the LP registers in its own name with the general partner or a designated Canadian representative as contact. Step 3 - The CRA Non-Resident Unit issues the 9-digit BN with RT suffix; a non-resident LP without a Canadian address must nominate a Canadian representative and maintain a Canadian books-and-records location. Step 4 - Register for GST/HST before issuing the first taxable invoice where worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; monthly, quarterly, or annual reporting frequency is assigned by CRA based on annual revenue. Step 5 - File T5013 partnership information returns and GST/HST returns under the BN, and withhold under Part XIII or Part I for non-resident partners unless treaty or statutory reduction applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - An Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (ITA s. 96): the LP itself pays no federal or Ontario income tax; each partner is taxed on its distributive share of Canadian-source business income. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is separately deemed a person under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and is the registrant that charges HST on taxable supplies and recovers input tax credits. Non-resident limited partners are taxable in Canada on their share of partnership business income attributable to the Ontario permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by legal form and the partnership agreement. Available elections and designations in this cross-border LP context: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration where the small supplier threshold is not met (ETA s. 240(2)); (2) designation of a partnership member or manager to file and remit the partnership's GST/HST returns under the ETA partnership rules; (3) fiscal-period alignment to the calendar year reference basis where a non-resident non-arm's length partner holds an interest (ITA s. 96); (4) the ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners, which is generally confined to Canadian real property/rental income and is not available for operating project cargo revenue; and (5) mandatory hybrid mismatch re-characterization under ITA ss. 18.2-18.4 and 20.2 (no electivity), requiring alignment of deduction and inclusion timing with the partner jurisdiction. Thin capitalization (ITA s. 18(4)) and transfer pricing (ITA s. 247) rules apply to cross-border partner transactions with no elective exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on the partnership's worldwide taxable supplies during the current calendar quarter and the four immediately preceding calendar quarters; exceeding CAD 30,000 makes GST/HST registration mandatory as of the first day of the calendar month in which the threshold is crossed. A heavy-haul project cargo contract will in practice exceed the threshold at the first milestone invoice, so the LP must register before invoicing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for OOG heavy project cargo logistics is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: s. 6 (supply of tangible personal property exported from Canada), s. 7 (supply of a transportation service, including heavy-haul inland trucking, where the goods are exported from Canada as part of the service), and s. 11 (freight-forwarding and arranging-for services in respect of a transportation service for exported goods). Conditions precedent: the goods must leave Canada as soon as reasonable after supply (CRA administrative benchmark of 60 days for most goods), the LP must obtain and retain prescribed export evidence before claiming zero-rating, the service contract must identify a foreign port or border destination, and the exported goods must not be subsequently returned to Canada. Services terminating at a Canadian port are zero-rated only where the goods are placed under export documentation and depart Canada; purely domestic Ontario haulage remains taxable at 13% HST.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (legacy Form B13A) filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System or the CBSA Single Window Initiative, mandatory where the value of exported goods exceeds CAD 2,000, for controlled goods, or on CBSA demand
  • Commercial export invoice and detailed packing list reflecting OOG dimensions, weight, marks, and Incoterms
  • Clean original or sea-waybill Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note naming the foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT / telegraphic transfer advice evidencing receipt of export consideration (where the LP invoices the foreign obligor directly)
  • Cargo insurance certificate, shipper's letter of instruction, and carrier booking confirmation
  • Terminal gate-out receipts, carrier milestone reports, and customs release records demonstrating departure from Canadian territory

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoice clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime; e-invoicing is voluntary and operates on PEPPOL and open XML formats. CRA requires full books and records (invoices, contracts, export declarations, bills of lading, payment evidence) for six years. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST E-file/NETFILE or commercial software; electronic filing is compulsory for registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million, and large registrants with worldwide annual taxable supplies above CAD 10 million are assigned monthly reporting periods. Late remittances attract prescribed daily-compounded interest and a 5% balance-due penalty plus 1% per full month (maximum 12 months).

Ordinary GST/HST reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298); the window is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established, enabling retrospective clawback of zero-rating benefits and input tax credits with prescribed daily-compounded interest. Understated amounts attract a 5% balance-due penalty plus 1% per full month up to 12 months, and gross-negligence or false-statement penalties of 25% apply under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 163.2. Knowingly false export declarations are subject to criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 (summary conviction: fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months; on indictment: imprisonment up to five years). CRA audit focus for freight forwarders centers on matching export declarations to bills of lading, gate-out records, and payment flows; missing evidence triggers 13% re-rating, disallowance of input tax credits, interest, penalties, and denial of future refunds until substantiation is produced. All export records must be retained for six years. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the dossier's contractual benchmark for milestone-wire and letter-of-credit proceeds, used by the CRA as evidence that the transaction constitutes a genuine export rather than a round-trip financing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar (CAD) under a free-floating regime. There are no exchange-control approvals for inward capital injections, equity contributions, or outward profit/distribution repatriation in respect of an Ontario limited partnership. Profit remittance for a Canadian-resident LP is unrestricted in cash or foreign currency, but any cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is subject to FINTRAC reporting by the transmitting financial institution; CAD 10,000 or more in physical currency or monetary instruments crossing the border requires a Form E677 (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report) through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their share of Canada-source partnership business income; bare partnership distributions do not attract Part XIII withholding, although passive payments (interest, rents, royalties, management or administration fees) paid to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25% unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. CRS/FATCA self-certification and Canadian beneficial-ownership reporting are mandatory at account opening and on any material change of ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export or cross-border service receipts. Project-cargo logistics income derived by the Ontario LP (covered by the zero-rated GST/HST freight-transportation and related services provisions) may be retained freely in CAD or in any foreign currency bank account maintained at a Schedule I or II bank. There is no Export Proceeds Surrender Certificate and no central-bank approval obligation. However, the CBSA requires electronic export reporting (Canada Customs Online / Automated Export Declaration) where goods physically cross the border as part of OOG heavy-haul services, and the bank is obliged to report international EFTs and currency movements equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC. Exporter/transporter records must evidence the underlying service contract, bills of lading, and commercial invoices to support the zero-rating claim and to satisfy bank-level KYC/AML document retention.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary policy and acting as lender of last resort; the Department of Finance (Ministère des Finances) retains responsibility for international financial policy and exchange-rate intervention reserves; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates and supervises federally chartered banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border payment reporting, AML/CFT, and sanctions-related due diligence. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange-control agency, no exchange licensing board, and no capital-import/export authorization body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Conditional / Medium feasibility for ancillary disbursement rails. Offshore digital EMI providers (non-Canadian, non-bank e-money institutions such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut-class platforms) cannot accept deposits or maintain operating accounts in Canada without a bank license under the Bank Act (Schedule I or II); they are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance and cannot lawfully hold the LP's escrowed milestone funds. They may, however, act as an auxiliary payment-completion channel for small vendor cross-border payments if, and only if, the provider is registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) and, where applicable, registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and licensed under the Ontario Money-Services Business Act, 2012. Primary capital, escrow, and project-disbursement balances should remain exclusively in OSFI-supervised Schedule I banks. Canadian banks will treat third-party EMI receipt of project funds as a high-risk red flag unless the EMI relationship is pre-disclosed and the EMI's registration credentials are furnished.

Root cause: High-value USD wire to or from a tax-haven or offshore EMI jurisdiction inconsistent with the physical route of the project cargo

Defense evidence: Commercial invoices and bills of lading evidencing cargo dimensions, chassis/vessel route, and physical plausibility; INCOTERMS 2020 designation; nexus letter explaining why the counterparty is incorporated in the flagged jurisdiction and confirming the beneficiary bank is not an offshore EMI; legal-entity transactional chart; explicit bank pre-approval request prior to executing any transfer with an EMI/offshore beneficiary; quarantine rule prohibiting undisclosed offshore EMI receipt.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: each non-resident limited partner or parent entity initiates a SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) to the LP's designated Schedule I account at RBC or TD using the bank's published Standard Settlement Instructions (SSI). The remittance information field must state: 'CA_LP ISIC_5229_04 PROJECT_CARGO_HEAVY_HAUL - CAPITAL INJECTION - [partner name and reference]'. CAD-denominated injections settle through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) upon arrival; USD injections are credited via the bank's U.S. correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS) and held in the multi-currency operating account. Before the first transfer, the bank must complete CIP on the LP, the General Partner, and all 25%+ beneficial owners; the bank must obtain CRS/FATCA self-certifications, a beneficial-ownership attestation, and a source-of-funds declaration. FINTRAC reporting of the inbound international EFT applies automatically at the CAD 10,000 threshold. Contributions in excess of CAD 100,000 should be pre-notified to the relationship manager for purpose-code confirmation and to avoid funds-hold or return-rejection. The inbound transfer must not be structured from an undisclosed third-party or over-the-counter cash deposit; any such attempt will be rejected under the bank's transaction-monitoring rules.Primary clearing rails for the project-cargo treasury are: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for operational vendor, sub-hauler, stevedore, and customs-broker payments; (2) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for upstream correspondent reimbursement where a beneficiary bank is not directly on the Schedule I bank's network; (3) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600, payable at sight or usance, with confirmation by a Canadian Schedule I bank, used for heavy-haul equipment acquisition and marine/rolling-stock charter deposits; (4) standby letters of credit under ISP98 serving as milestone performance and mobilization security; (5) documentary collections under URC 522 for OOG equipment cross-shipments where open-account risk is mitigated; and (6) domestic CAD high-value disbursements settled through LVTS, with USD cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent via Fedwire/CHIPS. Each outbound wire must reference the underlying invoice number, project stage, and cargo route; transaction descriptions must avoid generic text and must align with the approved sub-contractor registry. LC documents must be completed strictly to terms to prevent discrepancies: commercial invoice, original bills of lading, packing/weight lists, certificate of origin, and project-milestone certificates. All outbound disbursements above CAD 10,000 are captured in the bank's FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting and in the LP's global transactions inventory for audit.Deliverable outright USD/CAD foreign-exchange forward contracts with a Schedule I bank under ISDA 1998 FX Definitions, used to lock the project's USD-denominated OOG logistics contract revenues and milestone costs; permissible for maturities up to 24 months with no regulatory approval requirement.FX swaps (spot-against-forward and tom-next) to manage temporary USD funding gaps between milestone receipts and outbound disburssements, executed under the same master agreement; no exchange-control restriction applies.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term CAD-USD interest-rate and principal currency hedging on project debt or intercompany loan facilities, subject to bank internal ISDA/CSA credit documentation and mark-to-market collateral posting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible only in respect of non-convertible emerging-market currencies of cargo destination jurisdictions (e.g., local-currency conversion exposure in restricted African, Middle Eastern, or Central Asian project locations), where the counterparty bank offers a deliverable-alternative limitation; NDFs are unnecessary and not permissible as substitutes for CAD or USD because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible and deliverable.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional / Medium feasibility for ancillary disbursement rails. Offshore digital EMI providers (non-Canadian, non-bank e-money institutions such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut-class platforms) cannot accept deposits or maintain operating accounts in Canada without a bank license under the Bank Act (Schedule I or II); they are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance and cannot lawfully hold the LP's escrowed milestone funds. They may, however, act as an auxiliary payment-completion channel for small vendor cross-border payments if, and only if, the provider is registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) and, where applicable, registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and licensed under the Ontario Money-Services Business Act, 2012. Primary capital, escrow, and project-disbursement balances should remain exclusively in OSFI-supervised Schedule I banks. Canadian banks will treat third-party EMI receipt of project funds as a high-risk red flag unless the EMI relationship is pre-disclosed and the EMI's registration credentials are furnished.
