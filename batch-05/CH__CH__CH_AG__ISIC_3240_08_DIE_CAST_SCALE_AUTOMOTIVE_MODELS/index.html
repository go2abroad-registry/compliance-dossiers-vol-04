Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), VAT Main Division (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer), Berne; cantonal and communal tax administrations of the AG's registered seat; Swiss Customs Administration (Oberzolldirektion). Official portals: https://www.estv.admin.ch, eTaxOnWeb (electronic filing), https://www.uid.admin.ch (UID register), https://www.zefix.ch (Central Business Register).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the AG: notarize the articles of association (minimum share capital CHF 100,000, of which CHF 50,000 must be fully paid in), obtain the bank capital-confirmation certificate, and file for entry in the Commercial Register of the canton of seat. The Commercial Register transmits the entry to the Swiss Central Business Register (ZEFIX), which automatically assigns the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID, format CHE-123.456.789) under the Federal Act on the Business Identification Number (UIDG). Step 2 – VAT/TIN registration: the Swiss-resident AG applies electronically via eTaxOnWeb to the ESTV VAT Main Division for inclusion in the VAT register; the UID then serves as the statutory VAT identification number (MWST-Nr / TVA-No) under Art. 110 MWSTG. Registration is mandatory without delay once annual taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000; voluntary registration is available below this threshold and confers the right to deduct input VAT. A foreign entity without a Swiss seat or permanent establishment uses the simplified registration form and must appoint a tax representative domiciled in Switzerland; its MWST-Nr is likewise derived from its UID or a provisional VAT number. Step 3 – Profit-tax registration: upon incorporation, the cantonal tax administration of the registered seat opens the corporate tax file (cantonal and communal profit and equity taxes) and the ESTV opens the Direct Federal Tax file; all filings reference the UID. Step 4 – Obtain a certified UID/VAT extract from the UID register portal for banking onboarding (SWIFT), PayPal integration and counterparty validation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a resident corporate taxpayer with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income for Direct Federal Tax (8.5% of net profit) and cantonal/communal profit tax plus an annual equity tax (Kapitalsteuer). No pass-through regime, no territorial system and no tax-exempt zone applies to a manufacturing AG in Switzerland.

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no check-the-box classification and no general hybrid-mismatch election; OECD hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by mandatory anti-hybrid, CFC and interest-barrier rules. Genuine statutory elections available: (1) small-enterprise VAT option under Art. 11 MWSTG for taxable turnover below CHF 100,000, binding for at least five years; (2) VAT group taxation under Art. 13 MWSTG for closely linked enterprises (substantial financial, economic and organizational intertwining), binding for at least five years; (3) partial taxation of dividends from qualified participations (deemed 70% exemption) for individual shareholders; (4) cantonal patent-box and additional notional-interest-deduction election for self-developed qualifying IP under the Swiss Tax Reform Act 2020 (STAF); (5) customs AEO (Authorized Economic Operator) certification; and (6) for foreign-parented groups, the mandatory Swiss branch profit-tax and deemed-liquidation exit rules (non-elective).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual taxable turnover from Swiss supplies (including zero-rated exports; excluding exempt supplies) under Art. 10 para. 2 lit. c and Art. 110 MWSTG. Foreign suppliers of electronic or digital services to Swiss consumers are mandatorily registrable from the first franc under Art. 18b MWSTG. Domestic small businesses may voluntarily register from the first franc.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports under Art. 23 para. 1 lit. 1 MWSTG: the supply is zero-rated if (i) the die-cast products are physically transported from Swiss customs territory into a foreign customs territory; (ii) delivery occurs to the foreign purchaser or to a third party commissioned by the purchaser abroad; and (iii) the supplier substantiates the export through the Swiss customs export declaration (e-dec/PASSAR output, or CN22/CN23 postal customs declaration for parcel shipments) and an international transport document (CMR consignment note, bill of lading, sea/air waybill). Export sales through Swiss freight forwarders are zero-rated only where the supplier obtains an export certificate from the customs-clearing forwarder. Related offshore services: work performed on movable property in Switzerland (e.g., custom tampo printing, painting, assembly or repair of scale replicas) followed by export of the goods is zero-rated under Art. 23 para. 1 lit. 4 MWSTG. Pure services supplied to a business customer domiciled abroad whose place of supply lies abroad under Art. 18 MWSTG are not subject to Swiss VAT and are invoiced at 0% with a statutory place-of-supply reference. All export substantiation must be archived for ten years and presented to the ESTV on request.

  • Swiss customs export declaration accepted by Swiss Customs (e-dec/PASSAR output, or CN22/CN23 postal customs declaration) identifying the AG as exporter and stating goods description, value, destination and the exporter's UID/VAT number
  • International transport document evidencing physical departure: original bill of lading, sea/air waybill, or CMR consignment note signed by the consignee or forwarder abroad
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and, where applicable, the Swiss Federal Customs Administration export certificate for consignments cleared by a third-party freight forwarder
  • Payment substantiation: SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice or PayPal settlement/payout report, together with carrier delivery confirmation and acknowledgement from the foreign buyer (Switzerland imposes no fixed foreign-currency repatriation deadline; export proceeds may be held abroad and settled in EUR, USD or CHF)

No real-time fiscal clearance, no statutory e-invoicing mandate, no API clearance portal and no digital stamping exist under Swiss VAT law as of 2025. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) has been the mandatory standardized payment slip for all invoices payable in Switzerland since 1 July 2020, embedding the QR-IBAN, creditor data and UID/VAT metadata. Electronic invoices are voluntarily exchanged in standard formats (UBL 2.0 Swiss extension, Factur-X/ZUGFeRD, PDF/XML bundles); no authorization or clearance request is submitted to the tax authority before issuance. VAT returns are filed electronically through the ESTV eTaxOnWeb/ELM portal. Invoices, customs export declarations and transport documents must be retained in a traceable, chronologically complete archive for ten years (Art. 58 para. 4 MWSTG).

Export zero-rating claims are screened by the ESTV against Swiss customs exit records (PASSAR data matching) and are priority targets in VAT audits of e-commerce collectibles sellers. If the exporter cannot substantiate physical departure of the goods, the supply is retroactively reclassified as domestically taxable at 8.1% (standard rate), the corresponding input VAT credits are refused or clawed back, and the underpaid VAT is assessed with statutory default interest within the general 5-year limitation period (Art. 84 para. 1 MWSTG), extended to ten years in cases of qualified tax fraud (Art. 84 para. 3 MWSTG). Culpable failure to document exports or intentional misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion under Arts. 176–178 MWSTG, carrying fines of up to three times the tax evaded; qualified tax fraud under Art. 186 MWSTG read with the Federal Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR) is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a monetary penalty, with the evaded tax also due. Forged customs declarations, bills of lading or delivery receipts additionally trigger prosecution under Art. 251 of the Swiss Criminal Code (forgery of documents) and independent penalties under the Swiss Customs Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Swiss law imposes no exchange-control permit, prior-authorization regime, or repatriation approval for inbound equity, outbound dividends, intercompany loans, or trade settlements. Profit remittance by the CH_AG is unrestricted and executed freely via SIC/EuroSIC/SEPA/SWIFT, subject only to the Swiss federal withholding tax of 35% on dividend distributions (Verrechnungssteuer), which is refundable or reduced under double-taxation treaties or Swiss participation-relief rules. No circular-transaction clearance or currency-approval document is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland has no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. The die-cast model exporter may retain CHF, EUR, USD, or CNY receivables domestically or abroad, may adopt internal treasury netting, and may determine FX conversion purely by corporate treasury policy without notifying the SNB or SIF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank) acting under the National Bank Act and Art. 99 of the Federal Constitution, in conjunction with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF). Switzerland maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration; the SNB is responsible for monetary policy, the SIC/EuroSIC payment systems, and banknote issuance, while the Federal Council holds dormant emergency powers under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (WZG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as primary treasury but viable as ancillary rails. EU/UK EMIs cannot passport an EMI licence into Switzerland; Art. 1b of the Banking Act restricts Swiss fintech operations to licensed domestic players such as Alpian, Yuh, and Bank Cler, while foreign EMIs may only provide card-issuing or collection-aggregation services through Swiss partner banks. The statutory capital escrow account and the main treasury must remain with a fully licensed Swiss bank to establish commercial substance under CO Art. 633 and to satisfy bank assurance obligations imposed by FINMA Circular 2020/01.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory corporate income tax (calculated on profit after federal tax; pre-tax equivalent 7.83%; plus varying cantonal/communal corporate income tax, producing aggregate headline rates of approximately 14.6%–24.4% depending on canton/municipality).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends paid by an AG; final/relief under double tax agreements may reduce the rate to 0-15% depending on shareholding and treaty partner, with 0-5% for qualifying parent-subsidiary holdings under many Swiss DTAs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Generally due within 6-9 months after the balance-sheet date; calendar-year AGs in most cantons must file by 31 May or 30 June of the following year. A written extension may postpone filing to 30 September or beyond, but no later than the assessment notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: Swiss AG engaged in cross-border related-party transactions above CHF 1,000,000 in aggregate per fiscal year is expected to maintain arm's-length documentation in the form required by the Swiss FTA/OECD TP Manual; Master File and Country-by-Country Report: mandatory for ultimate parent groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900,000,000 or more. Documentation must be produced to the tax assessor on request in German/French/Italian or in translated/English form.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Abuse Rule and Federal Supreme Court practice require an AG to have real economic substance in Switzerland (business premises, competent staff, actual management decisions, bank account and commercial activity). A 'letter-box' company invoking treaty benefits and profit attribution without substance will be recharacterized: profits are allotted to a foreign permanent establishment or the controlling shareholder, DTA relief is denied, and the taxpayer faces reassessment, interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 175-176 DBG: fine equal to the tax evaded; where falsified documents are used, fiscal fraud is punishable by a fine up to three times the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; cantonal surcharges and default interest are cumulative.

Director personal liability: Any director who signs, prepares, or causes the submission of a knowingly false return is individually criminally liable; sanctions include prison, confiscation of personal assets, travel and banking restrictions, and the director may be required to make restitution of the tax loss.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Swiss GAAR recharacterization and reassessment: foreign parent is treated as having a Swiss permanent establishment or the AG as transparent in whole or part; DTA benefits are denied; under-declared profit is taxed with interest; cannot liquidate without payment; additional penalties for incorrect return.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow a sham structure can be sued by shareholders/creditors for damages under Art. 754 OR and Art. 41 CO; the Swiss court may pierce the corporate veil where assets and personal funds are commingled, leaving foreign directors subject to personal judgments and mutual legal assistance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Missing Local File

Statutory sanction: FTA upward adjustment of taxable income to the result that independent parties would obtain; additional federal/cantonal tax plus late-payment interest; administrative fine for incomplete documentation (commonly up to 30% of additional assessed tax); in serious cases treated as attempted tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length pricing are personally liable to the company for damages caused by profit leakage; criminal exposure attaches if false contractual documents are created to justify the adjustment; foreign directors may be summoned and income/provisional measures taken abroad.
Withholding Tax / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: FTA demands 35% WHT on the gross constructive dividend, plus default interest and procedural fine; failure to remit is a criminal offense; the amount due is secured by statutory first lien on the corporation's assets and can force insolvency.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or tolerate non-withholding are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted WHT where the AG is unable to pay; the FTA may pursue them personally, issue a payment order, and request exit restrictions for treating assets abroad.
Anti-Money-Laundering / Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violations of AMLA and related Swiss financial-penalty law carry imprisonment up to 3 years and fines up to CHF 500,000 for natural persons; for an entity, damage-causing fines and confiscation of all assets linked to the offense; the low-risk classification is withdrawn if such conduct appears.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable where they fail to take the necessary organizational measures when the AG's operations cross the financial-intermediary threshold or when they deliberately conceal suspicious payment flows; the Swiss courts may impose a professional ban, a travel ban, and extradition under mutual legal assistance.
