VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once exceeded, except where supplies are exclusively zero-rated or the non-resident supplier has no Canadian nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Raw cane sugar sold by a Canadian exporter to a non-resident is zero-rated under subsections 23(1) and 24(1) of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a consignee outside Canada 'as soon as is reasonable' after the supply, and documentary evidence of export is retained. Vessel-shipped VHP raw centrifugal sugar qualifies where title passes abroad and the supplier maintains proof of physical export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or Canadian Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign-currency payment
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Destination-country customs entry or import documentation

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable via the Peppol network; GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI, with no invoice-level tax authority pre-approval.

CRA may reassess improperly zero-rated supplies at the 13% Ontario HST rate, apply arrears interest, and impose a penalty of 5% of the understated tax plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated amount apply where a taxpayer knowingly or grossly negligently participates in a false export claim, and criminal prosecution may yield fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating market-determined CAD with fully convertible current and capital accounts. There are no general exchange controls, surrender requirements, or approval hurdles for profit remittance, dividend payments, interest, royalties, or trade settlements. Conversion into CAD or any foreign currency through a Canadian authorized financial institution is unrestricted, subject only to sanctions, anti-terrorism orders, and FATF-based due-diligence expectations. Non-residents are free to invest, hold proceeds, and repatriate funds net of Canadian withholding taxes; no repatriation tax or special FX levy exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or remittance timeline applies in Canada. Export receipts for raw cane sugar and any commodity shipments may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted into CAD freely. Canadian income tax rules require recognition of export revenue on an accrual basis under the Income Tax Act, but no central-bank repatriation deadline or conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary anchor; the Department of Finance holds exchange rate policy oversight; no dedicated foreign exchange administration; OSFI supervises federally regulated financial institutions; FINTRAC administers anti-money laundering and sanctions-related reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary banking conduit. A pure offshore electronic money institution (EMI) is not a Canadian authorized bank, cannot issue commercial LCs, and is generally unable to settle physical commodity trade through Canadian clearing rails. Any money service business acting for Canadians must register with FINTRAC and meet domestic compliance. A Schedule I Canadian bank remains indispensable for the capital escrow account, documentary collections, and LC facilities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 0 — no de minimis threshold exists for cross-border non-arm's-length transactions; contemporaneous local-file and master-file documentation must be retained for any transaction with a related party, and the Country-by-Country Report threshold is consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime in the UAE/Gulf coordination sense; instead the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), common-law substance-over-form, central management and control, and permanent establishment attribution tests. For a non-resident sugar wholesaler, its Ontario office must carry out substantive management, inventory ownership, contract negotiation, freight risk, and sales decision functions to prevent the arrangement from being disregarded or re-attributed to the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (False Returns)

Statutory sanction: On conviction under Income Tax Act s.239(1), a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; plus imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; civil reassessment attaches compound interest from the date of original filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly joined in or acquiesced to false filing may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted taxes and penalties under s.227.1; criminal disgorgement orders and travel restrictions are available in indictable tax evasion cases.
Shell Company / Sham Agency Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the legal entity under the GAAR, reassess the parent as carrying on business in Canada, and impose Part XIII withholding on all dividends or deemed remittances; treaty benefits are denied for lack of economic substance; interest and penalties up to 10% of the tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the full amount of unremitted Part XIII tax if funds are paid to shareholders while withholding debts remain unpaid; extended criminal liability may attach to nominee directors under tax evasion prosecutions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to maintain and report beneficial ownership information can trigger administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation; Ontario BCA register violations create daily fines for the corporation and directors.

Director personal liability: Directors of an Ontario corporation are strictly liable for failure to maintain a register of individuals with significant control (ISC), resulting in up to CAD 200 per day that the omission continues; in serious cases, directors can face up to 5 years imprisonment for knowingly providing false beneficial ownership information.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (Related-Party Sugar Purchases)

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess using the TNMM/CUP benchmark and apply a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment under s.247(3); the adjustment itself accrues daily interest and can trigger grossed-up withholding tax if re-characterized as a deemed dividend.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent transfer pricing documentation may be held personally liable for penalties only where they knew that the documentation was not made in good faith or made statements liable to mislead; directors cannot be indemnified by the corporation for tax penalties arising from its illegal acts.
