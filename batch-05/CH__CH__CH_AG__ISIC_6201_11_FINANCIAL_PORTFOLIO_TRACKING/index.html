Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV/FTA), Main Division Value Added Tax, Bern; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch. Customs and export-declaration competence: Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (Bundesamt für Zoll und Grenzsicherheit, BAZG; www.bazg.admin.ch). Central electronic Commercial Register and UID database: Zefix (www.zefix.ch).

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG obtains the unified business identifier (UID) automatically upon entry in the cantonal Commercial Register; VAT registration is completed via the FTA's online VAT registration form, which activates the UID with the 'MWST' suffix (format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST) within approximately 14-30 days. Non-resident foreign entities without a Swiss seat register directly with the FTA (no domestic registered office required) by submitting constitutive documents, AML/KYC identification of beneficial owners and legal representatives, and a declaration of expected Swiss-source turnover; they receive a UID-MWST for VAT filing and for the simplified VAT refund procedure. Registration with the competent cantonal tax administration is additionally required where a Swiss permanent establishment is maintained. For the ISIC 6201 financial portfolio-tracking software activity (non-banking fintech, AML low-risk tier, no statutory license), no other federal tax license applies; the same registration path governs resident AG and foreign branch alike.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (an Aktiengesellschaft is a separate, non-transparent tax subject for federal, cantonal and communal profit and capital taxes; residence-based worldwide taxation applies, subject to the territorial carve-out exemption for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate under Art. 52 DBG; no pass-through or flow-through classification is available).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Switzerland; tax classification is fixed by legal form. Available statutory elections and reliefs: (i) participation relief (Beteiligungsabzug) on dividends and capital gains from qualifying participations (≥10% or acquisition value ≥ CHF 1 million); (ii) notional interest deduction on qualifying surplus equity (Art. 25a DBG, introduced by the Swiss Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act); (iii) cantonal patent-box and additional R&D super-deduction options under the STAF framework; (iv) VAT small-business exemption election below CHF 100,000 turnover (Art. 11 VAT Act); and (v) VAT group taxation election for closely integrated entities (Art. 21 VAT Act). Hybrid-mismatch deduction disallowance is applied under Art. 58a DBG, aligning Swiss law with BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10(2)(a) VAT Act); non-established suppliers of services placed in Switzerland (including B2C e-services) are subject to registration once Swiss-source turnover exceeds CHF 100,000; below-threshold businesses may elect small-business exemption, forfeiting input VAT deduction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% with preservation of the input VAT deduction applies to tangible goods physically exported from Swiss customs territory and to services directly related to such exports (freight, handling, dispatch) under Art. 23(1) VAT Act. For the ISIC 6201 cloud portfolio-tracking software activity, cross-border supplies are generally outside the Swiss VAT scope: B2B services sold to non-resident principals are localized at the recipient's place of business (Art. 8 VAT Act), and B2C digital services are ex-Swiss when not effectively used or benefited in Switzerland; both treatments are economically equivalent to 0% with full input VAT recovery. Eligibility requires verifiable evidence of both the cross-border movement (goods) and the recipient's foreign seat or foreign place of benefit (services).

  • Customs export declaration (e-dec / Ausfuhrbeleg or official Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) discharged by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (BAZG) proving physical removal of the goods from Swiss customs territory
  • Transport and dispatch documentation evidencing cross-border carriage: Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill, together with the carrier delivery receipt
  • Inward remittance certificate: commercial invoice, bank credit advice, and payment-flow records for the export proceeds (Switzerland requires no statutory FX-repatriation certificate, but banking-settlement evidence is mandatory substantiation in FTA export audits)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transaction-control or digital stamping mandate exists at the Swiss federal level. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary but standardized via the Swiss QR-Invoice (QR-Rechnung) and the PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 network; suppliers to the Swiss federal administration must transmit e-invoices through the mandated federal e-billing channel. VAT returns and refund claims are filed electronically via the FTA portal; all VAT records and source documents must be retained for 10 years for tax-audit access.

The FTA conducts documentary post-audits of zero-rated export transactions; an unsubstantiated zero-rating claim is re-assessed at the standard 8.1% rate with default interest at 4.5% p.a. and a full clawback of correspondingly deducted input VAT. Negligent or wilful evasion triggers administrative fines of up to three times the tax evaded and criminal prosecution in serious cases (Art. 96-97 VAT Act); the statutory assessment and recovery period extends to 10 years in evasion scenarios (Art. 89 VAT Act), during which all records must be retained. Switzerland maintains no statutory FX-repatriation deadline and has no exchange controls; the 180-day parameter is the documentary-settlement benchmark for export invoice ageing and credit recycling in FTA and banking practice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under the OECD Code of Liberalisation. There are no exchange control statutes, no licensing requirements for inbound or outbound remittances, and no SNB approval gates for dividend, interest, royalty, or equity flows. The Swiss franc is a freely traded reserve currency. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than regulatory: dividends face a 35% anticipatory withholding tax automatically refundable or reduced under applicable double taxation treaties; otherwise, no administrative convertibility or repatriation barrier exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export service receipts. An exporting or cross-border SaaS company may retain export proceeds indefinitely in freely convertible currency in domestic or multi-currency accounts; conversion into CHF or any other currency is entirely at the company's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), in coordination with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) of the Federal Department of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium, ancillary only. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business or Revolut Business are not licensed in Switzerland, do not offer legally segregated client funds under Swiss Banking Act (BA), and are unacceptable as the primary treasury spine of a Swiss AG. A regulated European EMI can be used as a supplementary EUR collection rail, while the main operating account, share-capital escrow, FX hedging, and liquidity clearing must remain with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the payment purpose 'Equity Share Capital / Unrestricted Paid-in Capital' under Swiss Form A and OCO coding for cross-border remittance statistics, referencing the shareholder name and the company's Swiss corporate identifier. Funds are credited to the blocked Capital Escrow Account (Sperrkonto), with upfront bank attestation sent to the Commercial Register confirming the capital is at the board's free disposal. Post-incorporation intercompany injections use purpose code 'Intercompany Loan / Equity Contribution', accompanied by a certified loan agreement or capital increase protocol; no SNB approval is required, but all wiring, purpose-code, and supporting documents are retained for fiscal and transfer-pricing inspection.Supplier and operating disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 for non-European counterparties and through SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR or Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) for domestic and EEA flows. For material cloud-infrastructure or hardware procurements involving multi-million CHF values, Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 are recommended as secure trade-clearing rails. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium, ancillary only. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business or Revolut Business are not licensed in Switzerland, do not offer legally segregated client funds under Swiss Banking Act (BA), and are unacceptable as the primary treasury spine of a Swiss AG. A regulated European EMI can be used as a supplementary EUR collection rail, while the main operating account, share-capital escrow, FX hedging, and liquidity clearing must remain with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax share; combined federal + cantonal/communal headline rate normally ranges from 11.8% to 21.1% depending on the canton of registered office (e.g., approximately 14.9% for Zug, 21.1% for Zurich)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Swiss AG, regardless of residency of the shareholder, subject to tax treaty relief or tax exemption in certain domestic cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 500,000 (recommended audit threshold); CHF 900,000,000 for mandatory Master File/CbCR group threshold

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but it enforces a functional substance concept derived from OECD treaty-residency principles and Swiss tax-case law. An AG must maintain a real office, locally available books and records, substantive board activity, personnel with decision-making authority, and genuine economic risk-taking in Switzerland. A letter-box AG may be denied treaty benefits, temporary reliefs, and the patent box; the tax authority may reattribute profits to the foreign parent or deem hidden profit distributions, resulting in additional 35% withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (federal and cantonal direct tax)

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act (DBG), tax evasion entails a fine of up to 100% of the tax evaded (federal and cantonal portions); for aggravated false-document tax fraud, the fine may increase to three times the tax evaded and ordinary criminal fines up to CHF 36,000, or imprisonment up to three years in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers are personally, jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties if they committed, directed, or authorised the evasion. Travel bans may be imposed within Swiss debt collection proceedings, and imprisonment of up to three years applies for intentional fraudulent tax evasion.
Filing and Disclosure Defaults

Statutory sanction: Federal procedural fine of up to CHF 10,000; cantonal fine of up to CHF 5,000; risk of ex officio assessment with a security surcharge of up to 100% of the tax base; default interest of 4.5% per annum on all overdue amounts.

Director personal liability: The entire board of directors can be held individually responsible for the administrative duties of the AG; failure to comply after a formal order may lead to personal fines and coercive detention (maximum eight weeks) until the documents are produced.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withdrawal of patent box and participation relief; income is recharacterised and allocated to the parent jurisdiction; deemed dividend distributions subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax; regulatory authorities may compel striking off the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a shell AG are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and WHT on deemed distributions; they may be banned from board positions in Switzerland and are subject to criminal penalties for false declarations in commercial register filings.
