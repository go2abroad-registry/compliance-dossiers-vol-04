Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For income tax purposes, the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada); income, losses, and credits flow through to the partners proportionate to their profit/loss interests, and the partnership is not an income-tax-bearing person. For GST/HST purposes, however, section 51 of the Excise Tax Act treats the partnership as a distinct 'person' capable of registration, collection, and ITC claims; the general rule is that all partners must jointly act as the partnership's representative for GST/HST. The entity is subject to territorial scope: Canadian-source supply activity occurring in Ontario attracts the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) at the Ontario rate, while export supplies meeting Schedule VI requirements are zero-rated. The LP does not automatically benefit from any tax-exempt zone; Canadian customs duty relief zones and bonded warehouses may defer but do not exempt GST/HST at importation.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' elective entity classification regime equivalent to Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3; entity classification is statutory, and the LP flow-through treatment is mandatory under section 96 of the Income Tax Act. Available elections relevant to the frozen beef/seafood importing LP: (i) a fiscal-period election under subsection 96(4) of the Income Tax Act to establish a non-calendar partnership taxation year; (ii) the GST/HST quick-method election and net-tax calculation elections under sections 156 and 167.1 of the Excise Tax Act where eligibility thresholds are met; (iii) the closely related corporation/partnership election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for certain inter-entity supplies, subject to ETA strict eligibility; (iv) the non-resident agent election by which a non-resident LP appoints a resident agent for service and customs compliance; and (v) elections to rationalize the GST/HST treatment of imported goods subsequently re-exported, including the use of customs duty drawback frameworks administered by the CBSA. Where the LP has a corporate general partner, attention is required to the anti-hybrid and loss-streaming rules under sections 96(2.1) and 251.1 of the Income Tax Act; no legislative election, however, can convert the LP into an entity-level taxpayer for Canadian income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - Under subsection 240(1) and section 248 of the Excise Tax Act, a person ceases to be a 'small supplier' and must register once worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters (or a fiscal quarter for certain non-residents). The small-supplier threshold is unavailable if the non-resident partnership has a permanent establishment in Canada, and an Ontario LP carrying on an import wholesale business is ordinarily required to register regardless of revenue because the goods are acquired for commercial resale and the GST/HST paid at customs is only creditable to a registrant. Voluntary registration before the threshold is recommended to preserve ITC recovery on imported frozen beef and pelagic fish.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 24 of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI, Part V (sections 12 and 13), a supply of tangible personal property (including frozen beef carcasses and pelagic fish) is zero-rated when the supplier sells the property to a non-resident and the supplier or the supplier's agent exports the property in the course of the supply, with the destination outside Canada specified in the contract. The zero-rating applies only to the sale by the importer of record; the inbound importation itself is subject to GST/HST at the 13% Ontario HST, recoverable as an input tax credit by the registered partnership. The exporter must ensure that the goods are exported as soon as is reasonably possible following the supply and are not consumed, used, or further processed in Canada before export; domestic repacking in the original state does not defeat zero-rating provided the same identifiable goods are physically shipped abroad. Documentation must demonstrate the identity of the goods (HS 0202 frozen bovine carcasses/cuts and HS 0303 frozen pelagic fish), the non-resident purchaser, the export destination, and the actual export movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the authorized service provider, referencing the HS codes and the LP's RM program account number
  • Bill of Lading (house or master, ocean/air) or equivalent transport document naming the non-resident consignee and the foreign port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank proof of foreign exchange receipt (e.g., SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or LC settlement advice) matching the exported shipment in amount and currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List for the exported shipment, cross-referencing the purchase order, HS classification, cold-chain storage certificate, and the original import transaction
  • CBSA customs release documentation confirming the original import accounting declaration (CARM/B3-3) and the duty/GST/HST assessed at importation
  • CFIA export certificate (e.g., meat hygiene certificate or fish export certificate) where applicable, evidencing that the frozen product was not diverted from the export market

Canada does not require a continuous transaction control (CTC) or government-digital-stamping e-invoicing clearance regime comparable to Latin American or EU models. The Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing trust framework and the CRA-supported Business Payments / e-invoice standards are voluntary in Ontario. Mandatory electronic obligations nonetheless exist: (i) GST/HST registrants generally must file returns electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; (ii) all customs importers must use the CBSA Single Window Initiative, filing the electronic B3-3 accounting declaration, cargo control documents, and commercial invoices through the CARM Client Portal or certified EDI/API interfaces; (iii) the importer of record must maintain a CARM Client Portal account and post financial security as of the full CARM Release 2 mandatory date; and (iv) customs brokers require a valid non-resident importer power of attorney filed with CBSA. No real-time fiscal API stamp authorization applies to wholesale cold-chain meat import invoices, but the electronic documents must be retained in a readable format for CRA audit.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims for the frozen beef/seafood trade. If the exporter fails to produce the export declaration, B/L, and FX receipt within a reasonable period, CRA will deny the zero-rating, re-assess the supply at 13% HST, and disallow the corresponding input tax credits, with interest running from the original due date. The gross-negligence penalty under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act is the greater of CAD 250 and 15% of the net-tax understated; knowingly filing a false or deceptive export supply statement additionally attracts penalties under sections 285 and 286 of the ETA. Fraudulent export schemes may be prosecuted under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years, or under the Criminal Code. The normal reassessment window is four years, but it extends to ten years where the partnership, or a partner acting on its behalf, has misrepresented facts or committed wilful omission, neglect, or fraud, in which case the CBSA may also issue Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per contravention and seek forfeiture of in-transit goods. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is the operative substantiation window; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers heightened documentary rebuttal requirements and may result in retrospective clawbacks of the zero-rated ITCs on audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible; Canada maintains no capital controls, no exchange control legislation, and permits unrestricted current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation from a Canadian LP are free of FX approval, subject only to ordinary tax withholding and AML documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement. Exporters of goods, including frozen beef and seafood, may retain proceeds in any currency or convert freely; no statutory timeline or ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); currency oversight and AML supervision for financial institutions under OSFI and FINTRAC regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail: Canadian non-bank fintech EMIs cannot accept insured deposits, are not OSFI-licensed banks, and cannot issue native CAD accounts without a partner bank sponsorship. They may be used as ancillary payment corridors for isolated cross-border transfers, but not for holding LP capital or executing trade settlements at scale. High regulatory friction exists with offshore EMIs lacking a Canadian MSB licence.
