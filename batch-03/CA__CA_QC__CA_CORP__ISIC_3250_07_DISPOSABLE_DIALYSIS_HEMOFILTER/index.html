Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital required under QBCA; shares may be issued for any consideration authorized by the directors. No statutory escrow account is mandated. If the corporation is constituted under the CBCA, a minimum of one director and no minimum capital likewise applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST and provincial tax administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Revenu Québec CLIE (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Quebec must register proactively with the CRA for GST/HST using Form RC1 (Business Number account registration) and with Revenu Québec for QST by filing the Application for Registration (form LM-1-V / SD-1), providing Articles of Incorporation, proof of place of business, and director/officer identification. A Business Number (BN) plus program accounts (RT for GST/HST; RM for payroll; RC for corporate income tax) are required before invoicing taxable supplies in Canada. For Quebec, a Revenu Québec registration number and QST number are separately assigned; the CRA BN serves as the base federal TIN, while Revenu Québec uses its own distinctive numeric identifier. Out-of-province and non-resident suppliers exceeding the Canadian revenue threshold must also register under the normal and simplified regimes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-controlled private corporation or non-resident-incorporated federal/provincial corporation subject to Part I corporate income tax; no default check-the-box pass-through regime; certain fiscally transparent vehicles may be treated under look-through rules only where treaty or specific domestic provisions apply)

Available election forms

Available elections include: (i) subsection 256(1) associated corporation election to allocate taxable income among associated corporations; (ii) subsection 85(1) rollover election for transfer of property to a corporation at elected value; (iii) subsection 256.1(1)/(2) additional investment tax credit elections; (iv) section 216 election permitting a non-resident to file a Canadian tax return on a property-rental basis; (v) Quebec QST harmonization election under the QST system for inter-provincial registrants electing use of federal GST/HST returns; and (vi) hybrid mismatch elections under Canadian domestic rules aligning with BEPS Action 2 for controlled foreign entities and hybrid instruments, including the anti-hybrid election to treat certain entities as non-transparent where treaty relief would otherwise produce double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any consecutive four calendar quarters; QST same CAD 30,000 threshold for Quebec taxable supplies. Non-resident vendors selling to Canadian consumers may have a higher de minimis threshold unless they use a marketplace platform; Quebec specific digital economy rules may trigger registration at lower thresholds for certain supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods by commercial carrier or sends by mail, retains export documentation, has no unfulfilled conditions, and exports within specified timelines; zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied to non-residents where tangible property is situated outside Canada and services are performed wholly outside Canada, plus exempt supplies of medical devices including dialyzers when used exclusively for human use and supplied to specified institutions under Part II of Schedule VI. Claiming 0% requires documentary proof that the goods have been physically exported to a non-Canadian destination, or that the supply otherwise qualifies as a zero-rated export within CRA Technical Interpretation rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Commercial Export Declaration Form B13A) where applicable
  • Commercial invoice or Canada Border Services Agency accounting document
  • Bill of Lading (through bill, sea waybill, air waybill, or CUSRES accepted carrier manifest)
  • Packing list
  • Foreign customs entry or proof of destination customs clearance, if available
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign wire transfer evidence for export proceeds
  • Shipping order or forwarder's receipt from a licensed customs broker
  • Warehouse or terminal receipts evidencing removal from Canada

Canada does not operate a real-time electronic invoicing clearance or fiscal stamping mandate. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns through the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE using standard EDI or web services, and QST returns via Revenu Québec's EDI/CLIE system; invoices must contain the supplier's BN/RT, the QST registration number where applicable, the GST/QST amount shown separately, and be issued within specified times. No government pre-clearance or XML digital stamp is required; however, records must be retained and, upon audit, be available in an electronically readable format under the Electronic Records and Books regime in CRA Information Circular IC 2024-01 and Revenu Québec guidelines.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit export zero-rating claims intensely using cross-border data and customs transaction records. A supplier who claims zero-rating without obtaining acceptable export evidence is assessed GST/QST on the sale plus interest; penalties include the standard 6% per annum arrears interest, a 5% gross negligence penalty plus 10% on any understated tax, and a 100% penalty for wilful evasion; criminal prosecution may lead to fines of up to CAD 200,000 for GST and QST combined plus imprisonment up to two years per count; export claims reversed retroactively trigger net tax adjustments, reassessment beyond the normal four-year audit window where misrepresentation is proven, and disqualification from the audit relief program for subsequent clearances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada assumes IMF Article VIII obligations and adheres to the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements; capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no exchange-control permit, no central-bank authorisation of trade invoices, and no remittance quota on dividends, royalties, or intercompany charges. Residual frictions are tax-based rather than FX-based: non-resident withholding tax under Income Tax Act Part XIII at 25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% or 5% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention for a 10% or greater US corporate shareholder; thin-capitalisation limits of 2.5:1 debt-to-equity under ITA s. 18(4); and the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation regime applicable for taxation years beginning after 2024.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada; receipts may be retained indefinitely in CAD or any freely convertible currency. The operative obligations are compliance-based: FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting by the bank for every transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, T1135 specified foreign property disclosure once foreign account balances exceed CAD 100,000, and CBSA export documentation together with Export Control List screening if the dialysis consumables technology falls within controlled goods parameters.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act; foreign exchange administration is exercised jointly with the Department of Finance Canada (Minister of Finance) as to official international reserves and with Global Affairs Canada (export permit issuance) and the Canada Border Services Agency (border enforcement) under the Export and Import Permits Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium - acceptable as a supplementary collection and disbursement channel but not as the primary treasury spine. Legal constraints: non-bank payment service providers such as Wise and Payoneer may not accept deposits and must hold client funds in segregated trust accounts without CDIC deposit insurance; the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada, requires registration of payment service providers, with the mandatory registration phase taking effect during 2025; in Quebec, a money-services business must additionally be registered with the Autorité des marchés financiers under the Money-Services Businesses Act; and CAD clearing ultimately requires a domestic Schedule I bank settlement account, so EMI rails increase unit cost without eliminating the banking relationship. Health Canada-regulated medical receivables invoicing is better served by direct SWIFT and letter-of-credit rails that produce documentary evidence for customs and tax audits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred rail is a SWIFT MT103, or MT202 cover for amounts of USD 5,000,000 or more, from the non-resident parent's bank to the designated Canadian account, with same-day CAD settlement through the Lynx high-value payment system operated by Payments Canada on the books of the Bank of Canada. The wire must carry the Canadian institution/transit/account numbers because Canada does not use IBAN, a clear remitter identifier in field 50K, a narrative purpose of payment referencing the share subscription, and documentation enabling the receiving bank to post proceeds to paid-up capital. The receiving Canadian bank verifies the remitter BIC, conducts sanctions screening, and files a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report because the value exceeds CAD 10,000. No exchange-control approval is required, but the company must retain the subscription agreement, board resolutions, and bank credit advices to evidence paid-up capital for Quebec corporate filings and subsequent financing due diligence.Clearing rails are segregated by purpose: direct supplier payments for polyethersulfone polymer, polyurethane potting compound, and extruder components via SWIFT MT103 wires with the Canadian bank's published FX margin; capital-equipment acquisitions via irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 through MT700 with Incoterms DPU or FOB and documents-against-payment; standby letters of credit or bank guarantees for performance bonds; intercompany service and royalty payments via SWIFT gpi to create transfer-pricing evidence under ITA s. 247; and domestic Quebec supplier disbursements through the Automated Clearing Settlement System or Lynx with GST and QST invoices retained for input tax credits. Quebec Annual Declaration (Déclaration annuelle de mise à jour) under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises Annually by March 31 for an enterprise with a fiscal year ending on or before December 31; for a corporation with a fiscal year ending after December 31, the declaration is due no later than 90 days after the fiscal year-end. $0 USD Failure to file the annual declaration may result in administrative sanctions, suspension or cancellation of the enterprise's registration in the Registraire des entreprises. A cancelled enterprise loses the legal capacity to carry on activities in Quebec and cannot obtain the Registraire's attestation for banking, licensing, or procurement purposes. Reinstatement requires a formal application and payment of late-fee penalties.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under both the Canada Business Corporations Act (section 162) and the Quebec Business Corporations Act (section 225), a non-distributing private corporation is not required to appoint an auditor at the annual meeting if all shareholders, unless entitled to vote otherwise, unanimously waive the appointment by resolution each year. There is no general revenue, asset, or employee threshold for private manufacturing corporations; a mandatory independent audit becomes compulsory if the corporation is a reporting issuer, if any shareholder requests an auditor, or if the articles or unanimous shareholder agreement require a higher level of financial assurance.
