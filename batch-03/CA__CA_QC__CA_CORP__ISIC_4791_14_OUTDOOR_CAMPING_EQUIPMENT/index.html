Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), 1500-420 rue Saint-Jacques, Montréal, Québec; online portal: www.registraireentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

Governing statute

Quebec Business Corporations Act (Loi sur les sociétés par actions), CQLR c S-31.1, ss. 10-16 (articles of incorporation), s. 38 (attestation of incorporation), s. 49 (share consideration), ss. 105-106 (directors and Canadian residency), s. 127 (first board meeting); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-44.1, ss. 17-25 (enterprise registration, NEQ).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of incorporation (statuts constitutifs) filed electronically, accompanied by the enterprise registration declaration (déclaration d'immatriculation) and the written consent of each first director.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under the QBCA; shares may be issued for such consideration (cash, property or past services) as the directors determine (s. 49). Quebec requires at least one director, a majority of whom must be resident Canadians (ss. 105-106); the registered office must be situated in Quebec. The REQ tariff for electronic filing of articles is CAD 225.00 (approx. USD 165), plus the CAD 35.00 legal-publicity registration fee for the enterprise number (NEQ).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST and federal income tax, via My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec for Quebec corporate income tax and QST, via Cliquez Affaires portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or by registering online, using a Canadian representative if no Canadian address. The BN serves as the base TIN and is assigned program accounts: RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for customs. For Quebec, the corporation must also obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire d'entreprises and register for QST with Revenu Québec through the authorization form for non-resident establishments.

The BN serves as the base TIN and is assigned program accounts: RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for customs. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections. Available elections relevant to this structure include corporate fiscal-period elections under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act, section 85 rollover elections for transfers to a corporation, and subsection 216(1) elections for non-resident rental income; no hybrid mismatch election is available under domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any qualifying four-consecutive-calendar-quarter period, or the applicable immediate-quarter test; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST under Quebec legislation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supplier may zero-rate exported goods if tangible personal property is delivered to a non-resident and exported as part of the supply, or shipped directly by common carrier to an address outside Canada with supporting proof; zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied to non-residents that relate to exports if the services are performed outside Canada or are ancillary to exported goods. The supplier must have documentary evidence of export and, where applicable, payment in foreign currency at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA cargo declaration (including the Customs Self-Assessment export records)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Proof of export such as customs cargo control number, Canadian Export Declaration number, packing list
  • Bank remittance or payment evidence (e.g., inward remittance certificate from the banking institution)

Canada does not currently operate a federal real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. Businesses must retain full GST/HST-compliant invoices and electronic records for CRA audit. Quebec is progressively adopting mandatory B2B electronic invoicing through its digitalization of invoicing framework; foreign sellers registered for QST must ensure invoice data interoperability with Revenu Québec once the relevant phase comes into force.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating, reassess net tax, and impose interest plus penalties if export documentation is inadequate or the transaction is held to be a sham or misstatement. Civil gross-negligence penalties can reach up to 25% of the net tax understatement or the amount of refund claimed, plus daily compounding interest; intentional fraudulent claims may attract criminal penalties under the Excise Tax Act and possible Income Tax Act reassessment with gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the additional tax payable, together with suspension or revocation of registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating, fully convertible currency for both current and capital account transactions. No prior exchange-control approval, import license, or profit repatriation license is required for dividend remittances, loan repayments, or return of capital from a Canadian corporation. Non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25% on dividends, subject to treaty reduction to 5% under Canada-US tax treaty for substantial shareholdings, and 15% for other cases) is the only material tax friction on outbound cash flows. All payments may be settled in CAD, USD, or any freely traded currency without central bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no export proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory timeline for repatriating export receipts, and no mandatory conversion ratio to Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or offshore accounts, convert at market rates, or hedge forward. Books-and-records obligations under the Income Tax Act and excise/GST/HST zero-rating rules for exported goods apply, but there is no exchange-control filing or surrender certificate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is exercised by the federal Crown through the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, with no separate exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Mechanism rating: Medium-Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as primary treasury infrastructure. Canada does not recognize a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) as a bank or deposit-taking institution; such entities may only supply payment facilitation, multi-currency wallets, or card processing services through a money-services business (MSB) registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For a Quebec-based operational entity, the primary banking relationship must remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank or Schedule II subsidiary. Fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Stripe Treasury can be used as a secondary settlement rail for low-value customer payouts and currency conversion, but they cannot legally hold corporate escrow, settle payroll, or provide a formal bank reference without a Canadian MSB license or partnership with a Canadian bank. In practice, a hybrid structure is available: tier one liquidity in a Canadian chartered bank and tier two floating operational balances managed through a licensed MSB.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership held by a non-resident foreign shareholder without a clear source of wealth

Root cause: Beneficial ownership held by a non-resident foreign shareholder without a clear source of wealth

Defense evidence: Provide certified corporate registry extract from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, ultimate beneficial owner schedule, certified passport copies, a source-of-wealth statement supported by foreign bank statements and audit trails, plus a signed undertaking that the entity will maintain its registered office in Quebec and operate a real retail e-commerce business.
High-volume online payment processing (Stripe/PayPal) creating a perceived customer-not-known risk

Root cause: High-volume online payment processing (Stripe/PayPal) creating a perceived customer-not-known risk

Defense evidence: Submit three to six months of processor payout summaries, e-commerce platform storefront evidence, SSL certificate and domain registration, inventory purchase invoices from known brand suppliers, shipping/fulfilment records, and a written compliance narrative demonstrating customer verification by the processor under a card-not-present environment.
Cross-border wire transfers to low-income or tax-haven jurisdictions for equipment sourcing

Root cause: Cross-border wire transfers to low-income or tax-haven jurisdictions for equipment sourcing

Defense evidence: Provide master supply agreements, manufacturer registration/licence proof, commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, customs import declarations (Canada Border Services Agency), and an explanation of payment routes using Tier 1 bank SWIFT MT103 only, avoiding the use of shell-company intermediary accounts.
Expected high annual turnover in the first banking year without an established credit file

Root cause: Expected high annual turnover in the first banking year without an established credit file

Defense evidence: Deliver audited or reviewed financial projections, current lease agreement for Quebec premises, letters of intent from logistics providers, personal guarantees or cash collateral proposals, annual GST/HST filing history from Revenu Québec, and a detailed board-approved treasury policy capping single wire and same-day clearing limits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from non-resident shareholders, group treasury, or parent companies must be remitted to the Quebec corporate operating account through the SWIFT MT103 international wire protocol, or through a Canadian federal wire via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic CAD transfers. The account receiving funds should be a dedicated corporate capital/escrow USD or CAD current account. Use a descriptive remittance field such as 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'CAPITAL INJECTION - CA_CORP' for bank reference; Canada has no mandatory foreign-exchange purpose code, but bank forms may request an 'Entertainment, retail trade' or general commercial payment descriptor. For compliance, the company must hold a board resolution authorizing the capital issuance, issue share certificates or a conclusively fixed subscription ledger, and file the required Quebec corporate update with the Registraire des entreprises. Any wire exceeding CAD 10,000 or involving a foreign jurisdiction will be subject to standard AML monitoring; the bank may request the original funding bank's SWIFT confirmation and the ultimate beneficial owner's attestation.Primary clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 corporate wire transfers for direct import settlement and supplier prepayment, executed through the Canadian bank's correspondent network in USD/CAD/EUR/CNY; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) complying with UCP600 for bulk purchase of outdoor equipment, with issuing bank confirmation and shipment-based disbursements; and (iii) low-value global payments through SWIFT cross-border payouts or via licensed money-services business APIs for amounts below CAD 2,500. For China-based suppliers, use CAD-to-CNY conversion through the Canadian bank and direct MT103 to the Chinese receiving bank, or use an L/C payable through a Chinese advising bank.
