Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal GST/HST and federal income tax); Revenu Québec (Quebec Sales Tax, QST, provincial registration). Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CLIC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident limited partnership must obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online, selecting GST/HST, import/export, and partnership business accounts. It must then register with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V or the CLIC portal to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and a QST account before invoicing in Quebec. The BN is the federal tax identifier; the NEQ is the provincial registration identifier. Registration may be voluntary where the LP's only Canadian activities are zero-rated exports of goods and services to non-residents.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign non-resident limited partnership must obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online, selecting GST/HST, import/export, and partnership business accounts. It must then register with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V or the CLIC portal to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and a QST account before invoicing in Quebec. The BN is the federal tax identifier; the NEQ is the provincial registration identifier. Registration may be voluntary where the LP's only Canadian activities are zero-rated exports of goods and services to non-residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Under Income Tax Act subsection 96(1), a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners, and no entity-level federal or Quebec corporate income tax applies. The Quebec Taxation Act mirrors this result. Partners may make subsection 97(1) elections on the transfer of property to the partnership to adjust tax cost. For GST/HST and QST, limited partnerships may elect certain fiscal-year or reporting-period alignment mechanisms, and the Quebec QST registration may be aligned with GST/HST registration. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 12.2 and 18.2 of the Income Tax Act apply to deny or defer deductions where entity classification differences create double non-taxation, and no check-the-box relief is available to reclassify a Canadian limited partnership for federal or Quebec tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident exporter whose only Canadian activity is zero-rated export trade may register voluntarily below this threshold; the QST registration threshold in Quebec aligns with the federal GST/HST small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For granular elemental sulfur ore exported from Quebec, a GST/HST registrant may claim 0% GST/HST under Part V of Schedule VI where tangible personal property is sold to a non-resident person and is exported as soon as practicable after the supply, with possession and risk passing to the buyer or to a common carrier engaged to deliver the goods to a destination outside Canada. Brokerage and agency services relating to the ocean transport of the exported ore may also be zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident and performed in respect of movable property that is exported from Canada. QST zero-rating applies substantively in the same manner under Quebec's Taxation Act. No prior approval is required, but the exporter must obtain and preserve evidence of export and, if applicable, inward remittance evidence, and disclose the zero-rated supplies in the applicable GST/HST and QST returns.

  • Customs export declaration in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) - form B13A or equivalent electronic export report
  • Ocean bill of lading or equivalent multimodal/through bill of lading identifying the foreign destination terminal
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or financing bank records evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract with the non-resident buyer
  • Terminal loadout certificate, mate's receipt, or vessel manifest substantiating that the sulfur ore was physically loaded and departed from a Quebec port

Canada does not currently operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance system and does not require digital stamping or API transmission of invoices to the CRA. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically once specified thresholds are exceeded. Invoices must be retained in a readable, accessible electronic format acceptable to the CRA and Revenu Québec. Revenu Québec has introduced mandatory electronic invoicing standards for suppliers invoicing public bodies and is moving progressively toward broader B2B e-invoicing, but there is no general nationwide CTC clearance mandate for export invoices in the dry bulk mineral trade.

If the exporter fails to retain the required export documents or the inward remittance evidence within 180 days, the CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess the supply as taxable at the combined 14.975% GST/QST rate, deny related input tax credits and recover refunds previously paid, with interest. Knowingly making a false statement in a GST/HST return is subject to a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded, and criminal prosecution may lead to an unlimited fine and imprisonment. Revenu Québec applies equivalent civil penalties, interest, and recovery powers under the Tax Administration Act, and joint federal-provincial audit information sharing increases audit scrutiny of exported industrial minerals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a wholly liberalised current- and capital-account regime under a free-floating Canadian dollar. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no residual foreign-exchange controls, exchange rationing, or capital-export licensing requirements exist under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Profit remittances, partnership distributions to non-resident partners, intercompany loans, and repatriation of invested capital from a Quebec limited partnership are freely permitted. The only operative constraints are FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting (threshold CAD 10,000), Canadian Economic Sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act (S.C. 1992, c. 17), and documentary support required by commercial banks under their internal KYC/AML policies. For a dry-bulk sulphur trading LP, a dual CAD/USD treasury structure is recommended to exploit the deep USD clearing infrastructure and to minimise unnecessary spot conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. The sulphur trading LP may retain export receipts in USD-denominated Canadian bank accounts, convert to CAD at negotiated spot rates, or apply USD export receivables directly against USD import/trade payables without any central-bank approval or conversion obligation. Applicable compliance duties are limited to (i) FINTRAC Large Value Transaction Reports for cash or equivalent deposits/withdrawals above CAD 10,000, (ii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration and G13/G14 export reporting under the Customs Act for HS 2503.00 (sulphur) shipments, and (iii) Canadian income-tax recognition of foreign-exchange gains/losses under the Income Tax Act, with the CRA functional-currency election available on Form T275 to reduce FX volatility recognition.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of federally-chartered Schedule I banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign-exchange and anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17); payments-system oversight of the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and Real-Time Rail administered by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 223) now requires any payment service provider serving Canadians to register with the Bank of Canada, regardless of a foreign e-money licence, and to comply with the Retail Payment Supervision Framework. Offshore digital EMIs cannot rely on an EU/UK EMI passport in Canada and must either establish a supervised Canadian subsidiary or operate through a sponsored Canadian chartered bank, which creates cost and operational drag for a commodity-trading treasury. For bulk sulphur settlement via SWIFT MT103 and documentary Letters of Credit, traditional Schedule I banks remain the only fully compliant clearing backbone. Fintech EMIs are viable solely for ancillary disbursements below the CAD 10,000 FINTRAC reporting threshold, such as small freight-adjustment payments or brokerage commissions, and must not function as the principal clearing rail due to sanctions-screening liabilities and UCP 600 incompatibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions and partner loans from non-resident partners into the Quebec LP's Canadian account must route as SWIFT MT103 customer credits from the remitting bank to the Canadian bank's SWIFT BIC, using the Statistics Canada / IMF BPM6 purpose classification for direct investment equity injection or intercompany debt. CAD-denominated injections settle in real time over Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) with same-day finality and collateral-based risk management; USD-denominated injections are credited through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent via CHIPS or Fedwire and remain in the LP's USD operating account unless treasury policy requires spot conversion to CAD. The Canadian bank will apply FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting on any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and will record the IMF purpose code 'Direct Investment / Equity Investment in a Canadian Enterprise' or 'Intercompany Lending'. OSFI and Finance Canada impose no prior-notification threshold for inbound equity to a commercial trading LP; the only filing obligations are CRA and Revenu Québec reporting in the LP's annual partnership return. Documentary trail: executed capital-call notice, general-partner resolution, partnership-agreement excerpt, certificate of the LP, and the remitting bank's validation of the source-of-funds.Trade settlements for the purchase and resale of granular elemental sulphur in dry-bulk marine trades should clear through two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct settlement against invoice, where standard UCP/purpose codes are applied and both originating and beneficiary banks perform full sanctions screening of vessels and counterparties; and (ii) documentary Letters of Credit governed by UCC 600 and issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank, with advice and confirmation through the seller's bank at discharge ports, covering documents comprising clean-on-board bills of lading, commercial invoices, inspection certificates, weight certificates, certificate of origin, and the beneficiary's drafts. Usance L/Cs of 30 to 90 days are recommended to align payment with vessel transit, demurrage, and quality-assessment windows. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) under URC 522 are acceptable for established counterparties with proven performance history. All CAD-side payments clear via LVTS with same-day finality; USD-side payments clear through the Canadian bank's New York CHIPS or Fedwire correspondent. Each disbursement requires an approved payment instruction, the underlying sale/purchase contract, and the bank's trade-finance sub-limit approval, maintaining a complete KYC/AML audit chain and FINTRAC report records.CAD/USD plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards, executed bilaterally with the Canadian bank's FX desk, with tenors of up to 180 days and fully documented underlying USD trade receivables or payables; subject to the LP's written treasury-hedging policy and OSFI margining expectations for non-centrally cleared derivative transactions.CAD/USD cross-currency swaps, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with the Canadian bank as swap dealer, permitted for managing long-term conversion of USD revenue streams into CAD liquidity or for swapping injected foreign capital into CAD operating balances over matched tenors; deviations from the primary hedge instrument require documented treasury-policy authorisation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for ancillary emerging-market currencies arising in the multi-jurisdiction trade chain (for example CNY, BRL, IDR, EGP) where full deliverable interbank liquidity does not exist for a Canadian trading entity; such NDFs are governed by ISDA 1998 NDF terms and carry FINTRAC, OSFI, and applicable derivative-trade reporting obligations. CAD is a fully deliverable and freely floating currency, therefore CAD NDFs are unnecessary and would not satisfy prudent treasury policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 223) now requires any payment service provider serving Canadians to register with the Bank of Canada, regardless of a foreign e-money licence, and to comply with the Retail Payment Supervision Framework. Offshore digital EMIs cannot rely on an EU/UK EMI passport in Canada and must either establish a supervised Canadian subsidiary or operate through a sponsored Canadian chartered bank, which creates cost and operational drag for a commodity-trading treasury. For bulk sulphur settlement via SWIFT MT103 and documentary Letters of Credit, traditional Schedule I banks remain the only fully compliant clearing backbone. Fintech EMIs are viable solely for ancillary disbursements below the CAD 10,000 FINTRAC reporting threshold, such as small freight-adjustment payments or brokerage commissions, and must not function as the principal clearing rail due to sanctions-screening liabilities and UCP 600 incompatibility.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Fiscally transparent LP: no tax at partnership level; combined federal (15%) + Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate = 26.5% for corporate partners; top combined personal rate = 53.31% for individual partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable as a dividend: LP distributions are drawings/allocations of already-taxed or taxable profits. The operative non-resident rule is the Part I allocation withholding: 15% of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income, withheld and remitted by the LP (federal and Quebec), unless the partner files a Part I / Quebec return and pays the tax. A liquidation distribution in excess of the partner's credited capital is generally treated as a capital gain, not a dividend, and may be taxable under Part I only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation under s. 247(4) ITA applies when the aggregate value of the LP's non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in the fiscal year. The documentation must also be provided to the CRA within 3 months of a written demand. A Country-by-Country Report (RC4649) is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of €750 million or more. Quebec mirrors the federal TP rules under s. 1081.2 Taxation Act (Quebec).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. The applicable substance-related doctrines are: (i) GAAR under s. 245 ITA / s. 1079.7.1 Taxation Act (Quebec), which recharacterizes void avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide purpose; (ii) the common-law 'substance over form' and 'sham' doctrines; and (iii) the OECD Anti-BEPs minimum standards. For an LP, the 'economic substance' is evidenced by a real office, Quebec bank account, staff, and commercial risk in the sulfur ore trade; a pure shell LP is vulnerable to reassessment and penal sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Federal and Quebec)

Statutory sanction: Federal (s. 239 ITA): fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years (summary) or 5 years (indictable). Quebec (s. 1047 Taxation Act): equivalent fines and imprisonment; plus civil penalties (s. 163(2) ITA: 50% of the understated tax) and interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted withholdings (s. 227.1 ITA); a limited partner who participates in management loses limited liability and is treated as a general partner; criminal conviction triggers restitution orders, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition exposure.
Late/Non-Filing of Information Returns

Statutory sanction: Federal: C$25/day (min C$100, max C$2,500); increased tiers for large partnerships; C$100 per missing T5013 slip; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax. Quebec: identical daily penalties under the Tax Administration Act; a 10% penalty for a repeated third omission. Unremitted 15% withholding: 10% of the amount plus interest at the prescribed rate (currently 9% CRA quarterly rate).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted 15% withholdings, penalties, and interest under s. 227.1 ITA; Revenu Québec pursues equivalent director's certificate under s. 25.0.1 Taxation Act; continued non-compliance can lead to federal prosecution and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s. 247(3) ITA: 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; plus reassessment and interest. Quebec mirrors this penalty. The CRA may also apply GAAR and issue a tax evasion referral.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner's directors can be assessed for any 15% withholding defect, but more critically face personal negligence liability for approving non-arm's length pricing that causes a s. 247 penalty; civil suits by other partners; in egregious false-documentation cases, criminal liability under s. 239.
AML / Shell Company / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA: administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 per violation (corporation) and C$500,000 (individual); Criminal Code s. 462.31: laundering proceeds of crime — indictable offense with up to 10 years' imprisonment; FINTRAC can order compliance and freeze assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner who knowingly direct laundering face criminal prosecution, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, extradition, and up to 10 years' imprisonment; a corporate director who participates in management of the LP also becomes personally liable for the LP's obligations in Quebec; the 'corporate veil' is pierced for fraud or sham transactions.
