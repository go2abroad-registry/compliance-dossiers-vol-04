Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Québec limited partnership and no escrow bank account is mandated. Each limited partner must subscribe the contribution fixed in the limited partnership agreement; contributions may consist of money or property but not personal services unless otherwise provided, and no distribution may reduce a limited partner's contribution to the prejudice of creditors.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Quebec: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Quebec Limited Partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1, selecting the 'Partnership' account. The BN serves as the statutory TIN and the GST/HST registration number. Because the LP is fiscally transparent, each non-resident limited partner may require a separate CRA non-resident account, while the partnership itself files the T5013 partnership information return. If the partnership carries on taxable activities in Quebec or exceeds the QST small-supplier threshold, it must separately register with Revenu Québec and obtain a Quebec registration number linked to the federal BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; limited partnerships are mandatorily fiscally transparent and are not taxable persons for income tax purposes. Certain elections may be available for GST/HST related-party streamlining or tax treaty relief, but none alters entity classification. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act s. 18.4) operate independently to deny deductions on hybrid financial instrument arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in any calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act s. 23 and the corresponding QST rules, a supply of commercial CGI/VFX production and 3D animation services made to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated where: (i) the recipient is not registered under GST/HST/QST; (ii) the recipient is not an individual present in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (iii) the service does not relate to real/immovable property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Digital delivery and offshore acceptance are acceptable evidence. Related licensing of intangible intellectual property to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada may also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Border Services Agency export document - required only if physical deliverables (hard drives, film masters) cross the border; not required for pure digital transfer
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill - applicable physical export evidence for goods shipped outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice / GST-HST zero-rated invoice referencing the non-resident client and zero-rating provision
  • Signed service agreement or statement of work with non-resident counterparty and offshore acceptance terms
  • Bank transfer advice, SWIFT message, or Wise B2B credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident client

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing pre-approval regime at either the federal or Quebec level. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name and BN/registration number, description, consideration, and tax amounts) and must retain complete electronic records. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing and remittance of QST returns for specified thresholds, but invoices are not cleared through any government system.

If export zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA and Revenu Québec may retroactively deny the 0% rate and assess GST/HST/QST on the full consideration, plus interest. The normal four-year reassessment period is suspended or entirely inapplicable in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. Civil penalties for false or culpably negligent statements can reach 50% of the understated net tax; criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act may result in substantial fines and imprisonment for wilful evasion. Retrospective clawback is effective from the date of the invalid supply and includes all associated penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII; there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on capital injection, no prior approval for remittance of profits, dividends, partnership distributions, or trade-related payments. The only practical hurdles are the bank's client due diligence requirements, proof of source of funds/source of wealth, Canadian sanctions screening, FINTRAC reporting, and, where a non-resident partner receives Canadian-source income, any applicable withholding or Part I tax liability under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any prescribed timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign-currency proceeds in Canadian or offshore accounts indefinitely; the only requirements are accurate books and records, tax reporting in accordance with Canadian tax accounting rules, and compliance with anti-money laundering and sanctions legislation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada has no separate foreign exchange control administration; exchange-control and sanctions policy is coordinated by the Department of Finance Canada, financial institution prudential rules are administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF supervision is carried out by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High as a supplementary disbursement rail, but Low as a primary treasury platform. Wise B2B is a viable secondary rail for low-value cross-border invoices and contractor payments under USD/CAD/EUR/GBP, and aligns with the target payment rails. An offshore EMI such as Revolut Business, Mercury, or Airwallex can hold funds for a Canadian LP, but it should not serve as the capital escrow or main operating account unless it is properly regulated or registered with FINTRAC where required. The legal constraint is that money-service businesses providing services into Canada must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and banks may refuse to recognize unregistered offshore EMI accounts as satisfactory corporate treasury accounts. Therefore, maintain the primary account and capital escrow at a Schedule I Canadian bank and use EMI/Wise rails only for operational disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through the Canadian banking system using SWIFT MT103 from the foreign partner's bank to the Quebec LP's operating or capital escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The beneficiary is identified by the LP's full legal name, Canadian bank BIC, account number, and, where used, the domestic bank institution number, transit number, and account number. Canada does not use IBAN. There is no statutory 'purpose code' requirement, but the remitter must state the economic purpose in the payment details, for example 'LP capital contribution' or 'partnership capital under subscription agreement', and the bank will require contemporaneous evidence of the capital commitment, partner identification, source of funds, and corporate authorization. If the capital is denominated in USD or EUR, the receiving bank may credit a foreign-currency clearing account or execute a spot conversion into CAD. All funds are subject to Canadian AML, sanctions screening, and suspicious transaction reporting; there are no FX controls or exchange administration approvals.Outbound trade and production disbursements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 customer transfers and, for interbank settlement, SWIFT MT202 cover payments. For confirmed co-production financing or larger fixed-fee animation contracts, the Canadian bank may issue an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit under SWIFT MT700, payable at sight or at a deferred tenor, with the required documents being invoices, progress milestones, acceptance certificates, or delivery confirmations. Smaller recurring disbursements to remote contractors, voice talent, and vendors can be executed through Wise B2B or the Canadian ACSS/EFT rail. All outbound payments require an underlying commercial contract or invoice and must be screened against Canadian and international sanctions lists. Canadian banks offer SWIFT gpi for end-to-end payment tracking. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Limited Partnership entity level (fiscally transparent under Subdivision 96 of the federal Income Tax Act and Sections 8–17 of the Quebec Taxation Act); income, losses, and refundable tax credits flow through to partners. Reference combined corporate rate for corporate partners carrying on active business in Quebec: 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); individual Quebec-resident partners are taxed at progressive marginal rates up to 53.31%.
