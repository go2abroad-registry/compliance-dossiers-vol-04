Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian federal and Quebec income tax law treat the Limited Partnership as fiscally transparent, with income, gains, losses and tax credits flowing through to partners under the partnership agreement. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income and on disposals of taxable Canadian property, subject to applicable treaty relief and Part XII.6 reporting where applicable. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership itself is a distinct registrant and collector, with separate deemed-trust and joint-and-several liability rules applicable to partners.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or discretionary entity-classification election exists under Canadian law; partnership fiscally-transparent status arises by statute. Available elections and mechanisms include: (i) GST/HST joint election under Excise Tax Act sections 156 and 156.1, allowing the partnership and its partners to treat qualifying taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA section 272.1 partnership registration and notional-supply elections affecting self-supply and deemed input tax credits; (iii) equivalent QST elections under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; (iv) Income Tax Act section 216 election by non-resident partners to file Part I returns in respect of rental or timber income instead of Part XIII withholding; (v) partnership fiscal-period-end election under subsection 249.1(1) ITA aligning the partnership year with a designated commercial year-end; (vi) mandatory application of the BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid rules (ITA sections 18.2, 18.4, 20.2) without elective relief, requiring the LP and its partners to assess dual-inclusion income and impermissible hybrid mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold. Under ETA section 240 and the QST rules, the CA_LP must register for GST/HST and QST if its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident supplier making zero-rated exports of medical gloves may also register voluntarily to claim input tax credits and refunds on Canadian production inputs, even if below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of powder-free synthetic nitrile examination gloves (NAICS 339113 / ISIC 3250) under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST zero-rating provisions. Eligibility requires: (i) the goods are supplied to a non-resident purchaser and title passes outside Canada; (ii) the goods are physically exported from Canada as part of the supply; (iii) documentary proof of export is obtained and retained; and (iv) no goods are consumed, used or installed in Canada before export. A zero-rated exporter may claim full input tax credits and rebates for GST/HST and QST paid on raw materials, dipping machinery, electricity and production overheads. Although Canada and Quebec impose no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark applied in this dossier reflects standard documentary-credit and commercial-practice expectations; CRA and Revenu Québec may scrutinise prolonged non-receipt of proceeds as an indicator of domestic diversion or fictitious export and may require economic-substance evidence from the LP and its partners.

  • CBSA Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS/G13/CAED) containing the exporter's Business Number, harmonised-system classification of the gloves, quantity, unit value, destination country and mode of transport
  • Commercial invoice with complete exporter and consignee identification, description of sterile examination nitrile gloves, HS codes, Incoterms, currency, payment terms and the statement 'Zero-rated export — GST/HST and QST not applicable'
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a Canadian port or airport to the foreign destination, with carrier routing, shipment date and foreign delivery location consistent with the invoice and export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice (SWIFT MT103) confirming receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the non-resident purchaser, or equivalent evidence such as a matured letter of credit or telegraphic-transfer confirmation
  • Supporting proof of export, including Canadian customs broker summary, port-of-exit validation, destination-country import customs entry, carrier's proof of delivery or freight-forwarder certification
  • Revenu Québec QST export-claim forms and rebate applications (e.g., GST370 or VD-170 type filings) where the LP seeks a refund or rebate of provincial tax paid on inputs, together with the partnership registration number and NEQ

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory online API authorisation is currently imposed at the federal or Quebec level. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed information under ETA section 223 and QSTA article 442 and be issued no later than 15 days after the end of the month in which the supply occurs. Electronic invoices in PDF, XML, EDI or Peppol format are accepted provided they are complete and legible. Revenu Québec is phasing in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting for large enterprises from fiscal years beginning on or after 2026, with lower thresholds in subsequent years, but no such obligation currently applies to a non-resident limited partnership exporting nitrile gloves.

CRA and Revenu Québec apply enhanced audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate physical export with the full documentation bundle can result in reassessment of the supplies at the combined 14.975% GST/QST rate, denial or reversal of input tax credits and rebates, and statutory daily compounding interest on all unpaid amounts. False statements or omissions knowingly made attract a penalty of up to 25% of the federal tax amount under ETA section 285 and comparable Quebec penalties; gross negligence may attract an additional penalty of 50% of the understated tax. Criminal evasion under ETA section 327.1 and Quebec analogue legislation may lead to fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The deemed-trust provisions of ETA section 222 and Quebec rules can render general partners and, in certain circumstances, limited partners jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and QST. Reclamations and clawbacks are retroactive to the date of the original claim, with no protective relief, and subsequent zero-rating may be suspended until compliant export documentation is produced to the auditors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1952 and abolished its exchange-control apparatus in 1951. Non-resident capital injections into a Quebec limited partnership, and the subsequent CAD-to-USD conversion for profit remittance, are unrestricted and require no prior governmental authorization. A distribution of Canadian-source partnership income to a US-resident limited partner will otherwise attract Part XIII gross-basis withholding (25% statutory rate, 15% treaty-reduced) unless the limited partner elects to report the attributable business income on a net Part I basis as profit derived through the Quebec permanent establishment constituted by the manufacturing plant; a net-basis treaty election eliminates the gross-basis withholding on the distribution. No stamp duty, turnover tax, or capital-gains charge applies to the currency conversion itself. The partnership must maintain Canadian transfer-pricing documentation (Income Tax Act s.247) and a conservative debt-to-equity covenant (not exceeding 1.5:1) to remain bankable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds; the LP may freely retain USD export receipts in a USD-denominated clearing account or convert to CAD at its discretion. Reporting obligations are limited to: (i) the financial institution's FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer Report for every wire of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) CBSA export reporting (Form B13A or electronic EDI) for each export shipment valued at CAD 2,000 or more, with export records retained in accordance with CBSA record-keeping requirements (minimum six years); and (iii) CRA functional-currency tracking if a functional-currency election is made for the partnership's books.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and payments authority, holding no exchange-control function; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transfer reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); export declaration oversight by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); Quebec conduct and derivatives supervision by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) corporate rate on active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII Canadian Income Tax Act for Canadian-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Not applicable to LP distributions because an LP is a pass-through entity; profit allocations are not dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with non-residents triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation (local file/master file) for the LP; transactions below this threshold are subject to CRA discretionary documentation demands if 'reasonable' review requires.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to offshore jurisdictions; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act disregards artificial transactions lacking real substance. Treaty benefits are denied if the main purpose is to obtain treaty relief and the non-resident partner lacks 'beneficial owner' substance. The LP must maintain a substantive commercial footprint (production plant, employees, procurement contracts) in Quebec; a pure booking arrangement with no local manufacturing activity will be re-characterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Mischaracterized Partner Allocations

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax evaded; criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may assess transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment in excess of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue (whichever is lower).

Director personal liability: General partner and managing directors are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unpaid corporate tax and penalties under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; civil restitution claims by limited partners; travel restrictions and asset freezes under the Income Tax Act emergency collection measures.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization disallowing the tax-neutral partnership structure; reassessment of all income to the Canadian permanent establishment; 50% penalty for gross negligence; treaty benefits denied; potential criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham may be personally assessed under Canada's civil fraud rules; courts have pierced the LP veil to hold the general partner liable for 100% of tax amount plus interest and costs; travel bans under a tax-avoidance injunction.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for FINTRAC non-compliance; provincial sanctions up to CAD 10,000 per month for failure to maintain the beneficial ownership register; criminal penalties for false declarations under the Canada Business Corporations Act (as adopted for LP) up to CAD 200,000 and 6 months imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are individually liable for FINTRAC penalties if they knowingly acquiesced in the failure; personal asset forfeiture in serious money laundering cases; exclusion from holding board positions in any Canadian entity for up to 10 years.
