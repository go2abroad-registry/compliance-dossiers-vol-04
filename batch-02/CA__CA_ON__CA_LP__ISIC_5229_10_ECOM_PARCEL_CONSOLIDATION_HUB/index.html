Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html, supplemented by the CRA My Business Account platform. The Ontario Ministry of Finance administers provincial Employer Health Tax and the provincial HST component is collected by CRA, making CRA the sole national authority for GST/HST and income tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity acquiring an interest in an Ontario limited partnership must first obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA using Business Registration Online, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or, where only GST/HST is sought, Form RC226 (Non-Resident GST/HST Registration). The limited partnership itself must separately obtain its own BN and a GST/HST program account (BN + 'RT' code) where it carries on business in Canada and is not a small supplier. Required supporting documentation for foreign investors includes a certificate of existence or incorporation, the partnership agreement, resolutions of the foreign entity, and designation of a Canadian representative for service of process. The partnership must also register provincially under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through ServiceOntario and, if payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000, open an Ontario Employer Health Tax account with the Ministry of Finance.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Ontario limited partnership law, the entity is fiscally transparent: income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who report their shares directly on their own returns. The LP files an annual information return (T5013 SUM and T5013 slips) but pays no federal or provincial income tax at the entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Ontario limited partnership law, the entity is fiscally transparent: income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who report their shares directly on their own returns. The LP files an annual information return (T5013 SUM and T5013 slips) but pays no federal or provincial income tax at the entity level. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on income attributable to Canadian-source partnership operations and may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions of investment income.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a domestic check-the-box or statutory classification election; entity status follows statute and juridical characteristics. A non-resident partner earning Canadian-source rental or timber-resource income through the LP may file a section 216 election of the Income Tax Act to compute tax on a net rather than gross basis. Partners may also align the partnership fiscal year-end under subsection 249.1(4) where the agreement does not otherwise determine it. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, the Income Tax Act (sections 18.2 and 18.3) operates automatically without an elective mechanism, and filing of CRA Form RC312 is mandatory where a mismatch is present; a US member that is an 'eligible entity' filing IRS Form 8832 for U.S. classification does not bind Canadian characterization of the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person that is not a 'small supplier' (i.e., whose worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four calendar quarters) must register for GST/HST; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same registration obligation. The parcel consolidation hub is required to hold a GST/HST account where its group-wide revenue exceeds the threshold or where CRA determines it carries on business in Canada through its Ontario operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada by a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the property is delivered to a place outside Canada in the course of the supplier's commercial activity within a reasonable time after supply (CRA administrative practice generally treats 60 days as reasonable). Services performed directly in respect of tangible personal property destined for export, and freight/transportation services from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada, are likewise zero-rated. The parcel-consolidation hub operator must be a GST/HST registrant, issue a prescribed export invoice, and maintain contemporaneous documentary proof of physical export to the foreign consignee.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or approved electronic export report transmitted through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the foreign consignee, HS classification, value, and destination outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / carrier manifest evidencing physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Carrier certificate of export or licensed customs broker certification confirming goods left Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-exchange settlement record proving consideration was received or credited from outside Canada

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-control (CTC) e-invoicing, digital stamping, or transactional API reporting mandate for GST/HST. CRA requires only that registrants issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (vendor BN/RT number, date, description, HST rate and tax amount) and preserve records in an electronically accessible format per Information Circular IC-05-1; Peppol-based e-invoicing is permitted but voluntary in Ontario. For cross-border goods, the CBSA independently mandates electronic border data transmission through ACI/eManifest and the Canadian Export Reporting System, which constitutes customs clearance rather than fiscal clearance.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within the four-year normal reassessment period, which is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or willful default. Where export documentation is deficient, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 13% HST and the taxpayer is assessed net tax, interest at prescribed rates compounded daily, and a 6% late-filing penalty on net tax. Knowingly making a false statement or omission in a return attracts a gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the net tax understated under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act; deliberate evasion under section 327 is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment for up to five years and/or an unlimited fine. Partners may be held jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations under section 272.1 of the Excise Tax Act, although a limited partner's exposure is generally confined to its capital contribution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, including dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and limited-partner distributions, are freely remittable in CAD or any convertible currency, subject only to withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and standard bank reporting. Capital movements are generally unrestricted; non-resident equity injections into a Canadian limited partnership are not exchange-control reviewed. Certain greenfield investments and acquisitions of control of a Canadian business are instead reviewable under the Investment Canada Act, but routine LP capital contributions for a logistics facility fall below the national-security review threshold unless sector-sensitive or state-owned enterprise involvement exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, repatriate, or hold export proceeds in a specified currency or within a specified timeline. Export receipts may be retained in the original currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. However, for corporate tax compliance in Ontario, all worldwide income of the LP is reportable to the Canada Revenue Agency, and unrealized FX gains or losses must be tracked under the Income Tax Act. No CAD conversion ratio applies to export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; prudential bank supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency and no ex ante approvals for FX transactions under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Financial Administration Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canadian-domiciled payment service providers and fintech platforms such as Airwallex or Payoneer can serve as supplementary collection and disbursement rails for cross-border parcel payments, but they are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and are not eligible to hold statutory escrow or trust accounts without a licensed trustee arrangement. Offshore digital EMIs are generally accepted only for ancillary treasury functions; major Canadian banks may treat them as a heightened AML risk if settlement flows are routed through an unregulated intermediary. Any EMI must be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, for domestic payment services, must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act. Use of offshore EMI accounts as primary operating accounts is legally viable but operationally constrained by CDIC deposit insurance limits and by the Canadian bank's demand for direct clearing relationships.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound limited-partner capital contributions must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 direct wire to the Canadian bank's beneficiary account using the LP's CAD or USD account number and SWIFT/BIC. For CAD-denominated receipts, the bank settles through Payments Canada Lynx, Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system. Compliant inbound payments should include remittance information identifying the contributing limited partner, reference the capital call notice and subscription agreement, and use a clear purpose description such as 'Equity contribution to Ontario limited partnership #xxx for parcel consolidation hub'. Funds arriving from non-resident LP accounts are verified via SWIFT cover or third-party name checks. No FX control waiver is needed, but the bank will ask for supporting documents at onboarding and will screen each transfer against FINTRAC, OFAC, and OSFI expectations. Where funds are in a non-USD/CAD currency, the Canadian bank will request a purpose code or short-form natural identity; the LP should disclose the original currency and state whether a spot conversion is requested. Investment Canada Act notification is generally not required for a passive capital subscription into an existing Ontario LP, but a Form A or national security notification may be necessary if the investor is a state-owned enterprise or if the facilities are near a military installation or critical infrastructure.Trade settlement to freight forwarders, air and ocean carriers, customs brokers, warehousing partners, and international e-commerce suppliers should be made via SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border payment orders or via commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707 series) for substantial linehaul and carrier contracts. For CAD disbursements to Canadian domestic vendors, use Payments Canada Lynx or the Canadian automated clearing settlement system; for USD disbursements within Canada or the United States, use SWIFT USD clearing, Fedwire, or ACH as applicable. Commercial letters of credit should be issued only where counterpart credit risk insurance or carrier contractual terms justify them; the parcel consolidation hub's low-risk AML profile permits normal documentary trade flows with bills of lading, trucking manifests, and customs entry numbers as the underlying settlement records. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 100,000 for other transactions (requires contemporaneous documentation)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific ESR, but GAAR and substance requirements apply for treaty benefits; partnerships must have real economic substance (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) to avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount, plus interest; potential double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they directed or acquiesced to non-compliant pricing; civil restitution may be ordered.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they used the entity to evade tax; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to CAD 2 million for first offense, CAD 4 million for subsequent; criminal penalties up to CAD 5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML controls; travel bans and asset seizure possible.
