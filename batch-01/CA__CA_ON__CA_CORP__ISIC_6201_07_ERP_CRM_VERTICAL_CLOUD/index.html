Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation must first apply for a Business Number (BN) via the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service, by submitting Form RC1 or through an accredited service provider. The BN integrates the GST/HST account number, payroll account, and corporate income tax account. Where the foreign entity only makes supplies outside Canada or is a 'small supplier', registration for GST/HST may be voluntary but is generally mandatory if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. The CRA provides a Service Canada / CRA tax account application with identity verification and can validate the legal entity name and permanent establishment in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general 'check-the-box' election exists for Canadian federal tax purposes; a corporation is a separate person under the Income Tax Act and is not eligible for pass-through classification. Relevant elections include: (i) a non-resident corporation may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to file a return on net rental income from Canadian real property; (ii) a joint venture election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST purposes; (iii) a partnership or trust may be treated as a separate taxpayer or fiscally transparent depending on the source jurisdiction under hybrid mismatch rules; (iv) a corporation may elect to reduce taxable Canadian business income by claiming a 'foreign tax' choice under section 126 or a 'treaty-based' position under subsection 115(1).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible, fully floating currency on both current and capital accounts. Formal exchange controls under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations were abolished in 1951; no central-bank approval, license, quota, or registration is required for inward capital injections, dividend or profit remittance, intercompany loans, or trade-settlement flows. The Investment Canada Act (ICA) imposes only a notification obligation (and a net-benefit review above the 2024 WTO-investor enterprise-value threshold of C$1.335 billion) for acquisitions of control of an existing Canadian business; a greenfield non-resident-owned Ontario software subsidiary triggers no material ICA restriction. Outbound profit remittance is subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; dividend rate reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Treaty for a 10%+ corporate shareholder, with interest at 0-15% and royalties at 0-10% in typical treaty cases); such withholding does not constitute a convertibility barrier and payment in any freely agreed currency is permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for foreign-currency receipts from software/ERP licensing or vertical-cloud subscriptions. An Ontario exporter may hold USD or other currency balances freely in multi-currency accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank or offshore; the only compliance duties are CRS/FATCA self-certification, reporting of specified foreign property over C$100,000 on CRA Form T1135, and controlled-foreign-affiliate reporting on Form T1134 where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises monetary authority and payments-system oversight via the Canadian Payments Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises prudential conduct of federally regulated banks; FINTRAC administers AML/CTF registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value RTGS system, the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), and the pending Real-Time Rail (RTR). No standalone foreign-exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as primary operating banker, High as a supplementary collection/payout rail. Ontario-regulated Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and foreign MSBs (e.g., Wise Platform, Payoneer, Nuvei, Stripe Treasury) must be registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and licensed or registered under Ontario's Money Service Business Act, 2020. Such entities maintain CAD settlement accounts with Schedule I banks but are not deposit-taking institutions: balances are not CDIC-insured, capital-escrow segregation is not guaranteed, and they cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide secured treasury facilities. They are viable only for low-value SaaS collections, small cross-border payouts, and working-capital balances, not for structural capital injection custody.
