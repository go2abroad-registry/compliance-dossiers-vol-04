Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; no escrow account required. Each incorporator must subscribe for at least one share (QBCA s. 19). Shares may be issued without paid-up capital.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical office or server room, obtain a commercial occupancy permit (permis d'occupation) from the local municipality (e.g., Ville de Montréal). Obtain a fire safety clearance from the Service de sécurité incendie (fire department) verifying commercial building codes, fire extinguishers, and exit signage. A structural load inspection is required only if heavy racks or industrial backup generators are installed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; no escrow account required. Each incorporator must subscribe for at least one share (QBCA s. 19). Shares may be issued without paid-up capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html. Quebec: Revenu Québec, corporate tax portal at clicReQ (clic.revenuquebec.ca), with the CRA Business Number (BN) as the federal identifier and the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST number issued by Revenu Québec.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, with a GST/HST account where taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold; registration is performed online through CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC4601 (Non-Resident Corporation Registration). For Quebec, separate registration with Revenu Québec is required for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) account, using the Quebec enterprise number obtained through Registraire des entreprises (Form AR-1/ID-1) and simplified QST registration under the QST Act; Revenu Québec may require security from non-resident persons and generally requires a Quebec fiscal representative for non-resident registrants. The corporation must also file an initial corporate information return (CRA Form RC167) and, if incorporated extra-provincially, obtain a Quebec certificate of registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law; tax classification follows statutory legal form, and an incorporated business corporation is inherently taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Optional elections affecting tax classification include: (i) election under subsection 216(4) of the Income Tax Act for Canadian-source rental income of non-residents; (ii) elective joint-venture or partnership treatment in certain circumstances; and (iii) tax-deferred elections on incorporation/reorganization (rollover provisions under s.85), amalgamation, or winding-up. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss.18.2 and 18.3, as amended) apply automatically to neutralise deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; there is no domestic statutory mechanism to 'opt in' to check-the-box hybrid treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital property) over any four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons supplying digital services to Canadian consumers may be required to register under the simplified non-resident registration regime when the Canadian-source digital supply exceeds C$30,000 unless they are Canadian-resident small suppliers. QST: C$30,000 threshold of taxable supplies (including taxable services and intangible property) in Quebec or into Quebec for non-resident registrants; a foreign corporation with a permanent establishment in Quebec must register for QST when it exceeds the threshold, and non-resident vendors without a Quebec establishment must register and collect QST on taxable supplies into Quebec if the threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST, a supply of a service or intangible property made to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not a supply of a service performed or provided to individuals present in Canada. Commercial web scraping and proxy API services delivered to non-resident customers qualify for zero-rating where the contract, service delivery, payment inflow, and intellectual property use point to an offshore recipient and no Canadian benefit is enjoyed. For QST, zero-rating follows analogous principles for supplies made to non-residents of Quebec and delivered or used outside Quebec, provided the recipient is not registered for QST otherwise. The taxpayer must retain contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident status of the customer, the offshore place of supply, and the absence of Canadian or Quebec nexus.

  • For digital services and intangible property: export contract, scope-of-work statement evidencing offshore delivery, commercial invoice, customer non-residence declaration or certificate of residence, system-level API access logs, and payment settlement evidence.
  • For physical goods (where applicable): Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration), Bill of Lading / Cargo Control Document, commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of export such as dock receipts or customs carrier status.
  • Financial evidence: Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank settlement statement evidencing foreign-currency payment from the non-resident customer within the statutory period.
  • General record-keeping: Full books and records supporting export zero-rating, including underlying contracts, delivery records, and receipts, retained for at least six years per CRA and Revenu Québec record-keeping requirements.

No real-time government clearance, fiscal stamping, or continuous control transaction (CTC) reporting regime is currently mandated for Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoicing is permitted; the CRA supports the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework and is proceeding toward interoperability with European standards, but neither CRA nor Revenu Québec requires real-time API transmission of individual invoices. Billing and transactional data must be retained in a prescribed form for audit purposes (electronic records must be kept in readable format and preserved for six years from the end of the fiscal year). API-based filing applies to GST/HST returns via the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's electronic filing; payment rail data (Stripe, USDC) must be traceable and reconciled.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit export zero-rating claims and will retrospectively claw back input tax credits and net tax refunds where the supplier fails to substantiate the non-resident supply or the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated to Canada within the prescribed 180-day window. Penalties include: interest on unreported or overclaimed GST/HST or QST; false-statement penalties under s.285 of the Excise Tax Act (minimum C$250, up to 5% of the net tax misstatement) and equivalent QST penalties; gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of understated net tax where the export claim is fabricated or reckless; director liability for unremitted GST/QST; criminal prosecution for fraud or evasion, including fines and imprisonment; and extension of the normal reassessment window where misrepresentation or fraud is established, permitting CRA and Revenu Québec to reassess and claw back refunds retroactively without time limitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange-control licensing regime for equity capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Profit remittance from a Quebec business corporation is permitted without central bank approval, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), normally 25% unless reduced by treaty, and to Revenu Quebec compliance for provincial taxes. The practical corridor hurdles are FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers at or above CAD 10,000, beneficial-ownership disclosure at onboarding, and the requirement that the Quebec corporation file annual T2 returns with financial statements to evidence arm's-length revenue and surplus capacity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline on export or service-provider proceeds. A Quebec corporation may lawfully retain USD, EUR, or other foreign currency in a designated Foreign Currency Account at a Schedule I bank and may convert or repatriate at its commercial discretion. For businesses in ISIC 6201 providing web-scraping and API data services, no B13A customs export declaration applies because no physical goods cross the border; the relevant constraints are FINTRAC transaction reporting and the reconciliation of foreign receipts in the T2 corporate tax return.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, ancillary use only. Canada does not issue electronic money institution (EMI) licences; offshore EU/UK EMIs cannot provide a local Canadian clearing account and cannot substitute for a Schedule I bank as the principal corporate treasury account. An offshore EMI may be used for collecting USD, GBP, or EUR from international API customers, but if it provides retail payment services in or into Canada it must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime supervised by the Bank of Canada. The legal constraints are material: no EU passporting rights apply in Canada, deposit-taking without a bank licence is prohibited, and a Canadian corporation should cascade any EMI-held balances daily to its domestic bank account to reduce counterparty, sanctions-screening, and FINTRAC reporting risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Cross-border data-resale and intellectual-property intermediary profile, with perceived sanctions or copyright infringement risk from public-data monetization.

Defense evidence: A written data-source compliance manual limiting the service to publicly available data and lawful API terms; a data-flow map covering sub-processors and customers; a legal opinion from Canadian counsel addressing PIPEDA, contract law, and foreign copyright exposure; contractual prohibitions on personal-data resale; and a SOC 2 Type II report showing technical and organizational controls.
High-velocity USDC or cryptocurrency inflows via fintech/EMI gateways, conflicting with the bank's virtual-currency policy and FINTRAC expectations.

Root cause: High-velocity USDC or cryptocurrency inflows via fintech/EMI gateways, conflicting with the bank's virtual-currency policy and FINTRAC expectations.

Defense evidence: A written digital-asset policy restricting receipts to USDC only, with an express ban on privacy coins and peer-to-peer cash deliveries; a registered MSB or foreign MSB conversion arrangement that settles to the corporation's Canadian operational account; and monthly reconciliation reports proving that crypto counterparties never act as final payees and that all taxable proceeds flow into the regulated banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103, Fedwire, or the investor bank's correspondent network directly to the Quebec corporation's Capital Escrow or Operational Account. The remittance must state 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' as the purpose, with an ISO 20022 category purpose of 'EQTI' or the equivalent free-text 'Equity Contribution', and must reference the relevant share issuance resolution and jurisdiction identification 'Quebec, Canada'. Prior to settlement, the corporate banking file must contain a board resolution approving the issuance, an updated shareholder register, attestation from the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec, and source-of-funds evidence dated within 90 days for each non-resident subscriber. Transfers mislabeled as 'Loan / Related Party' will be held pending a shareholder loan agreement and the applicable Canadian tax elections; mislabeled wires are a primary cause of FINTRAC suspicious-transaction reporting.For outbound trade and infrastructure settlements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 in CAD, USD, EUR, or GBP through the Canadian bank's correspondent network. USD disbursements to United States beneficiaries should be executed through the bank's US clearing arm such as TD Bank, N.A., BMO Harris Bank, or an account maintained at BNY Mellon to avoid double conversion and reduce same-day settlement risk. International ACH/IAT can be used for recurring low-value US payments. Commercial letters of credit are not suitable for the sale of API subscriptions or data services because the documentary record cannot support typical LC presentation conditions; documentary collection is the preferable trade instrument only when a resale agreement expressly requests it. Every outbound royalty, license, or service fee must be screened for Part XIII withholding tax, with Forms NR4 and NR601 filed where required and treaty rate claims supported by a Canadian or foreign tax residency certificate.Deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) executed with a Schedule I bank under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's standard short-form FX frameworkForeign-exchange swaps (FX Swaps) for temporary repositioning between CAD and USD liquidityCross-currency swaps (CCS) for matching USD-denominated API revenue with USD cloud, infrastructure, or third-party data costs, subject to internal hedge designation and board approvalVanilla FX options for transactional hedging of known foreign-currency exposuresNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for currencies with offshore non-deliverable status and are not used for CAD/USD or CAD/EUR, which settle onshore as deliverable pairs

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, ancillary use only. Canada does not issue electronic money institution (EMI) licences; offshore EU/UK EMIs cannot provide a local Canadian clearing account and cannot substitute for a Schedule I bank as the principal corporate treasury account. An offshore EMI may be used for collecting USD, GBP, or EUR from international API customers, but if it provides retail payment services in or into Canada it must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime supervised by the Bank of Canada. The legal constraints are material: no EU passporting rights apply in Canada, deposit-taking without a bank licence is prohibited, and a Canadian corporation should cascade any EMI-held balances daily to its domestic bank account to reduce counterparty, sanctions-screening, and FINTRAC reporting risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services, interest, royalties). Master File required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion. Penalties for non-compliance: CAD 2,500 per month for failure to provide documentation, up to CAD 25,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. Additionally, treaty shopping is curtailed by the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) provisions in recent treaties (e.g., Canada-US treaty). For a web scraping API company, substance is evidenced by real employees, office, and operational decision-making in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they fail to withhold and remit employee source deductions or GST/QST. Civil restitution, travel bans (via court orders), and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying treaty benefits, additional taxes, and interest. CRA may also impose transfer pricing penalties (10% of net adjustment) and issue a 'shell company' designation leading to immediate tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for taxes arising from GAAR adjustments if they participated in the scheme. Travel bans and asset freezes possible under the Income Tax Act's collection provisions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly fail to comply. Travel bans and extradition possible for serious offenses.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income, impose a 10% penalty on the net transfer pricing adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million), and charge interest on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading TP documentation. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
