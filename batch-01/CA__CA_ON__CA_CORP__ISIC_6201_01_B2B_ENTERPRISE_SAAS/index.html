Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; online portal: My Business Account / Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Ontario corporate income tax is collected by the CRA on behalf of Ontario, so no separate provincial registration is required for corporate income tax.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident foreign entities carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the Business Registration Online service. The applicant may simultaneously request a GST/HST account under the same BN if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. Supporting documents include the entity's certificate of incorporation, registered address, directors/residency details, and a description of Canadian commercial activities. For Ontario purposes, if a non-resident corporation has a permanent establishment in Ontario, the Ontario tax registration is automatically integrated into the CRA corporate account; no separate Ontario tax ID is issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident foreign entities carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the Business Registration Online service. The applicant may simultaneously request a GST/HST account under the same BN if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable; Canada does not have a check-the-box entity classification election. Entity classification for Canadian tax purposes follows the domestic legal form and tax treaty tie-breaker rules. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Income Tax Act (sections 18.4, 18.5, 20.2, and 247.1-247.9) to neutralize certain deduction/non-inclusion outcomes. Other available statutory elections relevant to a business corporation include the fiscal year-end election and the election to have a non-resident corporation be deemed resident in certain limited cases; however, these do not alter the fundamental entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000; a person becomes a registrant when taxable supplies made in Canada over the prior four consecutive calendar quarters exceed $30,000, unless the small-supplier exemption applies. Non-resident providers of digital services to Canadian customers may have registration obligations depending on the place-of-supply rules and whether the supplies are made in the course of carrying on business in Canada; the same $30,000 threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service is zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and is not performed in Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, section 9). A supply of intangible personal property is zero-rated when made to a non-resident and is not for use in Canada with a Canadian resident (section 10). B2B SaaS services supplied via the internet to non-resident business customers, with no services performed in Canada by the supplier, generally qualify for zero-rating. The supplier must obtain and retain sufficient evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and that the supply is for use outside Canada, including contracts, IP addresses, and corporate registration details.

  • Service Agreement or Order Form identifying the non-resident customer and the cloud software subscription
  • Commercial Invoice describing the zero-rated supply and stating the GST/HST zero-rating code
  • Evidence of Non-Resident Recipient Status (e.g., foreign business registration, registered office address, and W-8BEN-E or equivalent certificate for US customers)
  • Bank/SWIFT payment confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing the receipt of consideration from abroad
  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) and Bill of Lading - applicable only where tangible media or hardware containing software is exported; not required for pure digital SaaS delivery

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no digital stamping, and no government pre-clearance of invoices in Canada. GST/HST registrants must retain complete transaction records and issue applicable tax invoices; records may be electronic. Certain registrants are required to file GST/HST returns electronically, and remittances above the prescribed threshold must be made electronically. B2B SaaS providers should maintain standard electronic invoice records in a format retrievable by the CRA upon audit.

The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period; if the zero-rating claim involves misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect, the limitation period is extended to ten years. False or misleading statements attract a penalty equal to the greater of $100 and 50% of the net tax under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act. Willful evasion or fraudulent export zero-rating claims may give rise to criminal prosecution, revocation of registration, and recovery of tax, interest, and penalties. Documentation must be robust because CRA audits of digital supplies focus on place-of-supply, non-resident status, and whether services were actually performed in Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates as a fully convertible, free-float currency regime. Both current-account (SaaS subscription revenue) and capital-account (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation) transactions are unrestricted and do not require Bank of Canada or OSFI approval. There are no exchange controls, no preferential/parallel rate, and no mandatory XOF-type conversion windows. A B2B enterprise SaaS entity may hold USD receivables indefinitely in a dual-currency bank account. The only practical repatriation hurdles are indirect: Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax on distributions (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty or relevant tax treaty), arm's-length transfer pricing obligations for intercompany charges, and the non-application of section 17 of the Income Tax Act to amounts receivable from non-residents unless the funds are left outstanding as an artificial foreign investment. Dividends from Canadian-incorporated holding entities may be paid without tax for Canadian-resident shareholders and are subject only to withholding in the hands of non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export or service-receipt proceeds. Section 17 of the Income Tax Act merely imputes interest on unpaid amounts owed by non-residents to a Canadian corporation if the receivable is left outstanding for more than one taxation year and exceeds reasonable credit terms; this is a tax-deeming rule, not a currency-control rule. A SaaS exporter invoicing in USD may keep USD balances in a Canadian dollar or USD operating account, use a USD concentration account in the United States, or convert freely. For Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services reporting, the receivable must be classified as computer services (EBOPS 2010 code 263, cloud/SaaS hosting) with secondary NAICS 511210 narrative.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration and no exchange-control permit regime; the Minister of Finance retains standby authority under the Currency Act to regulate dealings in gold and foreign exchange, but this authority is never exercised in ordinary commercial practice.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility for treasury-grade custody: Medium-to-Low. Canada does not recognize the UK-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; an offshore EMI cannot hold settlement balances at the Bank of Canada, cannot participate in Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS/RTR), and is not a regulated deposit-taker under the Bank Act. Such entities may operate domestically only as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), currently administered by the Bank of Canada with mandatory registration, and as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. A non-resident EMI (including a UK/FCA or EU/EMI licensee) is acceptable solely as a receivables-collection rail with mandatory daily or T+1 sweep of funds to a Tier-1 Canadian chartered bank; it must not be used for regulatory capital, escrow, or multi-day liquidity. Wise B2B is viable as an MSB/payment rail under FINTRAC registration, while Stripe Billing functions as a merchant-of-record gateway with funds settled through Stripe's Canadian PSP; neither is a substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses a SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for USD into a U.S. correspondent) to the Tier-1 Canadian bank's capital escrow account, with the wire narrative stating 'Equity injection for ordinary share subscription — ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210'. Where funds originate in CAD, settlement occurs through Payment Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS with Real-Time Gross Settlement) and is irrevocable at settlement. The bank will require a source-of-funds declaration and will screen the originator against OSFI's register of sanctions and FINTRAC's terrorism-financing list. No exchange-control approval is required. The bank may assign an internal purpose code (e.g., 'equity injection — share capital' or 'contributed surplus'). The corporation must update the CBCA/OBCA annual return and, for Ontario registrations, file a notice of change with Corporations Canada if the registered office changes.For B2B SaaS, outbound disbursements are primarily operating expenses, employee payroll, intercompany license fees, and shareholder dividends. Use SWIFT MT103 for non-repetitive international payments more than CAD 10,000, and Payment Canada's LVTS for CAD disbursements above CAD 25,000 requiring same-day value. For USD-denominated vendor payments, the Canadian bank clears via its U.S. correspondent (e.g., BMO Harris or RBC's US branch) using Fedwire with ABA routing; commercial letters of credit are inapplicable to service transactions and should be avoided except under exceptionally large government contracts. Payroll is routed through the bank's integrated payroll-direct-deposit clearing with pre-authorized debits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 5 million (for goods, services, or intangibles) in a fiscal year; Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and the corporation is part of a multinational group with cross-border transactions. Penalties for non-compliance: CAD 2,500 per month for each missing document, up to CAD 100,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the CRA applies the 'substance over form' doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. For SaaS companies, the CRA may scrutinize the location of servers, IP ownership, and decision-making functions to ensure profits are allocated to the jurisdiction where value is created.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Section 239): fine up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax (gross negligence penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to withhold and remit employee source deductions (Section 227.1). For tax evasion, directors may face personal criminal liability if they directed or participated in the evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the entity as a conduit, deny treaty benefits, and apply GAAR to reassess income. Penalties: 25% of the tax avoided, plus interest. In severe cases, the entity may be struck off the corporate registry.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement. Travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed under the Income Tax Act (Section 226).
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, and criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and 5 years imprisonment for individuals. For B2B SaaS, non-compliance with FINTRAC reporting can trigger a full audit.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs. They may also face imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust the transfer price, impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue), and charge interest on the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the penalty if they signed the tax return knowing the TP documentation was inadequate. In extreme cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.
