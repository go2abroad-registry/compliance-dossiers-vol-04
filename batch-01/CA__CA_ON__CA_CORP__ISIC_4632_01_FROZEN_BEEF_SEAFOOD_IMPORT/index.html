Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario provincial corporate tax; Business Registration Online (BRO) via Canada.ca for federal program accounts; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM portal for import/export accounts.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, then obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through BRO. The entity must open the CRA program accounts applicable to the wholesale cold-chain meat import business: Corporate Tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), Payroll (RP0001) if employees are engaged, and Import/Export (RM0001). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must also register for GST/HST; the importer of record must obtain a CBSA import/export account and bonded carrier/customs broker authorizations as required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, then obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through BRO. The entity must open the CRA program accounts applicable to the wholesale cold-chain meat import business: Corporate Tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), Payroll (RP0001) if employees are engaged, and Import/Export (RM0001). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must also register for GST/HST; the importer of record must obtain a CBSA import/export account and bonded carrier/customs broker authorizations as required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box or entity-classification election. Every incorporated entity is automatically a separate taxpayer at the entity level. Targeted elections available include the GST/HST Quick Method election for qualifying small suppliers, fiscal-period elections under ITA s. 249.1, the capital dividend election under ITA s. 83(2), associated-corporation elections affecting the small business deduction, and various GST/HST elections for self-assessment or division of tax on qualifying transfers. There is no elective hybrid mismatch regime; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies from Canada over the preceding four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); mandatory GST/HST registration applies to commercial importers and any person making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of commercial activity, regardless of the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property, including deep-frozen beef and seafood products, are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier, delivered to a common carrier or licensed export warehouse for export, or delivered outside Canada as part of a supply to a non-resident. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary proof of export within prescribed time frames; a non-resident customer's status alone does not justify zero-rating. Goods imported into Canada and subsequently re-exported without being supplied in Canada may be relieved of import GST/HST through customs duty/Tax relief or drawback programs where applicable.

  • Canada Customs Invoice / B13A Export Declaration (where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent Cargo Control Document (marine, air, rail, or highway carrier manifest)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export and delivery outside Canada (carrier certificate, export declaration, or government customs record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT/LC payment confirmation evidencing foreign currency proceeds

No national real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing approval, or digital stamping mandate exists for domestic GST/HST invoices in Canada. Registered suppliers must issue valid tax invoices containing prescribed particulars, including the BN, invoice date, vendor and purchaser identity, description of goods, and tax amount or rate, and must retain supporting electronic records accessible to the CRA. For import transactions, the CBSA operates the CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) digital platform for release and self-assessment accounting of duties and GST/HST; individual VAT invoices are not subject to CRA API stamping or continuous transaction control.

Where export zero-rating is claimed without valid documentary evidence, the CRA may reassess the supply at 13% HST, deny input tax credits and refunds, and apply interest and penalties. The Excise Tax Act penalty for false statements or omissions is the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be avoided; the normal reassessment period may be extended where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Fraudulent claims can also trigger criminal prosecution, additional fines, and imprisonment, together with joint and several liability for directors and officers in appropriate circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility with a free-floating Canadian dollar; no exchange-control permit, surrender, or non-resident conversion filing regime applies. Profit remittance is FX-unrestricted, but any non-resident shareholder dividend is subject to Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% under, e.g., the Canada–US Tax Convention) and must satisfy the CBCA/OBCA solvency test. All tax and customs reporting is recorded in CAD using prescribed Bank of Canada exchange rates; payment blockage arises only through Canadian autonomous sanctions, UN/OFAC-listed party prohibitions, or FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule applies in Canada: export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any currency, with no mandatory conversion ratio and no holding timeline. For income tax computation, foreign-currency proceeds are translated into CAD at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate on the transaction date; documentary evidence must be kept for a minimum of six years under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority and retail-payment regulator under the Bank of Canada Act and the Retail Payment Activities Act; foreign-exchange conduct supervision is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for chartered banks, and anti-money-laundering/sanctions enforcement is coordinated with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for FX conversion and low-value payment execution; Low as a primary trade-finance and liquidity host. Non-bank electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot accept deposits in the legal sense and are precluded from issuing documentary letters of credit under Canadian banking law; domestic EMI activity is regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with registration at the Bank of Canada and FINTRAC AML/ATF compliance. EMIs such as Wise Business or Airwallex should be used only as secondary disbursement conduits with the Schedule I bank retaining final settlement and LC-issuance authority.
