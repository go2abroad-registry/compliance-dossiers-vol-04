Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, administering corporate income tax (Income Tax Act Part I), GST/HST (Excise Tax Act Part IX), payroll and non-resident withholding; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial: Revenu Quebec (Revenu Quebec), administering Quebec corporate income tax, Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and source deductions; portal: Revenu Quebec CLIC (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). Customs and export reporting: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM and CERS electronic systems.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate a Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or in Quebec under the Business Corporations Act (Quebec) via the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec, obtaining the incorporation number and, in Quebec, the NEQ (Numero d'entreprise du Quebec). Step 2: register the corporation with CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, activating program accounts RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), RP0001 (payroll, if employees) and RM0001 (customs import/export). Step 3: register with Revenu Quebec within 30 days of the start of activities in Quebec through the CLIC portal or the REQ single window to obtain the QST registration number (TVQ account), the Quebec corporate income tax account and the deduction-at-source account. The federal BN and the Quebec NEQ are cross-referenced; the corporation must show its registration numbers on invoices and file its first corporate income tax return within six months after the first fiscal year-end. A foreign non-resident corporation that carries on business in Canada without Canadian incorporation may obtain a federal BN and program accounts by filing Form RC1 and providing a Canadian representative, mailing address or business address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian and Quebec business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the federal Income Tax Act and Part I of the Quebec Taxation Act, taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; no pass-through or check-the-box election exists for corporations. Active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may qualify for the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of qualifying Canadian active business income per associated-group annual business limit, while general corporate income is allocated to Quebec by permanent establishment formula; dividends paid are not deductible at the corporate level. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment is taxable on Canadian-source business income and may incur the ITA section 219 branch tax on its Canadian after-tax earnings, subject to treaty rate reductions.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows statutory and foreign law. Materially relevant elections and statutory mechanisms for the target structure include: (i) the GST/HST election under ETA section 156, and the parallel Revenu Quebec QST election, allowing closely related registrants to treat supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) the ITA section 216 election permitting a non-resident to file a Part I net-basis return on Canadian-source rents or timber royalties instead of Part XIII gross-basis withholding; (iii) the ITA section 85 rollover election for transferring business property to a corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (iv) the ITA section 93(1) election and foreign-affiliate surplus elections for characterizing foreign affiliate dividends; and (v) optional capital cost allowance allocation elections under the Income Tax Regulations. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 18.4, 18.6 and 18.8 apply automatically and cannot be elected around.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports. The same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to the Quebec QST. A Quebec-resident business corporation engaged in wholesale milk powder exports must register for GST/HST and QST once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is generally recommended for exporters so that input tax credits and refunds on freight, storage, testing and commissions can be claimed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, paragraph 5, a supply of whole or skimmed milk powder is zero-rated where the supplier: (a) ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada; (b) transfers possession or ownership of the goods to a common carrier, customs broker or consignee for export to a non-resident; or (c) exports the goods in accordance with the conditions of the Excise Tax Act. Title, risk and possession must pass to a non-resident purchaser, and the physical export must be completed as soon as circumstances permit; CRA will require the exporter to retain documentary proof that the goods actually left Canada and were received by the non-resident buyer. Ancillary services supplied in relation to the exported goods, such as international freight, demurrage and offshore storage, are generally zero-rated under Part V, section 7 if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not used in Canada. Claiming the 0% rate preserves entitlement to input tax refunds, but the regime is strictly documentary and is frequently audited.

  • CBSA Export Declaration or e-manifest submitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including the export entry number and proof of departure.
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or multimodal transport document evidencing shipment to a destination outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, inbound SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign currency purchase price and supporting the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation timeline.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and weight/quality certificate for the whole or skimmed milk powder shipment.
  • Dairy export permit issued under the Export and Import Permits Act by Global Affairs Canada and, where required, a Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) export certificate.
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder records showing delivery to the non-resident purchaser, consignee or destination port outside Canada.

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate for B2B invoices. Electronic invoices are admissible using any reliable format such as PDF, EDI, XML or API, provided the invoice contains the prescribed ETA information: supplier name, GST/HST and QST registration numbers, invoice date, description of the supply, consideration, and the tax amount or zero-rating reference. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST and QST registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million. Export reporting to the CBSA must be electronic through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) before goods depart, and import/export accounts operate under the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) environment. In Quebec, QST returns, remittances and payroll reports are filed through the Revenu Quebec CLIC portal; there is no separate clearance stamping requirement.

CRA and Revenu Quebec jointly audit export zero-rating claims through the GST/HST input tax credit and export refund program. Where an export is not substantiated, both authorities will reassess the supply at the combined 14.5% rate and charge compound daily interest from the date the refund or credit was originally allowed. Gross negligence penalties under ETA section 285 and the parallel QST provisions equal 25% of the net tax or refund sought, with a minimum of CAD 250, where a false statement or omission is made knowingly or in circumstances of gross negligence. Criminal tax evasion under ETA section 239 and the corresponding Quebec offence is punishable by fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and possible imprisonment. The CBSA may additionally impose Administrative Monetary Penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per violation for missing, inaccurate or late export declarations. Ordinary reassessments are generally limited to four years for GST/HST and QST, but the limitation period is suspended indefinitely where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud is established, allowing retrospective clawbacks across the entire open period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully floating exchange-rate regime without exchange-control legislation. Both current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are freely permitted, subject only to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory rate 25%, typically reduced to 5%-15% under applicable income tax treaties, notably the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention), and are payable immediately in any convertible currency. Foreign-investment screening under the Investment Canada Act is limited to net-benefit review for direct acquisitions above indexed thresholds and constitutes a competition/subsidy policy filter, not an FX convertibility restriction. No prior approval is required for capital inflows, and no licensing is required to hold or repatriate foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline exist in Canada. Exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in any currency, maintain foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, and convert to CAD at the prevailing market rate. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC for AML data purposes only, and this obligation is imposed on reporting entities, not as an exchange-control measure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential supervisor of federally regulated banks; there is no dedicated foreign exchange control administration in Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low for primary treasury function in Canada. Foreign digital EMIs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot accept demand deposits or lend without a bank/trust licence. They may operate only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and as retail payment service providers registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, where the Autorité des marchés financiers (Quebec) also applies. EMIs are viable for ancillary FX conversion, sub-account payment routing, and receive-only conduits; they are not viable for capital escrow, LC issuance, SWIFT correspondent clearing, or regulatory-grade settlement. A Schedule I bank relationship remains mandatory for the treasury spine of a dairy commodity trading entity.

Inbound equity injections must be executed via SWIFT/general wire transfer from the foreign investor's own account, with no cash deposits and no third-party aggregation, into a dedicated CAD/USD operational or capital escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remitter must include the corporate account number and a bank-specific payment reference (e.g., 'CA_QC_ISIC_4633_01 Capital Contribution'), and the bank must capture FINTRAC cross-border EFT details where the transfer equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. Supporting documentation comprises the share subscription agreement, board and shareholder resolutions, amended articles if applicable, REQ registration certificate, and bank KYC forms. Canada requires no central-bank declaration for inbound capital, but the contribution must be recorded as paid-up capital for corporate and tax purposes, conversion to CAD is executed at the bank's wholesale rate, and any foreign exchange gain or loss must be tracked for income tax purposes.Trade settlements for raw whole and skimmed milk powder should use (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct telecommunication wire payments to suppliers; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 under UCP600 ICC rules, with CAD legs cleared through Lynx, the Bank of Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system, and USD legs cleared through CHIPS/Fedwire via the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; and (iii) documentary collections (DP/DA) as secondary rails. CAD/USD conversion is executed at the bank's wholesale FX desk or via a recognized matching platform. All outbound EFTs equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are captured in FINTRAC cross-border EFT reports by the reporting institution. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-Low for primary treasury function in Canada. Foreign digital EMIs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot accept demand deposits or lend without a bank/trust licence. They may operate only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and as retail payment service providers registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, where the Autorité des marchés financiers (Quebec) also applies. EMIs are viable for ancillary FX conversion, sub-account payment routing, and receive-only conduits; they are not viable for capital escrow, LC issuance, SWIFT correspondent clearing, or regulatory-grade settlement. A Schedule I bank relationship remains mandatory for the treasury spine of a dairy commodity trading entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services (T106 filing threshold); Master File required for groups with consolidated revenue over CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance to prevent treaty abuse; no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but substance is relevant for transfer pricing and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus interest and penalties

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly participated; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 10% of transaction value, and potential criminal charges under GAAR

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties and taxes if they authorized or acquiesced to the arrangement; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations, criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply; travel bans and prohibition from serving as directors.
