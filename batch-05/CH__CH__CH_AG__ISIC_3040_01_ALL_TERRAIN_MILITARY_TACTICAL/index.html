Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA), Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV), Swiss Confederation; online portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch with e-services at https://ses.estv.admin.ch and VAT-specific portal at https://www.vat.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Swiss company UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) through the central business register database (Zefix) using authenticated proof of incorporation and legal existence. For VAT liability, the foreign entity files Form 004 'Registration for VAT purposes' with the ESTV. A Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nummer) is issued where the entity supplies taxable goods or services in Switzerland and annual Swiss taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, or where the foreign business is nonetheless liable under Article 21 of the Swiss VAT Act. A non-resident AG with no Swiss seat may need to appoint a Swiss tax representative for VAT enforcement and correspondence. Direct tax registration for non-resident shareholders is limited to Swiss-source nexus; the AG itself is not required to obtain a separate income tax certificate for foreign tax residency, but transfer pricing and permanent establishment rules apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Swiss law. An Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily taxed as a legal entity on its taxable profit at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Available elective or preferential mechanisms include Swiss VAT group treatment (Organschaft) through a control election, the participation exemption for qualified dividend and capital gains, the patent/IP box regime, notional interest deduction on shareholder loans, and the reimbursement of capital contribution reserves by means of tax-neutral repayment when properly documented. There is no hybrid mismatch check-the-box election; Swiss Federal Tax Administration circulars and the OECD-related GloBE rules govern controlled foreign company and hybrid instrument treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies connected with Switzerland (Article 21 paragraph 1 left Swiss VAT Act). Foreign suppliers with Swiss-source taxable turnover crossing this threshold must register; certain cross-border digital and electronic services may trigger Swiss VAT liability independent of a fixed domestic establishment, and a simplified registration procedure applies for foreign e-services providers with no Swiss establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the supplier proves that the goods were physically transported from Switzerland to a foreign destination, are delivered to a foreign buyer, and the customs export declaration is accepted by Swiss Customs. For military tactical vehicles and defense hardware, zero rating applies only if the goods are lawfully exported under the applicable war material or goods control license issued by SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs). Offshore services supplied by a Swiss exporter to a foreign recipient are zero-rated where the service recipient is abroad and the service is actually used or exploited outside Switzerland; services directly connected with exported goods (freight, insurance, logistics) also qualify. The zero-rating claim must be evidenced contemporaneously.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice/Purchase contract
  • Proof of export release by Swiss Customs (Ausfuhrschein / export voucher)
  • Transport documents (air waybill, CMR, or rail waybill)
  • Certificate of foreign import approval or end-user certificate for military controlled goods

Switzerland does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or fiscal stamp mechanism. Registered taxable persons must issue bookable invoices containing statutory VAT elements; e-invoicing through Peppol, Swiss QR-bill, EDI, or XML is permitted. For domestic payments the Swiss QR-bill with embedded QR code replaces traditional payment slips. VAT returns must be submitted electronically through the ESTV portal on a quarterly or semi-annual basis. No government API mandate for transaction-level data extraction is in force for B2B, but the ESTV may require access to accounting records during an audit.

If export documentation is insufficient, unauthentic, or incomplete, the ESTV will re-assess VAT at the standard rate of 8.1% with default interest from due date. Retrospective clawback extends to an assessment period usually governed by the 5-year VAT limitation, extending to 10 years in cases of tax fraud or misrepresentation. Claiming zero-rating on fictitious or non-exported goods constitutes tax evasion under the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law, punishable by fines of up to CHF 10,000 for simple offences; for fraudulent export with intent, imprisonment up to three years or monetary penalty up to three times the evaded tax may be imposed. Input VAT deduction is reversed correlatively, and the customs authorities may additionally impose penalties under the Customs Act. Although Switzerland has no statutory inward-remittance deadline for VAT zero-rating, a 180-day commercial benchmark for export payment reconciliation is used in practice to support beneficial ownership and transfer-pricing documentation; delayed repatriation alone does not immediately deny zero-rating but may trigger arm's-length adjustment if retained abroad without commercial justification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible; there are no exchange-control laws, no prior approval requirements, no currency-quantity limits, and no mandatory conversion requirements for cross-border capital movements or profit distributions. Profit remittance is unrestricted for Swiss Aktiengesellschaft, subject only to solvency tests and statutory balance-sheet protections under the Swiss Code of Obligations. Sanctions screening, AML checks, and FINMA KYC obligations remain applicable to all inbound and outbound transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist in Switzerland. Exporters are not required to repatriate export proceeds within any timeline, nor are they required to convert foreign currency receipts into CHF. Exporters may retain or freely convert proceeds through authorized Swiss banks or hedge them in the foreign-exchange market. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply, executed by banks through the Swiss National Bank's reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, SNB) — primary monetary, currency, and financial-stability authority; the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) administers Swiss war-material and dual-use export-control licensing, but retains no foreign-exchange control function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for a defense-tactical-hardware manufacturer. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as Swiss banks and generally lack FINMA authorization to take deposits or operate full payment-account services in Switzerland. Such EMIs routinely reject high-risk defense-sector clients because of sanctions exposure, export-control sensitivity, and AML enhancements. A Swiss fintech license under FINMA would be feasible only for niche payment services but would not replace the compliance and clearing capacity of a regulated Swiss bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and, where applicable, Master File are required when the Swiss entity's gross sales exceed CHF 50,000,000 or cross-border related-party transactions in any category exceed CHF 1,000,000 annually; below these thresholds, a simplified contemporaneous arm's-length analysis must still be maintained

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies an economic substance principle through the federal general anti-abuse rule (Art. 21 DBG), the effective place of management doctrine, and participation-exemption substance criteria; a manufacturer of all-terrain military tactical vehicles must demonstrate actual operational substance, including production facilities, engineering staffing, board decisions in Switzerland, and import/export authorization; purely passive letterbox entities are disregarded and denied treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties up to three times the evaded tax and imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years; civil administrative fine up to 50%–100% of the understated tax; final tax assessment reopened without limitation period

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax plus interest; personal asset seizure, Swiss travel ban, and imprisonment of up to 3 years for knowing signature of false tax declarations
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-abuse conversion of the company's income stream, denial of DTA benefits, deemed constructive dividend subject to 35% withholding tax, and administrative fines up to CHF 250,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be pierced under Art. 52 DBG for unpaid withholding taxes and corporate tax; deregistration from the commercial register, personal travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for tax concealment
AML and Defense Export Violation

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to CHF 500,000, confiscation of transaction proceeds, mandatory referral to the Money Laundering Reporting Office of Switzerland, and revocation of the Federal War Material Act manufacturing and trading license

Director personal liability: Personal criminal exposure under Art. 305bis/305ter Swiss Criminal Code, imprisonment up to 5 years, personal asset freeze, forfeiture, and permanent exclusion from defense-industry governance positions
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax audit adjustment of the transfer price to arm's-length value, supplementary tax assessment plus 4% default interest, penalty of up to 10% of the additional tax, and hidden profit distribution subject to 35% withholding tax

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the additional withholding tax through statutory veil-lifting, subject to criminal aiding-and-abetting charges, Swiss banking disclosure orders, and forfeiture of personal assets in favor of the tax authorities
