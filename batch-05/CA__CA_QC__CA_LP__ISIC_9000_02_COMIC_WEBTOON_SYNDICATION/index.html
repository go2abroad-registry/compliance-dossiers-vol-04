Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax registration; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST registration, provincial income tax, and enterprise number (NEQ) via Registraire des entreprises. Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec's online services.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA and, for Quebec nexus, register with Registraire des entreprises to obtain the NEQ. If taxable supplies are made in Canada/Quebec and registration threshold is met, the partnership must open a GST/HST account and a QST account. The LP must provide partnership declaration details, identify the general partner/agent in Canada, and use the BN/NEQ for all filings. For foreign entities without Canadian establishment, simplified non-resident registration for GST/HST/QST can be completed online.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election analogous to the US. The LP is a fiscally transparent partnership for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax; no entity-level tax is imposed. Partners report their allocable shares. Tax elections available include deemed taxable Canadian business establishment elections, partnership fiscal-period elections, and QST/GST joint registration elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part XII of the Income Tax Act may require adjustments for foreign hybrid entities, but do not permit elective classification of a Quebec LP as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies worldwide or in Canada for GST/HST; CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Quebec for QST registration, with special rules for non-resident digital-services suppliers. A non-resident making supplies through a Canadian platform or carrying on business in Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on supplies of digital webtoon content (classified as intangible personal property) made to non-residents of Canada if the supply is not performed in, or consumed/used in, Canada. Subscriptions unlocked via Apple IAP or Google Play Billing are zero-rated for GST/HST/QST where the recipient is outside Canada and the contract for digital syndication is delivered offshore. Physical exports of printed materials are zero-rated only when genuine export occurs and the supplier retains documentary proof of export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or electronic export reporting for physical media shipments
  • Commercial invoice and contract evidencing offshore recipient and passage of ownership
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill proving physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT confirmations, or payment settlement records from Apple/Google
  • Subscriber territorial data and platform payout reports showing non-resident download or unlock geography

No national real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping regime currently applies to digital webtoon syndication. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices. Businesses must maintain readable electronic records, and Quebec's evolving invoicing/electronic data exchange requirements do not currently impose API-based clearance for low-risk digital sellers. Third-party platform reporting rules apply separately to marketplace facilitators.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete. False statements or fraudulent claims can trigger reassessment, GST/HST civil penalties equal to 20% of net tax deficiency for false property basis, penalties up to 50% of the benefit sought, tax evasion criminal prosecution, interest charges, and retrospective QST/GST clawback plus fines. For digital export claims, CRA audits pay particular attention to IP location, platform user residency, and Apple/Google payout jurisdictions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There is no exchange-control authority approval for profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, or partnership distributions. The practical hurdles are tax-related: non-resident partners of a Quebec Limited Partnership receiving distributions may be subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding or deemed Part XIII withholding, reduced by treaty eligibility; FATCA and CRS reporting obligations apply; FINTRAC large-transaction reporting applies; and the substance of any non-resident partner must be documented. Quebec provincial law does not impose currency conversion restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A Quebec LP engaged in webtoon publishing may retain foreign currency proceeds in its Canadian or offshore operating accounts. Goods-based export declarations under the Customs Act are not applicable to digital content export. The only low-friction obligations are FINTRAC record-keeping and the CAD 10,000 threshold report for large international electronic funds transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada exercising ultimate foreign exchange policy responsibility and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions providing prudential supervision of authorized foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Conditional Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot open a domestic transactional account for a Quebec LP. They may only operate as a payment collection or disbursement gateway when engaging through a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business or a payment service provider registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Direct routing of webtoon subscription revenue from Apple IAP or Google Play Billing to an offshore EMI wallet, without a Canadian-registered intermediary, poses regulatory concern and will be rejected by Canadian banks as open-loop payment activity; the safer model is EMI-to-bank settlement through a licensed Canadian PSP.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal + Quebec general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); 12.2% combined reduced rate for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%). The Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: the LP files information returns only; each partner reports allocated income at its own corporate/individual rate, and the corporate general partner becomes the taxable entity for operations conducted through the LP.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII federal withholding tax (s. 212(2) I.T.A.) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation (e.g., a corporate general partner) to non-resident shareholders. Quebec imposes no additional dividend withholding tax. LP allocations of profit to non-resident partners are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding; instead, the non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income of the Quebec LP (and under Quebec's Taxation Act, subject to treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 reporting threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions in the taxation year. Country-by-Country Report threshold: CAD 1,000,000,000 (approx. EUR 750,000,000) consolidated group revenue. Contemporaneous OECD-aligned Local File/Master File documentation is not separately filed in Canada but must be prepared and furnished to the CRA on demand for any entity above the CAD 1M transaction threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is enforced through: (i) the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 I.T.A.) and the Quebec anti-avoidance rule; (ii) the requirement that non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only if the LP 'carries on business' in Canada through a permanent establishment; (iii) Quebec extra-provincial registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises; and (iv) GAAR's 'bona fide business purpose' and 'economic substance' tests. The Quebec LP must maintain substantial commercial activity in Quebec (employees, premises, server/logistics decisions, board-level management) to preserve its pass-through status, treaty access, and limited-liability shield.
