Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; for Quebec provincial tax: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) which administers the Québec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial income tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation establishing a Quebec registered branch or subsidiary must first obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises (via Revenu Québec) and then apply for a Federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or by sending Form RC1. The application requires: corporate legal name, registered office address in Canada (or province for QST), directors' names/residential addresses, North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) code, expected taxable sales, and a designated contact/representative for non-residents. Once the BN is assigned, separate accounts can be opened for GST/HST (Account RT), QST (Account RD), payroll (RP), and corporate income taxes (RC). Registration must be completed within 30 days after the entity becomes a registrant (i.e., exceeds the $30,000 threshold, or carries on business in Canada).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists in Canadian tax law; a business corporation is always taxed as a separate legal person. However, certain statutory elections are available: (i) an election under subsection 89(11) to deem a corporation to be a 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) for purposes of the small business deduction and reduced QST rate on certain inputs is not elective but depends on ownership; (ii) a 'taxable Canadian corporation' can elect under section 256 of the Income Tax Act to associate or de-associate with another corporation for allocation of small business thresholds; (iii) for indirect taxes, a registrant may elect to use the GST/HST Quick Method and the QST Simplified Method under section 121 of the Excise Tax Act and similar QST rules, adjusting ITC calculations; (iv) for non-resident corporations, an election under subsection 216(1) can be made to tax net rental income on a remittance basis; (v) for foreign subsidiaries, a 'controlled foreign affiliate' loss-stopping election under regulation 5907 may be utilized; and (vi) hybrid mismatch transitional elections may be available under sections 12.5 and 163.1 as enacted by Budget 2021.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15.47375%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) over any four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration for a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada or Quebec if the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of tangible personal property made by a GST/HST registrant is zero-rated if the property is exported as part of a supply to a non-resident person or if the registrant fulfils the export conditions in section 1 (e.g., delivery to a common carrier for export, or export by the registrant itself within 60 days). For Quebec, zero-rating under the QVTA mirrors the GST rules (input tax refund for QST on exported goods is available if the goods leave Quebec and Canada). Heparin sodium (pharmaceutical API) is not excluded; it qualifies as goods for export. The supplier must have reasonable measures and documentary proof of export before claiming zero-rating. Services (e.g., toll extraction or processing of heparin crude) can also be zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident person and, for GST, if not acquired for consumption in Canada; specific conditions under section 7 of Schedule VI for 'place of export' services require all or substantially all of the service to be performed outside Canada.

No government-mandated bipartite real-time clearance or digital stamping regime under CRA or Revenu Québec as of the current date. However, once registered, all GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically (mandatory if annual revenue exceeds CAD 30,000; non-residents and large registrants must e-file). Billing must comply with the Excise Tax Act (s. 223) and Act respecting the Quebec sales tax (QVTA) invoice content requirements (listing supplier BN, QST registration number, the consideration, the tax amounts, etc.). Revenu Québec is progressively adopting mandatory electronic invoicing (E-invoice) for businesses subject to QST effective in phases (per Schéma de facturation électronique du Québec); but as of the knowledge cutoff, no real-time tax authority clearance or stamping is required. Ontario and Alberta have no e-invoicing clearance; Quebec's plan mirrors the PEPPOL framework but remains non-real-time.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit zero-rating claims with regard to the onus of proof. If the exporter fails to provide satisfactory evidence of export, the CRA may apply GST at 5% and Revenu Québec may apply QST at 9.975% on the sale, plus arrears interest and late penalties. Under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, a penalty of 25% (up to CAD 25,000) can be assessed for gross negligence or false statements; under section 280, knowingly understated tax liabilities attract a 25% civil penalty. Revenu Québec may impose similar penalties and levy a 100% penalty for willful evasion. Export claims are subject to retrospective reassessment for six years (beyond the normal four-year limitation period) if misrepresentation or fraud is shown. Where goods are not actually exported within the statutory period, the zero-rating is denied, and input tax credits previously claimed will be reduced by the denied amounts, effectively clawing back refunds. Additionally, CRA may submit a referral to the Public Prosecution Service for unregistered/evasion, leading to criminal fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate and has no exchange-control legislation requiring government approval for acquisition, holding, conversion, or repatriation of foreign currency. Current-account transactions (trade settlements, service fees, royalties) and capital-account transactions (equity injection, debt subscription, loan repayment, dividend/management fee repatriation) are unrestricted. Profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder is not gated by any forex regime but is subject to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)): 25% statutory withholding on dividends, reduced under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement (1986) to 10% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting shares, otherwise 15%; the Canadian payer deducts and remits the withholding and issues NR4 forms. Relevant structural constraints: thin-capitalization ratio of 1.5:1 debt/equity (Income Tax Act s. 18(4)-(6)), the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation rules (EIFEL, s. 18.2/18.21, effective for corporations for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024), and transfer-pricing documentation under s. 247. A Canadian-resident corporation may retain foreign-currency balances offshore without restriction; CRA Form T1135 must be filed if the aggregate cost amount of specified foreign property (e.g., non-Canadian bank accounts, foreign affiliate shares) exceeds CAD 100,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. An exporter may keep USD/EUR proceeds offshore, hedge freely, or settle through Canadian banks without CBSA or Bank of Canada intervention. Obligations are limited to statistical reporting: CBSA Form B13A Export Declaration for each shipment (filed through the Canadian Online Export Reporting System), Commercial Invoice, and the monthly international-transactions declarations made by resident banks for Statistics Canada/IMF BPM6 purposes; this is informational and imposes no conversion duty. The only practical requirement for a Quebec exporter is that export documentation must support the GST/QST zero-rating of qualifying export sales and the permanent records required by section 169 and section 233.1 of the Excise Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing regulation by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (SC 2000, c. 17); cross-border currency reporting by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations (SOR/2002-184); export/import control administration by Global Affairs Canada under the Export and Import Permits Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. E-19).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: SWIFT MT103 from the parent's treasury bank to the CA_CORP operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using the Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada BPM6 purpose category 'Direct Investment in Canada — Equity — Injection' as the reason-for-payment narrative and referencing the share subscription agreement number. The receiving bank verifies: (i) UBO and board-resolution match against KYC file; (ii) the subscription agreement and cancelled share certificate after issue; (iii) for amounts > CAD 250,000, the source institution's SWIFT cover message (MT202) may be needed for settlement; (iv) application of the Investment Canada Act (ICA): a greenfield factory/quota injection by a WTO-member investor is non-reviewable but a notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada is mandatory within 30 days of implementation if the new business's gross assets exceed CAD 5 million; for non-WTO investors the review threshold for direct acquisitions is CAD 5 million (CAD 50 million indirect) and for state-influenced enterprises the 2024 review threshold is CAD 519 million, so the Chinese parent must provide a certified status letter confirming state or non-state ownership. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC approval is required for the conversion itself; the bank will apply its standard spot rate. The entity must complete CRA payroll/GST/QST registration before the injection to permit clean clearing, and the injected capital is to be booked as contributed surplus/share capital in accordance with the Quebec Business Corporations Act. Post-arrival, funds are held in a CAD settlement account cleared through Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS) and may be swept into an interest-bearing corporate treasury account under the treasury mandate.Clearing rails for payments against imported heparin crude and for export proceeds settlement: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct wire for commercial payments, with the payment narrative citing the invoice number, Commercial Invoice value, and CBSA entry number; settlement through the bank's global payments system, with the bank reporting the transaction to Statistics Canada under BPM6 category 'Goods — Import — General Merchandise'. (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (irrevocable, at sight or 90 days) issued via SWIFT MT700 by the Canadian bank at the request of CA_CORP in favour of the supplier, with presentation of documents (B/L, packing list, health certificate, Certificate of Analysis, commercial invoice); settlement upon presentation is executed via MT103/MT202 to the presenting bank, with the LC structured to satisfy UCP 600 and the clean-document requirements of Health Canada's Good Importation Practices for licensed API. The LC facility requires a board-approved Trade Finance Agreement, a revolving credit commitment with a standby-line, and compliance with OSFI leverage/liquidity guidelines. (3) Outbound receipts: export of finished LMWH to the US/EU should be settled by inbound MT103 or by the LC mechanism; proceeds may be retained in a USD concentration account for matching settlement against China/USD supplier payments, with no forced conversion. All transaction monitoring is continuous; any deviation from the approved price/titer corridor triggers an AML/LCT escalation rule to the bank's compliance unit.Deliverable USD/CAD FX forwards (plain vanilla) under a bilateral ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex with the Canadian bank; hedge effectiveness documentation required under IFRS 9 or ASPE 1650.European-style USD/CAD FX options (call/put) involving upfront premium and credit-line usage, permitted under the Bank Act Schedule I derivatives powers and OSFI Derivatives Sound Practices guidelines.USD/CAD cross-currency interest rate swaps, permitted to hedge the interest-rate and currency exposure arising from a USD trade finance facility; notional amount limited to the underlying debt and subject to board-approved Treasury Policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally available in the Canadian OTC market but are non-customary for CAD/USD given full convertibility; a bank will only offer an NDF as a bespoke trade — not recommended, use deliverable forwards exclusively.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for spot FX conversion, small-value supplier payments, and travel/expense disbursements; LOW for trade settlement and letters of credit. Cross-border EMIs accessible in Quebec (Wise Canada, Payoneer, Airwallex Canada, OFX, Knightsbridge Foreign Exchange) are Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) came into force on September 30, 2024, are required to register with the Bank of Canada as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) within the transitional window ending September 29, 2025. Legal constraints: EMIs are not banks, may not take deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit under the Bank Act; therefore they are not a permitted substitute for Schedule I bank account and LC rails for the heparin crude import programme. Use an EMI only as an overlay to obtain mid-market FX rates on non-trade wire payments below CAD 50,000 per transaction and only after verifying the entity's FINTRAC registration number and RPAA registration status.
