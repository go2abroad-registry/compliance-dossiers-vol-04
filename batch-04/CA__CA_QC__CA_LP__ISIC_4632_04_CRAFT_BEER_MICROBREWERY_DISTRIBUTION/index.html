Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - flow-through taxpayer for federal income tax (Income Tax Act s. 96); the LP is fiscally transparent and profits are allocated to and taxed at the partner level; a non-resident limited partner is taxable on Canadian-source business income only to the extent the partnership carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada or Quebec (ITA s. 253; Quebec Taxation Act s. 22); for GST/HST and QST purposes the partnership is treated as a separate registrant person; no territorial or tax-exempt-zone treatment applies to ordinary Quebec wholesale alcohol activities.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the governing partnership law. Available federal and Quebec elections include: (i) combined GST/HST and QST registration and joint remittance election with Revenu Quebec; (ii) optional GST/HST registration for a small supplier (ETA s. 240(2.1)); (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations (not relevant to this LP); (iv) partnership fiscal-period and capital-cost allowance elections affecting the computation of partner income; (v) Quebec QST group election for qualifying corporations; (vi) hybrid-mismatch and anti-deferral rules under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6, which operate mechanically rather than electively, so no neutrality election is available to shield cross-border deductible payments from recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold measured over the current calendar quarter and the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST; a non-resident LP making taxable beer supplies in Canada or Quebec must register once the threshold is exceeded; the wholesale revenue volume of this craft beer distribution LP will exceed CAD 30,000 in the first operating quarter, making compulsory registration immediate; voluntary registration remains available below the threshold (ETA s. 240).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Beer supplied to a foreign recipient qualifies for zero-rating where possession passes in Canada and the goods are exported as soon as is reasonably possible after delivery (ETA Sch. VI Part V; QST mirror rules in QSTA ss. 26 to 28.0.1). The LP must retain documentary evidence that the export occurred (shipment from Canada to a foreign destination and foreign customs clearance) and that the supply was made for use outside Canada; ancillary cold-chain freight and insurance services supplied to the non-resident buyer are also zero-rated where they form part of the exported supply; sales to US and other NAFTA/foreign buyers are zero-rated rather than exempt; a qualifying export permits the LP to claim input tax credits and Quebec input tax refunds in the same return cycle.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or G13, or an outward foreign cargo manifest evidencing actual exportation from Canada
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, air waybill or CMR issued to the LP showing a foreign destination and a consignee outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, keg-return evidence, certificate of analysis, and Excise Act, 2001 removal documentation for the alcoholic beverage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: SWIFT MT103, bank FX conversion voucher, or equivalent proof that the export sale proceeds were repatriated to the LP's Canadian bank account on time

Canada and Quebec do not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API tax pre-clearance, or digital stamping for B2B beer wholesale invoices. CRA and Revenu Quebec accept EDI and Peppol-based Pan-Canadian e-invoicing on a voluntary basis; exporters must report shipments through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), with G13-equivalent declarations and PARS/ACI e-manifest data for transported goods; Quebec SAQ packaging and distribution rules impose machine-readable labels and excise stamps, but these are not fiscal e-invoicing stamps.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reverse the zero-rating on reassessment where the documentary bundle is incomplete, restoring GST/HST and QST at 14.975% on the invoice value plus arrears interest and penalties; the false statement penalty is 25% of the net tax (50% for wilful misstatement) under ETA s. 285.1 and equivalent QST penalty provisions; tax evasion carries criminal fines up to 200% of the tax at risk and imprisonment under ETA s. 327.1; the 180-day repatriation covenant is a programmatic compliance condition consistent with CRA administrative expectations for export proceeds, and failure to repatriate within the deadline may trigger income inclusion for the non-resident partners, Part XIII withholding exposure, denial or clawback of input tax credits on the exported kegs, and retrospective recovery of previously credited refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account items. Profit distributions, capital repatriation, repayment of shareholder loans, and partnership redemptions are freely permitted without exchange control authorization. Non-resident limited partners receive CAD or foreign currency remittances without Canadian Foreign Exchange Act approval, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax and reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act, Canada Revenue Agency notification requirements, and FINTRAC large cross-border wire transaction reporting thresholds. The Canadian Dollar is a free-floating currency with no surrender or conversion quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed timeline for export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, deposited to a foreign-currency bank account licensed under the Bank Act, or converted at commercial rates at the exporter's discretion. Records of export invoices, proof of receipt, and underlying sales contracts must be retained for Income Tax Act and Excise Act purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); there is no separate Foreign Exchange Administration, as Canada operates under a market-determined exchange rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary payment services provider, Low as a primary settlement bank. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not Canadian chartered banks and cannot lawfully hold Canadian client deposits for business treasury settlement under the Bank Act. A Quebec Limited Partnership may use an authorized FX payment platform for cross-border payment execution, but the primary account must sit with a Canadian regulated deposit-taking institution or a registered foreign bank branch. Legal constraints include unauthorized deposit-taking exposure, FINTRAC MSB registration obligations if the platform handles Canadian funds, and uncertainty in Canadian insolvency law regarding funds held by unregulated EMIs.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
T5013 Summary — Box 20 (Active business income/loss) Total partnership income (loss) from active cold-chain wholesale beer distribution, before allocation to partners. Non-resident partners: report only the Canadian-source portion of that business income; the amount is treated as Canadian-source business income of the partner, attracting Part I filing obligations and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding. Late T5013 return: CAD 25 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500). Per unpaid/misfiled T5013 slip: CAD 50 per slip per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per slip), plus arrears interest on any unreported partner tax. A false statement can attract the s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the federally evaded amount.
T5013 slip — Box 21 (Inactive business income/loss) Income from investments conducted outside the active distribution business (e.g., interest on corporate deposits). For a non-resident limited partner, investment-type income may be a passive Part XIII source (25% statutory) rather than Part I active business income; review treaty character rules. Late T5013 return: CAD 25 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500). Per unpaid/misfiled T5013 slip: CAD 50 per slip per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per slip), plus arrears interest on any unreported partner tax. A false statement can attract the s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the federally evaded amount.
T5013 Summary — Schedule 50 (Allocation to partners) Allocable share of business income, taxable capital gains, charitable donations and tax credits attributed to each partner in accordance with the LP agreement. A non-resident partner's allocable share is calculated using the LP agreement's economic-sharing ratio; the LP must collect the partner's name, residence and TRC (if available). Late T5013 return: CAD 25 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500). Per unpaid/misfiled T5013 slip: CAD 50 per slip per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per slip), plus arrears interest on any unreported partner tax. A false statement can attract the s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the federally evaded amount.
