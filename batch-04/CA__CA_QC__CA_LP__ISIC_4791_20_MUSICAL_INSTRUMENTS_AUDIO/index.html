Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership in Quebec; no escrow account requirement. Capital contributions are fixed contractually in the partnership agreement and may be payable in cash or property; a limited partner generally may not contribute services as part of its capital contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec provincial income tax, QST, and registrations. Portals: CRA My Business Account; Revenu Québec Cliens et partenaires d'affaires.

E-government filing portal

A limited partnership with non-resident partners carrying on business in Quebec must first obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC348 (Request for Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The general partner or designated representative submits the partnership agreement, addresses of partners, and estimated annual worldwide taxable supplies. After obtaining the BN, the LP must register separately with Revenu Québec for the QST using the applicable QST registration form and, if relevant, a Quebec tax account; no minimum capital is required for the LP, but the partnership must maintain a Canadian/Quebec business address and authorized representative for service.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; for federal and Quebec income tax purposes, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay entity-level tax; each partner reports allocable income or loss. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; for federal and Quebec income tax purposes, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay entity-level tax; each partner reports allocable income or loss. For GST/HST and QST purposes, however, the LP is itself treated as a registrant/person and must collect and remit indirect taxes on taxable supplies made in Canada/Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch classification election; limited partnership status is mandatory under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act. Available ancillary elections include a partnership fiscal-period election under ITA section 96(3) and related-party GST/HST elections under ETA section 156. Anti-hybrid rules may nevertheless deny deductions where treaty or foreign law creates a different entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000: GST/HST registration is mandatory under the Excise Tax Act once worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000; QST registration is likewise mandatory once the worldwide annual taxable turnover exceeds C$30,000, subject to the small-supplier rules and non-resident supplier conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property, including musical instruments and studio audio gear, is zero-rated for GST/HST and QST when the LP exports the goods from Canada within the prescribed time and retains contemporaneous evidence of export. The sale must be made to a non-resident, and possession of the goods must transfer outside Canada through shipment by common carrier, courier, or domestic/international mail. Offshore services to non-residents may also be zero-rated when not performed in Canada and not related to Canadian real property; the LP should document the destination jurisdiction and each export transaction separately.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA B13A/RDEE or the courier's consolidated export manifest for low-value shipments below the C$2,000 export reporting threshold, showing the goods left Canada.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or postal customs declaration evidencing delivery to the non-resident buyer outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign bank payment confirmation/credit slip demonstrating receipt of funds from the non-resident customer.

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping mandate for e-invoicing. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they contain the required GST/HST/QST information (seller name, BN, QST registration number, invoice date, description, and tax amounts). Invoices may be transmitted by EDI, API, PDF, or similar systems, and records must be retained for at least six years and made available electronically to CRA and Revenu Québec upon audit. No prior government authorization is required for using invoice portals.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict CRA and Revenu Québec audit scrutiny; insufficient export documentation leads to reassessment of full GST/HST and QST plus interest and penalties. CRA may reassess within 4 years from the original return, and indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Civil penalties for false or negligent zero-rating claims include gross-negligence penalties, fines up to C$25,000, and, where applicable, the ETA false-statement penalty of 25% of the tax that would be payable; serious fraud can result in criminal prosecution, additional fines, and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks may also include revocation of input tax credits and denial of refunds previously issued for fraudulent export transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully convertible current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII. There are no statutory restrictions on converting CAD into foreign currency or on remitting partnership profits, trade proceeds, or capital distributions to non-residents. Practical hurdles are limited to compliance evidence: Canadian banks require clear source-of-funds documentation, tax information for non-resident partners, and adherence to FINTRAC monitoring thresholds. For a Quebec limited partnership engaged in online retail of musical instruments and audio gear, neither federal nor provincial law imposes prior approval for cross-border payments, but the partnership must file applicable Canadian income tax information returns and NR4/T106 forms to support non-resident distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-period requirements on export proceeds. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained in a multi-currency bank account opened with a Canadian Schedule I bank, or converted at the partnership's discretion. For transactions equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, the limited partnership must retain underlying commercial evidence such as invoices, customs declarations, and packing lists to satisfy FINTRAC record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control administration is distributed between FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering reporting and OSFI for prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions. Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange-control licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs and quasi-banking payment platforms are permitted only as secondary collection or vendor-payment channels, not as the primary treasury repository. To provide account-like services to a Quebec limited partnership, an EMI must be registered as a money services business in Canada, satisfy FINTRAC AML/ATF requirements, and if operating in Quebec, comply with the AMF's licensing regime. Legal constraints include lack of CDIC deposit insurance, inability to issue Canadian USD clearing documents under Payments Canada rules, and greater operational risk from using unregulated offshore settlement agents. Primary liquidity must remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be executed through the Canadian clearing system using the partnership's target Canadian bank account. Recommended protocol: remitting partner issues a SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008.mo9 wire instruction to a Canadian Schedule I bank, with field 70 clearly stating 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' and the partnership's Quebec establishment identifier. Use payment purpose code CAPC (capital contribution) and include the full legal name of the limited partnership as beneficiary. For CAD-denominated transfers, Canadian banks clear via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR), providing same-day irrevocable settlement. Verification must include matching the remitter's name against the LP agreement, confirming the source-of-funds declaration, and documenting the FX conversion rate if the injection is denominated in USD, EUR, or GBP.Outbound trade disbursements for inventory purchases should use SWIFT MT103 bank wires for standard supplier payments, with clear commercial invoice references and purpose code TRCP (trade payments) where accepted by the beneficiary bank. For higher-value or higher-risk manufacturing contracts, the Canadian bank may issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit in accordance with ICC UCP600, allowing suppliers to obtain payment through negotiated documents. Domestic Canadian disbursements in CAD settle through Payments Canada's ACSS or LVTS rails.
