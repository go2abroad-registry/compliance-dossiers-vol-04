Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax matters; Ontario HST is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial harmonized agreement. Principal online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/services-business/business-account.html). Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/business-registry) is the provincial portal for Limited Partnership registration and alcohol-related business authorizations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) must first register the LP with the Ontario Business Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act, filing a Declaration of Partnership and identifying at least one Ontario general partner and a registered head office address. After the provincial LP registration is complete, the partnership must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), Form RC1A (Request for a BN for a non-resident) where applicable, or by online registration through CRA's Business Registration Online service. The GST/HST account number is the 9-digit BN followed by the two-letter program identifier 'RT'. Non-resident partners must also provide the CRA with the partnership's foreign registration documentation, substantive constitutive documents, details of the Canadian general partner or appoint a Canadian representative, and in certain cases post security for GST/HST under section 307 of the Excise Tax Act. Before lawful distribution of craft beer commences, the LP must also obtain the applicable Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) license, such as a Brewery Master Licence or a Manufacturer's / Distiller's / Liquor Permit Endorsement, as the tax registration assumes a valid Ontario liquor regulatory authorization.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election for limited partnerships. An Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; partners are taxable on their allocated share of Canadian-source partnership income. For GST/HST, however, the partnership itself is a separate 'person' and a mandatory registrant once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. Available statutory elections include the closely-related corporation / partnership joint election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act, the nil-consideration election under subsection 156(2) for certain supplies between registrants, and simplified GST/HST filing options for non-resident registrants. Hybrid mismatch and treaty eligibility must be analysed under the Canada-U.S. or applicable tax treaty; no broad domestic election exists to override the fiscally transparent character of an LP for Canadian income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada before GST/HST, measured over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A GST/HST registrant is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, including the value of zero-rated export supplies of goods. For a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies if taxable supplies are made through a Canadian establishment; voluntary registration is available to an LP even below the threshold, which is common for craft beer distribution to obtain input tax credits on beverages, freight, warehousing, and export-related logistics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An Ontario LP distributing craft beer to customers outside Canada may claim a 0% GST/HST export rate under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Zero-rating is available where the beer is supplied by way of sale and (i) the supplier itself exports the goods and secures prescribed evidence of export, or (ii) the supplier delivers the goods by common carrier to a consignee outside Canada. The export must occur within the prescribed 60-day window after the supply; the beer must actually leave Canada; and the supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence showing the goods were not acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada. Cold-chain keg shipments of artisanal hazy IPA must carry temperature-logged BOLs and customs export reports; sales delivered into Ontario restaurants, brewpubs, or the LCBO are HST-taxable domestic supplies and cannot be zero-rated as exports. Services performed in respect of offshore distribution campaigns or foreign warehousing may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not used in Canada, but freight and logistics services to a Canadian distribution hub generally remain subject to Canadian HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Report or equivalent electronic ACI/export manifest where required by CBSA)
  • Bill of Lading / waybill (original or electronic) showing the consignee address outside Canada, port or border exit, carrier name, and vessel/flight/truck number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT wire/foreign exchange confirmation from a Canadian financial institution evidencing payment of the export invoice by the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the HS commodity code (HS 2203 for beer made from malt), quantity, keg serial numbers, net weight, and destination
  • Proof of export documentary file: Canada Border Services Agency customs acknowledgement, carrier terminal manifest, export port date stamp, and any cold-chain temperature log for the keg shipment

Canada currently has no national real-time electronic invoicing mandate, no continuous transaction control (CTC) model, no tax-authority fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory XML/API clearance for GST/HST returns or B2B invoices. A GST/HST registrant must, however, issue invoices containing the supplier's business number, the date, the recipient's name, the amount of consideration, and the applicable tax or zero-rating status under the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations. Required returns are filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or a certified third-party service for most registrants. CRA may also require prescribed digital records to be kept in a readable format and available on request. The LP should nevertheless implement structured electronic invoicing, export freight documentation retention, and automated HST ledger files to satisfy audit and input tax credit substantiation requirements in Canada and under the OECD's broader VAT digitalization agenda.

CRA and CBSA may reassess a zero-rated export where the Canadian supplier fails to provide prescribed evidence of physical export, export timing, or payment repatriation. If the export is not substantiated, CRA levies HST at 13% on the full invoice amount plus statutory interest and penalties, effectively clawing back the 0% rate. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating claims attract penalties under sections 280 and 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, including penalties up to 50% of the unreported net tax for false statements or omissions, and wilful evasion may result in criminal prosecution, additional pecuniary penalties, and director liability. The LP should maintain a 180-day FX repatriation and export-evidence workflow, reconcile outward shipments to customs records, and retain the inward remittance certificate and common-carrier proof for at least six years after the taxation year to avoid retrospective clawback and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada has been free of exchange controls since the repeal of the Exchange Control Act in 1951; there is no approval requirement for cross-border capital injections, profit remittances, or distribution payments to non-resident limited partners. Practical remittance hurdles are fiscal and documentary rather than exchange-control-based: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act on distributions to non-resident partners, GST/HST and Ontario beer tax compliance on Ontario deliveries, and bank-level source-of-funds verification for outbound wires. Liquor export must also be consistent with AGCO licensing and CBSA export-reporting rules, but FX clearance itself is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds earned by an Ontario limited partnership. USD and other foreign-currency receivables from cold-chain keg exports may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts, swept to CAD at the exporter's discretion, or used to pre-fund USD payables. The only mandatory reporting is customs-based: exporters must file a B13A Export Declaration (or EDI equivalent) through the Canada Border Services Agency for shipments valued above CAD 2,000, and retain commercial invoices, bills of lading, and freight documentation for six years for CBSA, Statistics Canada, and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Supplementary prudential and AML authority rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for chartered banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for money-service-business oversight, and Payments Canada for the national clearing and settlement infrastructure (Lynx and ACSS). No foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control board exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (secondary rail only). Canadian law does not recognize the EU-style electronic-money-institution passport; non-bank cross-border payment firms operate as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC or as payment service providers under provincial money-transfer licensing. Platforms such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, OFX, and Corpay may provide cost-competitive USD/CAD conversion, virtual multi-currency accounts, and SWIFT-equivalent disbursements. However, they are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits, commercial credit, or irrevocable letters of credit, and some restrict alcohol-sector payment flows due to merchant-category policy. Consequently, the blueprint mandates a Schedule I chartered bank as the primary treasury and clearing anchor, with one FINTRAC-registered EMI used solely for low-value cross-border collections and vendor payments below CAD 25,000 per transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended routing: SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV from the non-resident partner's bank to the LP's Schedule I account in Toronto. CAD-denominated contributions settle directly through Lynx; USD contributions credit the USD conversion account and are swept to CAD at the bank's commercial rate upon instruction. The wire narrative must cite the partnership name, the executed capital-contribution schedule number, and the purpose descriptor 'capital contribution — beverage wholesale trade'; banks do not require a government FX approval but will apply OFAC and sanctions screening and expect the pre-notified source-of-funds packet. For jurisdictions with domestic exchange controls, the contributing partner must obtain its local central-bank approvals before remitting. No capital-escrow account is required under Ontario law for a commercial LP; regulatory capital escrow applies only to immigration investment programs and is not part of this treasury. The general partner's acknowledgement letter should accompany the wire to prevent returns or AML holds. Local ACH and pre-authorized debit are used only for domestic CAD receivables from Ontario on-trade accounts via ACSS; they are not a cross-border capital-injection rail.Primary rails for cross-border trade settlement: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer for high-value vendor payments covering kegs, brewing ingredients, and freight, with full invoice and purchase-order references; (ii) irrevocable, confirmed commercial Letters of Credit for bulk ingredient and equipment contracts exceeding CAD 100,000, issued through the bank's trade desk and counter-guaranteed where the foreign beneficiary requires a confirming bank; (iii) Payments Canada ACSS pre-authorized debits and EFT credits for domestic distributor collections; (iv) host-to-host API banking (RBC Express, BMO eBanking, TD Business Central, Scotiabank Start) with dual-approval workflows for batch disbursements; and (v) same-day Lynx transfers for interbank and high-value CAD settlements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (secondary rail only). Canadian law does not recognize the EU-style electronic-money-institution passport; non-bank cross-border payment firms operate as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC or as payment service providers under provincial money-transfer licensing. Platforms such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, OFX, and Corpay may provide cost-competitive USD/CAD conversion, virtual multi-currency accounts, and SWIFT-equivalent disbursements. However, they are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits, commercial credit, or irrevocable letters of credit, and some restrict alcohol-sector payment flows due to merchant-category policy. Consequently, the blueprint mandates a Schedule I chartered bank as the primary treasury and clearing anchor, with one FINTRAC-registered EMI used solely for low-value cross-border collections and vendor payments below CAD 25,000 per transaction.
