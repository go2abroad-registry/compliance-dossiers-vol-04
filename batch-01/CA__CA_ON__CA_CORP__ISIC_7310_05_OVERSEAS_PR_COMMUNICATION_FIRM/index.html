Governing statute

Ontario Business Corporations Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. B.16, as amended (OBCA). Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

OBCA imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Articles must prescribe an authorized share structure (e.g., unlimited Class A common shares). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and corporate income tax registration portal: Business Registration Online (CRA My Business Account); provincial Ontario registration via Ontario Ministry of Finance for employer health tax / Ontario Registries for extra-provincial licence, coordinated through Canada Revenue Agency for GST/HST and payroll accounts.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada / Ontario must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), GST/HST account, payroll deductions account, and corporate income tax account. Application may be filed online through CRA Business Registration Online using Form RC1 or by mail/fax. The foreign entity must provide its jurisdiction of incorporation, director information, Canadian business address, and description of activities. Non-resident corporations are also required to file an initial return (T2) and may need a Form RC143 for non-resident tax registration if no BN is otherwise assigned. Ontario extra-provincial registration (Ontario Business Corporations Act) is required before carrying on business in Ontario, but the CRA BN serves as the unified federal-provincial tax identifier for GST/HST and corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada applies entity-level taxation; no general 'check-the-box' pass-through election comparable to the US. Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status and associated-corporation elections may affect the small business deduction, but do not alter entity-level taxation. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canadian domestic rules (per OECD BEPS Action 2) disallow deductions on hybrid mismatch arrangements and require elective or mandatory conformity in certain cross-border structures. A foreign entity may elect to file a deemed-disposition return under subsection 48(1) or make a section 216 election for non-resident rental income, but such elections are limited to specific fact patterns and do not convert the corporation into a flow-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per year – the small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s.148; once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter and the trailing four-quarter period (or in two consecutive quarters), registration for GST/HST becomes mandatory unless the entity is a small supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada by a registered supplier are generally zero-rated (GST/HST rate 0%) under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Services provided to a non-resident who is not resident in Canada and who is not registered for GST/HST in Canada may be zero-rated if the service relates to real property situated outside Canada or tangible personal property located outside Canada at the time the service is performed; certain intangible-personal-property and advisory services (including international public relations, media consulting, and communication services) supplied to non-resident clients may also be zero-rated if the recipient is not in Canada and the supply is not a taxable supply under section 9 of Part V of Schedule V. The supplier must have 'proof of export' or documentation showing delivery outside Canada and receipt by a non-resident. Canada does not operate a VAT refund scheme for export input tax; input tax credits are claimed on the GST/HST return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A / CADDEX electronic export declaration) for goods
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical shipment from Canada
  • Commercial invoice with complete supplier/recipient identification and zero-rating rationale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange record evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident customer
  • Contract or engagement letter confirming the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore supply nature
  • Any Form GST11 (proof of export) or permanent export/logicalshipment records for digitally delivered services

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance model, continuous transaction control (CTC), or government fiscal stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary; where used, invoices must satisfy GST/HST information requirements under the Excise Tax Act (e.g., supplier name, BN/GST/HST registration number, invoice date, tax amount, recipient info). GST/HST returns are transmitted electronically through CRA My Business Account or approved EDI/API channels; no CRA pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

Misapplication of zero-rating to a domestic taxable supply or to an in-Canada supply made to a resident is assessed as unremitted GST/HST plus penalties and interest under the Excise Tax Act s.280 (failure to remit), s.285 (false statements or omissions) and, for fraudulent misrepresentation, s.327. CRA may reassess within four years (or later in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default). Penalties include a fine up to CAD 10,000 plus an amount equal to 100% of the tax evaded for gross negligence under s.285; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries fines and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks apply for the entire reassessment period, and input tax credits claimed in respect of zero-rated exports may be denied or recovered if documentary substantiation is incomplete.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully freely convertible under a market-determined floating exchange-rate regime. Canada maintains no active exchange-control legislation; the former Exchange Control Act has been repealed, and neither current-account flows (cross-border retainer fees, media placement commissions, royalties) nor capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend and profit remittances) require prior regulatory approval or central-bank licensing. Profit remittance by a non-resident-owned Ontario business corporation is unrestricted from a convertibility standpoint but is subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory default 25%, reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g., to 5% on dividends under the Canada-United States treaty, with relief evidenced via NR301/NR302 filings). All cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and Canadian-dollar clearing operates through Payments Canada with settlement finality at the Bank of Canada. Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada require Business Direct Investment (BDI) survey reporting where a non-resident holds a direct investment interest of 10% or more, but this is statistical rather than restrictive. There are no quantitative ceilings on corporate foreign-exchange conversion or holding of foreign currency in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export or cross-border service proceeds in Canada. A Canadian corporation may retain receipts in CAD or any foreign currency in its authorized foreign-currency operating accounts at its full discretion. The only compliance obligations are: (a) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, whether single or aggregated within 24 hours; (b) ordinary record keeping and verification duties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); (c) use of the Bank of Canada annual average exchange-rate schedule for Income Tax Act conversion of foreign-currency revenue; and (d) BDI direct-investment survey filings where a non-resident shareholder's ownership or control reaches the 10% threshold. For an international PR communications firm classified under NAICS 541820, there is no CRA-mandated foreign-currency ceiling and no exchange-control certificate is required for the receipt of overseas retainer fees.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary rail; Low as the sole banking solution. Canadian payment fintechs and digital platforms (Wise Canada ULC, Stripe Canada Ltd., PayPal Canada, Nuvei, Neo Financial) operate lawfully where registered as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and may provide collection accounts, mass-payout APIs, FX conversion, and virtual cards; however, they are not Schedule I deposit-taking institutions, cannot clear Canadian-dollar items directly through Payments Canada without a bank sponsor, and cannot hold client escrow or provide final settlement under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act. Offshore digital EMIs authorized solely by a foreign regulator (e.g., FCA-authorised UK EMIs) are not recognized as deposit institutions in Canada, are legally restricted from maintaining a Canadian operating account or holding CAD client funds, and may act only as payment-initiation/open-banking or pre-funded virtual-card rails. Recommended architecture for the PR firm: maintain a Tier-1 Schedule I bank as the master treasury and settlement account, and layer one FINTRAC-registered Canadian PSP (Wise Business or Stripe) as a low-value international retainer collection and FX-netting overlay.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a standard SWIFT MT103 credit (or Fedwire for USD) from the overseas parent to the Ontario operational account, using the receiving bank's three-digit institution number and five-digit transit number, with the payment descriptor stated as 'Inbound direct investment / equity capital contribution'. No Bank of Canada exchange-control approval or prior licence is required because Canada imposes no capital controls; nevertheless, the remitting and receiving banks will complete FINTRAC large-EFT reporting for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, whether single or aggregated over 24 hours. To prevent holds beyond T+1, the corporate treasury must pre-submit to the relationship manager: (i) the executed subscription or shareholder loan agreement; (ii) a board resolution authorizing the capital contribution and naming the beneficiary account; (iii) source-of-funds evidence, including parent bank statements and audited financials; and (iv) non-PEP and sanctions certifications. The accountant records the receipt as non-taxable contributed surplus or share capital for an equity injection, or as intercompany debt for a loan; where debt is used, Part XIII withholding on interest (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced) must be addressed through NR301/NR302 filings and the interest must be charged at arm's-length rates. If the non-resident investor's direct or indirect voting interest reaches 10%, the corporation must also file the applicable Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada BDI direct-investment survey.For service-based disbursements (overseas subcontractors, media placements, licensing fees, partner commissions, and crisis-response retainers), the primary clearing rails are high-value SWIFT MT103 transfers with fully populated remittance information (tags 50, 59, and 70), Payments Canada EFT or Lynx RTGS for CAD payments, and Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's United States clearing arm for USD-denominated settlements. Recurring low-value international payouts may be aggregated through a FINTRAC-registered PSP such as Wise Business or Stripe under parent-level payout APIs. A commercial documentary letter of credit governed by UCP600 is legally permissible in Canada and may be used for physical-asset purchases, event production assets, or media-buying collateral, but it is not the standard rail for pure PR and communications services. Every outbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more triggers FINTRAC EFT reporting, all beneficiaries must be screened against OSFI/UN/OFAC/CBSA sanctions lists before transmission, and any royalty, license fee, or technical-service payment to a non-resident supplier is subject to Part XIII withholding (default 25%, treaty-reduced to 0–15% with NR301/NR302 evidence), remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency through the T2/NR4 process.Spot foreign-exchange contracts (T+2 settlement) executed through a Schedule I bank dealing room or an IIROC-regulated investment dealer for routine conversion of USD retainers into CAD operating funds, with no regulatory approval required for non-financial corporations.Deliverable outright CAD/USD FX forwards under an ISDA master agreement or long-form FX master confirmation, collateralized by a credit support annex, permissible for hedging the currency risk of committed USD-denominated retainer receivables; speculative open positions are outside board policy.Average-rate and window forwards for smoothing monthly conversion of cross-border retainer inflows where invoice timing is irregular; commonly sold by RBC Capital Markets, TD Securities, BMO Capital Markets, CIBC World Markets, and Scotiabank Global Banking and Markets.FX swaps and rollover contracts for pairing inbound USD collection timing with outbound CAD disbursement calendars, particularly for media-placement portfolios with weekly settlement cycles.Cross-currency swaps for re-denominating a non-resident parent's intercompany working-capital loan into the operating currency, subject to arm's-length pricing and Part XIII withholding compliance on the interest leg.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies that lack full deliverable convertibility (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL), cash-settled in freely convertible CAD or USD where the underlying exposure is a legitimate commercial retainer or tax obligation; lawful when executed through an OSFI-regulated bank or IIROC-regulated dealer.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars (buying a cap, selling a floor) for budget-rate insurance on multi-year client contracts with fixed foreign-currency pricing, documented under IFRS 9 hedge accounting and accompanied by a board-approved FX risk-management policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary rail; Low as the sole banking solution. Canadian payment fintechs and digital platforms (Wise Canada ULC, Stripe Canada Ltd., PayPal Canada, Nuvei, Neo Financial) operate lawfully where registered as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and may provide collection accounts, mass-payout APIs, FX conversion, and virtual cards; however, they are not Schedule I deposit-taking institutions, cannot clear Canadian-dollar items directly through Payments Canada without a bank sponsor, and cannot hold client escrow or provide final settlement under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act. Offshore digital EMIs authorized solely by a foreign regulator (e.g., FCA-authorised UK EMIs) are not recognized as deposit institutions in Canada, are legally restricted from maintaining a Canadian operating account or holding CAD client funds, and may act only as payment-initiation/open-banking or pre-funded virtual-card rails. Recommended architecture for the PR firm: maintain a Tier-1 Schedule I bank as the master treasury and settlement account, and layer one FINTRAC-registered Canadian PSP (Wise Business or Stripe) as a low-value international retainer collection and FX-netting overlay.
