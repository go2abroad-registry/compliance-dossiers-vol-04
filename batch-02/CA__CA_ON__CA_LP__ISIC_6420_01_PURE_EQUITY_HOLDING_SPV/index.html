Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The historical Exchange Control Act regime was dismantled in 1951, and no Currency Act, Foreign Exchange Act, or analogous instrument imposes exchange-control licensing, repatriation quotas, or conversion permits. The Canadian dollar trades freely, and capital injections, downstream dividends, partnership distributions, management fees, interest, and liquidation proceeds may move cross-border without Bank of Canada or CRA approval. Residual constraints are purely fiscal: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes a 25% statutory withholding tax on specified payments to non-residents, including certain distributions to non-resident partners of a limited partnership (for example, amounts deemed to be Canadian-source distributed income under subsection 212(13.1)), reducible under applicable double-tax treaties to as low as 5% for substantial-shareholding dividends; (ii) NR4 and CRS/FATCA reporting must be maintained; and (iii) the SPV must preserve flow-through classification under section 96 of the Income Tax Act. Profit remittance therefore faces documentary and withholding obligations rather than exchange-control hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters, treasury centres, and holding vehicles may retain foreign currency offshore or in resident foreign-currency accounts without conversion duty. For the CA_LP SPV, which conducts no exporting activity, downstream dividend inflows in USD or other currencies may be deposited in the currency received or converted at the commercial-bank spot rate at the SPV's election. A functional-currency election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act is available if the SPV's books are principally maintained in a qualifying foreign currency, thereby aligning reporting currency with treasury operations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established by the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of federally regulated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); payments and clearing infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada under the Canadian Payments Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-21) through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); anti-money-laundering supervision is performed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and operates without a dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority, a status that governs the opening and operation of the Ontario Limited Partnership (CA_LP) equity-holding SPV.

Turnover: The Ontario Limited Partnerships Act does not impose a mandatory audit on a passive holding LP. A certified independent audit is triggered only where (i) the partnership agreement or a lending/investor covenant requires audited financial statements; (ii) the LP is an investment fund or reporting issuer subject to National Instrument 81-106; or (iii) the corporate general partner is an offering corporation that cannot rely on the OBCA shareholder audit waiver. An entirely passive, single-purpose equity holding LP with corporate partners normally falls below all audit-triggering thresholds.
