Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenu Québec as the provincial tax authority for TPS/TVQ with portal at revenuquebec.ca; Canada Revenue Agency for federal matters with portal at canada.ca/cra

E-government filing portal

Canadian corporations obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the My Business Account online portal following incorporation. Quebec entities register with Revenu Québec to secure a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and enroll for TPS/TVQ. The process entails submission of articles of incorporation, business registration forms, and supporting documentation through the respective government portals.

Available election forms

No check-the-box elections available for Canadian corporations. Canadian Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs) may elect small business deduction treatment for reduced corporate tax rates. Hybrid mismatch rules are governed by the Income Tax Act and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada or services supplied to non-residents outside Canada qualify for zero-rating under the TPS/TVQ regime. For bulk petrochemical polymers, eligibility requires verifiable proof of export, no domestic consumption, and full compliance with customs export formalities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SED)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of FX repatriation

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API requirements, or digital stamping. Businesses must issue tax invoices upon request, maintain electronic records, and file periodic TPS/TVQ returns electronically through the Revenu Québec portal.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims may result in penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax, interest charges, and potential criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. Retrospective clawback applies if export status or documentation is deemed insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity investment, trade settlements, dividend remittances, interest payments, royalties, or capital repatriation. Profit remittances are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% absent treaty relief, commonly reduced to 5% for dividends and 10% for interest/royalties under the Canada-U.S. and other income tax treaties). Foreign-currency accounts and external borrowings are unrestricted, provided that documentation supporting the underlying trade obligation and source of funds is retained for bank compliance and Canada Revenue Agency audits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in US dollars or other freely convertible currencies, converted at market rates, or held in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian deposit-taking institutions. The only reporting obligations are the export declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Act and balance-of-payments statistics reporting to Statistics Canada; neither triggers exchange surrender or a forced conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no central foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control department.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for established Canadian-regulated payment fintechs that hold a FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration or a Bank of Canada retail payment system provider licence under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Low for offshore electronic-money institutions without a Canadian legal presence or MSB/PSP registration, because they lack direct access to Lynx/ACSS clearing, are generally not recognized as authorized deposit-taking institutions, and face correspondent de-risking. A Canadian subsidiary EMI can support FX conversion and disbursement workflows but cannot replace a tier-1 bank for documentary letters of credit, high-limit CAD settlement, and corporate treasury services.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined statutory corporate income tax rate (federal 15% general rate + Quebec 11.5% general rate); federal basic rate of 38% is reduced by the 10% provincial abatement and the 13% general rate reduction

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; Quebec imposes an additional 15% tax on dividends paid to non-residents, producing a combined gross statutory burden of 40% before treaty relief. Deemed dividends on corporate wind-up or redemption of shares are subject to the same Part XIII treatment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the fiscal year-end. Instalments are due monthly where federal tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000; the final balance of tax is due 2 months after year-end (3 months for Canadian-controlled private corporations claiming the small business deduction).

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17.T2 must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; balance of Quebec tax is due 2 months after year-end (3 months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations); instalments are monthly for large corporations and quarterly for smaller entities based on prior-year tax payable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Form T106 reporting when aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents equal or exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation must be prepared when aggregate reportable transactions with a non-resident related party equal or exceed CAD 1,000,000 for non-loan transactions or CAD 5,000,000 for financing transactions; the Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1 billion and the Local File threshold is met. All transfer-pricing documentation must exist by the T2 filing due date to demonstrate reasonable efforts and avoid the 10% penalty under ITA s.247(4).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is instead tested through the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245), treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions, the CRA sham doctrine, and the permanent-establishment tests under ITA s.253. A Quebec booking company owning no inventory, employing no staff, maintaining no physical premises, and assuming no price risk on bulk polymer resales is exposed to full reassessment, TP adjustment, GAAR denial of treaty benefits, and gross-negligence penalties even though it is legally incorporated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Willful

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 2 years' imprisonment; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years' imprisonment. Quebec Tax Administration Act s.102 imposes an additional fine of 100% to 200% of the evaded provincial tax and a term of imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2), doubled in Quebec under TAA s.1049, plus daily compounded interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid federal and Quebec income tax and Part XIII liabilities; criminal courts impose travel restrictions and may order passport seizure; Canada-US extradition applies to cross-border tax fraud; directors face personal fines equivalent to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA or Revenu Quebéc recharacterizes the transactions as a sham or abusive avoidance, denies all intercompany deductions under GAAR, levies a 50% gross-negligence penalty, applies a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on net adjustments exceeding the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue, and denies treaty benefits under the applicable LOB article; funds may be frozen as proceeds of unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a façade; directors become personally liable for all unremitted payroll source deductions and QST under ITA s.227.1 and Quebec TAA s.31.0.1; Quebec enforcement courts may issue an order prohibiting a director from leaving Quebec pending satisfaction of tax arrears; aggravated cases yield personal bankruptcy orders and director disqualification.
