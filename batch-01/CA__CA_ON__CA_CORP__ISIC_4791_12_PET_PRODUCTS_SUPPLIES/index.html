Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official tax administration portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (http://www.canada.ca/taxes, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario provincial corporate and consumption taxes are harmonized through the federal GST/HST system; Ontario Ministry of Finance remains competent for certain provincial levies such as Employer Health Tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation carrying on taxable activities in Canada must obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN). For GST/HST purposes, the BN is expanded into a 15-character GST/HST account number (9-digit BN + 'RT' program code + 4-digit reference number, e.g., 123456789RT0001). Application is made through CRA Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts' with the appropriate CRA Tax Centre. Non-resident vendors that exceed the small-supplier threshold or that are required to collect GST/HST must register before issuing taxable supplies. Upon registration, the entity must state the BN/GST/HST number on all invoices and must file periodic GST/HST returns (electronically through the CRA GST/HST NETFILE service or commercial software) showing net tax, including zero-rated export supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. A Canadian business corporation is taxed as a separate person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through election is available for federal or Ontario income tax purposes. Relevant statutory elections include: voluntary registration for GST/HST by a small supplier under section 240(1.1) of the Excise Tax Act; the GST/HST Quick Method election for registrants; income tax rollover elections under section 85 (transfer of property to a corporation) and related election mechanisms; and certain gross-basis elections for non-resident taxpayers. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules are mandatory anti-avoidance measures, not elective classification regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – aggregate worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made by the supplier and certain associated persons in any four consecutive calendar quarters. Once exceeded, the entity is a mandatory GST/HST registrant; a person below the threshold may elect to register voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility for the Ontario HST arises under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V. For the e-commerce supply of pet products and pet-care devices, a registrant may zero-rate tangible personal property when the supplier exports the goods outside Canada as part of the supply, including goods shipped by courier or common carrier to a foreign destination. The supply is zero-rated only if the goods are truly exported and not later returned or consumed in Canada; documentary evidence of export must be retained. Supplies of services to non-residents can be zero-rated where the services are performed wholly outside Canada or qualify under the offshore service provisions of Schedule VI. For a Canadian e-commerce vendor selling to U.S. or other foreign customers, the delivery of goods to a foreign address through an international carrier is the primary eligibility trigger for the 0% rate.

  • CBSA export declaration or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) filing (Canada Export Declaration / B13A, including eManifest cargo report where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice bearing the seller's GST/HST registration number, declared 0% HST/GST rate, and stated export terms (Incoterms)
  • Bill of lading or air waybill showing the shipment was exported from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Carrier proof of export, terminal receipt, or customs release documentation evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Foreign-currency inward remittance certificate or electronic payment settlement report (e.g., bank FX receipt, credit card acquirer settlement statement from Stripe/PayPal) showing export sale proceeds credited to the Canadian corporate bank account

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous electronic transaction controls, or digital fiscal stamping for GST/HST. GST/HST invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form and must contain the required information prescribed under the Excise Tax Act (supplier's BN, invoice date, purchaser's name, seller's name, description of goods, consideration, applicable GST/HST rate and amount). Voluntary e-invoicing interoperability is evolving through Pan-Canadian Peppol-based frameworks. Customs export reporting is electronic through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and eManifest for commercial goods; this is a customs clearance requirement, not a tax-invoice authorisation/clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within the normal four-year assessment period, with unlimited reassessment where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect has occurred, and may clawback the zero-rating together with arrears interest and penalties. The principal penalties for false export claims are set out in the Excise Tax Act: gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided; fraudulent or deliberate evasion penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided; and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act (fines and/or imprisonment). CRA audit programs cross-match GST/HST returns with CBSA cargo and export transaction data and payment-processing data to detect non-existent, inflated, or recycled export invoices. Retrospective clawbacks remove the zero-rating, reinstate 13% HST, and assess penalties plus compound daily interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada maintains no exchange-control licensing, approval, or declaration threshold for inbound equity, inbound debt, trade settlements, or dividend repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: Part XIII withholding of 25% on non-resident dividends (treaty-reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-US and Canada-China DTAAs) and a 15% branch tax on non-resident held corporations under ITA s. 219 for branch profits. Stripe and PayPal USD settlements are current-account receivables eligible for unrestricted credit to a Canadian USD ledger, with no mandatory conversion to CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished all statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements; there is no mandated timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no CFMS-style repatriation mechanism for e-commerce export receipts. Treasury best practice for the pet-supplies seller is internal: convert USD settlement balances to CAD within five business days when no forward hedge is active, and monitor single-transaction/24-hour aggregate flows of CAD 10,000 or more for FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor; Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime overseen by the Bank of Canada for non-bank payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — viable as auxiliary collection and disbursement channels only, not as primary treasury. Stripe and PayPal operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and will become RPAA-registered payment service providers, but Canadian law prohibits non-bank EMIs from holding demand-deposit accounts in CAD; primary clearing must therefore sit at a Schedule I bank. Offshore digital EMIs cannot provide true CAD clearing or SWIFT corporate-level liquidity rails and should be limited to USD payout automation and sub-ledger reconciliation, never as the legal-entity operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the designated Schedule I CAD operating account with a precise remittance narrative: 'Equity subscription / share capital contribution under OBCA, subscriber [name], share certificate [number]'. The remittance must reconcile to the share subscription agreement, board resolution, and minute-book entry; the bank will verify source of funds and any wire at or above CAD 10,000 enlivens FINTRAC EFTR/LCTR reporting.
