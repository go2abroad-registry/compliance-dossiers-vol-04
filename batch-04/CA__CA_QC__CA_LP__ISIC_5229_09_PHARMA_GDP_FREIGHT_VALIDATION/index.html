Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/account-registration-access-business.html); Revenu Québec — Mon dossier d'entreprise portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/); both jointly administer GST/HST and QST under the Excise Tax Act and Act respecting the Quebec sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity establishing a Quebec limited partnership (LP): (i) register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (ii) apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or RC1 form — the LP itself, not its partners, is the registrant for GST/HST under ETA s. 272.1; (iii) register for QST with Revenu Québec via the LM-1-V form or Clic Revenu, obtaining a QST number; (iv) file a partnership information return (T5013) and issue T5013 slips to partners; non-resident partners without a Canadian permanent establishment must provide the LP with their foreign addresses and are taxed on Canadian-source income; the LP must also file a GST/HST return (GST34) and QST return (FPZ-500) on an annual or periodic basis; a non-resident LP appointing no Canadian resident agent must appoint a representative for service of process. The process takes 5–10 business days for CRA/Revenu Québec issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a limited partnership is not a taxpayer under the Canadian Income Tax Act; income and losses flow through to partners pro rata. Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed on Canadian-source income (including business carried on in Canada and certain property dispositions). For GST/HST/QST, the LP is a separate person (ETA s. 272.1) and is jointly and severally liable with partners for remitted taxes. Limited partners generally enjoy limited liability for commercial debts but remain liable for certain statutory tax obligations.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) ETA s. 273 election — the partnership may designate one member to act as the representative and administer the GST/QST registration; (2) ETA s. 272.1(2) election — the partnership may elect a fiscal year different from the calendar year for GST/QST purposes; (3) ETA s. 156 election — eligible partners may compute net tax separately where the partnership is a 'qualifying partnership'; (4) QSA s. 411 election — analogous QST representative election for Quebec; (5) ITA s. 216 election — a non-resident partner may elect to file a Canadian tax return based on Canadian-source income; (6) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.3, 18.4, 20.25) — Canada follows a static OECD hybrid framework; no 'check-the-box' election exists for Canadian entities. The Canadian LP is not a per se corporation for Canadian federal tax; however, certain Canadian limited partnerships created under provincial law may be treated as corporations under U.S. check-the-box rules (default per se corporation) — requiring a U.S. entity classification election to preserve flow-through taxation for U.S. purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the repealed Exchange Control Act, subject only to anti-money-laundering reporting, sanctions screening, and tax withholding. There are no statutory exchange-control restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest payments, or liquidation proceeds. For a Quebec limited partnership, distributions to non-resident partners remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable Quebec provincial withholding rules, but no Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval is required for transferability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or statutory repatriation timelines for export receipts. A qualifying pharma GDP freight company may retain foreign currency receipts in Canadian or foreign-currency bank accounts. Tax review remains necessary under the Income Tax Act, including accrual taxation of offshore passive income, and export documentation must comply with the Customs Act and Canada Border Services Agency reporting thresholds. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to large electronic funds transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange compliance oversight is distributed among the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — Viable as a supplementary treasury channel only. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) operate legally in Canada either by engaging a registered Canadian payment-service partner or by self-registering as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, and, for Quebec activities, as a money-services business under the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). They cannot accept deposits as banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and may not be designated as the exclusive operating-account provider for the Quebec limited partnership. Their FX pricing and international payment rails are useful for low-value, quick-turnaround freight disbursements, but the primary account should reside with a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Undisclosed principal owners or unregistered limited partnership

Root cause: Undisclosed principal owners or unregistered limited partnership

Defense evidence: Certificate of registration for the Quebec limited partnership from the Registraire d'entreprises du Québec; partnership agreement; register of partners and general partner; corporate KYC pack for each general partner: constitutional documents, directors register, proof of address, government-issued photo IDs, and a current corporate beneficial-ownership search.
Non-resident capital contribution without a clear source of funds

Root cause: Non-resident capital contribution without a clear source of funds

Defense evidence: Bank statements evidencing the ultimate source (12 months preferred); notarised source-of-wealth declaration for each contributor; foreign tax filings where applicable; legal opinion confirming the contribution is an equity injection rather than a loan; remittance and withholding analysis under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act if distributions to non-residents are anticipated.
Pharma GDP freight counterparties in sanctions-affected or high-risk jurisdictions

Root cause: Pharma GDP freight counterparties in sanctions-affected or high-risk jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening reports; OFAC/UN/EU consolidated list results; positive identification of ultimate beneficial ownership of counterparties; end-user certificates and GDP-regulatory licences of counterparties; pharmaceutical transportation documentation (temperature-log BDO, packing slip, shipper's export declaration, quarantine release) to substantiate legitimate freight activity.
Unusual third-party payments or pass-through settlement instructions

Root cause: Unusual third-party payments or pass-through settlement instructions

Defense evidence: Flow-of-funds diagrams showing contractual nexus to the pharma freight transaction; executed logistics contracts and invoices; SWIFT remittance advice with precise transaction descriptions; letter of instruction signed by the general partner; bank confirmation that third-party payments are made only for documented subcontracted freight or customs-bond payments; evidence of goods movement, including airway bills, customs entry numbers, and proof-of-delivery records.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's main operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. A Quebec limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not pay federal or Quebec corporate income tax on its allocated partnership income. Each partner reports its share. For reference, a taxable corporation carrying on business in Quebec generally faces a combined federal–Quebec general corporate rate of 26.5% (15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec); a qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may access a combined 12.2% rate on eligible active business income within the applicable small-business limits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard Canadian Part XIII withholding rate on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident is 25%, subject to a lower rate under an applicable tax treaty. A partnership distribution is not automatically a dividend: its treatment follows the underlying tax character and partner-level allocation rules. A corporate distribution to a non-resident partner may be subject to withholding where it is legally and fiscally a dividend.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Quebec corporate filing and payment deadlines must be verified for the particular partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general CAD threshold that automatically triggers a conventional Local File or Master File for every taxpayer. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required under section 247 of the Income Tax Act where relevant cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exist. Form T106 generally applies when the reporting taxpayer's aggregate reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1 million, subject to the statutory tests. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a qualifying multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, subject to Canadian rules.

Economic substance scope: The United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations do not apply in Canada. Canadian tax outcomes remain subject to transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, specific anti-avoidance provisions, treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, and relevant corporate and partnership law. A partnership's contractual allocations must be supportable under the agreement and applicable tax rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Late or false partnership information reporting

Statutory sanction: Statutory fixed and daily penalties may apply, with higher consequences for failures after demand or knowing false statements. Interest and tax reassessments may also apply. Amounts depend on the applicable federal or Quebec provision and facts.

Director personal liability: A limited partnership has no directors merely by virtue of being an LP. General partners and responsible persons may face applicable statutory liability for their own conduct; corporate directors may face personal liability for specified source deductions and other statutory debts. Imprisonment requires an applicable offence and proof under the governing statute; travel bans are not an automatic tax sanction.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or failure to document

Statutory sanction: Section 247 adjustments may increase income or reduce deductions. A transfer-pricing penalty generally applies where the statutory threshold is met, ordinarily 10% of the relevant net adjustment, subject to statutory exceptions and reasonable-efforts rules. T106 penalties may apply separately.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for a corporation's transfer-pricing adjustment. Personal exposure may arise for knowing participation in tax offences, false statements, obstruction, or specified statutory liabilities.
Non-resident withholding failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unremitted withholding tax, interest, and penalties. The general penalty for failure to deduct or remit can be 10% of the amount required, increasing to 20% in specified cases of gross negligence or knowing failure, subject to the statute.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors may be jointly and severally liable for specified unremitted source deductions under statutory conditions, including applicable due-diligence defences. This is not a general piercing of the corporate veil.
Tax evasion, fraud, or false records

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment, interest, gross-negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution may apply. Tax-evasion offences can carry fines of up to 200% of tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years under the federal Income Tax Act; Quebec offences may carry separate sanctions.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, participate in, or knowingly assist an offence may be prosecuted personally. Imprisonment is possible following conviction. Civil restitution and travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of an ordinary tax assessment.
Mischaracterized freight, customs, or regulated pharmaceutical activity

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include tax and customs reassessments, interest, administrative monetary penalties, licence or permit suspension, product detention, and prosecution under applicable federal and Quebec laws. The specific penalty depends on the offence and regulator.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for directors or officers who direct, authorize, acquiesce in, or participate in an offence where the applicable statute provides. No general travel ban or imprisonment follows solely from an operational or filing error.
AML and financial-records breach

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties and criminal sanctions may apply where the entity or activity is within the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. A logistics business is not automatically a reporting entity solely because it uses wire or ACH payments.

Director personal liability: Individuals may face personal criminal liability for participation or counselling, and officers may be liable under applicable statutory provisions. Reporting-entity status and obligations must be assessed from the actual activities, not the payment rails alone.
