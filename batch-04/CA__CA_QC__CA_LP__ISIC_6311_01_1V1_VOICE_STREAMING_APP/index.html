Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by Quebec law for a limited partnership. Contributions are determined by the partnership agreement and may be deferred; no escrow account or capital deposit rule applies. The Declaration of Registration must identify the general partner and state a domicile in Quebec for the enterprise.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to the virtual service. For a leased Quebec office: (i) obtain from the landlord or the municipal fire-prevention department a certificate of fire-safety compliance under the Building Act (CQLR c B-1.1) and the Quebec Construction Code; (ii) register the establishment with the CNESST and comply with the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1), including a workplace first-aid kit, emergency evacuation plan, and electrical-equipment inspection as applicable; (iii) no structural load or special physical clearance is required for a standard administrative occupancy.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal income and GST/HST tax administration; Revenu Quebec (ARQ) - Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax and payroll obligations. Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Quebec CLIC.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec limited partnership (LP) must first file a declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ), obtaining a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). It must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC4601 or RC1 and register for GST/HST under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, providing the partnership's legal name, address and Canadian or foreign identification. For QST, the LP must register with Revenu Quebec using Form LM-1 or via CLIC. Non-resident partners must identify themselves with their foreign tax identification numbers and may be subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding or section 116 clearance, but this does not alter the LP's registration obligation. Foreign entities acting through the LP must evidence their existence and beneficial ownership through certified constituent documents, power of attorney and Revenu Quebec/short-form registration proof.

It must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC4601 or RC1 and register for GST/HST under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, providing the partnership's legal name, address and Canadian or foreign identification. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. The LP is automatically fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with income/loss allocated to partners. Available elections include: (i) election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 for GST/HST grouping of closely related registrants, if applicable; (ii) specified investment flow-through (SIFT) trust/partnership election under ITA s. 122.1 for publicly traded or certain listed LPs; (iii) Quebec QST registration and filing elections with Revenu Quebec, including joint election for non-resident registrants using an agent or mandatary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (for both GST/HST and QST; the small-supplier threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies by the partnership and mandatory registration applies once taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in any particular calendar quarter or in the preceding four calendar quarters, or whenever the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada while carrying on business in Canada and no small-supplier relief is available)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the LP may be zero-rated under s. 23 of the Excise Tax Act if exported in accordance with the customs laws and the LP retains proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident, including voice streaming, interactive audio and digital platform services, are zero-rated under s. 23(2) where the service is supplied to a non-resident and the service is not considered to be performed in Canada (i.e., the subscription or usage is delivered and enjoyed outside Canada). The LP must use customer location indicators such as billing address, IP geolocation and identifiable foreign network use to evidence non-resident supply. Quebec QST zero-rating for exports and external services follows analogous rules in the Quebec Taxation Act. The zero-rating criterion is not satisfied where the underlying service is rendered to Canadian users through the app, even if billing is made by an offshore payment provider.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CBSA electronic export declaration or prescribed customs form)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / electronic transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation showing receipt of foreign-sourced payment in Canadian currency; for digital/voice streaming services, supplementary contract, IP-geolocation report and platform payout statements

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping or government pre-approval of individual invoices is currently mandated by CRA or Revenu Quebec for GST/HST/QST. Invoices must contain prescribed data required by s. 169 of the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act, including supplier registration number, date of invoice, vendor and purchaser names, tax rates and amounts. E-invoices are permitted if in an accessible electronic format that is tamper-evident and retains a complete audit trail.

The CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess to disallow zero-rating, re-characterize the supply as taxable, assess unremitted GST/HST and QST, plus interest and penalties. Fraudulent or gross-negligent export zero-rating claims are subject to penalties under s. 285 of the Excise Tax Act (25% of the amount improperly claimed or refunded) and s. 281.1 (up to 200% of the evaded tax for evasion). Revenu Quebec may apply an additional penalty of up to 50% for false statements under the Taxation Act, and the normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. General partners may become personally liable where the partnership's claim is made for a sham or abusive transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency under a liberalized capital and current account regime. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for residents and non-residents; there are no general exchange control laws requiring central bank approval for capital inflows, dividend remittances, or repatriation. Profit remittance from a Quebec Limited Partnership is structurally treated as a distribution to partners, not a dividend, so no Quebec dividend withholding tax applies to non-resident partners if properly structured; however, the Canada Revenue Agency may apply withholding under Part XIII for payments that in substance are interest, rent, royalties, or management fees. Investor and trade compliance triggers include the Investment Canada Act for non-resident control acquisitions, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) reporting, Canada’s Beneficial Ownership Transparency regime, and the sanctions screening obligations under the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act. Practical conversion friction is limited to bank FX spreads, correspondent bank compliance, and FINTRAC cross-border wire reporting thresholds (C$10,000 or any designated amount).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no stipulated timeline for converting foreign currency received into Canadian dollars, and no mandatory conversion ratio. In-app marketplace proceeds denominated in USD, EUR, or other foreign currencies may be retained, swept, or converted at the taxpayer’s discretion, subject to the Quebec IT Partnership’s obligation to report income in Canadian dollars under applicable GAAP/tax rules and to substantiate foreign currency transactions for CRA audit purposes. High-value cross-border transfers above CAD 10,000 must be monitored and reported to FINTRAC where required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential oversight of federally chartered banks by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision by FINTRAC; sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada and the Minister of Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility subject to strict structure. Canada does not use the EU EMI licence concept; Canadian regulated equivalents are MSBs registered with FINTRAC and provincially regulated in Quebec, or federally regulated banks chartered by OSFI. An offshore EMI with no Canadian MSB registration may lawfully provide payment processing to the Quebec LP, but it cannot legally hold, transmit, or settle customer funds in Canada without a registrable money-services business licence. For treasury operations, an offshore EMI used only as a vendor settlement rail for app-store payouts is viable only where the EMI is not the primary custody account, is not used to collect from Canadian consumers, and does not create a PEP/sanctions concentration risk. The LP should require an internationally domiciled EMI with OSFI or FINTRAC equivalence, segregated client accounts, and full FATF membership in its home jurisdiction; the recommended reserve account remains a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners should be routed by SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire-style correspondent transfer to a dedicated Capital Contribution Clearing Account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. The CAD amount must be received as a clean corporate bank-to-bank credit, not as a consumer-style payment, and the payment message should include the LP’s Quebec registry number, the partner legal entity name, and the narrative 'Unrestricted Partner Capital Contribution.' The bank will require an underlying partnership capital call notice, evidence of partner identity, and confirmation that the contribution is not a loan. Where the contribution exceeds CAD 10,000, the bank must report the transaction to FINTRAC under cross-border funds transfer rules; where the contributing partner is resident in a FATF-listed high-risk jurisdiction, enhanced source-of-funds documentation, a sanctions sweep, and potentially an OSFI reporting obligation apply. The account should never be used for customer settlements or operational revenue; once the contribution is received, the LP’s general partner should transfer the cleared funds to the primary Operating Account using an internal journal entry or separate MT103, preserving a clean capital trace for the Quebec tax filings and the CRA foreign-sourced contribution schedule.Outbound treasury disbursements for trade settlement should use two primary rails: (1) standard corporate wire via SWIFT MT103 for habitual supplier payments, licensing fees, and marketing disbursements; and (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) issued by the Canadian bank or opened through a correspondent bank for substantial infrastructure procurements where the counterparty requires payment against shipping or delivery documents. For app-store payouts and recurring SaaS or CDN vendor payments, automated clearing (ACH/Electronic Funds Transfer in Canada plus SWIFT for USD/EUR vendors) is preferred over wire to reduce cost. All outbound payments must include correct purpose details, CRA source document references, and transfer pricing support where the payee is a related foreign entity. If applicable under Regulations, Part XII.2 tax on dividends or Part XIII withholding applies to certain cross-border payments; therefore the treasury policy must preclear any interest, royalty, management fee, or consulting fee through a withholding tax opinion before disbursement. Canada does not use the EU EMI licence concept; Canadian regulated equivalents are MSBs registered with FINTRAC and provincially regulated in Quebec, or federally regulated banks chartered by OSFI. An offshore EMI with no Canadian MSB registration may lawfully provide payment processing to the Quebec LP, but it cannot legally hold, transmit, or settle customer funds in Canada without a registrable money-services business licence. For treasury operations, an offshore EMI used only as a vendor settlement rail for app-store payouts is viable only where the EMI is not the primary custody account, is not used to collect from Canadian consumers, and does not create a PEP/sanctions concentration risk. The LP should require an internationally domiciled EMI with OSFI or FINTRAC equivalence, segregated client accounts, and full FATF membership in its home jurisdiction; the recommended reserve account remains a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Quebec 11.5% general corporate rate) for tax-paying corporate partners; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and does not pay corporate income tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders; however, an LP is fiscally transparent and its profit distribution to partners is not a dividend — a non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on allocated business income at progressive rates, with no Part XIII withholding on the distribution itself

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$250,000 for a single cross-border non-arm's-length transaction and C$1,000,000 for the aggregate annual value of all such transactions with a related party; Country-by-Country reporting is triggered for groups with consolidated revenue above C$750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain formal ESR, but the s. 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) deny treaty benefits and re-characterize transactions that lack economic substance; a Canadian LP created solely to route passive royalties or in-app revenue to a low-tax affilate without real employees, premises, or business decisions is presumed to be an artificial conduit and may be reassessed with penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 163(2), gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; under ITA s. 239(1), imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion; fines up to 200% of the evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 160; civil restitution, fraud charges, travel ban, and incarceration for up to 5 years
Withholding / Part XIII Failures

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 227(8), penalty of 10% of the amount not withheld and remitted, plus interest and arrears; failure to file an information return can add C$25/day penalties up to C$2,500

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the unremitted withholding, plus interest, penalties and civil suit; repeated violations can trigger travel restrictions
Transfer Pricing Documentation Gap

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the correction exceeds the lesser of C$2,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; the CRA may adjust to arm's-length terms and deny deduction

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for TP penalties, but can become liable under s. 227.1 if the adjustment leads to unremitted withholding; gross negligence could trigger s. 163(2) and personal criminal exposure for false documentation
AML and PCMLTFA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 per violation for a partnership; criminal charge under Criminal Code s. 462.31 for laundering proceeds — imprisonment up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner may receive personal fines up to C$1,000,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, asset freezes and travel bans; civil suits for restitution are common where the LP is used as a conduit
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR allows the CRA to disregard the arrangement and reallocate income to the Canadian entity; interest and penalties accrue on the reassessed tax, and the principal purpose test under the MLI may deny treaty protection entirely

Director personal liability: Directors can be held civilly liable for losses caused by reckless structuring, receive a ban on serving as officers, and face criminal charges for counselling tax evasion; extradition possible under mutual legal assistance treaties
