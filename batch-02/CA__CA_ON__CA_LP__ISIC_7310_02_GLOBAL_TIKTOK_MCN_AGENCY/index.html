E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: the partnership itself pays no federal or provincial income tax; each partner reports its allocable share of Canadian-source income/loss under ITA s. 96. Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide partnership income; non-resident partners are taxed only on the Canadian-source portion allocated to them, subject to treaty protection and permanent establishment thresholds. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none applicable. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate 'person' from its partners (ETA s. 272.1) and is the registered entity.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; entity classification follows the provincial legal form. Available elections: (a) Partnership fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) allowing a non-calendar fiscal year-end; (b) GST/HST joint partnership election under ETA s. 177(1) deeming supplies between the LP and a partner to be for consideration; (c) Non-resident partner net-basis election under ITA s. 216 (limited relevance to commercial trading income) and ETA s. 179 simplified registration/filing election for non-resident registrants; (d) Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 94) apply automatically without election — no opt-out is available when a foreign jurisdiction treats the CA_LP as a taxable entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a), measured on worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) of the LP and associated/affiliated persons over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters plus the current quarter. Registration is mandatory upon exceeding CAD 30,000 or when making taxable supplies outside Canada in the course of a commercial activity. For a global TikTok MCN agency invoicing non-resident licensees and foreign creator entities, voluntary registration is recommended to zero-rate exported services under ETA Sch. VI and to claim input tax credits on Ontario operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Sch. VI, s. 7, a supply of a service made to a non-resident person qualifies for 0% GST/HST unless the service is: (a) supplied to an individual present in Canada; (b) in respect of real property situated in Canada; (c) in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; or (d) a telecommunications/internet service or agency services rendered in Canada. The CA_LP's creator-incubation, MCN talent representation, and commission-overlay services invoiced to TikTok Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) or other non-resident MCN principals meet the zero-rating criteria provided: the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST; the services are not performed in respect of Canadian-situs property; and no Canadian-resident individual is the direct recipient. The LP must retain the non-resident status evidence (incorporation certificate, foreign tax residence certificate), the executed service agreement designating the offshore recipient, and invoices explicitly marked '0% HST — Exported Service, ETA Sch. VI s. 7'. Supplies to Canadian resident creators or registered GST/HST recipients are taxable at 13% HST and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — mandatory for goods movements; for digital creator services, the functional equivalent is the executed service agreement with TikTok B2B payout statements evidencing offshore delivery and receipt

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no government API clearance requirement exists in Canada. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL network is available (2024 onward). CRA accepts PDF, XML, and EDI invoices provided they contain the supplier's Business Number, the date of supply, a description of the supply, the amount of tax charged or a clear 'zero-rated' indication, and sufficient detail to substantiate the export. Records must be retained for six years (ITA s. 230; ETA s. 286); CRA may require maintenance of records at a Canadian location or remote inspection availability.

CRA conducts targeted exporter audits on zero-rated service claims. Penalties: (i) ETA s. 280(1) — 25% of net tax for false statements or omissions; (ii) ETA s. 285(1) — penalty for deliberately making a false statement, plus daily compound interest on the reassessed amount; (iii) gross negligence penalty up to 50% of the amount payable for culpable misrepresentation; (iv) ETA s. 298 / ITA s. 152(4) permit an unlimited reassessment period in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or willful default, with a four-year standard period otherwise. Criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 327–328 and ITA s. 239 may yield fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawback of zero-rating would additionally expose the LP to Ontario RST-type consequences and disqualification of the non-resident recipient's exemption in the counterparty jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Canada's IMF Article VIII commitments. No exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior approval regimes apply to inbound capital injections, dividend/distribution repatriation, or cross-border payments. For an Ontario Limited Partnership, the only pre-remittance hurdles are tax-related: non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 0-15% under an applicable income tax treaty such as the Canada-US Treaty) on taxable allocations and distributions, and the partnership must file CRA Forms T2046/T2047 and any required NR4/T4A slips. Conversion of USD/foreign-currency creator receipts into CAD is freely permitted at any bank or authorized foreign exchange dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian authorized deposit-taking institutions. For Canadian tax purposes, all export revenue and foreign-currency balances must be translated into CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or annual average rate as prescribed by the CRA. MCN creator earnings remitted by TikTok/ByteDance from offshore entities are treated as ordinary export service revenue and incur no surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Department of Finance Canada for monetary and exchange-rate policy; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates deposit-taking institutions; FINTRAC administers AML/CFT obligations. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration or exchange permit authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High with legal constraints. Canadian-regulated Money Services Businesses and payment fintechs (Wise Canada Corp., Payoneer, Stripe Connect Canada) are viable supplementary rails for creator payouts and low-value disbursements, but they are not authorized to act as the primary treasury settlement bank for an Ontario LP. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident partner capital contributions must route via SWIFT MT103 from a regulated depository institution to the LP's Canadian Schedule I bank account denominated in CAD or USD. Canada does not enforce a statutory purpose-code list; the wire narrative must reference the partnership agreement article, the specific partner name, and the words 'Capital Contribution to CA_LP'. For CAD settlement, the receiving bank clears through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS system; for USD clearing, funds arrive via Fedwire/CHIPS through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent and credit the LP's Canadian USD account. Any single or aggregated electronic funds transfer exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorized Wise Europe or EU-licensed EMI entities) are not permitted to open a primary operating account for the Canadian LP under Canadian banking and FINTRAC customer-funds custody rules; they may only be used as non-settlement beneficiary-designation tools. The Canadian LP must maintain a Schedule I or II chartered-bank account as the anchor for capital injections and tax-relevant cash flows.
