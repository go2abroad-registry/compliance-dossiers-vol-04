Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (federal TIN, GST/HST, payroll and corporate income tax); Revenu Québec at https://www.revenuquebec.ca (Quebec Sales Tax / TVQ, QPIP payroll and Quebec corporation tax). The federal Business Number (BN) is the primary TIN and the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) is the provincial identifier.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated fintech applying to operate in Quebec must (1) obtain a 9-digit Business Number from the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, opening program accounts RP0001 (GST/HST), RC0001 (corporate income tax) and RP0001 (payroll) as applicable; (2) file Form RC2401 (Application for a Determination of Residency of a Corporation) with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, articles of continuance and a foreign tax residency certificate if the entity is non-resident; (3) complete extra-provincial registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) and apply to Revenu Québec on the combined registration form (demande d'inscription) to obtain the NEQ and QST/TVQ accounts; (4) where a US parent or member is present, file Form RC4145 (Election for Treaty Benefits) to trigger the Canada-US Article IV(7) fiscally-transparent entity election. Processing times are approximately 2-8 weeks federally and 10-15 business days provincially.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a resident taxpayer subject to the Income Tax Act with worldwide income and a separate legal personality; no pass-through election exists. Because non-resident ownership is present, the entity is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), so the small business deduction is unavailable; the general combined federal and Quebec corporate rate is 26.5% (15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec). Taxation is residence-based, not strictly territorial, with foreign tax credits mitigating double taxation. For GST/HST/QST, the entity will be a financial institution engaged in lending, making its core interest and fee income exempt supplies; it is not automatically a selected listed financial institution unless statutory thresholds are met. — A foreign-incorporated fintech applying to operate in Quebec must (1) obtain a 9-digit Business Number from the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, opening program accounts RP0001 (GST/HST), RC0001 (corporate income tax) and RP0001 (payroll) as applicable; (2) file Form RC2401 (Application for a Determination of Residency of a Corporation) with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, articles of continuance and a foreign tax residency certificate if the entity is non-resident; (3) complete extra-provincial registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) and apply to Revenu Québec on the combined registration form (demande d'inscription) to obtain the NEQ and QST/TVQ accounts; (4) where a US parent or member is present, file Form RC4145 (Election for Treaty Benefits) to trigger the Canada-US Article IV(7) fiscally-transparent entity election. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a resident taxpayer subject to the Income Tax Act with worldwide income and a separate legal personality; no pass-through election exists. Because non-resident ownership is present, the entity is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), so the small business deduction is unavailable; the general combined federal and Quebec corporate rate is 26.5% (15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec). Taxation is residence-based, not strictly territorial, with foreign tax credits mitigating double taxation. For GST/HST/QST, the entity will be a financial institution engaged in lending, making its core interest and fee income exempt supplies; it is not automatically a selected listed financial institution unless statutory thresholds are met.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; however, the following elections and mandatory anti-hybrid settings apply: (i) Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(7) election via Form RC4145 that deems a US fiscally-transparent entity to be a resident of the US for treaty benefits; (ii) domestic hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.3, 18.4, 19.2, 20.2, 21.2 and 247.1) operate non-electively to disallow double deductions and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; (iii) GST/HST elections include the ETA s. 156 related-party election, the closely related corporation election, the GST/HST Quick Method election, and the annual filing election; (iv) for Quebec, parallel elections are available under the Tax Administration Act (CQLR c T-2.1); (v) Part XIV branch tax elections under ITA s. 219 do not normally apply once the Canadian entity is a separate resident corporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is free-floating and fully convertible. Canada repealed its exchange-control legislation in 1951 and currently imposes no restrictions, pre-approvals, or quota systems on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit and capital remittances (dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds) may flow freely, subject to statutory withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) — generally 25% on dividends and interest paid to non-residents, treaty-reduced where applicable — and to FATCA/CRS reporting. FINTRAC reporting thresholds (cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more) are informational AML/ATF obligations and do not constitute a convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory rule requiring surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds or foreign-currency receivables to the Bank of Canada or any licensed intermediary. A Canadian-domiciled P2P micro-lending platform may hold and reinvest foreign-currency operational balances abroad, subject to the functional-currency rules of section 261 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and to FINTRAC's cross-border EFT reporting duty for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, which must be reported within five business days.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of federally regulated deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). In Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) licenses money-services businesses under the Act respecting money-services businesses (CQLR c S-41.1), and the Office de la protection du consommateur (OPC) issues permits for consumer lending activities under the Consumer Protection Act (Québec).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as principal treasury and clearing rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary disbursement channel only. Canada does not recognize the European EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licence. An offshore EMI operating in Canada must register with FINTRAC as a money-services business (MSB) and, if active in Quebec, hold an AMF money-services business licence. Foreign EMIs cannot become direct participants in Payments Canada's ACSS or Lynx, cannot originate Pre-Authorized Debits, and do not provide CDIC deposit insurance or client-fund custody arrangements acceptable to Quebec financial regulators. The platform should therefore maintain its primary CAD liquidity and settlement accounts exclusively at a Canadian Schedule I bank or a Quebec-based cooperative institution.

Shareholder loans must be supported by an executed facility agreement, board resolution, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications; contractual interest must be at arm's length to avoid Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) and Quebec tax filing obligations.
