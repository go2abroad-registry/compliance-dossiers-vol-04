Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal tax portal: Canada.ca, My Business Account. Ontario provincial indirect tax administration is coordinated through the Ontario Ministry of Finance, with the GST/HST and Ontario HST portion administered federally by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (LP) carrying on business in Canada must procure a Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST program account. The standard procedure is to file Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts, online via CRA My Business Account, by mail, or by fax. The registration can be made effective up to 30 days before the date the application is filed. The same BN serves as the provincial identifier for Ontario HST purposes; no separate Ontario TIN is issued. The LP must register once it ceases to be a small supplier, meaning total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the last four calendar quarters. Non-resident LPs may be required to provide a Canadian contact address or authorized representative for CRA correspondence, and must remit HST through the BN's GST/HST account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity election, and hybrid mismatch elections are not available to Canadian limited partnerships. The LP is statutorily fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act; income and losses flow through to partners. Available statutory elections include: (i) a partnership fiscal period election under subsection 249.1(1) to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (ii) a non-resident partner's election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act with respect to Canadian-source rental and timber royalties; and (iii) various CRA administrative elections related to partnership fiscal reporting, such as the designation of a limited partner's share under the limited partnership audit rules. The pass-through classification is mandatory and cannot be changed by contractual agreement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold on taxable supplies made in Canada and, for non-residents, measured on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters. Once the threshold is exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. There is no separate Ontario VAT threshold; Ontario's 8% provincial component is collected through the single federal HST account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 22 of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods that are exported by the supplier, or supplied to a non-resident recipient for export, with export occurring within reasonable time. Services are zero-rated under sections 7 and 9 of Schedule V when supplied to a non-resident recipient, where the service is performed wholly or substantially outside Canada or is otherwise an exported service; for ESG/carbon accounting and offset brokerage services performed for non-resident clients, the supply is generally zero-rated provided the service is not in connection with Canadian real property or Canadian tangible personal property and the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant. Documentation must demonstrate the export, the foreign nature of the recipient, and the absence of Canadian consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CUSMA or via Canadian Export Reporting System / G7 Electronic Data Interchange)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport evidence showing the goods or deliverables left Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT wire confirmation, or equivalent banking evidence showing foreign currency repatriation to Canada
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract with the non-resident recipient
  • CRA zero-rating reconciliation and periodic export listing

Canada and Ontario currently impose no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping requirement for invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and retain all supporting books and records in a readable and permanently accessible electronic format. CRA audits may require e-invoices, CSV/XML extracts, and source accounting records; however, there is no national e-invoicing clearance platform or mandatory digital authorization code.

CRA scrutiny of export zero-rating claims is evidence-intensive. Where documentation is incomplete, the CRA may deny the zero-rating, reassess the LP for uncollected GST/HST, and charge arrears interest and penalties under the Excise Tax Act. A false statement on a GST/HST return can attract a penalty under subsection 285(1) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated net tax. Knowingly or under gross negligence making false statements can lead to an additional 50% penalty, criminal prosecution under section 326 of the Excise Tax Act, and retrospective clawback of refunds for up to four years after assessment, with unlimited reassessment periods where misrepresentation is fraudulent or wilful.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; no prior approval is required for opening CAD/USD accounts, bringing in foreign capital, or remitting profits. Non-resident limited partners may receive capital-return distributions without exchange-control restriction and income allocations without withholding where the partner files Part I returns or the partnership makes the subsection 216(2) election; however, non-resident management fees, most interest on participating debt, and certain specified payments are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding, reducible under the Canada-US Tax Convention or other applicable treaties. FATCA and CRS self-certifications are mandatory for foreign partners, and all wire transfers in excess of CAD 10,000 attract FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) obligations and Payments Canada Rule 7 travel-rule information transmission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratios for receivable balances, and no repatriation timeline for non-resident invoices collected in foreign currency; exporters and professional-services suppliers may freely retain CAD, USD, or EUR in licensed foreign-currency accounts. For this carbon-accounting and offset-brokerage engagement, no commodity-surrender or licensing obligation applies; the sole statutory crossing requirements are the CBSA CAD 10,000 currency declaration on physical transport of monetary instruments and FINTRAC's CAD 10,000 reporting thresholds for cash, EFTs, and large virtual-currency transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises statutory mandate over the Canadian dollar under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act; exchange-control and cross-border reporting domains are jointly administered by the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border currency declarations, and Payments Canada for clearing-system governance across Lynx and ACSS.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict constraints. Canada does not recognize the European e-money-institution licence, so offshore digital EMIs may only operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or, for pure payment services, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) oversight administered by the Bank of Canada. Platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, or OFX are suitable for low-value remittance corridors but cannot serve as escrow-grade counterparties for capital commitments or partnership treasury accounts because they lack Schedule I bank prudential standing, CDIC eligibility, and direct Lynx/ACSS membership. The blueprint therefore restricts all escrow and operational balances to chartered banks while permitting fintech rails solely for ancillary fee settlements, subject to a T+0 zero-float policy.
