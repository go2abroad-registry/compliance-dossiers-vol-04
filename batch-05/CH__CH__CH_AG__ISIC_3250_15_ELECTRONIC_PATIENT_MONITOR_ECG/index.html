Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC) — responsible for federal VAT and direct federal tax; cantonal tax administrations for cantonal and municipal taxes. Main portal: www.estv.admin.ch; Swiss business register / UID portal: www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and Swiss corporations must first be registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) to obtain the permanent Swiss Company Identification Number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID), which also serves as the tax TIN and VAT number (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx). For VAT purposes, the entity must file the official tax registration form with ESTV through the electronic VAT portal, providing expected Swiss taxable turnover, activity description, and a Swiss representative or address if not domiciled in Switzerland. Registration is compulsory once Swiss taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per annum; voluntary registration is available from the first franc. The registered UID must appear on invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities and Swiss corporations must first be registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) to obtain the permanent Swiss Company Identification Number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID), which also serves as the tax TIN and VAT number (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx). For VAT purposes, the entity must file the official tax registration form with ESTV through the electronic VAT portal, providing expected Swiss taxable turnover, activity description, and a Swiss representative or address if not domiciled in Switzerland. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Swiss law; an Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily taxed as a separate legal entity. Available elections are limited to substantive reliefs claimed in the annual tax return: participation relief reducing taxable dividends/capital gains on qualifying substantial holdings, patent box relief for qualifying IP income, R&D super deduction, and VAT group treatment for closely related entities. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are neutralized under Swiss implementation of OECD BEPS and the Swiss VAT Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT when they are physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination, the exporter holds title at the time of export, and the export is confirmed by Swiss Customs in the export declaration. Services qualify for zero-rating when the place of supply is outside Switzerland under B2B or B2C place-of-supply rules, including the effective use and enjoyment criterion. The supplier must issue an invoice indicating 0% VAT and maintain full evidentiary documentation proving the goods left Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss e-dec/NCTS export declaration and export confirmation) with movement reference number identifying the goods and exit from Switzerland
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing transportation and physical arrival of the goods outside Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/MT950 or credit advice) evidencing receipt of export sales proceeds from the foreign buyer

No government-mandated real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or pre-clearance requirement currently exists in Switzerland. Electronic invoicing is optional but widely supported through the Swiss Peppol network (BIS 3.0, QR-bill XML formats). VAT returns must be transmitted electronically to ESTV via the secure Swiss VAT online filing portal, and taxpayers must retain books, invoices, and export evidence for statutory retention periods.

Incorrect or fictitious export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment with interest and administrative fines up to four times the evaded VAT in cases of intentional evasion (Art. 70 Swiss VAT Act). Creating false customs declarations, forged shipping documents, or counterfeit proof of export constitutes tax fraud and forgery under the Swiss Criminal Code, punishable by imprisonment and/or monetary fines. ESTV and Swiss Customs may conduct post-clearance audits and deny refund claims until complete export documentation is produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible in law and practice on both current and capital account. Switzerland imposes no exchange controls, no approval thresholds for cross-border payments, and no repatriation time limits; the Swiss Constitution's property guarantee and freedom-of-trade protections shield international transfers from administrative blocking. Dividends from the AG are freely remittable; the only fiscal levy is the 35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which is reduced or refunded under applicable double-taxation treaties, and no capital-export tax or transfer tax applies. Practical frictions arise solely from Swiss anti-money-laundering (AMLA/GwG) transaction-clarity documentation duties, the Lex Koller real-estate restrictions on foreign buyers (not applicable to a manufacturing company), and, since 1 July 2025, the Federal Act on the Screening of Investments, which requires clearance only for acquisitions of Swiss companies operating in specific critical sectors and does not constrain routine treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Swiss law prescribes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no earmarking or surrender of export receipts. An ISIC 3250 medical-device exporter may lawfully hold, lend, hedge, net, or reinvest foreign-currency proceeds without central-bank consent and may convert at will in the interbank market. The sole binding obligations are statistical: residents with cumulative cross-border financial assets or liabilities above the regulatory thresholds must file periodic SNB balance-of-payments and direct-investment schedules, and banks must retain underlying trade documentation (contracts, invoices, transport evidence) to satisfy AMLA transparency requirements. These are reporting and record-keeping duties, not exchange-control restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank, French: Banque nationale suisse, Italian: Banca Nazionale Svizzera), established under the National Bank Act of 3 October 2003, with head offices in Berne and Zurich. Banking supervision is vested separately in the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) under the Financial Market Supervision Act (FINMASA) of 22 June 2007. The SNB conducts monetary policy, manages systemic stability, operates through the SIC and euroSIC payment and settlement systems (run with SIX Interbank Clearing), administers international reserves, and maintains Switzerland's balance-of-payments statistical reporting regime. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency analogous to a SAFE-type authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a standalone primary rail; acceptable only as a supplementary disbursement channel. Switzerland does not operate the EU/UK e-money institution (EMI) licence category: FINMA offers a fintech-bank licence (Art. 1b Banking Act; public deposits up to CHF 100 million without interest or investments) and a separate sandbox exemption, while foreign digital EMIs are not authorised to take Swiss deposits and cannot issue or confirm letters of credit. For a medical-device hardware distributor purchasing components in Asia and invoicing hospitals worldwide, a FINMA-regulated Tier-1 bank is effectively mandatory for MT103 execution, LC issuance, and the statutory capital-liberation escrow. An overseas licensed EMI may serve only incidental flows (remote-employee payroll cards, e-commerce receivables) with a daily sweep into the Swiss operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders wire subscription proceeds via SWIFT MT103 into the AG's dedicated capital-liberation escrow account, narrating the purpose as an equity subscription (Zeichnung / Einzahlung) and quoting the bank's inward BIC and the escrow account number. The bank issues the capital-payment confirmation (Einzahlungs- and Liberierungsbestätigung) required for the Swiss Commercial Register filing, and releases the funds to the operating account only after registration and the board's declaration that the funds are at the company's free disposal. For balance-of-payments purposes the inflow is classified as direct-investment equity capital and captured in SNB statistical returns at the applicable cumulative thresholds. Banks perform standard sanctions screening, UBO verification, and source-of-funds checks (notarised board resolutions, subscription list, and provenance statements) before value date. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An ordinary audit (ordentliche Revision) is mandatory for an Aktiengesellschaft if it exceeds two of three thresholds for two consecutive years: balance sheet total CHF 20 million, annual net turnover CHF 40 million, or average annual workforce of 250 full-time employees; it is also mandatory for listed companies, companies with outstanding collective bond issues, and companies whose shareholders representing at least 10% of the share capital request an ordinary audit. If an ordinary audit is not required, a limited statutory audit (eingeschränkte Revision) is mandatory unless the company validly opts out; opting out is available only where the company has fewer than 10 full-time employees, the shareholders' meeting adopts an annual opting-out resolution, and no shareholder holding at least 10% of the share capital demands an auditor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) levied on post-cantonal-tax profit, equivalent to 7.83% on the pre-tax base where cantonal profit tax is deductible; plus cantonal and communal profit taxes ranging from approximately 3.5% (Zug) to 12.0% (Geneva). Combined statutory headline rate for the reference Zug location: approximately 11.9%; national combined range: 11.9%-21.1% depending on canton.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross cash dividends, deemed dividends, and non-monetary distributions of a Swiss AG; withholding obligation rests with the AG and its paying agent; the tax is refundable or creditable under domestic law only to Swiss residents, while non-residents may claim relief under an applicable double tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Statutory filing deadline: within six months after the close of the commercial fiscal year under DBG Art. 124 for the federal direct tax; cantonal ordinances commonly impose 31 March for calendar-year companies, with automatic or discretionary extension to 30 September or later; arrears interest accrues from the original due date at approximately 4.5% per annum.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border related-party transactions (goods, services, royalties, or financing) triggers the obligation to prepare and present a transfer pricing study (Local File) on demand of the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and cantonal tax authorities; the TP annexe to the corporate tax return is mandatory for taxpayers with material related-party transactions; Master File obligations apply to taxpayers forming part of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CHF 900 million or more; country-by-country reporting (CbC, under LBG SR 642.2) applies to Swiss entities of groups whose consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CHF 900 million. Swiss practice follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines including the 2022 updates; no statutory penalty for non-documentation in itself, but failure to submit the TP study on request shifts the burden of proof and triggers estimated or adjusted assessments with fines for procedural non-compliance up to CHF 10,000.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to UAE or BVI, but economic substance is a determinative condition for treaty benefits, withholding tax relief, and specific tax incentives under the 2019 STAF package. The OEDC MLI PPT as implemented for Swiss treaties requires the principal purpose of an arrangement to be genuine business and not merely tax advantage; the FTA denies DTA relief to letter-box companies lacking real management, local staff, premises, and decision-making power. The federal GAAR under DBG Art. 56 and VStG Art. 21 disregards abusive corporate arrangements without commercial substance. For the Swiss patent box, the underlying qualifying IP must be developed in substantial Swiss R&D substance, and the company must continuously perform R&D activities in Switzerland; the participation relief presupposes the company is not merely a holding conduit. In practice, a CH_AG manufacturing patient monitors in Switzerland must maintain physical operations, qualified employees, board meetings in Switzerland, Swiss bank accounts, and full accounting substance; foreign ownership is permitted, but the registered office cannot be administratively empty. Pillar Two: as of 1 January 2024 Switzerland applies the OECD global minimum tax (Supplementary Tax Act) of 15% top-up assessment to Swiss constituent entities of multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, which overrides low effective cantonal taxation only for in-scope groups.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to the amount of federal tax evaded in standard cases, increased to up to three times the tax evaded in serious cases with false or destroyed records; interest on arrears; publication of final criminal judgments; administrative penalty in unpaid federal tax at 4.5% annual default interest; in aggravated instances criminal prosecution under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 251 forgery of documents) with custodial sentences of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Board members, executives, and the external auditor who instructed, arranged, or aided the misdeclaration are jointly liable and face personal fines under DBG Art. 181; direct criminal liability under SCC Art. 251 and Art. 305bis for falsification and money laundering; civil liability to the company and shareholders under CO Art. 754; disqualification from board service in Switzerland and severe reputational damage affecting global medical-device tenders and banking relationships.
