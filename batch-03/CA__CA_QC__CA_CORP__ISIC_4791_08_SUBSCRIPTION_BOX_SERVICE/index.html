E-government filing portal

(1) Apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; a non-resident corporation must provide constitutive documents, foreign corporate registration number, and a Canadian agent or contact address where no Canadian director exists. (2) Open the GST/HST account (RT) on the BN; register even if below the small-supplier threshold when carrying on business in Canada and seeking input tax credit refunds. (3) Register with Revenu Québec for QST and obtain a Québec establishment number (NEQ) using the Revenu Québec 'Register for a tax number' digital service. (4) File T2 corporate returns with CRA and Quebec corporate tax returns with Revenu Québec on a fiscal-year basis. (5) Maintain an export-documentation file (CBSA declarations, bills of lading, invoices, wire-transfer proofs) to substantiate export zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; legal-form classification governs (Business Corporation = entity-level taxpayer). Statutory elections available to the taxpayer include: ITA s. 216 election (net-basis taxation of Canadian-source rental income for non-residents); ITA s. 217 election (net-basis taxation of certain trust or estate payments to non-residents); ITA s. 93(1) election (deemed-disposition basis for foreign-affiliate FAPI distributions); ITA s. 87 amalgamation elections; and GST/HST annual-return election available to qualifying registrants under ETA s. 243. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 18.6 and 20.2(2)) are mandatory (no election) and disallow deductions in hybrid and branch-mismatch arrangements for taxation years commencing after 31 December 2023, with transitional timing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 of taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current quarter; non-residents measure only taxable supplies made in Canada. Registration is mandatory regardless of threshold when the non-resident carries on business in Canada or seeks input tax credit refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Sch. VI, Part V, s. 1, zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property that the supplier exports as part of the supply and for which documentary evidence of export exists, provided the property is not released into, or acquired for consumption or use in, Canada before export. Under Sch. VI, Part V, s. 7, services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is for use wholly outside Canada. For the subscription-box model, the physical-box component shipped to a foreign billing or shipping address is zero-rated at 0% GST/QST, including ancillary freight supplied with the exported goods; the exporter retains full input tax credits on costs despite the 0% rate. Goods must be exported 'as soon as practicable' (CRA administrative comfort: within 60 days of delivery) and the supplier must retain export proof to claim refunds on a regular GST/HST return.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A or Electronic Export Report via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms (FOB/FCA), ultimate non-resident consignee, and HS/NAICS classification
  • Bank inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103 or foreign-currency credit advice) — Canadian equivalent of the Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time government clearance or digital stamping of invoices exists under GST/HST or QST. Tax is self-assessed through periodic returns; invoices must contain the prescribed ETA s. 223 information and be retained. Electronic filing is mandatory for GST/HST registrants with annual revenues over CAD $1M (CRA), and Revenu Québec requires electronic remittance for large registrants. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is being adopted voluntarily for federal and provincial procurement (phased 2024-2026); there is no API-clearance or QR-code stamping requirement for ordinary B2C subscription-box exports.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export supply as taxable where documentary proof is absent or inadequate, requiring the exporter to remit 5% GST plus applicable QST (if the place of supply is Quebec) with compound daily interest under ETA s. 280 and penalties. Specifically: gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax amount (ETA s. 285.1); civil penalty of 200% of the tax evaded (ETA s. 326); criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment for evasion (ETA ss. 327-329); and CBSA administrative monetary penalties or goods seizure under the Customs Act. Revenu Québec applies parallel interest, penalties, and reassessments under the Tax Administration Act (CQLR c. A-6.002, ss. 71-97) and can claw back QST input tax refunds. Non-arm's length export sales with unpaid or deferred proceeds are treated as transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s. 247 (10% penalty on the adjustment) plus imputed interest under ITA s. 80.4 if proceeds are not received within a reasonable time (CRA administrative expectation of 180 days).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible, independently floating currency. Canada imposes no current-account or capital-account convertibility restrictions: imports, exports, cross-border e-commerce settlements, royalty and licence fees, intercompany financing, equity injections, and profit repatriation may all be settled in CAD or any freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada authorisation. The practical impediments for a Quebec subscription-box corporation are tax-and-reporting compliance rather than FX control: (i) non-resident dividend withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5%-15% under income tax treaties such as the Canada-US treaty and other G7/G20 double-taxation conventions), (ii) provincial obligations in Quebec (GST/HST and Quebec QST registration for importation of goods from abroad and for e-commerce merchant settlement), and (iii) international information returns such as Form T106 for non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents and Form T1135 for foreign property holdings above CAD 100,000. None of these constitutes exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds. A Canadian subscription-box exporter may retain export and cross-border merchant settlement receipts in CAD, USD, EUR, or any other currency, hold them in foreign-currency bank accounts abroad, and convert them to CAD at market rates at a time of its choosing. The only legal touchpoints applicable to the treasury are FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reports and Electronic Funds Transfer Reports at the CAD 10,000 threshold, Global Affairs Canada economic-sanctions screening, and the requirement that financial statements and the Quebec tax return correctly declare foreign currency position and realised/unrealised FX gains.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the domestic central bank and issuing authority for the Canadian dollar; however, Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority. FX-related mandates are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada (financial-sector policy and economic sanctions), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, which prudentially supervises federally incorporated banks and takes no exchange-convertibility decisions), FINTRAC (which enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, PCMLTFA, including cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting), and Payments Canada (which operates the Lynx high-value payment system and the Automated Clearing Settlement System).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as an auxiliary settlement rail, Medium as a primary liquidity rail. The European Union/United Kingdom Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence has no statutory equivalent in the Canadian Banking Act; offshore digital banks and fintech rails (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are legally usable by a Quebec-incorporated corporation as prepaid pass-through accounts, but they cannot obtain independent membership in Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS systems and therefore cannot originate Canadian domestic EFT/PAD debits or receive local CAD direct-deposit settlement in the national clearing scheme. For the ISIC 4791 subscription-box model, an offshore EMI is viable for: (1) receiving credit-card settlement from Stripe Billing in multicurrency payout balances and converting at interbank-plus-margin; (2) paying international suppliers (3PL, packaging, product sourcing) by SWIFT MT103; (3) holding FX working capital outside the Canadian banking perimeter. It is not viable as a replacement for a Schedule I bank account in Quebec for payroll, QST/GST remittance, collection of pre-authorized debits from domestic subscribers, or merchant settlement of Visa/Mastercard transactions through Canadian acquirers. The treasury blueprint therefore mandates one lead bank account at a Schedule I bank or Desjardins caisse for domestic rails, with the EMI layered as a secondary FX and international-payments tool.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended inbound rails. (1) Equity and shareholder-loan injections are executed by SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers to the designated CAD operating account. If both the remitting and beneficiary banks are Payments Canada Lynx participants, the credit is final and irrevocable in central-bank money on the same business day; sub-CAD 100,000 flows settle normally on T+1 through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). (2) Merchant settlement from Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net is routed through their Canadian acquiring entities as ACSS credit-file settlements to the corporate settlement account, reflecting a one-day funding delay and a standard merchant-clear charge. (3) Purpose coding: Canada imposes no forced FX-purpose-code regime, but the treasury must instruct each incoming wire to carry an accurate remittance narrative in the SWIFT Remittance Information field (e.g., Equity Injection, Shareholder Loan - Working Capital, Subscription Revenue - Cross-Border E-Commerce, Supplier Refund); banks use this narrative to underpin FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting at the CAD 10,000 level and to characterise the flow for CRA Form T106 and income-tax-treaty purposes. (4) File verification: the account-opening compliance file must pre-contain the share subscription agreement, board minutes authorising capital raising, a certified share-register extract, and bank reference statements for any non-bank originator. There is no central-bank approval or exchange-control permit step in Canada.The clearing-rail matrix for outbound settlements is: (i) SWIFT MT103 (or MT103 COV where third-party correspondents are interposed) for direct payment to foreign suppliers of subscription-box goods, providing full remittance instructions, end-to-end tracking, and same-day value where both sides are connected to the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT); (ii) commercial documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600, with document examination under ISBP 745 and, where the beneficiary requires a bank undertaking in the seller's country, a confirmed credit as the structured instrument for high-value ocean-freight or air-cargo inventory purchases from Asia; (iii) USD-denominated disbursements executed without double conversion by drawing on a USD operating account and clearing through Fedwire or CHIPS via the Canadian bank's US-dollar endpoint; (iv) domestic CAD disbursements to Quebec/Ontario warehouses, couriers, and 3PLs via ACSS EFT credits, or by a same-day Lynx payment for urgent or high-value outlays; and (v) recurring treasury obligations (hosting, SaaS licences, customs-broker fees) implemented by pre-authorized debits under the applicable Payments Canada rulebook. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High as an auxiliary settlement rail, Medium as a primary liquidity rail. The European Union/United Kingdom Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence has no statutory equivalent in the Canadian Banking Act; offshore digital banks and fintech rails (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are legally usable by a Quebec-incorporated corporation as prepaid pass-through accounts, but they cannot obtain independent membership in Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS systems and therefore cannot originate Canadian domestic EFT/PAD debits or receive local CAD direct-deposit settlement in the national clearing scheme. For the ISIC 4791 subscription-box model, an offshore EMI is viable for: (1) receiving credit-card settlement from Stripe Billing in multicurrency payout balances and converting at interbank-plus-margin; (2) paying international suppliers (3PL, packaging, product sourcing) by SWIFT MT103; (3) holding FX working capital outside the Canadian banking perimeter. It is not viable as a replacement for a Schedule I bank account in Quebec for payroll, QST/GST remittance, collection of pre-authorized debits from domestic subscribers, or merchant settlement of Visa/Mastercard transactions through Canadian acquirers. The treasury blueprint therefore mandates one lead bank account at a Schedule I bank or Desjardins caisse for domestic rails, with the EMI layered as a secondary FX and international-payments tool.
