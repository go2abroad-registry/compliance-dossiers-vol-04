Available election forms

A partnership is not a taxable entity for income tax purposes and no check-the-box election exists; instead, the Income Tax Act provides a deemed Canadian partnership concept (all partners resident in Canada) and rules under subsection 96(3) for fiscal period and reporting elections; the Excise Tax Act section 150 election allows a partnership to treat itself as a separate person for GST/HST netting with closely related corporations; hybrid mismatch (BEAT) election under subsection 18.2(11) ITA to designate the excess interest and financing expense limitation rules.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services made in Canada); for a non-resident partnership, registration is also mandatory where it carries on a commercial activity in Canada or solicits orders from persons in Canada via an agent; Ontario EDT (Employer Health Tax) is a separate provincial payroll levy with no analogous goods-supply threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating and fully convertible currency under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act, with no exchange-control or prior-approval regime for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance by a non-resident limited partner of an Ontario LP is constrained only by Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25 percent, treaty-reduced where applicable, e.g., 15 percent under the Canada-US Treaty), CRA NR4 and NR401 filing obligations, transfer-pricing compliance under ITA s.247, and thin-capitalization limits under ITA s.18(4)-(5). No central-bank approval or FX license is required for conversions or dividend distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation requirements for services rendered by a Canadian LP. CAD equivalent gains and losses are nonetheless recognized for tax under ITA s.39, and functional currency reporting is available only for CAD or USD under ITA s.261. FINTRAC requires reporting of all cross-border electronic fund transfers equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000 and all large cash transactions equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000, including record-keeping of the transaction purpose, counterparties, and settlement instructions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registry administrator; financial intelligence oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

MEDIUM — offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, CurrencyCloud) are legally permissible as ancillary collection or disbursement gateways, but are not a valid substitute for a Schedule I bank settlement account. Under the Bank Act, only a bank may accept deposits, and CDIC deposit insurance is available exclusively at CDIC-member institutions. Offshore EMIs active in Canada must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and, since November 2024, register under the Bank of Canada-administered Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). They also lack LVTS/Lynx settlement finality, cannot serve as a CRA Part XIII remittance account, and may weaken treaty-based tax characterization for a pass-through LP. EMI balances should be capped below CAD $25,000 and reserved for low-value retainer fee collection or sub-consultant payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner capital is injected by SWIFT MT103 to the designated Capital Escrow Account (CAD or USD) at the Schedule I bank, quoting the LP's CRA Business Number and purpose code 'Capital contribution — Limited Partnership Agreement'. Each credit equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000 activates a FINTRAC EFT report (Form F400) that must include the remitting partner's identity, source-of-funds documentation, and the governing capital-call voucher. US client retainer fees may enter via the ACH rail into a USD clearing account (provisioned with the US ABA routing number of the Canadian bank's US subsidiary, e.g., TD Bank N.A. or BMO Harris), then be swept intraday under a recorded spot FX contract to the CAD operational account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax in Canada. Combined headline corporate rate for an Ontario-resident corporate partner is 15% federal general + 10% Ontario general = 25%; individual partners are taxed at progressive rates up to approximately 53.53% combined federal/Ontario top marginal rate. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income and may be treaty-protected if no permanent establishment exists.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited to non-residents. For a Limited Partnership, distributions of Canadian-source business income to a non-resident limited partner are not technically dividends; they are taxed under Part I or Part XIII as applicable, with the 25% Part XIII rate applying to amounts that are deemed dividends or passive distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: mandatory contemporaneous Local File/Form TP-01 disclosure applies when the total annual value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions between the Ontario LP and the overseas PR group equals or exceeds CAD 1 million; a Master File is mandatory when the multinational enterprise group reports global consolidated revenue of at least CAD 1 billion. Form T106 reporting is required for reportable transactions regardless of tax adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to CARICOM or UAE ESR. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act) and the judicial 'economic substance' or 'sham' doctrine deny tax benefits from artificial transactions lacking a real business purpose. The Ontario LP must maintain actual substance: physical office, employees, client contracts, bank accounts, and board-approved budgets. Absence of substance triggers GAAR recharacterization, treaty denial under limitation-on-benefits clauses, and, where the overseas parent is resident in an ESR jurisdiction, independent ESR reporting obligations in the parent's jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; on indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% and imprisonment up to 5 years. Gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax; interest compounds at the prescribed rate; CRA may freeze bank accounts and seize receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings and GST/HST under subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and section 323 of the Excise Tax Act, subject only to a due-diligence defence. Criminal conviction exposes directors to personal fines, restitution orders, and imprisonment; a serious financial crime conviction may trigger inadmissibility or removal under the Canadian Immigration and Refugee Protection Act for non-resident principals.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Unpriced Intercompany Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA will make a transfer pricing adjustment under subsection 247(2) to the arm's length amount; the taxpayer must pay additional federal and Ontario corporate tax plus interest. Failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation invokes the 10% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment amount; late-filed T106/TP-01 forms attract per-month information penalties; repeat or deliberate conduct escalates to the 50% gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T106 declarations or knowingly approve overpriced management fees can be assessed for the 50% gross negligence penalty in their personal capacity if they caused the misrepresentation; CRA can seek a court order for civil restitution, and officers of the foreign parent who direct the sham are exposed to joint and several assessment and cross-border prosecutorial referral.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes service income as distributions subject to Part XIII withholding; CRA may apply a 25% GAAR penalty on unreported avoidance benefits; the Ontario Registrar may administratively dissolve the LP for failure to maintain a valid Declaration; treaty relief is denied under the principal purpose test where the arrangement lacks economic substance.

Director personal liability: Under subsection 14 of the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, a limited partner who takes part in control of the business forfeits limited liability and becomes personally liable for all partnership obligations as if a general partner; the corporate general partner's directors remain liable for partnership debts and unremitted withholdings, and may face travel restrictions through court-ordered undertakings or criminal proceedings in egregious cases.
Withholding Agent Failure on Distributions to Non-Resident Partners

Statutory sanction: Subsection 215(1) makes the partnership liable for the full 25% unremitted withholding on the gross payment, plus accrued interest and late-filing penalties under section 162; NR4 filing violations attract separate information penalties; CRA may assess the general partner directly for the amount and issue a Requirement to Pay to the partnership's banks.

Director personal liability: Subsection 227.1(1) imposes personal liability on directors who failed to exercise reasonable care to remit the withheld amounts; civil liability includes the outstanding withholding, interest, and penalties; criminal liability under section 239 applies where the failure is part of a deliberate scheme to repatriate profits, resulting in fines up to 200% of the amount and imprisonment up to 5 years.
