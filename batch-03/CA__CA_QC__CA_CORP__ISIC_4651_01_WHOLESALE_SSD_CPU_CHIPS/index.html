Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through the Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account; for Quebec, Revenu Québec (RQ) through the Quebec Business Number (NEQ) and registre des entreprises du Québec.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Quebec must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a corporate income tax account via CRA Business Registration Online. If the corporation makes taxable supplies other than zero-rated supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, it must open a GST/HST account under the same BN. For Quebec, the corporation must register with Revenu Québec for QST purposes, obtaining a Quebec Business Number (NEQ) and a QST account. Where the non-resident corporation has no physical presence in Quebec, registration may still be required based on solicitation, inventory, or deemed permanent establishment rules; a representative fiscale is commonly required for CRA and Revenu Québec communications.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Quebec must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a corporate income tax account via CRA Business Registration Online. If the corporation makes taxable supplies other than zero-rated supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, it must open a GST/HST account under the same BN. For Quebec, the corporation must register with Revenu Québec for QST purposes, obtaining a Quebec Business Number (NEQ) and a QST account. Where the non-resident corporation has no physical presence in Quebec, registration may still be required based on solicitation, inventory, or deemed permanent establishment rules; a representative fiscale is commonly required for CRA and Revenu Québec communications.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. A business corporation is always a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level federal and provincial income tax. Relevant elections include: (i) the section 216 ITA election permitting a non-resident to compute Canadian-source rental or timber royalties on a net basis; (ii) GST/HST related-party or joint venture elections; (iii) the small supplier election to accelerate GST/HST registration; and (iv) CRA-administered deemed-dividend rules for hybrid mismatch concerns. Quebec provincial tax classification follows the federal classification; no provincial check-the-box election exists.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
14.975%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
C$30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST and QST small-supplier status; registration becomes mandatory once a person exceeds C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Non-resident sellers may be required to register without the normal threshold where goods are drop-shipped or inventory is located in Canada.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Supplies of goods qualify for 0% GST/HST and QST if the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier, transferred to a non-resident, and delivered outside Canada as part of a continuous chain of transportation. A supply of services to a non-resident is eligible for zero-rating under Schedule VI, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act unless it relates to real property in Canada, goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is performed wholly in Canada in relation to a person who is not a non-resident. For open-market wholesale trading of SSD and CPU chips, the seller must maintain an unbroken chain of transport documentation from the Canadian supply point to the foreign customer, including transit through bonded logistics if applicable.

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance API, or digital stamping mandate. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices if they are complete, intelligible, and retained to support an audit. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL interoperability are voluntary standards for e-invoicing. There is no statutory requirement to obtain prior CRA or Revenu Québec validation of individual export invoices for zero-rating purposes.

      
CRA and Revenu Québec cross-match export declarations with freight manifests, payment data, and corporate income tax filings. Missing or inconsistent export documentation causes the zero-rating to be denied and the full 14.975% output tax to be assessed, plus interest from the original return due date. False export claims with knowingly false statements attract penalties up to the greater of C$25,000 and 100% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus potential director liability, penalty assessments under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, and a four-year normal reassessment period that is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is established. Fines for wilful evasion can reach up to 200% of the evaded tax in egregious cases, and export refund claims are subject to enhanced audit scrutiny where the foreign buyer is related or payment is repatriated later than the contractual remittance deadline.

Current-account convertibility

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
26.5% combined (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). Federal rate derives from the 38% base less 10% provincial abatement less 13% General Rate Reduction.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under Part XIII ITA (s. 212(2)); reduced by applicable tax treaty, commonly 5% where the beneficial owner is a company controlling at least 10% of the voting stock and 15% in portfolio cases.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
T2 due within 6 months of the fiscal year-end (December 31 year-end is due June 30). Final Part I balance due 2 months after year-end (3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction); Part XIII WHT remitted by the 15th of the month following payment.

      

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
CO-17 due within 6 months of the fiscal year-end; Quebec balance of tax due 2 months after year-end (3 months for an eligible CCPC). Late instalments attract prescribed interest.

              T2 Schedule 4 and provincial abatement lines
              Apportionment of taxable income among provinces plus the 10% federal provincial abatement.
              Allocate income to Quebec using gross revenue and payroll weights of the Quebec establishment. A foreign parent cannot direct an arbitrary override of the statutory allocation factors.
              ITA s. 235: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; repeat-filing penalty 10% plus 2% monthly. Automatic late-filing penalty under ITA s. 162(1): CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500. Arrears interest compounds daily at the prescribed rate.
            

            
            

              T2 Line 350
              General Rate Reduction: 13% of the lesser of taxable income allocated to a province and income not eligible for the Small Business Deduction.
              Wholesale gross margin is generally eligible; claim the reduction only on income allocated to Canadian provinces.
              ITA s. 235: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; repeat-filing penalty 10% plus 2% monthly. Automatic late-filing penalty under ITA s. 162(1): CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500. Arrears interest compounds daily at the prescribed rate.
            

            
            

              T2 Schedule 21 and Line 700
              Foreign tax credit claim and final federal tax payable after credits.
              Claim the federal foreign tax credit only for true foreign-source income and foreign taxes; a Canadian wholesale corporation cannot use an FTC to erase Part I tax on Canadian distribution income.
              ITA s. 235: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; repeat-filing penalty 10% plus 2% monthly. Automatic late-filing penalty under ITA s. 162(1): CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500. Arrears interest compounds daily at the prescribed rate.
            

            
            

              CO-17 Section A
              Reconciliation of federal taxable income to Quebec taxable income through Quebec tax adjustments.
              A corporation resident in Quebec pays Quebec tax on business income allocated to Quebec; non-resident shareholders do not compute Quebec corporate tax on their own account.
              Parallel late-filing penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act (5% plus 1% monthly up to 12 months); daily penalty for failure to file an information return; interest compounded daily at the Quebec prescribed rate; denial of the 10% federal abatement restoration not available until the Quebec return is filed.
            

            
            

              CO-17 Section C (General rate)
              Application of the 11.5% Quebec general corporate rate.
              No Quebec rate reduction or holiday exists for wholesale computer-component brokerage; compute the tax strictly on Quebec-allocated taxable income.
              Parallel late-filing penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act (5% plus 1% monthly up to 12 months); daily penalty for failure to file an information return; interest compounded daily at the Quebec prescribed rate; denial of the 10% federal abatement restoration not available until the Quebec return is filed.
            

            
            

              CO-17 Schedule of loss utilization
              Non-capital loss carryforward and carryback application for Quebec purposes.
              Quebec follows the 20-year carryforward / 3-year carryback of the federal non-capital loss regime; losses of a predecessor foreign entity cannot be used unless transferred under an amalgamation or winding-up.
              Parallel late-filing penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act (5% plus 1% monthly up to 12 months); daily penalty for failure to file an information return; interest compounded daily at the Quebec prescribed rate; denial of the 10% federal abatement restoration not available until the Quebec return is filed.
            

            
          
        

      

    

    

      
Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: T106 and RC4643 disclosures are mandatory where aggregate transactions between the Canadian wholesale corporation and each non-arm's length non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000 per fiscal year. CRA records thresholds under TPM-09: CAD 5,000,000 for tangible goods, CAD 1,000,000 for loans, services and intangible property. A Local File is expected at these levels; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group consolidates revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Quebec has no GCC-style Economic Substance Regulation filing. Substance is enforced through the GAAR (ITA s. 245), Quebec anti-avoidance provisions, CFC/FAPI attribution (ITA ss. 91-95), and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test embedded in Canada's treaties. A Quebec shell with no staff, office, management, physical inventory, or commercial risk will lose treaty protection and be reassessed as a conduit. Demonstrated substance: local employees performing procurement and sales, title passage in Quebec, storage or inspection of SSD/CPU inventory in Canada, and locally maintained books and records.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax evasion / wilful omission
        
Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2)). Criminal conviction under ITA s. 239: fine of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec parallel penalties under the Taxation Act. Reassessment window extends to 10 years for misrepresentation.

        
Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII WHT; personal assets subject to garnishment; departure prevention orders and passport seizure; directed civil restitution; tax evasion conviction with imprisonment up to 5 years.

      

      
      

        Shell company / artificial conduit
        
Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment (ITA s. 245) eliminating the tax benefit; deemed dividend Part XIII WHT at 25%; treaty benefits denied under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; 50% gross negligence penalty where facts were misrepresented; interest compounded daily.

        
Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors exposed for counselling unremittance; personal assessments for the 25% WHT default; director disqualification; asset-seizure orders and travel bans.

      

      
      

        Transfer pricing mispricing
        
Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) adjustment to the arm's length amount; penalty under s. 247(3) of 10% of the net upward adjustment plus prescribed interest; does not apply where the taxpayer made reasonable efforts and contemporaneous documentation exists.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve false intercompany invoices may be jointly liable for the penalty and interest; criminal exposure for fraudulent manufacture of books; bar from directorship under corporate law upon fraud conviction.

      

      
      

        AML / anti-money-laundering red flag
        
Statutory sanction: Laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s. 462.31: indictable offence with imprisonment up to 10 years and asset forfeiture; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per corporation under the PCMLTFA; tax evasion reassessment on undeclared proceeds.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who control wire tables and fail to implement AML/CFT controls may be prosecuted personally; travel bans, asset freezes, civil restitution orders, and voidance of D&O insurance for intentional misconduct.
