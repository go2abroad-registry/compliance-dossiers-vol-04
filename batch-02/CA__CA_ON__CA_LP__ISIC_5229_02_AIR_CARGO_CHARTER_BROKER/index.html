Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — GST/HST and business registration via 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); export and border clearance authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) with the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) and G7 electronic export reporting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or operating an Ontario limited partnership obtains the statutory Canadian Business Number (BN) as the universal tax identifier, then activates program accounts. Step 1: Register the LP with ServiceOntario (Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery); Ontario LP requirements include a general partner resident in Ontario or an appointed Ontario attorney for service of process. Step 2: Apply through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 for the 9-digit Base BN. Step 3: Open the GST/HST program account (program code 'RT') within the same application; a non-resident registrant with no Canadian director or place of business files through the CRA International Tax and Large Business Directorate or may voluntarily register under ETA s. 241(1). Step 4: Open the CBSA/CRA import-export program account (program code 'RM') to enable customs export reporting and carrier/broker clearances. Step 5: Add the payroll program account ('RP') if Canadian employees are engaged; retain all statutory books and records for six years. No notarized apostilled constitutive documents are required at initial registration, though CRA may later require Form 112/112A agent authorization.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime. Default classifications are mandatory: the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax under ITA s. 96(1), but for GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is a separate 'person' under ETA s. 123(1). Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a non-resident lacking Canadian commercial nexus (ETA s. 241(1)); (ii) partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1(5)); (iii) GST/HST elections between closely related registrants or co-venturers to simplify supply netting where eligibility criteria are met; and (iv) mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 94.2-94.5 (OECD BEPS Action 2), which are not elective and cannot be overridden by partnership or corporate classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the four following quarters (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 123(1)). For a non-resident LP not engaged in commercial activity in Canada, the measure is based on worldwide taxable supplies; once the threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory. Below the threshold, registration is optional.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when the broker's supply qualifies as an exported taxable supply under ETA Schedule VI Part V. For goods: tangible personal property delivered to a common carrier for export to a destination outside Canada. For air-cargo charter brokerage services: supply of a service of arranging transportation of goods from Canada to a destination outside Canada (ETA Sch. VI Pt. V), or supply made to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada. The LP must document: (a) non-resident status of the client, (b) the cargo's physical crossing of a Canadian customs border, and (c) separate invoicing of the brokerage fee from any domestic cabotage or Ontario-origin services. Place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 142 and the SOR/2010-184 Place of Supply Rules must be applied and retained in the compliance file.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED or G7 electronic export filing) with carrier crossing proof
  • Commercial Invoice and Air Cargo Shippers' Letter of Instruction (SLI) evidencing foreign consignee and destination outside Canada
  • IATA Master Air Waybill and House Air Waybill / Bill of Lading naming the LP as freight broker or charter arranger
  • Carrier certificate of export / ground handler manifest with flight date, airport code, and actual uplift confirmation
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT/Fedwire payment advice, and bank FX record showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Non-resident status file: export contract, client incorporation or tax registration evidence, and Canadian zero-rating certificate documentation

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation period becomes unlimited where misrepresentation is attributable to negligence, wilful default, or fraud. Invalid zero-rating claims trigger recovery of net tax plus arrears interest and penalties under ETA ss. 280-285, including up to a 20% gross-negligence penalty, a 50% false-statements penalty, and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraudulent export refund schemes. CRA export zero-rating integrity files also require six-year retention of the full customs documentation bundle; a single missing airway bill or FX repatriation record can shift the burden of proof to the registrant and support retrospective clawback of all input tax credits tied to the zero-rated brokerage services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in 1951 and maintains no surrender, rationing, or licensing requirements for inward or outward remittances. Profit remittance from an Ontario limited partnership (CA_LP) is unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII Canadian non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, subject to treaty reduction, e.g., 15% under Canada-U.S. Tax Convention for portfolio distributions; 0% on capital returns subject to partnership capital accounting) and to the submission of a T5013 or T2062 informational filing where applicable. No Currency Convertibility Certificate or Exchange Control Permit is required. Cross-border capital contributions, loan injections, and trade disbursements in CAD, USD, or other freely traded currencies may be routed without central bank pre-approval; however, banking counterparties must apply FinCEN/OSFI-style KYC and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 for funds transfers and virtual currency transactions).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists in Canada. Export receipts, airline charter brokerage commission income, and freight settlement funds may be retained in any currency of the broker's choosing. The CA_LP may hold USD-denominated operating accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks without conversion to CAD. Reporting obligations are limited to tax compliance (Form T2125 business income schedule within the T5013 partnership return for Ontario-situs partnership operations) and to FINTRAC's Large Cash Transaction and Electronic Funds Transfer reports (threshold C$10,000) filed by the Canadian financial institution, not the LP. Global Affairs Canada export permits may apply only to controlled goods and services (not applicable to air charter brokerage), but aircraft charter and overflight permits for foreign jurisdictions remain the charter counterparty's responsibility.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority, final settlement agent for CAD clearing via Lynx RTGS); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as Canadian bank prudential regulator under the Bank Act (Canada); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF regulator under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTF); Global Affairs Canada and Export Development Canada for trade facilitation; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export/customs interface. Canada maintains no exchange control authority; foreign exchange policy is fully liberalized and administered under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary clearing rail; MEDIUM as a supplementary foreign-exchange and disbursement conduit. Foreign digital EMI institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Corpay) are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot hold Canadian settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada nor issue Canadian branch deposit accounts. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC registration and are permitted to process cross-border payment instructions through a Canadian Schedule I bank partner; however, a CA_LP requiring escrow-grade capital tracking, full OSFI-regulated deposit insurance, and aircraft charter finance features will face legal and compliance friction if relying exclusively on a fintech EMI. Canadian-licensed EMIs (e.g., Shopify-based payments, Wealthsimple Cash) are similarly limited. Recommendation: retain Tier-1 Schedule I bank as primary account holder; use EMI rails only for vendor disbursements and low-value charter commission payouts where FX conversion differentials are favorable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident limited partners into the Ontario LP must follow a compliant wire protocol: (1) Remitter initiates a SWIFT MT103 (for CAD/EUR/GBP) or Fedwire/CHIPS transfer (for USD) to the LP's capital escrow account at the designated Ontario branch of a Schedule I Canadian bank; (2) the payment message must include the marked field /Beneficiary/ 'CA_LP Legal Name - Capital Escrow Account - Ontario LP Registry No.' and the payment details line 'Unapplied Capital Contribution — No Services Rendered'; (3) Swift Purpose Code should reference a capital transfer, typically using Category 'GRP' for general funds movement or 'CAPT' for capital contribution purposes, depending on the bank's approved purpose code table — in absence of a specific code, the memo line 'Partnership Capital Contribution pursuant to LP Agreement' is acceptable; (4) the Canadian receiving bank applies its FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer reporting for transactions over C$10,000, and the LP must ensure the funding source is documented (accumulated declarations, certified financial statements, or a source-of-wealth letter from the limited partner's existing private bank); (5) no Bank of Canada authorization is needed; however, if the inbound transfer will later be re-denominated into USD for aircraft charter obligations, the LP should pre-notify the bank of the FX conversion under its standing instruction to avoid temporary idle-currency friction; (6) the bank may require the LP to complete a 'Source of Funds for Capital Call' questionnaire when aggregate contributions exceed C$1 million, including destruction of any unusual routing via third-party intermediary accounts.Outbound treasury disbursements for air charter brokerage are cleared via three distinct rails: (a) CAD domestic settlement through Lynx (Payments Canada's Real-Time Gross Settlement system, successor to LVTS), which is directly operated on Bank of Canada settlement accounts — used for payments to Canadian aircraft operators, handling agents, and the LP's own GP management fees; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary clearing rail; MEDIUM as a supplementary foreign-exchange and disbursement conduit. Foreign digital EMI institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Corpay) are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot hold Canadian settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada nor issue Canadian branch deposit accounts. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC registration and are permitted to process cross-border payment instructions through a Canadian Schedule I bank partner; however, a CA_LP requiring escrow-grade capital tracking, full OSFI-regulated deposit insurance, and aircraft charter finance features will face legal and compliance friction if relying exclusively on a fintech EMI. Canadian-licensed EMIs (e.g., Shopify-based payments, Wealthsimple Cash) are similarly limited. Recommendation: retain Tier-1 Schedule I bank as primary account holder; use EMI rails only for vendor disbursements and low-value charter commission payouts where FX conversion differentials are favorable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable to the LP as a fiscally transparent pass-through entity. Corporate partners are taxed at the combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate of 25.0% (federal 15.0% + Ontario 10.0%) on allocated Canadian-source active business income. Individual partners are taxed at progressive federal and Ontario rates (up to 33.0% federal + 13.16% Ontario).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. The Ontario LP itself pays no dividends; distributions of partnership profits to partners are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding because the partnership is fiscally transparent. However, if the LP receives Canadian-source dividends and distributes them to non-resident partners, or if the corporate general partner later declares a dividend, the 25% statutory rate applies. The Canada-US tax treaty reduces the rate to 5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation owning at least 10% of the voting stock, and to 15% for portfolio shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose formal OECD-style Local File or Master File monetary thresholds. The obligatory T106 disclosure threshold is CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of transactions with non-residents. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and maintained for all non-arm's-length transactions to be available by the statutory filing date of the tax return, consistent with CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in ITA s.245 and judicial doctrines (Copthorne Holdings, Canada Trustco). An Ontario LP acting as an air cargo charter broker must maintain a registered office, records, an active Ontario bank account, employee payroll, board or partner meeting minutes, operational contracts, and a CTA-issued international non-scheduled air service licence. The absence of substance permits the CRA to disregard the LP, reallocate income to a non-resident limited partner, apply the 25% combined corporate rate by deeming a corporate general partner to be the true taxpayer, impose gross negligence penalties, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded under ITA s.239; criminal liability on indictment attracts imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction attracts imprisonment up to 2 years. Interest accrues daily and is not deductible.

Director personal liability: The general partner, corporate directors, and senior officers who directed, authorized, or participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for penalties and civil restitution; CRA may obtain freezing orders, departure/exit travel bans, and the Criminal Code provides asset forfeiture.
Shell Limited Partnership and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the LP under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, re-assess all brokered fees at the 25% combined federal/Ontario corporate rate, add a 50% gross negligence penalty, apply 10% transfer pricing penalties, and impose late-filing penalties on T5013 and T106. The LP may be struck from the Ontario Business Information System for failing to maintain a registered office.

Director personal liability: Under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, a limited partner who takes part in the control of the business loses limited liability and becomes jointly and severally liable for partnership debts. An accommodation general partner who is a shell corporation with no assets is a 'piercing the corporate veil' trigger; courts may impose personal liability on the natural-person founders for fraudulent conveyances.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations and CAD 100,000 for individuals; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years; financing of terrorism under s.83.03 carries up to 10 years; proceeds of crime forfeiture applies.

Director personal liability: Senior officers, the general partner, and directors are treated as 'directing minds'; they may be prosecuted criminally for knowing participation in laundering, are personally liable for FINTRAC monetary penalties if they authorized the deficiency, and may be subjected to asset freezes, travel bans, and Canadian border seizure of business records.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII and Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Liability for all unremitted amounts, plus a penalty of 10% of the amount under ITA s.227(8), plus daily interest; willful failure may result in up to 5 years imprisonment under the Income Tax Act; CRA may also impose a 100% director liability assessment if the general partner corporation is insolvent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner of the LP are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts; the CRA may issue a Tax Clearance Certificate denial, freeze bank accounts, impose a travel ban under the 'failure to satisfy tax obligations' procedures, and seek a court receivership over the partnership assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Mispriced Related-Party Contracts

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with a 10% penalty on the net adjustment, plus a 50% gross negligence penalty where the taxpayer has knowingly understated income, plus interest; CRA may reallocate income to the Canadian LP under ITA s.247(2) and deny deductibility of inflated fees; the reassessment window is not limited by the normal three-year period where misrepresentation is involved.

Director personal liability: Corporate general partner directors who signed false T106 filings or certified inaccurate financial statements may be prosecuted for false statements under the Criminal Code; civil liability arises where dividend or management fee distributions strip the LP of assets while Canadian tax liabilities remain unpaid; travel bans may be imposed where a director leaves Canada with unpaid tax liabilities exceeding CAD 50,000.
