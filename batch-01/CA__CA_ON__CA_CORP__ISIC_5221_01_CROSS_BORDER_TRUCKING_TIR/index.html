Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Registration Portal; provincial tax authorities administer Ontario-specific taxes (e.g., Ontario Ministry of Finance for employer health tax and provincial sales tax harmonization).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must apply for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through the CRA’s Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Non-resident carriers must also register under the International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) if operating in participating provinces. The application requires legal entity documentation (e.g., certificate of incorporation or equivalent), proof of foreign registration, and details of Canadian place of business or agent for service.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must apply for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through the CRA’s Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Non-resident carriers must also register under the International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) if operating in participating provinces. The application requires legal entity documentation (e.g., certificate of incorporation or equivalent), proof of foreign registration, and details of Canadian place of business or agent for service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not permit the U.S.-style check-the-box election. However, a Canadian corporation is taxable at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. A non-resident company carrying on business in Canada may elect under subsection 115.1(2) to file a Canadian income tax return and claim treaty relief. For GST/HST, a non-resident carrying on business in Canada may elect to register voluntarily under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, thereby triggering eligibility for input tax credits. Provincial harmonization elections apply in Ontario under the HST system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including exports) unless the supplies are zero-rated; non-resident carriers providing international transportation are generally required to register regardless of threshold if carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

International freight transportation services (including cross-border trucking under TIR carnets) are zero-rated for GST/HST under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act when the service is supplied to a non-resident or any freight transported to or from Canada. The carrier must maintain evidence that the transportation is provided to a non-resident or that the freight is exported. Zero-rating applies to the transportation service itself, not to fuel or ancillary domestic services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Form B13A for commercial goods)
  • TIR Carnet (International customs transit document)
  • Bill of Lading (through or multimodal highway trucking bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of settlement via SWIFT wire from the foreign consignee/payer
  • Customs invoice / commercial invoice reflecting export of goods
  • Proof of foreign destination (delivery receipts, customs stamps at border exit)

Canada does not mandate a national real-time e-invoicing clearance system. GST/HST filers must issue prescribed tax invoices retaining supporting customs documentation. The CRA requires electronic filing (NETFILE for corporations, GST/HST NETFILE) for most registrants above threshold. Ontario does not impose a provincial digital fiscal stamp. TIR carnets serve as customs transit documentation but do not replace GST/HST invoicing.

The CRA may deny zero-rating and assess uncollected GST/HST, plus interest and penalty under sections 280 and 281 of the Excise Tax Act (5% plus 1% per month for up to 12 months). False statements or wilful evasion may trigger gross negligence penalties (50% of understated net tax) and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act. Outbound payment rules under section 105 of the Income Tax Act require withholding for amounts deemed paid to non-residents. The CRA can record liens and seize assets for unpaid amounts. Failure to file a B13A export declaration or TIR irregularities may also result in CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) and denial of future TIR guarantees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible. Canada maintains full capital account and current account convertibility; there are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, all cross-border transfers are subject to anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds and sanctions compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administered by FINTRAC. Tax withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, and certain payments to non-residents applies under the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. For TIR carnet bonded trucking operations, export proceeds are settled under normal commercial terms, subject only to FINTRAC reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000 and CBSA customs documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs without Canadian regulatory licensing cannot provide primary domestic clearing or settlement rails. They may operate as payment facilitators or use a licensed Canadian MSB (Money Service Business) partner. A FINTRAC-registered MSB license is required for any entity providing foreign exchange dealing, remittance, or payment processing in Canada. Direct access to Payments Canada clearing is limited to banks and authorized payment service providers under the Retail Payments Activity Act (RPAA).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15.0% general corporate rate after provincial abatement and general rate reduction) and Ontario (11.5% general corporate rate) for large active business income; reduced 11.2% combined federal (9.0%) and Ontario (2.2%) for eligible active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) up to the Ontario SBD limit of CAD 500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. This rate may be reduced by an applicable income tax treaty to 15% for general dividend distributions and to 5% for a corporate shareholder that owns at least 10% (US) or 25% (many treaties) of the voting shares immediately prior to the dividend payment. Under the Canada-US treaty, the reduced 5% rate applies to a US corporate shareholder that owns at least 10% of the voting shares; the 15% treaty rate is the maximum for portfolio dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed on or before the day that is six months after the end of the taxation year. For example, a December 31 year-end corporate taxpayer must file by June 30 of the following calendar year. For a non-resident corporation with Canadian business income, the deadline similarly is six months after the end of the Canadian taxation year unless the CRA has granted an extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The T106 Information Return for non-arm's length transactions with non-residents must be filed when the aggregate fair market value of reportable transactions (including cross-border inter-company haulage fees, insurance allocations,, fuel reimbursements, and vehicle leasing) with any one non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Although the Income Tax Act does not impose a formal 'documentation threshold', the CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions. For audits, the CRA's risk assessment and the application of the 10% gross-negligence penalty under s.247(3) typically focus, in practice, on taxpayers with controlled transactions exceeding CAD 5,000,000; however, documentation should be prepared for any transaction involving a significant value or an inherently hard-to-price intangible. The Master File and Local File covering the group-wide value chain and Canadian taxpayer's pricing adjustments should be in place no later than six months after the corporation's tax filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Canada has not adopted a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute, but the substance doctrine operates through three main legal channels. First, tax treaty eligibility: a corporation must be a 'resident' of Canada by reason of 'central management and control' being exercised in Canada; if a Canadian incorporation is a mere shell without directors' meetings, accounts, or operational decisions in Canada, the CRA or a court may deem the corporation to be tax-resident in the foreign jurisdiction or disregard its separate identity for tax purposes. Second, GAAR (s.245 ITA): a transaction or series without bona fide economic substance, which defeats the object, spirit, or purpose of the Act, may trigger a re-assessment and the denial of tax benefits. Third, the 'associated corporations' and 'thin capitalization' rules (s.256 and s.212(3)) apply to prevent artificial allocation of income to a foreign group entity. For a cross-border trucking company, the CRA specifically examines: (i) where the driver dispatch decisions are made; (ii) where the carrier contracts are signed; (iii) where driver payroll and vehicle registrations are maintained; and (iv) where revenue and expense transactions are funded. A Canadian entity that is an empty booking office, with foreign controllers, will fail the 'conduct of business' analysis and its Canadian corporate attribution will be disregarded, resulting in attribution of all Canadian-source trucking income directly to the non-resident principal and potential Part I and Part XIII tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 2 years. Upon indictment, the fine is between 100% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. The court may order restitution and forfeiture of proceeds of the evasion. The CRA will re-assess the tax beyond the three-year normal reassessment period, and gross-negligence penalties under s.163(1) of 50% of the understated tax may apply. No statute-barring period applies where the misrepresentation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize the evasion are jointly and severally liable under s.160 (amounts transferred to non-arm's length persons) and s.239.1 and s.239.2 of the Income Tax Act. Civil restitution may be obtained through a criminal fine collection, and the individuals may be subject to travel bans (forfeiture of passports), asset freezes, and imprisonment under the Criminal Code's money laundering provisions if the tax evasion is linked to criminal proceeds.
Shell Company / Paper Corporation

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) permits the CRA to adjust the corporation's income, disallow any deduction claimed in respect of the avoidance transaction, and apply a penalty of 25% of the tax benefit if it is reasonable to conclude that the transaction is a sham. The law may also treat the foreign parent as having a permanent establishment in Canada, requiring a Canadian branch return and withholding taxes on branch profits. A corporation that files false statements with respect to its residency may be subject to penalties under s.162 and s.163 of the Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell corporation that fail to exercise independent commercial judgment are not automatically liable for the corporate tax, but if they are the cause of the misrepresentation and they knowingly sign false tax filings, they may be personally liable as 'persons required to file' for the penalty under s.163.2 (third-party civil penalty) up to CAD 100,000, and they may be subject to civil proceedings for unjust enrichment. The CRA may also hold the directors liable for the company's unpaid withholding amounts under the de facto director doctrine if they exercised effective control without legal registration.
Transfer Pricing / Persistent Non-Arm's Length Charges

Statutory sanction: The CRA adjust the transfer price to the arm's length figure, resulting in an upward adjustment of total income. A penalty of 10% of the net adjustment is imposed, regardless of whether the adjustment results in tax payable, unless the taxpayer has made or obtained 'contemporaneous documentation' in respect of the transaction. The penalty applies to the amount by which the transfer price exceeds the arm's length price, and is in addition to the tax on the adjusted income. Late-filing or failure to file a T106 carries an additional penalty of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 1,000) for each day the return is late. There is no statutory ceiling on the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not personally liable for the TP penalty unless they knowingly participate in the making of false entries or assent to the omission of facts. However, the CRA may assess a section 163.2 civil penalty against any person, including directors, who counsel or participate in a false statement on a tax return, up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation and CAD 100,000 for an individual. If the transfer-pricing scheme constitutes a sham and is used to evade tax, the directors may be subject to the same criminal and personal liability as under the tax evasion category.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), an administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for an individual may be imposed. Failure to report a suspicious transaction or a large cash transaction is a violation that carries a penalty up to CAD 500,000 and may be subject to criminal prosecution. Laundering the proceeds of a specified offence is an indictable offence under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code punishable by imprisonment for a term not exceeding 10 years. The court may order forfeiture of the accused's property where the proceeds of crime are identified; the CRA may also seize funds under tax provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct, participate in, or facilitate money laundering are jointly and severally liable to the corporation for losses caused by the violation, and are exposed to criminal liability under the Criminal Code and PCMLTFA; the corporate veil will be pierced upon conviction. Travel bans and asset seizure orders are commonly imposed on directors in connection with money-laundering investigations, and the CRA may seek personal recovery of tax from the corporation's funds as against the director if the director was the directing mind of the laundering enterprise.
