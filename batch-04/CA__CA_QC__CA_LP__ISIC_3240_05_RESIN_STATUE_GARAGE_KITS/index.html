Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by law for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are fixed in the partnership agreement and may be in cash or in kind. No escrow account, share premium account, or notarized capital deposit is required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a municipal fire safety clearance under the Quebec Fire Safety Act, CQLR c. Secure a structural load inspection and occupancy certificate from the local municipality verifying the floor can support casting machinery and storage racks. Register with CNESST under the Act respecting occupational health and safety, CQLR c. S-2.1, implement a written prevention program, and provide WHMIS training for workers handling resin, catalysts, and solvents.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by law for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are fixed in the partnership agreement and may be in cash or in kind. No escrow account, share premium account, or notarized capital deposit is required for registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST and provincial income tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Revenu Québec CléSec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Limited Partnership: 1) Submit Form RC1 to CRA to obtain a Business Number (BN) for the partnership, including GST/HST account (unless small supplier and elects not to register). 2) Register for QST with Revenu Québec via the Partnership Registration form (if taxable sales in Quebec exceed $30,000 CAD). 3) File T5013 Partnership Information Return annually for income allocation; each partner files separate T1/T2 reporting their share of income. 4) Non-resident partners without SIN require a CRA Individual Tax Number (ITN) via Form T1261. 5) For electronic services, consider CBCA/CRNRA compliance; nexus depends on place of supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for both federal and Quebec income tax; partners bear tax on allocated income. However, for GST/HST and QST, the LP is treated as a separate 'person' and must register and file separately if it makes taxable supplies and is not a small supplier.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Tax transparency is mandatory. Available elections: (i) partnership non-calendar-year-end election under ITA s. 249.1; (ii) GST/HST and QST input tax credit allocation elections under ETA s. 141.1 and QST Act s. 401 if the partnership has multiple businesses or related members.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST) and $30,000 CAD of taxable supplies in Quebec (QST) over any current or four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated exports count as taxable supplies for threshold determination.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to sales of goods delivered to a non-resident purchaser outside Canada, provided the goods are exported directly and leave Canada within 60 days. GST/HST Schedule VI Part V Section 1 and QST Act Schedule IV Section 1. Services (e.g., custom resin casting) performed for non-residents are zero-rated if not linked to real property or tangible personal property in Canada. Proof of export (customs declaration, transport documentation) and evidence of payment from the buyer are required. No statutory FX repatriation deadline; a 180-day documentary consistency rule is commonly applied by auditors for export corroboration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CBSA Electronic Export Report)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice (vendor's copy)
  • Foreign Purchase Order / Contract
  • Proof of Consideration in foreign currency (bank transfer, Stripe/PayPal payout record)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices if the supplier guarantees integrity, authenticity, and readability. Quebec has announced a phased e-invoicing mandate (B2B) targeted to begin around 2027, but it is not in force. No statutory API or pre-clearance approval is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec can reassess within four years (extended to 10 years for misrepresentation). Gross negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1 and QST Act s. 170 are up to 25% of the net tax evaded plus interest. False statements may attract a separate penalty of 25% of the tax attributable to the false statement. Criminal fraud charges under ETA s. 327 and QST Act s. 175.1 expose the perpetrator to fines up to $500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Export zero-rating will be reversed retroactively if documentation is incomplete, with full GST/QST, arrears interest, and penalties clawed back.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; no exchange-control approvals apply to profit remittance, distributions to non-resident limited partners, or repatriation of capital. A Quebec société en commandite may invoice export sales in USD or any currency and hold the proceeds in a foreign-currency account at a Canadian bank without conversion mandate. Legal constraints are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reporting of international wire transfers and large transactions of C$10,000 or more; (ii) cross-border currency declarations of C$10,000 or more; and (iii) Investment Canada Act notification if a non-resident limited partner acquires control of a Canadian business. No Exchange Control Act regime exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory deadline, no mandatory surrender percentage, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds. The Quebec LP may accumulate USD receivables indefinitely and convert at its chosen commercial timing, subject only to the functional-currency consistency rules of the Income Tax Act and the Taxation Act (Quebec). Mandatory administrative duties are: (a) FINTRAC large-transaction reports for each receipt of C$10,000 or more and for every verified international wire transfer of C$10,000 or more; (b) customer identification and record keeping under the PCMLTFA; and (c) retention of the underlying commercial invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations to evidence the trade origin of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-rate and foreign-exchange reserve policy is vested in the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act, with the Bank of Canada acting as fiscal agent. Prudential regulation of Schedule I and II deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada). In Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises financial-markets conduct, payment-service-provider registration, and derivatives, but does not regulate chartered-bank treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, with defined legal constraints. Stripe Payments Canada, Ltd. and PayPal Canada Co. are FINTRAC-registered payment service providers and the preferred settlement rails for card-based preorders; they must settle in CAD to the LP's Schedule I operational account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not Canadian licensed banks, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot hold client funds in a segregated deposit account protected under Canadian banking law. They may operate only as money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC or as foreign-entity conduits for FX conversion and small-value payouts. An offshore EMI cannot support a 'Capital Escrow Account' with enforceable Canadian segregation, and reliance on an EMI as the primary clearing bank creates FINTRAC reporting ambiguity and weakens the audit trail required by Revenu Québec. Recommended posture: use a Schedule I bank as the primary treasury and clearing tier; deploy EMIs only as secondary conversion and disbursement utilities.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Preorder deposits received via Stripe and PayPal prior to product delivery, flagged by transaction monitoring as possible layering, consumer-deposit-taking, or unexplained customer prepayments.

Root cause: Preorder deposits received via Stripe and PayPal prior to product delivery, flagged by transaction monitoring as possible layering, consumer-deposit-taking, or unexplained customer prepayments.

Defense evidence: Provide: (a) PSP merchant settlement statements mapping every preorder to customer email, order ID, and card digit; (b) the standard preorder terms of sale with refund policy; (c) the LP's public website and catalogue evidencing resin statue production; (d) fulfilment invoices issued at shipment; (e) a business-model narrative by the general partner; and (f) operational expense records demonstrating the deposits fund production rather than financial intermediation.
Non-resident limited partner capital contribution routed from a jurisdiction with weak AML enforcement, creating an unexplained source-of-wealth gap.

Root cause: Non-resident limited partner capital contribution routed from a jurisdiction with weak AML enforcement, creating an unexplained source-of-wealth gap.

Defense evidence: Provide: (a) signed partnership agreement and capital-account ledger; (b) a bank reference from the contributor's home institution; (c) six months of source-of-funds statements (salary, dividends, or disposition documents); (d) a sworn source-of-funds affidavit; (e) corporate registry extract and beneficial-ownership chart if the contributor is an entity; and (f) sanctions screening confirmation covering GAC, OFAC, and UN lists.
Large export disbursements to resin suppliers in higher-risk or sanctioned-adjacent jurisdictions, flagged for possible diversion, sanctions evasion, or export-control breach.

Root cause: Large export disbursements to resin suppliers in higher-risk or sanctioned-adjacent jurisdictions, flagged for possible diversion, sanctions evasion, or export-control breach.

Defense evidence: Provide: (a) supplier contract and purchase orders; (b) supplier KYC file with business registration and beneficial-owner declaration; (c) end-user and end-use attestation for the resin material; (d) ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930 trade classification with HS codes; (e) shipping documents (B/L or AWB) and customs declarations; and (f) completed sanctions screening matrix for all named counterparties.
Rapid conversion of USD export receipts to CAD with immediate onward disbursement, flagged as potential structuring or trade-based money laundering.

Root cause: Rapid conversion of USD export receipts to CAD with immediate onward disbursement, flagged as potential structuring or trade-based money laundering.

Defense evidence: Provide: (a) export sales forecast and production cash-flow model; (b) USD account statements tracing invoice-to-settlement-to-conversion; (c) FX spot or forward confirmations; (d) supplier payables schedule; and (e) a written treasury note confirming conversion timing is driven solely by supplier payment due dates and the LP's functional-currency election.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium, with defined legal constraints. Stripe Payments Canada, Ltd. and PayPal Canada Co. are FINTRAC-registered payment service providers and the preferred settlement rails for card-based preorders; they must settle in CAD to the LP's Schedule I operational account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not Canadian licensed banks, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot hold client funds in a segregated deposit account protected under Canadian banking law. They may operate only as money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC or as foreign-entity conduits for FX conversion and small-value payouts. An offshore EMI cannot support a 'Capital Escrow Account' with enforceable Canadian segregation, and reliance on an EMI as the primary clearing bank creates FINTRAC reporting ambiguity and weakens the audit trail required by Revenu Québec. Recommended posture: use a Schedule I bank as the primary treasury and clearing tier; deploy EMIs only as secondary conversion and disbursement utilities.
