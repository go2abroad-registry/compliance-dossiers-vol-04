Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal authority for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and excise; official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial counterpart for Quebec: Revenu Québec (RQ) — official portal: Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/on-line-services/for-businesses/), administering QST (Quebec Sales Tax / TVQ), Quebec corporate income tax, and provincial source deductions. Federal incorporation registry: Corporations Canada (ISED); Quebec enterprise registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec Business Corporation obtains its nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) automatically upon federal incorporation through Corporations Canada, or upon extra-provincial registration of a foreign corporation with the Registraire des entreprises. The BN serves as the master tax identifier; program accounts are appended as prefixes (e.g., RT+BN for GST/HST, RC+BN for payroll, RP+BN for Quebec source deductions). Activation/registration is done through CRA My Business Account or Forms RC1 (resident) / RC1W (non-resident), including GST/HST program account activation. For GST/HST, registration is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed C$30,000 over a four-calendar-quarter period (or C$30,000 in a single quarter), except that non-resident suppliers of specified digital products and services (e.g., downloadable video games delivered electronically) are required to register without a Canadian 'carrying-on-business' nexus if they are not a small supplier. The corporation must separately register with Revenu Québec for QST: registration with RQ (QST number with prefix TQ/RM) is mandatory when the corporation is not a small supplier (C$30,000 threshold) and makes taxable supplies in Quebec; digital-service and e-commerce suppliers to Quebec consumers are subject to QST registration under Quebec's destination-based place-of-supply rules, with no permanent-establishment requirement. The same BN must be referenced in the Registraire des entreprises annual declaration and all provincial tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act; a Business Corporation is automatically a Canadian-resident taxpayer subject to entity-level tax on worldwide income (with Quebec provincial allocation via permanent establishment). Available statutory elections relevant to digital publishing: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s.156 to treat intercorporate supplies as made for nil consideration and outside GST/HST (subject to carve-outs for real property, financial services, and regulated supplies); (ii) corresponding QST election under the Quebec Taxation Act (LQV s.21.1.1) for supplies between closely related corporations; (iii) deemed-supplier election for a resident agent acting for a non-resident principal; (iv) ETA s.156(3) election to treat a member of a corporate group as the single registrant; (v) income tax rollover elections (ITA s.85, s.87 amalgamation, s.88 winding-up), foreign affiliate elections (ITA s.93), and Canadian-controlled private corporation elections for the small business deduction; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules: Canada does not permit hybrid entity classification elections for Canadian corporations, but applies the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) provisions (ITA s.18.2/18.4) and hybrid mismatch arrangement rules to deny deductions for hybrid-financing payments, including where a Canadian payer is treated as pass-through in a foreign jurisdiction.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control approvals, no surrender quotas, and no minimum holding periods apply to capital injections, dividends, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance to non-residents is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) (statutory rate 25%; generally 15% for arm's-length direct recipients and 5% for a US corporate parent under the Canada–US Income Tax Convention, with a refund/relief mechanism available). Practical hurdles are bank-and-tax-driven: FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) for cross-border wires at or above CAD 10,000; CRA Form T106 information returns for reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents; thin-capitalization documentation for shareholder loans under ITA section 18(4); and applicable Quebec financial administration filings for a Quebec-incorporated publisher.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Canada. Steam and PlayStation Store payouts may lawfully be retained in USD, held in foreign-currency bank accounts, or converted at market rates. The relevant obligations are: (a) FINTRAC reporting for incoming/outgoing electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000 and Large Cash Transaction Reports at or above CAD 10,000; (b) record-retention requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); (c) CRA foreign-currency translation for income computation using Bank of Canada published exchange rates; and (d) CBSA cross-border currency and monetary instruments reporting (Form E677) for physical movement of funds at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and supervisor of retail payments under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) since 2024; exchange control is not centralized in Canada. Regulatory oversight is distributed among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF and wire reporting, and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax-related cross-border compliance. There is no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; the Canadian dollar is a fully floating currency and no emergency exchange-control machinery is currently in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rated Medium for operational sub-accounts only. Foreign digital EMIs (for example Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, or Payoneer) may lawfully service a Quebec corporation if they are registered with FINTRAC as a money services business or foreign money services business and, when engaging in retail payment activities, are licensed by the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. A foreign EMI cannot replace a Tier-1 Schedule I bank for treasury-critical functions: it has no access to Lynx (CAD RTGS), no Canadian clearing code for direct CAD settlement, no CDIC deposit insurance, and platform payout agreements with Valve/Steam and Sony PlayStation typically require a regulated commercial bank account. Recommended treasury architecture: maintain the Tier-1 Canadian bank operating account as the master treasury node; use USD-denominated EMI sub-accounts strictly for collecting Steam/PlayStation proceeds with same-day sweeping into the Canadian regulated account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate income tax rate: 15%. Quebec general corporate income tax rate: 11.5%. Combined general rate: 26.5%. For an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation, the federal small-business rate is generally 9% and the Quebec rate is generally 3.2%, producing a combined rate of approximately 12.2% on qualifying active business income within the applicable business limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty. Common treaty rates are 5% where the corporate recipient satisfies the required ownership threshold and 15% otherwise, but the Canada-residence treaty and limitation-on-benefits or anti-abuse conditions must be checked. The Canada-United States treaty commonly provides 5% for a qualifying corporate parent owning at least 10% of voting power and 15% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 and CO-17 are generally due six months after the end of each taxation year. Corporate tax balances are generally due two months after year-end; an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation may qualify for a three-month balance-due date if the applicable statutory conditions are met. Monthly installments are generally due on the last day of each month. Quebec and federal filing deadlines must be calculated independently where payment, installment, or electronic-filing rules differ.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly installments are generally due on the last day of each month. A qualifying corporation may pay quarterly installments on the last day of each quarter. The balance-due date is generally two months after year-end, with a possible three-month date for an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

NR4 slips and the NR4 Summary are generally due by the last day of March following the calendar year. Amounts withheld from non-residents are generally remitted by the 15th day of the month following the month of payment or credit, subject to the corporation's remitter status and specific rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general monetary threshold below which contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is unnecessary. Under Income Tax Act section 247, contemporaneous documentation is required for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents, and the documentation must generally be prepared by the return-filing deadline and provided to the CRA within three months of a written request. Form T106 generally applies where statutory reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transaction conditions are met, including the CAD 1,000,000 aggregate threshold for relevant transaction categories. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a Canadian-resident ultimate parent or surrogate parent of an MNE group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year. Canada does not impose a separate universal Local File or Master File threshold under a formal OECD-style filing regime, although CRA documentation should cover the substance of both concepts where relevant.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is enforced through the transfer-pricing arm's-length principle, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, treaty anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership analysis, sham and factual-reality doctrines, and source and residency rules. A Canadian game publisher should maintain Canadian decision-making, personnel, development or publishing functions, risk control, IP and contract records, bank-account control, and evidence supporting the actual functions performed. Conduit dividend, interest, or royalty structures with no genuine control or commercial purpose are high risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the federal Income Tax Act, conviction can result in a fine generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to two years. Civil reassessment, interest, gross-negligence penalties, and denial of credits may also apply. Quebec may impose separate penalties and criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal criminal liability where they participated in or directed the offence. Corporate status does not protect intentional criminal conduct. Imprisonment is possible; travel restrictions may arise through criminal-process orders but are not an automatic tax sanction. Restitution or repayment may be ordered where legally available.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: The corporation may be assessed for the unremitted tax, interest, and withholding penalties. Reduced treaty rates may be denied where residency, beneficial ownership, or treaty entitlement is unsupported.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for amounts deemed held in trust, including certain source deductions and statutory remittances, if the corporation failed to remit and the statutory due-diligence defence is unavailable.
GST/HST and Quebec QST Miscollection

Statutory sanction: Assessment of uncollected tax, denial of credits, interest, gross-negligence penalties, late-filing penalties, and possible prosecution may result. Platform-facilitator collection rules must be analyzed separately from direct-sales obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors can incur personal liability for certain GST/HST and QST amounts held in trust where statutory conditions are met. Liability may continue after dissolution or asset transfers.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess under section 247, impose a transfer-pricing penalty generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where the statutory conditions are met, deny deductions, impose withholding tax, and seek an extended reassessment period for serious misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally liable merely because a transfer-pricing adjustment is made, but personal exposure can arise for knowing participation, false statements, source-remittance failures, or criminal tax conduct.
Non-Filing of T106, Foreign-Affiliate, or Country-by-Country Information

Statutory sanction: Specific fixed or formula-based information-return penalties may apply, together with extended audit periods, transfer-pricing penalties, denial of treaty positions, and compliance orders where available.

Director personal liability: Ordinary information-return penalties are generally imposed on the corporation, but directors may be personally exposed for deliberate false filings or related trust and withholding failures.
Shell Company, Sham, or Treaty-Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The CRA may deny treaty relief under beneficial-ownership, anti-avoidance, principal-purpose, or GAAR principles, reassess tax, impose interest and penalties, and challenge the factual residence or commercial characterization of the arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax assessments connected with statutory trust obligations, and criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion if they knowingly implemented the sham. There is no automatic corporate veil piercing solely because the entity has limited substance.
Corporate Dissolution or Asset Stripping Before Tax Clearance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may assess the corporation, challenge transfers, apply deemed-trust rules, pursue transferees under applicable statutory provisions, and impose interest and penalties. Dissolution does not extinguish pre-existing tax liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for source deductions, GST/HST, QST, and other trust amounts if statutory conditions are satisfied. Liability may arise despite resignation if the person was a director during the relevant period.
Books, Records, and Revenue-Traceability Failure

Statutory sanction: The CRA or Revenu Québec may issue compliance demands, make indirect-method assessments, disallow deductions or credits, impose record-keeping penalties, and apply gross-negligence penalties where the evidentiary failure supports that conclusion.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal consequences for obstruction, false records, or trust-remittance failures. Ordinary poor bookkeeping alone does not automatically create unlimited personal liability.
AML, Sanctions, and Proceeds-of-Crime Risk

Statutory sanction: The ordinary game publisher is not automatically a FINTRAC reporting entity solely because it publishes software, but obligations can arise if it performs a regulated financial activity or operates as a money-services business. Sanctions breaches may result in administrative monetary penalties or criminal prosecution, including substantial fines and imprisonment under applicable Canadian legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or knowingly permit sanctions evasion, money laundering, or false records may face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, fines, and imprisonment. Travel bans are not automatic but may arise from court orders or immigration consequences.
