Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian law treats the LP as fiscally transparent for income tax; each partner is allocated partnership income under ITA s. 96; for GST/HST the LP is a separate registrant person under ETA s. 123; non-resident partners are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source income through deemed establishment rules).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by statute. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, a taxpayer may file an election under ITA s. 247.12(5) to adjust deductibility consequences once the business income or foreign entity classification is established. GST/HST related parties may use the nil-consideration election under ETA ss. 156/157 for qualifying supplies. Non-resident partners deriving Canadian rental or property income may elect under ITA s. 216 to be taxed on net income rather than gross withholding under Part XIII.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold for non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in Canada). Once crossed, registration is mandatory for any person carrying on commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated if the tangible personal property is exported in accordance with the regulations (normally within 30 days after the supply or as part of the vendor's commercial activity), or is shipped to a non-resident purchaser for delivery outside Canada, and the registrant retains documentary evidence satisfactory to CRA of physical exportation. Dental clear-aligner thermoforming sheets qualify as manufactured goods; if destined for foreign orthodontic laboratories or practitioners and actually removed from Canada, the supply may be zero-rated. Where export is effected by the customer, the vendor must retain the customer's export documentation and any documents identifying the foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System transmission, required for exports over CA$2,000)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with HS classification
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Consignment Note (proof of physical movement)
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Canada does not operate a continuous transaction controls (CTC) platform; there is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. A registrant must issue an invoice containing prescribed information (ETA s. 221) before physical delivery of goods, and may use paper or electronic format provided the records remain immediately retrievable. Exports are reported through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments exceeding CA$2,000, which also serves as proof of export for zero-rating.

CRA audits zero-rating substantiation at both GST/HST and income tax levels. If documentation is absent, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 13% HST, with assessment of tax, interest, and a penalty under ETA s. 285.1 equal to the greater of CA$100 and 25% of the tax materially reduced or not remitted. For fraudulent or deliberate export zero-rating, a registrant is liable, on indictment, to a fine of up to CA$500,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years under ETA s. 327(2). Retrospective clawback may extend beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established; CRA may reassess without limitation period and deny all related input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime. The Canadian Dollar is freely tradable in current and capital account transactions. There are no material restrictions on current account convertibility, dividend remittances, interest payments, or royalties, provided that: (i) withholding tax obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) are satisfied; (ii) the transaction is reported to FINTRAC if it involves a large cash transaction or cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) the inbound investment does not trigger Investment Canada Act net-benefit review (e.g., non-Canadian investor acquiring control of a Canadian business above the prescribed threshold). Profit remittance by a Limited Partnership is generally treated as a distribution to partners; no additional entity-level withholding is imposed, but non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on deemed taxable distributions or partnership income allocated to them. No exchange-control approval is required for genuine commercial remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory timeline for conversion of foreign currency receipts into Canadian Dollars. Exporters may hold foreign currency in Canadian bank accounts, convert freely, or use forward contracts to hedge exposure. However, exporters must: (i) retain books and records for at least six years as required by the Income Tax Act (Canada); (ii) report exports on Form B13A to CBSA or through the Canadian Export Reporting System for goods exceeding the prescribed threshold; and (iii) disclose any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC through the Canadian Cross-Border Currency Reporting regime. For customs valuation and export payment verification, documentary evidence, including the sales contract, invoice, and SWIFT payment advice, must be preserved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banking institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML and cross-border reporting; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Statistics Canada for merchandise trade reporting; and the Department of Finance Canada for foreign exchange policy and the Investment Canada Act review thresholds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Offshore digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions are not automatically recognized as banks in Canada. Non-Canadian EMIs intending to provide payment services or hold Canadian client funds must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada, and may require a provincial money-services business (MSB) registration and membership in FINTRAC as a money services business. A pure offshore EMI that does not maintain a Canadian presence or registration cannot offer CAD-denominated clearing accounts to an Ontario Limited Partnership. The recommended approach is to use a Canadian Tier-1 bank for primary treasury operations and a registered fintech payment processor (e.g., Stripe Canada, through a PSP agreement) for the credit-card and platform payment rails; the offshore EMI may be used only as a non-custodial payment gateway if integrated through a licensed Canadian partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Ontario Limited Partnership must be routed through a Canadian chartered bank account using SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers. The remitting foreign bank must specify the beneficiary as the full legal name of the Ontario Limited Partnership and the bank account number of the operational or escrow account. The purpose code should be accurately selected from the Canadian Banking Association (CBA) standard or the corresponding ISO 20022 Purpose Code: for equity contributions use 'EQUI' (Equity Securities) or 'CAPL' (Capital Increase in Cash); for non-resident direct investment use 'DINC' or 'FDI' as appropriate; for loans from non-resident shareholders use 'LOAN' or 'COVT' if capital contribution is made by way of debt. The remitting bank must complete the 'Details of Charges' and 'Remittance Information' fields to include the reference to the capital contribution agreement, the partnership's Ontario registration number, and the name of the contributing partner. The receiving Canadian bank will conduct a funds verification and may request the underlying contribution agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and the CBCA or provincial registration records. Canada does not require exchange-control approval for inward investment, but an investment of CAD 5 million or more by a non-Canadian acquiring control of a Canadian business may require a pre-closing filing under the Investment Canada Act. For amounts below the threshold, a post-closing net-benefit notification is generally not required, but the partnership should still document the transfer for tax and AML purposes. All large cash or wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported on a consolidated basis through FINTRAC reporting obligations and internal bank surveillance.Outbound trade disbursements for payment of thermoforming machinery, polymer raw materials, packaging, and orthodontic equipment should be settled through: (i) SWIFT MT103 single payment instructions for commercial invoices under CAD 500,000 or for immediate settlement; (ii) Documentary Letters of Credit (DC LC) or Standby Letters of Credit (SBLC) opened by the Canadian bank in favor of foreign suppliers, typically under ICC UCP 600; and (iii) direct CAD or USD clearing through the Canadian High Value Payment System (LVTS) or Lynx (newly adopted ISO 20022 real-time gross settlement system) for larger Canadian-dollar disbursements. For US Dollar denominated disbursements, the bank will clear through the US correspondent network with an ABA routing number or Fedwire participant number; for EURO and other currencies, clearing through TARGET2 or the respective RTGS system using the beneficiary bank's IBAN/SWIFT address. All outbound transfers must include a valid remittance code from the Canadian business classification; the correct ISO 20022 purpose code for trade payment is 'TRADE' (Trade of Goods and Services) or 'PURC' (Purchase of Goods and Services). For intra-group licensing or royalty payments, use 'ROYL' and include the Part XIII withholding tax documentation. For reimbursement of raw material imports, the importer should retain the B3-3 customs entry form and the carrier's bill of lading to support the outbound settlement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Offshore digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions are not automatically recognized as banks in Canada. Non-Canadian EMIs intending to provide payment services or hold Canadian client funds must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada, and may require a provincial money-services business (MSB) registration and membership in FINTRAC as a money services business. A pure offshore EMI that does not maintain a Canadian presence or registration cannot offer CAD-denominated clearing accounts to an Ontario Limited Partnership. The recommended approach is to use a Canadian Tier-1 bank for primary treasury operations and a registered fintech payment processor (e.g., Stripe Canada, through a PSP agreement) for the credit-card and platform payment rails; the offshore EMI may be used only as a non-custodial payment gateway if integrated through a licensed Canadian partner.
