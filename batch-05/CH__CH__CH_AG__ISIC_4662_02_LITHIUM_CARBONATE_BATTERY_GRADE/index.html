Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), operating under the supervisory authority of the Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt), Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR; Code des Obligations, CO), Articles 620-763 on the stock corporation, in particular Art. 621 (minimum capital CHF 100,000), Art. 626 (contents of the Articles of Association), Art. 629 (notarial form of the incorporation deed), Art. 632 (minimum payment of 20% and at least CHF 50,000), Arts. 635-635a (founding report and capital confirmation by the licensed audit expert), Art. 643 (acquisition of legal personality upon commercial register entry), Art. 716b (inalienable board duties), Art. 718 (representation and signature rules); supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV) and the revised Swiss company law in force since 1 January 2023.

Formation / constitutional filings

Public notarial deed of incorporation (Gründungsurkunde / acte authentique de constitution) together with the Articles of Association (Statuten / statuts), filed with the Commercial Register for entry (Eintragung / inscription), followed by publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt, SHAB).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Nominal share capital must be at least CHF 100,000. At registration, at least 20% of the nominal amount of each share must be paid up, and in any event the aggregate paid-up amount must be at least CHF 50,000 (Art. 632 OR). Contributions may be in cash or in kind; a licensed Swiss audit expert must issue a capital confirmation certificate (Kapitalbestätigung) verifying that the funds are at the free disposal of the company's management. No federal statutory escrow account is mandatory, but cantonal practice as applied in Zug commonly requires payment into a Swiss bank account blocked until registration, subject to the auditor's written confirmation.

Funding & unlock path: Applicable only if any premises with personnel are operated in Switzerland: fire safety certification and periodic inspection by the Zug Cantonal Fire Prevention Authority (Gebäudeversicherung Zug); occupational safety clearance under the Swiss Accident Insurance Act (UVG) and Ordinance 3 on Health and Safety Regulations (ArGV 3), including a workplace inspection by SUVA where staff are engaged; structural load verification for any racking or storage installations. All such clearances are waived for a virtual, office-only international trading company.

Paid-in capital rule: Nominal share capital must be at least CHF 100,000. At registration, at least 20% of the nominal amount of each share must be paid up, and in any event the aggregate paid-up amount must be at least CHF 50,000 (Art. 632 OR). Contributions may be in cash or in kind; a licensed Swiss audit expert must issue a capital confirmation certificate (Kapitalbestätigung) verifying that the funds are at the free disposal of the company's management. No federal statutory escrow account is mandatory, but cantonal practice as applied in Zug commonly requires payment into a Swiss bank account blocked until registration, subject to the auditor's written confirmation.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial licensing board is mandated for wholesale procurement and cross-border trading of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder under ISIC 4662. Where the company conditionally operates physical premises, the competent authorities are the Cantonal Office for Economic Affairs of Zug (Amt für Wirtschaft und Arbeit des Kantons Zug) for trade supervision and the Cantonal Building Inspectorate for construction and storage permits.

Category: No statutory operating license is required for the pure cross-border trading model. If the company elects to store, repack, or handle lithium carbonate in Switzerland, a cantonal construction permit (Baubewilligung) and a hazardous substance notification under the Swiss Chemicals Ordinance (Chemikalien-Risikoreduktions-Verordnung, ChemRRV) are conditionally required for the warehouse facility.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA), Operations and Information Domain; portal: FTA ePortal (https://www.estv.admin.ch), together with cantonal tax authorities for direct tax administration

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must enroll for VAT via FTA ePortal or Form 754.00 to obtain a Swiss VAT number (MWST/UID). Direct corporate income tax registration is effected through entry in the cantonal commercial register and subsequent registration with the competent cantonal and communal tax authority once permanent establishment or taxable nexus exists. A cantonal tax identification number is issued at that stage.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must enroll for VAT via FTA ePortal or Form 754.00 to obtain a Swiss VAT number (MWST/UID). Direct corporate income tax registration is effected through entry in the cantonal commercial register and subsequent registration with the competent cantonal and communal tax authority once permanent establishment or taxable nexus exists. A cantonal tax identification number is issued at that stage.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows Swiss Code of Obligations legal form. Available elections: VAT grouping election (Art. 15 VAT Act) for economically, financially and organisationally integrated entities; option to treat sale of controlling shareholding as tax-neutral; election under real estate laws for partial VAT deduction (Art. 30 VAT Act). No elective hybrid-mismatch classification is permitted beyond the OECD Hybrid Mismatch Rules implemented in Swiss domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover from supplies not exempt from VAT (Art. 10 VAT Act); foreign suppliers with no Swiss registered seat must register once this threshold is exceeded, except where turnover arises exclusively from supplies already subject to tax at source or from exempt activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods that are demonstrably exported out of Swiss customs territory qualify for 0% VAT (Art. 53(1) VAT Act). The supplier must possess a valid Swiss VAT number, obtain customs export confirmation, and maintain transport and payment evidence. Supplies of ancillary services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., freight, handling) are also zero-rated if billed to the non-resident purchaser or used overseas.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Swiss Customs Export Certificate (Ausfuhrschein)
  • Commercial Invoice

No real-time fiscal clearance, API transmission, or digital stamping is mandated. Switzerland permits electronic invoicing (e-billing) under the Swiss QR-bill standard and SUVE VSA messaging; invoices must contain the statutory elements of Art. 26a VAT Act. B2B exporters retain the right to invoice in paper or electronic format without state validation.

Exporters must keep zero-rating documentation for at least 10 years. If the tax authority determines that export proof is insufficient, the 8.1% VAT is retroactively assessed on the transaction, plus default interest at 4.5% per annum. Intentional evasion triggers a fine of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 70 VAT Act); negligent conduct attracts up to one-third of the tax. For false certifications, separate administrative fines up to CHF 10,000 may be imposed per document. Criminal prosecution and VAT registration revocation are additional exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Swiss franc is freely convertible and there is no general foreign exchange control law, no surrender requirement, and no central bank licensing for trade payments. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are unrestricted subject to Swiss anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35%, which may be reduced or waived under applicable double taxation treaties (e.g., DTT with China, Chile, Argentina, or ultimate beneficial owner countries). The Swiss National Bank does not screen individual commercial payments; all payment routing is executed via SIX Interbank Clearing (SIC) and the international SWIFT network under FINMA customer due diligence rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate or surrender export proceeds. Foreign currency receivables from battery-grade lithium carbonate sales may be held indefinitely in Swiss or foreign bank accounts. Only standard corporate income tax and commercial bookkeeping rules apply to foreign currency assets, requiring translation to CHF under Swiss GAAP FER at year-end.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank; financial market conduct and banking supervision co-regulated by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated EMIs or EU/EEA payment institutions) cannot lawfully provide Swiss domestic payment services unless they establish a Swiss branch or obtain authorisation under the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA) and Financial Services Act (FinSA). They do not have direct access to the SIC clearing system and generally lack commercial trade finance and documentary LC capabilities required for large battery-metals settlements. Use as a complementary receiving rail only for low-value, fully disclosed transactions, subject to enhanced underlying commercial documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant share capital injection of CHF 100,000 minimum (fully subscried and paid in for an AG) must be remitted via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's personal or corporate account to a bank-designated blocked capital escrow account in Switzerland. The wire narrative must clearly state 'Share Capital Payment — CH_AG', reference the UID-8087 commercial register identifier, and name the relevant incorporation jurisdiction (canton). No SNB or FINMA pre-authorisation is required. The receiving bank must issue a deposit certificate (Bestätigung) used by the notary and Commercial Register Office to complete registration. For subsequent trade funding injections, classify payments as 'Shareholder Loan' or 'Equity Contribution' with a formal loan agreement or financing resolution; apply arm's-length interest rates and maintain full audit trail for corporate income tax purposes.Outbound supplier payments for lithium carbonate purchases are to be executed principally through SWIFT MT103 priority wires for smaller lots and through Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 issuance, with MT798 negotiation messages) for larger battery-grade volumes. For CHF payments use the SIC system; for EUR, use EuroSIC/TARGET2; for USD, route via the bank's New York correspondent (CHIPS or Fedwire). Third-party disbursements (freight forwarders, warehousing, insurance, blending, quality inspection) must be supported by the operative commercial instruments and added as approved payees in the banking mandate. For China-based suppliers, an irrevocable, confirmed L/C at sight or 30/60-day tenor is the benchmark instrument; for Chile/Argentina suppliers, use the same L/C platform with SWIFT MT710/MT720 amendments as needed. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on net profit after federal tax (equivalent to approximately 6.4% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal and communal effective rate in Canton Zug approximates 11.6% to 12.8% for a standard trading AG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File is triggered when the Swiss entity's annual gross turnover reaches or exceeds CHF 80 million; mandatory Master File is triggered when the worldwide group's consolidated annual revenue reaches or exceeds CHF 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more. Below these thresholds, the tax administration retains the right to request contemporaneous documentation within 60 days and to shift the burden of proof in transfer pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, effective substance determines treaty entitlement under Swiss anti-treaty-abuse rules (Article 50 DBG), the applicability of the BEPS MLI principal purpose test, and the validity of cantonal tax rulings. In Canton Zug, the tax administration requires a qualifying company to maintain a registered office, a board acting with genuine decision-making at Swiss meetings, qualified personnel, bank accounts, operational trading systems and audited books; a mere brass-plate or mailbox AG is treated as an administrative or domiciliary shell, with denial of benefits, retroactive reassessment and criminal consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 175 DBG, a fine of up to three times the evaded federal tax; cantonal law imposes an analogous evasion surcharge of up to twice the evaded cantonal tax; arrears interest applies from the due date; under Article 176 DBG, failure to submit required documentation attracts an additional procedural fine; tax amnesty options are not available once fraud is detected.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is possible where the AG is a shell or where the board instructed or tolerated the evasion; directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes, are criminally prosecutable, may face travel bans and asset seizures, and in cases involving false documents may be sentenced to imprisonment under Article 186 DBG.
Tax Fraud and Falsification of Documents (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Aggravated tax fraud under Article 186 DBG: custodial sentence of up to five years or a monetary penalty; confiscation of the illicit financial advantage under Articles 70–71 Swiss Criminal Code; loss of the right to object or appeal; reopening of the statute-barred years up to ten years in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Directors, executive officers and beneficial owners who forged or ordered the falsification are personally prosecuted; corporate veil is pierced; civil restitution is ordered to the full extent of the unpaid tax; international arrest warrants and Interpol Red Notices can be issued where fraudulent proceeds were routed abroad.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance (Verrechnungssteuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Strict liability for the full 35% withholding tax on the Swiss AG, plus default interest; under the Withholding Tax Act the responsible directors face criminal fines and, for willful and repeated violation, imprisonment; the tax administration may order restitution against the company and its overseers; the shareholder loses the treaty refund right where the claim is filed late.

Director personal liability: The board is jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld tax; criminal prosecution attaches to the directors who signed the payment orders; travel bans, precautionary seizure of private assets and extradition may follow where the directors are non-residents.
AML, Shell Company Abuse and Bank Secrecy Defence

Statutory sanction: Violation of Swiss anti-money laundering due diligence obligations may be fined under the AMLA (GwG); criminal money laundering under Article 305bis Swiss Criminal Code carries imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, increased to five years for aggravated cases; bank freezes, MROS reporting, administrative dissolution of the shell AG and cancellation of all Zug tax benefits apply; the Federal Tax Administration may also treat the structure as an abuse of law and reassess concealed profit distributions as constructive dividends subject to 35% WHT.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is disregarded where the AG is a pure front; directors face criminal responsibility for laundering proceeds of foreign battery-metals transactions, civil claims for restitution of dissipated company assets, exit bans, asset freezes, deprivation of management capacity and imprisonment; extradition and mutual legal assistance to the home jurisdiction of the beneficial owner are routinely granted.
