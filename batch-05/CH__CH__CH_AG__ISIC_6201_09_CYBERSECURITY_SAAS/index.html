Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV/FTA), portal at https://www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal and communal tax authorities for direct taxes at the cantonal level.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office that performs taxable transactions in Switzerland must obtain a Swiss VAT identification number (MWST-ID/UID) by filing the official VAT registration form with the ESTV. The application is submitted in writing or via the Swiss e-service platform; the entity must declare its Swiss turnover, identify the type of taxable supplies, and, if it has no Swiss establishment or residence, appoint a tax representative domiciled in Switzerland. The ESTV issues the MWST-ID/UID upon acceptance, and the number is also entered in the Swiss UID register.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office that performs taxable transactions in Switzerland must obtain a Swiss VAT identification number (MWST-ID/UID) by filing the official VAT registration form with the ESTV. The application is submitted in writing or via the Swiss e-service platform; the entity must declare its Swiss turnover, identify the type of taxable supplies, and, if it has no Swiss establishment or residence, appoint a tax representative domiciled in Switzerland. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss stock companies are subject to ordinary corporate income taxation at federal, cantonal, and communal levels; there is no check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory electives include participation relief, capital contribution reserves treatment, notional interest deduction for Swiss domiciliary companies where applicable, and Swiss VAT group taxation for entities with closely linked economic, financial, and organizational relationships. Hybrid mismatch rules follow Swiss tax law and international treaty practice; no unilateral US-style entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover from supplies that are not exempt from VAT; lower or no threshold for foreign suppliers without Swiss domicile if they perform taxable supplies in Switzerland.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Switzerland are zero-rated provided the goods are physically exported to a foreign destination and the exporter can prove the export by an approved customs document. Services that are supplied to foreign recipients or used abroad may be outside Swiss VAT scope if the recipient-based place of supply rules are met. For cybersecurity SaaS supplied to foreign business customers, zero-rating or exclusion from Swiss VAT applies if the service is not effectively used and enjoyed in Switzerland and the customer is an entrepreneur with a foreign business establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec/export declaration accepted by Swiss Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or record of incoming foreign currency payment proving the service was supplied to a foreign customer

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory continuous transaction control regime for standard B2B VAT invoices. Invoicing must contain the statutory VAT elements and may be issued electronically. The Swiss e-bill and QR-bill standards are recognized, and for public procurement, electronic invoicing may be required by the contracting authority. There is no general API-based tax authority clearance or digital stamping obligation for invoices.

False or fraudulent claims of export zero-rating or VAT exclusion trigger reassessment of withheld VAT, interest, and fines for tax evasion. Swiss VAT law imposes administrative fines, and criminal penalties may apply for deliberate tax fraud, including imprisonment in severe cases. The tax authority may retrospectively revoke zero-rating benefits and claw back input VAT credits claimed on the underlying transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Swiss franc on both current account and capital account for residents and non-residents. No prior authorisation, licence, or foreign-exchange declaration is required for equity injections, loan disbursements, trade settlements, profit distributions, or dividend repatriations to and from a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. A Swiss AG may freely hold, borrow, and disburse funds in CHF, USD, EUR, or any other convertible currency. Practical obstacles are limited to (a) bank-side AML/KYC due diligence, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds verification; (b) Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividend distributions, which is a tax friction rather than a convertibility restriction and is fully mitigable through double taxation treaties or holding-company relief; and (c) statistical reporting obligations conducted by the banking counterparty to the SNB, which require accurate payment purpose information.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Switzerland. A Swiss exporter is legally permitted to retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts indefinitely in any currency, both onshore and offshore, without obligation to convert into CHF or to repatriate within any specified period. The only operational requirement is that cross-border payments must carry an accurate payment purpose description because Swiss banks report cross-border payment statistics to the SNB under the balance-of-payments framework; incorrect or missing purpose codes may cause bank-side processing delays but do not create direct penalties for the exporting company. Sanctions screening under SECO regulations remains applicable to all counterparties.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; official legal name: Swiss National Bank, German: Schweizerische Nationalbank), with statutory head office in Berne and administration in Zurich. The SNB is the sole monetary authority for the Swiss franc (CHF) under the National Bank Act (SR 951.11) and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (SR 941.10). It conducts monetary policy, oversees the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) payment system, manages the national payment statistics framework, and receives cross-border payment reports from Swiss banks. The Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervises banks and financial intermediaries for AML/CFT compliance; the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) administers targeted economic sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary treasury and FX collection tool; Low-to-Medium as the primary corporate banking relationship. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, or Airwallex hold EEA or UK e-money licences and are not FINMA-licensed. They cannot directly issue a Swiss IBAN or clear CHF payments through SIC because SIC participation is restricted to Swiss-regulated banks and licensed securities infrastructures. Legal constraints: (a) taking deposits or providing payment services to the Swiss public without a FINMA licence is impermissible under the Banking Act and the Financial Institutions Act; (b) foreign EMIs operating through a Swiss branch require appropriate Swiss authorisation; (c) CHF clearing must be routed through a licensed Swiss SIC participant. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an initial incorporation of the CH AG, the share capital must be at least CHF 100,000, of which at least 20% (CHF 50,000) must be paid up before registration. The funds are remitted by the foreign shareholder from the shareholder's own bank account using SWIFT MT103 with field /70 specifying EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - KAPITALEINLAGE CH AG - [shareholder legal name]. The funds must be credited to a capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the company in formation or, for an existing company, to the corporate operational account with the bank issuing a bank confirmation certificate (Bankenschein) for the commercial register. The transfer must originate solely from the direct shareholder account; third-party payments are generally rejected by Swiss banks for capital contributions. Accurate purpose-code classification must be provided on the bank form because Swiss banks must report cross-border payment data to the SNB. Non-CHF capital contributions are permissible and are recorded at the exchange rate on the date of conversion or payment. After registration, the escrow balance is released into the operational account. For subsequent capital increases, the same MT103 routing applies with the purpose tag CAPITAL INCREASE - SHARE ISSUE, and documentary evidence of the shareholders' resolution must accompany the payment.The primary clearing rail for SaaS invoices and cross-border disbursements is SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, executed from the Swiss corporate bank account to the beneficiary bank. For Swiss domestic counterparties, CHF payments settle through Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC), including the SIC5 instant-payment service with same-day settlement. For EUR payments to EU and EEA counterparties, Swiss banks participate in the Single Euro Payments Area via SEPA Credit Transfer with ISO 20022 pain.001 files; for USD payments outside the United States, settlement is routed through the Swiss bank's US correspondent using CHIPS or Fedwire. Documentary trade instruments, such as commercial letters of credit, are transmitted via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed through the bank's trade finance desk; this is rarely needed for pure SaaS but available for procurement contracts involving hardware appliances. Treasury standing payment instructions should include fixed beneficiary whitelists for recurring royalty and licensing payments, strict reference-field discipline, and automatic reconciliation against the Stripe settlement report to avoid purpose-code ambiguity.Deliverable OTC FX forward contracts (USD/CHF and EUR/CHF) executed with Swiss banking counterparties under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank standard FX terms; fully permissible without foreign-exchange or derivatives licensing for a non-financial corporate.FX swaps combining a spot transaction and a forward leg, used for short-term liquidity management and cross-currency funding.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for term conversion of USD-denominated liabilities into CHF and for synchronising interest and principal cash flows.Vanilla European FX options purchased to protect against adverse USD/CHF or EUR/CHF volatility while preserving upside participation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible in the Swiss regulatory framework but are not market-available or commercially necessary for CHF because the franc is fully convertible and a liquid deliverable market exists; the company should use deliverable instruments exclusively.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary treasury and FX collection tool; Low-to-Medium as the primary corporate banking relationship. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, or Airwallex hold EEA or UK e-money licences and are not FINMA-licensed. They cannot directly issue a Swiss IBAN or clear CHF payments through SIC because SIC participation is restricted to Swiss-regulated banks and licensed securities infrastructures. Legal constraints: (a) taking deposits or providing payment services to the Swiss public without a FINMA licence is impermissible under the Banking Act and the Financial Institutions Act; (b) foreign EMIs operating through a Swiss branch require appropriate Swiss authorisation; (c) CHF clearing must be routed through a licensed Swiss SIC participant. The compliant architecture is to use the EMI as a multi-currency collection and FX conversion layer for USD-denominated Stripe revenue, with daily sweeps into a primary Swiss IBAN at a tier-1 bank. Official corporate banking, statutory share capital escrow, and Swiss clearing must remain with UBS, ZKB, or another FINMA-regulated bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal rate: 8.5% (effective federal rate after deduction: 7.83%). Combined federal plus cantonal plus communal rate in Zug: approximately 12.2% for 2024 assessment, comprising a cantonal/communal component of approximately 4.3% plus federal component, before applicable IP-box and R&D deductions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends paid by a Swiss AG, irrespective of the shareholder's residence. The rate is reduced to 0% under the Swiss/EU parent-subsidiary privilege where the parent holds at least 10% for 12 months and is resident in a covered jurisdiction, or to 5-15% under an applicable double taxation agreement, depending on participation thresholds and treaty wording.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For companies using a 31 December fiscal year-end, the tax return must be filed by 31 March of the following year. Canton Zug routinely grants an initial extension to 30 June, and upon written request, a further extension to 30 September. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the return is due by the last day of the sixth month following the close of the fiscal year, with equivalent extension options.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax practice requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation when a Swiss taxpayer's aggregated cross-border controlled transactions exceed CHF 10 million per fiscal year. Companies that are part of a multinational group with worldwide consolidated revenue above CHF 1 billion are expected to maintain both a Master File and a Local File. Documentation must include a functional, risk and asset analysis, five-year comparison, a description of the selected OECD-approved method, and a benchmark study of comparable transactions.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate an ESR legal framework in the Gulf Cooperation Council sense, but its general anti-abuse rule and treaty entitlement doctrine impose an equivalent economic substance requirement. A Swiss cybersecurity SaaS company must demonstrate real substance in the Zug canton through a physical office, qualified engineering and management employees, strategic decision-making in Switzerland, board minutes and commercial contracts entered into by Swiss management. Failure to satisfy substance results in recharacterisation, application of DBT Art. 21 GAAR, and denial of treaty relief. The IP box is only available for qualifying IP developed in Switzerland, satisfying the modified nexus approach. Substance indicators should include Swiss R&D staff, threat-monitoring operations, and incident-response functions physically performed in Switzerland.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Qualified Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under DBT Art. 174, tax evasion is punished with an administrative fine of up to the tax evaded plus default interest. Qualified tax fraud under DBT Art. 175 involves a custodial sentence of up to 3 years or a monetary penalty, increased to up to 5 years in qualified commercial cases. Unpaid tax becomes due in full with 3.5% annual interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecuted when they direct or execute false filings. The corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution of unpaid tax, interest and penalties; directors' personal assets in Switzerland may be seized. A criminal investigation can trigger travel bans, exit controls, extradition under mutual legal assistance, and board disqualification for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority applies DBT Art. 21 (GAAR), recharacterises the transaction and allocates the full profit to the Swiss substance entity. Tax treaty refunds are rescinded, cantonal tax incentives are withdrawn retroactively, and administrative fines are imposed for false declarations. In severe cases the company is subject to judicial dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors who established the shell arrangement are jointly and severally liable for re-assessed tax and penalties. Where the company becomes insolvent, directors may face wrongful-trading liability. Travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed during enforcement proceedings.
Anti-Money-Laundering Violations Involving Digital Payments

Statutory sanction: Article 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code criminalises money laundering with imprisonment of up to 3 years, or up to 5 years when committed as part of organised crime. All instrumentalities and objects of the offence are confiscated. If the company acts as a financial intermediary without a FINMA licence, administrative fines up to CHF 100 million may be imposed; Directors may be removed.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are personally criminally liable. Indictment triggers bank account freezes and travel bans; conviction leads to disbarment from financial services directorships and enhanced foreign extradition exposure.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Covet Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax authority makes an upward profit adjustment, assesses 3.5% default interest and may apply a 10% to 15% penalty surcharge. In case of false books, the authority may proceed under DBT Art. 175. The added tax, penalty and interest frequently exceed 50% of the original adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors bear independent responsibility for transfer pricing compliance and cannot delegate that duty to a foreign parent. Knowingly signing a false tax return or documentation triggers personal criminal liability, and civil liability for the resulting tax deficiency can be enforced against directors' assets.
