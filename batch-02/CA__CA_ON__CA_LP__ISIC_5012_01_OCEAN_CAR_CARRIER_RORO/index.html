Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and income tax administration, via CRA My Business Account portal; for Ontario provincial components, the Ontario Ministry of Finance administers the Ontario HST part and provincial tax registration requirements.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership with Canadian taxable activity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account. Procedure: (1) determine whether the LP is 'carrying on business in Canada' or is required to register under Excise Tax Act s.240 as a non-resident; (2) gather governing documents (LP certificate, partnership agreement, Ontario registration if any, non-resident owner details); (3) file CRA Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) online via My Business Account or by mail; (4) obtain the BN and separate 15-digit program accounts, including the RT (GST/HST) account for registrants; (5) register with the Ontario Business Registry if the LP has an Ontario establishment or carries on business in Ontario, but no separate Ontario HST account is required as HST is collected and administered by CRA; (6) file annual T5013 partnership information returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian partnership law and tax law do not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default. Relevant elections and recharacterization mechanisms include: (i) partnership contribution rollover election under Income Tax Act s.97(2); (ii) disposition of partnership interest elections and s.98(3) wind-up elections; (iii) non-resident partner's withholding election agreements under tax treaties and s.116 certificates; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under s.18.2 and s.18.3 that may re-characterize arrangements where the same entity is treated differently in Canada and the foreign home jurisdiction; (v) for publicly traded 'SIFT partnerships', an entity-level tax applies under s.122.1 subject to structural limitations, with no general election to avoid that status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per annum of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) under the small-supplier threshold in Excise Tax Act s.148; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or soliciting orders in Canada through an agent may have a mandatory registration obligation irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An international RoRo ocean transportation service carrying vehicles from Canada to a foreign port qualifies for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part II, s.6 of the Excise Tax Act as a supply of a transportation service for goods exported from Canada. Eligibility requires the freight service to be rendered in respect of goods that are genuinely exported, sold to a non-resident or delivered to a foreign consignee, and the carrier must hold documentary evidence tying the charge to an actual export. Services that terminate in Canada or involve cabotage within Canada, or where the cargo is re-imported without substantive foreign processing, are not zero-rated.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (A6 or CAED) / Canadian Export Reporting System transmission
  • Original Ocean Bill of Lading or waybill showing Canada loading port and foreign discharge port
  • Commercial invoice and signed freight contract or charter party
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or SWIFT MT103 payment evidence evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • RoRo stow plan and vehicle cargo manifest
  • Marine insurance certificate covering the international voyage
  • Port clearance and departure records from the terminal operator

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or mandatory API-based digital clearance regime currently exists. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if they satisfy the documentation standards under the Excise Tax Act and Regulations. GST/HST returns are required to be filed electronically in most cases through GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI. Registrants must show a valid BN/RT number on invoices. Ontario does not operate a separate real-time clearance regime; the HST component is CRA-administered on a single federal return.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rated export claims by requiring contemporaneous proof of physical export, payment, and foreign landing. Failure to substantiate results in reassessment at 13% HST on the freight revenue, plus interest and penalties. CRA may claw back zero-rating and associated input tax credits for up to four years from the date of the return, with no limitation period where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Penalties include: failure-to-file penalties of 5% of the unremitted GST/HST plus 1% per month up to 12 months; gross negligence penalty of 25% of the understated net tax; daily compound interest at the prescribed rate plus 4%; CBSA administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act and AMPS up to CAD 25,000 per export violation; and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible under a free-floating regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No central-bank permit is required for inbound capital contributions, outward remittances, profit repatriation, or dividend distribution by an Ontario Limited Partnership. Non-resident limited partners are subject only to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income allocations or distributions (generally 25%, treaty-reduced to 15% for dividends and 0-15% for interest under the Canada-US treaty), administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. Establishment of the LP as a new Canadian business by non-Canadians triggers a post-closing notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act; acquisition-of-control review thresholds apply only to controlling investments, with the 2024 WTO-investor enterprise-value threshold set at CAD 1.337 billion, and a passive LP capital contribution does not confer control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds, including ocean Ro-Ro freight income. Freight receipts in USD or CAD are freely disposable in the entity's operational treasury accounts. Reporting obligations are statistical and customs-related only: goods exports are declared to the Canada Border Services Agency on Form B13A; international service receipts exceeding CAD 30,000 in a calendar year are reported to Statistics Canada via the Trade Information System forms BC1 and BC3; and all cross-border electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reported by the beneficiary financial institution to FINTRAC. Failure to file the statistics reports is an administrative offence but does not restrict access to funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority issuing the Canadian dollar. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority; exchange controls were abolished in 1951. Prudential oversight of scheduled banks' FX risk is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Department of Finance Canada administers the Financial Administration Act. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a supplementary settlement rail; Low as primary treasury infrastructure. Canada does not recognise a standalone Electronic Money Institution licence. Non-bank payment providers must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, effective under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, must be registered as payment service providers. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Revolut Business, etc.) may operate as FINTRAC-registered foreign MSBs but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, hold regulated escrow balances for vessel acquisitions, or provide committed trade finance. For this Ro-Ro shipping project, all LC operations (SWIFT MT700) and high-value SWIFT MT103 wire settlements must be executed through a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are wired by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's segregated Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Standard routing: (1) the remitting bank sends the MT103 to the Canadian bank's correspondent Nostro account, using the beneficiary account number of the escrow account and field 70 (Remittance Information) containing 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [partner name] - [vessel IMO number] - CLASS A', together with an ISO 20022 purpose code reflecting a capital transfer; (2) USD leg clears via CHIPS or Fedwire through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent (e.g., RBC New York or BMO Harris); CAD leg clears via SWIFT to the beneficiary bank for same-day finality on Lynx; (3) no Bank of Canada approval is required. The beneficiary Canadian bank files a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report for every international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more and performs source-of-funds verification against the onboarding dossier; (4) the LP must file an Investment Canada Act notification with ISED within 30 days of establishment; capital contributions are not taxable events for Canadian income tax purposes, and no Part XIII withholding applies to pure equity injections unless the contribution is recharacterised as debt under the Canada Revenue Agency's debt-equity rules.Trade settlements use two parallel clearing rails: (a) Direct Disbursement Rail: SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for voyage-level costs (bunker, port charges, tugs, stevedores, crew wages, and agency fees) executed through the bank's treasury portal with SWIFT gpi tracking; USD wires clear through Fedwire/CHIPS, EUR through TARGET2, and CAD domestically through Lynx. (b) Documentary Trade Rail: Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 for shipyard instalments (vessel new-builds), engines, spare parts, and terminal equipment; MT707 amendments for milestone schedules; Standby Letters of Credit via MT760 as advance-payment or performance guarantees in favour of shipyards and port authorities; bank guarantees for terminal concession agreements. The bank will collateralise LC exposure with a cash margin of 10 to 50 percent for a newly formed LP or require a parent-company guarantee. Every disbursement must be supported by invoices, bills of lading, class-society certificates, and the LP's board-approved cash-flow model.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts (tenors up to 12 months standard; extendable to 5 years for new-build instalments) documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the Canadian Financial Institutions Master Agreement, with a credit support annex where the bank provides a hedging line.FX swaps (CAD/USD, EUR/USD, JPY/USD) for managing voyage-month working capital mismatches.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps converting USD term-SOFR floating-rate ship-finance debt into CAD fixed-rate, hedging both FX and interest-rate risk under IFRS 9 hedge accounting.Vanilla OTC FX options (CAD put / USD call and CAD call / USD put) for asymmetric protection of USD freight revenues.Listed CAD/USD futures on CME or Montréal Exchange, available but rarely used for balance-sheet hedging due to margin mechanics.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on CAD are not permissible and are not offered by OSFI-supervised dealers, because CAD is fully convertible with a deep onshore deliverable interbank market; any request for a CAD NDF will be declined.All OTC derivatives transactions are subject to Canadian trade reporting under OSC Rule 91-101 and National Instrument 93-101 through a recognised trade repository; the LP, as a non-financial counterparty, may rely on the end-user clearing exemption but must adopt a board resolution documenting the hedging purpose.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a supplementary settlement rail; Low as primary treasury infrastructure. Canada does not recognise a standalone Electronic Money Institution licence. Non-bank payment providers must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, effective under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, must be registered as payment service providers. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Revolut Business, etc.) may operate as FINTRAC-registered foreign MSBs but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, hold regulated escrow balances for vessel acquisitions, or provide committed trade finance. For this Ro-Ro shipping project, all LC operations (SWIFT MT700) and high-value SWIFT MT103 wire settlements must be executed through a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general federal and Ontario provincial corporate income tax rate for active business income (federal 15% plus Ontario 11.5%); Ontario small business rate 12.2% combined on first CAD 500,000 of active business income where eligible

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at the partnership level due to the pass-through classification of a Canadian Limited Partnership; Canadian-source income allocated to a non-resident partner is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% under section 212 of the Income Tax Act on distributions, deemed payments, and certain allocations. Actual distributions of previously taxed income may be structured to avoid double withholding depending on the partnership agreement and tax allocations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million in gross revenue for mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under section 247(4) of the Income Tax Act for the Canadian entity/partnership; Local File and Master File requirements apply for transactions exceeding CAD 5 million in aggregate value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's length persons. Canada also requires a CRA Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) and Schedule 91 for any reportable transaction regardless of the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Ontario and Canada apply the economic substance doctrine through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the statutory transfer pricing rules under section 247. For Limited Partnerships, the CRA may reassess allocations if the partnership lacks commercial substance or is considered a conduit. For international shipping, Canada does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE, but the CRA's anti-avoidance rules, treaty shopping restrictions under the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) Principle Purpose Test, and residency/permanent establishment rules impose an equivalent substance requirement. A non-resident partner claiming treaty relief for shipping income must demonstrate the vessel operations create no permanent establishment in Canada and that the partner is the beneficial owner of the income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Partnership Allocation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum CAD 2,000) and imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil reassessments with gross negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be jointly and severally liable under section 160 for transfers of property at below fair market value; directors of a corporate general partner are liable for failure to remit Part XIII withholding tax under section 227.1; CRA can impose travel bans under the Income Tax Act and personal civil restitution orders; imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion convictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under section 247(2) with penalties under subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment, reduced by the portion of the adjustment that is due to a gross valuation misrepresentation; interest accrues on reassessed taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be liable for the penalty only if the corporation fails to remit and they were negligent in exercising duty; CRA can issue director's liability assessment under section 227.1; personal exposure generally limited to unremitted withholding amounts but severe cases can lead to director disqualification and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation for corporations and partnerships; criminal fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering proceeds; forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Limited partners are shielded from liability unless they take part in management; general partners and directors of corporate general partner may be personally liable for AML penalties, subject to travel bans and imprisonment for willful blindness; corporate veil may be pierced under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act and criminal law where the partnership is a façade for illicit shipping activity.
