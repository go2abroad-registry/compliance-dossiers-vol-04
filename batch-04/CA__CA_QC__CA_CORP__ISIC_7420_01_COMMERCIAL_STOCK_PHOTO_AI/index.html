Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal GST/HST, corporate income tax and Business Number administration); Revenu Québec (Quebec Sales Tax, provincial corporate income tax, payroll and consumption taxes). Electronic portals: CRA My Business Account / Mon dossier d'entreprise and Revenu Québec Mon dossier d'entreprise via CléNumérique.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation first registers for a federal Business Number (BN) with a program account using Business Registration Online, Form RC1-W or a CRA application; the BN is a 9-digit number with a two-letter account code (RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax). For Quebec, the entity must separately register with Revenu Québec for QST via the Register a Business service or Form LM-1-V and will receive a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Registration is mandatory once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded or if the non-resident elects to register voluntarily; the same BN is used for all federal accounts and QST filings, and Revenu Québec may accept the BN as the common registrant identifier after NEQ issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A business corporation incorporated federally or in Quebec is a legally separate taxable entity; it is not fiscally transparent and cannot be treated as a disregarded entity. As a resident corporation (by place of incorporation or central management and control), it is subject to Canadian income tax on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source income and can be subject to branch tax under Income Tax Act section 219.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law; corporations are statutorily taxable entities. Available elections relevant to cross-border digital asset platforms include: (i) Income Tax Act section 216(1) net-basis election for non-residents earning Canadian rental or certain Canadian-source income, (ii) the non-resident corporation election to file a Part I return instead of Part XIII withholding where treaty or statute permits, (iii) GST/HST registrant elections for closely related corporations and joint election for employer-employee rebates, and (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (sections 18.6 to 18.8 as enacted following BEPS Action 2) which disallow deductions where a hybrid entity or instrument creates a cross-border double non-taxation outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold): a supplier must register for GST/HST once total worldwide taxable supplies from Canadian economic activity exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act sections 148 and 240). The same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to QST registration by Revenu Québec under the Taxation Act; registration as a QST registrant is required for any person carrying on commercial activity in Quebec who exceeds the threshold, and voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies where tangible personal property is exported in accordance with Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, with documentary evidence that the goods have been shipped out of Canada and title transferred to the non-resident buyer. For services and intangible personal property, including commercial stock photography licences and generative visual assets: the supply is zero-rated when made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and the service or intangible is not principally for use in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. The platform must demonstrate that the buyer is outside Canada (billing address, IP geolocation, payment settlement data from Stripe or PayPal) and that the digital asset or licence is delivered offshore; domestic Canadian licences and downloads remain taxable at the combined GST+QST rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) for physical exports)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or courier delivery proof evidencing movement out of Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/FX transfer record demonstrating receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice / zero-rated tax invoice bearing the GST/HST and QST registration numbers and export statement
  • Stock photo or generative visual asset licence agreement identifying the non-resident licensee and territorial scope of the intellectual property
  • Stripe/PayPal settlement records and buyer geolocation metadata substantiating the offshore digital delivery

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing or tax clearance stamping regime is currently in force. CRA and Revenu Québec do not operate continuous transaction control (CTC) reporting; invoicing must comply with the Excise Tax Act disclosure rules and Quebec's French-language invoice rules. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard, based on the Peppol network, is being adopted voluntarily for B2B electronic exchange but is not a fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; electronic records supporting GST/HST and QST invoices must be retained for at least six years and made available in a readable format on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinise export zero-rating through complete export documentation and non-resident status evidence. If goods or digital supplies are not demonstrably exported or retained in Canada, or if invoices are fraudulent, the entire output tax is re-assessed at 5% GST and 9.975% QST with interest from the original due date. Gross negligence penalties may be assessed under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 (generally 20% of the net tax amount), Income Tax Act section 163(2) (50% of tax avoided), and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Excise Tax Act section 327 or Income Tax Act section 239, carrying substantial fines and imprisonment. The normal four-year GST/HST and QST reassessment window is extended without limit for misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default, permitting retrospective clawbacks of refunds previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status. There are no exchange controls, no Central Bank approval gates, and no repatriation licensing requirements for inward capital injections, profit remittances, dividend payments, or royalty outflows. The functional currency for accounting may be CAD or USD; Quebec corporate tax filings require CAD-denominated financial statements under IFRS/IAS 21. The only material statutory friction on profit outbound is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced), which is a tax compliance matter rather than an FX convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio (surrender requirement is 0%). Export receipts from stock-photo license sales and AI-generated visual asset subscriptions may be retained indefinitely in USD or other foreign currencies in Canadian or offshore accounts. For GST/HST purposes, export sales of digital photographic assets are zero-rated supplies; the exporter must retain documentation of non-resident customers to support the zero-rating. FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting applies to fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and CAD 10,000 or more in cash must be reported by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority for CAD; foreign-exchange policy administration rests with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision of banks is delegated to OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); cross-border transaction monitoring and AML reporting are administered by FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a payment-orchestration layer, not as a substitute for a regulated tier-1 bank account. Canada does not operate an EU-style EMI licence framework. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer can provide multi-currency accounts via pooled partner-bank arrangements and are useful for mass low-value disbursements to photographers and micro-suppliers, but they are not CDIC-insured, are not direct members of the Canadian clearing system, and cannot accept deposits in the legal sense. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers offering services to Canadians must register with the Bank of Canada; foreign EMIs that merely route funds into Canada generally operate under limited exemptions and remain under the AML supervision of FINTRAC. The recommended treasury architecture is a tier-1 Quebec bank account as the treasury spine, with EMIs and platform payout rails (Stripe Connect and PayPal) used as disbursement conduits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound equity or shareholder-loan proceeds via SWIFT MT103 with clear remittance narrative stating Share Capital Contribution or Shareholder Subordinated Loan. For CAD settlement use the Canadian Lynx real-time gross settlement system; for USD leg use Fedwire or CHIPS through the receiving bank. The remittance narrative should reference the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and the corporate legal name to satisfy FATCA/CRS classification. No official purpose-code taxonomy applies in Canada; however the bank will require a source-of-funds declaration and will report any transaction of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. Deposited funds should first be credited to a capital escrow sub-account and released to the operating account only upon board resolution specifying the capital-allocation purpose.For royalty and service-fee payments to photographers and content suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 for global payouts, Canada Lynx for domestic CAD EFT, and Fedwire or CHIPS for USD-denominated settlements; commercial letters of credit are inapplicable to digital stock-photo and AI-asset transactions, so disbursing against invoice with contractual payment terms is standard. For mass micropayments, use platform payout rails including Stripe Connect, PayPal MassPay, and Wise Batch. The jurisdiction of each payee must be verified to support income-tax and Part XIII withholding obligations: the statutory rate on non-resident royalties and services is 25%, with Canada-US treaty rates lowered to 10% for most royalties and to 0% for copyright royalties on production or reproduction of artistic and literary works, provided the payee submits a complete NR301 declaration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a payment-orchestration layer, not as a substitute for a regulated tier-1 bank account. Canada does not operate an EU-style EMI licence framework. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer can provide multi-currency accounts via pooled partner-bank arrangements and are useful for mass low-value disbursements to photographers and micro-suppliers, but they are not CDIC-insured, are not direct members of the Canadian clearing system, and cannot accept deposits in the legal sense. The recommended treasury architecture is a tier-1 Quebec bank account as the treasury spine, with EMIs and platform payout rails (Stripe Connect and PayPal) used as disbursement conduits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). For a Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the small business deduction on eligible active business income, the combined rate is approximately 12.2%; the stock-photo AI platform must assess whether it qualifies under the SBD rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders; the rate is reduced by Canada's tax treaty network (Canada-US: 15% for portfolio dividend, 5% if the beneficial owner holds 10% or more of the voting shares; Quebec does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents must be filed when the aggregate value of covered transactions with each non-resident non-arm's-length person exceeds CAD 1,000,000, or when the highest amount of unpaid intercompany debt exceeds CAD 1,000,000. OECD-aligned Local File, Master File, and Country-by-Country Report threshold: CRA expects contemporaneous documentation for entities with total gross revenue or assets exceeding CAD 50,000,000, or where cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000 for a single transaction class.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a Gulf-cooperation-type ESR, but the tax authorities enforce substance through the 'central management and control' residence test, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245, and the Quebec Taxation Act's abuse-of-rights doctrine. A stock-photo AI company must demonstrate real board meetings in Canada, operational decision-making, contracts, payroll, and datacentre/algorithm ownership in Canada; failure to maintain local substance may permit re-characterization as a foreign-resident shell and the denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act, s.239: fine on conviction of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to five years; court-ordered payment of arrears, interest and penalties; Quebec Taxation Act s.53 imposes additional penalties and provincial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate income taxes where they signed or participated in wilful evasion; CRA may issue director-liability assessments under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and under s.160 for insufficient consideration transfers; travel bans may be issued where a criminal charge is laid or where CRA believes the director is attempting to leave Canada with taxable assets.
Transfer Pricing Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act, s.247(3): 10% penalty on the largest transfer pricing adjustment where the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to use arm's length prices; CRA may also invoke GAAR to deny the entire transaction benefit; Quebec imposes additional reassessment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are not personally liable for simple transfer pricing reassessments, but where false invoices or fictitious royalties are used, the directors face s.239 tax evasion personal liability, money-laundering charges, and administrative civil penalties for gross negligence under CRA's reporting rules.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can re-characterize the Canadian entity as not 'resident in Canada' and deny treaty relief; GAAR allows cancellation of tax benefits; under the Quebec Companies Act, a reconsideration may lead to dissolution or revocation of authority to carry on business in Quebec.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign materially false returns or permit fraudulent invoicing may be assessed civilly under Quebec civil liability principles; CRA may pursue a third-party civil action for conspiracy, and Canadian courts may issue international travel bans under the Tax Court's interlocutory powers where a director fails to provide security for reassessed tax debts.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Financial Sanctions

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000; criminal liability for knowingly engaging in prostitution, tax evasion facilitation, or terrorism funding with a fine and imprisonment up to five years; Quebec's Attorney General may seize the corporate bank accounts under provincial civil forfeiture legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transactions with non-compliant third parties may be personally liable as de facto money transmitters; Canadian courts may impose asset-freeze injunctions, travel bans, and extradition orders for ML-activity, and the company's corporate veil is pierced when the board is found to be a mere façade.
