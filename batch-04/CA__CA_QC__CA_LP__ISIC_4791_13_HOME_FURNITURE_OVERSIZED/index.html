Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST). Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec CliquezAffaires (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign Limited Partnership must obtain a federal GST/HST account by filing Form RC1M – Request for a Business Number (BN) with CRA, and must separately register for QST with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V (Application for Registration – QST). Required supporting documents include the partnership’s constituent agreement, offshore proof of legal existence, the Canadian agent/representative appointment (if non-resident), and a completed Canadian Revenue Agency Business Number questionnaire. Processing generally takes 2–8 weeks; the LP must, from the registration effective date, charge GST/HST and QST on taxable supplies made in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' system. Under the Income Tax Act (sections 96–103), a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: income, gains, credits, and recaptures flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and is itself a registrant if it makes taxable supplies, with no equivalent to hybrid mismatch elections. Partners may separately elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for net rental income treatment only if the partnership derives solely from real property; otherwise, no pass-through election alternatives are available. No hybrid mismatch election exists under Canadian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies from all sales in the preceding 12 months; QST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Quebec in the preceding 12 months. A foreign online retailer without physical presence in Canada must register as soon as Canadian/Quebec taxable supplies exceed these thresholds; there is no small-supplier exemption for non-residents supplying digital or tangible goods through electronic channels.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of tangible personal property (e.g., oversized home furniture) is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes when the property is exported from Canada by the supplier, by the recipient, or at the direction of the supplier, and the supply is made to a person outside Canada. Goods sold online and shipped directly to a final customer outside Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST and, by reciprocal provision under Quebec’s QST regime, 0% QST, provided the supplier retains proof of exportation. Goods delivered within Canada, including to Quebec consumers, are subject to GST/HST and QST at the point-of-delivery rate; pure export transactions do not require charging tax, but the taxpayer must substantiate the physical removal from Canada within a reasonable period (normally 60 days from the time of supply).

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) or a commercial invoice signed by the exporter when exported through a carrier
  • Bill of Lading or through-bill of lading issued by the ocean/air/rail carrier confirming foreign destination
  • Airway bill or Canada Post/USPS/private courier waybill showing Canadian pick-up and overseas delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record evidencing foreign currency payment from the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • COO (Certificate of Origin) or any certificate issued by CBSA when required
  • Sales contract or purchase order containing Incoterms proving the risk transfer and export responsibility

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for GST/HST or QST invoices. Invoices must contain mandatory tax information (transaction date, supplier/recipient identification, description, GST/QST rates and amounts, and total tax) and be preserved in French or English for six years. Electronic invoices are permitted; if used, the system must ensure integrity, readability, and auditability. Revenu Québec does not require an API-based clearance, but the CRA and RQ may request access to a filer’s electronic billing records during audit. The voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard (Peppol BIS) is in progressive adoption but is not yet law.

CRA and Revenu Québec apply strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If export substantiation is insufficient or the goods are subsequently diverted back into Canada or consumed in Canada, the zero-rating is reversed, GST/HST and QST become due with interest, and penalties may apply under sections 280 and 285 of the Excise Tax Act and the QST Act. Reassessments can be issued within 4 years (no time limit in cases of willful misrepresentation or fraud). Penalties include a minimum of CAD 250, plus 25% of the net tax deficiency for knowingly making false statements, gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax claimed, and criminal liability for fraudulent intentions, including fines and imprisonment. For QST, Revenu Québec mirrors federal penalties and may also impose a 10% late-filing penalty plus 0.5% per month of unreported QST.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at entity level (fiscally transparent limited partnership). Allocated net income taxed in the hands of partners: combined federal and Quebec general corporate rate 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec); top combined individual marginal rate 53.31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on distributions and allocations to non-resident partners, statutorily deemed dividends under ITA s. 212(1.1); the same 25% applies to ordinary Canadian-source dividends paid by a corporation to non-residents. Treaty relief may reduce the rate (e.g., Canada-US Treaty Article X: 15% general, 5% for a company owning 10% or more of the voting shares).

Section C / Bénéfice net Net income or loss after Quebec-specific adjustments, including the Quebec capital tax allowance and investment tax credits. The allocated Quebec net income of a non-resident partner is subject to Quebec income tax; Revenu Québec may require withholding at source on remittances to non-resident partners. Failure to file the CO-511 attracts penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act (TAA), generally CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; failure to issue RL-15 slips attracts a penalty of CAD 25 per slip (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500); unpaid Quebec tax bears daily compound interest at the prescribed rate.
Section D / Répartition aux associés (RL-15) Allocation of net income/loss to each partner and issuance of RL-15 slips showing the partner's share of Quebec source income. Non-resident partners must receive an RL-15 and will file a Quebec return; the LP should retain a Quebec tax treaty reduction form where applicable. Failure to file the CO-511 attracts penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act (TAA), generally CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; failure to issue RL-15 slips attracts a penalty of CAD 25 per slip (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500); unpaid Quebec tax bears daily compound interest at the prescribed rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable controlled transactions with non-arm's length non-residents during the fiscal year — this mandatory threshold triggers the filing of Form T106 (Information Return on Transactions with Non-Residents) and the preparation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (a functional and economic analysis, local file, and, at group level, a recommended master file). The CRA may also require documentation for smaller transactions on a risk basis.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-format Economic Substance Regulations; the functional equivalent is the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the transfer pricing recharacterization power under s. 247(2), and the judge-made substance-over-form doctrine. The LP must maintain real economic presence in Quebec (inventory, contracts, fulfillment operations, management decisions); a paper-only LP whose profits are allocated to a foreign partner without local substance is vulnerable to GAAR, permanent establishment attribution, and denial of treaty benefits under the limitation-on-benefits provisions of the Canada-US treaty.
