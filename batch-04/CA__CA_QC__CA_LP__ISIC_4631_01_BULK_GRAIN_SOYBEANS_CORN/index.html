Contributions are agreed in the partnership agreement (contrat de societe); limited partners must contribute to the partnership fund and may withdraw only as permitted by law. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Revenu Québec – clicSUR business portal for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial accounts in Quebec

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with the CRA or using BRO. The BN must be activated for GST/HST (program account RT), payroll (RP), and income tax (RC) as applicable. If the partnership has a residence or establishment in Quebec, makes taxable supplies in Quebec, or has employees in Quebec, it must separately register with Revenu Québec to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ), QST registration, and provincial payroll accounts. A non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada should appoint an authorized agent resident in Canada for CRA and Revenu Québec communications and service of documents.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with the CRA or using BRO. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification regime. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent: income, gains, losses, and credits are allocated to partners, except for a deemed Canadian partnership under certain anti-hybrid rules. Available elections include: (i) an alternative fiscal period election under Income Tax Act section 249.1; (ii) a GST/HST election under Excise Tax Act section 273 to allow one partner to file and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership; and (iii) an election to zero-rate exported services under Schedule V, Part VI, Excise Tax Act when the supply is for exports and documentary requirements are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters in Canada (GST/HST small supplier threshold); same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST registration for taxable supplies in Quebec

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule V, Part V, Excise Tax Act where the seller retains evidence of export in the prescribed form within the applicable time period. The bulk grain brokerage service will be zero-rated if the brokerage service is a service of arranging for the export of tangible goods, or if it is a taxable service performed for a non-resident customer who does not ordinarily use or benefit from the service in Canada, provided the customer is not a registrant and the service is not related to real property situated in Canada, tangible property situated in Canada, or a service to be performed in Canada. QST zero-rating mirrors the GST/HST rules for exported goods and services supplied to non-residents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A / Canada Customs Coding Form)
  • Bill of Lading / Ocean Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/FX settlement evidence showing payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice and contracts identifying the non-resident buyer and commodity (soybeans/corn)
  • Customs broker certification or carrier certificate attesting export of the specified lot and HS code
  • Marine cargo report / port exit documents, including vessel notation and port of loading
  • Evidence of transfer of ownership and risk outside Canada (e.g., FOB/FCA Incoterms)
  • Dated proof of dispatch and export within the 60-day documentary window prescribed by CRA

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping is currently implemented federally or in Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper and standard electronic invoices if they contain all required information (supplier name and BN, date, description, consideration, GST/HST/QST amounts). Canada has voluntary B2B/B2C e-invoicing pilots; no statutory API clearance requirement exists. Suppliers must retain invoices and underlying export records for six years, or longer when required by Revenu Québec.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated supply as taxable if the exporter fails to produce documentary proof of export within the prescribed period. Consequences include additional GST/HST and QST, interest, and monetary penalties. Under Excise Tax Act section 309(1) and comparable QST provisions, a false or omitted statement made knowingly may result in penalties equal to 50% of the tax evaded or sought to be evaded; ordinary care disparities attract a 15% penalty. Repeated non-compliance can trigger criminal prosecution, director and partner liability, forfeiture of refund claims, and the restart of assessment periods under the normal reassessment limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital accounts; there are no exchange controls, no licensing of capital movements, and no restrictions on maintaining USD/EUR or other foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks. A Quebec limited partnership may receive capital contributions and disburse trading proceeds in any currency without central bank approval. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is unrestricted at the banking level; the Canadian tax net applies separately - non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on allocated Canadian-source business income, and Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced through NR301/NR302 procedures) applies to passive payments such as interest and management fees rather than to partnership drawings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline; export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any foreign currency. Compliance is documentary: (i) file export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System for physical exports above the prescribed value threshold (generally CAD 2,000 per shipment); (ii) obtain Canadian Grain Commission official inspection and weight certificates for bulk grain loaded at Canadian ports; (iii) retain bills of lading and commercial invoices to substantiate zero-rated GST/HST/QST export treatment; and (iv) comply with CBSA six-year record-retention requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority and operator of the Lynx real-time gross settlement system. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; the relevant mandates are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada (exchange-control policy and sanctions), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (prudential regulation of banks), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML and cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting), and the Canada Border Services Agency (export declaration and customs enforcement).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail; Medium as an ancillary collection/disbursement channel. Legal constraints: the Bank Act restricts banking business to chartered banks and authorized foreign banks; Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licence, so a foreign electronic money institution cannot access Lynx settlement, issue CAD-denominated instruments, or provide documentary credits. Foreign payment fintechs (e.g., Wise, dLocal, Airwallex) must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must operate through Schedule I bank partners. Their uninsured e-money balances, limited SWIFT coverage, and absence of LC/trade-finance capacity make them unsuitable for high-value bulk grain vessel brokerage. Recommended architecture: a Schedule I bank as treasury anchor, with a FINTRAC-registered MSB or bank-provided virtual IBAN rail used only for small foreign receipts and client collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the overseas partner's bank to the LP's account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Canada's exchange-control-free regime requires no central bank approval, but the receiving bank must file FINTRAC Large Electronic Funds Transfer reports for incoming international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more. CAD credits clear through the Bank of Canada Lynx RTGS; USD credits clear via the bank's US clearing agent over Fedwire or CHIPS for same-day value. Remittance information (field 70) must state 'Capital contribution to Quebec limited partnership - ISIC 4631 commodity brokerage' and carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'INVE'/'EQUI' (or SWIFT instruction 'INTC' for intra-group transfers); the LP must hold the executed partnership agreement, capital schedule, and source-of-funds banker's reference in the relationship file to satisfy any enhanced due diligence query.Primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 pacs.008 for high-value supplier, freight and demurrage payments; (2) irrevocable transferable documentary credits (sight or usance) conforming to UCP600, used where the LP acts as middleman between the overseas end-buyer and the grain originator - the LP instructs the issuing bank to transfer the LC to the ultimate supplier; (3) documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) for low-risk repeat buyers; (4) Fedwire/CHIPS for USD-denominated settlements staged through the bank's US clearing arm; and (5) Lynx domestic RTGS or EFT for CAD disbursements. Each instruction must carry the underlying contract reference in field 70 and a purpose code matching the commercial character of the payment to preserve an auditable trail.Outright deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/major trading currencies, typically up to 24-month tenor), permitted by Canadian Schedule I banks only where supported by a genuine underlying commodity-trading exposure.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD or CAD/foreign currency) for economically hedging multi-year supply contracts and aligning foreign-currency receivables with payables; must be documented in the LP's treasury policy.Vanilla FX options, zero-cost collars and participating forwards (CAD/USD), permitted for protecting USD-denominated brokerage commissions and grain sale margins.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), permitted only for hedging exposures in non-fully-convertible currencies (e.g., Brazilian real, Argentine peso, offshore CNY) where no deliverable forward market exists; CAD/USD hedges are executed as deliverable forwards because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible.

Their uninsured e-money balances, limited SWIFT coverage, and absence of LC/trade-finance capacity make them unsuitable for high-value bulk grain vessel brokerage. Recommended architecture: a Schedule I bank as treasury anchor, with a FINTRAC-registered MSB or bank-provided virtual IBAN rail used only for small foreign receipts and client collections.
