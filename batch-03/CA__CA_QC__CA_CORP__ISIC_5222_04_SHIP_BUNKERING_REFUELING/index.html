Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; CRA Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/), Revenu Québec Business Registration (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA for GST/HST purposes, along with a separate Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and QST registration from Revenu Québec. For marine fuel bunkering, the entity should complete CRA account creation through the Business Registration Online portal and Revenu Québec's Registraire des entreprises for QST and payroll deduction accounts, providing statutory proof of foreign incorporation, Quebec place of business, and expected Canadian revenue.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election in Canada; GST/HST and QST joint election for closely related corporations may apply under Excise Tax Act section 273 and Quebec Taxation Act equivalent for inter-corporate supply purposes; non-resident corporations may elect to file a separate GST/HST return for a Quebec permanent establishment through CRA Form GST494.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies for both GST/HST registration under Excise Tax Act and QST registration under Quebec Taxation Act; registration becomes mandatory once a foreign entity carries on commercial activity in Canada or Quebec and exceeds the CAD 30,000 threshold in a single calendar quarter or over twelve consecutive months; voluntary registration is available below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act to supplies of marine fuel, lubricants, and bunkering services made to a non-resident or to a person operating a ship used exclusively in international commercial transportation or international marine commerce, provided the fuel is delivered for consumption in or removal from Canada in connection with such international voyages. The supply must be invoiced as a zero-rated export in the GST/HST and QST returns, and the supplier must hold documentation demonstrating that the buyer is a non-resident or an international carrier and that the goods were physically sold for use outside Canada or in international shipping.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration or approved electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or duly signed ship manifest showing foreign port of destination
  • Bunker Delivery Receipt (BDR) generated by bunkering supplier and countersigned by vessel master or chief engineer
  • Inland or marine terminal delivery confirmation showing actual transfer of fuel or lubricants into the vessel
  • Final tax invoice and proof of price, volume, fuel grade, ISO 8217 specifications, and vessel IMO number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT wire / Standby LC settlement proof

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose a nationwide real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. CRA requires GST/HST returns and informational filings to be transmitted electronically in many cases; Revenu Québec mandates electronic billing for suppliers to Quebec public bodies through PEPPOL-based e-invoicing infrastructure and requires transmission of data required for QST returns through its online portal. Marine bunkering operators must preserve electronic invoices indefinitely and issue compliant GST/HST and QST tax invoices in French or English, including registration numbers.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated export claims within the statutory limitation periods if documentation is incomplete or fails to establish the international character of the bunkering supply. Missed documentation requirements expose the entity to full GST/HST and QST clawback at the combined rate, plus arrears interest. Penalties under Excise Tax Act include a 5% penalty plus 1% per month for unreported or misstated zero-rated amounts, up to a maximum of 10% of the understated tax; on gross negligence or wilful evasion, penalties may reach 200% of the tax evaded and fines up to CAD 50,000 for summary conviction, or CAD 500,000 on indictment, with imprisonment for wider criminal tax evasion offences. Revenu Québec imposes parallel QST penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act from 5% to 25% plus interest, increased for recidivism.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current account and capital account transactions under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; no Government of Canada exchange permit or prior approval is required for profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or loan repayments. Outbound dividends from a Quebec business corporation are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) (25% statutory, generally reduced by treaty), and distributions must satisfy the solvency test under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1). The Investment Canada Act may require a notification or review filing for direct foreign investment above applicable WTO-asset thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Bunker fuel sales proceeds may be held indefinitely in USD or other foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks or abroad. All electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; exporters holding foreign property above CAD 100,000 must file Form T1135 annually.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of banks and foreign exchange dealers is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration agency, as the Canadian dollar is fully convertible and the capital account is liberalized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-Low. Electronic-money institutions and payment-fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, PayPal) operate in Canada as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC and are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act licensing framework effective 2024. Such platforms are legally usable for small-value operational disbursements (crew payroll, routine supplier payments under CAD 50,000) but are structurally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail for ISIC 5222 bunkering because they do not provide documentary letters of credit, standby LCs, bank guarantees, or ISDA-based FX forward lines, and most apply internal sectoral restrictions on marine fuel suppliers. A full-service Schedule I bank relationship remains mandatory.
