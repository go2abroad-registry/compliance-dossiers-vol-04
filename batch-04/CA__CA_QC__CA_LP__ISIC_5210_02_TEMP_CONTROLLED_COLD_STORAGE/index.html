Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement. The limited partnership must have at least one general partner and one limited partner; capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement and credited to each partner's capital account. Non-resident ownership is permitted, but the general partner must maintain a Québec address for service, and the partnership may be subject to Québec taxation based on its establishment in Québec.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Federal Fiscal Portal: Canada.ca Business Registration Online; for Quebec, Revenu Québec, Portal: revenuquebec.ca

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must register for a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. For Quebec sales tax (QST), the partnership must register with Revenu Québec under the Quebec Business Number (NEQ) and obtain a QST registration number. The foreign general partner or designated Canadian representative must provide the LP agreement notarized/certified, proof of foreign registration, Quebec address for service, and identification of all partners for beneficial ownership. CRA BN and Revenu Québec QST registrations are mandatory before invoicing Canadian counterparties; specific accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) are added to the BN.

Tax ID type & application path: For Canadian federal tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a separate taxpayer; each partner is allocated income/loss under the partnership agreement. However, the LP itself is required to file an information return (T5013) and to register for GST/HST/QST in respect of its Canadian commercial activities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

For Canadian federal tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a separate taxpayer; each partner is allocated income/loss under the partnership agreement. However, the LP itself is required to file an information return (T5013) and to register for GST/HST/QST in respect of its Canadian commercial activities. The province of Quebec treats the LP as a pass-through for income tax, but imposes QST at the partnership level on taxable supplies made in Quebec; the LP is a registrant for QST purposes. The permanent establishment and territorial source rules apply to non-resident partners subject to Part XIV tax on Canadian-source business income, and withholding under Section 116 for dispositions of Canadian taxable property.

Available election forms

Partnership cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation under the Income Tax Act; however, for United States check-the-box purposes, the LP is a per se corporation if it is a Canadian entity organized as a limited partnership? Actually, Canadian LPs are generally not per se corporations for U.S. check-the-box; they can elect to be taxed as a corporation or partnership for U.S. purposes. Under Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (sections 12.8, 18.15, 18.2, 20.1), an eligible partnership can make a fiscal year-end election and a designated member election. Revenu Québec permits election for QST rebate on eligible capital property and joint elections under the Quebec Taxation Act mirroring federal provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Federal GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies worldwide); Quebec QST: CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies made in Quebec); a non-resident partnership with a Canadian permanent establishment is required to register for GST/HST regardless of the threshold; for QST, a non-resident carrying on business in Quebec must register regardless of threshold if it makes taxable supplies in Quebec

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Taxable supplies of goods and services made by the LP qualify for GST/HST zero-rating when the goods are exported directly by the supplier or delivered to a recipient outside Canada, and for QST when goods are delivered outside Quebec/Canada, provided proof of export is retained. Cold storage warehouse services supplied to a non-resident recipient in respect of exported goods may be zero-rated where the service is performed on goods that are for export and the recipient is a non-resident. Interjurisdictional commercial cold storage services for perishable pharmaceuticals intended for export are eligible for zero-rating if the services are rendered directly in respect of exported goods. The LP must obtain documentary evidence of export within prescribed time limits; if title passes in Canada, domestic GST/HST applies unless the goods are exported under a continuous journey with proper customs documentation.

  • Canada Customs Invoice (Form CI1), Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CADEX/CAED electronic export reporting
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with supplier BN/GST/HST registration number and zero-rating indication
  • Proof of payment and Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange contract or bank SWIFT confirmation
  • Signed written contract between the LP and the non-resident recipient
  • Packing list and transportation documents (truck/rail/ocean/air waybills)
  • CBSA-approved warehouse movement/export records for goods exiting via bonded or licensed cold storage facilities

Canada does not mandate a national continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance system; the CRA accepts XML/Electronic Data Interchange (EDI), Peppol interoperability, and PDF invoices. However, all GST/HST registrants must issue sales invoices containing prescribed information (vendor BN, date, description, tax rates, and amounts). Revenu Québec mandates that QST registrants issue prescribed invoices; as of 2023-2025, Quebec is gradually implementing mandatory electronic invoicing for B2B transactions in Quebec, with a phased mandate and future real-time reporting obligations under the Quebec government's e-invoicing roadmap (PEPPOL).

If the CRA or Revenu Québec audits and finds that the LP claimed zero-rating on goods/services not substantiated as exported, the LP is liable for uncollected GST/HST/QST, interest, and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, a 5% penalty plus interest applies to net tax deficiencies; Revenu Québec may impose a 15% penalty on a QST rebate/refund claim that is excessive, plus interest at the prescribed rate. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may constitute tax evasion under Section 239 of the Excise Tax Act / Sections 62 and 63 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act, leading to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. The CRA can deny input tax credits relating to the exported goods and reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (or ten years for misrepresentation).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no exchange controls, no capital-account restrictions and no requirement for prior central-bank approval on current or capital transfers. Trade invoicing, service remittances, partner capital calls, interest, dividends and profit distributions may flow freely into and out of Canada. The sole legal friction for profit remittance from a Quebec LP is tax-driven: non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source allocations or distributions under the Canadian Income Tax Act, and Revenu Québec may impose provincial withholding. The remitting bank will require tax identification, TP106/NR4 documentation, and a completed certificate of compliance where cross-border dividend-equivalent or partnership income is being paid.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any export proceeds surrender timeline, mandatory conversion percentage, or repatriation requirement. Export proceeds from cold-storage and logistics services may be held in foreign currency in Canadian banks or remitted in the invoiced currency. However, any physical or monetary instrument movement exceeding CAD 10,000 crossing the Canadian border must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency and FINTRAC; the underlying commercial contracts, GL001 export declarations and customs documentation must be maintained for statutory retention periods.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising Canadian-chartered banks and FINTRAC administering AML/ATF and cross-border transaction reporting. No separate foreign exchange control authority is vested with permitting powers in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. An offshore digital electronic money institution (EMI) may not act as a primary domicile for the settlement account of a Quebec LP unless it is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, where it provides retail payment services in Canada, holds authorization from the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot send CAD via Payments Canada clearing rails, have no access to CDIC-insured settlement, and will not be accepted as the main treasury counterparty by tier-1 supply chain partners. Viability improves only if the EMI provides a prepaid or pass-through card solely to a licensed Canadian schedule-I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound partner capital contributions should be made by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the LP's capital escrow or operational account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The remittance must include the payment purpose clearly identified in field 70 as 'PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION UNDER LP AGREEMENT DATED YYYY-MM-DD' and must reference the specific capital call number. Domestic and cross-border wire settlement occurs through Payments Canada Lynx for immediate CAD clearing or through correspondent USD settlement networks for foreign currency. If the capital amount exceeds CAD 10,000 in cash or monetary instruments, a FINTRAC cross-border currency report is triggered. No foreign-exchange approval certificate or Canadian Ministry of Finance authorization is required for capital injection. Supporting records must include the executed capital call, subscription/contribution schedule, and bank confirmation statement.Outbound trade and operating disbursements for the cold-storage warehouse are cleared using Payments Canada Lynx and EFT/ACH rails for domestic CAD obligations, while cross-border supplier payments in USD, EUR, GBP or CHF are routed through SWIFT MT103 wires with ISO 20022 structured remittance data. Standard commercial open-account payment is acceptable under low-risk supply chain terms; where suppliers require smoothing of default risk, issuance of commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) under UCP 600 with confirmed SWIFT MT700 series is permissible for capital-equipment purchases such as refrigeration compressors or pharmaceutical cold-chain vehicles. Canada does not require submission of import permits for most temperature-controlled logistics goods; however, import controls under Health Canada and cGMP documentation may be required for pharma-adjacent materials. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the limited partnership level (fiscally transparent); corporate partners are subject to a combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) headline corporate rate of 26.5% on allocated taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident person; ordinary profit allocations by a limited partnership are not treated as dividends but are taxable to the partner under Part I/Part XIV of the ITA, subject to treaty allocation and TRC.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the partnership fiscal period end; for a calendar-year LP, the federal T5013 and Quebec equivalent are due by June 30 of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 — when the total annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a non-resident related person exceeds CA$1 million, the LP must prepare, keep, and provide contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247(4), including a master file, local file, risk assessment, and functional analysis.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not a designated ESR jurisdiction; however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s. 245 ITA and interprets economic substance as a critical factor. A Quebec limited partnership operating in cold storage must maintain physical premises, employees, management, and real commercial activity; a shell that exists solely for treaty/conduit access will be reassessed and penalised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) carries a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and incarceration up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax; Quebec Revenu imposes parallel administrative and criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/QST under ITA s. 323 and the Quebec Taxation Act; a conviction triggers travel bans, asset seizures, and potential extradition for deceitful tax conduct.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterizes the arrangement and imposes tax, interest, and a 25% gross-negligence penalty; Revenu Quebec may deregister the LP and deny refunds; the arrangement can be prosecuted as a sham for tax evasion if deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors, promoters, and designated partners are personally assessed for the tax that would otherwise have been collectible; a criminal conviction can result in prohibition from acting as a director, travel bans, and civil restitution for unpaid taxes.
AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for non-compliance with client identification, record-keeping, and reporting; criminal laundering charges carry fines of up to $2,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are individually liable for failure to file FINTRAC reports and can be imprisoned up to 5 years for laundering funds; travel restrictions, a seven-year border inquiry, and mandatory reporting of the Canadian criminal record apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts taxable income under ITA s. 247(2) and imposes a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) on the net adjustment, plus interest and potential gross-negligence penalties; failure to file the T106 return attracts $100/day penalties up to $2,500.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the resulting Canadian and Quebec taxes and penalties; sustained evasion exposure may trigger CRA criminal referral, travel bans, and civil claims by aggrieved creditors.
