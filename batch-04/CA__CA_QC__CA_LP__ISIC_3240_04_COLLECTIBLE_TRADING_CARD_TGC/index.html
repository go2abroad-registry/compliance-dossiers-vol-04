Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; CRA portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity wishing to operate as a limited partnership in Quebec must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via online registration or Form RC1. The same BN is used for GST/HST account registration if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. Separately, the partnership must register with Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) using Form LM-1-V, providing the partnership agreement, proof of foreign address, and details of a Canadian resident representative or agent. Non-resident entities may use a tax representative and secure access through CRA's Represent a Client service. For income tax purposes, the limited partnership is not a taxpayer; partners report their shares, and the partnership files an information return (T5013).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity wishing to operate as a limited partnership in Quebec must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via online registration or Form RC1. The same BN is used for GST/HST account registration if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. Non-resident entities may use a tax representative and secure access through CRA's Represent a Client service. For income tax purposes, the limited partnership is not a taxpayer; partners report their shares, and the partnership files an information return (T5013).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not utilize check-the-box elections. A partnership may elect a non-calendar fiscal year end under subsection 249.1(7) of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST, a foreign partnership may elect to file under the simplified method or choose a fiscal year matching its reporting period. For QST purposes, Revenu Québec allows elections regarding method of accounting, including the accelerated refund method for certain businesses, and may allow a non-resident partnership to elect to provide security in lieu of a resident agent. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 12.2, 18.2, and 18.3 may apply to cross-border arrangements, but no entity classification election is available; the LP remains fiscally transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD annual global taxable supplies for both GST/HST and QST; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded worldwide, with Calendrical and consecutive-quarter measurement

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods supplied by way of sale where the goods are exported directly from Canada and title passes to a non-resident purchaser. Tangible personal property, such as collectible trading cards, qualifies for 0% GST/HST and QST when shipped to a destination outside Canada pursuant to a genuine export sale. Services provided to non-residents are zero-rated if the service is performed primarily outside Canada and the recipient is not resident in Canada. The supplier must retain full documentary evidence proving export, payment, and foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Report (Form E15) or Integrated Entry Declaration
  • Commercial invoice with explicit export terms and destination
  • Packing slip and shipping manifest
  • Proof of foreign delivery (signed delivery receipt, freight forwarding confirmation, or carrier tracking record)
  • Foreign port or customs entry clearance evidence where available

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API invoicing regime exists in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST returns must generally be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) for all registrants; Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns for businesses with remuneration above a prescribed threshold (CAD 50,000). No continuous transaction controls or government-approved e-invoice clearance platform applies. Electronic invoices must be retained and made available to the CRA and Revenu Québec upon audit.

The Canada Revenue Agency and Revenu Québec may undertake deep verification of export zero-rating claims. Failure to satisfy documentary requirements within 30 days of a formal request may result in reassessment of tax at the full combined rate, interest, and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, knowingly making false statements can attract a gross negligence penalty of 25% of the tax amount, plus fines up to CAD 10,000 and possible imprisonment; Revenu Québec imposes comparable penalties under the Taxation Act and may refuse or claw back refunds. Retrospective reassessments are possible for the full statutory limitation period (normally four years, extended to seven years for negligence or fraud), with immediate reversal of any refund previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely convertible, independently floating currency. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally unrestricted. Profit remittances, partnership distributions, and capital repatriation by non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership are lawful and do not require central bank approval. Payments to non-residents are subject only to standard Canadian withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act and, where applicable, treaty-based reductions, as well as FATCA/CRS reporting and FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting for transactions equal to or above CAD 10,000. There are no foreign exchange licensing requirements for ordinary trade payments. The strategic implication for a collectible trading card publishing entity is that CAD/USD conversion may be executed freely through Schedule I banks and regulated Canadian dealers; however, underlying commercial documentation must be maintained to evidence genuine business purpose and to resist any suspicion of money laundering or sanctions evasion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory foreign exchange conversion timeline, and no forced repatriation percentage for export receipts. A Quebec LP may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts maintained at a Canadian chartered bank, or convert such proceeds at commercial rates in the spot or forward market. The only operative obligations are prudential reporting and anti-money-laundering requirements: banks report international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and the LP must preserve underlying invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and counterparty due diligence records for all export sales. For intercompany or related-party export transactions, transfer pricing documentation under the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Canadian sections 247 of the Income Tax Act should be maintained to support arm’s length pricing of collectible trading card sales to affiliated distribution entities.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority, acting alongside the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); for foreign exchange reporting, FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency are the relevant control and enforcement authorities; Quebec-specific financial services supervision is conducted by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for market conduct and registration matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canadian non-bank fintech platforms may operate only as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or Restricted MSBs registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, may require AMF authorization for certain payment activities. Offshore digital e-money institutions regulated under foreign e-money licences do not qualify as Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide Canadian domestic clearing in CAD; their utility is limited to foreign-currency collection corridors such as USD or EUR receivables. Relying on an offshore EMI as a primary liquidity rail would create unacceptable regulatory risk under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, as the Quebec LP would be exposed to unlicensed deposit-taking and incomplete FINTRAC reporting. Accordingly, the optimum architecture is to maintain all Canadian CAD collections, capital escrow, and disbursements through a Schedule I bank, using licensed foreign EMIs only for ancillary inbound sales payments from e-commerce marketplaces, with daily or weekly sweeps into the Canadian operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner capital contributions must be routed in the name of the contributing partner from the partner’s own verified bank account directly to the Quebec LP’s operating or capital escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remittance must be a clean SWIFT MT103 message that states the LP’s full legal name, account number and branch transit, the Bank of Canada institution number, and a concise remittance narrative such as 'Capital contribution to [CA_LP] – ISIC 3240 collectible trading card operation'. Canada does not use fixed statutory foreign exchange purpose codes; however, the payment must pass internal screening and be capable of being classified as a direct investment or capital contribution under banking forms. Amounts equal to or above CAD 10,000 are reportable by the receiving bank to FINTRAC, so the LP must be prepared to provide the bank with the underlying partnership agreement, a capital schedule signed by the general partner, and evidence of the non-resident partner’s identity and source of funds. Funds routed from offshore fintech accounts, interposed shell entities, or third-party payment processors will create automatic enhanced due diligence and possible account rejection.For regular trade payments to overseas suppliers of security holographic foil, cold-foil printing substrates, laminates, inks, dies, and finishing services, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 payment executed from the LP’s Canadian USD or CAD account to the supplier’s bank account. For larger or higher-risk purchase orders, a commercial letter of credit (sight or term LC) addressed to the supplier’s bank is appropriate, with documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, quality certificate, and beneficiary certificate. For domestic Canadian clearing, high-value payments should be made through Lynx (previously Large Value Transfer System); for USD disbursements to US vendors, banks may use Fedwire or CHIPS through the US correspondent account of the Canadian financial institution. All outward payments must be accompanied by the underlying supplier contract or pro-forma invoice, and the bank should be advised of the expected payment date and amount in advance to avoid false positive sanctions screening and OFAC compliance friction.
