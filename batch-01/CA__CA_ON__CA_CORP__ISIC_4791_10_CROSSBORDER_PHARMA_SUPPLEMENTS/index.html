Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administering GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act and corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act; registration portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; customs administration through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM client portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Obtain federal or Ontario articles of incorporation (Corporations Canada or Ontario Business Registry). 2) Request a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA BRO online, by phone (1-800-959-5525), or by mailing Form RC1; the BN carries program accounts RT (GST/HST), RP (corporate income tax), and RC (payroll where applicable). 3) A non-resident corporation without a Canadian permanent establishment uses Form RC4607 (Request for Non-Resident Registration) or, for qualifying e-commerce vendors under the July 1, 2021 simplified GST/HST framework, Form RC4530; supporting evidence includes certified incorporation documents, director identification, and home-jurisdiction registration. 4) Ontario corporate income tax is collected by the CRA on behalf of Ontario under the single-administration agreement, so no separate Ontario Ministry of Finance registration is required for income tax; the Ontario HST component is also collected through the CRA RT account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident (ITA s.250(4)(a)) and taxed on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available for foreign-source income. Non-resident corporations pay Part I tax only on Canadian-source business income, certain employment income, and taxable capital gains from taxable Canadian property, plus Part XIV branch tax on net Canadian business earnings of a non-resident branch. Territorial scope: full worldwide taxation for resident corporations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Canadian Business Corporations are taxed as separate legal persons under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada has no check-the-box flow-through regime for corporations. A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident (ITA s.250(4)(a)) and taxed on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available for foreign-source income. Non-resident corporations pay Part I tax only on Canadian-source business income, certain employment income, and taxable capital gains from taxable Canadian property, plus Part XIV branch tax on net Canadian business earnings of a non-resident branch. Territorial scope: full worldwide taxation for resident corporations. No tax-exempt zone or preferential free-zone regime applies in Ontario for e-commerce retail of supplements.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available elections for cross-border corporate structures: (i) ETA s.156 election to treat closely related corporations as a single GST/HST registrant; (ii) ETA s.156.1 joint election for GST/HST filing among related registrants; (iii) ITA s.219 branch-tax treaty election (reduction of Part XIV transmission tax); (iv) ITA s.85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property; (v) ITA s.216/216.1 non-resident elections (rent/royalty and Canadian resource property - generally not applicable to an Ontario-resident supplement retailer). Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.18.2 and s.18.3, enacted by Bill C-59, applicable July 1, 2024) operate as mandatory disallowances and deemed-imputation rules and do not offer any elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (approx. USD 22,000) - small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148. A supplier making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity must register once worldwide taxable consideration exceeds CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or over the prior four quarters; non-resident digital and e-commerce sellers must register once Canadian-source remote-supply or fulfillment-warehouse revenues exceed CAD 30,000. Registration is voluntary below the threshold but is generally advisable for exporters seeking input tax credit (ITC) recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods: ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 zero-rates a supply of dietary supplements and nutraceuticals at 0% where the supplier (a) ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, (b) transfers ownership to a non-resident before export, or (c) delivers the goods to a common carrier or shipping agent for export, with export occurring as soon as reasonable in the circumstances (CRA practice: documentary proof of export generally within 60 days). Offshore services: ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST, where the service relates neither to Canadian real property nor to goods located in Canada at the time of supply. Condition: the supplier must retain satisfactory evidence of export (export declaration, bill of lading or courier tracking, customs-broker confirmation, foreign customs entry). Domestic note: sales to Ontario consumers of dietary supplements remain 13% HST taxable, except for natural health products qualifying for the zero-rated categories (vitamins, minerals, and certain herbal remedies with DIN or NPN) introduced by the 2022 Budget Implementation Act and effective October 1, 2025; that domestic relief is separate from and does not alter the export zero-rating.

  • CBSA/CARM export declaration (B13A or equivalent eExport and cargo mode reporting) evidencing exit from Canada
  • Commercial invoice / Canadian customs invoice with HS classification, Incoterms, value, and export destination
  • Bill of lading (ocean or air), waybill, or courier manifest with external carrier tracking number
  • Foreign customs entry or delivery confirmation, or carrier certificate proving arrival at destination outside Canada
  • Payment evidence supporting the cross-border transaction (e.g., bank inward remittance certificate or wire statement; Canada imposes no statutory inward remittance certificate requirement for export zero-rating, but payment evidence substantiates commercial reality)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API-stamping, or digital-invoice validation system. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE), and corporations with gross revenue above CAD 1 million must e-file T2 corporate returns, but invoice data flows have no government pre-authorization or digital stamping. Books and records must be retained for six years (ETA s.286). Any structured e-invoice format (EDI, PEPPOL, PDF/A) is acceptable provided it is legible and supports CRA audit reconstruction.

CRA audit may deny export zero-rating where export evidence is incomplete; the resulting assessment (ETA s.296) claws back uncollected 13% HST with compound prescribed-rate interest from the date the tax would have been remittable. Penalties: (i) ETA s.280 false statements - CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 or 25% of the tax involved; (ii) ETA s.285.1 gross negligence - 50% of the net tax understated; (iii) ETA s.285 evasion - up to 200% of the tax evaded; (iv) prosecution under ETA s.327 and ITA s.239 - fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to five years; (v) ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated Part I income. Reassessment windows: four years normally, extended to seven years for negligence or misrepresentation (ITA s.152(4)); refunds and input tax credits associated with false zero-rating are recoverable as debts due to the Crown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility for Canadian-dollar and foreign-currency transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no restrictions on maintaining multi-currency operating accounts (CAD, USD, EUR, GBP) at OSFI-regulated banks. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the banking level but triggers Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25%; treaty-reduced to 5-15%, e.g., under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty for qualifying dividends, interest and royalties) and related CRS/FATCA reporting. Inbound equity injections require only clear SWIFT purpose coding and FINTRAC EFT reporting for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000; no BOC or OSFI pre-approval is required. De novo establishment of a cross-border e-commerce operation by a WTO-investor parent is generally not subject to Investment Canada Act net-benefit review unless thresholds are triggered or the business is in a culturally sensitive or national-security-sensitive sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline for nutraceutical export receipts; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in OSFI-regulated accounts. The only binding requirements are: (i) FINTRAC large-transaction and EFT reporting for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000; (ii) accurate customs export declarations and controlled-goods classification under the Customs Act and the Export and Import Permits Act, including Health Canada GMP/health-product attestations where applicable; and (iii) inclusion of foreign-source revenue in the Canadian exporter's income tax filings. The statutory timeline for surrender is therefore N/A, and export receivables may be held in original currency under IFRS 9 without any forced conversion date.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as monetary authority and lender of last resort; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF and cross-border electronic-funds-transfer surveillance authority; Payments Canada as operator of the Lynx and ACSS clearing and settlement systems; Department of Finance Canada and Global Affairs Canada as sanction administration authorities. Canada has no foreign-exchange control authority analogous to SAFE; the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and the capital account is fully liberalized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary collection and disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury or capital-escrow provider. Legal constraints: any offshore EMI (EU/UK e-money institution) that directs money-service activity to persons in Canada must register as a foreign money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Where the Canadian nexus is Ontario, the EMI must also obtain an Ontario MSB licence from the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA) under the Money Service Business Act, 2019. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot open CAD clearing accounts, cannot access Payments Canada rails (Lynx/ACSS) without a domestic bank sponsor, and cannot perform Part XIII withholding or maintain a verifiable escrow chain for Canadian corporate capital. Use EMIs (Wise, Convera, Airwallex or similar) only for low-value supplier payouts and card-acquiring settlement; route statutory equity, escrow, tax remittances, and material liquidity through Schedule I banks.
