Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal administrator of the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act, accessible via Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance assesses Ontario corporate income tax, but collection is administered by CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

The non-resident Limited Partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), opened with a GST/HST program account (RT) and, if needed, importer/exporter (RM/RD) accounts; registration can be completed online through Business Registration Online or by courier/fax to the CRA Non-Resident Unit; the LP must file an annual T5013 partnership information return and report each partner's allocable share regardless of partner residency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership itself is not liable for federal/Ontario income tax; income, gains, deductions and losses flow through to partners; for GST/HST purposes the LP is a 'person' and registrant, while each partner remains jointly and severally liable for the LP's net tax; a non-resident partner is deemed under ITA s. 253 to carry on business in Canada through the LP, triggering Canadian filing and Part I tax on the allocated share.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election; status is determined by statute and partnership law. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election to contribute property to the LP on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) ETA s. 156 nil-consideration election for supplies among closely related registrants; (iii) ETA s. 229 Quick Method of accounting for GST/HST; (iv) ITA s. 249.1 alternative business-period election for Canadian-resident partners. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.5 operate non-electively.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies measured over the current and preceding three consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148); mandatory GST/HST registration once exceeded for supplies made in Canada; registration is optional below threshold; exports and qualifying zero-rated supplies count for threshold computation but are taxable at 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property sold and delivered by the Ontario LP to a foreign destination is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier; services performed entirely outside Canada or supplied to non-residents in connection with export logistics are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 5–7; the LP must maintain contemporaneous proof of export and be a GST/HST registrant to claim input tax credits and generate refundable net tax positions on export-only activities.

  • Supplier-generated commercial invoice with Incoterms, value, currency and HS classification
  • Carrier-issued bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Customs export declaration data, including CBSA export reporting or US AES filings transmitted by the logistics provider
  • Carrier tracking event history demonstrating delivery at the foreign destination
  • Inward remittance and foreign settlement records, including COD logistics remittance reports and Airwallex or local remittance proof of payment

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory API pre-clearance exists in Canada. Mandatory electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE/XML) applies to registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1,500,000. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL-based Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Framework (pilot 2024–2025); CRA enforces compliance through audit trails, documentary proof, and electronic records rather than transactional fiscal authorization.

CRA will deny zero-rating where proof of export is deficient, reassessing 13% HST with arrears interest; knowingly false or omitted information triggers the ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax); a false refund claim triggers a 50% penalty under ETA s. 288.01; all LP partners are jointly and severally liable under ETA s. 272.1; export misstatements may also attract ITA s. 163(2) (50% gross-negligence penalty) and extended reassessment periods. The 180-day repatriation figure is a cross-border documentary benchmark, not a Canadian statutory deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, licensing requirements, or Central Bank approval mechanisms for cross-border capital movements. Profit remittances and capital repatriation by non-resident limited partners are freely permitted, subject only to corporate income tax withholding under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and, where applicable, reduced by treaty under the Canada-US Tax Convention or other income tax treaties. No forced conversion or repatriation deferral exists for operating accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in any Canadian chartered bank account or held abroad at the election of the Limited Partnership, subject only to general anti-money laundering reporting, e.g., FINTRAC large virtual currency transaction reports or cross-border currency reporting for cash movements over CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as monetary authority and lender of last resort. No standalone foreign exchange administration agency exists; foreign exchange and financial intelligence oversight are coordinated through FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions), and the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High viability with strict framing. Airwallex and similar digital cross-border EMI/FI platforms are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC in Canada, not as chartered banks. Such platforms are legally permissible for cross-border collections, vendor disbursements, and foreign exchange settlement; however, they cannot hold client funds as a Canadian chartered bank and must maintain funds in trust/ledger accounts with a Schedule I bank. The Ontario Limited Partnership may use such platforms for operational liquidity and COD remittance, but the primary capital escrow account must remain at a domestic, OSFI-regulated bank to satisfy substantive capital preservation and AML/KYC expectations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident Limited Partners located in emerging-market or FATF-monitored jurisdictions, with opaque custody of funds.

Defense evidence: Provide certified Limited Partnership Agreement, Ontario registration certificate and annual returns, complete beneficial ownership schedule up to natural persons, tax residence certificates for each non-resident partner, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth supporting documentation (bank references, audited financial statements), notarized resolutions, Finish proof of ongoing commercial relationship, and a comprehensive Canadian tax compliance opinion confirming passive-partner nature.
Limited partnership structure with layered capital contributions and no single clear Executive officer, triggering ineffective beneficial ownership transparency concerns.

Root cause: Limited partnership structure with layered capital contributions and no single clear Executive officer, triggering ineffective beneficial ownership transparency concerns.

Defense evidence: Deliver an organizational chart identifying the General Partner, all Limited Partners, ultimate individual owners, and entity registration numbers. Attach signed declarations confirming no bearer shares, nominee arrangements, or undisclosed trusts. Authorize the bank to register the account under the LP's CRA Business Number and provide sanctioned confirmation clearance from UN, EU, OFAC, and FINTRAC lists.
High-volume cash-on-delivery (COD) retail flows creating potential risk for layering or unlicensed money transmission from logistics remittance patterns.

Root cause: High-volume cash-on-delivery (COD) retail flows creating potential risk for layering or unlicensed money transmission from logistics remittance patterns.

Defense evidence: Submit executed logistics agreements, COD reconciliation procedures, daily turnover reports in the target market, refund and exception-handling policy, KYC/AML policies for end-customer transaction limits, and monitored settlement dashboards. Use of foreign non-bank payment intermediaries or fintech EMIs as source or destination accounts for capital injection.

Root cause: Use of foreign non-bank payment intermediaries or fintech EMIs as source or destination accounts for capital injection.

Defense evidence: Maintain full trail of intermediary settlement: platform license/MSB registration certificate, independent audit report of platform custody accounts, direct PDD (Payment Detail Document) statements linking each transaction to the underlying sales invoice, and sworn declarations that no third-party pass-through or commingled funds are involved. Where feasible, begin through a domestic bank and scale fintech rails only after bank relationship is established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident Limited Partners must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's home bank to the LP's Tier 1 Canadian operational account. Wire narrative must clearly designate 'Limited Partnership Capital Contribution — CA_Ontario_LP — [Unit No.] / [Date]'. Canadian banks do not mandate a formal statutory purpose code, but the wire should indicate an actual business purpose, e.g., 'Capital contribution for working capital financing of ISIC 4791 mail-order retail operations', and reference the LP's Ontario registration number and Partner name. For any single contribution exceeding CAD 10,000 in cash, or CAD 10,000 in virtual currency, a FINTRAC report is triggered; in practice all interbank wires are automatically reported through payment system data. For non-resident contributions, retain a duly executed subscription agreement, schedule of contributions, and a statutory declaration confirming source of funds; if the investor is a company, append corporate registry extract and beneficial ownership declaration.Trade-related disbursements for COD settlement logistics, vendor payments, and refunds should be executed via (a) CAD SWIFT MT103 for international beneficiary transfers, (b) domestic fund transfers via Canada's Lynx high-value payment system or the Retail Batch Settlement System for CAD-running accounts, and (c) same-day interbank EFT for Canadian counterparties. For large or recurring merchandise imports, a commercial letter of credit can be issued by the Canadian bank using SWIFT MT700, with settlement on sight tenor to protect against emerging-market counterparty risk. For cross-border logistics payment at COD, banks permit outward SWIFT wires to local aggregators or licensed logistics remitters using invoice descriptions that identify the merchant order; the LP must maintain a corresponding payable/receivable sub-ledger to satisfy AML audits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total aggregate value of specified non-arm's length transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year, triggering Form T106 and mandatory transfer-pricing documentation); for multinational groups with Canadian gross revenue exceeding CAD 50,000,000, CRA expects a Master File and Local File

Economic substance scope: Canada has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to offshore centers; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and OECD BEPS substance principles apply. A foreign-owned Canadian LP must maintain real economic presence (employees, office, business activities) in Canada; otherwise CRA may disregard the entity or attribute a permanent establishment to the foreign owner

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax amount sought to be evaded under ITA s. 238/239; fines of up to CAD 200,000 for persons and CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may also reassess beyond the normal limitation period in case of misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and managing partners can be personally assessed for the 50% penalty and tax shortfall; civil liability for restitution; restrictions on cross-border travel; potential seizure of personal assets
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the tax benefit; gross-negligence penalty up to 50% of the avoided tax (ITA s. 163.2); transfer-pricing penalties of 10% on the net adjustment amount for each transaction; penalties for failure to file Form T106 up to CAD 2,500 plus daily surcharges

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors of the corporate general partner may be held joint and severally liable for GAAR reassessments and transfer-pricing penalties; non-tax sanctions include civil restitution and asset freezing orders
AML / Proceeds of Crime (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; criminal sanctions up to CAD 2,000,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for officers; FINTRAC may order freezing of assets; prohibition on continuing business operations

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers responsible for AML compliance can be personally fined; travel bans under special investigative measures; personal criminal liability for knowingly engaging in or failing to report suspicious transactions; civil liability for damage caused to counterparties
