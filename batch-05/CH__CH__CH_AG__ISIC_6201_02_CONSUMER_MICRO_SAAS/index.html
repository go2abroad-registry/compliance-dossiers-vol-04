Conditional acceptance. Swiss corporate law (Art. 637 CO) requires that the registered office be the place from which the company is actually managed and administered. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Federal Tax Administration FTA); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax authorities administer direct cantonal and communal corporate taxes.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Swiss AG, the cantonal commercial register office (Handelsregisteramt) assigns a Swiss Business Identification Number (UID), which serves as the statutory company identifier. VAT registration with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration is required if taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000; the VAT UID is obtained by submitting the VAT registration form (Form 500) through the ESTV e-portal. Foreign entities not resident in Switzerland that have no commercial register entry must file Form 500, appoint a Swiss VAT representative (Art. 66 VAT Act) if not using the simplified non-established supplier procedure, and obtain a Swiss VAT UID for supplies made in Switzerland. A non-resident foreign corporation with a Swiss permanent establishment must also register with the cantonal tax authority for income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. A Swiss AG is mandatorily taxed at entity level under Swiss federal and cantonal law. Available statutory elections include: (i) profit and loss transfer agreement with a Swiss resident group company (tax grouping / Organschaft) for corporate income tax, requiring 90% direct or indirect participation and a five-year minimum term; (ii) Swiss VAT grouping (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) for closely held Swiss entities; and (iii) declaration of Swiss capital contribution reserves by the AG to repatriate reserves tax-free. Hybrid mismatch rules follow BEPS Action 2 under Swiss tax law; no elective pass-through classification is available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the Federal Act on Currency and Means of Payment and the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank. No exchange controls, no repatriation restrictions, no licensing requirements for profit remittance or capital outflows. Dividend remittances are subject only to statutory Swiss withholding tax at 35% (reduced by applicable double tax treaties to typically 5%–15% upon proper substance documentation). A Swiss AG conducting consumer micro-SaaS may freely invoice global customers and retain/convert export receipts in any convertible currency. The only compliance duties arise under tax law, AML/KYC obligations imposed by FINMA-regulated banks, and SNB statistical reporting for payments exceeding CHF 10,000 or CHF 50,000 depending on instrument type.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation deadline for export receivables. A Swiss exporter may hold foreign currency indefinitely in a Swiss or offshore account, provided the balance is disclosed for Swiss tax (accounting) purposes and, if applicable, for SNB reportable payment statistics. For VAT and customs purposes, no FX conversion obligation arises; the tax authorities apply monthly Swiss Franc reference rates.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium legal viability for the Swiss micro-SaaS treasury, but not as a substitute for a FINMA-licensed bank. Switzerland is not an EEA member, so the PSD2 Single Passport is unavailable. An offshore EMI (e.g. Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) can provide disbursement rails and currency conversion, but holding Swiss client funds or providing banking-type deposit services in Switzerland requires a Swiss banking license or a fintech authorization under Art. 1b Banking Act. For the Swiss AG’s own treasury, an offshore EMI may serve as a payment collection gateway for SaaS subscriptions (e.g. Paddle, Lemon Squeezy), but the primary operating account should remain with a Swiss bank to ensure legal certainty, substance, and FINMA-supervised asset segregation. Non-bank EMIs also face stricter due diligence on inbound founder capital injections due to opaqueness.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Swiss AG minimum capital of CHF 100,000 (immediately 100% paid) or simplified CHF 20,000 with the payroll trust exception, the founder/shareholder transfers the capital via SWIFT MT103 for international currencies or SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for domestic CHF. The remittance should identify the beneficiary as the not-yet-incorporated company or the established AG with the escrow account reference. The bank opens a 'Freigabekonto' (escrow account) in the AG’s name with a restricted instruction until the commercial register confirms incorporation. The MT103 remittance information field (Field 70) should state 'SACHEINLAGE/APPORT EN NATURE/EINZAHLUNG' or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' to avoid misclassification. No central bank approval or official purpose code is required under Swiss law; for statistical balance-of-payments reporting, the bank captures the code 'Capital investment – share capital' if so instructed. The commercial register office requires a bank confirmation that the fully paid-up share capital is at the free disposal of the board before issuing the registration certificate. Source of funds verification is conducted in accordance with the Anti-Money-Laundering Ordinance and the bank’s risk-based due diligence manual.For trade settlements the Swiss AG uses the following clearing rails: 1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for international high-valiue CHF, USD, EUR or other convertible currencies, with beneficiary bank details and purpose of payment (e.g. software license, cloud hosting); 2) SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR-denominated payments to EEA counterparts (Sorbet clears via EACH, with the Swiss AG as participant); 3) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) instant and batch systems for domestic CHF; 4) API-based payment platforms (e.g. Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) for merchant settlement and franchise royalties; 5) Commercial letters of credit are disproportionately rare for SaaS transactions; they apply only if the counterparty requires an underlying goods transaction, while artistic and software licensing is invoiced under service contracts with SWIFT or SEPA rails. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory corporate income tax rate is 8.5% (effective federal burden approximately 7.83% because cantonal taxes are deductible). Combined federal, cantonal and communal headline effective rate in Canton Zug is approximately 14.6%–15.1%, depending on the commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) applies to dividend distributions. Under tax treaties the rate may be reduced, typically to 5% for a foreign corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting capital and to 15% for portfolio holdings; some DTAs provide 0%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF: No fixed statutory CHF threshold obliges a company to file a Local File or Master File with the annual tax return. Documentation must be produced upon request where cross-border related-party transactions are material. A Country-by-Country report is mandatory in Switzerland for any Swiss-resident ultimate parent entity with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations. Swiss anti-abuse and treaty-entitlement rules nevertheless require genuine economic substance: a registered office, physical premises, effective management decisions, personnel, books and records in Switzerland. A letterbox AG is treated as lacking substance, may be denied treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test and may be disregarded or reconstructed as the foreign shareholder's Swiss permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and tax fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: For direct federal tax, an evasion penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus interest; for tax fraud using forged evidence, a prison term of up to three years or a fine. Cantonal tax law imposes analogous fines and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or approve the fraudulent return may be jointly and severally liable; intentional manipulation of corporate separation can pierce the corporate veil, exposing personal assets. Criminal convictions may trigger travel restrictions and imprisonment.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the Swiss company as a permanent establishment of the foreign owner; denial of Swiss tax treaty benefits; deemed distribution of accumulated profits subject to 35% anticipatory tax; added tax and penalty surcharges; potential administrative dissolution.

Director personal liability: If the company is held to be a façade, shareholders and directors can be personally liable for corporate tax and creditor claims. Directors may also face disqualification orders and cantonal fines for false statutory declarations.
AML and money-laundering exposure

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Penal Code, money laundering (Art. 305bis) is punishable by up to three years' imprisonment or a fine; aggravated money laundering (Art. 305ter) carries up to five years' imprisonment plus fines of up to CHF 500,000. Assets are subject to confiscation and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Board members and compliance officers face direct criminal liability, including imprisonment and personal fines. Assets of directors may be seized and international travel bans imposed while the criminal investigation is pending.
Transfer-pricing mispricing and unlawful profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Upward tax assessment based on the arm's-length principle, compensatory interest, reclassification of the price difference as a constructive dividend subject to 35% anticipatory tax, plus penalty surcharges of up to 200% of the additional tax found.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute artificial pricing and do not ensure withholding of the Swiss anticipatory tax may be held personally liable for the withheld-but-unpaid tax. Criminal tax-fraud prosecutions may result in imprisonment and director disqualification.
