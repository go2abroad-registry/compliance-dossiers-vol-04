Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including Business Registration Online, My Business Account, and Represent a Client. The CRA administers the federal Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST), corporate income tax, Business Number (BN), and relevant non-resident tax accounts. Ontario corporate income tax is administered jointly by the CRA through the T2 corporate income tax return. Ontario Ministry of Finance administers certain provincial taxes but does not issue a separate general VAT identification number.

E-government filing portal

1. Determine whether the corporation is incorporated federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, and obtain its certificate of incorporation and registered office information. 2. Apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number using Business Registration Online, Form RC1, telephone registration, or an authorized representative. A non-resident entity may register by telephone, mail, or through an authorized representative if it cannot use the online service. 3. Open the applicable CRA program accounts, including an RT GST/HST account where registration is required or voluntarily elected, an RC payroll account if employees are employed in Canada, and an RP or other account where applicable. 4. Register for GST/HST if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the applicable small-supplier threshold, or register voluntarily where permitted. Non-resident status does not by itself eliminate GST/HST registration obligations. 5. File the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return where the corporation is resident in Canada or otherwise subject to Canadian corporate income tax. 6. For a corporation incorporated outside Canada that carries on business in Canada, assess whether a Canadian corporate tax filing and provincial extra-provincial registration are required. The CRA BN is the principal Canadian tax identifier; Canada does not issue a separate EU-style VAT identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for corporations. A Canadian corporation is generally taxed as a separate legal person. Relevant classifications include Canadian-controlled private corporation, non-Canadian-controlled private corporation, and public corporation, with different corporate tax rates and incentive eligibility. A corporation incorporated outside Canada may be resident in Canada based on common-law central management and control or applicable treaty rules. A non-resident corporation may be taxable on income from a business carried on in Canada, taxable Canadian property, and other specified Canadian-source amounts. Canadian partnership and trust classification rules apply only where the entity is legally structured as a partnership or trust. Hybrid and imported mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not operate as a general entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, measured under the applicable single-quarter and preceding-four-consecutive-quarter tests for a person that is not a public-sector body. The threshold is not a general exemption for non-residents: a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register based on the place-of-supply and carrying-on-business rules. Voluntary registration may be available below the threshold, subject to statutory conditions. A non-resident digital or intangible-property supplier may also be subject to specialized registration rules where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the statutory GST/HST export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or shipment outside Canada and retention of prescribed evidence. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated only if the specific provisions in Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act apply. For independent film copyright distribution and licensing, a copyright or other intangible-property right may qualify for zero-rating where supplied to a non-resident and the statutory place-of-supply, non-resident, and use-or-enjoyment conditions are met. Zero-rating is not automatic merely because the customer pays from outside Canada. The supplier should verify the customer's non-resident status, the contractual territory, the location where the licensed rights are to be used or exploited, and any Canadian use, enjoyment, commercial exploitation, or statutory exception. A licence of rights for exploitation in Canada may be taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate. A zero-rated supply permits recovery of eligible input tax credits, subject to ordinary documentation and allocation rules.

  • For exported tangible goods: CBSA export declaration through CERS where required, commercial invoice, purchase or licensing contract, packing list where relevant, transport document such as bill of lading or airway bill, and records establishing shipment and delivery outside Canada.
  • For pure film copyright, royalty, or intangible-property licensing with no physical goods export: no customs export declaration or bill of lading is ordinarily required. Maintain the executed licence or distribution agreement, customer legal name and address, evidence that the recipient is non-resident, invoice showing the applicable zero-rating basis, territorial grant and permitted-use provisions, payment and banking records, correspondence, and evidence supporting use or exploitation outside Canada.
  • Canada does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate for GST/HST zero-rating or refund claims, and there is no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation certificate requirement. Banking records and payment evidence remain relevant audit support.

Canada has no general real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance, government digital-stamping, or universal business-to-business e-invoicing mandate. A registrant must issue GST/HST documentation containing prescribed information, maintain books and records, and retain electronic records in an accessible and reliable form. CRA electronic filing and payment requirements may apply based on registrant category, reporting period, and statutory rules. Export declarations for reportable goods are generally filed electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), unless an exemption or alternative reporting process applies. Pure copyright licensing and other offshore intangible-property transactions normally do not require a customs export declaration because no goods cross the Canadian border.

Canada has no general mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the CRA may reassess the supplier for GST/HST that should have been collected, deny related input tax credits, impose arrears interest, and apply penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally be 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, subject to statutory rules; other failure-to-file, failure-to-remit, false-statement, and repeated-assessment penalties may also apply. Fraudulent or knowingly false claims may expose the entity and responsible persons to civil penalties and criminal prosecution. A reassessment period may be extended or remain open where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud. A non-resident recipient's royalty payment may separately raise Canadian Part XIII withholding-tax issues, generally at 25% before applicable treaty relief; that withholding regime is separate from GST/HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Act. Capital account transactions require prior Bank of Canada approval for certain non-resident investments. Profit remittances abroad are permitted with mandatory tax reporting via T1134 and withholding at source under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service exports including film licensing fees, proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender but must be reported to the Bank of Canada through the Electronic Data Interchange Reporting System (EDIR). Export contracts, invoices, and proof of delivery constitute primary evidentiary documentation for compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
