Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000 (approx. USD 112,000). At least CHF 50,000 (approx. USD 56,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration; the deposited amount must be held in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss-regulated bank and a bank blockage confirmation must be filed with the Commercial Register. Funds are released only after registration. In-kind contributions (Sacheinlage) require full payment of the corresponding shares and a statutory audit report in lieu of cash deposit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration Fédérale des Contributions – AFC / Amministrazione Federale delle Contribuzioni – AVC), official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; electronic filing via Swiss eTax and ePortal for VAT (www.estv.admin.ch/mwst) and corporate income tax at cantonal level.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Switzerland performing taxable supplies in Switzerland must register with ESTV for a Swiss VAT number and Company Identification Number (UID). The online registration (via ESTV eServices) requires: foreign certificate of incorporation, proof of the foreign business address, evidence of taxable supplies in Switzerland, name of a Swiss VAT representative if no Swiss seat/branch, and designation of payment rails. The UID and VAT number are issued within approximately 5–10 business days; effective VAT liability commences from the date of taxable business activity in Switzerland.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law has no pass-through or check-the-box election. Available elective statutory grouping mechanisms include Swiss VAT group taxation under Art. 12 MWSTG for economically, financially and organisationally integrated entities, and tax consolidation at the federal/cantonal level under Art. 18 Federal Corporate Income Tax Act (DBG) and Art. 2 Swiss Harmonised Tax Act (SHIG) for a group of resident subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable; Swiss law aligns with OECD BEPS recommendations requiring the relevant entity's tax treatment to follow its local legal classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7.7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 in annual turnover from taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies) for suppliers with Swiss seat/place of business; non-established suppliers are subject to registration without a de minimis threshold where they carry on a taxable activity in Switzerland and their B2C supplies are not covered by the Swiss recipient's reverse-charge mechanism; a non-resident supplier may elect to register voluntarily for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods out of Swiss customs territory to a foreign country are zero-rated under Art. 23 MWSTG where the supplier or a third party proves that the goods have been physically exported and the destination is a foreign customs territory. Export-related direct services, including freight, dispatch, and services dealt with directly on exported goods, are also zero-rated. For goods sold by a Swiss AG to a foreign buyer under CIF/FOB terms with delivery outside Switzerland, the zero rate applies upon possession of the fully executed Swiss export declaration and foreign import evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollinhaltsdeklaration / Swiss electronic customs declaration)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR Convention consignment note)
  • Commercial invoice with explicit export VAT zero-rating clause
  • Foreign customs entry or import customs declaration evidencing arrival in destination country
  • Bank settlement statement / SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment of the cross-border consideration

Switzerland does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. VAT-compliant invoices must be issued in accordance with Art. 26 MWSTG; electronic invoices are legally recognized. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the standardized payment slip format commonly used but is not an indirect-tax clearance instrument. VAT returns and periodic statements are filed electronically via ESTV eTax; invoices and export proof must be retained for 10 years.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration and cantonal tax authorities may retroactively audit export zero-rating documentation within statutory limitation periods. Missing, incomplete, or found-false export proof triggers retrospective VAT assessment at the standard 7.7% rate plus default interest. Abuse or intentional fraudulent invocation of export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Art. 96 MWSTG, subject to a fine up to the amount of tax evaded, confiscation, and in aggravating organised cases criminal penalties under Art. 98 below; repayment of wrongly credited input VAT is enforced with penal interest and administrative surcharge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted currency convertibility on both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control laws, no prior-approval requirements, and no licensing barriers apply to FX transactions or profit remittance by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG). Dividend distributions are subject to a 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax, which may be reduced or refunded under applicable double-taxation treaties, whereas capital repayment and current-account trade settlements are free of withholding tax. The CHF is classified as a freely usable and fully convertible currency by the IMF; no material profit-repatriation hurdles exist beyond standard corporate income tax and withholding tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation deadline for export receipts. The AG may retain export proceeds in any convertible currency, hold foreign-currency accounts with licensed Swiss banks, and freely time conversion to CHF. The only applicable obligation is indirect: monetary financial institutions report Swiss balance-of-payments statistics to the SNB under the National Bank Ordinance; the exporting AG itself carries no filing duty except standard bookkeeping and tax disclosure.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank), acting as sole monetary authority for the Swiss franc (CHF), in coordination with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), which supervises banks, securities dealers, and AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH with material legal constraints. A Swiss AG may lawfully open payment accounts with FINMA-licensed fintech banks or FINMA-licensed fintech licensees (e.g., Swissquote-type entities operating under Art. 1b Swiss Banking Act), as well as with EEA or UK electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or MultiPass. However, foreign EMIs are not Swiss banks: they do not participate in SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing), do not hold a Swiss banking license, do not benefit from the Swiss depositor-protection privilege (Art. 37b Banking Act), and cannot hold Swiss-resident client funds without FINMA authorization. Consequently, an offshore EMI should be used strictly as a pass-through settlement rail for cross-border receivables and payables, never as the primary CHF treasury hub. Domestic FINMA-regulated fintechs offer fully digital onboarding but generally cap deposits at CHF 100 million per client and charge premium per-transaction fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital injection must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the AG's designated CHF provisional capital account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank. The bank issues a paid-up capital confirmation (Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) for notarization under Art. 632 and 633 of the Swiss Code of Obligations. Purpose codes must align with the Swiss Payment Standards and SNB balance-of-payments statistical classification: the transfer is categorized as 'equity capital injection / direct investment' rather than trade receivables. Verification bundle: notarized deed of subscription, bank confirmation of blocked funds, board minutes approving the capital increase, and final Commercial Register entry for the paid-in capital. Shareholder loans are permissible and may be denominated in foreign currency; they must be evidenced by formal loan agreements with arm's-length interest rates to avoid recharacterization as hidden equity and to prevent exposure to Swiss anticipatory tax, which applies to distributions but not to genuine loan servicing.Primary clearing rails: (1) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for domestic CHF and SEPA EUR payments; (2) SWIFT MT103 for international cross-border wires, with mandatory structured narrative referencing invoice number, Incoterms, and commodity description for collectible trading cards; (3) irrevocable and confirmed commercial Letters of Credit opened by UBS AG or ZKB trade-finance desks for wholesale shipments exceeding defined risk thresholds; (4) documentary collections for mid-tier distributor purchases; and (5) third-party card-acquiring rails (Stripe, Authorize.Net) for retail e-commerce settlement, which are treated as pass-through payment collection and swept daily to the Swiss treasury account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal direct tax 8.5% on profit after tax (7.83% on pre-tax profit) plus cantonal and municipal profit tax; combined effective Zug rate for an AG approximately 11.9%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) withheld at source on any distribution; the AG must gross up the dividend so that the recipient bears the tax and the cash cost to the AG equals the net declared dividend

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory local-file or master-file thresholds exist in Swiss law; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for group consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900,000,000. Under the Federal Tax Administration's transfer pricing guidance (Wegleitung 2023), taxpayers must prepare and present on request contemporaneous arm's-length documentation for all material cross-border controlled transactions, commonly including the ten largest transaction flows, intercompany agreements, comparability analyses and financial benchmarking studies

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulation in the Gulf sense, but equivalent anti-abuse substance principles apply through Art. 50 DBG (place of effective management and administration), Art. 57 DBG (substance over form) and the DTA beneficial-ownership test; a collectible-trading-card AG must maintain operational substance in Switzerland — physical printing and lamination equipment, technical staff for holographic cold-foil processes, decisive board meetings, local bank accounts, and business premises — otherwise the tax authorities may disregard the legal form, attribute the income to the effective managers, or deny treaty benefits, triggering the full 35% anticipatory tax, restitution and criminal fines

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to the full amount of the evaded tax per Art. 175/176 DBG; aggravated evasion (Art. 177 DBG) carries imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of up to CHF 30,000, compounded by confiscation of the evaded amount and arrears interest of 4.5% p.a.; the assessment limitation period extends for cases involving documentary forgery

Director personal liability: Directors signing inaccurate filings incur co-perpetrator liability: imprisonment up to three years, personal fines, civil damages under Art. 754 CO toward creditors, and the claim of the cantonal tax administration against the board in bankruptcy proceedings (Verantwortlichkeitsklage)
Late or Non-Filing and Procedural Violations

Statutory sanction: Procedural fines up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG, discretionary default assessment with a surcharge of up to 5% of the assessed tax, loss of loss-carryforward claims and of participation relief, and cantonal late-payment penalties; a repeated default is prosecuted as procedural criminal offence

Director personal liability: Board members are personally liable for the procedural fines and for the company's tax debt where they wilfully or negligently caused the default; Swiss enforcement authorities may additionally impose travel restrictions (Ausreisesperre) on controlling directors for outstanding tax debts
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Hidden Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Upward tax adjustment to the arm's-length result per Art. 58 DBG; recharacterisation as a constructive dividend subject to 35% anticipatory tax; the underpaid tax is recovered with 4.5% p.a. interest and possible 5% default surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length terms are liable to the company for the unpaid withholding tax under the knowledge attribution rules, and the 35% WHT claim can be asserted against the signatory directors personally where the AG becomes insolvent
AML, Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years in qualified cases; the enterprise may additionally be sanctioned with a fine of up to CHF 5,000,000 per Art. 102 StGB, and all illicit proceeds are subject to confiscation; tax authorities will deny treaty benefits and reverse any DTA withholding relief with interest

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment of up to five years, disqualification as board members of Swiss companies, extended confiscation orders, extradition exposure on mutual legal assistance, and personal civil liability to the defrauded co-shareholders and creditors
