Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax accounts

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity incorporates a business corporation (federal or Ontario provincial) and obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or online through My Business Account. The BN serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration identifier; GST/HST account is established at the same time. Ontario provincial tax registrations, if required, are made through the Ontario Business Registry or combined CRA registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity incorporates a business corporation (federal or Ontario provincial) and obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or online through My Business Account. The BN serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration identifier; GST/HST account is established at the same time. Ontario provincial tax registrations, if required, are made through the Ontario Business Registry or combined CRA registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election is available in Canada. Canadian corporations are per se taxable entities. Limited elections exist under the Income Tax Act, including the subsection 111(6) loss carry-back waiver, section 256 associated-corporation election, and amalgamation/wind-up elections under sections 87/88; hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.3 restrict interest deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the current quarter or the prior four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered corporation may zero-rate digital image licences and stock media services supplied to non-resident clients where the service is delivered outside Canada, is not in respect of real or tangible property in Canada, and the customer is not registered for GST/HST. Exports of physical goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V and supported by documentary evidence.

  • B13A Customs Export Declaration (if physical media is shipped)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of FX receipt)

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping at the federal or Ontario level. GST/HST is reported periodically via CRA NETFILE; electronic records must be retained in accessible machine-readable format. No API-based continuous control regime applies.

CRA will reassess unsupported zero-rating claims at 13% HST plus interest and penalties. Negligence penalties under ETA s. 280.1 can reach 50% of the net tax; wilful evasion or false statements under s. 280 attract penalties up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. Misrepresentation can extend the normal four-year reassessment period and permit retroactive clawback claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a regime of current account convertibility with minimal restrictions for residents on foreign exchange transactions involving trade, services, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions, including portfolio investments and certain equity contributions, require prior approval from the Bank of Canada or are subject to reporting thresholds. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted following corporate tax filing, beneficial ownership verification, and compliance with the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required under Canadian foreign exchange regulations. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained offshore or converted to CAD at the prevailing market rate without statutory conversion ratios. All foreign exchange transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Bank of Canada by authorized dealers for statistical purposes. Importers and exporters are required to maintain supporting documentation for Canada Revenue Agency compliance and anti-money laundering reviews.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is subject to Canadian AML/KYC standards and FATF compliance requirements. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe and PayPal are permissible for low-risk transactions but necessitate full customer due diligence, source-of-funds verification, and ongoing transaction monitoring. High-volume or high-risk cross-border flows may trigger enhanced due diligence or account restrictions.
