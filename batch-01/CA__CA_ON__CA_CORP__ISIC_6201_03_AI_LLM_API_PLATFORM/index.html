Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office services are generally accepted in Ontario and at the federal level insofar as the registered office address is not a post office box and is a physical location in the jurisdiction where documents may be served (Ontario: OBCA s. 14(2), a registered office must be in Ontario at a specified street address; federal: CBCA s. 19(2), registered office must be in the province in which the head office is situated, at a place within that province capable of being specified accurately). In practice, registered agent offices and commercial virtual office suites providing a physical street address, document reception, and service-of-process arrangement are regularly used by AI/LLM API companies without physical premises. For an AI/LLM API platform generating recurring cross-border revenue, the registered agent's address is permissible for registry purposes, but substantive bank onboarding (USDC via crypto-exchanges and Stripe merchant account) will generally require disclosure of an operating address, website traffic, and transaction history; purely virtual offices are accepted at incorporation but physical presence is recommended for obtaining a full-service corporate bank account.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registrations under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes.html; Ontario provincial registrations are integrated through the Ontario OneStop Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/one-stop-business-registry) and administered by the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A CBCA-incorporated federal corporation is a Canadian-resident taxpayer and must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), with program accounts for corporate income tax (RC) and GST/HST (RM). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making Canadian supplies may also need a BN and GST/HST account. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1FF or complete online registration through CRA Business Registration Online; (2) provide articles of incorporation, registered office information, by-laws, and officer identification for a federal/provincial corporation; (3) for Ontario, register through the OneStop Business Registry to obtain Ontario corporate tax, Employer Health Tax, and other provincial accounts; (4) receive the 9-digit BN and subsequent 15-digit program account numbers; typical turnaround is 1-5 business days if fully electronic, with possible wait times for non-resident applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Canadian entity is taxable in Canada on worldwide income as a separate taxpayer; it is not a pass-through for Canadian federal and Ontario tax purposes. A Canadian-resident corporation pays federal corporate income tax plus Ontario provincial corporate income tax; if it qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), it may access the small business deduction. Non-resident shareholders are generally not deemed to carry on business solely by receiving dividends, but the corporation must withhold non-resident tax on dividends, interest, and royalties.

Available election forms

Canada does not adopt the U.S. check-the-box regime. Relevant elections include: (a) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s.156, allowing taxable supplies between parent and 90%-owned subsidiary to be treated as nil for GST/HST; (b) ETA s.265 election for financial institutions in special situations; (c) income tax elections under s.85 or s.97 for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation or partnership; (d) an election under ETA s.272.1 for imported services or intangibles treated as supply made in a different place. For hybrid mismatch concerns, no domestic entity classification election is available; Canada's anti-hybrid rules in the Income Tax Act (s.18.4) and OECD MLI treaty application must be assessed based on the foreign entity's legal classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – the small-supplier threshold for GST/HST registration. A Canadian-resident business must register when its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding supplies that are taxable at 0%? actually zero-rated supplies are included in the threshold calculation) exceed CAD 30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada applies the same CAD 30,000 threshold with respect to its taxable supplies made in Canada. If the non-resident does not carry on business in Canada but makes online sales to Canadian consumers, it must nevertheless register and collect GST/HST once Canadian sales exceed CAD 30,000 unless the supplier falls under a special cross-border digital services rule; registration may be voluntary to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported goods under ETA s.22 and Schedule VI, Part V, provided the exporter has proof of physical export from Canada. For services, including generative AI / LLM API access, zero-rating applies when the service is supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST in respect of that service and the service is for consumption or use wholly outside Canada (ETA s.143 and place-of-supply rules). A Canadian supplier exporting AI APIs to a non-resident business or consumer should retain the contract evidencing non-resident ordering, the foreign payment method, and technical data showing the API token is consumed outside Canada. If the supply is made to a Canadian GST/HST registrant, or the service is delivered to a recipient resident in Canada, 13% HST applies unless another special rule applies. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating; the 180-day figure in this dossier is used as an internal documentary reconciliation backstop to ensure payment and export evidence are contemporaneous.

  • Form B13 – Canada Export Declaration (submitted electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System / CBSA portal for physical goods; not required for services or digital API supply)
  • Commercial invoice / signed SaaS or API service agreement referencing the non-resident customer and delivery jurisdiction
  • Proof of export conveyance for goods: bill of lading, air waybill, express courier receipt, or postal documentation showing export from Canada
  • Proof of payment from abroad: foreign bank statement, SWIFT/FX confirmation, wire transfer record, or regulated cryptocurrency settlement record (USDC) demonstrating the non-resident source of funds
  • Technical delivery evidence for AI/LLM services: API usage logs, server session records, IP origin logs, and a statement by the customer confirming access and use wholly outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-level API filing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is voluntary, though CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing and remittance for registrants with taxable revenue above CAD 1 million (GST/HST electronic remittance). The PEPPOL network is being adopted in Canada but remains a private-sector convention, not a statutory compliance requirement. Invoices must contain the supplier's BN, date, supplier name and address, recipient name, description, amount and tax rate/tax paid, and are accepted in PDF or structured digital format. There is no CRA pre-authorization of invoice format or real-time tax nexus declaration.

CRA will deny zero-rating and reassess at 13% HST plus statutory interest if export documentation is insufficient or the place-of-supply evidence does not establish non-resident use. Normal GST/HST reassessment limitation is four years from the date of assessment; this limitation does not apply where the supplier has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud. Civil penalties under ETA s.280 include 25% of net tax for a first false statement and 50% for a repeated false statement; gross negligence penalties under ITA s.163(2) can be 50% of understated income/benefit, and serious tax evasion may trigger prosecution with 50%-200% penalties and imprisonment. Records must be retained for at least six years after the fiscal year to which they relate; failure to retain records may support an unlimited reassessment and additional penalties under ETA s.284 and ITA s.230.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and floats freely. Current-account items (API subscription revenue, royalties, interest, dividends) and capital-account items (equity subscription, shareholder loans, capital reductions, buybacks) are exchange-free with no Bank of Canada or FINTRAC pre-approval requirement. Profit remittance frictions arise solely from tax law: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement to 10% dividends for a company beneficially owning at least 10% of voting power, 15% otherwise; interest 10%; royalties 10%). Greenfield Ontario incorporation and subsequent operating remittances are not subject to Investment Canada Act net-benefit review; only acquisitions of control of an existing Canadian business above the WTO enterprise-value threshold trigger that review, and the national-security regime is fact-specific.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada maintains no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds. The Ontario AI-LLM API corporation may retain USD, USDC, and other foreign currency offshore or in domestic foreign-currency accounts. The only obligations are statistical reporting (Statistics Canada services-export surveys) and FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers or large virtual-currency transactions individually at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank, with the Department of Finance Canada retaining exchange-control policy authority and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising the domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs). Canada repealed its Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; no federal agency issues exchange-control approvals today. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) administers cross-border currency and virtual-currency reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), while Payments Canada operates the clearing and settlement infrastructure (Lynx, ACSS, and the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime managed by the Bank of Canada).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the UK/EU EMI licence; a foreign EMI cannot take deposits or maintain customer payment accounts in Canada without a Schedule I/II bank licence or a provincial trust-company licence. Offshore digital EMIs may only act as payment facilitators, not as the treasury ledger bank. Fintech rails (Stripe, Wise for Business) are viable for disbursements, card acquiring, and USDC conversion settlement, but the capital escrow ledger and core operating balances must remain at a Schedule I Tier-1 bank. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force November 1, 2024; Bank of Canada registration transitional into 2025), the Ontario entity should require its processors to evidence FINTRAC MSB/VASP and RPAA registrations before contracting; no direct deposit-accepting function may be performed by an unlicensed foreign EMI.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium.
