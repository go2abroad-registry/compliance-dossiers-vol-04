Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement for an Ontario limited partnership; no escrow account or capital deposit mandate. The general partner must designate a registered office in Ontario; limited partners contribute capital solely as provided in the Limited Partnership Agreement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Registration and Business Number (BN) via CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Ontario must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across the last four consecutive quarters, or if the entity is non-resident and seeks simpler registration; registration is done online through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service, by mail using Form RC1, or by phone. The LP must also obtain an Ontario provincial BN if registering provincially, but CRA BN integration covers Ontario. Required supporting documents for foreign entities include: proof of legal existence (certificate of foreign registration or partnership agreement), name and address of each non-resident partner, and a completed Form RC2 (Request for Update of Partners' Information) if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian Limited Partnership is a fiscally transparent entity for Canadian income tax purposes; partners are taxable on their share of partnership income or loss. GST/HST registration is generally required at the partnership level if the partnership carries on commercial activity in Canada and meets the threshold. Non-resident partners are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source partnership income, with withholding tax on certain allocated amounts, but resale of goods by an e-commerce LP is generally business income subject to Part I tax on the Canadian attributable share.

Available election forms

The LP as an eligible Canadian partnership may elect under section 96 of the Income Tax Act for a fiscal period consistent with all members; no check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. For GST/HST, the partnership may elect under section 156 to use the net tax calculation for closely related corporations, or elect under subsection 225(1) to treat a qualifying supply as a taxable supply, and may elect streamlined accounting methods under section 229 with CRA approval. Foreign partners may be eligible for a discretionary treaty election regarding permanent establishment; however, no hybrid mismatch election under Canadian domestic law applies beyond the specific mismatch rules in sections 18.3, 18.4, 61.5, and 12(1)(y).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – measured on total worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada; once exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four quarters, registration is mandatory from the end of the month supplying threshold. No separate Ontario provincial sales tax registration applies for HST-registered suppliers; Quebec P.S.T. and provincial sales tax (British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba) are not relevant to Ontario. The LP must separately determine whether it is a 'non-resident' carrying on business in Canada; substantial physical presence in Ontario (warehousing, employees, inventory) creates nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available for tangible personal property supplied by a Canadian registrant to a non-resident person and exported by the supplier or the recipient as part of the contract for the supply, provided the property is shipped directly out of Canada within a reasonable time (generally 60 days). Under section 39(1) of the Excise Tax Act, exports are zero-rated. The supply aligns with ISIC 4791 online retail; goods must be physically exported from Canada; digital or electronically supplied goods to non-residents are eligible only if the supply is not made in Canada, which is determined under the place-of-supply rules, requiring an indication of the recipient's ordinary residence and the location of use. Services that are performed directly for and entirely to the benefit of a non-resident and not performed in Canada are also zero-rated; offshore e-commerce support services qualify if they meet the 'substantially all' test. The LP must retain evidence showing the goods left Canada and the recipient is non-resident.

  • Commercial Invoice indicating zero-rated supply and HST registration number
  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or electronic equivalent) for goods valued above CAD 2,500 (or any value for controlled or regulated goods)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Canada to foreign destination
  • Proof of recipient's non-resident status (e.g., overseas address, foreign passport, business registration outside Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer records demonstrating receipt of payment from outside Canada, when available
  • Shipping documents showing the goods were exported within the time limit prescribed under ETA subsection 39(2)

Canada does not currently impose a real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate (no digital stamping or national e-invoicing B2B clearance system). However, registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier BN, date, HST rate, tax payable). For returns, mandatory electronic filing applies to registrants with annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million, except limited exceptions. E-commerce platform operators (third-party sellers) may be deemed to make certain supplies through the platform under the 'platform model' rules effective July 1, 2021; where applicable, the platform rather than the underlying seller is responsible for GST/HST, and the platform must file electronically. The CRA requires GST/HST returns, including Schedule A for non-resident registrants, to be filed online via GST/HST NETFILE or a third-party electronic filing service.

CRA assesses export zero-rating claims on the basis of documentary evidence and contemporaneous records. If the LP has claimed zero-rating on supplies that did not qualify, or if goods are not exported or are subsequently brought back to Canada without CRA authorization, the LP is liable to reassess the output tax at 13% HST plus interest and penalties. Negligent misrepresentation or false statements carry a penalty of 50% of the additional tax payable under subsection 285.1(2), and deliberate omission or false claims may lead to criminal prosecution for tax evasion. CRA may claw back input tax credits (ITCs) previously claimed and deny refunds, with a 5% 'assessment' penalty under subsection 280(1) for making a false statement in a return, plus a further 5% per month for recurring errors to a maximum of 50%, and gross negligence penalties of 50% of the additional tax under section 285.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime. There are no exchange controls separating capital versus current account convertibility, and no prior approval is required for foreign direct investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or royalty payments. Remittance of profits is unrestricted, subject only to compliance with Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, transfer-pricing documentation, and reporting thresholds under the Income Tax Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any mandatory surrender of export proceeds, statutory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines. Exporters may retain foreign currency receivables in offshore or domestic accounts and select the timing of conversion at their discretion, provided that all export transactions are properly documented for customs and tax purposes and that any cash transactions above CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist
