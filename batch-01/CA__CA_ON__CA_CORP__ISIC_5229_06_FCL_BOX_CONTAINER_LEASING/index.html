Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act and GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act are administered by the CRA. Primary digital portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and Represent a Client. Ontario corporate income tax is legally imposed by the Ontario Ministry of Finance but is assessed and collected federally under the Canada-Ontario Tax Collection Agreement, meaning one consolidated T2 corporate tax return covers both federal and provincial liabilities.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned container leasing entity must first be incorporated under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA) or the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) and must satisfy director-residency rules (at least 25% of directors resident in Canada; if the board has fewer than four directors, at least one director must be a resident Canadian). Step 1: incorporate and obtain the certificate and articles. Step 2: apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online using the incorporation data, or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) where online access is unavailable. Step 3: activate the corporate income tax program account (RC) and, before the first taxable supply, the GST/HST program account (RT); specify the fiscal year-end, principal business activity as NAICS 532411 (Commercial and Industrial Machinery and Equipment Rental and Leasing), and the first date of taxable supply for Ontario HST. Step 4: where the corporation is foreign-controlled, file Form T106 information returns and maintain transfer-pricing documentation for any cross-border lease or financing arrangements with non-resident shareholders. Step 5: register separately with the Ontario Ministry of Finance only for the Employer Health Tax if Ontario payroll later exceeds the statutory exemption. The BN is the single statutory identifier for corporate income tax, GST/HST, customs transactions, and most provincial tax programs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or equivalent elective classification regime. A Business Corporation is statutorily a separate taxable person subject to Part I tax; no flow-through classification is available. Practical statutory elections relevant to the container leasing structure include: (i) the Excise Tax Act s.156 election between closely related corporations to treat GST/HST taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, reducing administrative removals and invoicing; (ii) the Excise Tax Act s.273 joint venture election permitting one participant to operate a single GST/HST registration for the jointly owned container pool; (iii) the Income Tax Act s.85 rollover election to transfer container fleet assets into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis at an elected amount; and (iv) the Ontario/Federal consolidated T2 filing does not permit a separate Ontario classification election because Ontario corporate tax is fully harmonized under the Tax Collection Agreement. Hybrid-mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act restrict deduction/income mismatches in cross-border structures and must be respected despite the absence of an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supply revenue (the small-supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s.148). For a foreign lessor of tangible personal property, a supply nexus in Canada arises where the container is situated in Canada at the time of supply or is delivered or made available for use in Canada; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the lessor carries on business in Canada and total taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold. Zero-rated export supplies must be included when measuring the small-supplier threshold. Registration is also strategically advisable before the threshold is reached because a registrant may claim input tax credits on importations and purchases that would otherwise be irrecoverable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods, including a lease of intermodal ISO containers, is zero-rated for GST/HST where the goods are exported by the supplier or by the recipient in the course of the transaction and are not consumed or used in Canada before export, in accordance with Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. For a container lessor, the ordinary commercial test is that the leased container is placed directly into international traffic and leaves Canada through a documented export movement such that the lease payment qualifies for the 0% rate. Ancillary repair, inspection, storage, or positioning services supplied in respect of containers situated outside Canada are equally zero-rated under the offshore-services rule. Zero-rating is unavailable where the container remains in Canada for domestic carriage; lease payments for containers used solely within Canada are subject to 13% HST. The supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous documentary evidence proving export, and the zero-rated supply still counts toward the taxable-supply figure used to determine small-supplier status and filing frequency.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA eManifest cargo-control data and, where prescribed, the export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System, identifying the specific ISO container numbers, commodity classification, commercial value, and foreign destination.
  • Bill of Lading: a through-issued multimodal or ocean bill of lading (or equivalent combined transport document) naming the lessor or lessee as shipper, listing the ISO container number, and evidencing a foreign port of discharge with onboard carrier notation.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: a bank SWIFT credit advice, account statement, or receivable reconciliation evidencing receipt of the lease payment from the non-resident or foreign-facing counterparty within the export substantiation window.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based tax clearance for invoices. E-invoicing is not legally mandated at the federal level. Mandatory requirements are limited to electronic return filing: a GST/HST registrant with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or EDI, and registrants above CAD 10 million must file monthly. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information, including the supplier's registered Business Number, invoice date, a description of the supply, and the tax amount charged or a clear indication that the supply is zero-rated. Supporting records must be retained for at least six years under Excise Tax Act s.286 and must be adequate to allow the CRA to verify export claims.

Under the Excise Tax Act, the CRA may generally reassess within four years of the filing date, but the limitation period is extended indefinitely where the return contains a misrepresentation attributable to carelessness, neglect, wilful default, or fraud. Where zero-rating is successfully challenged, the supplier is assessed 13% HST on the full lease or sale proceeds, plus compound arrears interest at prescribed rates compounded daily from the date the supply was made, and a further penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of any overstated net tax or rebate claimed. Gross negligence or knowing misrepresentation attracts the 50% civil penalty and may result in criminal prosecution under the Customs Act, including seizure and forfeiture of container inventory. Although Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for general export sales, the 180-day internal repatriation and documentary-matching benchmark is applied in this dossier because longer open-account positions materially increase the CRA's audit scrutiny of export claims and transfer-pricing outcomes; a clawback assessment will retroactively reverse input tax credits and zero-rating benefits with interest and penalties on the full implicated value.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for residents and non-residents under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no quantitative exchange-control restrictions on profit remittance, dividend payments, capital repatriation, or trade settlements. The operative constraints are non-exchange: (i) solvency tests under section 42 of the Canada Business Corporations Act and section 38 of the Ontario Business Corporations Act before dividends or capital reductions; (ii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% federal, treaty-reducible) under the Income Tax Act; and (iii) FINTRAC monitoring and reporting thresholds. Physical cash movements crossing the border above CAD 10,000 trigger CBSA declaration duties; bank wires are unrestricted. USD accounts are routinely permitted; conversion is at free-market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts and may operate USD multi-currency accounts with Schedule I banks. Residual obligations are: (i) CBSA export documentation (B13A Export Declaration or EDI e-manifest) for goods exceeding CAD 2,000; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for transactions above CAD 10,000, filed by the financial intermediary; and (iii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments data supplied via the clearing bank. For the ISO container leasing business, lease rental receipts are classified as trade-in-goods ancillary services and should be logged against container movement records, bills of lading, and lease schedules to evidence arm's-length service export treatment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance; no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration exists, reflecting Canada's liberalised capital regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium — legally usable as an ancillary FX conversion and payment-initation rail, but not as the primary treasury layer for capital escrow, lease-rental collection, or dividend repatriation. Canada does not administer an EU-style Electronic Money Institution licence or passport; a foreign digital EMI must either register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and implement a full AML/KYC programme, or obtain a limited-authorized-branch order from OSFI if it holds deposits or client float. For a non-resident-controlled Ontario container-leasing company, an MSB-registered EMI can collect USD lease rentals and originate SWIFT MT103 payments, but the float is uninsured and balance complexity increases audit and tax-reporting burden. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Schedule I bank as the trunk treasury and settlement provider, with a FINTRAC-registered MSB portal used only for competitive FX forward execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Domestic receipt settles in real time over Payments Canada Lynx RTGS; USD legs may be channelled through the Canadian bank's US correspondent over CHIPS or Fedwire. The remitter should include the full account number and corporate remittance reference; FINTRAC EFT reports are filed by the receiving bank at the CAD 10,000 threshold. For injections above CAD 50,000, preparatory source-of-funds validation is triggered, so the bank package must contain the subscription agreement, the board resolution, and the shareholder bank reference letter.
