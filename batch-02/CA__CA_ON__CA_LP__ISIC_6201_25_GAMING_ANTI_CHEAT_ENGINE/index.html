Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - An Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA section 96: no entity-level corporate income tax; income and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners on a calendar-year basis. Limited partners may only deduct losses up to their at-risk amount (ITA s.96(2.1)) and subject to the limited partnership loss rules (ITA s.96.1). Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on their share of Canadian-source business income attributable to the Ontario permanent establishment; the LP files T5013 information returns, not T2. No tax-exempt zone applies; Ontario does not levy a separate capital tax on LPs.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canadian tax law; CRA classifies the LP according to its statutory legal form and ITA jurisprudence. Relevant elections: (i) election under ITA s.152(4) to waive the normal four-year reassessment limitation; (ii) election by a non-resident partner under ITA s.115.1 to compute Canadian taxable income on a Canadian-business basis; (iii) fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1 (practically restricted to a calendar year for Canadian partnerships); (iv) optional streamlined accounting on a net-tax formula for non-resident GST/HST registrants using CRA Form GST492; (v) ETA s.156 closely-related-corporation elections are not available to partnerships; (vi) Canada has not enacted OECD/BEPS hybrid mismatch rules (Bill C-59 proposals remain unproclaimed), so no hybrid mismatch elections are currently operative.

13%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may claim a 0% HST rate on exports of the kernel-level anti-cheat engine licence and associated cloud middleware services under ETA Schedule VI, sections 7 (services) and 23 (intangible personal property), where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person (verified by foreign tax ID and residency documentation); (ii) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply; and (iii) services are not rendered to an individual physically in Canada. For B2B supplies to Canadian GST/HST registrants, 13% HST applies unless the licence is for use wholly outside Canada, in which case the export zero-rating may apply. The LP recovers Canadian input tax credits (ITCs) through regularly filed GST/HST returns; no separate export refund application is required for ITCs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only if physical media/prototype hardware is shipped; CBSA electronic export declaration required for goods exceeding CAD 2,000; not applicable to pure digital software delivery).
  • Commercial invoice and software licence/SaaS agreement evidencing the non-resident recipient, foreign tax identification number, delivery/use location outside Canada, and clause confirming no Canadian real or tangible property nexus.
  • Bank SWIFT/TT inward remittance certificate or payment-rail reconciliation (Stripe/Wire) demonstrating receipt of export proceeds within 180 days.
  • Contemporaneous zero-rating memorandum citing ETA Schedule VI ss.7/23, including the recipient's non-resident status certificate or equivalent KYC evidence.

No statutory continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime in Canada. Voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing under the Peppol BIS 3.0 framework has been adopted by Ontario for B2G procurement; GST/HST returns are submitted to CRA electronically (NETFILE/EDI), and registrants with prescribed revenue levels must e-file. Books and records must be retained for six years (ETA s.286), kept in English or French, and be accessible electronically to CRA auditors on request.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims and ITCs within the normal four-year limitation period, and without time limit in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default (ETA s.296; ITA s.152(4)-(5)). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating currency regime under which the Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, no convertibility restrictions, and no licensing thresholds for cross-border fund movements related to an Ontario limited partnership. Non-resident limited partners may subscribe capital into, and receive distributions from, an Ontario LP without central-bank authorization. The sole statutory friction affecting profit remittance is Part XIII withholding tax under section 212 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on distributions to non-resident partners, which is a tax measure rather than a currency-control measure. The Investment Canada Act does not capture passive LP interests and therefore does not impede foreign capital participation in this structure, except in the rare event of national-security review of a transaction conferring control of a Canadian business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and residual exchange controls were fully terminated by the early 1960s. An Ontario LP providing anti-cheat middleware and gaming security software may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts either onshore or offshore without any surrender obligation. The only applicable reporting duty is FINTRAC's mandatory reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction, which is an AML monitoring obligation, not a surrender or conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/CFT oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no centralized foreign-exchange control agency; the relevant legal framework comprises the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and FINTRAC regulations thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for disbursement operations, Low for primary treasury holding. An offshore fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) that is not registered as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA cannot lawfully maintain a Canadian-facing account or process payment flows for an Ontario LP. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) may be layered onto a Schedule I bank primary structure solely for low-value, high-frequency licensing disbursements and global developer payouts, provided the EMI operates through a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB or a bank-regulated partner. Capital escrow, investor subscription proceeds, and surplus treasury balances must remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank; EMIs are not acceptable custodians for escrowed or regulated funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner capital contributions must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or by domestic Fedwire/ACH from U.S. partners with a corresponding SWIFT confirmation) to the LP's segregated capital account at a Schedule I bank. The wire narrative must be standardized as: 'Capital contribution - [LP legal name] - [capital call number] - [partner legal name]'. Canada operates no mandatory FX purpose-code scheme; the bank requires only a narrative description that clearly distinguishes capital contributions from trade payments. Any inbound international electronic funds transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 triggers automatic FINTRAC reporting. The bank will demand, at first credit, executed subscription agreements, the partnership agreement, a capital call notice, and source-of-funds statements for each non-resident partner. Sanctions screening is conducted against OSFI, OFAC, and UN consolidated lists; no government approval is required for the remittance itself.Primary clearing rails for trade settlement are (i) SWIFT MT103/Mail-gpi wire transfers for high-value enterprise licensing fees and contract-developer payments; (ii) Stripe (including Stripe Treasury and Stripe Connect) for low-value, high-frequency global subscription fees from gaming studios and end-user integrity-monitoring subscriptions; and (iii) Wise Business or comparable FINTRAC-registered MSB rails for small-ticket cross-border disbursements to remote contractors. Commercial letters of credit are not recommended and are generally inapplicable to software IP licensing because anti-cheat middleware is intangible, recurring, and subscription-style rather than physical goods. For all payments from an Ontario LP to non-resident payees that constitute royalties, Part XIII withholding tax at 25% applies under subsection 212(1)(a) of the Income Tax Act unless a treaty reduction is available (e.g., Canada-U.S. Tax Convention or Canada-EU treaty); the bank will require a completed Form NR301 or a treaty-residency certificate to execute the wire without over-withholding.Spot FX outright trades (CAD/USD and cross-currency pairs) executed with a Schedule I bank dealing deskOutright deliverable FX forwards with tenors up to 24 months for hedging of USD, EUR, and GBP licensing receiptsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or illiquid settlement currencies, booked through a Canadian Schedule I bank or its offshore branch, with CAD or USD as the notional reference currencyCross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for managing CAD/USD liquidity mismatches and rolling short-dated foreign-currency revenue balancesPlain-vanilla FX options and zero-premium collars under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex with a federally regulated dealerTom-next and spot-next swaps for operational treasury liquidity management across the partnership's multi-currency accounts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for disbursement operations, Low for primary treasury holding. An offshore fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) that is not registered as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA cannot lawfully maintain a Canadian-facing account or process payment flows for an Ontario LP. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) may be layered onto a Schedule I bank primary structure solely for low-value, high-frequency licensing disbursements and global developer payouts, provided the EMI operates through a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB or a bank-regulated partner. Capital escrow, investor subscription proceeds, and surplus treasury balances must remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank; EMIs are not acceptable custodians for escrowed or regulated funds.
