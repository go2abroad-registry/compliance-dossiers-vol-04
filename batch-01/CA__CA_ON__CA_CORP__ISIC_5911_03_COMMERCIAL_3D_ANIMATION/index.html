Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST registration and Business Number issuance through My Business Account (MyBA) portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial corporate and retail sales tax matters

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), mail Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number', or call CRA. If the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, it must register for a GST/HST account. Ontario registration is required for employer health tax or Ontario-specific levies if payroll thresholds are met. The BN is a single identifier for federal program accounts including GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are separate taxpayers; no 'check-the-box' partnership classification exists under Canadian law. Available elections include: the section 216(1) election for a non-resident corporation to file a return for Canadian rental or timber royalties; the GST/HST quick method election; the annual GST/HST filing election for large businesses; and the closely-related election under section 234 of the Excise Tax Act for intercompany supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier satisfies CRA that the goods were exported by the exporter (or delivered to a common carrier for export) within two years of the supply. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada, or when rendered directly in respect of exported goods. For 3D animation and visual effect services, the supply to a foreign customer is zero-rated only if the service is made to a non-resident and is not delivered in Canada, with the burden of proof on the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13/A or Canadian Automated Export Declaration) or official exporter records
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Manifest evidencing physical export or service delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of payment received in Canadian currency from the non-resident customer
  • Executed service agreement naming the non-resident recipient, delivery terms, and performance location

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through EDI or secure data interchange, with CRA requiring GST/HST registrants to retain electronic records. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, approved third-party software, or online banking for most registrants, with an annual filing election permitted for qualifying large businesses.

CRA audit may disallow zero-rating without adequate export evidence, reassessing net tax due plus interest. Penalties include a 5% per-year late-reassessment charge on unpaid tax, a 10% penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act for false statements (5% of understated tax), a 50% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 285.1(1), and potential criminal prosecution for wilful evasion, carrying unlimited fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full, unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is not blocked but is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, reduced to 5–15% under most tax treaties including the Canada-US Treaty). Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees require CRA Form NR4 reporting and remittance. No exchange control approval is required for capital transfers, dividend distributions, or repatriation of invested funds; however, certain payments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported under FINTRAC's large cash transaction and cross-border wire reporting rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender or convert export proceeds in Canada. Exporters are free to maintain foreign currency accounts either domestically or with foreign financial institutions. CAD settlement is permitted at commercial rates. For tax purposes, all foreign currency gains and losses must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) using the daily Bank of Canada exchange rate, but no mandatory conversion timeline or ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC for cross-border transaction monitoring; no separate foreign exchange administration body exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Canadian law does not provide a standalone 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence; foreign-regulated EMIs and digital banks (e.g., Wise) may operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) in Canada only after registration with FINTRAC and compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Such entities cannot accept deposits or hold funds in a manner equivalent to a bank. They are suitable for low-value operational payments but not for statutory capital escrow or trade settlement requiring a bank guarantee or documentary credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols must be structured via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) with payment narrative clearly identifying the purpose as 'equity capital injection' or 'shareholder loan' in accordance with the Ontario corporate structure. Domestic clearing within Canada should use the Bank of Canada's Lynx high-value payment system for settlement in CAD. Purpose codes for Canadian balance-of-payments reporting: use 'E1 – Direct investment in Canadian enterprise' for equity, or 'E2 – Other capital transfers' for shareholder loans. Beneficiary account details must include institution number (3-digit), transit number (5-digit), and bank account number; avoid IBAN, which is not used domestically. All inbound wires exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC tracking, and the receiving bank will require a completed Foreign CTA (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments) report if cash or bearer instruments are involved. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (Federal 15% + Ontario general corporate 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; treaty rates generally reduce this to 15%, and to 5% where the recipient is a corporation owning at least 10% of the voting stock under the Canada-US DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair-market value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in the taxation year; Country-by-Country report notification applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime such as those in zero-tax jurisdictions, but the statutory general anti-avoidance rule (s.245) and judicial substance-over-form principles apply. Corporate residence requires maintenance of 'mind and management' in Canada, and a non-resident corporation's Canadian tax presence turns on permanent establishment and commercial substance facts. Treaty benefits require the recipient to be the beneficial owner and to conduct genuine substantive business activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 2 years; civil reassessment, interest, and penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, authorize, or acquiesce to evasion are jointly and severally liable for tax amounts under the directors' liability provisions, and personally subject to criminal conviction, restitution orders, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3): 10% of the upward adjustment to taxable income, plus daily compound interest; CRA may also apply gross negligence penalties and reassess the entire transaction under the arm's length principle.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct, approve, or implement transfer pricing schemes may be named in reassessments and assessed personally for unremitted liabilities in cases of concealment, reckless conduct, or fraudulent breach of fiduciary duty.
Shell Company and Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under the GAAR/sham doctrine and reassess the true economic beneficiary; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA may reach C$250,000; Criminal Code money laundering charges carry imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for money laundering and are subject to asset seizure, restraint orders, travel interdictions, and lengthy imprisonment; civil liability may extend to restitution, disgorgement, and joint and several assessments for taxes and penalties.
Withholding and Source Deduction Failure

Statutory sanction: Statutory withholding tax liability at 25%, plus penalties under s.227 of the Income Tax Act (typically 10% of the amount withheld plus aggregate daily interest) and all arrears interest; the tax cannot be recovered under the payee treaty where proper forms are absent.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted withholding taxes, penalties, and interest under s.227.1; CRA may enforce against the director's personal bank accounts, garnish wages, or pursue civil restitution and travel bans for continuing non-payment.
