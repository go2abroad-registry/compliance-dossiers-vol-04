Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Swiss AG is a resident taxpayer under the classical corporate tax system: unlimited tax liability on worldwide income (Direct Federal Tax Act DBG Art. 50; tax harmonization laws apply cantonal/communal levels), with relief for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate under the exemption method (cantonal) or credit method (federal). Federal corporate income tax of 8.5% plus cantonal/communal income and capital taxes (aggregate effective rate typically 11%–21% depending on canton; Zug/Zugerberg rates circa 11.9%). Distributions are subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax, refundable in whole or in part under applicable treaty or domestic restitution rules. The AG is not a pass-through entity; no entity-level tax exemption applies in the standard regime.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Swiss tax law. Available statutory elections relevant to this entity: (1) Voluntary VAT registration under Art. 11 Federal Act on Value Added Tax (MWSTG) for persons exempt from tax liability on turnover not exceeding CHF 100,000. (2) VAT group taxation election (Organschaft) under Art. 21 MWSTG, allowing closely controlled legal entities to be treated as a single taxpayer with consolidated VAT reporting and internal supplies outside the scope of VAT. (3) Participation deduction election (Art. 69/70 federal; cantonal equivalents) for dividends and capital gains from qualifying participations. (4) Hybrid mismatch rules implementing BEPS Action 2 apply mandatorily since 1 January 2020 with no elective carve-out; where a hybrid instrument or entity produces a deduction-without-inclusion outcome, the federal tax administration (FTA Circular No. 32) aligns treatment by denying the deduction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 worldwide annual taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT; Art. 10 para. 2 MWSTG). Turnover below this threshold is exempt from tax liability on a mandatory basis, subject to voluntary registration. Special thresholds and simplified flat-rate methods may apply to non-resident digital suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export of goods under Art. 23 para. 1 MWSTG applies where the cosplay prop apparel, theatrical costume robes, and synthetic wigs are physically transported to a foreign destination (outside Switzerland, including the Principality of Liechtenstein under the Swiss-Liechtenstein VAT treaty) by the supplier, the recipient, or a third party carrier. The supplier must obtain adequate documentary proof of export before the expiration of the legal assessment period; transport and export must be evidenced by customs exit confirmation from Swiss customs (e-dec process) or, for goods supplied to customers in EU/EEA states, by the export declaration together with transport documentation. Services closely connected to exported goods and certain services supplied to foreign recipients may also qualify for zero-rating under Art. 23 para. 2 MWSTG. The supplying AG should maintain the export evidence in the order of the shipment for immediate production to the ESTV upon request.

  • Official Swiss customs export declaration (e-dec / Ausfuhranmeldung) with electronic customs exit certification and transit evidence from Swiss Customs Administration (OZD).
  • Transport document proving physical departure from Switzerland: Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR consignment note (road), postal/courier customs declaration for low-value parcels, or Swiss Customs exit stamp.
  • Commercial invoice (original) referencing the exporter's Swiss UID/MWST number, describing the goods, stating the delivery terms (Incoterms), and confirming the Israel? none; stating the consideration; non-dated.
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/MT103 bank confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the company's Swiss bank account (employed as a supporting audit-trail document for standard 180-day repatriation review).
  • For goods exported via an EU intermediary: the recipient or customs agent confirmation of importation in the country of destination or the EU electronic transit record (NCTS) referencing the Swiss export declaration.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government-approved API digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control in Switzerland. Invoicing is governed by Art. 26 MWSTG, requiring the invoice to identify supplier and recipient (name and address), the supplier's UID, invoice date, description of goods/services, consideration, applicable VAT rate and tax amount; failure to include VAT in compliant form shifts the tax liability. Electronic invoicing is permitted on B2B and B2C with recipient consent; voluntary standards include the SIX eBill framework and the Peppol BIS-CH network. The Swiss Federal Administration is progressively introducing PEPPOL-based e-invoicing for public procurement suppliers, but no statutory private-sector e-invoicing mandate is currently in force. Statutory retention of invoices and export evidence: 10 years.

Swiss law does not impose a statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; however, the 180-day benchmark is applied by the ESTV as a prudent evidentiary window for receipt of export consideration. Export zero-rating is subject to retroactive reclassification if the documentary proof is incomplete or untruthful: the supply is treated as a taxable domestic supply at the standard 8.1% rate, with interest on the additional tax due at 4.5% per annum. Penalty provisions of the Swiss VAT Act (MWSTG): Art. 95 - tax evasion penalty up to four times the tax evaded; Art. 96 - criminal fraud (Leistungsbetrug) with imprisonment up to ten years for intentional schemes; Art. 98 - procedural violation fines up to CHF 50,000 in serious cases; limitation periods of 5 years generally and 10 years in cases of tax evasion (Art. 42/43 MWSTG). Customs law parallel sanctions apply for false export declarations under the Federal Customs Act. Companies operating via Stripe or Shopify Payments should reconcile payment-rail settlement records with export documentation to preempt nexus-based audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the free movement of capital enshrined in the Swiss Federal Constitution and OECD liberalisation codes. No prior exchange control approval is required for inbound equity, shareholder loans, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Dividends, royalties, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred abroad, subject only to Swiss withholding tax of 35% on qualifying distributions, which may be reduced or refunded under applicable double taxation treaties. Banking relation risk is not FX regulatory but counterparty compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may hold, invest, or disburse foreign currency receipts freely. For manufacturing sales of cosplay props/apparel, there is no FX surrender ratio, no declaration threshold for routine current-account receipts, and no requirement to route through a designated local bank, although bank account opening requires compliance with the Due Diligence Ordinance (VSB 20) and Anti-Money Laundering Act.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Swiss Federal Department of Finance's State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for sanctions and financial market regulation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Under FinIA and FinSA, non-Swiss digital EMI passporting does not exist under an EU PSD-equivalent regime. Offshore EMI-only accounts are legally constrained because they lack Swiss SIC membership and cannot directly settle CHF; they require interoperability through a Swiss licensed bank sponsor. For a low-risk cosplay apparel manufacturer, a FINMA-licensed neobank or banking-as-a-service solution with UBS/ZKB is viable, whereas a pure unregulated offshore EMI account will generally be rejected as an invalid deposit-taking vehicle and cannot support corporate payroll, VAT refunds, or commercial LC issuance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (statutory rate; effective federal rate is 7.83% because the federal tax itself is deductible); in the target Zug/Crypto-Valley location the combined federal, cantonal and communal ordinary effective headline rate is approximately 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) imposed on all gross dividend distributions by the Swiss AG; the 35% deduction is final for Swiss-resident individuals but refundable or reducible to treaty rates for non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed within 30 days of service of the return form; forms are ordinarily issued by the cantonal tax administration after the annual financial statements are approved, typically within six months after the fiscal year-end. Ordinary extensions are granted to 30 September following the tax year-end; non-filing triggers a formal reminder, then estimated assessment by the tax authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required where annual cross-border related-party goods or service transactions exceed CHF 5 million; Master File documentation is required for multinational groups with consolidated gross revenue of at least CHF 80 million; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more. Regardless of thresholds, the Swiss AG must produce contemporaneous arm's length documentation within 60 days of an ESTV request.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain a codified Economic Substance Regulations regime, but applies the economic-reality principle and substance-over-form doctrine (Art. 2 DBG and Federal Tax Administration circular practice). The Swiss AG must demonstrate real local substance — board meetings held in Switzerland, locally resident management and qualified staff, commercial premises, actual business decision-making and risk-bearing capacity — to sustain participation exemption, patent box relief, DTA treaty benefits and any ruling of the Zug tax authority; letterbox shell structures are reassessed, denied relief and exposed to full taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Federal sanction: additional tax equal to the evaded amount plus a fine of up to three times the evaded federal tax (Art. 175 DBG); tax fraud committed by falsifying books, registers or using counterfeit documents is punished with imprisonment of up to three years and ancillary financial penalties (Art. 186 DBG), with publication of the judgment and confiscation of illegal profit.

Director personal liability: Directors, de-facto managers and signatories are individually prosecuted; personal imprisonment, board disqualification, and civil damages under Art. 754 OR may be imposed. Swiss criminal procedure allows seizure of private assets, security for the tax claim and a travel ban (Ausreiseverbot, Art. 239 ff. StPO) where flight risk exists; the corporate veil is pierced when directors have used the AG as a mere instrument of personal enrichment.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: ESTV enforces payment of the full 35% against the Swiss AG without time limitation (Art. 14 VStG), plus default interest at 5% per annum and a fine of up to CHF 5,000 per offense; the unpaid WHT blocks any treaty refund for the non-resident shareholder.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally personally liable for uncollected or unremitted withholding tax when the company is insolvent (Art. 12 VStG); direct enforcement against personal assets, criminal fines and coercive measures including travel bans apply to finance-responsible directors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation (Gewinnverlagerung / Konstruktive Dividende)

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the Swiss AG with add-back of the excess expenses to taxable profit, default interest, and potential penalty; the recharacterized amount is treated as a constructive dividend subject to 35% withholding tax; incomplete documentation can trigger fines of up to CHF 10,000 and estimated assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders who arrange abusive intercompany pricing are jointly liable for the additional corporate tax and the 35% WHT (Art. 12 VStG and Art. 57 DBG); documented deliberate evasion leads to personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, forfeiture of assets and travel bans.
Shell Company Abuse and AML Violations (Geldwäscherei / Mangelhafte Sorgfalt)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable by up to three years' imprisonment and/or a fine of up to CHF 500,000 (Art. 305bis CP); negligent failure in financial due diligence is punishable by a fine of up to CHF 500,000 (Art. 305ter CP); all illicit proceeds are subject to confiscation (Art. 70 CP); the tax administration will annul all planning benefits and reassess hidden distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal conviction, civil restitution to creditors, worldwide asset freezes and travel bans imposed under the Swiss Code of Criminal Procedure; the corporate veil is pierced and personal assets are forfeited where the AG is operated as a sham.
