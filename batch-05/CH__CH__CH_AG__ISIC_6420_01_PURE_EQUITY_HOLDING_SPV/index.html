Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% (in all cases a minimum of CHF 50,000) must be fully paid up in cash or by contribution in kind before registration. For a pure equity holding SPV, contributions in kind are permitted for the subscription of subsidiary shares and must be evidenced by a capital payment confirmation from the Swiss bank or fiduciary holding the funds in an escrow account.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety certification, structural load inspection or workplace occupancy clearance is mandatory for a mailbox-style registered office of a pure holding SPV without employees. If physical premises are eventually staffed, the company must obtain the cantonal fire department acceptance certificate (Feuerpolizeiliche Abnahme), comply with the Labour Act (Arbeitsgesetz) on workplace conditions, and register employees with the compensation fund and the caisse de prévoyance before commencement of work.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% (in all cases a minimum of CHF 50,000) must be fully paid up in cash or by contribution in kind before registration. For a pure equity holding SPV, contributions in kind are permitted for the subscription of subsidiary shares and must be evidenced by a capital payment confirmation from the Swiss bank or fiduciary holding the funds in an escrow account.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV), portal https://www.estv.admin.ch, responsible for VAT, federal withholding tax and stamp duties; cantonal tax administrations (Kantonale Steuerverwaltungen) assess federal, cantonal and communal direct taxes; the Uniform Business Identifier register is at https://www.uid.admin.ch and the commercial register portal at https://www.zefix.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Swiss Aktiengesellschaft: (1) notarise the articles of association and file incorporation with the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregisteramt), disclosing the ultimate beneficial owner; (2) after registration the Federal Statistical Office automatically allocates the UID (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx), which functions as the general tax identification number; (3) for VAT, submit the electronic registration to the FTA via the e-VAT portal or the UID/VAT registration form, stating the opening balance sheet date and the expected annual turnover; a foreign-resident business with no Swiss establishment must simultaneously appoint a Swiss VAT representative/agent for service of documents; (4) file the opening tax declaration with the competent cantonal tax administration for federal, cantonal and communal profit tax; total timeline approximately 7–14 business days after commercial register publication.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident-owned Swiss Aktiengesellschaft: (1) notarise the articles of association and file incorporation with the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregisteramt), disclosing the ultimate beneficial owner; (2) after registration the Federal Statistical Office automatically allocates the UID (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx), which functions as the general tax identification number; (3) for VAT, submit the electronic registration to the FTA via the e-VAT portal or the UID/VAT registration form, stating the opening balance sheet date and the expected annual turnover; a foreign-resident business with no Swiss establishment must simultaneously appoint a Swiss VAT representative/agent for service of documents; (4) file the opening tax declaration with the competent cantonal tax administration for federal, cantonal and communal profit tax; total timeline approximately 7–14 business days after commercial register publication.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law has no US-style check-the-box election and an AG is not pass-through eligible; the relevant statutory elections are: (a) the participation deduction (Beteiligungsabzug, Art. 70 DBG and Art. 28 SHIG) for qualifying dividends and capital gains from participations of at least 10% or with an acquisition cost of at least CHF 1 million, the decisive relief for a pure equity holding SPV; (b) the VAT group election under Art. 11 MWSTG; (c) the option to waive the VAT registration threshold exemption; (d) binding advance tax rulings (Verbindliche Auskunft) from the cantonal/FTA authorities; (e) the transitional step-up facilities and notional interest deduction on new equity approved under the 2020 corporate tax reform; hybrid mismatch adjustments under Art. 61a DBG override elective outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A resident business is obliged to register for Swiss VAT when its worldwide annual turnover from taxable supplies exceeds CHF 100,000 (uniform threshold since 1 January 2018; CHF 150,000 for non-profit, cultural or sporting bodies); voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident businesses without a Swiss place of business supplying digital or electronic services to Swiss private consumers are registrable from the first franc, whereas qualifying B2B services are dealt with by the Swiss recipient under the reverse-charge procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied by a Swiss VAT-registered entity are zero-rated under Art. 23 MWSTG where the goods physically leave Switzerland and the export is proved by a Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhr- bzw. Exportzollanmeldung) with the corresponding customs release; services connected with exported goods (transport, handling, inspection, freight) qualify where performed directly for the exporter or for a foreign buyer. Dividend and capital-gain income received by a pure equity holding SPV is outside the scope of Swiss VAT (Leistung ohne Entgelt), so no output tax is charged; recharged management, financing or guarantee services are assessed under the place-of-supply rules and may be zero-rated, exempt, or subject to reverse charge for the Swiss recipient.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (Exportzollanmeldung) with customs release/visa evidencing physical removal from Switzerland
  • Commercial invoice and sale or service contract establishing the foreign purchaser/recipient and delivery terms
  • International transport document (bill of lading, CMR waybill or air waybill) covering the whole export movement
  • Proof of receipt of the foreign payment (bank transfer confirmation such as SWIFT MT103 or other inward remittance evidence)

Switzerland imposes no continuous transaction controls, no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-mandated invoice stamping and no mandatory e-invoicing; invoices must nevertheless satisfy Art. 26 MWSTG (seller's name and UID, invoice date, description, consideration and VAT rate). The Swiss QR-bill and eBill are the standard structured invoicing/payment instruments, the electronic VAT return must be submitted through the FTA's e-VAT portal or certified XML interface, and the standardised ESTVAudit file must be produced during fiscal audits.

An inadequately substantiated export is retroactively reclassified as a domestic taxable supply: output VAT at 8.1% is clawed back with statutory default interest and corrected input VAT deductions are recovered from the exporter. Under Arts. 92 and 94 MWSTG, intentional evasion is sanctioned with a fine of up to four times the evaded tax and gross negligence with a fine of up to twice the evaded tax (ordinary negligence usually without fine); the assessment limitation extends to ten years in evasion cases, recurring false zero-rating can lead to loss of the right to use simplified accounting, and the FTA may publish final sanction decisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland applies full convertibility on both current and capital account under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments and the Swiss Debt Collection and Bankruptcy Law; there are no foreign-exchange restrictions on inward or outward remittances. Profit repatriation in the form of dividends is unrestricted from an FX perspective, but a 35% Swiss dividend withholding tax applies unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable double taxation treaty; the holding entity must therefore manage Swiss and foreign WHT recovery timelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement in Switzerland; no mandatory timeline or conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Export earnings may be retained in any convertible currency or freely sold back to CHF. The only relevant requirement is statistical/reporting in nature, as Swiss banks must report cross-border payment transactions to the Swiss National Bank when prescribed thresholds are met, according to the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), assisted by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and financial-market supervision under the National Bank Act and the Financial Market Supervision Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services are feasible only as a secondary payment channel, not as a primary treasury or settlement rail. A non-Swiss EMI holding only a e-money licence in a foreign jurisdiction cannot access Swiss SIC clearing or provide a native Swiss IBAN without additional authorisation; any such provider would be classified as a third-party intermediary and may trigger enhanced due diligence. The viability rating is Low for primary liquidity and Low-Medium as an auxiliary receivables-collection gateway, subject to Swiss AML outsourcing rules and FINMA guidance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax is 8.5% on taxable net profit; because the federal tax is deductible in computing its own base, the effective federal rate is approximately 7.83%. Cantonal and communal profit taxes are added, producing combined nominal headline rates of approximately 11.9% in Zug, approximately 15%-17% in most business cantons, and approximately 21.1% in Zurich.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is withheld on distributions of dividends and similar profit distributions made by a Swiss AG; the distributing Swiss company is the statutory debtor and must remit the tax to the Federal Tax Administration.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed within 6 months from the financial year-end; for calendar-year companies the ordinary deadline is 30 June of the following year, with short extensions commonly granted upon written application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland does not prescribe statutory minimum thresholds for Master File/Local File, but the Federal Tax Administration expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation when the consolidated group turnover exceeds CHF 80 million and aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 8 million annually; a pure holding SPV with only passive dividend flows should nevertheless keep participations, financing agreements, and arm's-length pricing evidence proportionate to its functions, assets, and risks.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no statutory Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) comparable to Cayman Islands law, but the Swiss tax authorities apply OECD-aligned substance-over-form principles, abuse-of-law doctrines, and treaty anti-abuse rules. A pure equity-holding SPV must maintain real and proportionate economic substance in Switzerland: strategically involved Swiss-resident directors, adopted board minutes actually controlling participation rights, a maintained Swiss registered office, a local Swiss bank account, and sufficient physical or administrative infrastructure to make the owning presence credible.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to three times the tax evaded, imprisonment in aggravated cases, forfeiture of the improperly obtained fiscal benefit, plus full reassessment, penalty interest, and exclusion from amnesty or voluntary disclosure relief.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, signatories, and de facto managers can be criminally prosecuted; the company may recover damages through liability suits, but the State can attach personal assets, impose travel bans to guarantee the criminal/procedural process, and order civil restitution against directors acting in wilful breach of duty.
Reckless or Negligent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine determined with reference to evaded tax, generally up to the amount of the tax evaded, plus 5% default interest and possible 30% assessment surcharge for non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who remain passive after knowing of incomplete records may be held jointly liable; personal liability extends to the penalty, the additional tax, and restitution if the company is insolvent.
Anticipatory Tax / Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: The Swiss AG remains liable for the unremitted 35% anticipatory tax, plus default interest at 5% per annum and any assessed penalty; interest and penalty amounts are not deductible for profit tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Where the company is insolvent or being liquidated, the Federal Tax Administration can pursue directors who were responsible for remitting withholding taxes; director liability includes personal civil restitution, travel restrictions, and criminal sanction if the default was wilful.
Sham Company / Lack of Economic Substance / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, refusal of the participation exemption, reclassification of income streams into constructive dividends subject to 35% anticipatory tax, retroactive reassessment, fines for tax evasion, and possible countermeasures under Swiss unilateral international tax measures.

Director personal liability: Directors signing tax returns while knowing the company lacks substance are exposed to personal penalties, civil damages to the tax authority, and criminal prosecution for aiding tax fraud; the corporate veil may be pierced so the ultimate foreign shareholder bears the Swiss tax liability.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment, disallowance of excessive interest/expenses, recharacterization of the excess as a hidden dividend distribution subject to 35% anticipatory tax, additional tax assessment, default interest, and penalty fines up to 100% of the recharacterized amount.

Director personal liability: Officers approving fraudulent or negligent pricing may be personally sued for damages under the Swiss Code of Obligations; criminal liability arises where pricing documents were fabricated or where the director knowingly aided in tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering / Criminal Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Art. 305bis (money laundering) of up to three years' imprisonment, or five years for aggravated offences; corporate fines of up to CHF 5 million; freezing and confiscation of assets; regulatory measures by FINMA against involved Swiss financial intermediaries.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners knowingly participating in laundering face imprisonment, travel bans, and asset seizure; the courts may disregard the corporate form entirely, impose civil restitution, and permanently bar the director from holding Swiss corporate office.
