Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec provincial corporate tax and QST — Registre des entreprises du Québec (NEQ) and Mon compte RQ.

E-government filing portal

For a CA_CORP business corporation: (1) obtain Articles of Incorporation (federal or Quebec); (2) receive the 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA upon incorporation; (3) register program accounts via BRO — RT (GST/HST) account, RC (federal corporate income tax) account, and RP (payroll) if employees are engaged; (4) register with Revenu Québec for the NEQ (10-digit enterprise number), Quebec corporate tax registration, and a QST account (TVQ) using the combined federal-provincial registration form or RQ Form LM-1/VF; (5) for non-resident vendors without a Canadian presence, use CRA Form RC1/RC1A interim-BN procedure and RQ non-resident registrant procedures, then apply for GST/HST and QST accounts once the CAD 30,000 registration threshold is met or exceeded, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits; (6) maintain the BN, NEQ, and program account numbers in billing systems, e-commerce platforms, bank account records, and contractual counterparty files.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate resident taxpayer subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Quebec corporate income tax; no pass-through treatment for corporate shareholders; dividends are taxed at the shareholder level with applicable integration mechanisms. A Quebec-resident corporation's taxable income is computed federally (T2 return) and mirrored for Quebec Form CO-17 purposes, with separate provincial tax attributes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; entity classification is determined by domestic law and applicable tax treaties. Available elections and statutory mechanisms include: Income Tax Act s. 39(6) functional-currency election; s. 216 election for non-residents to be taxed on net rental income; s. 217 election respecting certain non-resident withholding taxes; s. 85/88 rollover elections for tax-deferred corporate reorganizations and wind-ups; Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election for nil consideration supplies between closely related corporations; ETA s. 179 election for financial services; and equivalent QST elections under the Quebec Taxation Act (LTVQ). Anti-hybrid rules under ITA ss. 18.2–18.4 and 95.2, effective 1 January 2023, neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and dual-deduction mismatches and must be reviewed before any cross-border financing, licensing, or intellectual-property structure is adopted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of world-wide taxable supplies (GST/HST and QST combined basis for small-supplier status) in any single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold registration is voluntary; digital-platform vendors must also assess the deemed-supplier rules for marketplace sales and fulfilment-warehouse arrangements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST and QST applies to goods exported by the vendor (ETA Schedule VI, Part V; QST Schedule III, Part V) where: (1) the supply is made to a non-resident purchaser; (2) the goods are removed from Canada as soon as reasonably practicable after the supply is completed; (3) the vendor obtains contemporaneous, credible documentary evidence of export before the filing of the applicable return; and (4) the evidence is retained for at least six years. For online e-commerce shipments, delivery to a foreign address by courier, freight forwarder, or postal service with customs declaration satisfies the proof-of-export requirement. Services may be zero-rated where performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident recipient and not related to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services to persons in Canada; maternity and infant-care digital advisory or platform services must be reviewed against the specified digital services rules.

  • CBSA Export Declaration filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or an authorized courier/low-value shipment manifest exemption
  • Commercial Invoice / Canada Customs Invoice identifying exporter, consignee, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or courier consignment note evidencing delivery outside Canada
  • Proof of payment from the non-resident purchaser, including Stripe, PayPal, or bank settlement records and foreign-currency conversion documentation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds (best practice and commonly requested by Canadian banks under AML/KYC procedures, though not a statutory GST/QST condition)
  • Revenu Québec QST export substantiation file, including invoice-level export worksheets supporting the 0% QST rate on the provincial return

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API e-invoicing clearance regime is currently in force in Canada or Quebec. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information under ETA s. 223 and equivalent Quebec QST invoicing rules; electronic invoices are valid where the recipient consents or where the marketplace/platform arrangement so provides. CRA's Budget 2024 roadmap proposes mandatory e-reporting and e-invoicing in phased implementation after 2026. Digital-platform economy reporting (PAN) rules effective 1 January 2024 require platform operators to report seller income and tax identifiers to CRA (Form RC385) and Revenu Québec; vendor business numbers and residence status must be verified by the platform.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny the 0% rate and reassess at the combined 14.975% (5% GST + 9.975% QST) plus interest where export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or insufficient; limitation periods are extended in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Civil penalties under ETA s. 280.1 (and Quebec equivalents) equal to 50% of the overclaimed refund or rebate may apply where a person knowingly, or under circumstances of gross negligence, makes a false statement or omission; criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 239(1) (mirrored in Quebec's Tax Administration Act) carries fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment. Retrospective clawbacks of zero-rated export claims, denial of input tax credits, and disclosure obligations under the Voluntary Disclosures Program are available compliance-softening routes only before an audit is commenced by the tax authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, and Canada imposes no exchange controls or split-currency conversion regimes. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including profit remittances, do not require central bank approval. Non-resident investors may freely repatriate capital, dividends, interest, and royalties, subject only to domestic withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (for interest, dividends, rents, royalties) and arm's length transfer pricing compliance under Section 247. Quebec corporate law adds no additional repatriation restriction. In-bound investment screening by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada applies only to acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above the WTO enterprise value threshold, which is inapplicable to a greenfield e-commerce establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not mandate surrender, conversion, or forced repatriation of export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts. For tax and statistics purposes, the exporter must track transaction values in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate, report monthly export data through the Canadian International Merchandise Trade regime where applicable, and retain all underlying invoices for a minimum of six years for Canada Revenue Agency and FINTRAC review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as a licensed account-holding vehicle; MEDIUM as a payment gateway only. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are not a recognized chartered bank category in Canada and cannot hold deposit accounts for corporate treasury without being registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). In Quebec, an EMI would also need recognition under the Money-Services Business Act (chapter M-30.1) and, if it lends or takes deposits, a Quebec financial services licence. Products such as Wise Business or Payoneer can be used as borderless pay-out channels, but settlement risk is borne by the corporate treasury and their balances are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance. For the target e-commerce business, the most compliant design is to use a tier-1 Canadian bank as the master treasury account and route cash inflows through PCI-compliant cross-border payment providers (Stripe, PayPal) that fund into that account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder loans and equity injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 messages through a correspondent bank to the Quebec operational account or capital escrow account held at a Schedule I bank. The wire should reference Canada's interbank payment clearing code (the 9-digit Canadian bank code and 5-digit institution/transit number) or use the Canadian route on SWIFT (CA). Inbound funds require the following: SWIFT purpose code TRFR (for transfers not related to trade) or used 'PAYMENT OF SHARE CAPITAL' in field 70; supporting remittance letter; and for amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC's Large Cash Transaction Report rules apply only to cash, but international electronic funds transfer (ETFs) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by financial intermediaries to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. CRS/FATCA notification of account holder foreign tax status may be required.For outbound payment to overseas suppliers of maternity and infant commodities, the appropriate clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers against invoice, (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) at sight or deferred payment for substantial purchase orders, and (iii) Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A) as a lower-cost alternative for shipments of high-value goods enumerated under HS Chapter 96 or similar. Open account with wire is acceptable for established suppliers. Outbound wires must include the intended trade-purpose descriptor, and the underlying commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin (if required) must be retained for CRA and customs verification. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW as a licensed account-holding vehicle; MEDIUM as a payment gateway only. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are not a recognized chartered bank category in Canada and cannot hold deposit accounts for corporate treasury without being registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). In Quebec, an EMI would also need recognition under the Money-Services Business Act (chapter M-30.1) and, if it lends or takes deposits, a Quebec financial services licence. Products such as Wise Business or Payoneer can be used as borderless pay-out channels, but settlement risk is borne by the corporate treasury and their balances are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance. For the target e-commerce business, the most compliant design is to use a tier-1 Canadian bank as the master treasury account and route cash inflows through PCI-compliant cross-border payment providers (Stripe, PayPal) that fund into that account.
