Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal tax authority; business portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/account-individuals.html). Ontario's portion of HST is administered federally by the CRA; the Ontario Ministry of Finance separately administers provincial non-HST taxes (e.g., Employer Health Tax).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or by using the CRA Business Registration Online service. The application must identify the entity's legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, evidence of corporate existence (e.g., articles of incorporation), registered office or authorized representative in Canada, directors, principal business activity (NAICS 523920 / ISIC 6430), and intended effective date of Canadian commercial activity. The BN is the universal statutory tax identifier; a separate GST/HST program account (RT) may be activated in the same application. If the foreign entity becomes resident in Canada for tax purposes, the BN also anchors income tax, payroll, and other program accounts. An Ontario extra-provincial licence under the Ontario Business Corporations Act may additionally be required before carrying on business through an Ontario presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not permit a check-the-box classification election for corporations; a Canadian Business Corporation is a person taxable at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections include: subsection 216(1) election for a non-resident to file an income tax return respecting Canadian rental income; section 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation; section 83(2) capital dividend election. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part VI.1 (ss. 12.6-12.76) operate independently of entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold), after which GST/HST registration is mandatory; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must register unless the small-supplier exception applies or all supplies are zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier, supplies of services performed exclusively outside Canada, and services supplied to a non-resident recipient if they are not related to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property physically in Canada at the time the service is performed. For an Ontario multi-family office, discretionary portfolio advisory services to non-resident private client families may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not attributable to Canadian situs assets. The supplier must demonstrate the export or non-resident service nexus, retain contracts, remittance instructions, SWIFT/custody wire records, and documentary evidence of non-resident status, and file the zero-rated supply in the GST/HST return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency - B13A Export Declaration or equivalent electronic export report)
  • Bill of Lading or other through-carrier transport document evidencing physical export of goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/remittance documentation evidencing payment for the exported goods or services and the non-resident source of funds

Canada does not impose a national real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based tax stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. GST/HST registrants must file periodic returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account when the relevant threshold is met; mandatory electronic filing applies to persons whose taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million per financial year. Electronic records (billing, supporting export documentation) are accepted by CRA if readable and auditable. No digital stamping or government pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

CRA may disallow zero-rating, reassess previous returns, recover net tax refunds and input tax credits with retroactive effect, and impose interest and penalties. Incorrect claims are subject to penalties under sections 285 and 286 of the Excise Tax Act (including gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the net tax or refund misstated), false statement penalties, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion or fraud. Documentary insufficiency or failure to retain export/non-resident evidence within the audit retention period triggers denials and potential clawback of refunds plus statutory interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both capital and current accounts pursuant to IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on capital inflows/outflows, and no advance approval requirements for profit or dividend remittance. The principal frictions are non-tax regulatory: strict AML/KYC, OFAC/UN/Canada sanctions screening, FATCA and CRS reporting, and FINTRAC obligations for cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more. For non-resident investors, Canadian income tax on remitted dividends/interest applies under Part XIII at a 25% statutory rate, often treaty-reduced. Equity capital injections into the MFO are not taxable in Canada, but must be supported by a full corporate KYC package and recorded for investment Canada/tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts and repatriated at the exporter's discretion. Nevertheless, Canadian residents must comply with prescribed foreign-property reporting (Form T1135) when the aggregate cost of foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000, and any physical movement of currency or monetary instruments into or out of Canada in excess of CAD 10,000 must be declared to CBSA/FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no dedicated foreign exchange administration — exchange-control and compliance duties are distributed among OSFI (prudential supervision), FINTRAC (AML/CFT reporting), Global Affairs Canada (economic sanctions), and CBSA (cross-border currency declaration).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium (35/100) for primary treasury operations; acceptable only as an ancillary FX conversion or disbursal channel. Legal constraints: an offshore digital EMI cannot hold client funds or act as a settlement bank in Canada without being registered as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and licensed under Ontario's Money Service Business Act administered by FSRA. An unregulated EMI would be deemed to carry on unauthorized deposit-taking or foreign-remittance business in Canada. Any use requires full due diligence, proof of home-jurisdiction authorization, a FINTRAC foreign-MSB registration, and an executed ACH/SWIFT integration with the primary Canadian bank. No custody or client-asset concentration may be placed with an offshore EMI for this high-risk MFO account type.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank directly to the MFO's designated Canadian corporate bank account. The instruction must identify the MFO by full legal name, the receiving bank's SWIFT BIC, Canadian institution number, transit/account number, and include payment reference: 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – [Shareholder/Investor Name]'. The payment purpose should reflect an equity contribution rather than a service fee. Supporting documents required by the receiving bank include a board resolution authorizing the account and issuance, a signed subscription agreement, source-of-funds declaration, and investor KYC/UBO disclosure forms. All transactions are screened against OFAC, the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, and UN sanctions; suspicious or high-volume flows trigger enhanced due diligence and potentially an FINTRAC Suspicious Transaction Report. Equity capital injections are not subject to Canadian withholding tax, but must be recorded in the MFO's audited capital schedule for corporate tax and investment Canada reporting.Trade settlements and disbursements are cleared through: (1) domestic CAD large-value payments via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day CAD clearing; (2) cross-border foreign-currency dispersals via the SWIFT network using tracked MT103s with correspondent banking; (3) commercial letters of credit issued through SWIFT MT700 and MT707 for documentary trade finance. For USD outflows, routing is normally through the receiving U.S. bank's Fedwire/CHIPS network via a Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent. Every outbound disbursement requires dual authorization, an approved investment or trade instruction, sanctions screening of the beneficiary, and retention of the MT103 confirmation as an AML/regulatory record. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million in aggregate value of transactions with non-residents (penalty threshold under subsection 247(4)); documentation must be prepared if transactions exceed this threshold

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Canada; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., OECD BEPS) require genuine economic substance for treaty benefits; shell companies without real business activity may be challenged

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties for gross negligence (50% of understated tax)

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax benefit; potential criminal charges for tax evasion if intent is proven

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax benefits obtained through sham arrangements; travel bans and asset freezes under tax enforcement
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): fines up to CAD 1 million for individuals, CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment)

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement compliance; travel bans and extradition possible
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s.247(4): 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation not provided); interest on reassessed tax; potential double taxation

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they directed or acquiesced to non-compliance; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for willful evasion
