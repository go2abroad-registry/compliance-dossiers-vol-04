Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec — Quebec QST (TVQ), provincial income tax, and payroll source deductions; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) — enterprise legal registration (NEQ). Corporate/registration portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html), CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), and Revenu Québec's 'Mon Entreprise' portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: If the foreign LP carries on business in Québec, register with the REQ (declaratory registration as an extra-provincial limited partnership under An Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises) to obtain the NEQ; a Québec-domiciled LP would be constituted by declaration of registration filed with REQ. Step 2: Register with the CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or the non-resident business registration procedure to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with GST/HST account (RT prefix); the partnership is a 'person' for GST/HST purposes even though fiscally transparent for income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Québec limited partnership ('société en commandite' / LP) is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: no entity-level tax; each partner pays tax on its allocable share (ITA s. 96), and non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source shares subject to Part XIII or Part I. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership itself is a separate 'person'/registrant required to register, collect, and remit, with the economic incidence passing through to the partners. There is no corporate 'opaque' default for an LP; however, Canadian anti-hybrid rules may recharacterize arrangements where the jurisdiction of a partner treats the same LP as taxable at entity level.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to a foreign-owned Québec LP: (i) 'qualifying partnership' GST/HST election under ETA s. 273, and the parallel QST provision, consolidating the partnership and a designated member as a single registrant for filing/ITC purposes; (ii) elective GST/HST and QST registration by a small supplier under ETA s. 240(1) to claim input tax refunds before the mandatory threshold is reached; (iii) non-resident partners' election to be taxed on net Canadian-source income under ITA s. 216 (rental or timber) rather than 25% gross Part XIII withholding; and (iv) no election is available to neutralize hybrid mismatches — ITA s. 18.4 et seq. (enacted 2020) automatically denies deductions or includes amounts where another jurisdiction treats the LP as an opaque entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). A partnership carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST where the value of its worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; Quebec's QST threshold mirrors this (CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Québec). Zero-rated supplies generally count toward the threshold; non-residents apply the threshold to their taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI Part V where (a) the supplier exports the bunker fuel/lubricants as ships' stores as part of the supply and retains prescribed evidence; or (b) the goods are supplied to a non-resident person for delivery to a destination outside Canada. Bunker fuel loaded at a Canadian port onto an oceangoing vessel for consumption during an international voyage is zero-rated as an export/ships' stores supply; lubricants and replenishment supplies similarly qualify if they form part of the vessel's stores for international use. Supplies taking place entirely outside Canadian territorial waters fall outside the GST/HST/QST net (no Canadian tax). Where fuel is sold for domestic (coastal) voyages, the standard 14.975% GST+QST applies unless a provincial rebate applies. The supplier must act with due diligence and retain contemporaneous evidence; CRA may otherwise assess the tax as collected but unremitted.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System, CERS) for goods exported from Canada with a value exceeding CAD 2,000
  • Ocean bill of lading (port-to-port or through B/L) evidencing delivery of the bunker fuel to the named vessel and its intended international voyage/destination
  • Inward remittance evidence (bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank confirmation of receipt of export proceeds; Canadian banks do not issue a separate 'inward remittance certificate', so the banking record serves as the equivalent)
  • Bunker delivery note (BDN) and independent quantity survey document evidencing the marine fuel/lubricant quantity delivered to the vessel as ships' stores
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and INCOTERMS clause evidencing sale to the non-resident ship owner/charterer/operator

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or central invoice pre-clearance exists at the federal level. Invoices under ETA s. 223 must contain prescribed particulars (supplier identity, BN, date, description, tax charged) and may be issued electronically if authentic and legible. Federal GST/HST returns must generally be filed electronically via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE (paper returns restricted); QST returns must be filed electronically via Revenu Québec's transmission portal. Quebec has enacted a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing/e-reporting regime (Revenu Québec's PEAM modernization initiative) scheduled to begin for large businesses in 2026 and broaden to smaller enterprises thereafter; API/EDI exchange with Revenu Québec will be required once the mandate applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; Canada maintains no general exchange-control regime, no requirement to repatriate or convert export proceeds, and no restriction on capital inflows or outflows. Profit remittance is unrestricted; however, for a Quebec limited partnership (CA_LP), allocations of Canadian-source business income to non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) via a T5013 partnership information return and corresponding non-resident T1/T2 filings, while only amounts characterized as interest, royalties, management fees, or certain deemed dividends attract Part XIII withholding at 25% (subject to treaty reduction). Non-resident investors must also comply with the Investment Canada Act notification if non-control acquisitions exceed prescribed asset thresholds (CAD 5 million in 2025 for trade-agreement WTO investors, updated annually); no restrictions exist on dividend or distribution repatriation by the LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds from marine fuel bunkering; receipts may be held in CAD or foreign currency; however, the LP must report foreign-currency accounts on CRA Form T1135 when total foreign-specifie property exceeds CAD 100,000, and FINTRAC requires reporting of all cross-border electronic fund transfers (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more, plus Large Virtual Currency Transaction Reports (LVCTR).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and fiscal agent; foreign exchange administration is otherwise governed by the Minister of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act and the Currency Act; anti-money-laundering surveillance sits with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Quebec entities also face the Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) for securities and payment-services matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low (restricted). A non-Canadian electronic money institution (EMI) cannot act as the primary treasury or settlement account for the Quebec LP unless it holds a FINTRAC Money Services Business (MSB) registration and, if it processes retail payment obligations in final settlement, a registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Quebec's Act respecting payment services (Loi concernant les services de paiement) further requires authorization to offer deposit or electronic-money services in Quebec. A structure using a licensed foreign EMI as a virtual-IBAN cost-optimization overlay on top of a Schedule I bank account is viable; using the EMI as the sole liquidity rail is not compliant for CAD-denominated clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Funds are routed via SWIFT MT103 (with UETR and SWIFT gpi tracking) from the foreign parent or partners into the LP's capital escrow account at a Schedule I bank. Instruction must include the full legal name of the LP (as registered in Quebec), the 19-digit registration number from the Registraire des entreprises, and a purpose narrative 'Capital contribution to limited partnership' with a clear reference for the compliance record. For incoming EFTs over CAD 10,000 the beneficiary bank files the FINTRAC EFT report; ensure the remitter and beneficiary identifiers match KYC records. Use purpose code 'TRD' (trade) or 'CFO' (corporate finance operation) per SWIFT usage guidelines; no MT202 COVER is needed for a same-bank or single-currency transfer, but split payments (MT103 + MT202) require exact beneficiary naming to avoid rejection. The LP should verify that the funds do not originate from a FATF high-risk jurisdiction and, for inbound USD, use CHIPS or FEDWIRE to intermediate in USD then convert to CAD at the bank's spot rate.Clearing rails for bunker fuel purchases and lubricant replenishment use (i) SWIFT MT103 with UETR for standard trade settlement; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600) with MT700/MT707 series for high-value cargo purchases, payable at sight or deferred; (iii) standby letters of credit (ISP98) securing supply contracts and customs/tax guarantees; and (iv) domestic CAD payments cleared through Lynx (high-value) or ACSS (ACH) for smaller disbursements. For USD-denominated settlements, payments clear via CHIPS or FEDWIRE. Supporting documentation must include the commercial invoice, bunker delivery note (BDN), loading port quantity, independent surveyor certificate, bill of lading, and any required ISPS/MARPOL certificates. Since the LP is not a bank, any LC must be issued by a Canadian chartered bank in favor of the bunker supplier, with the LP as applicant under an approved credit facility.Over-the-counter FX forwards (ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Canadian Schedule I bank counterparty; credit support annex under Quebec civil law pledge).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) but only for cross-currency exposure to non-convertible or emerging-market currencies where CAD/USD deliverability is absent.FX swaps (FX swap) and cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for matched funding of USD receivables and CAD payables.Vanilla FX options and barrier options (covered by ISDA/CSA and requiring OSFI-qualified counterparty approval).Listed FX futures and options on a recognized Canadian exchange such as the Montreal Exchange (TMX Group) for liquid CAD/USD and CAD/EUR hedging.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-Low (restricted). A non-Canadian electronic money institution (EMI) cannot act as the primary treasury or settlement account for the Quebec LP unless it holds a FINTRAC Money Services Business (MSB) registration and, if it processes retail payment obligations in final settlement, a registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Quebec's Act respecting payment services (Loi concernant les services de paiement) further requires authorization to offer deposit or electronic-money services in Quebec. A structure using a licensed foreign EMI as a virtual-IBAN cost-optimization overlay on top of a Schedule I bank account is viable; using the EMI as the sole liquidity rail is not compliant for CAD-denominated clearing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15.0% general corporate rate plus Quebec provincial rate of 11.5%, reflecting the 10% federal abatement for Quebec-based income; limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent so the effective rate applies at the partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident partner/shareholder. Treaty-reduced rates generally 5% for a 10% voting shareholder or 15% for portfolio dividends under the Canada-US treaty and other Canadian DTAs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total controlled transactions requiring a local file and master file). CbCR threshold CAD 1,000,000,000 (consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the economic substance doctrine through section 247 of the Income Tax Act, Quebec through section 1007 of the Taxation Act. A bunkering LP must show substantive presence (storage infrastructure, vessels, personnel, invoicing, risk management) in Quebec. A shell conduit without real substance will be denied treaty benefits and re-characterized under GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act subsection 239(1): fine of 50% to 100% of tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years. Quebec Taxation Act section 1069: fine of 50% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2): 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners personally liable for unremitted withholdings under subsection 227.1(1). Civil restitution orders, travel bans under tax enforcement provisions, and imprisonment for failure to deduct or remit Part XIII taxes.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment: denial of treaty benefits; 25% Part XIII withholding on amounts paid; 25% gross negligence penalty; interest at prescribed rates.

Director personal liability: Jeopardy orders freezing assets, travel bans, seizure of personal bank accounts, joint and several liability for withholding obligations, and possible extradition for tax crimes under mutual legal assistance treaties.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA monetary penalties up to CAD 105,000 for individuals and CAD 525,000 for corporations/partnerships; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31.

Director personal liability: Personal prosecution for missing AML compliance program; civil forfeiture of personal assets; travel bans; imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for organized crime connection).
Environmental / Maritime Licensing

Statutory sanction: Quebec Environment Quality Act fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for clandestine discharge; Transport Canada civil penalties up to CAD 250,000 plus cleanup costs.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for cleanup costs under Environment Quality Act unless due diligence proven; travel bans possible pending criminal trial; imprisonment for wilful spills.
