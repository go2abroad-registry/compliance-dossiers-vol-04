Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) with Business Registration Online portal (canada.ca/business-registration); Revenu Québec administers QST and Quebec provincial income tax via the CliquezAffaires portal (soutien.revenuquebec.ca)

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Canadian corporation must first be incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act. Tax registration is then completed online through CRA Business Registration Online to obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) and the applicable GST/HST program account (RT account for regular registrant). For Quebec, Revenu Québec must be separately registered for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) through the CliquezAffaires portal, which issues a Quebec file number linked to the BN. The application typically requires the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, notice of registered office, directors' information, and proof of a Canadian address for service or a designated agent. No minimum capital requirement applies; non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. A Business Corporation is a statutory separate taxpayer resident in Canada and is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income. Available elections include foreign tax credit elections, foreign affiliate active business surplus elections, tax treaty tie-breaker relief, transfer pricing documentation elections, and hybrid mismatch alignment under Canadian implementation of BEPS Action 2. No pass-through election is available for a CBCA/QBCA corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable revenues (small-supplier threshold) for both GST/HST and QST; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded. Remote digital services supplied to Quebec consumers may trigger separate registration obligations for non-resident vendors under Quebec's electronic services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under GST/HST Schedule VI Part V for a supply of a service made to a non-resident recipient where the service is not in connection with real property situated in Canada, is not in connection with tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not performed in the course of a business of providing services to persons in Canada. For medical clinic EMR SaaS supplied electronically to foreign clinics, zero-rating generally applies if the subscription is invoiced to and paid by the non-resident clinic, and the software is delivered and consumed outside Canada. QST zero-rating rules under the Quebec Taxation Act mirror the GST/HST export criteria for services supplied to non-residents.

  • Executed SaaS subscription agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the foreign jurisdiction
  • Commercial invoice issued in CAD or agreed foreign currency referencing the zero-rated export supply
  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13) if any tangible medium or physical goods are included in the supply; not required for pure cloud-delivered SaaS
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill for any physical component of the export
  • Bank wire transfer confirmation, SWIFT record, or Stripe Billing payout statement evidencing receipt of consideration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Canadian receiving bank or a corresponding bank statement
  • Foreign recipient business registration extract or tax identification number confirming non-resident status
  • Service delivery evidence such as server logs, client access logs, or IP address records demonstrating offshore use

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, or digital stamping requirement currently applies in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and may be issued through PEPPOL-compliant platforms. Quebec is progressively introducing mandatory electronic invoicing for B2B and B2G transactions based on the PEPPOL model; however, at present no API clearance or fiscal authorization seal is required. Invoices must satisfy the statutory GST/HST/QST content requirements and the taxpayer must retain a complete audit trail.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and reassess the supply at the applicable GST/HST and QST rates plus interest where the required export documentation is not contemporaneous and sufficient. False statements, wilful misrepresentation, or inadequate records can trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act, including gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the amount claimed, interest, and potential criminal prosecution. Retroactive clawbacks and reassessments can cover statutory limitation periods, which may be extended in cases of neglect, misrepresentation, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with zero exchange-control filing, approval, or repatriation screening. The Canadian dollar is unrestricted under IMF Article VIII. Profit remittances, dividends, intercompany royalties, and liquidation proceeds may flow without Bank of Canada or OSFI authorization; taxation at source applies under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25% non-resident withholding, typically reduced to 5%-15% under treaty, e.g., Article X of the Canada-US Tax Convention), and Revenu Quebec QST obligations continue to apply. For this EMR SaaS entity, the sole constraint is the Investment Canada Act review threshold: a direct acquisition of control of a Canadian SaaS business by a WTO investor in 2024 requires prior-notification review only above CAD 1.336 billion of enterprise value, so a routine parent-equity injection into CA_CORP triggers no regulatory approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. The Quebec EMR software vendor may retain USD/EUR subscription receivables in operating accounts, convert them at its treasury discretion, or keep balances offshore without reporting to the Bank of Canada. All cross-border intercompany licensing arrangements must, however, satisfy the arm's-length and transfer-pricing rules of Section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and the associated documentation must be available for CRA and Revenu Quebec audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted by the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions and foreign-exchange settlement by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); anti-money-laundering and financial-intelligence supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA, S.C. 2000, c. 17); retail payment-activity licensing by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 157). Canada maintains no exchange-control board; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and there is no sovereign FX licensing agency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The capital injection should be wired from the parent treasury to the CA_CORP CAD-denominated capital escrow account via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or ISO 20022 pacs.008, with field 70 stating: 'INWARD DIRECT INVESTMENT, EQUITY CONTRIBUTION, OWNERSHIP CAPITAL, SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]'. The receiving Schedule I bank will map the flow under Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments direct-investment classification (inward equity) and will register the beneficial ownership and ISC-record evidence in its FATCA/CRS file, not as a reportable tax event. The bank's credit and compliance teams require: certified board resolution; signed subscription agreement; parent audited financial statements; Revenu Quebec GST/QST registration; and confirmation of the 'New Business' industry code (NAICS 511210) used in the Canadian corporate profile. The wire settles over the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day availability in Canadian funds; USD or EUR parent funds are converted at the bank's standing order rate, then hedged per the instruments below.
