Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 20,000 (20%) must be paid up before registration. The paid-up amount may be held in a blocked bank escrow account until the Commercial Register entry is made, and the bank confirmation must be filed with the application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance (Feuerpolizei) for office and technical rooms; structural load inspection for server racks exceeding 5 kN/m² floor loading; occupational health and safety checks under the Labour Act and ArGV 3, including workplace lighting, ergonomic workstation assessment, and accident insurance coverage through Suva or another approved insurer.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 20,000 (20%) must be paid up before registration. The paid-up amount may be held in a blocked bank escrow account until the Commercial Register entry is made, and the bank confirmation must be filed with the application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/AFC/FTA) in Berne; cantonal tax administrations; corporate and VAT registrations are handled via the Federal Tax Administration's ePortal (www.eportal.ch) and the Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) for UID issuance.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG), the statutory identification is the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID), issued by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office after entry into the Commercial Register (canton) and published on Zefix. The UID automatically serves as the VAT identifier for Swiss VAT purposes (MWSTID/TVA). For foreign entities without a Swiss registered office, registration for Swiss VAT is required when carrying out taxable supplies in Switzerland and can be performed electronically through the FTA ePortal. Tax registration steps: (1) incorporate the AG with local commercial registrar; (2) obtain UID from Zefix; (3) if taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed CHF 100,000, electronically submit Form 0810 (VAT registration questionnaire) to the Federal Tax Administration through ePortal; (4) designate a Swiss VAT-collecting agent (representative) if no branch or residence in Switzerland; (5) await validation and issuance of MWSTID, and register for use of the e-billing/online portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss federal and cantonal tax law does not provide a check-the-box election. The AG is always taxed at entity level (corporate income tax on profit and cantonal/communal taxes on profit and equity after the federal and cantonal tax harmonization). Equity tax (capital tax) is assessed at cantonal level and is not avoidable by entity classification election. A Swiss group might elect for 'Status of the fiscal year' (fiscal period) or group taxation ('Steuerliche Dekonsolidierung/Organschaft') only for certain cantonal and federal purposes, notably carrying forward losses and allocating consolidated profit; no hybrid mismatch check-the-box elections are possible. For foreign hybrid instruments or entities, Switzerland applies transparent treatment under Swiss law; entity-level elections are not available. The AG must file its statutory and tax accounts in CHF, using Swiss GAAP or IFRS for accounting purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Turnover threshold for VAT registration: CHF 100,000 per year (Art. 10 para 2 (a) LTVA, since 1 January 2019) where taxable supplies are made in Switzerland or from abroad into Switzerland; foreign supplies of electronic services are generally covered by a separate rule (Art. 107 para 1 LTVA) requiring VAT registration without the CHF 100,000 de minimis when supplied to Swiss private individuals, unless the supplier uses the 'simplified registration' procedure for electronically supplied services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

In Switzerland, supplies of goods and services to foreign destinations can be zero-rated (0% VAT) if the goods are physically exported and the supplier retains documentary proof, or if the services are supplied to a foreign business or foreign recipient at a place of supply outside Switzerland and meet the conditions of Art. 23 para 4 LTVA and the OLTVA (VAT Ordinance). For an esports community platform, zero-rating is commonly available for: (1) electronically supplied tournament services, matchmaking subscriptions, virtual goods, and advertising services to foreign players/partners where the recipient is a foreign business (B2B) or the private recipient is established in a foreign country; (2) export of physical esports merchandise; (3) ancillary services (e.g., tournament organization) directly related to exported goods. Required proof: the supplier must be able to demonstrate the ultimate recipient's business location abroad via a Swiss or EU VAT identification number, contracts, and invoice, and demonstrate actual use of the service abroad. Where the platform provides services to Swiss residents, Swiss VAT at the standard rate (currently 8.1%) applies unless a lower rate is indicated.

  • Swiss Export Customs Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung/e-dec) with electronic filing reference (e-dec number)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (transport document) confirming physical export of goods
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign customer (B2B) with full description and VAT ID
  • Import certificate from destination country when required (e.g., for precious metals, pharmaceuticals)

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary. The mandatory electronic e-invoice standard (eBill and the QR-bill invoice standard) applies to B2G transactions as of 2023, but there is no central tax-authority clearance of every invoice. For VAT purposes, all invoices must satisfy the statutory content requirements of Art. 26 LTVA, including the MWSTID/TVA identification, date, supplier and recipient names, nature and quantity of supply, consideration, and tax amount. The Federal Tax Administration offers an online 'VAT invoice generator' but does not require prior approval. For export documentation, e-dec (electronic customs declaration) and statcards are used.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may conduct targeted audits of zero-rated supplies for up to five years (ordinary limitation), and up to ten years post-assessment where intentional tax evasion is discovered. Penalties for false zero-rating or claim of VAT refund without proper export substantiation: (a) tax payable is reinstated with default interest at up to 5% per annum; (b) a penalty of up to three times the evaded tax for intentional tax evasion (Art. 174 para 1 LTVA); (c) negligence fines up to twice the evaded tax (Art. 175 LTVA); (d) administrative fines up to CHF 10,000 per violation for formal non-compliance; (e) criminal prosecution for tax fraud, counterfeiting, or false documentation. The FTA also denies refund claims and imposes clawback of previously refunded amounts. Internal compliance guidance requires permanent retention of all export and remittance documentation for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both capital and current account. Switzerland maintains no foreign exchange controls, no mandatory central bank approval for current payments, no prior licensing for profit remittance, and no prohibition on holding, converting, or transferring foreign currency. A Swiss AG may freely receive revenue from esports matchmaking, tournament fees, subscription services, and prize-pool settlements in EUR, USD, or other currencies, and may remit dividends, loan repayments, service fees, and royalties abroad. The only meaningful friction is Swiss anticipatory tax of 35% on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, which is generally refundable in full or in part under an applicable double taxation agreement; this is a withholding tax matter and not an FX convertibility or capital-control restriction. Switzerland does not in practice require any balance-of-payments licensing even for large cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law and the Swiss National Bank impose no statutory timeline for repatriation of export or service proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio to CHF, and no surrender requirement. Proceeds related to the esports platform, whether received as SWIFT credits, card settlements, or via third-party payment gateways such as Stripe and PayPal, remain freely disposable in the treasury account. The SNB retains only statistical balance-of-payments reporting powers, which do not require conversion, escrow, or repatriation of funds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque nationale suisse, Banca nazionale svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary receivables-collection rail, but Low as the sole treasury banking channel. Non-Swiss EMIs and neobanks cannot participate in Swiss SIC/euroSIC clearing and cannot issue domestic CHF IBANs; they must settle indirectly through Swiss or EU correspondent banks. Regulatory constraint: a Swiss AG that accumulates player funds or merchant settlement proceeds on a foreign EMI may be treated as accepting deposits from the public without FINMA authorization, contrary to Article 1 of the Swiss Banking Act, and may also create insolvency-exposure risk outside the Swiss deposit-protection framework. EU or UK EMI licenses are not passportable into Switzerland. The legally robust route is to operate a Swiss tier-1 operational account as the treasury anchor while using Stripe and PayPal solely as acquiring rails settling into that Swiss account; the platform should not rely on any offshore EMI for liquidity custody or for serving as the account of record.

Defense evidence: Swiss Commercial Register excerpt, notarized and apostilled passport and identity documents, full ownership-chain chart, statutory declaration of beneficial ownership under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act, 12 months of bank statements and tax returns for each UBO, negative sanctions/media/PEP screening report from a regulated screening provider, and a Swiss legal opinion confirming the corporate ownership structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: foreign shareholders must transmit equity contributions by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA shareholders) into the Swiss operational or capital account, denominated in CHF, EUR, or USD. The payment instructions should include the ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC (Capital Payment) and remittance text identifying the contribution as 'Einlage zur Erhöhung des Aktienkapitals der CH_AG'. The receiving Swiss bank applies AMLA-mandated payer screening and source-of-funds verification before the funds are released from any capital-blocking confirmation. For incorporation and capital increases, the Swiss Commercial Register requires a notarized bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) that the minimum statutory capital is paid up and at the free disposal of the board. No Swiss National Bank approval or foreign-exchange permit is required for any amount, but amounts above CHF 10,000 will require documentary source-of-funds evidence. Crypto-asset contributions should be converted to fiat through a FINMA-supervised exchange before routing to the Swiss account; direct token transfers into a corporate bank account are operationally disfavored and may trigger extended due diligence.Clearing rails: (i) domestic and cross-border euro payments through SIC, euroSIC, and Swiss Instant Payments (ISO 20022 credit transfers) for recurring prize payouts, supplier disbursements, and intra-group treasury flows; (ii) cross-border commercial settlements through SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking, referencing suitable purpose codes such as TRAD, ROYA, or RENT and supported by invoices, tournament-results documentation, or licensing agreements; (iii) commercial and standby letters of credit issued through SWIFT MT700/MT799 for large media-rights acquisitions, broadcasting underwriting, and strategic tournament infrastructure contracts; and (iv) card and API acquiring rails, including Stripe and PayPal, which settle through European banking partners into the Swiss treasury account via SEPA or SWIFT. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on taxable net profit (statutory rate on profit after tax; equivalent to approximately 11.9% on pre-tax profit), plus cantonal/municipal net profit tax of approximately 4.5% to 17.5% depending on canton; in Zug the combined ordinary effective rate is approximately 14.6%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross Swiss withholding tax on dividends distributed by the Aktiengesellschaft under Article 4 VStG; non-residents may obtain treaty relief typically to 15% portfolio rate, 5% substantial participation rate (at least 10% holding), or 0% under aligned parent-subsidiary treaty provisions, subject to the beneficial ownership condition and satisfaction of economic substance requirements

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland imposes no statutory monetary threshold that mandates the automatic filing of a Local File or Master File; however, documentation obligations arise on request during assessment or audit under Articles 58 and 175 DBG and Article 96 VStG, and the ESTV expects contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation whenever controlled transactions exist; the mandatory country-by-country reporting threshold is CHF 900,000,000 consolidated group revenue per Article 129a DBG, filed by the ultimate parent and exchanged automatically; taxpayers below that threshold are still required to retain sufficient arm's-length benchmarking documentation to substantiate all intercompany pricing on examination

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has not enacted the BRC/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, substance is enforced through (1) Swiss GAAR under Article 56 DBG which disregards constructions lacking genuine economic purpose, (2) Article 21a VStG anti-abuse rules denying withholding tax and treaty relief to shell and letter-box companies, (3) the principal purpose test of the Multilateral Instrument, and (4) specific substance requirements for administrative, holding and domiciliary companies under ESTV circular practice; the Zug AG must maintain its registered seat, local statutory books, an operating bank account, locally resident directors with real decision-making authority, and adequate personnel and premises for the esports tournament platform activity to preserve the beneficial effective tax rate and to guarantee treaty access and dividend WHT reduction

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud — intentional false declarations

Statutory sanction: Federal judicial tax fraud under Article 186 DBG: fine up to three times the evaded federal tax plus principal tax due with 4% to 5% default interest, and imprisonment up to three years; cantonal fraud penalties replicate at 100% to 300% of evaded tax; the assessment limitation period extends to ten years for fraudulent concealment and unpaid tax claims remain enforceable against the AG's assets for fifteen years; no amnesty applies

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is authorized under Article 754 CO where directors intentionally or negligently cause tax damage; civil restitution claims are pursued jointly and severally against all board members; criminal convictions under Article 186 DBG expose resident and non-resident directors to arrest warrants, travel bans, and extradition under Switzerland's mutual legal assistance framework; the cantonal tax collection office may order an exit block against directors who fail to secure unpaid assessed corporate tax
Withholding Tax Default and Undeclared Dividend Remittances

Statutory sanction: Article 67 VStG penalty of up to ten times the unpaid withholding tax plus 5% arrears interest from the due date; the AG's claim to repayment rights is forfeited and the unpaid WHT becomes a secured federal claim enforceable by asset attachment; directors who signed the distribution minutes are subject to direct liability notices

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated tax representative are personally liable under Articles 12 and 67 VStG even where the AG is insolvent; the ESTV routinely issues personal payment orders and opens criminal proceedings against at least one locally resident director; personal bank accounts and real property of directors are seizable and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment up to three years is imposed for intentional non-collection of withholding tax
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Artificial Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Primary reassessment under Article 58 DBG within the seven-to-ten-year reassessment window: TP adjustment increases taxable profit, attracts constructive dividend taxation at the 35% WHT level and triggers the 8.5% federal plus Zug cantonal taxes at the denied rates; negligence surcharge up to CHF 10,000 under Article 175 DBG; fraud enhancements apply up to three times the reassessed tax; the MLI principal purpose test allows treaty benefits denial with retroactive effect

Director personal liability: Board members and CFOs are personally liable under Article 754 CO for TP adjustment damages, including penalties and arrears interest; foreign parent directors are reachable through administrative assistance and mutual legal assistance treaties; Switzerland permits civil seizure of directors' personal assets where the AG is structured as a profit-shifting conduit; criminal conviction as accomplices under Article 186 DBG carries imprisonment up to three years
AML and Shell Company Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Article 11 AMLA: imprisonment up to three years and a fine up to CHF 500,000 for intentional violation of due diligence, reporting, and record-keeping duties; FINMA notices the Federal Tax Administration and treats the AG as non-substantive, triggering withdrawal of payment processor accounts and bank facilities; the AG may be struck from the commercial register following insolvency

Director personal liability: Directors who control payment settlement or sign platform agreements bear criminal liability independently of the corporate entity; the five-year AMLA prescription does not shield board members; Swiss financial institutions blacklist involved directors, and compliance officers may be personally banned from holding regulated banking roles; civil restitution to defrauded stakeholders and tax authorities is enforced against directors' personal estates
Regulatory Contamination from Gambling and Chance-Based Tournament Mechanics

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to CHF 500,000, disgorgement of gaming revenue, and closure orders from the Cantonal Lottery and Betting Commission; tax consequences include disallowance of prize expense deductions, full taxation of undeclared prize pools, revocation of the reduced Zug cantonal rate for the relevant assessment period, and retroactive re-assessment with 5% interest

Director personal liability: Directors and tournament organizers face criminal responsibility under the Money Games Act, with imprisonment up to three years for organized unlicensed gaming; repeated violations are treated as commercial fraud, enabling civil claims by participant losers against directors' personal assets; a criminal conviction additionally voids directors' suitability under Swiss corporate law and precludes the AG from obtaining future cantonal licensing
