Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Business Number (BN) registration via Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for the 8% Ontario component of HST, administered federally; no separate Ontario VAT filing). Partnerships and non-residents file T5013 via CRA's Internet File Transfer or approved software.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the Ontario LP is 'carrying on business in Canada' — with a registered LP office, Canadian bank account, and EMR SaaS contracts, the CRA will normally treat the LP as carrying on business, making it eligible and generally required to register for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed the threshold. Step 2: Obtain a statutory TIN by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' (paper) or using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); a non-resident partner/agent can file RC1 electronically; the LP receives a 9-digit BN with an 'RT' program account (e.g., BN RT0001) for GST/HST. Step 3: Simultaneously or later register the extra-provincial limited partnership declaration via the Ontario Business Registry (one-window filing under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act). Step 4: For GST/HST, complete program account RT, declaring estimated Canadian and worldwide taxable supplies, reason for registration (compulsory vs voluntary), and effective registration date; voluntary registration is available from the date of approval. Step 5: File T5013 partnership information return annually (designated partner responsibility) and provide T5013 slips to each partner; the BN is that of the partnership, not the partners. Step 6: Non-resident partners without Canadian presence must appoint a resident/non-resident representative through CRA 'Represent a Client' (Form RC59) for GST/HST and income-tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian limited partnership is a flow-through vehicle for income tax: the LP computes income at the partnership level (T5013) but pays no income tax; each partner is liable on its allocable share. For GST/HST, the LP itself is a separate 'person' and a registrant required to charge and remit HST on its taxable supplies. No territorial tax-exempt zone applies; Canadian-source income from the SaaS business is taxable to resident and non-resident partners under Part I of the Income Tax Act (and Part XIII for passive/non-business income, if applicable).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act (ITA); an Ontario LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Available elections and regimes: (i) GST/HST election under ETA s. 273(1) designating a Canadian representative for non-resident partners; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a Canadian return on net rental income (not applicable to active SaaS business); (iii) ETA s. 156 election between closely related group members if the LP sells to a related Canadian controlled corporation (would make supplies deemed nil consideration); (iv) Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 20.2, 94B, in force for payments made after July 1, 2023) which recharacterize or deny deductions where a payment to a US LLC treated as opaque in the US but transparent in Canada (or vice versa) produces deduction-without-inclusion — an LP with US partners must test each cross-border payment against these rules; (v) partnership-level election under ITA s. 96(3) is limited to pension-plan partnerships and is not available here. GST/HST registration is elective below the $30,000 threshold (ETA s. 240(2.1)).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); a person making taxable supplies (including cross-border SaaS delivered to Canada) exceeds the threshold when, in a single calendar quarter over the trailing four quarters, worldwide taxable supplies exceed $30,000 CAD, making GST/HST registration immediately mandatory. For non-residents without a Canadian permanent establishment, CRA's administration (GST/HST Info Sheet GI-157) generally requires registration where the non-resident is carrying on business in Canada or is a distribution-platform operator under the July 1, 2024 digital-economy rules; otherwise voluntary registration applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

EMR medical-clinic workflow software delivered as SaaS is characterized by CRA as a supply of a service (electronic commerce supply, CRA GST/HST Policy Statement P-226). A service supplied to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, s. 7 of the ETA provided: (a) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of the supply; (b) the service is not rendered to a person in Canada (i.e., the clinic users are physically located outside Canada); (c) the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada (integration, data hosting and support must be performed offshore or in the cloud); and (d) the vendor does not acquire the service for consumption/use in Canada. If the non-resident clinic is instead GST/HST-registered and acquires the EMR service exclusively for use in a Canadian commercial activity, the supply is taxable (not zero-rated) — place-of-supply rules therefore require the LP to capture each client's GST registration status. The zero-rating applies regardless of the fact that the software publisher holds an Ontario statutory licence; NAICS classification (511210) is irrelevant for GST zero-rating determinations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) — required only if EMR software is exported on physical media/hardware (data-centre appliance); for pure cloud SaaS no B13A exists and no customs clearance is required.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — evidence of physical export only; for SaaS substitute technical delivery logs (CDN/server logs, IP origin of clinic users) proving the service is consumed outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank FX conversion/CAD settlement statement or SWIFT MT103 evidencing non-resident clinic payments; accepted as 'consideration received' evidence by the CRA for export substantiation.
  • Executed EMR SaaS master subscription agreement and order forms — showing non-resident counterparty status, absence of a Canadian establishment of the clinic, and offshore data residency.
  • CRA BN/GST-HST registration certificate and client non-resident documentation (foreign incorporation certificate or foreign VAT/GST registration number) retained in the vendor's audit file.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-authorization of invoices exists in Canada. CRA requires that invoices for taxable supplies contain specified information (ETA s. 3 of Schedule I — seller name, BN, date, description, HST payable), but accepts any format (PDF, EDI, XML, e-commerce receipt). Canada operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing network (PDX) based on the Peppol framework (launched 2024); some federal government buyers may require PDX-compliant invoicing, but this is a procurement condition, not a tax clearance. For EMR SaaS delivered through Stripe/auto-billing, the invoice generated by the Stripe Billing record must be programmed to show the 13% HST for Canadian clients at ON rates and 0% for qualifying export clients, with the BN of the LP; digital records must be retained for 6 years under ETA s. 286.

Canada has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline for GST/HST export zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark reflects CRA's documentary expectation that consideration be received and matched to the export supply within six months. If the CRA audits and disallows zero-rating, the supplier is assessed the full 13% HST (plus provincial component) on the gross invoice amount, with prescribed interest compounded daily from the date the tax should have been remitted; the assessment window is 4 years from the filing/waiver date or 8 years for non-arm's length matters, and is unlimited where misrepresentation (including erroneous export claims) is attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default. Additional penalties: late/insufficient GST/HST remittance penalty of 5% of net tax plus 1% per month (up to 12 months) under ETA s. 280; false statements or omissions penalty under ETA s. 285 of the greater of CAD 25,000 and 25% of the amount assessed (gross negligence); and, where a professional prepares the export substantiation, liability under ITA s. 163.2 for penalties of up to CAD 100,000+. Relief (cancellation of penalties/interest) under the Fair Taxpayer Provisions (ETA ss. 281.1/281.11) is available only where the failure arises from circumstances beyond the taxpayer's control, not for unsubstantiated zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada grants unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account items. There is no central-bank approval, surrender requirement, or differentiated FX regime for a medical-clinic EMR SaaS exporter. Current-account flows (licence fees, subscription services, intercompany software royalties) are freely receivable and payable; capital-account flows (LP capital contributions, non-resident partner distributions, redemption proceeds) are also unrestricted at the FX layer. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: section 212 withholding tax under the Canadian Income Tax Act applies to amounts deemed to be interest, dividends, rent, or royalties paid to non-resident partners; treaty relief is available if NR301/NR303 forms are filed and beneficial-ownership documentation is maintained. PIPEDA and Ontario PHIPA data-privacy restrictions do not block currency conversion, but they may affect the routing, hosting, and contractual treatment of medical data.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Exporters, including EMR SaaS entities, may retain foreign currency in foreign-currency accounts, invoice in USD/EUR, and repatriate funds at their own discretion. FINTRAC obligations require Canadian financial institutions to report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and to keep records for large transactions over CAD 10,000; these are reporting thresholds, not surrender or conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential bank supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); financial-transaction monitoring is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a separately designated foreign-exchange control authority or exchange-approval clearing house.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not issue 'EMI' licences analogous to the European Union; non-bank payment service providers must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act is fully in force, obtain registration as retail payment activity providers with the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs may be used only as limited collection/wallet rails and are not a substitute for a Canadian tier-1 bank account for a medical-clinic EMR SaaS LP. Principal legal constraints include: absence of CDIC deposit insurance; inability to settle directly through Lynx or ACSS unless licensed; uncertainty regarding protected health data; and restricted access to commercial credit and FX advisory services. Recommended use: backup collection rail or low-value FX disbursement tool after separate legal and privacy review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors fund the Ontario LP via an international SWIFT MT103 wire. Required tags include ordering customer (50K), beneficiary LP and account (59), beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC (57C), and remittance information (70) clearly reading 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION UNDER CA ON LP AGREEMENT' or a bank-approved equivalent. Canada has no mandatory national FX purpose-code regime, but if an intermediary bank requires a purpose code, the code must be pre-agreed with the Canadian receiving bank and must be consistent with the stated capital-contribution purpose. Canada does not issue 'EMI' licences analogous to the European Union; non-bank payment service providers must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act is fully in force, obtain registration as retail payment activity providers with the Bank of Canada.
