Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and customs-related tax administration; Quebec: Revenu Québec for Quebec corporate income tax and QST. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec Mon dossier entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated federal/provincial business corporation: (1) file articles of incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through Corporations Canada or by filing the Initial Return with CRA; (3) establish CRA program accounts for corporate income tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), payroll (RP0001), and import/export (RM0001) using Form RC1 or online via Represent a Client; (4) register with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial remittance accounts using the corresponding Revenu Québec registration form or the CRA/Revenu Québec coordinated one-step registration. Foreign entities not yet incorporated in Canada may register for a non-resident business number and program accounts by filing Form RC1 and may be required to provide a Canadian representative for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate tax resident entity taxed in Canada on worldwide income when it is resident in Canada under common law or deemed resident under the Income Tax Act; no pass-through classification is available; Quebec provincial income tax is imposed on taxable income allocable to Quebec, and non-resident shareholders are not subject to deemed Canadian tax classification merely by reason of shareholding. — For a newly incorporated federal/provincial business corporation: (1) file articles of incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through Corporations Canada or by filing the Initial Return with CRA; (3) establish CRA program accounts for corporate income tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), payroll (RP0001), and import/export (RM0001) using Form RC1 or online via Represent a Client; (4) register with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial remittance accounts using the corresponding Revenu Québec registration form or the CRA/Revenu Québec coordinated one-step registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate tax resident entity taxed in Canada on worldwide income when it is resident in Canada under common law or deemed resident under the Income Tax Act; no pass-through classification is available; Quebec provincial income tax is imposed on taxable income allocable to Quebec, and non-resident shareholders are not subject to deemed Canadian tax classification merely by reason of shareholding.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canadian tax law. A Canadian corporation cannot elect pass-through status; entity classification is determined by the Income Tax Act and corporate law. Relevant elections for cross-border structures include the capital dividend election under subsection 83(2) of the Income Tax Act and the section 216 election for non-residents earning Canadian rental income. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act apply statutorily without an elective regime and may deny deductions or require inclusion for payments subject to hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; same threshold applies for GST/HST registration and Quebec QST registration for persons carrying on business in Quebec, subject to special exemptions for taxi, ride-sharing, and other regulated sectors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies when the supplier exports tangible personal property as part of the supply, export occurs within specified timelines (generally within 60 days after delivery or, if consideration is due before delivery, within 60 days after the due date), and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident person, including international trade-remedy legal advisory and anti-dumping defense consulting, are zero-rated if provided to a non-resident and not acquired for use exclusively in Canada. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the goods left Canada or the service was performed outside Canada, that payment is from a non-resident source, and that title and risk passed outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit/foreign exchange receipt evidence
  • Commercial Invoice and signed contract for the export supply
  • Customs broker or freight forwarder export records demonstrating physical movement of goods

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) system. There is no mandatory digital stamping of invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted and must be supported by an audit trail under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act. E-filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for most registrants and Revenu Québec requires electronic remittance and filing of QST returns for businesses above prescribed thresholds; export transactions must be transmitted electronically to the CBSA where an export declaration or eManifest is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec will deny zero-rating if export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or fails to evidence physical export or non-resident service recipient. Reassessment can claw back uncollected GST and QST plus interest; civil penalties may include the gross negligence penalty under the Excise Tax Act equal to a substantial percentage of understated tax, and Quebec may impose analogous provincial penalties. Retrospective audit periods may be extended beyond the normal reassessment period in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims can lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both capital and current account. Canada imposes no exchange controls, no prior-approval regime, no profit-remittance license, and no restriction on dividend, royalty, or service-fee flows to non-resident shareholders under the Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, or the Bank of Canada Act. Dividends paid by a Quebec business corporation to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at a 25% statutory rate, generally reduced to 5% for a U.S. corporate shareholder under the Canada-United States Tax Convention and to 5-15% under other tax treaties. Professional consulting fees in the ISIC 7490 trade-remedy defense scope constitute treaty-protected business profits absent a Canadian permanent establishment, and no exchange-control hurdle impedes retainer invoicing in CAD, USD, EUR, or any other freely convertible currency. The principal compliance burdens are documentary: FINTRAC reporting for any wire transfer at or above CAD 10,000, sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, beneficial-ownership disclosure at account onboarding, and Form T1135 reporting for foreign investment property exceeding CAD 100,000. The CAD floats freely; no convertibility restriction, profit-blockade mechanism, or capital-repatriation quota exists for this corporate profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandated conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for service revenue, export receipts, or any other foreign-currency earnings. A Canadian exporter or consulting firm may lawfully retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts. For Income Tax Act purposes, foreign-currency revenue must be translated into CAD under the functional-currency rules of section 261 or at the Bank of Canada published exchange rate; unrealized and realized foreign-exchange gains or losses are taxable or deductible under section 39. Foreign affiliates and foreign investment property exceeding CAD 100,000 aggregate cost must be reported annually on Form T1135. No approval from the Bank of Canada or the Department of Finance is required for conversion, disposal, or transfer of export or consulting receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as the central monetary and financial-system stability authority; the Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account and all exchange-rate policy matters; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates all federally chartered Schedule I and Schedule II banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and supervises all reporting entities; under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 24), the Bank of Canada also registers and supervises retail payment service providers. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration bureau and issues no exchange-control permits.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury concentration, escrow custody, or capital-injection routing, because foreign fintech platforms that are not licensed Canadian banks may not take demand deposits or extend credit and expose the corporation to uninsured counterparty and regulatory risk. MEDIUM for ancillary, low-value disbursement clearing after funds have first settled through a domestic Schedule I bank. Any non-bank payment provider operating in Canada must be registered as a retail payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and, if engaged in money services, registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC; in Quebec, money-services businesses must also hold registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Offshore digital EMIs lacking Canadian registration cannot lawfully provide payment processing to this entity on a recurring basis, and their use must be supported by a written PSP/MSB registration certificate, sanctions-screening evidence, and a documented third-party provider risk assessment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions from non-resident shareholders are routed by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire from the United States) to the corporation's operating account at a Schedule I bank in Canada. Field 50K must name the remitting shareholder; Field 59 must carry the exact legal name of the Quebec business corporation, its Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), and the 12-digit Canadian account number formatted as institution-transit-account, together with the beneficiary bank's SWIFT/BIC code. The payment description should state 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - [Corporation Name] - shareholders' resolution dated [YYYY-MM-DD]' to preserve a complete audit trail. No Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control filing is required, but any transfer at or above CAD 10,000 triggers a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report by the receiving bank. The corporation must update the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, record the share issuance in its minute book, file the annual return reflecting share capital, and retain the subscription agreements and non-resident investor declarations. Operating retainers received from foreign clients enter under the narrative 'PROFESSIONAL SERVICES / TRADE REMEDY CONSULTING'; no conversion mandate applies, and CAD, USD, or EUR balances may be settled at prevailing market rates through authorized Canadian banks.Outbound international disbursements for expert fees, foreign-counsel retainers, co-defendant cost sharing, and settlement payments are cleared through the SWIFT MT103 rail. Domestic Canadian-dollar payments above CAD 25,000 settle in real time over the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), while smaller CAD payments clear through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). For goods-based settlements connected to anti-dumping mandates, a documentary letter of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 by the Canadian corporation's bank is the appropriate instrument, with CAD or USD drafts, a full set of clean onboard bills of lading, commercial invoices, certificates of origin, and any requisite CBSA or CITT duty or security documents. USD disbursements are debited from a domestic USD clearing account and settled via Fedwire or CHIPS through a U.S. correspondent bank. Cross-border CAD payments to U.S. beneficiaries clear through the Canadian Direct Clearing and Settlement System or SWIFT correspondent arrangements. Any non-bank payment provider operating in Canada must be registered as a retail payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and, if engaged in money services, registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC; in Quebec, money-services businesses must also hold registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Offshore digital EMIs lacking Canadian registration cannot lawfully provide payment processing to this entity on a recurring basis, and their use must be supported by a written PSP/MSB registration certificate, sanctions-screening evidence, and a documented third-party provider risk assessment.
