Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for an Ontario limited partnership. Contributions are determined contractually in the limited partnership agreement. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general rate for Ontario corporations (Canadian federal 15.0% + Ontario 11.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Canada's statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; an LP's distributions are not dividends, but profit distributions to non-resident partners are treated as Part I taxable income allocations).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border transactions with non-arm's length non-residents triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under ITA s.247(4), consistent with OECD Master File/Local File expectations. Country-by-Country reporting applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council jurisdictions. Instead, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245, OECD MLI principal purpose tests, and treaty permanent establishment analyses to deny tax benefits where an Ontario LP lacks real business purpose, physical substance, or risk-bearing capacity. The LP must maintain a physical Ontario office, employ qualified inspection personnel, hold documented partner decisions, and avoid circular financing or artificial profit routing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Willful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239(1) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years; civil reassessment with gross-negligence penalties under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner may be personally prosecuted; Ontario courts may pierce the LP veil where fraud or sham is proven, and judicial orders can include civil restitution, travel bans, asset seizures, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA s.247(2) increases Canadian taxable income; ITA s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment applies, plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership tax obligations; directors who knowingly implement aggressive mispricing face personal exposure under willful evasion provisions and fraudulent conveyance statutes.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallows the claimed tax benefit, resulting in reassessment, penalties, interest, and possible revocation of any necessary inspection agency licensing by provincial regulators.

Director personal liability: Absence of substance alone does not pierce the veil, but if the LP is a sham or alter ego, Ontario courts will disregard the partnership form and hold the controlling partner-director personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Integrity Violations

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative penalties can reach C$500,000 for individuals and C$1,000,000 for entities; criminal sanctions under the Criminal Code s.462.31 (possession of proceeds of crime) carry imprisonment of up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners who authorize money laundering or deliberately fail to implement required compliance controls may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to personal fines, and prohibited from acting as directors.
