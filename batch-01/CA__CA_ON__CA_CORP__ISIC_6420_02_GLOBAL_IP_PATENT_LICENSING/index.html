Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – official portal: Canada.ca (My Business Account / GST/HST Accounts)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or earning Canadian-source income must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering with the CRA via the 'Register for a Business Number' online service or Form RC1; (2) separately register for a GST/HST account (Form RC1 or online) if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, or if it enters the Canadian market through a permanent establishment; (3) register under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act by filing Form ON through the Ontario Ministry of Finance if it has taxable presence in Ontario; (4) for IP-holding structures, obtain a non-resident corporation account and complete Form RC820 (Return of Non-Resident Corporation carrying on business in Canada) or file under the applicable income tax return where required. The CRA will issue a BN with a program account suffix (e.g., RTxxxxxxx for GST/HST, RC for corporation income tax), and all registrations must be completed before the first taxable supply or payroll remittance in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime for federal tax purposes. A Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Entity classification is determined by legal form; a corporation cannot elect to be fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under Section 18.4 and the hybrid mismatch arrangements legislation (addressing deductible/franked payments) apply to cross-border IP royalty arrangements. A non-resident entity may, however, choose to be treated as a corporation or a branch/permanent establishment for treaty and Part XIII purposes depending on the existence of a Canadian permanent establishment; this is a factual determination, not an elective mechanism. A GST/HST 'election' is available for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration (Form GST25), and a non-resident can elect to use the 'simplified GST/HST registration' regime. An Ontario 'notional election' may be made to allocate taxable income under the Corporations Tax Act where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; however, a zero threshold applies to non-resident businesses that 'carry on business in Canada' or that solicit orders in Canada and supply goods or services in Canada. For a non-resident IP licensor with a Canadian permanent establishment or Canadian-resident licensee, the CRA generally requires GST/HST registration regardless of the small supplier threshold unless the supplies are zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier or for the purchaser and subsequently exported as part of the supplier's commercial activity; (b) supplies of services to a non-resident person if the service is supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST and who is not carrying on business in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (c) royalties and licensing arrangements for intellectual property where the royalty is paid by a non-resident for use outside Canada. For an ISIC 6420 IP HoldCo sub-licensing patents globally, zero-rating generally applies if the licensee is a non-resident and the IP is exploited only outside Canada. The supply of a patent license to a Canadian resident for use partly or wholly in Canada is subject to GST/HST at the applicable rate (13% in Ontario) unless specifically zero-rated. The zero-rating must be demonstrated by documentation establishing the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore use of the IP.

  • Canada Export Declaration (via CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System, Form B13A or electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice evidencing royalty/license fee and payment terms
  • Executed license agreement or sub-license agreement identifying the non-resident licensee and territorial rights
  • Evidence of non-resident status: certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate, or non-resident declaration
  • Bank remittance/Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 confirming receipt of royalty proceeds
  • CRA record-keeping evidence of zero-rated supply claim (e.g., Form GST111 Return or schedule)
  • Certificate of Analysis/Use if custom authorities require proof of export beyond Canadian territory

Canada has no federal electronic-invoicing clearance mandate or real-time fiscal stamping framework. The CRA requires business records and invoices to be kept in a readable format, but does not require API-based real-time transmission of invoice data to the CRA. The Business Number must be displayed on all GST/HST invoices where the supplier is registered, and invoices must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act. Ontario does not operate a separate provincial e-invoicing clearance system; the HST portion is administered by the CRA. For customs export purposes, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires electronic export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System for most exports, with the final export declaration transmitted digitally.

Under the Excise Tax Act, a false or unreasonable claim for zero-rating may result in reassessment of GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Knowingly making a false statement or omitting material facts can trigger penalties of C$250 or, for gross negligence, 50% of the understated tax. Attempting to evade GST/HST may lead to criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. For income tax, a royalty payment that is zero-rated but not actually repatriated within 180 days may be challenged under the transfer-pricing rules and foreign-accrual property income rules; the CRA may disallow deductions, impose a 10% penalty on failed cross-border pricing documentation, and reassess Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on the gross royalty. Retrospective clawbacks can cover a six-year period, or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange arrangement. Canada does not operate exchange controls; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. Both capital and current account transactions are unrestricted, and profit or royalty remittances to non-residents are freely transferable, subject only to source-based non-resident withholding tax under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), which may be reduced under an applicable income tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, no surrender requirement, and no mandatory conversion ratio applicable to export receipts or royalty proceeds. Exporters and IP licensors may retain and hold CAD or any foreign currency without central bank approval. Routine cross-border transfer reporting is limited to FINTRAC large-transaction thresholds and Statistics Canada international-transactions data collection for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange-control administration carried out by the Department of Finance Canada and AML/CFT supervision by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; OSFI supervises all authorized banks (Schedule I, II, and III) under the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury banking relationship; Medium as a supplementary digital collection rail. EMIs are not recognized as 'banks' under the Bank Act; non-bank cross-border payment service providers must be registered as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut can facilitate royalty receipts and disbursements, but they cannot hold regulated deposits, provide CAD liquidity management, issue commercial letters of credit, or offer deposit insurance through CDIC. A regulated Schedule I bank should remain the liability-holding clearing counterparty and treasury anchor.

Compliant protocol: upstream parent sends a SWIFT MT103 to the Ontario entity's Schedule I bank, clearing through LVTS for CAD or CHIPS/Fedwire for USD.
