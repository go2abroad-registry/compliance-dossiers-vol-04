Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. No government approval, license, or administrative permit is required to convert CAD, hold foreign currency, remit dividends, or repatriate capital. Profit repatriation by a non-resident shareholder is subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 25% statutory, reducible to 5–15% under the Canada–US tax treaty or other applicable treaty); no exchange-control clearance is required. Minor residual obligations are limited to statistical reporting of direct investment positions to Statistics Canada and to the CRA's T106/T1134 filings for foreign affiliates and controlled foreign corporations. The federal Business Corporations Act (CBCA) and Ontario Business Corporations Act impose no foreign-exchange authorization or central-bank pre-approval requirement for capital injections or operating disbursements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for bringing export receipts onshore. Exporters may retain receivables indefinitely in foreign currency, hold them in any Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency account at a Canadian chartered bank, or deploy them for offshore working capital. Voluntarily reported export documentary information is collected under the International Merchandise Trade Survey for statistical purposes only and carries no compliance consequence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); statutory AML/ATF oversight is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, Payoneer) may operate in Canada only as registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must be registered with FINTRAC. They are not 'banks' for Canadian purposes, are not members of CDIC, do not participate in the Lynx high-value settlement system, and cannot hold client funds in insured deposit accounts. They are viable as ACH/dedicated payout rail for low-value kitting/fulfillment disbursements and for multi-currency settlement in corridors where SWIFT is excessive, but must not be used as the primary treasury custody layer for escrowed capital or for cross-border trade settlements above $50,000 CAD per transaction without a concurrent chartered-bank shadow account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: route the inbound shareholder equity injection from the foreign parent to the Canadian entity's CAD operational or escrow account using a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 in the Lynx/Global Payments Innovation Hub when participating banks support it). Use the most accurate OECD/ISO purpose codes: for equity injection use 'E001' (Capital Contributions) or 'G160' (Direct Investment – Equity) and cite the corporate resolution as supporting evidence. The message must include the legal entity identifier (LEI) of the Canadian entity, the CRA Business Number, the invoice/reference number of the capital call, and the description 'Share Capital Subscription under CBCA/ONBCA'. Canadian receiving banks remit in CAD or USD; if USD is injected, the receiving bank will post to the USD sub-ledger with no compulsory conversion. For amounts above the Large Value Transactions threshold (usually $25,000+), be prepared to provide the source-of-funds declaration, the board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares, and an updated corporate registry showing the equity issuance. Payment is settled via SWIFT to the receiving bank's USD nostro account, converted onshore at the bank's prevailing spot rate, or held as a USD clearing balance.For trade settlements (supplier payments, kitting component purchases, cross-border sub-contractor fees), the primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value CAD/USD wires with full remittance information; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit via MT700 for larger procurements with documentary compliance requirements under UCP 600; (3) Canadian domestic rails — Lynx (high-value real-time settlement) and ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) for Canadian-dollar pre-authorized debits and credits; (4) the U.S.-dollar correspondent network via the bank's U.S. clearing partner (CHIPS/Fedwire) for USD-denominated supplier invoices; and (5) fintech EMI rails (Airwallex/A254 dedicated payout networks) for low-value, high-frequency reimbursements to overseas contract packagers, using local currency accounts in the destination corridor to avoid double FX conversion. They are viable as ACH/dedicated payout rail for low-value kitting/fulfillment disbursements and for multi-currency settlement in corridors where SWIFT is excessive, but must not be used as the primary treasury custody layer for escrowed capital or for cross-border trade settlements above $50,000 CAD per transaction without a concurrent chartered-bank shadow account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I general rate + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate), applicable to a Canadian corporation not eligible for the Small Business Deduction (e.g., a corporation controlled by non-residents). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may enjoy a combined 12.2% rate (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, subject to the strict CCPC control tests.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax imposed under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Provincial dividend withholding tax is not separately levied. The 25% statutory rate may be reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than six (6) months after the end of the fiscal period. For a December 31, 2024 year-end corporation, the due date is June 30, 2025. Instalment due dates are the last day of each month (monthly payers) or the last day of each three-month period (quarterly payers). A non-resident-owned Canadian corporation that carries on no business in Canada in the year may be subject to a reduced filing obligation only if no balance of tax is payable. Ontario instalments are integrated with federal instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents for each fiscal period (Form T106 threshold). Upon request by the CRA, corporations must supply contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (both a Local File and, for large multinationals, a Master File) within three months; failure to provide required documentation triggers a fixed penalty of CAD 24,000 per non-compliance under subsection 247(3). There is no Ontario-specific TP documentation requirement; federal TP rules control the Ontario allocation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but federal and Ontario tax authorities strictly apply: (1) the common-law 'central mind and management' corporate residence test; (2) the modernized General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in section 245, which since 2024 includes a measured 'economic substance' requirement in subsections 245(4.1) and 245(4.2); and (3) treaty-facing anti-abuse clauses, including the Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and DTA limitation-of-benefits provisions. A Canadian kitting fulfilment company must demonstrate real office premises, employees on payroll, operational management decisions taken in Canada, substantive board minutes, local accounting records, and commercial contracts executed in Canada. A shell Ontario entity that merely books revenue or holds assets for a non-resident parent without active Canadian operational activity will be disregarded, reassessed on an attribution basis, denied treaty benefits, and the shareholder may be retroactively taxed under Part XIII withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under subsection 163(2): 50% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded. Criminal prosecution under paragraph 239(1)(d): fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years for an indictable offence; summary conviction carries imprisonment up to 2 years and an equal fine. Interest is compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate, and a criminal conviction can lead to a disclosure order and forfeiture of proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for corporate unremitted source deductions and GST/HST amounts, but not for Part I income tax; however, a director who authorizes or acquiesces in a false T2 can be charged personally under section 239 for aiding or assisting the evasion, and upon conviction may face imprisonment, a criminal fine, a travel ban, asset seizure, and an order of civil restitution in favour of the Crown.
