Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online portal and the CRA My Business Account portal. Ontario corporate and partnership registrations are administered separately by the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery through the Ontario Business Registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario limited partnership should first be legally formed or registered in Ontario, obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), and register for applicable program accounts. The BN is the principal federal tax identifier. The partnership may apply through CRA Business Registration Online where eligible, or by submitting CRA Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts, through an authorized representative or CRA non-resident registration channel. A GST/HST program account is issued under the BN with the RT extension. A payroll account uses the RP extension, and an import-export account uses the RM extension. A partnership required to file a T5013 information return must obtain or use its CRA partnership identification information. Non-resident partners generally use their existing foreign tax identification numbers and may require Canadian non-resident tax accounts where withholding, Part XIII tax, or other filing obligations arise. GST/HST registration does not by itself create Canadian income-tax residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. An Ontario limited partnership is generally treated as a partnership for Canadian income-tax purposes, with income, losses, and relevant tax attributes allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and the Income Tax Act. The partnership is generally not subject to income tax at the entity level, although it may have information-return, withholding, payroll, GST/HST, customs, and other filing obligations. The classification is not an exemption from Canadian tax and does not prevent Canadian taxation of Canadian-source income or income allocated to Canadian-resident partners.

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an Ontario limited partnership. Available mechanisms may include a subsection 97(2) Income Tax Act rollover election for qualifying transfers of property to a Canadian partnership, partnership allocation and designation elections where statutory conditions are satisfied, GST/HST joint-election mechanisms for qualifying transactions, and treaty-based elections or filings in limited circumstances. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for specified hybrid arrangements; they do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules. The threshold generally applies over four consecutive calendar quarters or when the supplier exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, subject to the statutory timing rules. Ontario taxable supplies generally bear 13% HST; supplies in non-participating provinces may bear 5% GST, and place-of-supply rules determine the applicable rate. Non-resident digital-economy suppliers and platform operators may be subject to simplified GST/HST registration and collection rules at the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the supplier delivers or ships the goods outside Canada, or otherwise satisfies the statutory export conditions and retains sufficient evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify for zero-rating under the applicable GST/HST rules where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not subject to an exclusion. Common exclusions include services related to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services required to be performed in Canada. Digital streaming, application, advertising, licensing, and platform supplies require a separate place-of-supply and recipient-status analysis; a non-Canadian customer does not automatically establish zero-rating. Input tax credits or GST/HST refunds require valid supporting documentation and must relate to commercial activities.

  • Canadian export declaration submitted through the Canada Border Services Agency's Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where reporting is required
  • Commercial invoice or equivalent transaction documentation identifying the exporter, consignee, goods, value, and destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier documentation evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Proof of delivery or other documentary evidence establishing export and destination
  • Supplier invoice and GST/HST accounting records showing the zero-rated treatment
  • For services, contract, customer identification, service-delivery records, payment records, and evidence supporting non-resident status and the statutory zero-rating conditions
  • Import or customs records in the destination jurisdiction where relevant

Canada has no general real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance, government e-invoicing, or digital-tax-stamp mandate for ordinary commercial transactions. Suppliers must issue invoices or other prescribed documentation containing required GST/HST information, including the supplier identity and GST/HST registration number, date, description, consideration, applicable tax rate, and tax amount where required. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if their integrity, accessibility, and auditability are maintained. CRA may require electronic filing for specified returns and information returns, but this is not real-time invoice clearance. Apple, Google, Stripe, and similar payment intermediaries do not replace the supplier's GST/HST collection, invoicing, remittance, and record-retention obligations.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement to obtain an inward-remittance certificate to substantiate GST/HST zero-rating. CRA may reassess tax, deny input tax credits or refunds, and assess GST/HST, interest, and penalties where export evidence or non-resident status is insufficient. Incorrectly zero-rated supplies can be reassessed at the applicable GST/HST rate, with interest and penalties. False statements or omissions may attract gross-negligence penalties generally equal to 50% of the understated tax or improperly claimed credit, while knowing or fraudulent conduct can attract more severe penalties and extended or unlimited reassessment periods where permitted by law. Failure to file, late remittance, failure to register, and failure to maintain adequate records may generate additional statutory penalties. Customs misdeclarations may result in Administrative Monetary Penalty System assessments, seizure, loss of privileges, and other enforcement action. Voluntary disclosure may mitigate certain penalties if the statutory conditions are satisfied.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current- and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, prior approvals, or licensing requirements for cross-border capital movements. Profit remittance impediments are limited to (i) Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident limited partners at a 25% statutory rate, generally reduced to 10–15% under applicable income tax treaties, and (ii) Ontario and federal income tax on Canadian-source income allocated to the limited partners, with advisory requirements for non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, so proceeds from the global distribution of vertical micro-dramas may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency at an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank or a permitted foreign bank account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada, prudential banking supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF supervision is vested in FINTRAC. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration authority comparable to a capital-controls regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMIs and payment institutions cannot operate as banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and do not qualify for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, providing virtual USD/CAD settlement accounts, local receiving accounts, and faster onboarding for cross-border disbursements. For the LP's core operating treasury, an OSFI-regulated tier-1 bank is required as the primary settlement anchor, with a FINTRAC-registered EMI permitted only as a supplementary collection rail, subject to ring-fenced balances and periodic reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/ACH for US-source funds) to the LP's operational account at an OSFI-regulated tier-1 bank. Canada does not prescribe statutory purpose codes, but the reason-for-payment field must clearly state 'Capital contribution under Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date]'. The paying bank's correspondent must include ISO 20022 structured remittance information, and the receiving Canadian bank will require the capital call notice, executed subscription agreement, and source-of-funds confirmation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario) applicable to a corporate partner's share of active business income; individual partners are taxed at progressive rates up to the 53.53% Ontario top marginal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. The Ontario Limited Partnership itself is not a dividend-payer; for a non-resident corporate partner, the profit repatriation of after-tax partnership earnings through its Canadian permanent establishment may trigger a 25% Part I.1 Canadian branch tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period (e.g., May 31 for a December 31 fiscal year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a corporate partner resident in Canada, the T2 return must be filed on or before six months after the end of its taxation year (e.g., June 30 for a December 31 year-end). The income tax balance is due two months after year-end (three months for an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period (e.g., June 30 for a December 31 year-end).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 mandatory filing threshold is CAD 1,000,000 annual aggregate of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. CRA generally waives transfer-pricing penalties for small taxpayers: gross revenues for the year less than CAD 5,000,000 and total assets less than CAD 10,000,000. Contemporaneous documentation (local file/master file) should be prepared whenever related-party cross-border transactions exist, regardless of threshold, to support reasonable efforts.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone Economic Substances Regulation (ESR) in the form used in the UAE or other zero-tax jurisdictions. Instead, the Canada Revenue Agency enforces substance requirements through the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), case law such as Backman v. Canada, and the prevalence-of-business-purpose test. An Ontario limited partnership that is a shell, holds no meaningful assets or employees, and merely allocates losses or credits to non-resident partners will be scrutinized and may be disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years (ITA s.239). On summary conviction, a fine between 100% and 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to two years. Civil reassessment plus gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and tax preparers who counsel willful evasion are criminally liable as parties to the offence; they may face Criminal Code fraud charges, confiscation of assets, travel bans during investigation, and professional discipline. Directors and officers of the general partner may also be assessed for unpaid source deductions under ITA s.227.1.
Shell Limited Partnership / Artificial Loss Allocation

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR (ITA s.245) to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the partnership arrangement, disallow losses back to earlier years with compound interest, and impose gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax understated. Ontario may claw back OFTTC/OIDMTC credits on a retroactive basis.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be jointly liable for the denied credits, unpaid corporate taxes, interest and penalties where they signed the partnership return or knowingly participated in the artificial loss allocation. In flagrant cases, directors can be charged under ITA s.239 and the Criminal Code.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment when the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 1,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Late T106 penalties of CAD 100 per month up to CAD 2,500, plus gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated federal tax. Double taxation arises if the foreign counterparty is assessed without a corresponding adjustment under a mutual agreement procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved mispriced intercompany contracts and failed to obtain arm's-length appraisal may be held liable under director-liability provisions for unpaid Part XIII withholding and related penalties. CRA may seek civil restitution and, in serious cases, an injunction or travel ban preventing the corporate director from leaving Canada.
AML / Financial Compliance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 for a corporation can be imposed for non-compliance. Failure to register, if required, is an offence punishable by a fine of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to two years. CRA penalties and confiscation orders may also apply where funds are untraceable.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and in-house counsel who knowingly fail to establish an AML compliance program, do not report suspicious transactions, or conceal beneficial owners are personally exposed to criminal prosecution and fines; a due-diligence defence is available but requires documented policies and audit trail. Travel bans and freezing orders may be issued during the investigation.
Failure to File and Recurring Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: For T2 non-filing, a 5% penalty plus 1% per month up to 12 months and a 10% repeated-failure penalty; T5013 late filing attracts CAD 10/day up to CAD 2,500; GST/HST penalties are 6% plus AR dollars per day; courts impose fines for each offence, and imprisonment is available for persistent avoidance under ETA s.280. CRA may also assess a third-party service provider for books on freshwater optimized drains.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for unpaid corporate-partner GST/HST and employee source deductions under ETA s.323 and ITA s.227.1, even if the partnership or corporation is dissolved. Director liability can exceed the nominal tax due and is supported by CRA's ability to pursue personal assets, bankruptcy opposition, and foreign judgment enforcement.
