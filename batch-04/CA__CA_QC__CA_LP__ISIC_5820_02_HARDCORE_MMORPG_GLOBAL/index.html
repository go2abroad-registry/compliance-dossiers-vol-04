Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial tax administration; Registraire des entreprises du Québec for LP legal registration. Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-account.html); Revenu Québec clic Revenu Québec (https://clic.revenuquebec.ca); Registraire d'entreprises (https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca).

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec société en commandite (LP): (1) file a declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ (Québec enterprise number); (2) apply to CRA via Business Registration Online or Form RC1 for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST 'RT' program account; (3) register with Revenu Québec for QST using Form LM-101-V or the online clic Revenu Québec portal once taxable supplies in Quebec exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; (4) file annual T5013 (federal) and CO-17 (Quebec) partnership information returns and report income allocations to partners; (5) for non-resident partners, evaluate Canadian Part I filing obligations under ITA s.115 for Canadian-source partnership income and apply Part XIII withholding to any interest, rent, or royalty components distributed, reporting via Form NR4 and obtaining non-resident partner declarations to establish whether the partnership constitutes a 'foreign partnership' for CRA purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; a Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent. Available elections and designations: (a) ITA s.103 partnership income/loss allocation election where the allocation is reasonable; (b) ITA s.220(3.1) off-calendar fiscal-period election, with Form T1139 filing; (c) Excise Tax Act s.156 election for GST/HST treatment of transactions between closely related registrants (limited utility for an LP); (d) compliance positions and designations under Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (BEPS Action 2) applicable to partnership interests causing deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (e) QST reporting-frequency election with Revenu Québec (monthly/quarterly/annual); and (f) for non-resident partners, an NR6 undertaking to reduce Part XIII withholding where appropriate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the GST/HST small-supplier threshold (Excise Tax Act s.148(1)) is exceeded when total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the parallel QST threshold is aligned at CAD 30,000 under the Act respecting the Québec Sales Tax. For non-resident digital suppliers of intangible personal property to Quebec consumers, registration with CRA and Revenu Québec is required once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded, while a person carrying on business in Canada may also be required to register regardless of threshold. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec LP supplying MMORPG downloadable games, in-app virtual property, and platform support services to Apple/Google (non-resident platform operators) or directly to end users located outside Canada qualifies for zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, item 17 (services supplied to non-residents) and item 22 (intangible personal property supplied for use outside Canada), with equivalent QST zero-rating mirroring the federal list. Under the July 1, 2021 federal digital platform supply rules, where App Store/Google Play control the essential elements of the supply, the platform is deemed to make the taxable supply to the Canadian consumer, and the LP's underlying B2B supply to the non-resident platform is not subject to GST/HST/QST. The CRA's digital services place-of-supply rules generally deem a supply to occur at the recipient's normal residence, supporting zero-rating for non-Canadian geolocated end users. To claim the 0% rate and substantiate any net GST/HST/QST refund, the LP must retain contemporaneous evidence of non-residency, including platform attestations, billing addresses, IP geolocation logs, and ordinary invoice or transaction records.

  • Executed Apple Developer Program Agreement and Google Play Distribution Agreement evidencing principal-to-principal supplies to non-resident platform operators
  • Platform payout statements, payment processor settlement records, and inward remittance advices evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Canada
  • Contemporaneous non-residency evidence: billing addresses, IP geolocation logs, platform attestations, and end-user country records
  • If physical media is exported (e.g., collector's discs): Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A), bill of lading or air waybill, commercial invoice, and proof of outward movement

No real-time continuous transaction controls, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. E-invoicing is optional; the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard is endorsed. Registrants must file and remit electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's clic Revenu Québec portal. Non-resident digital merchants must use CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration and remittance portal. No API-based invoice authentication, clearance, or cryptographic stamping is imposed.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period, extended to 8 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Penalties include: Excise Tax Act s.280 (false statements or omissions) CAD 100 to CAD 10,000; s.285 (wilful evasion) 50% of the net tax evaded; s.285.1 (false deduction or refund claim) CAD 1,000; s.239 criminal offences with fines and imprisonment; and s.323 director liability for unremitted GST/HST. Quebec's Tax Administration Act provides a 50% penalty under s.59 where a person is reckless, fines up to CAD 100,000 for s.58/59 contraventions, and statutory interest under s.28. Insufficient export documentation triggers denial of the 0% rate, clawback of input tax credits and refunds, recalculation as taxable Canadian supplies, and interest charges. While Canada has no codified 180-day FX repatriation deadline, prolonged failure to repatriate export proceeds is an audit red flag and may be treated as evidence that the supply was not genuinely made to a non-resident, leading to reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime with no current-account exchange controls. The Canadian dollar is freely transferable for both capital and current transactions; non-resident investors may repatriate capital and profits without quantitative limits. Profit remittances from a Quebec limited partnership distribution to non-resident limited partners are, however, subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and to Quebec tax rules where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts, including proceeds from global MMORPG in-app sales routed through Apple App Store and Google Play, may be retained in foreign currency, provided the underlying economic reality is reflected in Canadian-dollar functional-currency financial statements and any foreign-exchange gain or loss is recognized for tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is decentralized and conducted through federally regulated deposit-taking institutions under the supervision of the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Quebec financial-market conduct for payment services is overseen by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot provide a domestic clearing or CDIC-insured cash account. They can be used as supplemental payout-aggregation rails for Apple App Store and Google Play receipts, but the provider must comply with Canadian anti-money-laundering obligations as a foreign money services business under FINTRAC if it delivers payment services in Canada. The primary treasury account must remain with a Schedule I bank to ensure regulatory certainty and access to Payments Canada clearing.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Pass-through limited partnership structure with opaque limited partner ownership

Root cause: Pass-through limited partnership structure with opaque limited partner ownership

Defense evidence: The full limited partnership agreement, a notarized certificate of incumbency, a current extract from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, a complete beneficial-ownership chart, tax identification numbers for all partners, and source-of-wealth statements for each limited partner.
High-volume in-app purchase receipts routed through Apple and Google foreign payout entities

Root cause: High-volume in-app purchase receipts routed through Apple and Google foreign payout entities

Defense evidence: Apple/Google payout statements showing gross and net proceeds, a reconciliation ledger mapping payout streams to the bank account, FINTRAC money-services-business registration if payment services are performed, a documented AML compliance program, transaction-monitoring procedures, and audited financial statements.
Non-resident directors and cross-border corporate ownership

Root cause: Non-resident directors and cross-border corporate ownership

Defense evidence: Valid passports or government-issued ID, proof of residential address, bank or professional references, original board resolutions approving account opening, signed delegated authority documents, and a legal opinion from Quebec counsel confirming the validity of the limited partnership and the foreign entities.
Large one-time capital injection from an overseas related company

Root cause: Large one-time capital injection from an overseas related company

Defense evidence: Capital call notice, executed subscription agreements, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documentation, full organizational chart of the funding affiliate, wire instruction with a transparent capital-contribution purpose code, and confirmation from the sending bank that the payment is not structured to avoid reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from foreign limited partners should be routed through the SWIFT MT103 wire network with a clearly stated purpose code matching the receiving bank's capital-subscription classification. The sending bank must transmit full originator and beneficiary information in compliance with the travel rule and FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting thresholds. The receiving bank requires the executed subscription agreement, the capital call notice, an updated register of partners, and confirmation that the contribution is an equity injection rather than a loan. If the investment crosses applicable thresholds, the bank may request evidence that the transaction complies with the Investment Canada Act and Quebec enterprise-registration requirements.Outbound trade settlements for MMORPG development, cloud infrastructure, and third-party publishing services should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for open-account vendor payments and through an irrevocable commercial letter of credit for documentary trade transactions requiring shipment control. CAD payments clear through Payments Canada via Lynx for large-value real-time settlement and ACSS for batch retail clearing. USD payments clear through the bank's correspondent network in the United States.
