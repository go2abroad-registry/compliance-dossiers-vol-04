Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer, Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; portal: www.esty.admin.ch and https://estvplus.admin.ch (eServices). For Zug: Kantonales Steueramt Zug, Industriestrasse 26, 6301 Zug; portal: www.zg.ch/steuern.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporation of the AG: notarial deed with entry in the Commercial Register of the Canton of Zug. Step 2 – Automatic issuance of the Swiss Business Identification Number (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, Format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX) via the central register (Zefix). Step 3 – VAT registration: the AG must register with the ESTV within 30 days after the statutory condition is met (worldwide taxable turnover exceeding CHF 100,000 p.a.) using the ESTV registration form/eServices, effective from the beginning of the month in which the obligation arises. Step 4 – A foreign entity with no Swiss seat must appoint a VAT representative domiciled in Switzerland (Art. 67 para 2 LTVA) unless the ESTV waives the requirement; the representative files on behalf of the foreign principal. Step 5 – Income tax registration: the Commercial Register automatically notifies the Zug Cantonal Tax Office; the AG files annual corporate tax returns (Form B) for federal, cantonal and communal profit/capital taxes; provisional quarterly prepayments are set by the tax office. Foreign shareholders need no separate Swiss tax ID for the AG, but receive a specific shareholder identification number for withholding tax/quotas where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Aktiengesellschaft is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax (Impôt fédéral direct, Art. 49 ff. DBG) at 8.5% of net taxable profit (approximately 7.83% effective after deduction of the tax itself) plus canton/commune corporate taxes in Zug (combined Zug effective total approx. 14.6% in 2025), plus annual capital tax at the cantonal level. The AG is not a pass-through entity; dividends paid to shareholders are subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax (reduced by treaty). Switzerland taxes resident companies on worldwide income, with foreign permanent establishments and real estate exempted via allocation (subject to progression) and participation deduction for qualifying dividends/capital gains.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box exists under Swiss law; the AG is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Available elections and regimes: (1) VAT group (Mehrwertsteuergruppe, Art. 21 LTVA) – related entities may form a single VAT unit via an election filed with the ESTV. (2) Participation deduction (Art. 70 DBG) – eligible dividends and capital gains on qualifying participations (≥10% or fair market value ≥CHF 1 million) are deducted; application in the tax return is mandatory once conditions are met. (3) STAF tax regimes: election of the patent box (Art. 24b DBG and cantonal rules) giving a preferential tax base on IP income, and election of the R&D super-deduction of 50% (Art. 25a DBG) – both require prior cantonal approval. (4) Hybrid mismatch avoidance: Swiss law denies deductions for payments that are not taxable for the recipient under BEPS Action 2; there is no election to re-characterize hybrid instruments except via structuring and documentation in the tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT) from supplies of goods and services (Art. 10 para 2 let a LTVA); non-profit organizations, public entities and certain charitable businesses use a CHF 250,000 threshold (Art. 10 para 2 let b LTVA). A foreign digital service supplier supplying Swiss private consumers (B2C) is registrable at the same CHF 100,000 worldwide threshold under the simplified foreign supplier procedure, without entitlement to input VAT deduction. A Swiss-resident AG distributing micro-dramas through a streaming app must register once subscriptions, licensing and advertising revenues exceed CHF 100,000. Reduced rates apply: 2.6% for food, press, pharmaceuticals and books and 3.7% for accommodation services; the 8.1% standard rate applies to digital streaming subscriptions and licensing fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating under Swiss VAT arises in two forms: (A) Export of goods (Art. 23 para 1 LTVA) – not applicable to the core micro-drama business as there is no physical movement of goods; applicable only to ancillary export of hardware/servers. (B) Services with the place of supply outside Switzerland, i.e. 'export of services' (Art. 23 para 2 LTVA). For the streaming app: subscriptions sold to private consumers resident abroad are not subject to Swiss VAT because electronically supplied services to consumers are located in the consumer's country of residence; global licensing of micro-drama copyrights and production output to foreign commissioning platforms (B2B) is zero-rated when the recipient's place of business is abroad. The Swiss supplier retains the full input VAT deduction linked to such exported supplies. Subscriptions by Swiss consumers are taxable at 8.1%. Documentary proof of the foreign place of supply (Art. 45–46 LTVA) must be kept by the supplier, including evidence of the customer's domicile or business seat (e.g. foreign VAT number, commercial register extract, bank/card billing country, IP/geo-location logs and app-store settlement statements from Apple, Google and Stripe).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / déclaration d'exportation) lodged electronically in the Swiss customs system 'Passar' – required only for physical exports in the content-production supply chain (e.g. filming equipment, hardware, promotional materials shipped abroad)
  • Transport document evidencing international movement: Bill of Lading (seafreight) / Air Waybill (airfreight) / CMR (road) – only where physical goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice and service agreement with the foreign customer, including the exact description of the licensed micro-drama catalogue or streaming subscription and the foreign place-of-supply indicators
  • Payment evidence: bank/credit-card settlement records, Stripe or Apple/Google app-store remittance reports proving execution and FX (or stablecoin) inflow from the foreign counterparty
  • For financing an invoice, an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a Swiss statutory document; Swiss banks issue payment confirmations that serve as the practical substitute

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API data transmission or digital stamping regime as of 2025. The ESTV accepts paper, PDF or EDI invoices if the data of Art. 26 LTVA are present (supplier/recipient name and address, supplier VAT ID, date, nature of supply, amount, applicable VAT rate). Since 30 June 2020, all invoices intended for payment in Switzerland must include the Swiss QR-code (QR-Rechnung); however, QR-payment slips are a payment facilitation instrument, not a fiscal control mechanism. Business-to-business e-invoicing (eBill, EDI/XML) operates on private standards, and the Federal Council has announced possible mandatory e-invoicing for B2B after 2026, but no final law is in force for the current period.

Swiss law does not set a specific statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark is the documentary best-practice window applied by tax auditors to verify that export proceeds were actually received. During a VAT audit (Art. 78 ff. LTVA), the burden of proof of the export/foreign place of supply lies with the supplier; missing evidence triggers a corrective assessment (Nachbelastung) plus late-payment interest (3.5% p.a. in 2025). Negligent or intentional false zero-rating constitutes VAT evasion (Art. 96 LTVA) with penalties from 1x to 3x the tax evaded; deliberate tax fraud (Art. 97 LTVA) is a criminal offence punishable by fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The VAT assessment limitation period is 10 years, and is unlimited in the case of fraud (Art. 99 LTVA); in practice the ESTV may also claw back wrongly deducted input VAT retroactively for 10 years and publish decisions on wilful offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible. Switzerland is bound by the IMF Article VIII obligations and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Current Invisible Operations and Capital Movements. Both current account payments and capital transfers are unrestricted; no exchange-control authorization is required for capital injection, dividend distribution, or the opening of foreign-currency accounts. The main profit-remittance hurdle is the 35% federal withholding tax on dividends and certain interest payments, which is reduced or eliminated under an applicable double-taxation treaty. A Swiss AG may distribute only net retained statutory profit; any distribution from hidden reserves must be properly documented to avoid tax recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds under Swiss law. Foreign-currency export receipts may be retained in the original currency without limit, and no obligation arises to convert them into Swiss francs. The only requirements are commercial bookkeeping and tax-transparency rules, accurate custody of underlying invoices, and the normal bank AML due-diligence obligation to identify the economic purpose and source of funds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), acting under the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank and the Foreign Exchange Ordinance; the State Secretariat for International Financial Affairs (SIF) also coordinates international financial-market policy in this domain.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. An offshore EMI (e-money institution licensed outside Switzerland) may be used as an app-store collection or payout aggregator, but it cannot serve as the primary treasury bank. A FINMA-licensed Swiss bank remains mandatory for lawful custody of corporate funds, access to Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC), and issuance of a proper CHF/EUR treasury account. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a bank under the Swiss Banking Act, cannot hold client monies as a Swiss bank would, and has no direct access to SIC or euroSIC. Any use of an EMI must be structured strictly as an agent/technical payment provider with full reconciliation and an evidentiary paper trail for the underlying vertical-drama subscription transactions. The feasibility is higher for payment collection from Apple IAP/Google Play than for concentration of corporate treasury balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founding shareholder or institutional investor wires the subscription price via SWIFT MT103 (or SIC for CHF-denominated domestic payments). The payment instruction and reference field must identify the transaction as a capital contribution to the Swiss AG, referencing the share-subscription agreement, the notarized deed of incorporation, and the relevant provision of the Swiss Code of Obligations. The receiving Swiss bank verifies the remitter's identity, confirms the value date, and matches the funds to the commercial-register entry and board resolution on share issuance. For cross-border EUR or USD flows, the regional correspondent bank will validate the MT103 against OFAC/EU/CH sanctions lists and the beneficiary name in the exact commercial-register denomination. No Swiss exchange-control approval is required; however, the bank may require the statutory balance-of-payments statistical declaration to be completed by the corporate client where cross-border direct investment thresholds apply.Trade settlements and production disbursements use Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) for CHF payments, euroSIC and TARGET2 for EUR payments, and SWIFT MT103 for non-CHF cross-border transfers. SWIFT MT202 is reserved for bank-to-bank payments and liquidity movements. Commercial documentary letters of credit are issued in accordance with UCP 600 using SWIFT MT700/MT710 and may cover advance production deposits, license-fee guarantees, and minimum-guarantee obligations. Standby letters of credit and documentary collections may be used to secure royalty receivables from foreign distribution partners. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium. An offshore EMI (e-money institution licensed outside Switzerland) may be used as an app-store collection or payout aggregator, but it cannot serve as the primary treasury bank. A FINMA-licensed Swiss bank remains mandatory for lawful custody of corporate funds, access to Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC), and issuance of a proper CHF/EUR treasury account. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a bank under the Swiss Banking Act, cannot hold client monies as a Swiss bank would, and has no direct access to SIC or euroSIC. Any use of an EMI must be structured strictly as an agent/technical payment provider with full reconciliation and an evidentiary paper trail for the underlying vertical-drama subscription transactions. The feasibility is higher for payment collection from Apple IAP/Google Play than for concentration of corporate treasury balances.
