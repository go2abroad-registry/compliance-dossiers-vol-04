Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); federal portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency (Business Registration Online and My Business Account). Provincial counterpart for Quebec: Revenu Québec at revenuquebec.ca; partnership registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign limited partnership (CA_LP): (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 with the GST/HST program account (RT) through CRA's Business Registration Online, identifying each partner and the partnership; (2) register the extra-provincial LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), obtaining the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and filing the required partnership declaration; (3) use the NEQ to open the QST account (RM) with Revenu Québec, plus QPIP and CNESST accounts if payroll is created; (4) mandatory registration arises when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold and is necessary to claim input tax credits on platform and streaming costs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For income tax purposes the LP is fiscally transparent; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to general and limited partners, and non-resident partners remain assessable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on Canadian-source partnership business income. For GST/HST and QST purposes, however, the partnership itself is a separate person and must register and report in its own name; partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations to the extent provided by the Excise Tax Act.

Available election forms

Canada has no United States-style check-the-box election. Available elections include: the Excise Tax Act s. 273 designated-member election, allowing the LP to designate a partner to file and remit GST/HST for the partnership; the Income Tax Act s. 96(3) election to compute partnership income as if the partnership were a separate person; the ITA s. 97(2) election for a tax-deferred rollover of property into the LP; and the Quebec QST joint-election for members of a closely related group. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by the ITA's foreign affiliate, FAPI, and specific anti-avoidance provisions rather than by an elective classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: GST small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(2); the Quebec QST threshold is harmonized at CAD 30,000. A non-resident LP exceeds the threshold where its taxable supplies, including zero-rated cross-border digital supplies, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; Quebec deemed-supply rules may also require non-resident digital vendors to register for QST where their taxable sales into Quebec exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of intangible personal property, including virtual concert access, digital tickets, and metaverse admission, made to non-resident recipients under ETA s. 23(1), provided the right to use is not applied in connection with a commercial or taxable activity in Canada. Physical goods must be exported within 60 days to obtain zero-rating. For the CA_LP, sales to recipients whose billing residence and IP geo-location indicate a non-resident and whose use occurs outside Canada qualify; sales to Canadian or Quebec residents are subject to GST at 5% and QST at 9.975%. Input tax credits on costs related to zero-rated sales are claimable only with valid registration and retained export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13/CAED) — mandatory for physical ancillaries exported from Canada; not applicable to pure digital metaverse access, but absence of export proof for goods revokes zero-rating
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — evidencing movement of tangible merchandise to a foreign destination; for digital ticket tokenization, an electronic service-delivery log is substituted
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / Stripe payout report — evidencing receipt of consideration from the non-resident purchaser for the zero-rated export
  • Non-resident status evidence — KYC record, billing address, IP geo-location, and payment card country code establishing consumption outside Canada
  • Contract or purchase order expressly stating that the virtual access right is delivered to and consumed by a non-resident purchaser outside Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime exists federally or in Quebec. Registrants must issue tax invoices satisfying ETA s. 223(a) (supplier identity and GST/HST BN, recipient identity, invoice date, consideration, and tax charged) and retain books and records in Canada or accessible via electronic media. CRA and Revenu Québec accept EDI and API-driven electronic invoicing; Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing adoption is voluntary and does not involve government clearance.

On audit, CRA and Revenu Québec require the full export-evidence bundle; missing or conflicting evidence reverses the zero-rating, the combined 14.975% GST/QST becomes payable with prescribed arrears interest, and previously claimed input tax credits are clawed back. False statements or omissions expose the LP to penalties under ETA s. 280 of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought, gross-negligence penalties, and accrued interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute indictable tax evasion under ETA s. 329, subject to criminal fines and imprisonment, and the normal four-year reassessment limitation is suspended for wilful misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital account transactions. Canada does not impose exchange controls, approval requirements, or quantitative limits on profit remittance, partnership distributions, or capital repatriation. The principal frictions are regulatory and tax-oriented: non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership must provide Canadian/Quebec tax self-certifications and may face Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source amounts unless relief is available under an applicable tax treaty. USDC and other virtual currency proceeds may be converted at commercial rates through regulated Canadian money services businesses or chartered banks, but the handling of virtual currency creates FINTRAC registration and record-keeping obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in CAD, USD, or other currencies in accounts held at Canadian authorized banks, subject only to general foreign-currency reporting for amounts above C$10,000 and to FINTRAC transaction reporting. Virtual-currency transactions related to digital ticket tokenization are reportable if they meet threshold criteria, and the commercial invoice narrative should align with ISIC 7110 / NAICS 711310 to facilitate clean bank and customs/routing classification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the currency and monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential bank supervisor; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF regulator; no standalone foreign-exchange control agency exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary settlement rail; High as a supporting collection and conversion front-end. A foreign electronic money institution cannot be a direct Lynx or Real-Time Rail participant and cannot provide final CAD settlement in Canada. If it operates as a money services business in Quebec, it must be registered with FINTRAC and compliant with the Retail Payment Activities Act. The recommended architecture is to use a licensed foreign EMI or digital-bank partner exclusively for USDC receipt, ticket checkout, and conversion, with daily sweeps to a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank account that remains the legal settlement apex.
