Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/business-tax) for federal Business Number, GST/HST, and import/export accounts; Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) for the Québec enterprise number (NEQ), QST registration, and provincial tax remittances.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using the Business Registration Online service or Form RC3482, and must register for a GST/HST program account when carrying on commercial activity in Canada unless the small-supplier threshold is not exceeded. The partnership must also obtain a Québec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec for provincial registration, and open a QST account with Revenu Québec. Non-resident partners may appoint a Canadian representative (agent/attorney) with signed authorization; CRA issues the BN as a nine-digit number with program-account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST), while Revenu Québec issues the NEQ and the QST account number separately. The LP itself, not the partners, is the registrant and information-filer for indirect tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity-classification regime. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes by default and cannot elect to be taxable as a corporation. No hybrid-mismatch election is available to convert the LP into a taxable entity. Registrants may, however, use limited administrative elections under the Excise Tax Act and the Québec Sales Tax Act that do not alter the pass-through character of the partnership, such as elective related-party joint registration/streamlining arrangements for closely related registrants and optional annual-filing or net-tax assessments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or in the current calendar quarter where the threshold is crossed). For QST, the analogous registration threshold is CAD 30,000 in taxable commercial activity in Québec. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST and QST registration is mandatory; a non-resident supply of imported apparel for wholesale liquidation could trigger registration on a lower threshold if the supply is made in Canada and the carrier/agent nexus exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property, including finished branded overstock apparel, sold to a non-resident recipient when the supplier exports the goods directly from Canada or makes them available to an independent carrier/consignee for export abroad. The supplier must retain documentary evidence showing that the goods were shipped to a destination outside Canada, that possession passed outside Canada, and that the export occurred within the statutory timeframe. For QST, the equivalent zero-rating rule applies to goods delivered outside Québec, subject to substantiation rules under the Québec Sales Tax Act. Off-price liquidation sales qualify if the invoiced goods are physically exported and are not retained for sale or consumption in Canada.

  • CBSA export declaration or electronic export reporting through Canada Border Services Agency (Canadian export reporting system/window or EDI equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire/ACH proof of payment in CAD
  • Commercial invoice describing the finished garments, quantities, sale to non-resident buyer, and Incoterms evidence of export
  • Packing list and delivery/carrier proof evidencing physical removal from Canada

Canada and Québec do not currently impose a real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate. There is no mandatory digital stamping (fiscal stamp) requirement. CRA/Revenu Québec require established, verifiable invoice controls and mandatory electronic filing of most GST/HST/QST returns for large registrants, but invoice data is not transmitted for fiscal clearance in advance or in real time. Pan-Canadian electronic invoicing infrastructure exists on an optional Peppol-compatible basis; it is non-mandatory.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating where documentary evidence is incomplete, late, or inconsistent; this results in reassessment of GST and QST at the applicable rate, arrears interest from the date the tax was payable, and penalties for false or negligent statements. Repeated or deliberate misuse of zero-rating constitutes tax evasion and is subject to serious monetary penalties, potential criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code, and director/partner liability for unremitted federal and provincial tax. Assessments may generally be reopened within four years, or seven years in cases of carelessness, negligence, or misrepresentation, allowing retrospective clawback of zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate with full convertibility on both current account and capital account. The Canadian dollar faces no exchange-control licensing, no advance approval for capital movements, and no profit-remittance obstacle for non-resident limited partners of a Quebec limited partnership. Distributions to foreign partners are subject only to normal Canadian tax compliance, including Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source partnership allocations where applicable, treaty-based reductions, CRA reporting requirements, and bank-level source-of-funds due diligence. No Bank of Canada or Department of Finance Canada authorization is required for inbound capital injection, trade settlement, or outbound profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. A Quebec LP may receive and retain export receipts freely in CAD or USD, and may maintain USD-denominated operating accounts with OSFI-regulated banks. Receipts must be supported by underlying invoices, bills of lading, and customs documentation for CRA audit purposes. Banks must file FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for international wires of CAD 10,000 or more, but this is a reporting obligation, not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange control is not implemented. Banking prudential supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF reporting is governed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex may on-board a Quebec LP only if the EMI is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC in Canada and the LP completes full corporate KYC. EMIs are not OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions, hold funds through bank partners, and are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance. They are legally viable as a supplementary payment corridor for cross-border supplier payouts, but they should not replace a Schedule I commercial bank account. The business description 'off-price liquidation and wholesale trading' must be supported by supplier agreements and commercial invoices or it will be rejected by the EMI's own compliance function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partners inject committed capital by SWIFT MT103 wire to the LP's CAD or USD operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CABI' (capital increase) or 'TRAN' with a narrative referencing the capital call notice and Limited Partnership Agreement. The bank will verify the wire against the outstanding capital call, match remitter to the register of limited partners, and complete FINTRAC EFT reporting for wires of CAD 10,000 or more. No Bank of Canada approval or Investment Canada Act pre-clearance is required unless the partnership acquires control of an existing Canadian business enterprise; in that case, notification thresholds under the Investment Canada Act must be assessed. Supporting documentation includes the LPA, capital call notices, source-of-funds files, and a funds-flow statement.Supplier settlements for finished-garment purchases use SWIFT MT103/MT202 for open-account wire payments, and SWIFT MT700 confirmed documentary Letters of Credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank for larger or sight-draft purchase orders. Domestic clearing within Canada is executed via Payments Canada's ACH/ACSS and the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) in CAD, while USD disbursements may be settled through the bank's US correspondent network (Fedwire/ACH) for same-day or next-day value. Where the LP maintains a USD clearing account, funds can be disbursed to US suppliers through direct ACH debits and credits. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex may on-board a Quebec LP only if the EMI is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC in Canada and the LP completes full corporate KYC. EMIs are not OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions, hold funds through bank partners, and are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance. They are legally viable as a supplementary payment corridor for cross-border supplier payouts, but they should not replace a Schedule I commercial bank account. The business description 'off-price liquidation and wholesale trading' must be supported by supplier agreements and commercial invoices or it will be rejected by the EMI's own compliance function.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million in annual cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for transactions. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, transactions lacking commercial rationale may be recharacterized. For LPs, the partnership must demonstrate real business activity, especially for cross-border transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they fail to exercise due diligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 10% of transaction value

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for corporate taxes if the LP is deemed a sham; personal civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal fines and imprisonment for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful blindness.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income, penalties of 10% of net adjustment, plus interest; documentation penalties up to CAD 24,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the mispricing; personal civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes.
