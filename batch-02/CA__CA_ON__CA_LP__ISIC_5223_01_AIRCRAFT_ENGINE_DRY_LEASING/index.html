Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; registration portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account. Ontario Ministry of Finance (harmonized provincial HST component and Employer Health Tax) and Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery (Companies and Personal Property Security Branch) for Limited Partnership registration under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Ontario limited partnership (domestic or extra-provincial) with the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch (Form 1 for domestic LP; Form 3 for extra-provincial LP) to obtain the Ontario registration number. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the single statutory tax identifier for federal income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and Ontario corporate tax administration under the federal-provincial collection agreement. Step 3: Open a GST/HST program account (RT) with the BN; registration is compulsory if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a four-consecutive-quarter period, and a non-resident aircraft-engine lessor must register voluntarily (or as required by nexus rules) to zero-rate exported engines and to recover input tax credits on acquisition and carrying costs. Step 4: File an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return (no federal income tax at the LP level because Canadian LPs are fiscally transparent) and issue T5013 slips to partners; administer Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source rental income allocated to non-resident partners. Step 5: Appoint a Canadian resident representative (Form RC59) if the LP has no physical Canadian establishment, and maintain all GST/HST and income tax records in Canada or accessible electronically to CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; partnerships are statutorily transparent. Relevant elections include: (i) Income Tax Act (ITA) s.102 — non-resident members may elect to render the partnership a non-Canadian partnership; (ii) ITA s.96(3) and s.97(2) — fiscal-period and cost-amount property transfer elections; (iii) ITA s.216 — non-resident lessor election to file a Canadian return and be taxed on net rental income instead of 25% gross-basis Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(1)(d) (rents/royalties for use of property in Canada); (iv) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.156 — nil-consideration election for closely related persons; (v) ETA s.240(2.1) — small-supplier election to register for GST/HST; and (vi) ETA s.252 — non-resident registration/rebate mechanism for GST/HST paid on Canadian expenses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148(1): taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) of CAD 30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters. Non-resident lessors supplying an aircraft engine by dry lease must also assess the ETA s.240 registration obligation based on whether the supply is made in the course of carrying on business in Canada and whether the engine is physically made available in Canada; voluntary registration is available for any commercial activity, and once registered the LP must remain registered for at least one year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The dry lease of a turbofan aircraft engine is a supply of tangible personal property by way of lease under ETA s.136.1. The supply is eligible for the 0% (zero-rated) export rate if: (a) the engine is exported by the supplier as part of the lease (ETA Schedule VI, Part I, s.1 — supply to a non-resident recipient with delivery outside Canada); (b) the engine is supplied to a non-resident air carrier for use principally in international commercial aviation; or (c) the engine qualifies as an aircraft part under ETA Schedule VI, Part VI, s.6 and the corresponding administrative position in CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.5.2 (Taxable Supplies of Aircraft, Aircraft Parts and Related Services). Where the engine is physically delivered into Canada and kept by a Canadian-resident lessee for use primarily in Canada, the lease is taxable at 13% HST and the lessor must collect and remit. The lessor bears the burden of proving, through contemporaneous documentary evidence, that the engine left Canada and was used outside Canada.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CAED) transmitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (or carrier-generated cargo control record), showing the 13-digit export code, engine classification (HS 8411 series), and ultimate foreign destination.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house, where applicable) evidencing physical movement of the engine from Canada to the foreign lessee's jurisdiction.
  • Duly executed dry-lease agreement, engine serial number (ESN) log, and export delivery/redelivery certificate signed by the foreign lessee.
  • Temporary export documentation (CBSA Form E15 or ATA Carnet) if the engine is deployed internationally and returns to Canada.
  • Inward remittance / forex-inflow evidence — SWIFT MT103 payment order, escrow settlement statement, or bank credit advice for each lease rental instalment, reconciled to the invoice and lease schedule.

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime; there is no statutory national e-invoicing mandate, no tax-authority API clearance, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction controls applicable to aviation asset leasing. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (Form GST34-2) and payments remitted through CRA My Payment; commercial invoices exceeding CAD 30 must display the LP's Business Number and GST/HST registration number (ETA s.223). PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is accepted for Canadian public-sector e-invoicing but is not intermediated or validated by the CRA, and books and records must be made available electronically to the CRA upon demand.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of the assessment date (or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default), disallow the zero-rating, and reassess 13% HST together with compound daily interest on the full lease consideration where the export substantiation bundle is incomplete, documents are not contemporaneous, or lease receivables are not repatriated and traceable within the evidentiary 180-day window. Penalties include: (i) failure-to-register penalties of CAD 250 per month up to CAD 2,500 under ETA s.280(1); (ii) late-filing penalties of 1% of unremitted net tax plus 25% of that amount per month, capped at 12 months, under ETA s.280(2); (iii) false-statement and gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the net tax sought to be evaded under ETA s.285 and Income Tax Act s.163(2); (iv) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s.327 and ITA s.239 with fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years; and (v) assessments against partners on a joint and several basis. A taxpayer may obtain penalty relief by making a voluntary disclosure before CRA contact, but no relief is available once an audit or compliance letter has been issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no general exchange-control legislation and no approval or registration is required for inbound capital injections, outbound loan disbursements, lease payments, distributions, or profit repatriation by a non-resident-owned Ontario limited partnership. Withholding taxes apply only to specific Canadian-source payments (e.g., interest, rents, royalties) allocated to non-resident partners under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, with treaty reductions available. Distributions of partnership earnings that retain their character are taxable to non-resident partners under Part I on the allocable share of income; no repatriation permit or conversion quota is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory surrender, forced conversion, or minimum-repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters and lessors must, however, file cross-border currency reports for any physical or wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or above in accordance with FINTRAC's large transaction and cross-border movement reporting rules, and must comply with the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) and the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) sanctions. Dry-leasing arrangements that involve no physical export of controlled aircraft-engine goods from Canada are not subject to any foreign-currency surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority with no statutory foreign-exchange control mandate. Exchange-rate and international capital-payments policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada; cross-border transaction and currency-movement reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Cross-border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations. Ontario-licensed financial institutions are supervised by OSFI and the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for core treasury functions. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorized banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot accept Canadian deposits. They may operate strictly as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and must clear CAD through a Schedule I Canadian bank partner. EMIs are not viable for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, F.X. hedging, or aircraft-trade settlement; they are acceptable only for low-value ancillary disbursements when linked to a compliant Canadian operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All eligible capital contributions must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's bank to the Ontario LP's designated CAD account. The SWIFT remittance-information field must cite the legal basis, e.g., 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — LIMITED PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]', and must reference the capital-call notice number so the receiving bank can reconcile the transaction against the LP's contribution schedule. Canadian banks do not apply a uniform country-level purpose code; instead, the payment narrative, originator KYC, and matching reference are the verification standard. Transfers of CAD 10,000 or above are automatically captured in FINTRAC large-transaction reporting. Additional supporting documents include a signed capital-call notice, an updated partner register, and a certification confirming no sanctioned persons are involved.Primary outbound clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for lease-rental distributions, maintenance-reserve releases, and vendor payments.
