E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Ontario statutory registration: file the declaration for the Limited Partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, through the Ontario Business Registry to obtain the Ontario Business Identifier (OBI) and certificate of registration. Step 2 - Federal Business Number (BN): apply to the CRA for the 9-digit BN using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via BRO or by mail, selecting program accounts (RT for GST/HST and RM for import/export). Step 3 - Non-resident partner compliance: each non-resident partner must supply evidence of tax residence; if the general partner is non-resident, the LP must designate a Canadian agent for service and may appoint a GST/HST representative under section 267 of the Excise Tax Act (Canada). Step 5 - Partnership return: the LP must file the annual partnership information return (Form T5013) with CRA, allocate income to partners per the partnership agreement, and retain books and records for a minimum of six years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent flow-through). Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada), the Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer; income and loss are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who bear Canadian tax on their respective shares. Ontario conforms under the Taxation Act, 2007 (Ontario); therefore, no entity-level corporate income tax is payable by the LP in Ontario. Territorial scope: Canadian tax applies to Canadian-source income allocated to Canadian and non-resident partners; non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding or Part I tax on Canadian-source allocations. Tax-exempt zone: not applicable - Canada does not grant income tax amnesty for domestic processing zones; only customs duty relief (e.g., bonded warehouse regimes) is available under the Customs Act.

Available election forms

Election under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act: a Canadian partnership satisfying the statutory conditions may, with partner consent and using the CRA prescribed election form, be deemed a corporation for federal and provincial income tax purposes, thereby terminating the automatic flow-through treatment. Excise Tax Act section 273 election: the partners may jointly elect a single designated partner to act as agent/representative for GST/HST filing, remittance, and input tax credit claims. Canada has no check-the-box classification regime for LPs; the default flow-through status is mandatory absent a valid 96(3) election. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss.17.3-17.4 and the excess interest and financing expense limitation, ss.18.2-18.3) are mechanical and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s.148(1)): if the LP's total consideration from taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceeds CAD 30,000, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory effective the first day of the month following the qualifying quarter. Below the threshold, the LP is a small supplier, need not register, and generally cannot claim input tax credits. The LP may nevertheless register voluntarily under ETA s.240(1.1) to recover federal/ provincial HST paid on inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under ETA s.22 when the goods are exported by the registrant from Canada to a destination outside Canada as part of the LP's commercial activity and the LP retains full documentary proof of export. Services performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident for use outside Canada may qualify under ETA s.23. Domestic Canadian sales of freeze-dried pet food are taxable at 13% HST because pet food is not a zero-rated basic grocery item for human consumption. For export transactions, the LP must invoice at 0%, mark the invoice as a zero-rated export, ship the goods within a reasonable period (CRA administrative expectation: generally 60-90 days from supply), and preserve eligibility to claim input tax credits attributable to the exported goods.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration, CAED) with the CBSA confirmation number, mandatory for shipments valued over CAD 2,000.
  • Original carrier-issued Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the freeze-dried pet food from Canada to the foreign destination.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (e.g., HS 2309.10/2309.90, dog/cat food preparations) identifying the foreign buyer, Incoterms, and export sales price.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or bank payment confirmation demonstrating receipt of the export sale proceeds in the LP's bank account.
  • Supporting trade documents including the signed purchase order/export contract, transport insurance certificate, terminal/warehouse receipt, and foreign customs entry evidence (e.g., US Customs Entry Summary) confirming arrival at destination.

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API pre-authorization, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies to GST/HST in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework built on the PEPPOL network and the Canadian National Standard (CNR); federal procurement is moving toward mandatory acceptance of PEPPOL BIS 3.0 invoices through certified Canadian Access Points. The LP must issue valid GST/HST invoices showing the vendor BN, transaction date, description, and HST charged, and must retain all electronic and paper records for six years in an auditable format per Excise Tax Act s.286.

On audit, CRA may deny the 0% rate entirely where the export documentation bundle is incomplete or internally inconsistent, then assess GST/HST payable with prescribed interest (ETA s.280.1) and late-filing penalties (ETA s.280). A false statement or omission in a GST/HST return, including an inflated or fabricated export zero-rating claim, attracts a penalty under ETA s.285 equal to the greater of CAD 4,000 and 25% of the net tax that would have been payable had the false statement not been made. Knowingly or recklessly claiming export zero-rating without the actual export exposes the LP and its partners to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s.327: on summary conviction a fine of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months; on indictment a fine of CAD 5,000-CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, in addition to forfeiture or seizure of goods under the Customs Act. Where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or where fraud is established, CRA may reassess and claw back previously allowed zero-rated refunds and input tax credits without statutory time limitation pursuant to Excise Tax Act s.298(6).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. There are no Canadian exchange-control laws requiring central bank approval for profit remittance, capital repatriation, trade settlement, or dividend and partnership distributions. Non-resident partners may receive distributions freely; however, Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) may apply, and certain large cross-border transfers attract FINTRAC reporting and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandated conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines. Export receipts may be retained offshore or held in foreign currency within Canada. For audit and tax substantiation, the limited partnership should retain export invoices, customs declarations, bank settlement advices, and SWIFT records to support revenue recognition and GST/HST return positions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as federal banking regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor. Canada has no separate foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for an unlicensed offshore digital EMI to act as the primary treasury clearing rail. An offshore electronic money institution is not automatically permitted to hold deposits, originate payment transactions, or clear funds in Canada; it must comply with the Bank Act (Canada) deposit-taking prohibitions, the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada), FINTRAC money-services-business registration, and provincial money-services or prepaid payment licensing. Cross-border EMIs may be used only as a payment facilitator through a licensed Canadian partner; the Ontario limited partnership must maintain a deposit account at a Payments Canada member bank for regulated clearing.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Limited Partnership is tax-transparent: no entity-level Canadian income tax. Corporate partners resident in Ontario pay combined federal/Ontario general rate of 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario) on allocated income; small-business rate 12.2% on active business income up to C$500,000 if eligible CCPC. Individual partners pay graduated provincial/federal rates up to 53.53%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Canadian Part XIII withholding on payments to non-residents of dividends from Canadian corporations and on partnership distributions or deemed allocation of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners. A reduced rate applies under applicable income tax treaty (e.g., 5% for a US corporation owning at least 10% voting shares; 15% otherwise; for partnership distributions, treaty rate may be 0% on business profits if no permanent establishment).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous documentation to avoid transfer-pricing penalties when the taxpayer has cross-border non-arm's-length transactions. A safe-harbor penalty threshold applies to any single transaction with gross proceeds exceeding C$1 million or to transactions with an aggregate amount exceeding C$5 million in a taxation year. The CbC reporting threshold is C$750 million in consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) and a substance-over-form doctrine; arrangements that are solely tax-motivated without real economic impact (and not statutory avoidance transactions) may be recharacterized. Limited partnerships are scrutinized under s.96 and s.102 to ensure allocations have economic substance and the partnership carries on a real business with employees, premises, and business plan. An Ontario LP must maintain minimum defined substance or risk denial of treaty benefits and application of anti-shell rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Representation

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239: criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded or imprisonment up to 5 years (indictable) and disgorgement of profits; civil penalty under s.163(2) equals 50% of the understated tax. CRA may revoke the partnership's registration and reassess with negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners can be assessed personally under s.160/227.1 for unpaid withholdings and taxes; civil liability for fraudulent transfers; criminal conviction can trigger travel bans, extradition, and incarceration. Directors cannot rely on the corporate veil for tax evasion.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable allocations using the arm's-length standard and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on every adjustment exceeding C$1 million per transaction or C$5 million aggregate, plus interest and late penalties; localization of profits may be challenged under GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for unremitted withholding and for penalties related to their own gross negligence; they can be held jointly and severally liable for tax adjustments in cases involving deliberate transfer-pricing concealment, and may face imprisonment and travel restrictions if criminally prosecuted.
Shell Conduit / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership arrangement under s.245 (GAAR) and reallocate income to the true Canadian partners; adverse DTA relief denied (e.g., for a US partner without a permanent establishment); a substantial penalty (50% gross negligence) and interest may apply; Ontario Ministry may dissolve the LP for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its directors face unlimited liability for partnership obligations under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act; tax liabilities may attach to their personal assets on the basis of piercing the corporate veil; criminal fraud charges can result in travel bans and imprisonment.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The partnership is liable for the entire uncollected withholding amount, a 10% late-remittance penalty (if unpaid), plus daily interest; CRA can issue a requirement to pay under s.224 and seize assets; fines and criminal charges can arise if repeated.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable under s.227.1 of the ITA for withholdings not remitted; CRA may assess them immediately, place a personal lien on their passport through court order, and prosecute criminal failure to remit, leading to imprisonment.
