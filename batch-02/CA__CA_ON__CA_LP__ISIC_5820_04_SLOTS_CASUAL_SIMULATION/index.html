Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA) are accessible at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

An Ontario Limited Partnership obtains a statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST program account through the CRA either online via BRO, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts) with the International Tax Services Office in Sudbury, Ontario where the applicant is a non-resident. The general partner must provide Form RC59 (business consent), the LP's registration certificate or declaration filed under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, and identity documentation for the partners. No minimum capital is required. The BN is issued to the LP in its own name as a separate person for GST/HST purposes; partners are not assigned a BN through the LP. For income tax, the LP is fiscally transparent and files only a T5013 partnership information return when required; each partner reports its allocated share under its own tax identifier. A non-resident partnership should appoint a Canadian representative to accept CRA correspondence and service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada), an Ontario Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer; all income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits flow through to partners pursuant to ITA s. 96. The LP is nonetheless treated as a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes under ETA s. 123(1). Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income including income from a partnership business carried on in Canada, and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain distributions unless relief is available under an applicable income tax treaty. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada), an Ontario Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer; all income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits flow through to partners pursuant to ITA s. 96. The LP is nonetheless treated as a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes under ETA s. 123(1). Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income including income from a partnership business carried on in Canada, and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain distributions unless relief is available under an applicable income tax treaty. The LP is not located in a tax-exempt zone and has no federal or provincial entity-level income tax liability.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election comparable to U.S. IRC s. 7701; entity classification follows legal form and the LP is automatically fiscally transparent. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240(2.1) before the CAD 30,000 threshold is reached; (ii) election of a non-calendar partnership fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1 where all partners are resident in Canada, subject to CRA acceptance; (iii) GST/HST quick method election for qualifying small businesses where the eligibility conditions of ETA s. 153 and Schedule VIII are met; (iv) for a U.S. partner that elects on U.S. Form 8832 to treat the Ontario LP as a corporation for U.S. tax purposes, Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21, reflecting OECD BEPS Action 2) apply automatically to partnership payments where there is a deduction/non-inclusion outcome; no election is available to exempt a taxpayer from these hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies measured worldwide, including supplies made by the LP and associated persons, over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). Registration becomes mandatory at the beginning of the calendar month following the quarter in which the threshold is exceeded. Non-resident suppliers of digital services and downloadable software to Canadian consumers are subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold and may register under the CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration framework.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST-registered Ontario LP may zero-rate supplies of its social casino software to non-residents under ETA Schedule VI, Part V if: (i) the intangible personal property is supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada; (ii) the property is not to be used or exploited in Canada; and (iii) the supplier possesses contemporaneous documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, the foreign destination of use, and the foreign payment trail. For digital distribution through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, zero-rating depends on app-store transaction reports showing the end-user's billing address and the Apple/Google payout reports identifying foreign jurisdictions. Where Apple or Google is the supplier of record under CRA marketplace guidance, the LP must obtain an underlying software license agreement and supporting platform reports to substantiate that the end-supply was made to non-resident consumers and is not taxable in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) - required only where software is embedded in tangible media such as disks or servers; no ACD/AES customs filing is required for download-only delivery
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing export from Canada (tangible media only)
  • Commercial invoice describing the software product, referencing ISIC 5820 / NAICS 511210, and stating 'Zero-rated export under ETA Schedule VI, Part V'
  • Executed software license, distribution, or reseller agreement identifying the non-resident counterparty and the licensed foreign territory
  • Certificate or other evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including foreign VAT/GST registration number, foreign business registry extract, or foreign address documentation
  • Inward remittance evidence, including bank statements and FX settlement records from Apple, Google, or the foreign distributor showing receipt of foreign currency within 180 days; Canada does not issue a formal Inward Remittance Certificate so bank records serve as the equivalent
  • Apple/Google app-store payout and tax reports identifying foreign end-user jurisdictions and supporting the attribution of zero-rated supplies

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping at either the federal or Ontario level. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Service, which is based on the Peppol network and CIUS-EN 16931 standard, is voluntary for B2B and B2G transactions. Registrants must retain books, tax invoices, and source documents in readable and machine-readable form for at least six years under CRA Information Circular IC 05-1. Larger registrants are generally required to file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or by XML/EDI transmission; no CRA pre-approval or digital stamping of invoices is required.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within the normal four-year reassessment period, and no limitation period applies where a registrant has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). A fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers: (i) a gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280 equal to 25% of the net tax assessed; (ii) a false-statement penalty under ETA s. 281 of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; (iii) daily compound interest on the unremitted HST; (iv) on criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327, fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment for up to five years; (v) clawback of the refund, denial of input tax credits, reassessment of both the LP and its partners, and potential inclusion of the LP's name in the CRA's public list of deliberate non-compliance. The CRA may also audit third-party platform records, treasury settlement files, and partner-level documentation during export-supply reviews.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under Canada's free-floating exchange-rate regime. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions under the Bank of Canada Act or the Currency Act. Profit remittances and capital repatriation from an Ontario limited partnership are permitted without Bank of Canada approval, but remain subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax, CRS/FATCA reporting, and applicable Ontario partnership reporting obligations. The social casino and virtual slots software-publishing activity ISIC 5820/NAICS 511210 is not a restricted or regulated sector for inward investment under the Investment Canada Act, and no gaming license is required because no real-money wagering or prize redemption is conducted; however, provincial consumer-protection and advertising standards may still apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds. All export receipts, including software licensing royalties and app-store settlement receivables, may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held in Canadian or offshore foreign-currency accounts, and converted to Canadian dollars at market rates. No central-bank filing or approval is required for receipt, retention, or conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); bank regulation and supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Investment Canada Act screening by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for unlicensed foreign EMIs: Canada does not recognize the European EMI passport, and no separate 'electronic money institution' licensing class exists federally. A non-bank fintech or foreign payment institution seeking to provide treasury services to the Ontario LP must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, may require provincial money-services licensure in Ontario, and cannot accept deposits in the manner of a bank. Foreign EMI accounts may be used only as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail for Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements, never as the primary bank or escrow host. Treasury policy should require all cross-border principal balances to settle in a Canadian Schedule I bank account.

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be routed by SWIFT MT103 to a Canadian-dollar account held in the exact name of the Ontario limited partnership. Banks typically require a certified copy of the Ontario registration, the partnership agreement, a capital-commitment letter, and prior completion of the bank's foreign-exchange or international-services onboarding pack. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services transactions with non-residents; CAD 5 million for goods transactions; CAD 1 million for other transactions (e.g., royalties, interest). Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like UAE, but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. For treaty benefits, the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause in Canada-US treaty requires substantive business operations. Social casino publishing must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, office, decision-making) to avoid treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to withhold or remit (s.227.1). Travel bans and asset freezes possible under tax collection measures.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying treaty benefits, imposition of 25% withholding on all payments, and penalties up to 10% of the avoided tax. CRA may also apply transfer pricing adjustments with penalties up to 10% of net adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate general partners may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they authorized the sham arrangement. Criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, criminal fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals. Non-compliance with record-keeping and reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML policies. Personal travel bans and seizure of assets may occur under criminal forfeiture provisions.
