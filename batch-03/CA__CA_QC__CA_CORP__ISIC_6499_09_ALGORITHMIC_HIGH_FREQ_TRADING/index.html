Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec (RQ), the provincial tax authority administering QST, Quebec corporate tax, and source deductions via CléNumérique d'affaires (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/). Under the Canada-Quebec Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA), registrants with a permanent establishment in Quebec file GST/HST and QST returns with Revenu Québec as a single combined return.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or BRO online, providing legal name, registered office address in Canada, and, for non-resident applicants, evidence of Canadian residency of the agent for service of process; (2) open BN program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (corporate income tax), and RP (payroll/remittance), as applicable; (3) where a Quebec establishment exists, register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and with Revenu Québec for the QST account (extra-provincial entity classification) and, if employees, for CNESST and source deductions; (4) for GST/HST purposes in Quebec, file the combined GST/HST-QST registration through Revenu Québec, which administers both taxes under the CITCA; (5) submit certified copies of the certificate of incorporation/articles (federal or extra-provincial registration in Quebec), director and officer identification, and board resolution authorizing the tax filings. Normal processing takes 5-10 business days; no minimum capital, notarized charter, or local shareholding requirement applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Quebec-incorporated business corporation is a Canadian tax-resident corporation (central management and control presumed in Quebec) subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income (federal general corporate rate 15%) and Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Quebec general rate 11.5%); no flow-through or check-the-box classification is available. Territorial scope: worldwide income taxation for residents with foreign tax credits and treaty relief for foreign-source income; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding on dividends (25% treaty-reduced) and could face Part XIV branch tax if the corporation is thinly capitalized. The proprietary HFT entity is a 'financial institution' under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and, if gross revenues exceed CAD 10 million, a 'selected financial institution' (SFI), engaging the special attribution method (SAM) for GST/HST and QST input tax credits. — Foreign entities must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or BRO online, providing legal name, registered office address in Canada, and, for non-resident applicants, evidence of Canadian residency of the agent for service of process; (2) open BN program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (corporate income tax), and RP (payroll/remittance), as applicable; (3) where a Quebec establishment exists, register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and with Revenu Québec for the QST account (extra-provincial entity classification) and, if employees, for CNESST and source deductions; (4) for GST/HST purposes in Quebec, file the combined GST/HST-QST registration through Revenu Québec, which administers both taxes under the CITCA; (5) submit certified copies of the certificate of incorporation/articles (federal or extra-provincial registration in Quebec), director and officer identification, and board resolution authorizing the tax filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Quebec-incorporated business corporation is a Canadian tax-resident corporation (central management and control presumed in Quebec) subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income (federal general corporate rate 15%) and Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Quebec general rate 11.5%); no flow-through or check-the-box classification is available. Territorial scope: worldwide income taxation for residents with foreign tax credits and treaty relief for foreign-source income; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding on dividends (25% treaty-reduced) and could face Part XIV branch tax if the corporation is thinly capitalized. The proprietary HFT entity is a 'financial institution' under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and, if gross revenues exceed CAD 10 million, a 'selected financial institution' (SFI), engaging the special attribution method (SAM) for GST/HST and QST input tax credits.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available elections include: (1) ETA s. 225.2(6)/(7) group elections for SFIs to use an aggregated calculation of input tax credits and the special attribution method (SAM) schedules; (2) ITA s. 128.1(1) deemed-disposition/emigration election for departure from Canada, relevant if the HFT corporation relocates; (3) ITA ss. 216 and 217 net-basis elections generally for non-residents receiving Canadian rental/trust income, not applicable to HFT business profits earned through a permanent establishment (taxed under ITA s. 115); (4) hybrid mismatch and foreign affiliate elections under ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, and 95(6) where the HFT entity holds foreign trading subsidiaries; (5) ETA s. 150 election for qualifying non-registered financial institutions and ETA s. 151 election to use a special ITC calculation for financial institutions; (6) election with Revenu Québec to file a combined GST/HST-QST return on an annual rather than monthly basis for non-SFI filers. All elections require prescribed forms filed with CRA or Revenu Québec before the statutory filing deadline and are generally irrevocable or subject to CRA discretion to revoke.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies such as financial services) over any four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA s. 148 and the Quebec QST Act. Special deeming rules apply to financial institutions: an entity whose principal business is the buying and selling of financial instruments for its own account (HFT proprietary market maker) is a financial institution under ETA s. 123(1), and ETA s. 148(1) deems such a person not to be a small supplier unless the total consideration for its financial services in the preceding four quarters is CAD 30,000 or less. In practice, an active HFT trading entity must register for GST/HST and QST immediately at incorporation, and a 'selected financial institution' (gross revenue greater than CAD 10 million) must register regardless of the small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, sections 1-5 where the supplier exports goods 'as soon as practicable' after the supply and retains prescribed proof of export (B13A declaration, carrier waybill, customs entry, or evidence of mail export). Services: financial services supplied to a non-resident person, and not supplied to the recipient's Canadian establishment, are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7; proprietary self-account HFT trading is generally an exempt supply (not a chargeable supply) because no financial service is supplied to any counterparty, so no GST/HST/QST is collectible and input tax credits on trading-related inputs are correspondingly restricted. Where the HFT entity separately charges non-resident customers for market-making services, liquidity rebates, or colocation-related services, those supplies require a written contract, trade confirmation, and evidence that the recipient is a non-resident to sustain zero-rating. QST zero-rating mirrors the GST/HST federal rules. Refunds of net GST/HST/QST (refundable ITC balances) are paid after CRA/Revenu Québec assessment, generally within 30-60 days of filing and subject to a four-year application period.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate - SWIFT MT103, bank statement, letter of credit, or payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the Canadian entity's account

GST/HST and QST returns are transmitted electronically through Revenu Québec's Transmission électronique (XML/EDI) system or CRA's GST/HST NETFILE service; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and may use EDI or PDF-based formats. Every invoice must contain the supplier's name and BN/QST number, date, description of supply, consideration, and GST/QST amounts or a clear zero-rating/exemption indicator. Records must be retained for six years and be accessible from Canada, with electronic records accepted in readable format. SFIs must file an annual GST/HST information return (Form GST111) reconciling the SAM, and Revenu Québec may impose penalties for late or incomplete filing, missing invoice content, or failure to remit QST collected.

Zero-rating claims attract elevated audit scrutiny. CRA and Revenu Québec auditors require the full export documentation bundle to be contemporaneous; failure to substantiate within the normal four-year reassessment period results in reassessment of the zero-rated supplies as taxable at 14.5% combined, denial of ITCs, and refund clawback. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully convertible current and capital account regimes consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations; there is no exchange-control licensing, no surrender requirement, and no pre-approval barrier for bona fide capital injections, operating dividends, treasury repatriations, or intercompany FX flows. The Canadian dollar is free-floating, and market settlement occurs without central bank rationing. The only substantive remittance hurdle is taxation rather than FX: cross-border profit distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding at the 25% federal statutory rate, treaty-reduced under the Canada-U.S. and Canada-EU arrangements to 5%-15% for dividends and 0%-15% for qualifying interest; Québec applies its own provincial withholding overlay on dividends paid by a Québec-resident payer. For a proprietary algorithmic HFT treasury, the correct assessment is full convertibility with tax-costed remittance; capital account flows require no Bank of Canada approval, but must be documented as paid-up capital or arm's length debt to prevent Part XIII and section 15 recharacterization of shareholder advances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation: the mandatory conversion ratio is zero, there is no transfer-and-repatriation deadline, and no rule forcing liquidation of foreign-currency receipts into CAD. Trading and clearing proceeds may remain indefinitely in USD, EUR, or third-currency Nostro balances. Practical constraints are limited to: (i) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting by the financial institution for every wire of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Income Tax Act record retention for six years from the relevant tax year-end; and (iii) OSFI large-exposure and prudent liquidity reporting once balances exceed prescribed thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Exchange Fund Account; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while Québec securities and derivatives market conduct is overseen by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW-TO-MODERATE as a treasury auxiliary, but NOT bankable as a primary account surface. Canada has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing track under the Bank Act; an offshore fintech or EMI serving a Canadian treasury is jurally characterised as a money services business (MSB) and must be FINTRAC-registered with a dedicated AML/ATF program, a Canadian agent, and beneficial-ownership disclosure. The AMF may additionally trigger Québec money-services licensing and derivatives-related registration where the fintech routes payment instructions for a proprietary trading vehicle. Non-resident EMIs cannot custody capital escrow as a deposit, cannot hold direct Lynx settlement membership, and must in practice maintain all client funds through a Schedule I chartered bank sub-account. The legal consequence for treasury architecture is therefore that offshore EMIs are suitable only for virtual-IBAN sweeping, back-office FX conversion, payroll, and low-value vendor payments; the entire inbound capital base and every clearing-chain obligation must remain on the Tier-1 banking surface.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be routed as a straightforward electronic wire via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV where an intermediate correspondent is used, with the ISO 20022 purpose code CAPT or INTC and a reference narrative quoting the shareholder subscription agreement and board resolution. The remitting and receiving banks concurrently file FINTRAC Cross-Border Electronic Funds Transfer reports for every leg of CAD 10,000 or more. No Bank of Canada authorisation window, exchange-control certificate, or Form-based FX approval exists; the receiving Canadian account becomes fungible on T+0 through the Lynx large-value settlement system. Treasury verification sequence: (1) confirm board resolution number and subscription amount appear in the wire text; (2) reconcile the inward amount to the share-capital ledger line; (3) update the share-capital table at the Registraire des entreprises du Québec; (4) obtain written KYC acceptance of the remitting entity from the receiving bank; and (5) for USD injections, deliver via Fedwire or CHIPS to the Canadian beneficiary's USD correspondent with the same MT103 UETR discipline. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW-TO-MODERATE as a treasury auxiliary, but NOT bankable as a primary account surface. Canada has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing track under the Bank Act; an offshore fintech or EMI serving a Canadian treasury is jurally characterised as a money services business (MSB) and must be FINTRAC-registered with a dedicated AML/ATF program, a Canadian agent, and beneficial-ownership disclosure. The AMF may additionally trigger Québec money-services licensing and derivatives-related registration where the fintech routes payment instructions for a proprietary trading vehicle. Non-resident EMIs cannot custody capital escrow as a deposit, cannot hold direct Lynx settlement membership, and must in practice maintain all client funds through a Schedule I chartered bank sub-account. The legal consequence for treasury architecture is therefore that offshore EMIs are suitable only for virtual-IBAN sweeping, back-office FX conversion, payroll, and low-value vendor payments; the entire inbound capital base and every clearing-chain obligation must remain on the Tier-1 banking surface.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec general corporate rate: 26.5% (federal general rate 15% + Quebec general rate 11.5%). The federal base tax is nominally 38%, reduced by the 10% provincial abatement and the 13% general rate reduction, yielding 15%. A Canadian-controlled private corporation may qualify for the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, producing a combined rate of approximately 12.2% (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%), subject to eligibility and associated-group rules. Proprietary algorithmic high-frequency trading income must be tested as active business income versus specified investment business income.
