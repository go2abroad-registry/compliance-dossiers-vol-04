Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account is mandated for Ontario limited partnerships. Each limited partner must make or agree to make a contribution of money or other property to the partnership under s. 8 of the Limited Partnerships Act; contributed capital must be credited to the partnership's operational bank account maintained at a Canadian chartered bank or authorized credit union. A deficiency in a required contribution may expose the limited partner to liability for the outstanding balance under s. 11.

Funding & unlock path: Approval from the Ontario Fire Marshal / local fire prevention office under the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07); structural load review and certification of dock and fuel-transfer infrastructure by a licensed professional engineer; occupational health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act; WSIB industrial registration and clearance; TSSA inspection of fuel handling and loading equipment; and an approved marine spill response and emergency contingency plan.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Income Tax administration; CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario HST component is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial HST agreement. Ontario Ministry of Finance administers related provincial levies; Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services maintains the Ontario Limited Partnerships Register.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Ontario Limited Partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) program account. The non-resident LP applies using CRA Form RC3480 (Application for Registration under the Excise Tax Act for Non-Resident Registrants) or through CRA My Business Account if a Canadian establishment exists. The application must disclose the partnership's foreign registration details, Canadian residential address or registered Foreign Agent, and the date on which taxable supplies in Canada exceeded CAD 30,000 or are expected to exceed it. Once registered, the LP is assigned an RT (GST/HST) account; separate Ontario income tax registration is not required because the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes and the HST is collected and remitted through the CRA. If the LP carries on business in Canada, it must also file a T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to resident and non-resident partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An Ontario LP is automatically fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; no election is available to treat it as a corporation. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is itself a registrant person and cannot elect to be disregarded. Non-resident partners may, where applicable, file under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for net-base Canadian-source rental income, but this is not relevant to marine bunkering operations. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under subsections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act are mandatory and do not offer elective filtering. There is no statutory election to reclassify the LP for Canadian tax purposes, and the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act provides no alternative tax status election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide? No, in taxable supplies made in Canada over any period of four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada that exceeds this small-supplier threshold is required to register for GST/HST; registration may also be required if the non-resident solicits orders or sells goods in Canada through an employee, agent, or permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of marine bunker fuel and lubricants supplied in Canada is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when the supply is made to a vessel used in international commercial navigation, including cargo or passenger vessels operating between Canada and a foreign destination. The LP must obtain documentary proof that the fuel was delivered to the vessel and that the vessel is engaged in international shipping. For goods generally, zero-rating also applies where the goods are exported by the supplier within a prescribed time, are shipped directly to a non-resident person outside Canada, and are not consumed, used, or acquired while in Canada. Service charges for delivery of bunker fuel are generally zero-rated only if they are incidental or attributable to the zero-rated supply of goods. Input tax credits on expenses attributable to zero-rated exports are fully claimable, and the LP may claim refunds of any HST paid on inputs. Export proceeds should be documented and received within 180 days of export to support the economic substance and documentary chain of the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13 or Canada Border Services Agency EDI equivalent) evidencing the goods left Canada
  • Commercial invoice issued by the bunkering LP and marked 'Zero-rated export — GST/HST not charged'
  • Bill of Lading, shipping manifest, or vessel departure record confirming foreign destination
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) and marine fuel delivery receipt setting out vessel identification and quantity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank payment instruction evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident purchaser
  • Charter party, voyage order, or letter from the vessel operator confirming the vessel's international commercial voyage status
  • GST/HST zero-rated export register maintained by the LP mapping invoices to proof of export and payment

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, nor digital stamping regime. GST/HST invoices must meet prescribed information requirements under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act and must include the LP's Business Number, tax status, supplier name, date, description of supply, and amount of HST charged. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they are legible and retained in a CRA-compliant audit trail. The CRA supports voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing, but there is no statutory clearance or pre-approval mechanism for export invoices.

The CRA treats export zero-rating claims as a high-risk audit area. If the LP cannot substantiate with the required customs and freight documentation, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at the Ontario HST headline rate, and any previously claimed input tax credits or refunds are clawed back with compound interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include the 50% gross negligence penalty for knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence making false statements or omissions, penalties for failure to file GST/HST returns, and fines for false documentation. Additional civil penalties apply for providing misleading export evidence, and criminal prosecution for willful evasion is possible. The normal four-year reassessment period is suspended or extended where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or fraud is established, permitting retrospective clawback of export benefits beyond the ordinary limitation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no general foreign exchange controls or mandatory central bank approval for cross-border fund transfers. Profit remittance from a Canadian limited partnership is unrestricted subject to applicable Canadian withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act and relevant tax treaty relief. Financial institutions apply internal compliance controls for large transfers and must report suspicious transactions under FINTRAC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any designated authority. Export receipts for marine bunkering services may be retained in foreign currency or converted at a commercial bank's prevailing rate. Reporting obligations arise only when cross-border currency flows exceed CAD 10,000, triggering the FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) regime; trade documentation must satisfy CBSA customs and export reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML and cross-border reporting requirements. The Investment Canada Act governs review of foreign direct investment in Canadian limited partnerships.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium. Ontario-regulated fintech and foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) may provide payment services if licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is administratively supervised by the Bank of Canada. Offshore EMIs without Canadian registration face material constraints for CAD-denominated clearing and cannot issue letters of credit or trade financing in Canada. FINTRAC MSB registration, PIPEDA privacy compliance, and OSFI guidance are mandatory. For bunkering operations, a Canadian-insured bank or an OSFI-regulated fintech subsidiary is preferred to support escrow balances and LC issuance; a pure offshore EMI is deemed low viability for medium-risk marine fuel settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Ontario general corporate (11.5%) rate; the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and does not pay income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. For an Ontario LP, profit repatriation is a partnership distribution, not a dividend; income allocated to non-resident partners is taxed under Part I, and a non-resident corporate partner may additionally incur a 25% Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings left in Canada, subject to DTA reduction.

Late filing penalty: CAD 25 per partner per month, up to 12 months; for partnerships with more than 500 partners, CAD 50 per partner per month. Knowingly delivering an incomplete or false return can trigger an additional penalty of 10% of gross revenue under s.162(2.2).
Line 800 Enter total business income, including the corporate partner's allocated share of the Ontario LP's bunkering and fuel-refueling profits. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through the LP must file a T2 and include the partnership allocation; it cannot treat the distribution as a dividend. Late filing penalty: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (for repeat non-filers: 10% plus 2% per month). A further penalty of up to 10% can be imposed for repeated failure, and interest accrues at the CRA prescribed rate (currently 8%).
Line 810 Enter taxable income after applying deductions, contributions, loss carryovers, and any treaty adjustments. Claim DTA relief only where a valid certificate of residence exists and the non-resident does not constitute a permanent establishment under the applicable treaty. Late filing penalty: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (for repeat non-filers: 10% plus 2% per month). A further penalty of up to 10% can be imposed for repeated failure, and interest accrues at the CRA prescribed rate (currently 8%).
Line 950 Enter total tax payable, being the combined federal and Ontario tax after all non-refundable tax credits. Non-resident corporate partners must ensure that any part of the tax not remitted through withholding is paid by the balance-due date; late remittance accrues interest. Late filing penalty: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (for repeat non-filers: 10% plus 2% per month). A further penalty of up to 10% can be imposed for repeated failure, and interest accrues at the CRA prescribed rate (currently 8%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 reporting is mandatory where all non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 per taxation year. Taxpayers with prior-year gross revenue of at least CAD 20,000,000 must maintain a Local File; MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 must also maintain a Master File and file the associated Country-by-Country report (Schedule 25/CBCR).

Economic substance scope: Canada has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE or other tax inversions; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s.245 and the substance-over-form doctrine apply. An Ontario LP engaged in bunkering must demonstrate real personnel, physical facilities, operational contracts, and local decision-making. A structure with no commercial substance in Canada will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (s.239 of the Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty amounting to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction can result in a fine equal to 100%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 2 years. CRA may also seize assets and freeze bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize, concur in, or knowingly fail to correct false returns are personally liable for the penalty portion and may be prosecuted. Civil courts may pierce the limited liability shield where the general partner's design was a sham; travel bans are possible in criminal proceedings and imprisonment may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (s.247)

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the transaction at arm's length and apply a penalty of 10% of the gross adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the taxpayer's reasonable-effort threshold; interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: For an LP, the general partner and its directors are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax and penalties if the general partner acted in the direction of the mispricing. Knowing participation can lead to personal civil restitution and, under s.327, criminal liability for obtaining a rebate or deduction to which the entity is not entitled.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization denies the anticipated tax benefit; CRA can impose transfer-pricing penalties, deny treaty exemption, and use subsection 227.1 to hold the director liable for unreported GST/HST and employee withholdings. Administrative penalties of up to CAD 50,000 for false corporate statements may apply.

Director personal liability: Where the LP is a sham, the corporate veil may be pierced to hold directors personally and civilly liable. The Crown may seek an asset freeze and travel ban during the assessment and enforcement phase; prison is available for unlawful false statements or fraud.
AML / Sanctions Non-Compliance (FINTRAC and Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code money-laundering prosecutions may impose up to 10 years imprisonment and asset forfeiture. Banks involved may be fined and the trading licence revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or reckless disregard of AML obligations can be personally charged. Responsible-person provisions of FINTRAC allow penalties directly against directors. Criminal proceedings may result in travel bans, frozen bank accounts, and personal restitution orders.
