Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 fully subscribed in registered bearer or registered shares; at least 20% of the nominal value per share, and in any event no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash prior to registration. The paid-up funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto / compte de consignation) at a Swiss bank, with the bank's blockage confirmation presented to the Commercial Register; the funds are released only upon successful registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable where the registered office is a virtual or administrative address without employees physically present. If a physical or coworking office is leased, the company must obtain fire safety certification from the Cantonal Fire Police (Kantonale Feuerpolizei), ensure compliance with structural load and earthquake safety requirements under SIA standards, and register the workplace for occupational safety inspection under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG), the Accident Insurance Act (UVG), and the EKAS operational safety directives; a workplace risk assessment and SUVA inspection clearance may be required.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 fully subscribed in registered bearer or registered shares; at least 20% of the nominal value per share, and in any event no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash prior to registration. The paid-up funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto / compte de consignation) at a Swiss bank, with the bank's blockage confirmation presented to the Commercial Register; the funds are released only upon successful registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration Fédérale des Contributions AFC / Amministrazione Federale delle Contribuzioni AFC), official portal: https://www.esty.admin.ch; Swiss central UID register portal: https://www.zefix.ch.

E-government filing portal

Switzerland does not issue a combined national corporate TIN for all tax purposes. A Swiss AG receives a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) upon commercial register entry and obtains a cantonal taxpayer file from the competent cantonal tax authority. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office or branch must obtain a Swiss VAT number (UID/MWST-Nr) directly from the FTA through the eService online registration portal or prescribed VAT registration form; the foreign entity must appoint a Swiss-area fiscal representative acceptable to the FTA when it has no Swiss domestic seat or establishment. A foreign AG with a Swiss permanent establishment additionally registers for Swiss federal/cantonal direct taxes via the relevant cantonal authority.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a separate legal person and a fully autonomous corporate income taxpayer at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. It is not transparent, pass-through, or eligible for partnership-style fiscal flow-through treatment. Non-resident AGs are subject to Swiss tax only on income attributable to a Swiss permanent establishment or on specifically Swiss-source taxable items.

Available election forms

Swiss direct tax law contains no US-style check-the-box or general flow-through election for an AG. Available mechanism: (i) election for VAT group taxation (Organschaft) under the Swiss VAT Act; (ii) election for voluntary VAT registration even where the mandatory turnover threshold is not met; (iii) election to use simplified flat-rate VAT assessment (lump-sum/Saldo method) instead of actual input VAT deduction where conditions are met; (iv) transitional special statuses for qualifying holding, mixed, or principal companies under the 2020 Swiss Tax Reform, only during the applicable transition/expiry periods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 net turnover from supplies of goods/services the place of supply of which is in Switzerland within a business year (mandatory threshold raised from CHF 100,000 to CHF 250,000 effective 1 January 2025). A foreign supplier of digital services, including SaaS/SEO analytics, must register for Swiss VAT once this Swiss turnover threshold is exceeded, and must charge Swiss VAT at the applicable Swiss rate on taxable supplies made to Swiss recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export supplies of goods physically removed from Switzerland and to services supplied to recipients abroad where the place of use and enjoyment is not Switzerland. For digital SaaS/SEO analytics services, the supply is zero-rated when the customer is located outside Switzerland and the service is effectively consumed/used abroad; the taxpayer must establish the recipient's residence and the actual foreign place of benefit, with contemporaneous commercial documentation.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (electronic or paper export declaration with customs office exit proof)
  • Commercial invoice containing export statement and supplier/receiver details
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Waybill, or CMR/road transport document evidencing physical export
  • Contract and payment evidence with the foreign purchaser (an inward remittance certificate is not a Swiss statutory requirement, but should be retained as audit evidence)

No mandatory real-time tax-authority clearance, no national API-based e-invoicing clearance obligation, and no digital tax stamp for standard Swiss B2B/B2C invoices. Switzerland permits electronic invoicing and uses optional QR-billing and private-sector eBill channels. Swiss public-sector procurement is progressively moving toward e-invoicing, but there is no federal tax authority clearance mandate or live stamping mechanism for ordinary Swiss VAT invoices.

The Swiss FTA actively audits export zero-rating claims and requires full, reliable evidence of export or foreign use. Missing or unreliable documentation triggers reassessment of VAT at the standard or reduced rate, plus default interest. Intentional or grossly negligent VAT evasion under Article 96 LTVA is punishable by fines of up to four times the evaded tax; negligence-based evasion carries fines up to twice the evaded tax. The assessment limitation period is generally five years after the end of the relevant tax year, extended to ten years where tax evasion is committed deliberately, and serious cases may be prosecuted under Swiss criminal tax law with additional clawback and publicity consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully convertible currency regime with no distinction between current and capital account transactions. The Swiss Franc (CHF, ISO 4217) is freely tradable, and there are no exchange control laws, prior licensing requirements, or import/export FX restrictions. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are unrestricted; however, dividend distributions from a Swiss AG are subject to Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35%, which is normally reduced to 0-5% under an applicable double tax treaty (DTA), provided the recipient is a treaty-eligible corporate resident and an exemption certificate (Entlastungsverfügung) is obtained from the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA). Repatriation of capital (liquidation proceeds or repaid shareholder loans) is exempt from withholding tax. For SaaS/MarTech operations, no sector-specific currency licensing applies; cross-border subscription payments in USD, EUR, or CHF may be received freely into Swiss bank accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland abolished export proceeds surrender and compulsory conversion requirements in the 1960s; all export receipts, including SaaS subscriptions, digital tool licensing, and analytics service fees, may be retained in the original currency, converted freely, or held abroad. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Only statistical reporting obligations may apply to the SNB for balance-of-payments purposes (direct investment declarations above CHF 10 million), which do not restrict FX flows.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, headquartered in Zurich and Bern; foreign exchange and monetary policy functions consolidated solely at the federal level under the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank (National Bank Act, NBA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High with strict constraints. FINMA-regulated Fintech companies (FinIA/FinSA and banking licence with FinTech deposit facility under Art. 1b Banking Act) may accept public deposits up to CHF 1 million without paying interest, but foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) operating cross-border into Switzerland are not automatically passported; they must operate through a Swiss-regulated partner bank or obtain a FINMA licence for regulated deposit-taking activities. [X]' or 'Share capital increase per notarized deed of [date]' – accurate purpose code assignment is mandatory for SNB balance-of-payments statistics: use 'S0100' (exceptional financing) for equity injections, 'S0110' for subordinated shareholder loans, and 'S0990' for ordinary trade advances. Verification required by receiving bank: original notarized board resolution or shareholder resolution (Beschluss der Generalversammlung), certified list of shareholdings, and confirmation that paid-up capital meets the CHF 100,000 statutory minimum per AG law (Art. 621 CO, with CHF 50,000 mandatory paid-in); escrow account for pre-corporate-formation funds must be labeled 'Gründungskonto / Capital Escrow' and frozen until registration in the Swiss Commercial Register (SHAB).For SaaS tool subscription royalties and licensing fees, primary clearing rails are (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT / SCT Instant) for EUR-denominated payments to EU beneficiaries, (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA USD/CHF/GBP and other currency cross-border trades, and (3) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for domestic CHF transactions, all executed through SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 pain.001 messages if API-connected. Commercial LC (documentary credits UCP600) is generally unsuitable for intangible software licensing and is discouraged; instead, for high-value AI-data licensing deals (> CHF 250k), use SWIFT MT103 with advance payment, or bank payment obligation (BPO) under URBPO 750 where the bank verifies digital performance obligations; if customer insists on LC, use a standby LC (SBLC, ISP98) as a performance guarantee, not as the settlement rail. Card-network settlements from Stripe/Paddle (Visa/MC/Amex settlement currencies USD/EUR/GBP/CHF) transit through acquiring banks to the operating account with a 2-day rolling settlement.Deliverable FX Forwards (outrights) in USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, GBP/CHF – standard tenors up to 12 months, OTC with UBS/ZKB, requiring minimum CHF-equivalent exposure of CHF 250,000 and ISDA or Swiss Master Agreement documentation.FX Swaps (Rolling Spot) – permissible for short-term liquidity smoothing between CHF, EUR, and USD without changing underlying exposure; commonly settled via CLS or bilateral with bank.FX Options (plain vanilla calls/puts, EUR vs CHF and USD vs CHF) – available from tier-1 counterparties; premiums treated as treasury expense deductible for Swiss tax purposes if documented as hedging under the entity's confirmed treasury policy.Cross-Currency Swaps – permitted but rarely used for a low-risk SaaS exporter; require a fully negotiated ISDA CSA and are not advisable below CHF 5 million notional; obtainable from UBS Global Markets or ZKB Structured Products.NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards) – generally NOT permissible for CHF pairs (CHF is fully deliverable) and not recommended; NDFs available only for counterparty-requirement transactions in restricted currencies (e.g., INR, KRW, CNY), executed through UBS/Lombard Odier, and prohibited in the ordinary course unless the treasury policy explicitly authorises exotic restricted-currency hedging.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High with strict constraints. FINMA-regulated Fintech companies (FinIA/FinSA and banking licence with FinTech deposit facility under Art. 1b Banking Act) may accept public deposits up to CHF 1 million without paying interest, but foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) operating cross-border into Switzerland are not automatically passported; they must operate through a Swiss-regulated partner bank or obtain a FINMA licence for regulated deposit-taking activities. An offshore EMI account is viable as a cash-in/collection rail for low-risk SaaS revenue (Stripe/Paddle settlements), but it cannot serve as the primary fiduciary treasury account for capital injections or statutory distribution escrow. Optimal solution: maintain the tier-1 operational account at UBS/ZKB for statutory compliance and use a FINMA-supervised fintech (e.g., Rellevate, Yapeal, or Swissquote) for high-velocity card-settlement sweeps, with daily automatic sweeps into the tier-1 account.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory rate 8.5% imposed on profit after tax, equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit; combined cantonal/municipal corporate income tax headline rates range from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to 21.0% (Geneva). The expected combined headline rate for the Zug-based AG is approximately 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% on any dividend, profit share, or other distribution of profits to shareholders under Art. 1 to 4 of the Swiss Federal Withholding Tax Act (VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due within 60 to 90 days of the fiscal year-end or of service of the forms; the canton may grant extensions, commonly to 30 June for calendar-year companies; after reminder, the tax authority sets a binding final deadline under Art. 132 DBG and corresponding cantonal law.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The withholding tax on each distribution must be declared and paid within 30 days after the end of the quarter in which the distribution was made; the annual reconciliation return is due by 31 March of the following year with limited extension possibilities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is required where the worldwide group's consolidated gross revenue equals or exceeds CHF 50 million, consistent with BEPS Action 13. Switzerland imposes no statutory Local File monetary threshold; however, the AG must produce transfer pricing documentation within 60 days of a tax audit demand for all cross-border related-party transactions. In audit practice, related-party transactions below CHF 100,000 per category are treated as de minimis and may not require a full comparability study; above this level, arm's length pricing must be evidenced for software royalties, SaaS subscription fees, and intercompany service charges.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulation regime in the manner of offshore hubs; economic substance is nevertheless enforced through: (1) the principal purpose test in all Swiss DTAs, which denies treaty benefits to letterbox companies; (2) the nexus requirement of the patent/IP box, which requires qualifying R&D activity in Switzerland; (3) the permanent establishment doctrine and anti-abuse jurisprudence, which reattribute income to the place where the real management and value-creating functions occur. A Zug-registered AG with no local employees, no office, and no autonomous board cannot claim treaty protection or the cantonal tax advantages.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through under-declared software revenue (Steuerhinterziehung, Art. 175 DBG and cantonal equivalents)

Statutory sanction: Payment of the evaded tax plus a surcharge of one to three times the evaded federal tax; cantonal surcharges are added; default interest accrues on the entire unpaid balance; repeated evasion leads to higher penalties and criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt under Art. 754 CO and Art. 12 VStR where they caused the under-declaration; foreign directors are subject to judicial custody requests and extradition under bilateral assistance; Swiss debt enforcement may withdraw passports and impose exit restrictions under Art. 91 SchKG to secure payment of large public-law claims.
Tax fraud with forged books or documents (Steuerbetrug, Art. 186 DBG; Art. 14/15 VStG)

Statutory sanction: Intentional tax fraud carries imprisonment of up to five years and a monetary fine of up to three times the evaded tax; the criminal proceeds and the software platform used in the offence are subject to confiscation under Art. 70 ff. StGB; corporate criminal liability permits fines up to CHF 5 million under Art. 102 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face pre-trial detention and automatic travel restrictions upon indictment; civil liability is unlimited for intentional wrongdoing; a criminal conviction triggers disqualification from serving as directors or auditors and may lead to deregistration of the AG; passports are withdrawn by the criminal judge where flight risk is established.
Shell or letterbox company lacking economic substance and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority reallocates income to the entity performing the value-creating functions under Art. 129 DBG, denies treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, revokes patent box relief, and imposes default penalties of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; withholding tax refunds are reclaimed with interest; the commercial register may initiate dissolution proceedings for abuse of law.

Director personal liability: A director acting as a straw person is personally assessed for the unpaid tax where the corporate veil is pierced for intentional abuse; civil courts apply Art. 754 CO to attach personal assets of directors who knowingly maintained a sham structure; travel and asset restrictions are enforced through ordinary debt collection once corporate assets are insufficient.
Anti-money laundering violations under the Swiss AML Act and Art. 305bis/305ter StGB

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis StGB: imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years where the perpetrator knows that the assets originate from a criminal organisation; Art. 305ter StGB punishes inadequate organisational measures with an additional fine; corporate fines up to CHF 5 million under Art. 102 StGB; the illicit funds are irrevocably confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal criminal liability for failing to implement and supervise AML controls; personal confiscation of benefits, pre-trial detention, and extradition to foreign authorities are available; conviction results in disqualification from board positions and in restrictive conditions on the AG's banking relationships, including potential forced account closure.
