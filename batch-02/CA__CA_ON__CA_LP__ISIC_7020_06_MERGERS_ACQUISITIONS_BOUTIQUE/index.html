Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and Business Number registration, accessed via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial program accounts, accessed via the Ontario Business Registry and ONT-TAXS (Ontario Retail Sales Tax, Employer Health Tax).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990, c. L.16) with ServiceOntario/Ontario Business Registry to obtain the statutory Ontario LP declaration number. Step 2: Complete CRA registration through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 in the partnership's name; select the GST/HST program account (program code RT). The registered general partner acts as the representative registrant; the Excise Tax Act treats the partnership as a separate person for GST/HST purposes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96; no entity-level income tax applies and each partner is taxed on its allocated share. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a deemed registrant person and is the entity required to charge, collect, and remit tax on its Canadian taxable supplies. Territorial scope: Canada taxes partners on a worldwide basis through the look-through of partnership income; export supplies are zero-rated.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows legal form. Available mechanisms for an Ontario LP: (1) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election (Form T2059) permitting a tax-deferred transfer of eligible capital property to the partnership; (2) ITA s. 96(3)/96(3.1) elections regularize late or misfiled partnership information returns and fix deemed fiscal-period dates; (3) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240 for small suppliers, enabling full input tax credit recovery on Canadian establishment costs when the boutique makes exclusively zero-rated export supplies; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, and 20.2 apply mandatorily and may override partnership pass-through status in cross-border structures involving non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in world-wide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated financial services) measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, with a separate CAD 30,000 single-calendar-quarter test determining loss of small-supplier status; below this threshold registration is not mandatory but is available voluntarily under ETA s. 240.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For M&A advisory services, zero-rating applies under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where all of the following hold: (1) the recipient is a non-resident person (an individual without a primary establishment in Canada or a corporation not resident in Canada); (2) the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant acquiring the service for use in a commercial activity carried on in Canada; (3) the service is not rendered to the recipient while physically present in Canada; (4) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply; and (5) the service is not supplied in the course of a business carried on in Canada by the recipient. If any condition fails, the supply is taxable at 13% HST and the invoice must reflect that tax; the zero-rated character must be evidenced by written contract clauses and records retained for at least six years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A) or CBSA eManifest export filing where goods accompany advisory deliverables (not required for pure cross-border services)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing cross-border movement, where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 wire transfer advice showing payment from the non-resident client to the LP's Canadian bank account
  • Signed engagement letter and final success-fee agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, the offshore target and transaction, and the place of benefit
  • Commercial invoice containing the LP's Business Number and the notation 'Zero-rated export — ETA Sch. VI Part V s. 7 — 0% GST/HST'
  • Non-resident declaration or permanent establishment appointment letter obtained from the foreign client confirming its non-resident status

Canada operates no government-run real-time e-invoicing clearance, mandatory API authentication, or digital stamping regime. Mandated mechanics are: (i) GST/HST registrants with annual world-wide taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE and CRA web services; (ii) every registrant must issue commercial invoices containing supplier legal name, Business Number and GST/HST registration number, transaction date, description, and the tax charge or explicit zero-rating notation; (iii) cross-border goods movements require CBSA eManifest (ACI) filings and the Single Window / CARM portal; (iv) Ontario RST and EHT returns are filed through ONT-TAXS.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period under ETA s. 298 and without time limit where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. If an export zero-rating is disallowed, the LP is liable for 13% HST on the gross success fee plus daily compound interest and a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated under ETA s. 285 for false statements or omissions, including gross negligence; any input tax credit refund embedded in the same assessment is retroactively clawed back. Deliberate schemes attract criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years), publication of taxpayer defaults, and joint and several assessment of the general partner. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but CRA audit practice treats the 180-day mark as the documentary benchmark for collection: invoices unpaid after 180 days trigger heightened scrutiny of commercial substance, bad-debt rebate positions under ETA s. 231, and potential revocation of zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1951, and imposes no exchange-control restrictions on capital inflows, profit repatriation, or principal return. Remittance of Limited Partnership distributions to non-resident partners is unrestricted in currency terms, but tax filtering applies: a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the Ontario LP may be taxable under Part I/Part XIV of the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless treaty-relieved through absence of a permanent establishment, and specified partnership distributions or management/advisory fees paid to non-resident persons may attract Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (generally treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% or 5% under the Canada-U.S. and Canada-China income tax conventions). No prior central bank approval, no 'bank letter' requirement, and no convertibility restriction applies to an Ontario LP; compliance obligations are limited to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports (CAD 10,000 or more), CRA NR4/NR6 non-resident disbursement filings, and economic sanctions screening under Canadian federal law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts, cross-border service revenues, or advisory success fees; proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency and repatriated or converted at market rates without governmental authorization. The only compliance gates are (i) FINTRAC's mandatory Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) for cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) the bank's OSFI-aligned internal trade-transaction monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting, and (iii) Canadian economic sanctions administration under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Criminal Code terrorist-financing provisions, which require blocking (not surrender) of property connected to designated persons.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada (exchange control policy), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (cross-border transaction monitoring and AML/ATF reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA)). Canada operates a fully liberalized, free-floating foreign exchange regime and does not maintain a central-bank exchange-control licensing or approval system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Canadian law permits foreign digital e-money institutions (EMIs) to provide payment services in Canada only if they are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and maintain full PCMLTFA compliance programs; an offshore EMI (e.g., UK FCA-authorized or EU-licensed) cannot lawfully open a domestic operating settlement account for an Ontario LP, clear CAD through Lynx (Payments Canada RTGS), or act as treasury custodian for large M&A success-fee wires without such Canadian registration or a corresponding banking relationship with a Schedule I bank. Offshore EMIs may serve only as secondary FX conversion vehicles alongside a licensed Canadian MSB for small-volume transactions; primary treasury custody must reside at a Schedule I bank for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced M&A advisory boutique.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital contributions route by SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022-ready) to the LP's Schedule I operating account; the remitting bank must include the LP legal name and the purpose notation 'Partnership Capital Contribution'. No exchange-control approval or Bank of Canada reporting is required.
