Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec — provincial portal 'Business Services Online' / CléNumérique (https://www.revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

1. Register the Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). 2. Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or the 'Register a Business with the CRA' online service, and open a GST/HST program account when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. 3. Register separately with Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and, if applicable, employer source-deduction/QPIP/CNESST accounts using Form RM-2 or the online CléNumérique service. 4. The LP retains pass-through status: it does not pay income tax at the entity level, but it must file partnership information returns T5013 (Federal) and TP-600 (Québec) and issue T5013/RL-15 allocation slips to partners. Non-resident partners must obtain a Canadian individual tax number (ITN) using Form NR/T1 or T1 adjustment procedures, and may be subject to Canadian withholding tax on allocable Canadian-source income absent treaty relief.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; entity classification follows statutory income tax law. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent by statute. Relevant mechanisms: (a) the partnership may elect, in certain continuity events, to be deemed a Canadian partnership under Income Tax Act subsection 96(9); (b) a non-resident partner may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to file and pay tax on net Canadian rental income, or claim treaty-reduced withholding; (c) hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act automatically deny deductions or recharacterize actions where a fiscally transparent entity such as the LP creates differential cross-border tax outcomes for foreign partners. No election is available for an LP to be taxed as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies from carrying on a commercial activity in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST registration with Revenu Québec. Non-residents and LPs supplying only zero-rated export services may register voluntarily to claim input tax/input tax refunds even if below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services by the LP to a non-resident client may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not performed in Canada for consumption in Canada. For the study-abroad and golden-visa residential advisory business, contemporaneous evidence must establish: a written contract with a non-resident client; delivery of the advisory work offshore (e.g., meetings, analytics and visa-application preparation undertaken from the client's home jurisdiction); invoices issued to the foreign payor; and cross-border wire remittance. Services physically performed in Canada, such as in-person representation or filings carried out in Quebec before immigration authorities, are generally treated as supplied in Canada and attract full GST/QST; such supplies may fall outside zero-rating. Zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies and entitle the LP to recover input tax credits and input tax refunds for GST/QST incurred on related inputs.

  • Form B13A Canadian Export Declaration / CBSA Customs Export Declaration and carrier outward manifest where physical goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Canada and destination abroad for any tangible property
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange bank remittance advice demonstrating receipt of export proceeds from a non-resident client

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory transaction-level e-invoicing API. GST/QST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed data (supplier name and BN/GST/QST numbers, invoice date, description, tax amounts). Electronic invoices are permissible when the records are reliable and auditable. Revenu Québec imposes electronic filing mandates for certain payroll returns but not for GST/QST transaction pre-approval.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, or beyond in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect, and disallow zero-rating if export substantiation is incomplete or the 180-day cross-border remittance record is deficient. The LP may then be reassessed for unpaid GST/QST, with statutory interest at the prescribed daily rate (e.g., 9% simple per annum for 2025, adjustable) and penalties. Gross negligence penalties can reach 25% of the tax sought to be avoided, and wilful evasion may trigger criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, resulting in substantial fines and imprisonment for directors/managing partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. Canada has not maintained exchange control legislation since the Foreign Exchange Control Orders lapsed in 1951. Profit remittance, partner distributions, and capital repatriation by non-resident partners of a Quebec Limited Partnership are unrestricted from an FX perspective. The only operative constraints are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25% on distributions from a pass-through partnership unless treaty-reduced), PCMLTFA reporting duties for international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act. The Quebec LP should not expect any FX approval gate for inbound capital or outbound client settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds or service fee receipts. Foreign currency receipts may be freely retained in USD, EUR, GBP, or other currencies, converted at negotiated bank rates, or applied directly to outbound settlements. The only compliance layers are FINTRAC ETR threshold reporting, record-keeping of FX conversion contracts, and applicable economic sanctions screening before any cross-border movement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange monitoring and AML/ATF administration jointly conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — viable only as an ancillary disbursement channel, not as a primary treasury or regulatory escrow repository. Non-bank digital payment providers operating in Canada are classified as money services businesses (MSBs) requiring FINTRAC registration under the PCMLTFA; providers active in Quebec must additionally hold a money-services business registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Global EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) clear CAD exclusively through partner Schedule I banks and are not chartered banks; therefore they cannot lawfully hold client funds in escrow for immigration advisory engagements, which under the CICC (College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants) rules must be isolated in a regulated trust or escrow account. EMIs may be used for low-value operational payouts, foreign university tuition disbursements, and supplier settlements after full counterparty verification, subject to a documented prohibition on mixing client deposits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 for customer-initiated transfers and MT202 for interbank cover, with the domestic CAD leg settling over Payments Canada LVTS or the emerging Real-Time Rail for same-day value. Wire instructions must name the Quebec LP as beneficiary with descriptive text 'Capital contribution — partnership equity — ISIC 7810 advisory services' and reference the capital-call schedule. FINTRAC requires banks to report incoming international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within five business days; the LP must simultaneously preserve capital-call notices, GP resolutions authorizing each contribution, and identity evidence for each foreign partner. Banks commonly hold inbound funds for a one-to-five business day confirmation window and may request proof of source of funds in the form of foreign bank statements before releasing funds to operations or escrow.Primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 for customer trade settlements covering university tuition deposits, immigration government fee reimbursements, sub-agent commissions, and refunds. For transactions exceeding approximately CAD 1,000,000 or contractually guaranteed obligations, a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank may be used, although for ISIC 7810 advisory services MT103 is the prevailing standard. Every outbound wire is sanctions-screened; outbound international ETRs of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC. Banks require supporting invoice copies, a disbursement authorization signed by the general partner, and, for escrow releases, evidence matching the escrow agreement. When payment must be made in a foreign currency, the bank executes an offsetting spot FX conversion and settles through the corresponding-correspondent network rather than a separate rail.Deliverable over-the-counter FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, CAD/AUD) with Canadian Schedule I banks or OSFI-regulated counterparties under ISDA/CSA or standard bank master forward agreementsFX options (plain vanilla European-style calls and puts on CAD crosses) for contingent future obligations such as client refund liabilitiesCross-currency swaps (fixed-for-floating or floating-for-floating) for term-converting tuition deposit liabilities or phased non-resident partner capital repatriationFX swaps and swap-style rollovers extending forward positions without mark-to-market renegotiationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not necessary or standard for CAD given full convertibility; Canadian banks quote deliverable forwards with settlement in CAD and are the compliant market convention

Non-bank digital payment providers operating in Canada are classified as money services businesses (MSBs) requiring FINTRAC registration under the PCMLTFA; providers active in Quebec must additionally hold a money-services business registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). EMIs may be used for low-value operational payouts, foreign university tuition disbursements, and supplier settlements after full counterparty verification, subject to a documented prohibition on mixing client deposits.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level for the limited partnership (flow-through); reference combined federal+Quebec general corporate rate: 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ITA s.247 does not specify a fixed dollar threshold for TP documentation. CRA administrative policy requires contemporaneous documentation when the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds C$1 million in a taxation year. Large taxpayers with annual gross revenue exceeding C$50 million must prepare and provide a Local File and Master File consistent with OECD TP Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to offshore financial centres. Instead, the CRA deploys the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and transfer-pricing recharacterization (ITA s.247(2)) to disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. Revenu Québec's Taxation Act s.1079 contains an analogous anti-avoidance rule. For a CICC-regulated immigration consultancy, physical premises in Quebec, real employees, client-facing operations, and business records are key indicators of economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Partnership Allocation

Statutory sanction: Indictable tax evasion under ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years. False-statements civil penalty under ITA s.163(2): 10% or 50% (gross negligence) of the understated amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted Part XIII withholding under ITA s.227.1(1), including interest and penalties. CRA can issue director liability certificates and enforce recovery against personal assets, and in egregious cases lay criminal charges against directing minds.
Unlicensed Immigration Consulting

Statutory sanction: Under the CICC Act (SC 2019, c.29): on indictment, a fine of up to C$50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; on summary conviction, a fine of up to C$10,000 and/or imprisonment for 6 months. Quebec licensing bodies may also issue administrative fines and cease-and-desist orders.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who direct, authorize, or benefit from unlicensed practice may be prosecuted as parties to the offence under s.21 Criminal Code, and are personally liable for disgorgement of fees and restitution to affected clients.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can reallocate income to the true economic owner under ITA s.247(2), disallow the loss of the partnership structure under GAAR (s.245), and impose a penalty under s.163(2.1) for misstatements attributable to the GAAR, generally 15% of the benefit obtained.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who participate in management of the LP lose their limited-liability shield and become jointly liable. Directors of the corporate general partner may be held personally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties if they knowingly permitted a sham structure; CRAs can also obtain court injunctions requiring winding-up.
AML / Money Services Business (MSB) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, up to C$1 million per violation for MSBs; criminal fines and imprisonment for knowingly dealing in funds derived from crime or for intentionally avoiding FINTRAC registration.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who participate in, direct, or consent to a FINTRAC violation are personally liable for the administrative monetary penalty; courts may also order restitution, compliance audits, and injunctions freezing firm assets.
Immigration Misrepresentation / Client Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under IRPA s.127(b), knowingly making a false representation is an offence punishable by a fine up to C$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The client additionally becomes inadmissible to Canada for misrepresentation for 5 years, and the consultancy's CICC licence may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors or the general partner who orchestrate or supervise the misrepresentation are personally liable as 'authorized representatives', subject to criminal prosecution, disgorgement of fees, civil claims from the visa applicant, and a permanent ban from the CICC.
