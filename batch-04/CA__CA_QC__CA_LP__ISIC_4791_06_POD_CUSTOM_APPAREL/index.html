Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST and income tax administration, through the Business Registration Online portal and CRA My Business Account; and Revenu Québec — provincial QST and Quebec income tax administration, through the Revenu Québec online business portal.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Québec limited partnership (LP): (i) verify whether the LP is 'carrying on business in Canada' and whether the CAD $30,000 small-supplier threshold has been crossed; (ii) obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; (iii) register for the federal GST/HST account under the BN, and register for the QST account with Revenu Québec (BN suffix PQ, using the Revenu Québec registration form LM-1-V); (iv) where no Canadian presence exists, designate a Canadian representative for service and tax communication and expect possible security or bond requirements under ETA section 300; (v) register the partnership itself as the GST/HST/QST registrant, but treat income as flowing through to partners. File the T5013 Partnership Information Return with the CRA and the corresponding Revenu Québec partnership information return where required; each partner reports its allocated share of Canadian business income in its own jurisdiction.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident-owned Québec limited partnership (LP): (i) verify whether the LP is 'carrying on business in Canada' and whether the CAD $30,000 small-supplier threshold has been crossed; (ii) obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; (iii) register for the federal GST/HST account under the BN, and register for the QST account with Revenu Québec (BN suffix PQ, using the Revenu Québec registration form LM-1-V); (iv) where no Canadian presence exists, designate a Canadian representative for service and tax communication and expect possible security or bond requirements under ETA section 300; (v) register the partnership itself as the GST/HST/QST registrant, but treat income as flowing through to partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. An LP is treated as a fiscally transparent partnership for Canadian income tax; no election is available to tax it as a corporation. For GST/QST, the LP is a taxable person separate from its partners. Income allocations may be set in the partnership agreement but must reflect the partners' entitlement to partnership profits under the Income Tax Act. Unpaid current allocations may create hybrid mismatch exposure under Canada's anti-hybrid rules; no statutory election can cure that misalignment. The sole relevant sales-tax election is voluntary registration as a small supplier or non-resident supplier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 of worldwide taxable supply consideration (excluding sales tax) made by the LP together with associated persons in any four consecutive calendar quarters; this threshold applies to both the GST/HST (CRA) and the QST (Revenu Québec) and triggers mandatory registration and tax collection once exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered GST/HST-QST supplier may zero-rate the POD custom apparel supply when it exports the goods from Canada to a destination outside Canada, provided the LP holds reliable evidence of physical export. The design/printing service and the custom merchandise are treated as one composite zero-rated supply of goods if the service is incidental/ancillary to and charged with the exported goods. Export zero-rating applies to GST and QST; the LP can then claim input tax credits/refunds for GST and QST paid on Canadian inputs within the standard substantiation rules.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or courier/customs-broker equivalent) where the export exceeds the reporting threshold
  • Commercial invoice containing the foreign customer name, foreign address, item description, consideration, and HS classification
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or postal/courier shipment receipt showing dispatch from Canada
  • Third-party proof of delivery outside Canada: foreign customs entry, delivery confirmation from carrier, or foreign border crossing stamp
  • Payment record from the foreign buyer (Stripe/PayPal settlement, SWIFT/remittance, or card acquirer record)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API stamping, or electronic-invoicing clearance mandate currently applies in Canada or Québec. Businesses must issue invoices or simplified sales vouchers for taxable supplies and retain all records, including electronic records, for six years. EDI/PDF invoices are accepted if they contain the required information and are legible and auditable.

Canada has no statutory export-fx repatriation deadline; the zero-rating condition is physical export plus documentary proof. If the CRA or Revenu Québec later disallows the zero rating, the LP is assessed for uncollected GST/QST, compound daily interest, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Tax Administration Act (failure to file, false statements, gross negligence). Fraudulent zero-rating claims can lead to criminal prosecution, seizure of records, and monetary fines. The six-year record-retention requirement must be respected; the normal reassessment period for GST/HST is four years but is extended for misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada operates an independent floating exchange-rate regime with no general exchange-control legislation requiring government approval for conversion or remittance. Profit distributions, capital repatriation and liquidation proceeds may be converted and remitted freely to non-resident limited partners. Convertibility is enshrined by Canada's OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and CUSMA obligations. Banking frictions are documentary only: financial institutions must perform customer due diligence (CDD) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and file FINTRAC reports for electronic funds transfers (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion or repatriation timeline for export receipts; the LP may freely retain POD sales revenue in CAD or foreign currency. For income tax purposes, subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the equivalent Quebec Taxation Act provisions require foreign-currency gains and losses to be measured in CAD using the Bank of Canada noon or daily average exchange rate; GST/HST and QST returns likewise convert foreign sales into Canadian dollars per published CRA/Revenu Québec rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX administration and monetary policy under the Bank of Canada Act; AML/ATF transaction surveillance by FINTRAC; prudential oversight of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Quebec financial-sector licensing (when applicable) by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EMI rails that operate through a regulated Canadian partner bank (e.g., Stripe Treasury, Wise Business, or Airwallex gateway clearing into a Schedule I bank account); Low for purely offshore digital EMIs lacking a Canadian branch or FINTRAC money-service-business (MSB) registration. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI processing CAD settlement or executing customer wires for the LP without either (i) FINTRAC MSB registration in Canada or (ii) an exemption under section 8 of the PCMLTFA would be non-compliant and would be refused by the onboarding bank as a circular-payments counterparty. Recommended architecture: use EMIs solely as an e-commerce collection conduit to the LP's Schedule I CAD/USD accounts; execute all supplier disbursements and FX through the chartered bank.
