Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), operated under Revenu Québec; filings made through the OneStop Business Registry online portal (entreprisesquebec.gouv.qc.ca).

Governing statute

Civil Code of Quebec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), Book IV, articles 2236-2267 (société en commandite) and articles 2186-2229 (partnership generally); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1), in particular sections 9-43 on the declaration of registration, name distinctiveness, and filing deadlines; if the general partner is a Quebec corporation, also the Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation d'une société en commandite (Declaration of Registration - Limited Partnership); base state filing fee CAD 84 (approx. USD 62, indexed annually).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership (art. 2236 C.C.Q.). The declaration of registration must state the cash and in-kind contributions made and the balance, if any, promised by each partner (ss. 24-25 L.P.L.E.). No escrow account or deposit is required; partner capital may be funded progressively according to the subscription schedule.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required for the office occupancy class (C/D per Quebec Fire Safety Code), including evacuation plan and extinguisher inspection; electrical and structural compliance per the Quebec Building Code; CNESST workplace registration and hazard prevention program for employees; no civil defense clearance applies to intangible cloud software services.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal income tax and GST/HST administration, via Business Registration Online (BRO) and the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST), source deductions, and other provincial levies, via the CLIC portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). Step 2: Apply to CRA via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) using BRO to obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST account; non-resident partners must also obtain an Individual Tax Number using Form T1151. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec on CLIC to open a QST account and, if employees are engaged, a source deductions and employer contributions account. Step 4: File partnership information on prescribed forms, including each partner's name, address, TIN/SIN/BN/ITN, and agreed income or loss allocation percentage. Step 5: The partnership itself holds the BN and QST account while remaining fiscally transparent; each partner reports its allocable share of Canadian-source income, and non-resident partners may be subject to 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions unless an election under ITA section 216(1) is filed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope (the limited partnership is a non-taxable flow-through vehicle under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; Canadian-source business income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level; non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian tax only on Canadian-source income and may face Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal law. Relevant statutory elections and classifications include: (1) the ITA section 102(2) election to cause the partnership to be a 'Canadian partnership', which limits withholding and branch tax exposure for non-resident partners; (2) the ITA section 216(1) election by a non-resident partner to file a Canadian net-basis return instead of being subject to flat withholding; (3) the ITA section 216(4) election dealing with a non-resident partner's share of Canadian business income; and (4) hybrid mismatch denial rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 (Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test, which restrict favourable treatment where an entity is fiscally transparent in one jurisdiction but opaque in another.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies (trailing four-quarter small-supplier threshold) for both GST/HST and QST; zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold but no tax is collectible; a non-resident supplier whose only Canadian supplies are zero-rated exports is generally not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of vertical cloud ERP/CRM software subscription services to non-resident customers are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, which zero-rates a supply of a service or intangible personal property made to a non-resident person where the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or personal services supplied to an individual in Canada. The supplier must demonstrate that the customer is a non-resident and that the digital service is received and used outside Canada; similar zero-rating principles apply to the QST under the Quebec Sales Tax Act. The vendor must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the customer's non-resident status and of the place of digital consumption.

  • Signed cloud SaaS/CRM subscription or master services agreement stating the customer's non-resident status, country of consumption, and delivery mechanism
  • Commercial invoice in a prescribed GST/HST/QST format, expressly marked as zero-rated export supply
  • Non-resident evidence (e.g., W-8BEN-E, certificate of residence, foreign corporate registry extract, or foreign billing address)
  • Inward remittance evidence (e.g., SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or card acquirer settlement showing funds originating from a foreign bank account)
  • Service delivery and access logs (e.g., IP geolocation records, server access logs, and tenant data location evidencing active receipt outside Canada)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API filing, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is voluntary and is implemented through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 via certified access points. CRA accepts electronic records and invoices provided they contain the prescribed GST/HST particulars (e.g., supplier name/BN, recipient, date, description, tax amounts) and remain legible and retrievable; Revenu Québec requires that invoices be issued in French, or bilingually, to comply with the Charter of the French Language.

Canadian tax law does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure represents the customary commercial and CRA audit benchmark for demonstrating the bona fide offshore collection of export proceeds. If CRA or Revenu Québec determines that the zero-rating evidence is deficient or false, the supplier is retroactively reassessed for GST at 5% and QST at 9.975%, with interest. Penalties include the Excise Tax Act section 285(1) penalty of 25% on false statements or omissions, the section 285.1 gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the net tax understated, and section 280 interest at prescribed rates. The normal four-year reassessment period is unlimited where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, and fraudulent export-credit claims may give rise to Criminal Code tax-fraud prosecution, director and partner liability, and disqualification from GST/HST registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls or prior approvals for inward or outward capital transfers, profit remittances, or partnership distributions. Non-resident partners can freely receive CAD or foreign-currency distributions, subject only to Quebec withholding tax obligations (governed by the Taxation Act) and FINTRAC reporting for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000. No material remittance hurdles exist; however, Quebec requires the partnership to report payments to non-residents via prescribed forms and to obtain a Relevé 24 style certificate if tax is withheld.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation deadlines for export receipts (including software licensing proceeds under ISIC 6201). Exporters may hold foreign currency in designated-currency or export accounts at any Canadian Schedule I bank. FINTRAC rules only require record keeping and reporting for single transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) may require commercial invoice declarations for services rendered abroad for customs statistics.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with supplementary regulation from the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium with specific constraints. Foreign fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex) can be used as settlement agents for cross-border disbursements, but they are not licensed as banks under the Bank Act and cannot provide direct membership in Payments Canada. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada before offering services to Canadian residents. A foreign EMI without a Canadian MSB license (FINTRAC) and RPAA registration cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for the Quebec LP. It is viable exclusively as a supplemental rail for low-value, recurring payments via embedded APIs, subject to an AML audit and contractual data-residency clauses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners shall be routed via SWIFT MT103 wires. The payment instruction must include a purpose code conforming to Canada's Balance of Payments / Financial Monitoring framework—use code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital (PURPOSE: CAPINV)' for Statistics Canada reporting. A document package must accompany the wire: (a) executed capital commitment certificate; (b) LP subscription agreement; (c) a legal opinion from a Canadian securities lawyer affirming compliance with Regulation 45-106; and (d) evidence of source of funds. For contributions exceeding CAD 5 million, the transaction is reportable to Statistics Canada under the Canada Investment Act, and a Certificate of Compliance with the Quebec Partnership Act must be provided. The bank will perform a beneficial-ownership check on all partners under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Transactions must be settled in CAD at the Bank of Canada daily reference rate; the receiving bank must use a funding account with the Bank of Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Real-Time Rail if the value is under CAD 1 million. For transfers in a foreign currency, the LP must open a designated-currency account and use the registered bank's FX desk for conversion before the intraday cut-off.Standard vendor payments for licensing, cloud infrastructure, and professional services are settled through SWIFT MT103 (single beneficiary) for amounts up to CAD 250,000. For strategic outsourcing contracts exceeding CAD 250,000, the bank will issue an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) under UCP600 terms, with the beneficiary bank in the target jurisdiction. Within Canada, domestic vendor payments use the Real-Time Rail (RTR) or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). For subscription refunds and CRM-vertical micro-transactions under CAD 100, the LP may use Stripe's Canadian direct-debit rail (Pre-authorized Debit) subject to the Payments Canada rulebook.
