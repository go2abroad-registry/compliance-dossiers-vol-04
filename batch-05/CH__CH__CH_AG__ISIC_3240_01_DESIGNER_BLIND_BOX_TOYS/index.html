Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000; at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank and released only upon presentation of the Commercial Register extract evidencing registration; in-kind contributions require a statutory audit report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Fire Prevention Office of the Canton of Zug (Feuerpolizei) for the production premises; structural load inspection in accordance with SIA 261 standards; workplace clearance and mandatory accident insurance confirmation (AGR number) issued by SUVA, with occupational health verification for solvent handling areas.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000; at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank and released only upon presentation of the Commercial Register extract evidencing registration; in-kind contributions require a statutory audit report.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC), VAT Division (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; UID registry: www.uid.admin.ch; e-filing: www.estv.admin.ch/e-services. Cantonal authorities (e.g., Tax Administration of Canton Zug) assess corporate income tax on federal and cantonal/municipal levels; VAT is exclusively a federal tax administered by the ESTV.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarise the AG deed and file it with the Commercial Register (Handelsregisteramt); the company automatically receives the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, e.g., CHE-123.456.789), while the minimum capital of CHF 100,000 (at least CHF 50,000 paid in) must be evidenced. Step 3: A foreign entity with neither registered office nor place of business in Switzerland that owes Swiss VAT must appoint a tax representative (Steuervertreter) under Art. 67 MWSTG and register before supplying taxable services/goods in Switzerland. Step 4: The AG must additionally register with the competent cantonal tax office for corporate income and capital tax assessment, which is verified against the Commercial Register entry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Swiss AG is an independent corporate taxpayer subject to federal income tax at 8.5% (after deducting the federal tax itself from the base), plus cantonal and municipal income taxes (combined effective rates typically 11.5%–21%, approximately 14.6% in Zug), and capital tax on equity. A resident AG is taxable on worldwide profits, with territorial allocation/exemption for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate; pass-through treatment is unavailable for an AG.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Switzerland; a Swiss AG is a per se corporation under US Treas. Reg. §301.7701-2(b)(8), so no US disregarded-entity election is possible. Elections available under Swiss law include: (i) automatic participation exemption (Beteiligungsabzug) for qualifying dividends and capital gains on substantial holdings; (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) on excess equity, available in adopting cantons such as Zug; (iii) patent box relief and additional R&D super-deduction under TRAF 2020; (iv) depreciation methodology elections and accelerated write-offs; (v) hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by general anti-abuse rules, the Swiss participation exemption conditions, and treaty provisions – no formal hybrid-mismatch election exists, and Switzerland has no group taxation consolidation election for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 worldwide gross taxable turnover per fiscal year (Art. 10 MWSTG), effective 1 January 2025 increased to CHF 250,000; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and small businesses with turnover not exceeding the threshold may claim simplified exemption relief (Art. 109 MWSTG).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rate (0% VAT with full input tax deduction) applies to: (i) export of goods to a foreign country where the goods are physically transported abroad (Art. 23(1) MWSTG); (ii) services directly connected with exported goods, including transport and logistics (Art. 23(2)); (iii) specified services rendered to foreign customers, such as licensing of intellectual property and consultancy supplied from Switzerland (Art. 23(3)). For designer blind-box toys (ISIC 3240), only actual physical exports of finished collectibles from Switzerland qualify for the 0% rate; domestic sales are taxable at 8.1%, and consignment transfers to EU fulfilment centres do not qualify as final exports and trigger local VAT registration obligations. Export evidence must exist at the time the VAT return is filed and must be retained for ten years.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (e-dec electronic declaration with movement reference number) and the stamped export certificate (Ausfuhrschein)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), air waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note naming the foreign consignee and destination port
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT bank confirmation evidencing payment in foreign currency and FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, declared customs value, full product description, and HS code 9503.00
  • Packing list, container manifest, and transport insurance certificate covering the export leg
  • For services exports: signed declaration from the foreign recipient confirming its foreign residence and the actual use/destination of the service outside Switzerland

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, API-based approval, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for VAT. Invoices need only satisfy Art. 26 MWSTG content requirements (names, VAT numbers, date, description, rate, amount) and may be issued in paper or electronic form. The QR-bill and eBill standards (Swiss Payment Standards 2024) are widely used and the ESTV accepts electronic archives, while Peppol-based exchange is emerging for public procurement; no government pre-clearance or cryptographic stamping is required.

The ESTV routinely audits export files; missing, incomplete, or falsified export proofs cause re-taxation of the supply at the standard 8.1% rate, with input VAT clawback, default interest, and possible retrospective adjustment of prior tax periods. Negligent tax evasion (Art. 102 MWSTG) incurs a fine of up to 300% of the tax evaded; wilful VAT or customs fraud (Art. 103, Abgabebetrug) may lead to imprisonment for up to five years and a fine of up to 400% of the evaded tax; procedural retention violations (Art. 105 MWSTG) are fined up to CHF 8,000. The 180-day FX repatriation figure is a Swiss banking and export-compliance convention rather than a statutory VAT requirement, because Switzerland has no mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; nevertheless, Swiss banks and export credit insurers treat 180 days as the standard payment settlement norm for documenting completed export transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) enjoys full current and capital account convertibility consistent with Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Switzerland imposes no exchange controls, no capital-flow restrictions, no mandatory FX conversion regime, and no central bank authorization requirement for foreign direct investment, dividend remittance, royalty payments, or intercompany financing. The only structural profit-remittance friction is Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividend distributions, which is frequently reduced to 0% to 5% under Switzerland's double tax treaty network or eliminated within Swiss groups, while the separate issuance tax and certain interest withholding tax points were dismantled under the 2023 corporate tax reform. No inward investment screening applies to a designer-toy manufacturing and merchandising entity, although banking due diligence will require documented source-of-funds and beneficial ownership evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Swiss law imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters are freely entitled to retain, remit, convert, or hold export proceeds in any currency without FINMA or SNB permission, and there is no repatriation or liquidation obligation. The SNB collects only statistical balance-of-payments data: banks report cross-border payment flows on behalf of clients, and a resident enterprise with material aggregate cross-border payments (typically above the SNB's published entity-level reporting threshold, where direct reporting forms apply) must file statistical returns. No FX surrender or export-conversion duty exists.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse), headquartered in Bern and Zurich, operating under Article 99 of the Federal Constitution. Banking and AML conduct supervision is exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA); residual sanctions administration and export controls rest with the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for Swiss-licensed fintechs and payment service providers (FINMA banking license under the FinTech Ordinance / Art. 1b Banking Act, public deposit cap of CHF 100 million, no interest on deposits), but Low for offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA EMIs or EU PSD2 EMIs). Offshore EMIs have no passporting rights into Switzerland, no SIC interbank clearing access, and cannot issue Swiss IBAN accounts for domestic settlement or accept Swiss retail deposits without a Swiss banking or AMLA financial-intermediary license. For the target payment rails (Shopify Payments, Stripe, Local POS), the compliant architecture is a Swiss merchant acquiring agreement with a FINMA-supervised PSP and final settlement into a Swiss tier-1 bank account; an offshore EMI may serve only as an auxiliary receiving corridor if its underlying provider is registered under Art. 20 AMLA, and never as the primary treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR) to the Swiss capital escrow account at a tier-1 bank (UBS or ZKB), with the mandatory wire narrative 'Share capital contribution / Liberierung Aktien / CH_AG' and explicit reference to the bank's escrow deposit certificate. The deposit must satisfy the statutory incorporation verification under Art. 632 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (minimum share capital CHF 100,000 with at least 20% of the nominal value of each share paid in cash before registration). Switzerland does not operate a national purpose-code authority; for SEPA ISO 20022 payments, the ExternalPurpose code 'EQUI' (equity) or 'INTC' (intra-company) is commonly used, and the exact accepted code must be confirmed with the receiving bank. For CNY-denominated inbound flows, routing via CIPS or through the Swiss bank's offshore CNH desk is recommended. All wires must be matched to the notarized capital increase resolution and the certified UBO declarations to withstand AML review.Trade settlements to Chinese, Southeast Asian, and European manufacturers use SWIFT MT103 for USD/EUR wires via the Swiss bank's correspondent network, with Incoterms-compliant shipping documents. For large or consignment production runs, confirmed documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT740) drawn on Swiss banks with China desks are preferred. EUR payments clear via SEPA (ISO 20022 pain.001, purpose code 'SUPP'); CHF domestic payments clear via the SIX SIC interbank system; USD wires run through CHIPS or Fedwire via the New York correspondent; CNY payments settle through offshore CNH clearing or CIPS. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Swiss federal corporate income tax (Erwerbssteuer) 8.5% flat rate imposed on after-tax profit; approximately 7.83% on pre-tax profit. Combined federal + cantonal/communal statutory headline rate for an Aktiengesellschaft domiciled in the Zug benchmark location: 14.6% (Federal 8.5% + Zug cantonal/communal 6.1% on taxable earnings before federal tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Swiss statutory dividend withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is 35% on gross distributions by a Swiss AG. Treaty-reduced rates using DTA protocol: 0% for a qualifying corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of voting power (subject to MLI Principal Purpose Test); 5% for holdings ≥10% where the treaty provides; 15% for most portfolio investors. Repayment of capital contribution reserves (agio) is exempt from Swiss WHT where properly audited.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has not codified an explicit statutory threshold in the Federal Direct Tax Act. In practice, the tax administration applies a materiality benchmark of CHF 1,000,000 annual aggregate intercompany turnover (goods/services/IP/interest) for requiring a Local File; a Master File is expected where the Swiss group entity has cross-border related-party transactions in excess of CHF 10,000,000 or where the international group turnover exceeds CHF 50,000,000. Swiss CbCR is mandatory for groups with consolidated annual revenue of CHF 900,000,000 or more. Contemporaneous documentation must be completed within the statutory deadlines (tax return preparation time) and in any event before final tax assessment.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no Economic Substance Regulations akin to the UAE/Caribbean regimes. However, economic substance is an implicit self-standing principle applied through (i) Art. 58 DBG proper and complete commercial books, (ii) the EU/OECD Principal Purpose Test and Multilateral Instrument (MLI) treaty-abuse rules, (iii) the tax administration's transfer-pricing functional analysis, and (iv) the Federal Court practice under which an artificial legal arrangement without genuine business purpose is disregarded. An AG must maintain a real location, employees/board meetings in Switzerland, assume operative risks and own functional assets; otherwise tax authorities may recharacterise intercompany transactions, deny expense deductions, treat royalty or interest flows as hidden profit distributions, and refuse WHT refunds under DTA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Art. 175 DBG: payment of the evaded tax plus a fine equal to one-quarter to twice the evaded amount; on aggravated professional tax evasion, fine up to CHF 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Under federal criminal law, value confiscation and disgorgement of the non-taxed profit is ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors who falsely sign the corporate tax return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid Federal and cantonal tax claim if they caused the evasion intentionally or negligently; they may be prosecuted as perpetrator or abettor and face personal fines, seizure of their Swiss bank accounts and, in aggravated fraud cases, a departure ban (travel ban) pending criminal proceedings.
Dividend Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 12 Verrechnungssteuergesetz (VStG) the AG is liable for the full 35% tax not collected, plus 5% default interest and procedure costs; intentional non-payment triggers a criminal fine up to CHF 30,000 and, on conviction, confiscation of equivalent hidden reserves.

Director personal liability: The board of directors is jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax liability to the Federal Tax Administration in case of payment inability of the AG; individual board members who signed the payment instructions can be named as debtors in administrative enforcement proceedings and are subject to asset attachment, prohibition to serve as a director in Switzerland and, if the tax debt is large, criminal seizure of private assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement under Swiss substance and TP rules: management fees, royalties and interest reclassified as non-deductible hidden profit distributions; additional Swiss corporate income tax and 35% WHT assessed on the recharacterized amounts; penalties of 10–100% of the evaded direct tax; DTA refunds, including any treaty protections, are denied.

Director personal liability: The de facto and de jure directors are personally liable for the company's tax debts to the extent that assets were diverted to the foreign shell after the debt arose; civil veil-piercing is granted in case of significant undercapitalisation and fraudulent intent, leading to personal restitution orders, freezing of personal Swiss real estate and bank balances, and, for non-resident directors, entry bans to Schengen territory.
AML / Payment-Routing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA) Art. 37: fine up to CHF 500,000; laundering of criminal funds under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to three years (five years in qualified cases), with confiscation of assets and publication of judgment; a legal entity may be ordered to pay compensation not exceeding the amount of the laundered value.

Director personal liability: Directors who design the payment routing or knowingly tolerate the omission of beneficial-owner identification are individually criminally liable and can be imprisoned; their personal fiduciary or corporate assets are attachable, and a travel ban is imposed by the investigation judge where there is a risk of collusion or flight.
