Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Number (BN) registration via Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA 'My Business Account' portal; Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement, with the Ontario Ministry of Finance retaining responsibility for Ontario-only tax programs such as Employer Health Tax and Ontario-specific credit claims

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario Business Corporation obtains its statutory Business Number (BN) automatically at the time of federal or provincial incorporation; the BN is the universal tax identification number for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, import/export, and Ontario corporate tax accounts. For a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, the registration steps are: (i) file a T2 corporation income tax registration with the CRA and establish a Canadian resident agent for service; (ii) submit Form RC1 (Request for Business Number) to activate the GST/HST program account, attaching certificate of incorporation, foreign certificate of incumbency, and evidence of Canadian nexus (including deemed nexus under the ETA digital-economy rules where applicable); (iii) open payroll and customs accounts only if payroll or import/export activities exist; (iv) confirm Ontario corporate tax registration is aligned to the same BN, as no separate Ontario TIN or GST/VAT ID is issued for HST purposes — the 8% Ontario component of HST is administered and collected by the CRA under the single BN

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Ontario Business Corporation obtains its statutory Business Number (BN) automatically at the time of federal or provincial incorporation; the BN is the universal tax identification number for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, import/export, and Ontario corporate tax accounts. For a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, the registration steps are: (i) file a T2 corporation income tax registration with the CRA and establish a Canadian resident agent for service; (ii) submit Form RC1 (Request for Business Number) to activate the GST/HST program account, attaching certificate of incorporation, foreign certificate of incumbency, and evidence of Canadian nexus (including deemed nexus under the ETA digital-economy rules where applicable); (iii) open payroll and customs accounts only if payroll or import/export activities exist; (iv) confirm Ontario corporate tax registration is aligned to the same BN, as no separate Ontario TIN or GST/VAT ID is issued for HST purposes — the 8% Ontario component of HST is administered and collected by the CRA under the single BN

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box regime; classification is determined by the corporate statute under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and Excise Tax Act (ETA). Relevant elections include: ITA s. 85 rollover elections for corporate reorganizations; ITA s. 216 election allowing certain non-residents to compute Canadian rental/royalty tax on a net basis; ETA s. 156 election to treat a supplier and recipient as a single person for GST/HST; ETA s. 211.1 election for supplies between closely related corporations; ETA s. 225.2 elections for financial institutions to use an alternative input tax credit method; and Ontario corporate tax elections for loss carryover and amalgamation. Under the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 and analogous ETA provisions), an electronic money institution must contemporaneously document the tax classification of its foreign parent or investor entities to avoid deduction/non-inclusion mismatches

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold) for compulsory GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240; for non-resident suppliers of digital services and operators of distribution platforms under the July 2021 digital-economy framework, the simplified GST/HST registration obligation under ETA Schedule VIII is triggered once taxable supplies to persons in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold, although specified non-resident digital-service suppliers are deemed to be carrying on business in Canada for GST/HST purposes

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V. Services provided to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII where the recipient is a non-resident who is not a registered non-resident at the time the service is performed, and the service is not performed wholly in Canada for the direct benefit of a person in Canada. Financial services supplied by an electronic money institution to a non-resident recipient who is not a registered non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part IX, including the issuance of virtual IBANs and digital wallet reserve management services, provided the fee is attributable to offshore use. Services rendered to Canadian-resident merchants remain exempt (not zero-rated) financial services under ETA Part VII, meaning no GST/HST is charged but no input tax credit is ordinarily available unless the institution is eligible to elect to treat the consideration as taxable or qualifies under prescribed financial institution rules

  • CBSA/CERS export declaration (Form B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System or evidence of export through a licensed customs broker
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (ocean, air, or highway) evidencing physical movement of goods outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT payment evidence demonstrating receipt of settlement funds from the non-resident recipient

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping or tax authority API approval for invoicing. GST/HST is self-assessed and remitted through periodic returns filed via GST/HST NETFILE or TELEFILE. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and may follow the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing standard for B2B interoperability. CRA requires taxpayers to maintain books and records in a prescribed electronic or paper format for at least six years, and e-commerce platform operators under the 2021 framework must collect and remit GST/HST on taxable digital supplies with records of the gross value of supplies and the seller and recipient identification details

CRA audit programs compare zero-rated claims on a transaction-by-transaction basis against export documentation and banking records. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating results in reassessment of the full 13% HST with compound daily interest, gross-negligence penalties of up to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded under ETA s. 285, administrative penalties under ETA s. 280 of CAD 250 to CAD 10,000 per false statement, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. Amounts clawed back may be collected retroactively, and directors and senior officers are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and ineligible input tax credits under ETA s. 323, with documentation retention required for six years

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains a liberalized exchange control framework with no Exchange Control Act or centralized prior approval regime for cross-border fund flows, provided the transaction meets AML/CFT, sanctions screening, and tax information reporting requirements. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange control restrictions, but are subject to withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees) and must be supported by appropriate corporate resolutions, audited financial statements, and a valid Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) clearance certificate where applicable. Dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident are generally subject to a 25% statutory withholding tax, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty. Each remittance must be processed through a Canadian financial institution under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) compliance framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any governmental authority, and maintains no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Canadian law respects commercial freedom in contracting the currency of settlement; proceeds may be retained in CAD or any permitted foreign currency in an account held with a Canadian or foreign financial institution. However, an exporting Canadian entity is required to report cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC within the prescribed reporting windows, must comply with economic sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada, and must accurately report foreign-source revenue and foreign exchange gains or losses for Canadian income tax purposes. If the entity maintains foreign currency bank accounts outside Canada, the foreign affiliate reporting rules under section 233.3 of the Income Tax Act (Form T1134/T1134A information returns) or the foreign reporting verification statement on Form T1135 may apply where the aggregate cost amount of specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Department of Finance Canada with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) overseeing AML/CFT obligations; Ontario's Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) may also have supervisory relevance for money services businesses operating in Ontario.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH with significant legal constraints. Canadian corporate entities in the electronic money institution space may lawfully open accounts with foreign fintech/EMI platforms (e.g., UK FCA-authorized EMIs or EU-licensed payment institutions) for liquidity and payment-rail purposes, provided: (1) the Canadian entity maintains a valid money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC if it engages in foreign exchange dealing, money transferring, or issuing/redeeming stored value instruments regardless of the absence of provincial prudential licensing; (2) the foreign EMI's product terms permit Canadian-domiciled corporate clients; and (3) the Canadian entity remains fully compliant with Ontario's Payday Loans Act, 2008 only if applicable, and does not hold itself out as a deposit-taking institution. However, OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prohibits unlicensed persons from carrying on deposit-taking business in Canada, and an EMI cannot accept deposits from Canadian residents unless deposit-taking with a bank regulated under the Bank Act. For an Ontario-focused entity, using a foreign EMI for virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet reserve management is viable only if the entity does not solicit deposits from the Canadian public and instead sources funds from licensed financial institutions, non-resident corporate treasury entities, or under an exempt institutional arrangement. The entity should also ensure that bridge funding flows do not create unintended carrying-on-business exposure in the foreign jurisdiction.

Include a compliance program outline covering the appointed Compliance Officer, AML/CFT policies, risk attestation, training records, and prescribed record-keeping procedures.

A current and consolidated shareholder registry must be provided in electronic searchable format.

Include corporate bank references from a regulated financial institution evidencing 12 months of clean transactional conduct.
Electronic wallet and virtual IBAN business model indicating potential for unlicensed deposit-taking or unregulated stored value issuance

Include the user agreement disclaiming the absence of CDIC deposit insurance, the compliance policy governing reserve management, and evidence of segregation of client funds through bank trust accounts or statutory trust arrangements under provincial consumer protection frameworks.
Cross-border payments and FX conversion functionality without provincial money transmitting licensing clearance

Defense evidence: Provide an opinion from Ontario-licensed counsel or a Blue Book analysis confirming the entity's operation either (a) falls within the FINTRAC MSB framework only, (b) is exempt under the Securities Act (Ontario) from dealer registration if the product is a security, or (c) operates solely as a technology provider to licensed financial institutions without exercising control over the transmission of funds. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders or treasury entities to a Canadian EMI corporation should be executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfer from the originating bank to the Canadian bank of the corporate account, using the beneficiary's legal name as registered with the bank, a precise corporate account number, and an appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code identifying the transaction as an equity contribution (e.g., 'Equity injection' or 'Capital - share capital subscription' represented through SWIFT field 70-77 / tag 50k remittance information). No Canada Border Services Agency or Bank of Canada approval is required. Each inbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more triggers FINTRAC large cash transaction report and cross-border wire transfer reporting obligations upon receipt, so the CFO/treasury must embed a confirmation procedure that includes immediate capture of the sender's identity, the legal entity identifier (LEI) of both parties if applicable, the originating jurisdiction, and supporting board resolution and subscription agreement. For shareholder loan capital, the inbound transfer should be routed under the corporate loan purpose code, with an executed intercompany loan agreement reflecting arm's length interest and repayment terms, supported by a Canadian transfer-pricing analysis if the lender is a non-resident related party. Tax compliance requires: (1) immediate recording of contributed surplus or stated capital depending on the corporate law route applied; (2) filing of Form T106 where the loan is from a foreign affiliate; and (3) reporting the account on Form T1134 if evidenced by a foreign affiliate. Although Canadian dollar is fully convertible, the receiving bank may conduct enhanced due diligence on the underlying source of funds; the entity should prepackage the complete corporate resolution, shareholder identification, source-of-fund statement, and the original wire instruction document.Outbound trade settlements and operational disbursements from a Canadian corporate account should be cleared through the SWIFT network via correspondent banks using MT103 customer credit transfers for direct payments and MT202 COV for interbank cover payments, supported by fully populated remittance information and sanctions screening against the consolidated Canadian sanctions list, OFAC's SDN list, UN sanctions, and the EU consolidated list if EU EMIs are used as counterparties. If the outbound payment funds a letter of credit (LC), the standard rail is a commercial documentary LC issued through a Canadian bank's trade finance unit, routed to the beneficiary's bank via SWIFT MT700 series. For cross-border disbursements the entity must: (1) include Canadian GST/HST registration and CRA Business Number if the underlying import is subject to GST/HST on importation; (2) apply the correct NAFTA/CUSMA (USMCA) origin verification certificate if applicable; (3) complete FINTRAC wire transfer report for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (4) maintain the underlying commercial invoices, packing lists, and shipping documents in a structured trade file to demonstrate authenticity and trade purpose. For outbound profit distributions, dividend remittances, and management fees, the disbursement must be made via MT103 with a remittance code clearly identifying the payment as a dividend, with the Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25% or treaty-reduced rate) remitted to the CRA through the corporation's payroll/remittance account. If disbursements exceed the entity's settled positions, the entity can instruct its Canadian bank to sell CAD against USD (or other currency) at the prevailing spot rate, provided the bank's FX desk has received a designated board resolution authorizing treasury transactions and naming the treasury signatories.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts entered into with a Canadian Schedule I bank or recognized counterparty pursuant to an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, where the instrument is designated as a hedge of a forecasted cross-border transaction under the entity's documented FX policy and hedge accounting consideration is applied under IFRS/ASPE.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in closed or restricted currencies where the Canadian counterparty has a valid internal registration with the Bank for International Settlements or clearing access, provided such instruments are permitted by the counterparty's internal credit committee and the underlying exposure is a non-CAD transaction; CAD itself is fully convertible and NDFs are not normally required for CAD, but NDFs may be used for settlement of transactions involving CNY, KRW, INR, or similar currencies.Cross-currency swaps (including CAD/USD and CAD/EUR swaps) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement and cleared through a recognized central clearing counterparty if required by OSFI guidelines, or bilaterally under an approved credit annex, with appropriate daily variation margin and initial margin posting in compliance with uncleared margin rules applicable in Canada under OSFI's Margin Requirements for Non-Centrally Cleared Derivatives.Plain vanilla FX options (call and put) on major traded currency pairs purchased from authorized banks with the option premium treated as treasury expense or capitalized depending on hedge designation.Exchange-traded FX futures and options traded on recognized derivatives exchanges such as the Chicago Mercantile Exchange via a registered futures commission merchant, which may be used to achieve directional or hedge objectives under the entity's board-approved derivatives policy and embedded risk limits.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH with significant legal constraints. Canadian corporate entities in the electronic money institution space may lawfully open accounts with foreign fintech/EMI platforms (e.g., UK FCA-authorized EMIs or EU-licensed payment institutions) for liquidity and payment-rail purposes, provided: (1) the Canadian entity maintains a valid money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC if it engages in foreign exchange dealing, money transferring, or issuing/redeeming stored value instruments regardless of the absence of provincial prudential licensing; (2) the foreign EMI's product terms permit Canadian-domiciled corporate clients; and (3) the Canadian entity remains fully compliant with Ontario's Payday Loans Act, 2008 only if applicable, and does not hold itself out as a deposit-taking institution. However, OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prohibits unlicensed persons from carrying on deposit-taking business in Canada, and an EMI cannot accept deposits from Canadian residents unless deposit-taking with a bank regulated under the Bank Act. For an Ontario-focused entity, using a foreign EMI for virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet reserve management is viable only if the entity does not solicit deposits from the Canadian public and instead sources funds from licensed financial institutions, non-resident corporate treasury entities, or under an exempt institutional arrangement. The entity should also ensure that bridge funding flows do not create unintended carrying-on-business exposure in the foreign jurisdiction.
