Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; a business corporation is a separate taxable person by default. Relevant elections and rules: (i) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.3-18.6 and 19.5 apply to disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, with no elective opt-out for most cross-border structures; (ii) a foreign-owned Canadian subsidiary may elect under ITA s. 111(4.1) for deemed paid-up capital ordering; (iii) ETA Schedule VI Part IX s. 23(3) permits a financial institution to elect to waive zero-rating for supplies of financial services to non-residents; (iv) selected financial institutions may elect between the general and specific methods of the GST/HST Financial Institution Method under ETA s. 225.2; (v) QST mirrors the federal elections under Quebec Taxation Act and QTA regulations, including the election for the 'use and enjoyment' rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and over the preceding four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold) for both GST and QST; registration is mandatory without threshold for a Selected Financial Institution (SFI) and for any person whose principal activity is the supply of financial services to persons other than 'persons at risk' under ETA s. 123(1)/QTA definitions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physical export from Canada with documentary proof of export zero-rates the supply under ETA Schedule VI Part V. Services/financial services: a supply of a financial service (including virtual IBAN issuance, wallet reserve management, and cross-border payment clearing) is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part IX s. 7 when the recipient is a non-resident of Canada and the service is not for use or enjoyment in Canada, subject to exceptions for services rendered to a non-resident's Canadian permanent establishment or relating to certain Canadian securities. Quebec QST applies the same zero-rating treatment for qualifying exported financial services, with entitlement to input tax refunds. The EMI must substantiate non-resident status of each account holder (e.g., certificate of corporate existence, permanent address, non-resident declaration) and demonstrate that the payment rails and settlement activities occur outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Reporting System (CERS), applicable only to physical goods; not required for pure cross-border financial services)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for goods) / SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages evidencing cross-border transfer of funds (for virtual IBAN and digital wallet services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice or transaction statement showing repatriation of export proceeds into the Canadian entity)
  • Executed written service agreement and AML/KYC file establishing the non-resident identity and offshore use of the digital wallet or IBAN
  • Internal ledger reconciliation of wallet reserve balances and fee records supporting the zero-rated treatment

Canada and Quebec do not operate real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory transactional API reporting, or digital stamping. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing initiative (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is in place and federal government suppliers are being phased into mandatory PEPPOL electronic invoicing from 2024. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically for registrants with annual taxable revenue over CAD 10 million and for all selected financial institutions, using CRA GST/HST Financial Institution portal and Revenu Québec's e-filing system (CL-X/EDI). There is no per-invoice clearance obligation, but issuers must retain digital invoice records in compliance with CRA IC05-1 and Revenu Québec retention rules.

CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default), retroactively claw back the zero-rating, assess GST/QST at 14.975% on the full consideration, deny input tax credits, and charge prescribed daily interest. Statutory penalties include: ETA s. 285 — CAD 500 or 25% of net tax, whichever is greater, for false statements; ETA s. 284.01 — gross negligence penalty of up to 25% of the amount of tax; ETA s. 286 — fines up to 250% of the tax evaded and imprisonment on conviction; parallel QST penalties under Quebec Tax Administration Act s. 1015.15 (up to 70% for tax evasion) plus daily compound interest under QTA ss. 1021-1027.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions; no exchange-control permits, no prior Bank of Canada approval, and no profit-remittance licence are required. Practical hurdles are limited to (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced under applicable DTAs), (ii) the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA) solvency/liquidity test before dividends may be declared, and (iii) for the EMI float, segregation obligations under the RPAA and the AMF Payment Services Providers Act which prohibit using safeguarded end-user funds for remittances. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and repayments of shareholder loans may be wired freely once tax and corporate formalities are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in any domestic or foreign account. Compliance duties are limited to FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, border-declaration of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000+, and continuous sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central bank operating the Lynx RTGS and ACSS retail clearing systems, and the federal supervisory authority for retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency; FX policy is a floating-rate regime administered through normal central-bank monetary policy. Complementing authorities are FINTRAC (AML/CFT registration and cross-border reporting), Global Affairs Canada (sanctions), and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) of Quebec (provincial payment services provider licensing).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: conditional-medium. Canadian law has no 'EMI' licence; the entity must combine a FINTRAC MSB registration, a Bank of Canada RPAA registration, and an AMF payment services provider licence in Quebec. Offshore EU/UK digital EMIs cannot act as the primary settlement or safeguarding bank for CAD because Canada's clearing links (Lynx/ACSS) require membership in Payments Canada. SEPA Credit reach is legally obtainable only through a white-label partner holding an EU PSD2 EMI/bank licence or through the Canadian entity registering as an agent of such partner; the 'virtual IBAN' product must be understood as a virtual account number backed by a real Canadian deposit account, because the IBAN format is not native to Canada. Tier-1 Canadian banks remain the only viable rails for CAD float safeguarding and clearing; an offshore EMI is a complement for EUR payments, never a substitute for domestic banking.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: FINTRAC registration circular dependency: banks decline the account until the entity holds an MSB registration, while FINTRAC's practical review expects an operational Canadian bank reference.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent initiates an MT103 in EUR or USD to the Canadian entity's operating account at a Schedule I bank, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'INTC/EQUI' and the narrative 'share capital contribution under the Quebec Business Corporations Act'. Pre-credit verification requires: certified board resolution authorising share issuance, executed share subscription agreement, updated shareholders' register, source-of-wealth evidence for the investor, CRS/FATCA self-certification (e.g., W-8BEN-E), and confirmation that the funds are corporate capital rather than e-money float. CAD conversion is executed at negotiated spot or forward rates and settled same-day on Lynx; FINTRAC receives an international EFT report for every CAD 10,000+ wire. The capital is posted to the operational ledger and is never commingled with safeguarded customer funds.Domestic CAD trade payments clear through Payment Canada's ACSS (batch) or Lynx (same-day high-value), each wire passing the bank's payment-sanctions filter. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: conditional-medium. Canadian law has no 'EMI' licence; the entity must combine a FINTRAC MSB registration, a Bank of Canada RPAA registration, and an AMF payment services provider licence in Quebec. Offshore EU/UK digital EMIs cannot act as the primary settlement or safeguarding bank for CAD because Canada's clearing links (Lynx/ACSS) require membership in Payments Canada. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate value of specified cross-border transactions per taxation year requiring Form T106 disclosure; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected under CRA administrative guidance for all non-arm's length transactions, with no separate statutory Local File/Master File filing threshold in Canada.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(2)) and the judicial substance-over-form doctrine may disallow arrangements lacking genuine economic substance. For an electronic money institution claiming treaty benefits, Canadian courts require real commercial and operational activity in Canada; CRA also reviews beneficial-ownership and substance of the recipient. In addition, FINTRAC registration as a money service business requires documented management, compliance employees, and operational systems located in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under Income Tax Act subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under section 239 with fines from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in falsifying records are jointly and severally liable for civil penalties; judicial interim release may impose passport surrender and travel restraints pending trial; a conviction can result in imprisonment and orders for personal restitution of the avoided taxes.
Failure to Withhold / Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess the payer for the full unreported withholding amount, plus interest and penalties; repeated failures trigger gross negligence penalties and seizures of bank accounts; Quebec may apply parallel assessments under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Subject to the due-diligence defense, directors are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for the unremitted withholding amounts and related interest/penalties; CRA can enforce through director liability notices, asset garnishment, and civil court judgments.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under subsection 247(2); penalty under subsection 247(3) of 10% of the net upward adjustment where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts and maintain contemporaneous documentation; interest accrues from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not personally liable for pure transfer pricing adjustments; however, directors who sign false or incomplete declarations or deliberately obstruct CRA examinations may be exposed to gross negligence penalties, criminal fraud liability, or professional disqualification.
Shell Company / AML Licensing Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may disallow treaty benefits and defeat artificial arrangements under GAAR; Revenu Quebec can disregard transactions lacking substance; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC may impose administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 per violation, and criminal summary convictions can result in fines and imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directly or instruct others in the operation of an unregistered money services business are subject to personal criminal liability if they knew or acquiesced in the non-registration; the corporate veil may be pierced where client funds were intermingled or fraudulent conveyances occurred, with civil restitution, travel restrictions pending criminal investigation, and imprisonment.
