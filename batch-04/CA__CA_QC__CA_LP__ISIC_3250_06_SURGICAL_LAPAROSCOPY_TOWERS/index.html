Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Quebec limited partnership; a limited partner may contribute money or property but not services, and the agreed contribution must be disclosed in the partnership agreement. No statutory escrow is imposed; if external trade financing is used, a banking escrow or committed capital facility equal to at least 20% of projected purchase orders is commonly required.

Funding & unlock path: Montréal Fire Prevention Service (Service de sécurité incendie de Montréal) fire safety permit and inspection; Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ) building structural load and occupancy certificate; Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) workplace health and safety clearance, machine-guarding inspection and employer prevention program.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Provincial: Revenu Québec, Mon dossier d'entreprise portal; Business registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account from the Canada Revenue Agency via Form RC1 or BRO, submitting the partnership agreement, foreign formation documents, and Canadian business substance details. For Quebec, the LP must then obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and register for QST and, if applicable, Quebec income tax and source deductions with Revenu Québec. No minimum capital is required; non-resident partners are permitted. The partnership must file T5013/T3-slate partnership information returns and, for Quebec, RL-15 partnership slips.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account from the Canada Revenue Agency via Form RC1 or BRO, submitting the partnership agreement, foreign formation documents, and Canadian business substance details. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act by statutory default. A non-resident supplier may elect under section 240(2.1) of the Excise Tax Act to be registered as a Canadian resident for GST/HST purposes. No equivalent QST registration election exists. Foreign partners may invoke entity-classification provisions of applicable tax treaties, including Article IV(6) of the Canada-US treaty for fiscally transparent hybrid entities. Hybrid mismatch rules may apply to deny deductions on payments through the transparent partnership that result in no taxable inclusion in the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for GST and QST; calculated on taxable supplies made in Canada in the current and immediately preceding four calendar quarters. Zero-rated export supplies delivered outside Canada may not trigger the threshold, but voluntary registration is required to claim input tax credits and substantiate export refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The surgical laparoscopy tower qualifies for 0% GST and 0% QST when the supplier is registered, the goods are supplied to a non-resident recipient, the goods are not for consumption or use in Canada before export, and the supplier ships the goods or delivers them to a common carrier for shipment to a destination outside Canada, retaining contemporaneous documentary proof. Offshore services directly related to the tower and performed outside Canada on goods located outside Canada may also be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI. Domestic sale of general endoscopic towers is not automatically zero-rated under the qualifying medical device provisions.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A/B13B or Canadian Export Reporting System filing)
  • Through Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and confirming final delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/TT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or digital stamping mandates. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL interoperability framework. Invoices must satisfy the GST/HST and QST statutory content requirements, be retained for at least six years, and, when issued in Quebec, comply with French-language rules unless a prescribed waiver or B2B exception applies.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and claw back all zero-rated export claims with statutory interest if documentary substantiation is insufficient or the export transaction is held to be artificial or fraudulent. Civil penalties include false-statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Tax Administration Act, generally up to 25% of understated tax for gross negligence. Criminal tax evasion sanctions may reach fines of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, together with imprisonment of up to five years, depending on indictment; Revenu Québec may impose equivalent sanctions and administrative penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. There are no restrictions on repatriation of profits, dividends, partnership distributions, or loan repayments to non-residents. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible at market rates through Schedule I banks and authorized foreign exchange dealers. The principal regulatory friction affecting a Quebec limited partnership with non-resident limited partners is not exchange control but the Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax, generally 25% on partnership distributions to non-resident partners, reducible under applicable tax treaties, and the Quebec provincial tax administration (Revenu Quebec) reporting requirements. In addition, essential import-export transactions in the ISIC 3250 medical-device manufacturing sector may be subjected to FINTRAC's international electronic funds transfer reporting rules, particularly the 24-hour detection threshold of C$10,000, but there is no prior-approval requirement for either capital injection or profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists. Canada repealed wartime exchange controls and all export proceeds repatriation and mandatory conversion requirements decades ago and currently imposes no statutory timeline, minimum conversion ration, or central-bank surrender mechanism for foreign exchange earnings. A Quebec exporter of ultra-high-definition 4K minimally invasive rigid endoscope systems may lawfully retain export receipts in USD, EUR, or CNY in Canadian chartered bank accounts, in USD clearing accounts at the same bank, or in permitted offshore correspondent accounts. Foreign-currency export receivables are booked at the prevailing Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for financial statement purposes, and the partnership is free to convert those proceeds at commercial rates on a when-needed basis. There is no requirement to pre-clear the utilization of export proceeds with the Bank of Canada, OSFI, or FINTRAC beyond standard AML reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and operator of the high-value settlement accounts framework; however, Canada does not maintain a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration. Currency convertibility and exchange-control oversight are not centralized in a single agency. The Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act govern the applicable rails, while FINTRAC exercises anti-money-laundering supervision over cross-border transfers, and OSFI supervises authorized banks and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low_Feasibility as a primary liquidity rail. Canada does not recognize European Union or United Kingdom E-Money Institution authorizations as a basis for deposit-taking or payment-account services on Canadian soil. A non-resident digital EMI seeking to serve the Quebec laparoscopy tower manufacturer must instead register with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and obtain a separate Payment Service Provider licence from the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, which came into general application in September 2024. Furthermore, the Quebec Charter of the French Language imposes French-language requirements on contracts, directed advertising, and interface documentation, emphasizing additional compliance burden for foreign EMIs. The strategic, legally sound approach is to use a FINTRAC-registered cross-border payment processor (Wise, Corpay, or Payoneer) exclusively as a companion rail for moderate-value supplier disbursements while concentrating transactional liquidity, LC issuance, and correspondent-bank relationships within a Tier 1 Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec) for taxable Canadian corporations; the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and does not pay income tax, income being allocated to partners and subject to tax at their respective rates

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; Quebec imposes no separate dividend withholding tax; for a limited partnership, distributions of profits are not dividends, but the non-resident partner is subject to Canadian Part I tax on its allocable share of Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty relief

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million aggregate same-year non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a related non-resident triggers the mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation requirement and Local File / Master File preparation under CRA administrative policy; a net transfer pricing adjustment exceeding CAD 500,000 engages the 10% penalty under subsection 247(3); Country-by-Country filing is triggered when the consolidated group revenue is at least CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in the UAE, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act and section 1079.22 of the Quebec Taxation Act denies the tax benefit arising from an avoidance transaction lacking business purpose and economic substance; for a limited partnership manufacturing ultra-HD 4K laparoscopy towers, CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinize the presence of actual employees, premises, decision-making functions, assumption of commercial risk and independent accounting; the partnership must demonstrate that functional and risk profiles support the income allocation and transfer prices; the CRA may also recharacterize transactions under section 247 to reflect the economic substance and reallocate income to the entity performing the manufacturing functions; no treaty resident partner may claim treaty benefits where the principal purpose of the LP structure is to obtain a tax benefit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment for up to 5 years (Income Tax Act section 239, Taxation Act section 1640); civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA subsection 163(2))

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and senior employees who instruct or acquiesce in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the penalty; CRA may invoke subsection 160(1) to assess personal liability for assets transferred to non-arm's length persons to avoid tax; travel bans and asset seizures can be ordered where unpaid assessments exceed CAD 50,000; general partners of the LP are fully liable for the partnership's tax debts, and limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts income to the arm's length amount and imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment if the adjustment exceeds CAD 500,000 and the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts (ITA subsection 247(3)); interest accrues on the reassessment; the statute of limitations is extended by 3 years where misrepresentation is involved

Director personal liability: Directors who approved transfer prices without adequate valuation or documentation may be assessed for director liability with respect to withholding obligations and unremitted payroll; civil breach of fiduciary duty claims may require directors to compensate the company for penalties and interest; Canadian courts may issue a compliance order imposing a personal reporting or financial penalty
Shell Companies / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and imposes a penalty of 15% of the tax benefit under subsection 245(7) of the ITA, with a parallel rule under section 1079.23 of the QTA; the transactions are recharacterized and the income is reassessed as if the commercial reality controlled; additional interest and gross negligence penalties may apply

Director personal liability: The general partners are fully liable for all resulting taxes, interest and penalties; limited partners who become involved in the management or control of the partnership lose their liability shield under the Partnership Act (Quebec) and are personally liable for the partnership's obligations and tax arrears; directors can be ordered to reverse unlawful distributions and may be subject to a travel prohibition during the audit
AML / FATCA & CRS Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for individuals under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) Terrorist Financing Act; repeated non-compliance can result in multi-year penalties and revocation of any necessary business license; conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to 200% of the laundered amount

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and designated compliance officers are personally liable for failure to establish a compliant AML/ATF program; individuals found to have knowingly made false reports are subject to criminal liability and imprisonment; directors may also be held personally liable in civil proceedings for losses incurred by the partnership or investors as a result of AML non-compliance
