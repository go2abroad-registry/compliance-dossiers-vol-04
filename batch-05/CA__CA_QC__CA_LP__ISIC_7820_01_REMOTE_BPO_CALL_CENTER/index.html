Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None — no statutory minimum capital for a Quebec limited partnership. The declaration of registration must state each partner's agreed contribution and the aggregate contribution value. No escrow account is legally required; contributions may be made in cash, property, or rights, but a limited partner may not contribute services or labour.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (fire alarm, emergency lighting, and evacuation plan) issued by the local fire department or municipal authority; building structural load clearance required for server rooms and high-density IT racks; CNESST occupational health and safety program covering ergonomic workstation assessments, telework policy, and workplace violence prevention; emergency response plan and first-aid kit configuration per the Regulation respecting occupational health and safety.

Paid-in capital rule: None — no statutory minimum capital for a Quebec limited partnership. No escrow account is legally required; contributions may be made in cash, property, or rights, but a limited partner may not contribute services or labour.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html, with Quebec-specific administration by Revenu Québec — www.revenuquebec.ca (GST/HST and QST are filed through Revenu Québec for a Quebec establishment)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident establishing a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must first register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), then register for the CRA Business Number (BN; 9-digit RT number) and open GST/HST and QST accounts. Registration is completed online via REQ 'Mon dossier' / CRA 'Business Registration Online'; Form RC1 (CRA) and Revenu Québec's LM-1-V request apply. Non-resident partners are not required to be resident but the LP must appoint a Canadian agent/representative for service and tax administration; the partnership must file an information return (T5013) and may be required to remit Part XIII withholding/Part I instalments on behalf of non-resident partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Quebec LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax; for GST/HST and QST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' that is a registrant for the partnership's commercial activities. The non-resident partners are not subject to Canadian entity-level tax on the partnership's active BPO income unless a Canadian permanent establishment exists and the income is allocated to them; Part XIII withholding applies only to certain passive amounts.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. The LP's pass-through status is statutory under the Income Tax Act and the Civil Code of Quebec. Relevant elections include: (i) partnership income/loss allocation elections under s. 96 ITA and the s. 103 fair-sharing agreement; (ii) deemed partnership accounting election for provincial allocation in Quebec; (iii) for GST/HST, the 'nil consideration' election for supplies between closely related registrants (s. 156 ETA) and the s. 150 election to combine/separate large corporations do not apply to LPs; (iv) no hybrid mismatch election is available domestically, but Canada's BEAT/GILTI-style hybrid mismatch rules under s. 12.2 and s. 18.2 ITA (post-BEPS) may need to be considered by non-resident partners in their home jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. Small-supplier threshold under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Sales Tax Act: registration becomes mandatory once total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of a service made by a Quebec registrant to a non-resident client is zero-rated under Part V, s. 23 of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act (and the parallel QST zero-rating rule) if the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance, and not rendered to an individual in Canada. Offshore BPO/call-center services provided to foreign commercial clients therefore qualify for 0% GST and 0% QST if the recipient is a non-resident and does not use or enjoy the service in Canada; the provider should obtain a written declaration/contractual representation of non-residency and document that no Canadian end-user benefit is conferred.

  • Customs Export Declaration (goods-only; not applicable to exported BPO/call-center services)
  • Bill of Lading (goods-only; not applicable to exported BPO/call-center services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/wire record evidencing foreign-source payment for the service
  • BPO service export file: signed master services agreement with non-resident client; client non-resident status evidence (foreign registration number, registered office, tax residence certificate); itemized commercial invoices; call volume/performance reports; records of payment received via Wise B2B or international wire transfer

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada/Quebec. Electronic invoicing is permitted; invoices issued for QST/GST purposes must contain mandatory information under the ETA and QSTA and, for Quebec customers, must be in French unless an exemption applies. Records must be retained for six years. Revenu Québec may require electronic file formats upon audit; PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 is used voluntarily in B2B exchanges.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess improperly zero-rated supplies within the normal reassessment period (four years) or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation. A person who knowingly makes a false statement or participates in evading GST/QST is liable, under ETA s. 280 and QSTA, to a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the uncollected or unremitted tax, plus interest and arrears. Gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of additional tax payable may apply under the federal Income Tax Act to the partners if misstatements affect income allocation. Failure to maintain the export substantiation file (non-resident client evidence, contracts, invoices, remittance records) will shift the burden to the taxpayer and may result in full GST/QST reassessment, penalty, and interest; in fraud cases, criminal prosecution and unlimited fines or imprisonment may follow.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted the obligations of Article VIII, Sections 2, 3 and 4 of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There is no exchange control legislation, no prior approval for profit remittance or capital repatriation, and no repatriation tax beyond normal withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) for non-resident partners (typically 25% unless reduced by an income tax treaty such as the Canada-US treaty for US partners). A Quebec Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada remain taxable under Part I on their share of Canadian-source income. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) for cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the bank's own source-of-funds and sanctions screening; (iii) Revenu Québec obligations for tax instalments. No distinction between current and capital account restrictions exists, but Quebec does require an attestation of registration from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for corporate bank account opening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds. A BPO call center's invoices denominated in USD, EUR or other foreign currency may be settled directly into a Canadian or offshore bank account. If the receivables are received by a foreign account of the Quebec LP, Quebec's tax attribution rules and Canada's foreign affiliate / controlled foreign affiliate (CFA) rules will apply under the Income Tax Act for taxation purposes. Once an EFT crosses the Canadian border in an amount equal to CAD 10,000 or more, the reporting obligation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) rests on the financial intermediary, not the client; however, the LP must complete the bank's standard Cross-Border EFT Survey and confirm the underlying commercial contract for BPO services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and monetary policy coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential and AML/ATF supervision of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec-specific money-services business (MSB) licensing and supervision by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Act respecting money-services businesses (CQLR c E-12.000001).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for operational B2B money movement, Medium for capital escrow. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Corp.) reinforces its FINTRAC MSB registration and, for Quebec, an AMF licence under the Act respecting money-services businesses; it offers CAD-denominated account details, USD receiving details, and multi-currency routing across local clearing rails (Canada's EFT/Interac Acsys, US ACH/Wire, SEPA, GBP Faster Payments). Wise B2B API is viable for mass disbursements for a remote BPO call center (invoice payments, vendor and payroll payouts in 40+ currencies). Legal constraints: (i) Wise is an MSB, not a Schedule I bank, so it cannot provide credit, escrow, or overdraft facilities; (ii) it must apply enhanced due diligence to a Quebec LP with non-resident partners; (iii) Quebec's AMF requires the MSB to disclose the underlying commercial purpose for any cross-border transaction above its threshold. The LP should maintain a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank and use Wise only as a liquidity rail for low-value, high-frequency settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard compliant corridor: foreign limited partner instructs its bank to issue an SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire) to the Quebec LP's operational or escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. SWIFT Field 70 (Remittance Information) should state: 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / PARTNERSHIP CONTRIBUTION / INVOICE-REF-NUMBER' plus the LP's REQ registration number to satisfy bank CDD. Canada has no numeric ISO purpose-code regime for inbound transfers, but banks will require a narrative purpose code such as 'B02 – Business Partnership Contribution' on their internal onboarding forms. For amounts >= CAD 10,000, the receiving bank files EFTR with FINTRAC; for amounts above CAD 100,000 or conspicuous transactions, additional source-of-funds disclosure is triggered. Verification steps: (i) reconcile the SWIFT sender with the partner name on the capital call; (ii) verify LP's capital account balance and issue a capital receipt; (iii) update the LP's Beneficial Ownership Register and REQ annual declaration; (iv) if the contribution is in-kind (e.g., equipment for the call center), file the applicable Revenu Québec election for the capital contribution and set up an equalization-date journal entry.Primary rail for high-value single disbursements (vendor settlements, subcontractor licensing, partner distributions) is SWIFT MT103 from a Schedule I bank for CAD/USD/EUR, with Field 70 citing 'SERVICES / BPO CALL CENTER OPERATIONS / INVOICE-XXXX' and, for related-party payments, the required transfer pricing description. For mass payroll and low-value high-frequency operational payments (remote agents, software subscriptions, phone carriers), use Wise B2B's automated payout rails in the local clearing of each destination (US ACH, UK FPS, SEPA, etc.). If a client or vendor insists on trade finance, the permissible instruments are: (i) an irrevocable standby letter of credit (SBLC) issued under UCP600 by a Canadian Schedule I bank as performance security; (ii) a commercial documentary letter of credit (MT700) only for freight-forwarded equipment tied to a call-center build-out, but this is atypical for unfettered services. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for operational B2B money movement, Medium for capital escrow. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Corp.) reinforces its FINTRAC MSB registration and, for Quebec, an AMF licence under the Act respecting money-services businesses; it offers CAD-denominated account details, USD receiving details, and multi-currency routing across local clearing rails (Canada's EFT/Interac Acsys, US ACH/Wire, SEPA, GBP Faster Payments). Wise B2B API is viable for mass disbursements for a remote BPO call center (invoice payments, vendor and payroll payouts in 40+ currencies). Legal constraints: (i) Wise is an MSB, not a Schedule I bank, so it cannot provide credit, escrow, or overdraft facilities; (ii) it must apply enhanced due diligence to a Quebec LP with non-resident partners; (iii) Quebec's AMF requires the MSB to disclose the underlying commercial purpose for any cross-border transaction above its threshold. The LP should maintain a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank and use Wise only as a liquidity rail for low-value, high-frequency settlements.
