Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; business registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO); post-registration administration via CRA My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Extra-provincially register the Ontario limited partnership (LP) with the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch to obtain the Ontario registration number. Step 2: Obtain a federal CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by using Business Registration Online (BRO); the BN is a 9-digit enterprise number with program account identifiers (RT = GST/HST, RC = payroll, RP = T2 corporate, RZ = T5013 partnership information return). Step 3: Activate the RT (GST/HST) account in the LP's legal name; the LP registers as a 'person' for the partnership's taxable activities and reports Ontario HST on a single federal GST34 return. Step 4: Ontario issues no separate provincial TIN to partnerships; Ontario tax obligations are administered through the same federal BN. Ontario requires the Partnership Information Return (Form ON126) where at least one partner is an Ontario resident or carries on business in Ontario. Step 5: Each non-resident corporate partner deriving Canadian-source business income through the LP must independently obtain a BN and file a T2 Part I return; a non-resident limited partner with no Canadian permanent establishment may be subject to Canadian withholding rather than Part I filing, depending on the nature of the income. Expected approval: immediate to 5 business days via BRO; 6 to 8 weeks where RC1 paper review is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96, with income or loss allocated annually to partners via T5013. Available elections include: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration before the 30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(2.1); (2) ITA s. 216.1 election by a non-resident partner to file a Part I return on Canadian-source business income, obtaining access to Canadian deductions and losses; (3) ETA s. 273 joint election between a partnership and its specified members to treat intra-partnership taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (4) no hybrid-mismatch election applies at the LP level, but U.S. partners may file IRS Form 8832 to reclassify eligibility of the Canadian LP for U.S. federal income tax purposes, without altering Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than financial services) in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier definition, ETA s. 148(1)); non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold under ETA s. 240(1) and (1.1). Voluntary registration is expressly permitted under ETA s. 240(2.1) to claim input tax credits on export-related start-up costs, subject to the two-year ITC limitation rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (custom double-shot PBT keycaps) are zero-rated under ETA s. 6 and Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the supplier or its agent/common carrier exports the goods as soon as is reasonable after transfer of possession to the buyer and the supplier maintains proof of export. US-bound Shopify Payments / Stripe orders qualify where the LP ships physical keycaps to the US customer's foreign address with no delivery, consumption, or change of possession in Canada. Offshore design or prototyping services performed for non-resident customers may qualify under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (services performed wholly outside Canada) or s. 9 (services supplied to a non-resident), subject to exclusions for services relating to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or services rendered to a Canadian resident. The LP must issue HST zero-rated invoices (13% HST stated as 0%), claim matching input tax credits for domestic inputs, and reconcile zero-rated revenue on the GST34 return.

  • Customs Export Declaration: all exports of goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS, Form G49 / legacy CAED) prior to departure; the export report reference number evidences exit from Canada.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Courier Waybill: international waybill (UPS, FedEx, DHL) naming the LP as shipper and the US customer as consignee, evidencing departure from Canada and destination delivery.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: no statutory inward remittance certificate exists under Canadian law; the LP retains Shopify Payments / Stripe payout records and US bank statements converted to CAD as substitute consideration evidence supporting the zero-rated sale.
  • Supplementary Proof of Delivery: signed POD or courier delivery scan confirming receipt at the foreign consignee address, retained with the commercial invoice for six years.

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. All GST/HST registrants that registered after July 1, 2010 (including a newly formed Ontario LP) must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE regardless of revenue; legacy registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000 are also subject to mandatory e-filing under ETA s. 278.1. Remittances above CAD 10,000 must be made electronically through CRA My Payment or financial-institution bill payment referencing the RT program account. E-invoicing is voluntary; the CRA participates in the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing framework launched in 2024, and XML/Peppol invoices are accepted as equivalent to paper records. Registrants must retain all source records for six years in readable and exportable digital format under ETA s. 286, subject to CRA e-audit inspection.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims through the GST/HST proof-of-export verification program. Where documentation is inadequate, the zero rating is reversed, output tax at 13% HST is assessed on the full export consideration, and corresponding input tax credits are denied, effectively clawing back any refund. Penalty exposure includes ETA s. 285 (25% of misstated net tax for gross negligence), ETA s. 280 interest and the CAD 250 minimum administrative penalty, ETA s. 327.1 / s. 329 penalties for false statements or omissions, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud) carrying imprisonment of up to 14 years. The CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation arises from neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, and may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) to deny refunds on artificial export arrangements. Where zero rating is substantiated, the LP retains full ITC recovery on domestic PBT resin, injection-molding tooling, industrial electricity, and warehouse costs attributable to exported keycaps.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized, freely floating exchange-rate regime with no foreign-exchange-control legislation; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was abolished in 1951, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without central-bank pre-approval. Dividend, management-fee, interest, and capital-repayment remittances by an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident partners face no convertibility barrier; the only tax overlay is Canadian Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, commonly treaty-reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-US Tax Convention or applicable Canada-Asia treaties) and potential Part I tax if the non-resident partner carries on business through a Canadian permanent establishment. Practical remittance hurdles are documentary only: FATCA/CRS self-certification, source-of-funds evidence, and bank-level sanctions screening. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a G10 currency with deep interbank liquidity in USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, and GBP/CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no requirement to route export receipts through a designated domestic account. Export proceeds from keyboard keycap sales may be held in foreign currency, converted commercially, or swept to CAD at the exporter's discretion. The sole compliance duty is FINTRAC reporting of large transactions (CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction or within 24 hours for cash, EFT, and certain wire transfers) and the maintenance of complete KYC/AML records under PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary policy, currency issuance, and financial-system oversight under the Bank of Canada Act; foreign-exchange and trade-compliance administration sits with the Department of Finance Canada, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as primary treasury rail; Medium as auxiliary collection-and-disbursement conduit. Canada has no statutory Electronic Money Institution licensing class under the Bank Act; a non-resident EMI cannot accept deposits in Canada without federal authorization under Bank Act s.57, and any Canadian-nexus money-services business must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with provincial consumer-protection rules. Offshore EMIs (UK-authorised or EU-authorised under PSD2) may legally be used for supplier disbursements, cross-border overhead payments, and pre-funding of procurement, provided they do not hold customer funds in a manner that constitutes a Canadian deposit. Shopify Payments and Stripe function as Payment Service Providers (PSP/aggregator model); settlement of e-commerce keycap sales must route to a CAD-denominated Schedule I bank account to minimise FX conversion cost and to preserve clean treasury traceability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires the remitting bank to execute a SWIFT MT103 (converting USD or other currency to CAD at a locked corporate FX forward rate) in favor of the Ontario LP's CAD capital-escrow or operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire must state the legal name of the corporate General Partner, the exact Ontario LP name matching the account title, and a purpose description of Capital Contribution - Limited Partnership - ISIC 3240 / NAICS 334118 keycap manufacturing. For USD-denominated incoming wires, the originator must include the receiving bank's US correspondent-bank field (e.g., RBC's New York correspondent or TD's US clearing branch) to avoid failed routing. A pre-notification wiring-instructions letter on bank letterhead must be transmitted to the remitting institution before the wire; receiving banks must report deposits of CAD 10,000 or more under FINTRAC large-transaction rules. Shopify Payments and Stripe function as Payment Service Providers (PSP/aggregator model); settlement of e-commerce keycap sales must route to a CAD-denominated Schedule I bank account to minimise FX conversion cost and to preserve clean treasury traceability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Ontario general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Ontario general 11.5%). A Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; this rate applies to corporate partners on their allocable share of LP income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. Treaty-reduced rates apply, typically 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of the voting shares under the Canada–US DTA. For the Ontario LP, ordinary business income allocated to a non-resident partner is taxed under Part I at the partner level and distributions of previously taxed profits are not subject to dividend WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident in a taxation year. If exceeded, the taxpayer must file Form T106 and maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File); Master File documentation is required for multinational groups with consolidated gross revenue of at least C$750,000,000. CRA requires documentation to be prepared before the tax return is filed.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the CRA enforces substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the 'carrying on business in Canada' test, and the permanent establishment doctrine. The Ontario LP engaged in custom mechanical keycap manufacturing must maintain a genuine manufacturing presence, personnel, actual costs, and economic risk in Ontario; a shell LP used to divert profits to non-residents will be disregarded.
