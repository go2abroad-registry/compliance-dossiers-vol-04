Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box entity-classification election exists in Canada; a Business Corporation is always a separate taxable person for federal Part I tax and Quebec Taxation Act purposes. Available elections and regimes: (1) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status electing the small-business deduction automatically on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income — combined federal 9% and Quebec 5.0% reduced rates; (2) IRS Form 8832-style transparency is unavailable to Canadian entities, but hybrid mismatch deduction-denial rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.6 govern conduit arrangements; (3) GST/HST Quick Method (Form GST74) for registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000 — 2.5% remittance rate for goods in Quebec; (4) Annual GST/HST filing election (Form GST20) for registrants below CAD 1.5 million in annual taxable supplies; (5) QST quick-method and ITR simplified-calculation positions recognized by Revenu Québec for small and medium enterprises; (6) Excise Tax Act s. 216(1) net-basis election for non-resident rental/royalty income — not applicable to trading profit; (7) Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules effective 1 October 2023 cap interest deductibility at 30% of EBITDA for affiliated-party and third-party financing, a limitation rather than an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies, Excise Tax Act s. 148(1)); QST threshold is the CAD 30,000 base adjusted annually for the Quebec consumer price index — approximately CAD 35,100 for 2024 (CAD 34,300 for 2023). The ISIC 4649 wholesale distribution model for clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents will exceed this threshold in the ordinary course of trade; registration is mandatory from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Exported tangible personal property is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 and AQST Schedule IV, Part IV, where the wholesale distributor (i) contracts to supply IVD assays, ELISA kits, and diagnostic reagents (HS 3822.1000 / HS 3002.1400), (ii) ships to a destination outside Canada by common carrier, (iii) exports the goods as soon as commercially reasonable after delivery, and (iv) retains proof of export per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-4 and Revenu Québec's parallel interpretation. Services: Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 applies to services performed for a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST at the time the service is performed, and where the service does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada — covering cold-chain logistics for offshore consignees, regulatory-dossier maintenance, and after-sales technical support. Domestic IVD reagent sales to private laboratories and hospitals are taxable at 14.975% unless the goods qualify for the narrow medical-device exemption under Schedule VI, Part III (generally not available to wholesale distributor resales); the commercial model is therefore a fully taxed domestic supply and a zero-rated export supply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API pre-clearance, or digital stamping protocol exists in Canada or Quebec. Canada has voluntarily adopted the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework (EN 16931) for interoperable B2B/B2G exchange; participation is elective. Mandatory electronic filing of returns does apply: GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file via GST/HST NETFILE; Revenu Québec mandates electronic filing of QST returns and payments through its online business portal. Statutory invoice content under ETA s. 169 and AQST requires the supplier's legal name, GST/HST and QST registration numbers, invoice date, recipient name, description and quantity of goods, taxable amount, and separate GST and QST amounts; export invoices must carry the 0% zero-rating notation. Quebec's Charter of the French Language (CQLR c. C-11) mandates French-language invoices, customs declarations, contracts, and labels for commercial activities in Quebec; bilingual documentation is accepted, and French-only copies must be produced on request. Records supporting zero-rated exports must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286; Quebec Tax Administration Act s. 39).

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit exporters claiming zero-rating; failure to produce the proof-of-export bundle within the six-year retention period triggers reassessment of the supply at the combined 14.975% rate (5% GST + 9.975% QST) with compounded daily interest from the original reporting date. Penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 280(1): the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of understated tax; s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty: 50% of avoided tax; s. 327 criminal evasion: fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Quebec Tax Administration Act s. 59 imposes a 50% penalty on the QST avoided (100% for a second offence within five years), and s. 59.1 a 25% penalty for false statements, both cumulative with federal amounts. Clawbacks apply retrospectively to all input tax credits/input tax refunds claimed, to rebates issued on zero-rated export invoices, and to de-registration inventory upon cancellation of the GST/HST registration; CRA may revoke registration for repeat non-compliance, causing a deemed supply of all assets on hand. Appeals lie to the Tax Court of Canada and the Tribunal administratif du Québec within 90 days; penalty relief is granted only for reasonable care in reliance on written CRA/Revenu Québec rulings. Canadian tax law imposes no fixed statutory export-FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard international trade settlement and banking AML/KYC confirmation practice, and carries no Canadian tax penalty absent a fictitious-export finding.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating Canadian dollar and has no substantive exchange-control regime; the currency is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. Inbound equity contributions, loan repatriations, dividends and royalty payments do not require central bank approval. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory rate, reduced to 5-15% under an applicable income tax treaty (typically 5% for a substantial US corporate shareholder; 10% for PRC residents), provided Form NR301 and FATCA/CRS self-certifications are filed. The Investment Canada Act may screen acquisition of control of a Canadian wholesale business if the enterprise value exceeds the WTO-investor threshold (approximately CAD 1.3 billion for 2024), but this is not a convertibility barrier and does not apply to a newly formed distribution operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline or conversion ratio for export proceeds; wholesale distributors may retain receipts indefinitely in CAD, USD or EUR accounts. Banks are required to report to FINTRAC every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (inward or outward), and physical currency movements above CAD 10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Exporters must retain supporting commercial documentation (invoices, airway bills or bills of lading, and customs declarations where required) to evidence the business purpose of each cross-border credit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange policy and exchange-control supervision rest with the Minister of Finance and the Bank of Canada; AML/EFT reporting is administered by FINTRAC; prudential regulation of clearing banks is conducted by OSFI; Payments Canada owns and operates the LVTS, ACSS and Real-Time Rail (RTR) settlement systems.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 5 million for other transactions (per year) – triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but applies General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) and requires substantive economic activity for treaty benefits. CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial rationale or substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty) plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted withholdings (e.g., payroll, GST/HST) and for tax evasion penalties if they directed or acquiesced in the conduct. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and impose transfer pricing adjustments with penalties up to 10% of the adjustment (if not reasonable efforts).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes arising from recharacterization if they signed false certificates or knowingly participated in a sham. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties for non-compliance with record-keeping and reporting obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to implement AML policies or directed non-compliance. Travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for criminal violations.
