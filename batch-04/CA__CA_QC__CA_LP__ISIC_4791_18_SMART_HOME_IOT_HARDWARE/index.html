Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), online portal: www.registraireentreprises.gouv.qc.ca

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), arts. 2186-2251 (partnerships and limited partnerships); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-45), ss. 1, 2, 21-24, together with its General Regulation. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not apply because the registered vehicle is a Québec limited partnership, not a federal corporation. The general partner is subject to the ordinary rules of civil liability and to any express governance terms contained in the limited partnership agreement.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de société en commandite / Declaration of a Limited Partnership, filed electronically through the REQ's immatriculation registration service.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership. Capital contributions are contractually fixed in the limited partnership agreement, credited to each limited partner's capital account when called, and need not be escrowed before registration. A nominal initial contribution is legally acceptable because creditor protection flows from public registration rather than capital maintenance.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership. Capital contributions are contractually fixed in the limited partnership agreement, credited to each limited partner's capital account when called, and need not be escrowed before registration. A nominal initial contribution is legally acceptable because creditor protection flows from public registration rather than capital maintenance.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) the general partner forms the Limited Partnership under the Civil Code of Québec; (2) the capital commitment subscribed by each limited partner is accepted and credited to that partner's capital account; (3) the principal establishment and registered office is set at [complete address], Province of Québec; (4) [name of representative] is appointed partnership representative, empowered to complete registration, open bank accounts, obtain tax accounts and engage professional advisors; and (5) all necessary declarations, filings and fee payments to the Registraire des entreprises du Québec are approved.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — retail and warehousing of smart-home IoT hardware under ISIC 4791 is not a regulated industrial activity in Quebec; no provincial industrial licensing board must approve the operation.

Category: No provincial operating or manufacturing licence is required. A municipal business permit and fire safety clearance may apply if a physical retail location is opened; such permits are municipal in nature rather than provincial industry licences.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Agence du revenu du Québec (Revenu Québec) - Portal: Revenu Québec Online Services (clients.revenuquebec.ca); Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Portal: My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec must: (1) register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (2) open a GST/HST account (program account RT) and, if importing goods, an import/export account (RM); (3) register separately with Revenu Québec for the QST using the BN, via Revenu Québec Online Services or Form LM-1-V; (4) for non-resident partnerships, registration is mandatory when the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada or solicits orders in Canada, subject to the small supplier threshold; (5) each registration must be completed before the obligation to collect tax arises, typically before the first sale to a Quebec consumer or before goods are imported into Canada; the QST registration number is displayed on all invoices as 'QST registration number'.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec must: (1) register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (2) open a GST/HST account (program account RT) and, if importing goods, an import/export account (RM); (3) register separately with Revenu Québec for the QST using the BN, via Revenu Québec Online Services or Form LM-1-V; (4) for non-resident partnerships, registration is mandatory when the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada or solicits orders in Canada, subject to the small supplier threshold; (5) each registration must be completed before the obligation to collect tax arises, typically before the first sale to a Quebec consumer or before goods are imported into Canada; the QST registration number is displayed on all invoices as 'QST registration number'.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian and Quebec income tax purposes: no tax is payable at the partnership level; income or loss is allocated to partners in accordance with the limited partnership agreement. There is no 'check-the-box' election for Canadian LPs; however, partners may elect under section 96(3) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and section 681.1 of the Taxation Act (Quebec) to compute income at partnership level differently for certain inter-provincial rules. Hybrid mismatch elections are available under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act and corresponding Quebec rules, addressing mismatched payments between Canada and foreign jurisdictions where an LP is treated as opaque abroad.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. QST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (excluding financial services) in Quebec or Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons are also required to register if they solicit orders in Canada or enter Canada to perform services, regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) supplies of goods exported by the supplier (Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act); (ii) supplies of services performed in respect of exported goods; (iii) supplies of services to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and where the service is not for use in Canada; and (iv) certain supplies of goods delivered by the supplier to a common carrier for export. For QST, analogous zero-rating rules apply under Schedule III to the QST Act. Conditions: goods must be exported 'as soon as is reasonable' and documentary proof of export must be retained; if goods are not exported within 60 days of delivery, zero-rating is denied unless the delay is due to exigent circumstances. A registrant that zero-rates must maintain evidence of export in its books and records for six years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / A1/A6 or electronic CAERS declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or truck waybill)
  • Commercial invoice showing the vendor's and purchaser's names and addresses, description, and value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire or payment receipt evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the foreign buyer)
  • Packing list
  • Proof of delivery to the foreign destination (signed delivery receipt or customs entry at destination)
  • Carrier certificate of export (if relied upon to show export within 60 days)

Quebec mandates electronic invoicing and real-time reporting for B2B transactions through the Revenu Québec 'facturation électronique' system (based on the PEPPOL network). Registered suppliers must transmit invoice data to Revenu Québec in near real time (no later than the time of issue for larger taxpayers; phased implementation for smaller suppliers). The invoice must include QST registration number, BN, date, supplier/customer names, total amount, and QST/GST breakdown. There is no federal real-time clearance mandate for GST/HST; however, all electronic records must be retained and made available to the CRA on request. For export goods, an electronic export declaration must be filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) before goods exit Canada.

The CRA and Revenu Québec regularly audit exporters' zero-rating claims. If an export is not substantiated with the required documentation, the tax authority may reassess the supply as taxable, and the supplier becomes liable for the uncollected GST/QST (currently 14.975% in Quebec), plus interest and penalties. Penalties include: GST/HST false statement penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 50% of the amount at issue), Revenu Québec penalties under section 1083 of the Taxation Act (Quebec) (up to 50%), and gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act where applicable. In aggravated cases, criminal prosecution may be pursued, with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for tax evasion. Retrospective clawbacks are generally limited to the standard statutory reassessment period (four years for GST/HST and QST), which is extended to seven years or unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains liberalized current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement. No exchange control approvals are required for inward or outward remittances, profit repatriation, or partnership distributions. Non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership may freely remit earnings in CAD or other convertible currencies, subject only to Part XIII withholding taxes of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable treaty relief. Banks independently perform source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership due diligence before effecting transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender rules operate in Canada. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no minimum conversion ratio, and no obligation to force-convert export receipts into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances and convert at negotiated commercial rates. Cross-border movements of CAD 10,000 or more require reporting to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with foreign exchange supervision administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). The Minister of Finance retains residual policy authority over exchange matters under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Non-resident electronic money institutions may clear CAD payments only if they have registered as a money service business (MSB) with FINTRAC, maintain a Canadian legal agent, and implement a compliant AML/ATF program. Pure offshore EMIs without Canadian registration cannot open direct clearing accounts or operate as payment intermediaries for a Quebec LP. The practical clear path is to use Canadian licensed payment processors such as Stripe or Shopify Payments for retail transactions and restrict offshore EMIs to settlement convenience products.

Defense evidence: FINTRAC MSB registration confirmation or a legal opinion confirming exemption; written AML/ATF policy and risk assessment; appointed compliance officer; transactional records demonstrating the LP is selling its own inventory and does not operate a payment intermediary service; explanation of the retail business model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or capital contributions from foreign partners are routed through SWIFT MT103 (or domestic large-value wire via the Canadian Payments Association's Large Value Transfer System, LVTS) with the payment purpose coded as 'EQUITY INJECTION' or 'CAPITAL INVESTMENT'. The Quebec LP must provide a board/partner resolution approving the injection, the concession or partnership agreement, the source-of-funds declaration, and the Canadian Business Number. No exchange control permit is required; the receiving bank verifies the sender and the transaction narrative, and may require confirmation that the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime.Outbound trade payments to foreign IoT hardware manufacturers are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank USD/EUR/CNY transfers, or through Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for high-value procurement. Domestic CAD payments are cleared via the CPA Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or LVTS for same-day settlement.
