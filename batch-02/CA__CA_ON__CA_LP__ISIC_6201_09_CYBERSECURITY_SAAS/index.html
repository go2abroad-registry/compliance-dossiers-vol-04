Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under section 2 of Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Offshore services, including SaaS provided to non-residents, may qualify if the service is performed outside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods shipments)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from foreign customer
  • Service contract evidencing export of digital services

Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns via CRA My Business Account; large-volume registrants required to file electronically. No real-time fiscal clearance system; quarterly returns with supporting documentation.

Failure to substantiate zero-rating claims results in full GST/HST liability, interest charges, and penalties up to 50% of the tax amount under the Excise Tax Act. Retrospective reassessment possible within normal limitation periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no general exchange-control legislation and no central-bank pre-approval for SaaS cross-border invoices, capital contributions, or profit repatriation; Canada complies with IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar is freely traded, and USD/EUR invoicing, clearing, and offshore retention are unrestricted. Remittance friction is limited to tax layers: non-resident partners of an Ontario limited partnership are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory) on Canadian-source income distributions unless reduced by treaty (e.g., Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty rates of 0-15%); management or administration fees paid to non-residents are withheld at 25% subject to applicable treaty reduction. Ontario HST (13%) applies to taxable SaaS supplies but does not impede currency conversion or capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls and the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations in 1951; there is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation horizon, or forced surrender of export or cloud-subscription receipts denominated in foreign currency. An Ontario LP may retain USD/EUR proceeds in multicurrency accounts at a Schedule I bank or a Canadian branch of a foreign bank, subject only to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000) and general banking disclosure rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/CTF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) may establish CAD settlement accounts for a registered Ontario LP only if FINTRAC-registered as money services businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; unregistered provision of cross-border clearing to Canadians is a strict-liability offence. Major Canadian banks also apply policy-level de-risking and reject or suspend wires originated by unregistered or grey-market EMIs. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to the LP's CAD/USD account at a Schedule I bank with remittance information reading 'Capital Contribution to [Ontario LP Legal Name] – [Partner Name] – [Reference]'. USD inbound wires should clear via Fedwire through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (e.g., RBC Bank (Georgia), N.A. for RBC; BMO Harris Bank N.A. for BMO); CAD inbound wires settle through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). No exchange-control permit or central-bank approval is required; the receiving bank will complete FINTRAC reporting for EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000. Verification bundle comprises an executed partnership agreement, updated capital account schedule, source-of-funds declaration, and a solicitor or CPA letter confirming the contribution is equity and not a loan for Canadian income tax purposes.Outbound trade settlements are executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) and, for high-value USD cover payments, MT202/MT202COV. Domestic Canadian-dollar liabilities settle via LVTS (same-day irrevocable) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value instant credits. U.S. vendor payments use the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent clearing (ABA routing) or an in-house USD clearing hub; no IBAN is used for Canadian accounts (institution, transit, and account numbers apply). Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are permissible but not baseline for SaaS licensing; they are reserved for physical cybersecurity hardware procurement (e.g., penetration-testing appliances, network sensors). Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties for gross negligence (50% of understated tax)

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, GST/HST, and payroll deductions if they failed to withhold or remit. This includes civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for willful evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to taxable income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation not provided), and interest on underpaid taxes

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in or acquiesced to the non-compliance, leading to personal financial liability and potential criminal charges.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations, and up to CAD 500,000 for individuals, plus criminal penalties for willful non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML violations if they directed, authorized, or acquiesced to the non-compliance, leading to fines, imprisonment, and travel bans.
Failure to File Information Returns

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they were responsible for filing and failed to do so, including civil restitution and potential criminal charges for gross negligence.
