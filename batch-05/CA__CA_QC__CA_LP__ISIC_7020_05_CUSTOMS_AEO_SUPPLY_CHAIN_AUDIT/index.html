E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the société en commandite (Québec limited partnership) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ); an extra-provincial or foreign partnership carrying on business in Québec must register within 60 days of establishing an establishment in Québec, thereby obtaining the NEQ which anchors all Revenu Québec tax accounts. Step 4: Maintain program accounts by notifying CRA and Revenu Québec of changes within 30 days, including changes of place of business, general partner, or non-resident status; keep a complete technical file evidencing the LP's Canadian permanent establishment position and partnership registration for treaty and customs purposes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account bases. There are no exchange-control licences, no prior Central Bank authorizations, and no statutory hurdles for inward/outbound wire transfers, equity injections, partnership capital contributions, profit distributions, or foreign consulting-fee repatriation. Profit-remittance friction is exclusively tax-driven: for a Quebec limited partnership, a non-resident limited or general partner's allocable share of Canadian-source business income is reportable on NR4-T4A and distributions to that partner are subject to Income Tax Act (ITA) Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, reducible under an applicable tax treaty via Form NR7/NR301). For the AEO supply-chain audit consultancy, exported management-consulting services are generally zero-rated for GST/QST (Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, section 7 and the Quebec QST equivalent) when the recipient is non-resident and the services relate to property outside Canada. No additional convertibility tax, remittance levy, or IOF-style charge applies in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation, or conversion-ratio rules exist under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Formal foreign-exchange controls were abolished in 1951. The LP may retain USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency receivables from AEO audit services indefinitely, either offshore or in a domestic foreign-currency account at a Schedule I chartered bank; the surrender ratio is 0% and there is no statutory timeline for conversion. The only mandatory obligations are: (i) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting (EFTR) by the financial institution for each international wire of CAD 10,000 or more or its foreign-currency equivalent; (ii) conversion of foreign-currency income into Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada daily rate for income-tax computation under ITA subsection 261(1); and (iii) standard anti-money-laundering documentation required at account establishment. No approval from the Bank of Canada or the Department of Finance is required to repatriate proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises monetary authority and operates the national large-value settlement system; prudential banking regulation is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction-level anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec-specific money-services business registration is supervised by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Canada does not operate a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration because the Canadian dollar is governed under a non-restricted, free-float legal regime that is not subject to exchange-control permits.

(Wise Payments Ltd.) is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business in Canada and must satisfy Quebec AMF licensing under the Act respecting money-services businesses (M-30.1) if it maintains an establishment in Quebec. Wise is legally usable by the Quebec LP as a supplementary low-value collection/disbursement rail (matching target payment rail 'Wise'), but it cannot replace a Schedule I chartered-bank relationship: it offers no escrow, no commercial letter of credit, no Canada Revenue Agency payroll source-deduction clearing, no LVTS access, and no institutional-grade settlement status. If Wise does not hold a valid AMF Quebec endorsement, its viability drops to LOW and should be replaced with bank FX rails.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Quebec provincial rate); the Canada Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and no entity-level income tax is imposed on the partnership itself.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends and deemed dividends paid or credited to non-residents of Canada. For a Canadian limited partnership, current distributions are not generally treated as dividends; rather, non-resident partners are taxed on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income under Part I unless relieved by an applicable tax treaty. A treaty may reduce the statutory withholding rate to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on shareholding level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Income Tax Act imposes no explicit statutory monetary threshold for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation; however, CRA administrative practice requires documentation for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions and focuses review on specified transactions exceeding CAD 5 million annually. Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750 million or more, and such groups must also maintain OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. The applicable substance doctrine is the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, which denies a tax benefit arising from an avoidance transaction lacking genuine economic substance or a bona fide business purpose. Revenu Québec applies a parallel anti-avoidance rule in section 315 of the Quebec Taxation Act. For partnerships, the CRA and courts will also consider whether the LP has a real commercial presence, active management, and an actual risk-bearing role in the supply-chain consulting services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Unreported Canadian-Source Partnership Income

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax, plus interest on arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knew or consented to the omission are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unremitted source deductions; the Canada Border Services Agency may issue a departure order or travel ban where tax debt exceeds CAD 10,000; directors may also face criminal proceedings as parties to the evasion.
Transfer Pricing - Arm's-Length and Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 247(2) permits the CRA to redetermine the arm's-length amount; section 247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the gross transfer-pricing adjustment if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts and did not maintain contemporaneous documentation. Reassessment interest compounds daily, and Quebec may assess a parallel provincial penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the pricing without genuine reliance on professional advice may be held civilly liable for the resulting corporate penalty and interest; the CRA may assert transferee liability under section 160 where assets were transferred to a director at less than fair market value.
Shell Company and General Anti-Avoidance Rule Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denies the tax benefit and re-characterizes the transaction under section 245; false statements in the T5013 attract a 50% gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2); failure to report foreign-related transactions or treaty-based positions may trigger additional penalties of CAD 2,500 plus CAD 25 per day for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sole corporate general partner may be assessed personally as transferees, as trustees for unpaid partnership tax liabilities, or as participants in a fraudulent disposition; Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil in cases of sham, agency, or over-controlling management by a corporate partner.
Customs and AEO Supply-Chain Security Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Customs Act section 109.1 provides monetary penalties of CAD 10,000 to CAD 100,000 for making false statements to the Canada Border Services Agency; AEO authorization may be suspended or revoked; Criminal Code section 380, fraud over CAD 5,000, is punishable by up to 14 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally direct or approve the false certification may be charged as parties to the offence under the Criminal Code; the CBSA may issue administrative penalties directly against a professional adviser, and professional-liability claims from clients may strip corporate veil where the director provided personal guarantees or exercised day-to-day control.
