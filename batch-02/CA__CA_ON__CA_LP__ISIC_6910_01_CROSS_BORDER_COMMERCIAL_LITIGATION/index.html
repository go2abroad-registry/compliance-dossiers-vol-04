Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance — ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance; Ontario Business Registry — beta.ontariobusinessregistrations.ca (Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration and CRA Business Number/GST-HST program account via Business Registration Online (BRO))

E-government filing portal

A non-resident general or limited partner establishing or registering an Ontario Limited Partnership must (1) register the LP in Ontario through the Ontario Business Registry if it has an Ontario address/office or carries on business in Ontario (extra-provincial registration where formed outside Ontario); (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST (RT) program account, by filing Form RC1 (or RC1A, the Non-Resident Request, where no Canadian presence) with the CRA; and (3) for non-resident partners, file Form NR7 'T1 Application for a Waiver of the Requirement to File a Canadian Income Tax Return' or complete the partnership information return (T5013) processes as applicable. Registration may be completed online through CRA's Business Registration Online service and the Ontario Business Registry; the GST/HST small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; a Canadian limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (income/loss allocated to partners). Relevant elections and administrative classifications include: (a) CRA's flow-through (fiscally transparent) classification of foreign entities (including US LLCs) for Canadian tax purposes based on the entity's legal characteristics under its governing law; (b) election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for non-resident partner rental income; (c) GST/HST 'qualifying partnership' election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act (Form GST25) to treat an electing partnership as a single registrant; (d) hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act (applicable to cross-border payments subject to Canadian EIFEL/deduction disallowance where a hybrid mismatch arises); and (e) taxpayer relief and voluntary disclosure regimes for late GST/HST elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; no registration threshold for non-resident carrying on business in Canada that supplies legal services through a permanent establishment; mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (a) goods exported — a sale of tangible goods is zero-rated where the supplier delivers the goods outside Canada (s. 1) or exports them under a prescribed export program; (b) offshore services — a supply of a service to a non-resident recipient (other than a non-resident registered for GST/HST or a non-resident acquiring the service for use in Canada) is zero-rated under s. 7, provided the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not a service rendered to a non-resident who is a registrant. Legal services (ISIC 6910 / NAICS 541110) rendered to a foreign client in connection with cross-border commercial arbitration, asset tracing, or litigation conducted outside Canada will qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is a non-resident not registered, the service is not connected to Canadian situs property, and documentary evidence demonstrates the foreign nexus; services connected to Canadian real property or Canadian tangible assets remain taxable at 13% HST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration / CBSA Export Manifest (CERS filing for goods valued over CAD 2,000) where goods are exported as part of the engagement
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / proof of physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT, wire, or bank payment confirmation from the foreign client demonstrating receipt of consideration in Canada
  • Executed engagement letter and invoice evidencing that the recipient is a non-resident and that the service is performed outside Canada
  • T5013 partnership information return schedules or partner allocation records supporting the export revenue allocation

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing/clearance regime as of the knowledge cutoff date; the federal government published the 2023 'Roadmap for Electronic Invoicing in Canada' proposing future Pan-Canadian Trust Framework interoperability (Peppol BIS 3.0) on a phased voluntary basis. For GST/HST purposes, invoices must satisfy the prescribed information in the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations (e.g., supplier BN, date, registrant name, description, tax amount); e-invoices are permissible if the format is readable/legible, but no CRA API clearance or cryptographic stamp is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose currency-conversion quotas, mandatory repatriation, capital controls, or approval requirements for non-resident ownership of an Ontario limited partnership. Profit, income, and capital remittances may be made subject to available cash, partnership documentation, lender or client-fund restrictions, Canadian tax withholding, transfer-pricing requirements, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering review, and accurate characterization of the payment. Client or litigation-proceeds funds held in trust are not distributable partnership liquidity and must remain segregated until legally payable. A legal-services entity must also comply with applicable Law Society of Ontario trust-account and professional-conduct requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No Canadian statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation applies. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be sold for Canadian dollars or deposited with a Canadian bank. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances offshore or in Canadian foreign-currency accounts, subject to tax reporting, sanctions, proceeds-of-crime controls, contractual restrictions, and accurate customs and revenue records. Cross-border monetary instruments and currency or monetary-instrument transfers of CAD 10,000 or more into or out of Canada may trigger reporting by the carrier or financial intermediary under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act framework; this is not an export-conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange control administration or mandatory central-bank approval regime for ordinary commercial receipts, payments, capital contributions, dividends, partnership distributions, or loan flows. The Canada Border Services Agency, Canada Revenue Agency, Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and Global Affairs Canada may have separate reporting, tax, sanctions, or financial-sector mandates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary operating collections and payments; Low for regulated legal trust funds, large client settlements, escrow-like balances, and complex multi-jurisdictional flows. Canada does not use a single European-style electronic-money-institution passport. An offshore digital EMI cannot automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, payment, trust-account, or banking services. The provider must rely on a properly authorized Canadian financial institution or otherwise comply with applicable Canadian payment, deposit-taking, money-services-business, consumer, sanctions, privacy, and anti-money-laundering requirements. A foreign payment service provider may be required to register with FINTRAC as a foreign money services business or satisfy other applicable registration and agent requirements. The LP should not place client trust funds with an unapproved offshore EMI and should confirm deposit insurance, legal ownership of funds, segregation, insolvency protection, Canadian dollar settlement capability, and Law Society of Ontario compatibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions and shareholder or related-party funding through a Canadian bank account held in the LP's legal name, using a transparent payment narrative such as 'capital contribution under limited partnership agreement' or 'partner loan under agreement dated [date]'. Canada has no general central-bank capital-registration or mandatory foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for these receipts, but the bank will require originator, beneficiary, ultimate-beneficial-owner, source-of-funds, tax-residency, and economic-purpose verification. Maintain the executed partnership or loan agreement, partner resolution, bank statements, tax analysis, foreign-exchange conversion record, and accounting entry. Use SWIFT cross-border wires with complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, and purpose information; avoid third-party funding unless documented and approved. Do not mix capital contributions with client trust funds or settlement proceeds. For non-resident partners, assess Canadian withholding, partnership allocation, taxable Canadian property, interest, and permanent-establishment consequences before remittance or distribution.For ordinary operating payments, use Canadian EFT, wire transfer, or SWIFT cross-border payment rails with a valid invoice, contract, beneficiary verification, sanctions screening, and accounting classification. SWIFT MT103 is the traditional customer-credit-transfer format; banks are migrating to ISO 20022 payment messages, so the bank's current message standard controls. Commercial letters of credit are legally available through Canadian banks but are generally unnecessary for a litigation or arbitration practice unless the entity purchases goods or services under documentary trade terms. Client settlements, court-ordered payments, counsel fees, expert fees, and recoveries must be processed through the appropriate operating or Law Society-compliant trust account, with matter-level authorization, ledger support, reconciliation, and beneficiary verification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% aggregate (Federal 15% general corporate rate + Ontario 11.5% general corporate rate); directly applicable to corporate partners; the Ontario LP itself is fiscally transparent and not subject to corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividend payments made by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaty rates are generally 15% or 5%. Partnership profit distributions are not dividends; for the LP, allocations are subject to Part I tax at the partner level, not Part XIII dividend withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — the aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with each non-resident related party in a taxation year that triggers Form T106 filing; contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation must be prepared for transactions above this threshold. Country-by-Country Reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE/ESR-style economic substance regulations. However, the CRA applies the OECD Permanent Establishment threshold, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), and s.247 transfer-pricing recharacterization to deny benefits where the Ontario LP lacks real decision-making, active case management, staffing, and treasury functions in Canada. A separate and distinct commercial mandate for litigation revenue allocations is required.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under s.239 Income Tax Act, criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded (minimum CAD 10,000) and imprisonment up to five years; civil s.163(2) penalty equals 50% of the deliberate omission, plus daily compounded interest; Ontario Corporations Tax Act penalties can also be imposed.

Director personal liability: General partners/managing partners are personally and severally liable where they knowingly participated in the omission; the CRA can issue s.160 assessment against assets transferred personally; a criminal conviction can include restitution, travel ban, and seizure of personal real estate; limited partners lose the statutory limited-liability shield if they engage in management or deliberate concealment.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA applies s.247(2) to recharacterize revenue or deny deductions; GAAR under s.245 can eliminate a tax benefit; transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) applies when overstatement exceeds the greater of CAD 50,000 or 10% of gross revenue; interest on deficiencies compounds daily.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners can be held jointly and severally liable under s.160 for the tax benefit shifted to related parties; personal guarantee and trust account garnishment remedies are available; tax evasion charges arise if the shell arrangement is found to be a sham; travel ban and disbarment are possible professional consequences.
Money Laundering / Trust Account Misuse

Statutory sanction: Federal PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 2,000,000 for a reporting entity; Criminal Code s.462.331 provides imprisonment up to 10 years; Ontario Law Society rules require prompt reporting and disgorgement of dishonoured trust funds; a lawyer's licence may be revoked.

Director personal liability: A general partner or law firm managing partner who knowingly directs or fails to correct laundering through trust accounts is personally criminally liable; courts can issue restraint orders, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets; limited partners may be liable if they induced the transaction knowing it embodied criminal proceeds.
