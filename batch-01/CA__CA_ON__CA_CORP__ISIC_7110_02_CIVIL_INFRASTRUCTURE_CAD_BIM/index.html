Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); principal online portal is My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST returns and registrations filed via GST/HST NETFILE / REGMOR.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation automatically obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) at federal or provincial incorporation through the online incorporation linkage; otherwise, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) is filed with CRA. The 9-digit BN serves as the corporate tax identifier, with Program Accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (import/export). Ontario entities incorporated through the Ontario Business Registry receive the BN through CRA's auto-registration linkage. For foreign-entity branches, CRA requires a Form RC1 application, a certificate of existence or equivalent, and appointment of a representative for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate tax resident liable at the entity level for federal Part I tax (base rate 38% reduced to 15% as the general rate; 9% effective with the small business deduction on first CAD 500,000 if a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Ontario corporate income tax (general 11.5%; 10% for manufacturing and processing profits narrowly applied; 3.2% for small business CCDA). Non-resident shareholders bear Part XIII withholding on dividends; the corporation remains the primary taxpayer. — A Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation automatically obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) at federal or provincial incorporation through the online incorporation linkage; otherwise, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) is filed with CRA. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate tax resident liable at the entity level for federal Part I tax (base rate 38% reduced to 15% as the general rate; 9% effective with the small business deduction on first CAD 500,000 if a Canadian-controlled private corporation) plus Ontario corporate income tax (general 11.5%; 10% for manufacturing and processing profits narrowly applied; 3.2% for small business CCDA). Non-resident shareholders bear Part XIII withholding on dividends; the corporation remains the primary taxpayer.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box entity classification regime. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST section 156 election to exclude supplies between closely related corporations; (ii) quick method of accounting election for GST/HST (CRA Form GST74); (iii) federal and provincial section 85 income tax rollover elections for property transfers into a corporation; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections and determinations under ITA sections 94.3-94.7 to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; (v) non-resident corporation election under ITA section 216 for rental income; (vi) foreign accrual property income (FAPI) groupings for controlled foreign affiliates under ITA s. 91-95.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - mandatory registration for a person making taxable supplies in Canada at or above CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); non-resident suppliers providing services relating to Canadian real property or goods are generally deemed to make supplies in Canada and must register even below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI Part V (services) and Part VI (intangible personal property) for supplies made to a non-resident. Offshore Building Information Modeling (BIM) and structural drafting qualify as zero-rated exports when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident individual or business not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the service is not performed in whole or in part in Canada; (iii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada; and (iv) the recipient is outside Canada at the time the service is performed. If drafting/BIM deliverables relate to real property situated in Canada (e.g., Ontario infrastructure stamping or permit drawings), the supply is subject to HST at 13% notwithstanding the non-resident recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration / CAED or Cargo Control Document) - mandatory for physical media or export of goods components; for pure digital BIM/CAD data services, a written statement of no goods export is substituted
  • Bill of Lading (or contractual delivery receipt / electronic acceptance certificate) proving shipment or offshore delivery of deliverables
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation, Wise/third-party processor transaction statement, or credit advice) evidencing foreign currency inward remittance for the exported services

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-driven e-invoicing adjudication mandate. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (unless a written waiver is granted). PEPPOL-based B2B e-invoicing is available and promoted by the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework but remains voluntary; CRA accepts electronic invoices and documents as valid books and records, which must be retained in readable format for six years from the end of the taxation year.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims retroactively for up to six years (ten years for misrepresentation) and claw back uncollected HST plus arrears interest when non-resident status, foreign performance, or receipt of foreign consideration is not contemporaneously documented. Civil penalties include ETA s. 285 gross negligence (25% of the tax), ETA s. 280 false statements (up to CAD 25,000), and ITA s. 163.2 third-party penalties. Knowingly creating a sham export transaction constitutes tax evasion punishable under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 by criminal fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years, with corporate directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted HST under ETA s. 323.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There is no central-bank exchange-control approval for profit remittance, dividend payment, interest repatriation, or capital injection. Repatriation constraints arise only from corporate solvency rules, tax withholding at source, FINTRAC reporting thresholds, and correspondent-bank due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion into CAD, and no stipulated timeline for repatriation of foreign-currency export receipts. Exporters of BIM/CAD services may retain USD/EUR/GBP in permitted bank accounts; cross-border service receipts are reported only for balance-of-payments statistical purposes via Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF reporting; no separate Canadian foreign-exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as an ancillary collection and low-value disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury backbone. Wise Canada and Payoneer act as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs), not Schedule I chartered banks under the Bank Act. They cannot provide a true demand-deposit bank account with CDIC deposit insurance nor direct Payments Canada membership. They offer borderless multi-currency accounts, virtual account details, and low-cost SWIFT/local-market transfers. Legal constraint: the EMI must be registered as an MSB with FINTRAC before servicing Canadian entities; foreign-owned EMIs should not be used to hold substantial intercompany treasury balances because funds may be pooled or held under trust arrangements rather than as a Canadian bank deposit.
