Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC). Official portal: estv.admin.ch (also eService and Swiss UID portal uid.admin.ch)

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft obtains its federal company number (UID CHE-Nr.) through the commercial register procedure upon incorporation; this UID automatically serves as the VAT registration number for Swiss VAT purposes when required. Foreign entities (non-resident in Switzerland or Liechtenstein) with VAT-liable activities in Switzerland must appoint a Swiss VAT representative (domiciled in CH) and file a registration form each. Non-resident businesses without a Swiss permanent establishment are not subject to Swiss corporate income tax but must register for Swiss VAT if they generate taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeding the statutory threshold. Foreign agencies supplying digital services to Swiss private customers from abroad must register for Swiss VAT regardless of turnover, applying the simplified procedure for electronic services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss AG is a corporate taxpayer; federal and cantonal/communal profit taxes apply at entity level; no pass-through)

Available election forms

No check-the-box (Genossenschaftswahl) regime exists in Swiss tax law. Available elections: (1) Swiss VAT group election (Art. 15 VAT Act) granted to closely linked entities, (2) Swiss tax group election for federal and cantonal profit taxes under Art. 61 f. of the Federal Tax Harmonisation Act, allowing offsetting of results among Swiss group companies, (3) participation exemption is automatic for qualifying dividends/capital gains, with no formal election required. Hybrid mismatch elections under OECD BEPS are generally no longer available; Switzerland complies with ATAD principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (Art. 10 VAT Act); non-resident suppliers of digital services, telecommunication and broadcasting services to Swiss private consumers must register regardless of threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating for goods is available under Art. 23 VAT Act for direct exports of tangible goods; the exporter must hold proof of transport out of Switzerland. For digital services (electronically supplied games, download codes, software), the usual place of supply rule (Art. 7 VAT Act) places B2B and B2C supplies to customers outside Switzerland outside Swiss VAT, so effectively no Swiss VAT is chargeable; this is not an export zero-rating mechanism. For B2C supplies to Swiss consumers, the standard rate applies. Offshore services listed in Art. 23(3) VAT Act (e.g. services relating to foreign real estate) may be zero-rated if the recipient is abroad and the service is used abroad.

  • Customs export declaration (E-dec or NCTS document)
  • Transport document evidencing physical export (CMR waybill, bill of lading, air waybill or courier receipt)
  • Commercial invoice and sale/purchase contract
  • Evidence of payment (bank statement, inward remittance certificate where applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping regime as of 2024. E-invoicing is permitted if the recipient consents and the invoice is archived in an electronic form compliant with Art. 63 VAT Act (punctual and complete retention). The ESTV does not require API-based clearance for VAT documents. Swiss taxpayers are obliged to retain electronic records for 10 years and must provide them to authorities upon request.

Claiming export zero-rating without valid proof of export triggers VAT reassessment, default interest (5% per annum) and potential fine for tax evasion (up to 3× the tax evaded) or VAT fraud (up to 4× the tax evaded) under Art. 96–99 VAT Act. In addition, wilful violation of record-keeping duties is punishable with fines up to CHF 10,000; for a stock corporation the law targets the governing body representatives, and civil clawback options apply in case of deliberate misstatement. Swiss anti-fraud procedure permits retrospective reclassification of a supply, removal of the zero-rating benefit, and assessment of unpaid VAT for up to 10 years in tax evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility regime. CHF is freely convertible on both the current and capital account; there is no exchange control law and no foreign exchange administration permit is required for cross-border payments, profit remittance or dividend distribution. The only friction is the 35% anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends distributed by a Swiss AG, which is fully or partially refundable under an applicable tax treaty or, for Swiss resident shareholders, via refund. There are no restrictions on holding foreign currency balances and no obligation to convert export revenues into CHF.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules. Switzerland guarantees free movement of capital under Article 27 para. 2 of the Federal Constitution. Export proceeds from digital publishing (Steam/PlayStation payouts) may be retained in USD/EUR, held onshore or offshore, and are not subject to any statutory repatriation timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the SNB apply, without any conversion or confiscation mandate.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB - Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse, BNS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as auxiliary rails, Low as primary banking. Foreign EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, other non-Swiss licensed payment institutions) may be used as secondary treasury corridors, but they cannot replace a licensed Swiss bank for statutory capital deposits, payroll tax, VAT settlement, or a corporate account with full banking substance. A Swiss FINMA-regulated fintech/payment institution may hold client funds up to CHF 100 million under the fintech licence regime, but the CH_AG blueprint should anchor all platform payouts in a fully licensed Swiss banking relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (assessed on after-tax profit, equivalent to approximately 7.83% of pre-tax profit), plus Zug cantonal and communal corporate income tax of approximately 4.0% to 4.5% of pre-tax profit, producing a combined effective total corporate income tax rate in canton Zug of approximately 11.8% to 12.2%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) must be withheld by the Swiss AG on all dividend distributions regardless of the shareholder's residence (Art. 4 VStG). The gross 35% rate applies at source; the shareholder can recover the excess through treaty relief and refund procedures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The statutory deadline is six months after the close of the commercial financial year; the Zug tax authority routinely grants extension upon request, making the practical due date approximately eight months after year-end (around 30 September for a 31 December balance sheet date). The AG must also settle quarterly corporate tax prepayments during the assessment year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a Identification of the tax period (Steuerperiode) and the full financial year covered; the audit year normally equals the calendar year unless the AG has a non-calendar commercial year-end. Non-resident shareholders have no filing obligation in Switzerland. The Swiss AG files in its own name and must enter the same fiscal year-end used in the statutory commercial accounts. Late filing attracts a procedural administrative fine (Ordnungsbusse) of up to CHF 10,000 under Art. 194 DBG; additionally, the AG owes late-payment interest at 3% p.a. on federal tax and approximately 5% p.a. on Zug cantonal tax. Understatement of taxable profit triggers an evasion surcharge of up to three times the federal tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG) and a parallel cantonal surcharge, while qualified tax fraud (Steuerbetrug) is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of up to three years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Master File and Local File compliant with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Chapter V, as endorsed by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's circular of 2020/2021) are mandatory where annual consolidated group turnover exceeds CHF 50 million or where volume of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds CHF 10 million per category; below those thresholds, a proportionate contemporary TP record must still prove the arm's length nature of royalties, platform fees and profit allocations.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland is not covered by the Offshore Economic Substance Regulations applicable to the ESR-listed jurisdictions. However, Swiss law and treaty practice impose substance requirements that the AG must satisfy: genuine management and board activity, operating staff, premises and records in Switzerland. For digital Steam/console publishers claiming the Zug licence box or treaty benefits, the substance doctrine requires locally performed R&D, decision-making and commercial control. In addition, the OECD Pillar Two GloBE rules apply to multinational groups exceeding EUR 750 million consolidated turnover, and the Swiss qualified domestic minimum top-up tax came into force on 1 January 2025, effectively anchoring a 15% minimum effective tax rate for in-scope groups.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and tax fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Federal surcharge of up to three times the federal tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG); Zug cantonal surcharge of up to three times the evaded cantonal and communal tax; recovery of the evaded tax plus statutory interest; qualified evasion through false or forged documents constitutes Steuerbetrug punished under Art. 186 DBG by a monetary fine or imprisonment of up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare, approve or sign a false return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded taxes and surcharges; personal liability is asserted directly against board members, Swiss bank accounts may be attached, travel bans can be issued under Art. 91 SchKG, and criminal conviction may lead to disqualification from holding board office.
Shell company and abuse of Domicile status

Statutory sanction: Denial of Zug licence box relief, denial of treaty benefits, retroactive full taxation of privileged profit at the combined Zug effective rate, reassessment of hidden profit distributions, and potential administrative liquidation under Art. 731b OR where the AG lacks a lawful organisation or effective seat; capital tax and interest surcharges follow the reassessment.

Director personal liability: Board members who knowingly maintained a sham substance structure are personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies and damages; enforcement may proceed against personal assets, the directors may face prosecution for attempted evasion, and exit from Switzerland may be formally blocked.
Transfer pricing mismatching and incomplete documentation

Statutory sanction: Upward profit adjustments by ESTV and Zug with qualified interest at 3%–5% p.a.; denial of royalty expense documentation; in aggravated cases of gross negligence an administrative penalty surcharge of up to 30% of the reallocated income; systematic non-compliance may trigger a formal transfer pricing audit with retroactive reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed declarations based on undocumented or fictitious transfer prices bear personal financial liability for the unpaid differential tax and interest; where fabricated comparability studies are produced, directors expose themselves to criminal sanctions for false records before Swiss tax and criminal authorities.
Anticipatory tax and dividend mis-declaration (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unremitted 35% anticipatory tax together with a penalty of up to the full amount of the withheld tax (Art. 175–177 VStG); interest accruals; intentional failure is criminally punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to one year; refund claims are irrevocably barred where the statutory three-year filing period is missed.

Director personal liability: Board members and responsible tax officers are strictly and severally liable for unremitted anticipatory tax; collection proceedings extend directly to their personal Swiss bank accounts and real estate, travel bans may be imposed on the managing directors, and in aggravated crypto/treasury diversion cases, prosecution follows under Art. 14 VStrR and the Swiss Criminal Code.
