Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC); cantonal tax authorities; portal: www.estv.admin.ch / ePortal VAT services

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft must first notarize the articles of association and register with the cantonal commercial registry, thereby obtaining the permanent Swiss company UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Numéro d'identification des entreprises). The UID is issued by the Federal Statistical Office through the cantonal register. VAT registration with the ESTV must be made separately via the ePortal/e-REG form once taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 or voluntarily if thresholds are not met. The UID is used as the VAT identification number and must be listed on invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no general check-the-box classification. An AG is per se a taxable legal entity subject to federal, cantonal, and communal income tax. Certain hybrid mismatch elections are available under Swiss implementation of BEPS Action 2 for foreign hybrid instruments or entities, and qualified Swiss holding or finance companies may opt for deemed substance tax transparency in specific intra-group financing structures. No US-style pass-through election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding real estate costs; Art. 10 para. 2 lit. a MWSTG); voluntary registration permitted for businesses below threshold; special lower thresholds apply for certain marginal activities

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods transported from Switzerland to foreign jurisdictions where legal ownership passes to the recipient abroad, including cross-border commodity trading in raw milk powder, provided the supplier retains export proof. Services relating to movable goods destined for foreign use may also be zero-rated. The supply must be demonstrably delivered outside Switzerland with the Swiss customs export declaration confirming exit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss e-dec / NCTS declaration with accepted export summary)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Transport Document (Bill of Lading for sea freight, CMR for road, or Air Waybill for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire evidence showing repatriation of proceeds from foreign buyer
  • Sales contract or order confirmation stating Incoterms and delivery destination
  • Export delivery certificate (Lieferschein) and, where applicable, customs transit accompanying document T1/T2

Electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandatory. There is no domestic real-time fiscal clearance API or digital stamping mandate. All invoices must contain the UID and VAT rate; QR-billing standards apply for payment references. Export VAT substantiation requires electronic customs declarations using Swiss Customs e-dec/NCTS; attested export documents must be retained for audit.

Under Swiss VAT law, export zero-rating is strictly audited against the full evidential chain. Missing, false, or contradictory export documentation triggers retroactive VAT assessment at the applicable rate (8.1% standard or 2.6% reduced for foodstuffs) with statutory interest. Fraudulent or deliberate misdeclaration may lead to penalties up to 30% of the assessed tax, plus further tax evasion fines and criminal penalties under Art. 186-187 MWSTG. The statute of limitation for clawback is generally 10 years from the end of the year in which the supply was made.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) on both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls, no licensing of currency transactions, and no central bank approval is required for foreign investment, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or royalty payments. Profits may be remitted freely, subject only to federal withholding tax on dividends (35% statutory rate, typically reduced to 0% for qualifying domestic/inbound holdings or to treaty-lowered rates for treaty residents through an actuality match) and standard corporate taxation. The Capital Transactions Act explicitly guarantees the free movement of capital, rendering any repatriation-limiting restrictions inapplicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Switzerland. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency in Swiss or foreign bank accounts indefinitely. The SNB collects statistical data on cross-border payments for balance-of-payments purposes but imposes no currency conversion mandate. For dairy commodity traders, export invoices in EUR, USD, or other freely convertible currencies can be retained offshore or converted into CHF at market rates at the discretion of the treasury.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, Bundesplatz 1, CH-3003 Bern, operating under the Currency and Payment Act of 22 December 1999 and the Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital (Capital Transactions Act, revised 1995). SNB overseers the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system and publishes FX statistics.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory corporate income tax: 8.5% on post-tax profit (effective 7.83% on pre-tax profit); cantonal/communal rates add approximately 11.9%-21.0% combined effective burden. In Canton Zug, the strategic seat of the entity, the effective combined rate is approximately 11.9%-14.6%, one of the lowest in Switzerland.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss Verrechnungssteuer on gross dividends paid or credited by the Swiss AG. Reduced DTA rates (commonly 0%-15%, with 5%-15% for qualified shareholdings, subject to each treaty) apply only through FTA refund or prior FTA approval for reduced withholding at source.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax period equals the financial year. The return must be filed within six months after financial year-end; for a calendar-year AG, the standard deadline is 30 June of the following year. Extensions may be granted only on timely written application, generally up to 31 December. Quarterly provisional instalments are payable in advance.

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Reporting mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million. Full OECD Master File and Local File documentation is expected by the FTA when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 5 million; reduced documentation may suffice below this threshold. All TP files must be submitted on formal request within 30-60 days.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate an industry-based Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to UAE or Caribbean regimes. However, substantive economic substance is a hard requirement derived from Art. 58 and Art. 65 DBG, the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, the Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test and Swiss anti-abuse case law: the Swiss AG must demonstrate actual local management, physical premises, qualified staff, independent decision-making, real intercompany contracts, and genuine commercial risk absorption. A letterbox company that merely routes milk powder trade flows or holds invoices without operational functions will be denied treaty benefits, participation relief and possibly reclassified as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax evasion penalty of up to three times the amount of tax evaded (DBG Art. 175), plus default interest of 4.75% p.a. Tax fraud through forged records (DBG Art. 186) entails criminal fines and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to five years, confiscation of illicit profits and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under Art. 754 OR for corporate losses caused by wilful or negligent mismanagement, including unpaid corporate taxes. Where the company is insolvent, tax authorities recover the unpaid tax directly from the responsible directors. Criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years, travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed during proceedings.
Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The FTA recovers the full 35% WHT from the Swiss AG plus interest at 4.75%, irrespective of the beneficiary's treaty position. Criminal prosecutions may impose fines of up to CHF 30,000 on the company and its responsible executives. The company is barred from effective treaty relief for all shareholders during the default period.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly distribute dividends without WHT in liquidation or asset-deficiency scenarios are personally liable for the withheld amounts under Swiss withholding tax responsibility rules. Directors face criminal charges for participation in the infringement and personal travel bans pending final payment.
Shell Company Structure and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization and upward adjustment of Swiss taxable profit by the FTA; denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test; cancellation of participation relief and refund of dividend WHT; back taxes plus interest; potential requalification of the Swiss AG as a transparent conduit resulting in immediate taxation at the level of the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to operate as a letterbox may face civil restitution for the benefit obtained by the foreign shareholders, personal liability for uncollectable taxes, criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion of hidden principals, imprisonment up to three years for accessory acts, and confiscation of their personal assets.
AML / KYC / UBO Transparency Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines under Anti-Money Laundering Act Art. 21-23 of up to CHF 500,000 per violation; disciplinary measures by self-regulatory organizations; confiscation of proceeds under Art. 70 Swiss Criminal Code; exclusion from Swiss banking relationships and cancellation of the tax clearance certificate for commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to imprisonment of up to three years for money laundering (Art. 305bis SCC) or five years for participation in a criminal organization (Art. 260ter SCC); personal travel bans, asset freezes, and joint and several civil liability for repatriation of diverted funds. Directors cannot rely on corporate indemnity where their personal criminal conduct is proven.
