Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — GST/HST registration, returns, and compliance via the My Business Account (MyBA) portal and GST/HST NETFILE; customs and export filing interfaces administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal and Canada Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign (non-resident) entity obtains its Canadian statutory Business Number (BN), which serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration number, by filing Form RC1-F (Request for a Business Number - Non-Resident) with the CRA, or Form RC1 where a Canadian resident agent is appointed. Registration is available online via CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, fax, or telephone; the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) is established simultaneously with the BN. The CRA conducts identity validation, assigns the BN within 5 to 10 business days, and issues a written confirmation letter; the non-resident corporation must disclose its registered foreign address, Mandarin/official corporate name, place of incorporation, and any Canadian agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal or provincial Business Corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act is a separate taxable legal person resident in Canada and is taxed at Part I corporate rates on worldwide income. No pass-through or US-style check-the-box regime exists; the corporation files its own T2 corporate income tax return and cannot flow losses or income through to shareholders.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch classification system; entity classification follows legal form, domestic residence principles, and applicable tax treaties. Statutory elections available to a private business corporation include: (i) GST/HST group registration election under Excise Tax Act s. 273, consolidating closely related Canadian entities into a single registrant; (ii) the closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156, permitting zero-rated supplies among qualifying related corporations; (iii) the Quick Method of accounting election under ETA s. 281.1 for small suppliers; (iv) the non-resident rental income election under Income Tax Act s. 216; and (v) the deemed-resident election for non-residents disposing of taxable Canadian property under ITA s. 216.1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies — the Small Supplier Threshold under ETA s. 148. Registration becomes mandatory once annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000. Non-resident suppliers making 'specified supplies' (digital goods, intangibles, and services) to consumers in Canada are also required to register and collect GST/HST once the same CAD 30,000 worldwide threshold is exceeded, regardless of physical presence in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier is a GST/HST registrant and exports the goods directly or through a common carrier as part of the sale, with title and risk passing to a foreign purchaser and physical export occurring within a reasonable time after supply. Services and intangible personal property supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 6 and 7, subject to exceptions for services performed on tangible property in Canada or services relating to Canadian real estate. For online retail of desktop 3D printers and resin filaments, export sales qualify for the 0% HST rate when the goods are shipped from Canadian inventory to a foreign consignee, the shipment terms are FOB/EXW or equivalent with foreign destination, and contemporaneous shipping, customs, and payment evidence is retained by the vendor.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A6) with CERS electronic filing reference for shipments of CAD 2,000 or more
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee, Canadian exporter, and foreign destination port/airport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or payment-processor bank credit evidence matching the invoice value, currency, and settlement date
  • Commercial invoice with terms of sale (EXW/FOB/DAP), HST zero-rating notation, and description of goods (HS code 8516/8473 category for 3D printers and resin consumables)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government-authored e-invoicing regime for B2C e-commerce. The CRA accepts electronic records in standard formats (PDF, XML, Peppol/UBL) and has adopted the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol standards on a voluntary basis since 2023. GST/HST returns must be transmitted electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or approved API-based accounting software. For exports, the CBSA requires electronic filing of the Canada Export Declaration (Form A6) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more; all customs accounts and export/import release documents are processed via the CARM portal and Canada Single Window.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims; failure to produce statutory export documentation within the required retention window results in reassessment of the full 13% HST on the invoice value, plus interest computed under ETA s. 280. Civil false-statement penalties under ETA s. 285.1 add 25% of the tax understated, and gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163.2 may apply to any associated income-tax adjustment. Intentional evasion, fraudulent zero-rating, or refund misrepresentation is prosecuted under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239, attracting fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment of up to five years, retrospective clawback of input tax credits, and suspension of the corporate GST/HST registration with director/personal liability under the ETA joint and several liability rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada has not maintained statutory foreign exchange controls since 1951. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payments, and capital repatriation are permitted without central-bank approval, subject only to normal tax compliance, including Part XIII withholding taxes on payments to non-residents (typically 25%, reduced by applicable tax treaties). FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to large cash transactions and international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but these are informational requirements and do not constitute convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency into Canadian dollars. Export-denominated receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks or maintained offshore. FINTRAC reporting applies to cross-border wire transfers and large cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, and businesses must keep records of such proceeds for five years as part of their AML compliance obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange policy oversight by the Department of Finance Canada; transaction monitoring and AML enforcement are administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Offshore digital EMIs may operate in Canada only if they are registered as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and maintain a Canadian agent or principal. Since 2024, payment service providers must also register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). An offshore EMI without a Canadian MSB registration and RPAA compliance can provide informational dashboards but cannot legally offer Canadian-dollar clearing or hold settlement balances. A practical model is pairing an offshore EMI with a Canadian Schedule I bank trust account or using an RPAA-registered payment service provider; however, full banking secrecy and account aggregator reliability are reduced relative to a domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended routing: a SWIFT MT103 wire from a parent or overseas shareholder to a Canadian-dollar operating account maintained at a Schedule I bank. The beneficiary is the Ontario Business Corporation in its exact legal name, and the remittance information must state the purpose code appropriate to Canada's balance-of-payments classification, e.g., D300 for direct investment equity injections or C110 for intercompany loans, if required by the receiving bank. Standard bank verification steps include a corporate resolution authorising the transaction, a copy of the underlying loan or share subscription agreement, and an inbound remittance response form.
