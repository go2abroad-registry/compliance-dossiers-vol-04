Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Ontario is mandatory under OBCA s. 14(1), and the articles must state the municipality in Ontario where the registered office is situated; the address must be a physical, serviceable street address, and a P.O. box is prohibited. Virtual and co-working office arrangements are acceptable for this B2B marketplace industry only where the provider leases a physical suite in Ontario at which legal process can be served and OBR correspondence can be handled during business hours; a pure mail-forwarding virtual address without human service-of-process capacity is not acceptable. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full de jure and de facto convertibility for both capital and current account transactions; no exchange controls, central-bank approval, quota, or surrender requirements restrict cross-border payments, dividends, interest, royalties, or repatriation of capital by non-resident shareholders. The principal profit-remittance hurdle is tax: dividends and other passive payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), generally 25% statutorily, reducible to 5-15% under applicable income tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US Treaty: 5% dividends with sufficient shareholding; 10% general dividends; 10-15% interest and royalties). Wage and service payments may trigger payroll or GST/HST compliance obligations, and banks may impose documentary scrutiny under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for outgoing wires above CAD 3,000 or where enhanced due diligence (EDD) triggers apply. No FX quota, import-licence, or convertibility approval is required for profit remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines in Canada. Exporters and B2B platform operators may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in onshore foreign currency accounts or offshore accounts, without obligation to convert into CAD or to remit within any prescribed period. Regulatory reporting requirements are limited to: (i) export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) under the Customs Act for controlled or Export Control List (ECL) goods; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer (EFT) reports for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) general record-keeping under the Income Tax Act, including transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany transactions under Section 247. B2B marketplace receipts from digital sales of non-controlled goods are not subject to any foreign-exchange surrender or capital control filing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; foreign exchange administration and financial-crime supervision are distributed among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Department of Finance Canada, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control statute; the former Exchange Control regime was abolished in 1951 and Canada has since adhered to IMF Article VIII obligations for currency convertibility in current international transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, dLocal) are not licensed as Canadian banks under the Bank Act and do not enjoy CDIC deposit insurance. They may lawfully operate in Canada only as Money Services Businesses registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA) and, where active in Ontario, registered under the Ontario Money Services Business Act, 2020. Feasibility rating: Medium. EMIs are viable ancillary payment and disbursement rails for small-value cross-border B2B merchant settlements, but they must not serve as the primary treasury, capital escrow, or liquidity layer. Canadian structural sub-accounts held with such EMIs should be segregated and fully disclosed to the principal relationship bank to avoid negative-pooling, opaque omnibus-account, and AML-related red flags. For enterprise-grade treasury, a Schedule I chartered bank account remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital should be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with precise beneficiary legal name, Ontario registered office, and a purpose reference such as 'Equity subscription - ISIC 4791 B2B marketplace' or 'Shareholder loan - working capital'. Canada does not impose a centralized purpose-code filter, but banks apply transaction reporting under FINTRAC for electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000 and may request the corporate resolution, share subscription agreement, and source-of-funds documentation for amounts above CDD/EDD thresholds. CAD settlement is cleared through Payments Canada Lynx (real-time gross settlement successor to LVTS); same-day value should be confirmed by a bank closing statement for escrow release and audit trail. For US-dollar capital, use SWIFT USD clearing via the Canadian bank's US correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire) with a sanctioned-wire-format remittance reference. All inbound injections must be booked against share capital or intercompany loan accounts in the Canadian corporate ledger for tax and securities compliance.Outbound trade settlements to overseas suppliers and merchants should use SWIFT MT103 with attached commercial invoice and contract references; for large-value or irrevocable commitments, use documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Canadian bank, standby LCs, or documentary collections. Domestic CAD disbursements clear through Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) with end-of-day settlement; cross-border FX conversions are executed by the Canadian bank at interbank-addressed prices or through a registered FX dealer with trade tickets retained. For B2B marketplace merchant payouts, platform settlement funds should be held segregated from operating funds (trust or agent omnibus arrangement compliant with Ontario rules) and disbursed by batch electronic funds transfer or SWIFT gpi with unique beneficiary identifiers. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% combined federal and Ontario general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Ontario 10%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to reduction under an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 filing due no later than six months after the taxation year-end. Balance-due day is two months after year-end for a non-CCPC or a CCPC exceeding the small business limit, and three months after year-end for a CCPC within the small business limit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 threshold per non-arm's-length cross-border transaction under ITA section 247(4); mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting (Form RC5091) applies for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA will disregard transactions lacking commercial substance under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245). Ontario corporate residence is established by incorporation and central management and control; therefore the company must maintain substantive management, employees, board minutes, payroll, bank accounts, and business records in Ontario. For a B2B marketplace, the location where contracts are negotiated, pricing decisions made, and platform infrastructure managed must be verifiable to avoid a transfer-pricing recharacterisation and to prevent a no-substance shell finding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misreporting

Statutory sanction: ITA section 163(2) civil penalty equal to 50% of tax understated; ITA section 239 criminal sanctions: on summary conviction a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment a fine of 100%–200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may issue a tax warrant, seize bank accounts, and garnish trade receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause, participate in, or acquiesce to evasion are jointly and severally liable; CRA may assess directors under the corporate tax withholding provisions (section 227.1) and section 160 for transfers of property for less than fair value. Criminal conviction permits asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment of the individuals.
Offshore Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the GAAR (section 245) to deny treaty benefits and recharacterise transactions; the OECD Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test may also remove entitlement to reduced dividend, interest, or royalty rates. The denial can give rise to federal and provincial reassessments, late-payment interest, and a 10% transfer-pricing penalty where an adjustment is made.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute artificial structures without commercial purpose may face joint and several liability for the resulting tax debt if assets are transferred out of the corporation; CRA may impose a travel ban to prevent departure pending investigation, and evidence of fraud leads to criminal prosecution.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure / Non-Arm's-Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: ITA section 247(2) adjusts the transfer price to arm's length; section 247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment, unless the taxpayer made reasonable efforts and maintained contemporaneous documentation. Reassessment periods are suspended until complete documentation is produced, and interest accrues on the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing declarations or who direct diversion of income to related entities are personally liable for civil penalties if they were the controlling mind; corporate veil protection is lost on proof of sham or fraud, and CRA can seek criminal sanctions where there was a wilful attempt to evade tax.
AML and Wire-Transfer Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can exceed CAD 1,000,000 for entities for systematic reporting failures; violation of the Criminal Code money-laundering provision (section 462.31) carries imprisonment up to 10 years and a potential fine at the court's discretion. CRA and FINTRAC may impose import/export restrictions and freezing orders on the operating bank account.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers with operational control may be personally named in enforcement actions, required to pay civil restitution, restricted from travel while an investigation is pending, and sentenced to imprisonment if they knowingly facilitated the placement or layering of criminally sourced funds through the corporate bank account.
