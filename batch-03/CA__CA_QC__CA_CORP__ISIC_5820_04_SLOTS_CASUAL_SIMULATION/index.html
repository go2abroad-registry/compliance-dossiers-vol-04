Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or foreign entity classification election. Available elections include: Section 216(1) election for non-resident rental income, Section 273 joint venture election (optional), and GST/HST quick method election (for eligible small businesses). Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4-18.6 disallow certain hybrid mismatch deductions and require income inclusion adjustments; no golden share election or similar is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in any calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST; non-residents subject to Canadian digital economy rules may have a different threshold, but the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold generally applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of electronic software or services (simulated social casino and virtual slots games) is zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 (services) and section 23 (intangible personal property) if the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, is not carrying on business in Canada, and the supply is not for consumption or use in Canada. Physical goods exported from Canada are zero-rated with proof of export. For digital platform sales through Apple App Store/Google Play, the app publisher must maintain records demonstrating the end-user's non-residency and that settlement occurs outside Canada.

  • App platform sales and royalty export reports (App Store Connect / Google Play Console) with SKU-level transaction details
  • Apple/Google payment settlement and payout remittance statements (gross revenue, commissions, net payment, and currency conversion)
  • Commercial contracts, license agreements, and end-user non-residency evidence (billing country, IP geolocation, app storefront)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary (PEPPOL adoption in Ontario/Quebec pilots). Registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million per year must file GST/HST returns electronically. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are payment rails, not tax clearance mechanisms, and no API-based government fiscal clearance applies.

CRA may reassess outside the normal 4-year period for misrepresentation or fraud (unlimited assessment period). For knowingly or negligently making false statements, gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 apply at 25% of the tax that would have been understated or overclaimed. Improperly claimed export zero-rating is reversed (clawback) and tax is assessed at the applicable GST/HST and QST rates (combined 14.975% in Quebec), plus arrears interest (currently 5% per annum statutory rate) and administrative penalties. CRA increasingly uses platform payment data from Apple/Google to cross-check zero-rated digital exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Canada is a signatory to the OECD Codes of Liberalisation; there are no capital controls, no approval or registration gate for foreign direct investment into a Quebec Business Corporation, and no restriction on profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders. Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees may be remitted freely, subject to federal Part XIII withholding tax (general 25%, treaty-reduced as applicable, e.g., 5/15% dividends under the Canada-US DTA) and provincial withholding on certain payments made to non-residents (Quebec levy analogous to Part XIII). A non-resident-owned Quebec corporation is not subject to branch tax unless it carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada via an unincorporated form. Compliance watchpoints: aggregate inbound transaction value must be screened against the Investment Canada Act (non-reviewable below the 2025 WTO enterprise-value threshold of approximately CAD 1.3 billion for WTO-treaty investors); and all cross-border electronic funds transfers must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for conversion or repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory surrender ratio, and no requirement that export receipts be converted to Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain income in any freely usable currency. The only binding obligations are reporting-related: (i) the depository bank must report cross-border SWIFT EFTs to FINTRAC (all SWIFT-originated cross-border transfers, no de minimis threshold, whereas non-SWIFT transfers above CAD 10,000 are reportable); (ii) physical cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable under the PCMLTFA; and (iii) Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada balance-of-payments surveys may require periodic disclosure of large transactions. The Quebec corporation may therefore hold USD/EUR balances in its operating account or in linked foreign-currency clearing accounts without fiscal penalty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal prudential regulator of deposit-taking institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisor; and the Minister of Finance for residual authority under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada operates a freely floating exchange-rate regime with no discrete foreign-exchange control statute or foreign exchange administration certificate requirement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Canada does not belong to the EU/UK electronic money institution (EMI) passporting regime; an offshore EMI licence confers no authorization to offer deposit-taking or payment services in Canada absent compliance with the Bank Act and the PCMLTFA. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or EMI-as-a-service platforms) may be used only as narrow treasury utilities for vendor disbursements, payroll, and currency conversion, and only after the EMI registers with FINTRAC as a foreign money services business (MSB) where it has a Canadian nexus or solicits Canadian clients. A Quebec Business Corporation may legally maintain an offshore EMI account for operational float, but capital escrow, settlement, and blockchain treasury-grade liquidity must remain in a Canadian Schedule I bank because the EMI channel cannot provide deposit insurance (CDIC), LVTS settlement, or regulated correspondent clearing. Recommendation: use a Canadian-licensed bank as the primary clearing account and a FINTRAC-registered fintech (e.g., Wise for cross-border FX) as a secondary, capped-flow corridor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

For a corporation carrying on business in Quebec through a permanent establishment, the general combined rate is ordinarily 26.5%: 15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may generally access a combined 12.2% rate on eligible active business income within the applicable federal and Quebec business limits: 9% federal plus 3.2% Quebec. Eligibility, business limits, associated-corporation rules, taxable-capital limits, and income-type restrictions apply.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII withholding tax is generally 25% on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident, subject to a tax treaty or other statutory relief. Treaty rates vary by recipient residence, beneficial ownership, and shareholding; many treaties provide a reduced rate for qualifying corporate direct-investment dividends and a higher rate for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a general OECD-style Master File and Local File filing threshold. Under Income Tax Act section 247, contemporaneous documentation is required for relevant non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents and must generally be provided within three months after a written request. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 1 billion, subject to statutory filing rules.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general regime named Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Canadian transfer-pricing, treaty anti-abuse, general anti-avoidance, sham, beneficial-ownership, and business-purpose doctrines may nevertheless apply. Maintain evidence of decision-making, personnel, functions, assets, risks, contractual rights, and actual conduct, especially for intellectual property ownership, intercompany services, financing, and royalty arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion, false statements, or wilful failure to comply

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239 provides for criminal fines and imprisonment. On summary conviction, the fine may generally range from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, with imprisonment up to two years; indictable prosecution can carry imprisonment up to five years. Other penalties, interest, reassessments, and provincial consequences may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate income tax solely because they are directors. Personal criminal liability may arise from their own participation, authorization, or legally attributable conduct. Separate statutory director-liability rules can apply to specified remittances and other obligations. Civil recovery, prosecution, and travel restrictions require applicable legal authority and process; they are not automatic consequences of a tax assessment.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 247 permits transfer-pricing adjustments. A penalty may apply where the net transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the statutory threshold, generally the greater of CAD 5 million and 10% of gross revenue, and the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to determine and use arm's-length amounts. The penalty is generally 10% of the relevant net adjustment; interest and consequential reassessments may also apply.

Director personal liability: The adjustment and penalty are generally imposed on the corporation. Directors may face personal exposure for their own knowing participation in an offence or under a separate statutory liability provision; ordinary director status alone does not establish personal liability.
Late filing, late payment, or deficient records

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties, arrears and instalment interest, reassessments, and possible prosecution for wilful non-compliance may apply under federal or Quebec law. Penalties depend on the return, filing history, tax owing, and conduct; no single daily surcharge applies generally.

Director personal liability: Corporate tax and filing liabilities ordinarily remain corporate liabilities. Personal liability may arise under specific statutory provisions or from a director's own offence, not merely from holding office.
Improper treaty claims or withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unremitted withholding tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Treaty benefits may be denied under treaty terms or domestic anti-avoidance rules. Separate reporting penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for corporate withholding-tax assessments. Statutory director-liability provisions may apply to specified unremitted amounts, subject to their conditions and available defences; personal criminal liability requires the applicable offence and proof.
Unlicensed gambling or misleading game monetization

Statutory sanction: The absence of a stated statutory licence requirement for the described simulation does not establish that every implementation is exempt. Criminal Code gambling provisions, provincial gaming laws, consumer-protection rules, platform policies, and payment-provider requirements may apply depending on game mechanics and monetization. Penalties are offence- and jurisdiction-specific; obtain a Quebec gaming-law assessment before launch or feature changes.

Director personal liability: Corporate status does not protect individuals from liability for their own participation in an offence or from applicable officer or director liability provisions. Civil, administrative, and criminal consequences depend on the conduct and governing law; travel bans or imprisonment are not automatic.
Shell-company, sham, or artificial intellectual-property arrangements

Statutory sanction: Possible outcomes include denial of treaty benefits, transfer-pricing adjustments, application of the general anti-avoidance rule or other anti-avoidance provisions, withholding-tax assessments, interest, penalties, and reassessment. A shell structure alone is not a standalone offence; sanctions require the applicable statutory basis and facts.

Director personal liability: Corporate separateness is not automatically disregarded. Directors may face personal exposure for their own misconduct, statutory liabilities, or participation in an offence. Civil restitution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment require separate legal grounds and due process.
Anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime exposure

Statutory sanction: Whether a game publisher or payment intermediary is a reporting entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act depends on its activities and statutory status. Criminal laundering offences can carry severe fines and imprisonment; reporting and recordkeeping penalties apply only where the relevant statutory duties attach. Assess Apple and Google payment flows, third-party processors, cash-out features, and any direct financial services separately.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be liable for their own knowing participation or for offences established under applicable law. Director status alone does not create automatic criminal liability; civil recovery, travel restrictions, and imprisonment require legal authority and process.
