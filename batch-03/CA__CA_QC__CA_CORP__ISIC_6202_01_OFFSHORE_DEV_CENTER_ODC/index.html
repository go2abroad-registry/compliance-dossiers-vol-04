Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal portal canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; for Quebec: Revenu Québec / Revenue Quebec — provincial portal revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or register as an extra-provincial corporation with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). The corporation then applies to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registration; it separately registers with Revenu Québec for QST and applicable source deductions. There is no distinct TIN; the BN with its RT (GST/HST) account and the NEQ/QST number serve as the statutory VAT identifiers. Non-resident corporations carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Quebec must register even without a physical establishment, subject to the small supplier threshold rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box regime; a Canadian business corporation is taxed at entity level on worldwide income. Elections permitted include Income Tax Act s. 216 for non-residents' net rental income, s. 217 for employment income, joint GST/HST registration elections for closely related entities under the Excise Tax Act, similar QST registration elections under the Quebec Sales Tax Act, and hybrid mismatch limitation provisions in ITA ss. 18.5–18.7 that cannot electively reclassify a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (gross taxable supplies threshold for both GST/HST and QST; small suppliers may voluntarily register)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied to a non-resident of Canada qualify for zero rating under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the Excise Tax Act if the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST, the service is performed while the recipient is outside Canada or the benefit is consumed outside Canada, the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property located in Canada, and no resident Canadian person directly benefits from the supply. For Quebec, parallel QST zero-rating provisions apply under the Quebec Sales Tax Act. An offshore software development center can zero-rate its development, testing, hosting, and maintenance services when supplied to a foreign parent or foreign affiliate and all documentation objectively demonstrates the foreign destination and non-resident user.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A) for tangible deliverables; pure ODC services are evidenced by service contracts and delivery logs
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or electronic proof of export for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 copy evidencing foreign-currency receipt in Canada
  • Zero-rated export service invoice with non-resident recipient name/address and contractual non-residence representation
  • Technical delivery/host location evidence (server logs, time records, repository metadata) showing service was delivered from the Quebec ODC to the non-resident offshore recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoice clearance exists in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they satisfy the prescribed information requirements under the Excise Tax Act Regulations and QST regulations. CRA and Revenu Québec provide API/XML portals for electronic filing and remittance (GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec e-services). Revenu Québec has announced a phased roadmap toward structured e-invoicing/e-reporting, but it is not currently a legal clearance condition.

If zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess the supply at the full 5% GST and 9.975% QST rates plus compounding arrears interest from the reporting period. Missing or fraudulent export documentation triggers retroactive clawback for the normal four-year limitation period, extended to six years or indefinitely for misrepresentation, willful default, or fraud. Penalties can include a 25% gross negligence penalty on the net tax understated, higher civil penalties for tax evasion, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime; Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control statute and imposes no capital-account restriction on cross-border transactions. Current-account and capital-account flows, including dividend remittances, intercompany loan servicing, equity injections attributable to this Quebec offshore development centre (ISIC 6202 / NAICS 541512), and full profit repatriation, are freely permitted without prior central bank approval, quota, or currency licence. The operative constraints are fiscal rather than exchange-control driven: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest (25% statutory, reduced to 5%–15% under applicable income tax treaties) and the earnings-stripping / excessive interest and financing expense limitation rules under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in USD or any other foreign currency without Bank of Canada approval or filing obligation; for Canadian income tax and GST/HST purposes, the ODC must translate foreign-currency revenue into CAD on a consistent documented basis (invariably the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate policy), and books and records must be retained pursuant to section 230 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the currency and exchange control framework is administered by the Department of Finance Canada, with AML/ATF oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The Bank of Canada is additionally the retail payment activity supervisor for payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as supplementary operational disbursement rails, Low as the principal or escrowed capital deposit institution. Wise B2B and Payoneer are acceptable auxiliary channels for cross-border client collections and subcontractor or freelancer settlements, but they are not CDIC-insured banks and cannot support Quebec statutory payroll remittances, Revenu Québec tax debits, or high-value Lynx settlement. Offshore digital EMIs are legally usable as payment service providers, but one that is active in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; in practice, domestic banks treat foreign EMIs as unlicensed deposit takers and will reject them as the designated treasury account, requiring a Schedule I bank relationship before any cross-border EMI is integrated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent or shareholder funding is routed through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for same-bank interbank concentration) into the Quebec operational account, citing the appropriate Statistics Canada BOP purpose code for either direct investment equity or intercompany working capital. No Canadian exchange-control approval is required; however, the receiving Schedule I bank will require a funding direction letter, source-of-funds evidence, and the underlying Investment Canada Act notification file where the establishing investment meets the applicable threshold. CAD conversion is executed at the bank's corporate spot rate or by booking a deliverable FX forward, with the wire narrative containing full remittance details to satisfy the bank's AML and transaction-monitoring obligations.CAD-denominated domestic disbursements clear through Lynx, Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch retail payments; payroll and Quebec statutory remittances are settled by pre-authorized debit to the CRA and Revenu Québec. Cross-border supplier settlements and subcontractor payouts are executed via SWIFT MT103 with full 70-character remittance information, while high-value procurement transactions from non-WTO counterparties may be structured through commercial letters of credit (sight or usance under UCP600). Late filing: Federal penalty of 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delay up to 12 months; further 10% can be imposed where non-filing persists after CRA demand. Quebec imposes parallel provincial late-filing penalties and daily interest. Amounts owed on incorrect returns attract a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under section 163(2) ITA, plus compound daily interest.

Non-resident carrying on business in Quebec reports only revenue attributable to the Quebec permanent establishment and allocates inter-provincial revenue. Late filing: Federal penalty of 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delay up to 12 months; further 10% can be imposed where non-filing persists after CRA demand. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50,000,000 combined gross revenue of the taxpayer and associated enterprises, with CAD 5,000,000 aggregate related-party cross-border transactions (Form T106 must be filed where reportable non-arm's length transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country report is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue over CAD 750,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a dedicated ESR. Instead, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 ITA) and transfer-pricing rules (section 247 ITA) act as substance-over-form doctrines. A Quebec ODC must demonstrate a real Canadian presence—employees capable of performing software development, active decision-making and contract execution in Quebec, credentials, equipment, and financial risk—otherwise CRA can recharacterize the arrangement, allocate profit to the foreign parent, disallow intercompany deductions, and impose penalties for income artificially shifted offshore.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Misrepresentation of Revenue or Expenses

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; imprisonment up to five years; unpaid tax, interest, and penalties cannot be discharged in bankruptcy.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize evasion are jointly and severally liable under section 227 ITA for corporate taxes not remitted; travel restrictions may be imposed while charges are pending and prison terms are available on conviction.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust intercompany charges to arm's length; penalty under section 247(3) equals 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment; interest runs from the reassessment date; if no documentation exists, a gross-negligence penalty may be asserted.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly sign a false return or cause TP documentation to be false can be held personally liable for civil penalties and prosecuted under section 239 ITA; personal fines and imprisonment up to five years may be imposed.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: The corporation is liable for the entire unreanmitted withholding amount, daily compounding interest, and a 10% penalty under section 227(9) ITA for failure to withhold or remit; provincial exposure may arise under Revenu Québec rules.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted withholding amounts under section 227(1) of the Criminal Code as well as under ITA section 227.1; travel bans can be ordered where criminal proceedings are commenced, and imprisonment applies for wilful non-remittance.
Shell Company and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR CRA can recharacterize the entity as a sham, disallow claims, and impose penalties; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 500,000 for a corporation and CAD 250,000 for an individual for AML non-compliance; directors can be assessed personally for excessive tax and interest.

Director personal liability: Courts can lift the corporate veil in tax and AML proceedings; directors may face civil judgments for unpaid tax, bans on leaving the jurisdiction, seizure of assets, and imprisonment for knowingly participating in a sham or money-laundering scheme.
