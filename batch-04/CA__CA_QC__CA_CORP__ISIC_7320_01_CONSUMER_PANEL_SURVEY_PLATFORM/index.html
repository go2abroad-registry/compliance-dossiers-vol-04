Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; online portals: My Business Account (CRA) and GST/HST NETFILE. Quebec provincial counterpart: Revenu Québec (Québec Government) for QST – portal: Clic Revenu Québec / Mon dossier d'entreprise.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Quebec must register for a Business Number (BN) with CRA, using Form RC66 or online GST/HST registration for non-residents. Revenu Québec issues a separate provincial tax number for QST upon registration; the BN and QST number are effective as of the first taxable supply, unless the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. No separate TIN is issued beyond the BN/QST registrant identifiers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Quebec must register for a Business Number (BN) with CRA, using Form RC66 or online GST/HST registration for non-residents. Revenu Québec issues a separate provincial tax number for QST upon registration; the BN and QST number are effective as of the first taxable supply, unless the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. No separate TIN is issued beyond the BN/QST registrant identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A corporation's resident status and entity-level taxation are determined by legal facts. Available statutory elections include section 216 for non-resident owners of Canadian rental or timber income, foreign-affiliate joint elections, and anti-hybrid mismatch documentation procedures under ITA sections 18.15, 18.16, and 20.33 (no elective mismatch treatment).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; for Quebec QST, the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold applies to taxable supplies in Quebec or with a Quebec nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a Canadian-registered market research SaaS platform, supplies of services made to a non-resident recipient are generally zero-rated under Part VII of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the services are performed outside Canada or are not performed in relation to Canadian real property or goods for use in Canada; the recipient must be resident outside Canada and the contract and delivery records must establish the offshore nature of the service supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/B3 for goods; service contracts and transfer-of-funds records for SaaS exports)
  • Bill of Lading (for goods; for services, an executed service agreement or e-commerce transaction log from PayPal/Wise)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank/FTD record evidencing foreign-source payment and FX repatriation)

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous e-invoicing stamping regime. GST/HST returns and certain scheduled information must be filed electronically; QST returns are filed through Revenu Québec's accelerated digital services. E-invoicing may be issued in EDI/XML/PDF/JSON formats, and CRA requires adequate electronic record linking, but there is no centralized pre-clearance or digital stamping requirement.

CRA may reassess zero-rating within four years (or longer for misrepresentation); penalties include 25% of the net GST/HST amount falsely claimed, interest accruals, and penalties under Excise Tax Act sections 280 and 286 for knowingly false statements. Revenu Québec imposes similar QST reassessment and penalty rules, including fines and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Canada fully complies with IMF Article VIII, so there are no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, no discriminatory currency arrangements, and no multiple-currency practices. Capital-account transactions are also unrestricted, subject to non-resident investment screening under the Investment Canada Act: establishment of a new Canadian business by a non-Canadian triggers a mandatory ISED notification within 30 days, and acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above the WTO review threshold (C$1.043 billion enterprise value in 2024; updated annually) require a net-benefit review. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without prior approval, but must satisfy the solvency and retained-earnings (realizable value) tests of the Quebec Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1) and the Canada Business Corporations Act. Dividends paid to non-residents are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, generally reduced to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (substantial holding) under the Canada–US tax treaty, 15%/5% under the Canada–France treaty, or comparable treaty relief; the payer must file NR4 information returns with the CRA. All transactions may be denominated in CAD or any convertible foreign currency at the corporation's option.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export proceeds or cross-border service revenue from market research, panelist data, or SaaS subscriptions. A Quebec corporation may freely retain foreign currency in non-resident or domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, convert receipts at commercial spot rates, apply them to payables, or hedge them. The only applicable constraint is transactional transparency: every international electronic funds transfer (IETF) of C$10,000 or more, or multiple transfers totaling C$10,000 within 24 consecutive hours, must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial intermediary under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the corporate client is required to provide accurate remittance information on all wire instructions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), as monetary authority and manager of the Exchange Fund Account held for the Minister of Finance; prudential regulation of banks and authorized foreign banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction surveillance and AML/CFT reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no exchange-control authority for private commercial payments since Canada accepted IMF Article VIII, Sections 2, 3, and 4 obligations effective 15 December 1952; the Foreign Exchange Control Act regime was abolished in 1951. No currency-licensing or central-bank prior-approval applies to current or capital-account remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM, subject to strict legal constraints and a ring-fenced treasury architecture. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, formerly TransferWise; PayPal via its licensed PSP entities) offer excellent multi-currency payout functionality, rapid remote onboarding, and lower-value disbursement economics, but they are not Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II banks and do not carry CDIC deposit insurance. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI may not take deposits in Canada as a bank; its balances are uninsured e-money claims, creating counterparty risk; (ii) any EMI that performs foreign exchange dealing or money transfer for Canadian-resident clients must be registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA as a money services business (MSB), and if it carries on such services in Quebec it must obtain registration from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Act respecting money-services businesses; (iii) to avoid characterization of the platform itself as an MSB, the platform must not hold, direct, or control client funds as a payment intermediary; disbursements to panellists must be treated as the corporation's own principal payments for services rendered, executed by a licensed PSP as an agent; (iv) Wise and PayPal Mass Payout should be used only as disbursement rails funded on a just-in-time basis from a tier-1 Canadian bank operating account, never as primary treasury custody; (v) all PSP accounts must be onboarded in the legal name of the Quebec corporation, with executed terms, sanctions screening, and sub-processor privacy safeguards under Quebec Law 25 (Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector) for panellist data.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident capital injection: (1) Execute a written share subscription agreement and board resolution approving the issuance of common shares to the non-resident shareholder; if a shareholder loan is used, execute a promissory note with arm's-length interest and clearly severable terms. (2) Settlement must be initiated from the shareholder's regulated bank account via SWIFT MT103 into the Quebec corporation's CAD-denominated tier-1 operating account (National Bank, RBC, or TD), with remittance information specifying: remitter legal name and UBO, purpose 'Inbound direct investment / share subscription', and reference to the subscription agreement number; USD or EUR funds may instead be wired to a foreign-currency account and converted by the Canadian bank's spot desk. (3) Canadian large-value clearing occurs over Payments Canada's Lynx system (formerly LVTS) for same-day final settlement of CAD wires. (4) Compliance checkpoints: file an Investment Canada Act 'Notification of Establishment of a New Canadian Business' with ISED within 30 days of the new Quebec business establishment if the direct owner is a non-Canadian; confirm Statistics Canada Balance of Payments direct-investment reporting obligations; the receiving financial institution will report an IETF of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC and may require a source-of-wealth questionnaire. (5) Loan principal repayments and interest to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding (25%, reduced by treaty) unless an exemption applies; interest paid at arm's length to a non-resident lender generally qualifies for the Canadian 'fully exempt interest' provisions under the Income Tax Act and must be reported on Form 105 / NR4 if withheld. (6) Maintain strict separation between the capital/operating treasury account and panellist-liability sub-ledgers. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents), subject to DTA reduction (e.g., 5% on DTA treaty dividends for 10%+ ownership; 15% portfolio dividends).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared when a taxpayer has transactions with a non-arm's length non-resident and the total aggregate value of such transactions exceeds CAD 1 million in the taxation year. However, CRA may request documentation for smaller transactions if required; a de facto threshold of CAD 1 million is frequently used as a safe-harbor.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no statutory Economic Substance Regulations similar to Caribbean regimes, but it applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 ITA and Quebec's anti-avoidance rules under the Taxation Act. CRA and Revenu Québec disregard transactions that have no legitimate business purpose and recharacterize the profit flow. A pure shell entity with no substance in a no-tax jurisdiction will be ignored, and its principal/controller will be taxed as having received the income directly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239 and Quebec Taxation Act, criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; interest and arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and controllers who authorized or consented to the false filings are personally liable as co-perpetrators; personal bank accounts can be seized, passports canceled and travel bans imposed, and imprisonment served.
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying the tax benefit and imposing a penalty of 50% of the tax benefit; Quebec's equivalent penalties apply; recharacterization of the shell's contracts and invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the tax benefit if they signed statutory declarations or knew of the artificial structure; civil restitution orders, asset freezes, and potential director criminal liability for fraud if steps were executed without substance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) ITA penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment plus interest; adjustment to taxable income; loss of deductions; additional provincial reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false intercompany pricing may be found jointly responsible for the corporate tax debt if they signed the declaration of compliance; personal liability for unpaid source deductions is common, and TP penalties can be converted into a director assessment under wilful blindness.
AML / Payment Platform Abuse

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations and CAD 100,000 per officer; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31: imprisonment up to 10 years; asset forfeiture; potential revocation of the Quebec enterprise registration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally prosecuted for money laundering if they knew of the illicit flow and derived benefit; convictions lead to travel bans, restitution orders, and imprisonment; personal assets are included in the criminal asset forfeiture process.
Late or Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty (5% + monthly 1% up to 12 months) and the 50% gross negligence penalty; interest at prescribed rates; for repeated non-filing, Information Return penalties up to CAD 7,500 (not filed after demand) each.

Director personal liability: Directors of a non-filing corporation are jointly and severally liable for any unremitted source deductions or GST/HST; they may be assessed personally under s.227.1 and equivalent Quebec provisions, and may be subject to travel bans under CRA collection enforcement.
