Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and Represent a Client. Ontario permits and provincial tax registration are administered by the Ontario Ministry of Finance and the Provincial Nominee/Labour authorities respectively, but the income and consumption tax registration is exclusively federal via CRA.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident person carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account. The standard procedure is to file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' online through Business Registration Online or by mail to the applicable CRA Tax Centre. Where a limited partnership is involved, the LP itself is registered as a GST/HST registrant using its own BN, while income-tax reporting is performed by each partner using the partner's own Canadian tax identification number (TIN) where applicable. For an Ontario limited partnership that is an EOR/payroll service provider, the LP must also register for a CRA Payroll Deductions program account if it has Canadian employees or is itself the employer of record. Foreign partners without a Canadian TIN must file Form NR3010 or otherwise obtain a CRA non-resident individual/corporation number, as required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Canada's Income Tax Act, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer at the entity level, and income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to general and limited partners according to the partnership agreement. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source income, principally income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment in Ontario. For GST/HST purposes, however, the Excise Tax Act treats a partnership as a separate 'person' capable of being a registrant, so the LP must collect and remit HST on taxable supplies made in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election for entity classification. An Ontario limited partnership is mandatorily classified as a pass-through entity for income tax. Relevant statutory elections that may be available include the partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(7) of the Income Tax Act (with CRA concurrence) to adopt a non-calendar taxation year, and elections concerning partnership property dispositions under subsection 13(4.1) and section 96(2.1) in certain reorganizations. No election exists to make the LP taxable at the entity level, and no hybrid-mismatch elective regime applies beyond the mandatory anti-hybrid rules in sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000. A person is generally required to register for GST/HST if, at any time in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in the current calendar quarter, the total value of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds C$30,000 (excluding GST/HST). Non-resident suppliers with no physical presence in Canada may be subject to remote-seller rules for digital economy services; however, for a Canadian Ontario limited partnership carrying on business through a permanent establishment, the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold applies in the normal manner. Registrants must remain registered even if their taxable supplies later fall below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Part I of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier or recipient exports the goods in due course before use or consumption in Canada and documentary proof of export is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident person may be zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI if they are not excluded services, such as services rendered in connection with real property located in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For a Global EOR and cross-border payroll agency, payroll-administration services performed in Ontario for Canadian employees will normally be subject to 13% HST; back-office services supplied to a non-resident client for an entirely offshore workforce may qualify for zero-rating if the statutory place-of-supply rules and documentation conditions are satisfied. Each supply must be analyzed separately because EOR arrangements often involve a bundle of services with different places of performance and consumption.

  • Canada Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export report where required, including CESARS electronic reporting)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying EOR/client services contract
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / truck waybill showing the goods departed Canada for a foreign destination
  • Proof of export accepted by CRA, such as a foreign customs entry, destination port receipt, or signed delivery confirmation
  • Written evidence that the recipient is a non-resident person, including address, tax registration, and status documents
  • Bank transfer records, SWIFT confirmation, or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing payment received from the non-resident buyer
  • GST/HST zero-rating substantiation records sufficient to satisfy subsection 286(3) of the Excise Tax Act

Canada has no mandatory national electronic-invoicing clearance regime and no real-time digital fiscal stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) requirement. CRA accepts electronic invoices and electronic GST/HST returns via NETFILE, EDI, and approved API-based commercial software. A GST/HST registrant must issue an invoice showing prescribed information under section 3(1) of the Excise Tax Act, including the supplier's BN/GST/HST registration number, date, nature of the supply, consideration, and amount of tax. All invoices and zero-rating support must be retained for six years. Ontario does not impose an additional e-invoicing or tax clearance obligation.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, Canadian-dollar equivalent receipts must be reported under CRA's published exchange-rate conventions, and export documents must be retained for six years. CRA's Export Verification and GST/HST refund review programs scrutinize zero-rated export claims. If the exporter fails to provide contemporaneous documentary proof of export, the zero-rating is retroactively denied, the uncollected 13% HST is reassessed against the Ontario supplier, and interest runs from the date the tax was originally payable. False or misleading statements in returns or rebate applications attract civil penalties, including the 50% gross-negligence penalty under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, and knowing participation in a fraudulent export-refund scheme may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment, plus full recovery of the clawed-back amount with statutory arrears interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both the current account and the capital account. The Canadian dollar is free-floating and is not subject to exchange-control licensing, quotas, margin deposits, or prior-approval requirements. The Ontario LP may receive unlimited foreign equity contributions, hold CAD and USD bank accounts domestically and abroad, and repatriate capital and profits without Bank of Canada authorization or tax-clearance certificates. Profit repatriation mechanics for a non-resident limited partner are: (i) a tax-free return of contributed capital; (ii) net-basis Part I federal and Ontario tax on the partner's allocated share of Canadian-source business income where the LP carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; and (iii) treaty-reduced Part XIII gross-basis withholding only for passive payments such as interest, rents, royalties, and certain management fees. FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting applies to each cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more per business day; this is a reporting obligation, not a convertibility permission gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline exists. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951; service exporters, including an EOR platform invoicing global employer-of-record and payroll-administration services, may retain 100% of export or service receipts in any currency and for any duration without conversion obligations. The LP's only compliance duties are: (i) FINTRAC EFTR filing for aggregated cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per business day; (ii) translation of foreign-currency revenue into CAD at the Bank of Canada published exchange rate for Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) reporting; and (iii) Statistics Canada export declarations where goods are physically exported, which is not applicable to pure cross-border EOR services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration because statutory exchange controls were repealed in 1951. Supervisory roles are distributed: OSFI regulates banks and foreign-exchange dealers; FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT, currency-reporting, and cross-border EFT reporting; and the Bank of Canada registers payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM — auxiliary disbursement rail only, not treasury core. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise Canada Ltd. and Airwallex are legally permitted to operate in Ontario as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and must also register with the Bank of Canada as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, S.C. 2021, c. 23; registration regime effective November 2024). These EMIs may convert currency, hold safeguarded client float, and execute cross-border contractor and wage disbursements. However, they are not banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46): they cannot take deposit liabilities, offer CDIC-insured demand deposit accounts, provide commercial letters of credit, or serve as the sole repository for client payroll trust funds. The blueprint therefore (i) maintains the primary operating and capital escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank; (ii) uses the EMI exclusively for low-value recurring contractor disbursements below CAD 50,000 per transfer; and (iii) verifies the EMI's RPAA safeguarding arrangements and FINTRAC MSB registration status annually.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the foreign parent or contributing bank issues a SWIFT MT103 with field 70 stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — ONTARIO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP [NAME]' and a purpose narrative of 'Equity injection / partnership capital — no goods or services supplied; non-taxable capital receipt'; (2) CAD funds settle in real time through Lynx, Payments Canada's high-value payment system in central-bank money; USD funds clear through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent or CHIPS with SWIFT gpi tracking and are booked as a USD balance or internally converted to CAD; (3) the receiving bank performs an inbound source-of-funds inquiry and accepts the supporting capital call notice, serviceOntario Certificate of Limited Partnership, LP Agreement, UBO schedule, and the non-resident partners' IRS and CRS self-certifications; (4) every aggregated cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or more within a single business day is reported by the bank to FINTRAC, and where the LP clears foreign funds through an EMI directly, the LP remains separately responsible for its own EFTR filings; (5) no central-bank permission, capital-control permit, or tax clearance certificate is required, and the contribution is credited to the partners' capital accounts without withholding, provided it is genuinely equity rather than shareholder debt.Clearing rails: (1) domestic Canadian payroll — ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) direct deposits to employees' Canadian bank accounts, with Interac e-Transfer for ad-hoc emergency wage payments; (2) cross-border contractor settlements — SWIFT MT103 through the Schedule I bank for transfers above CAD 50,000, and FINTRAC-registered EMI (Wise) transfers for low-value recurring contractor payments below that threshold; (3) client-funded trade flows — Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 and standby letters of credit where an EOR client requires a performance, escrow, or security instrument; (4) USD payroll conversion — internal multi-currency book transfer from the USD revenue account to the CAD operating account, priced at the hedged forward rate or same-day spot rate; (5) governance — a dual-signature disbursement mandate executed by the general partner plus the CFO or compliance designee, SWIFT gpi end-to-end tracking, and FINTRAC EFTR filing for each aggregated outbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more per business day.Spot FX contracts (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD) for immediate conversion of EOR client receipts into payroll funding — non-speculative, same-day or next-day settlement through the primary bank dealing desk.Deliverable FX forwards (USD/CAD) with 1, 3, 6, and 12-month tenors to lock the EOR margin between USD client invoices and CAD payroll obligations, documented under an ISDA or Canadian FX master agreement and classified as ordinary-course hedging for Part I tax purposes.FX options (USD/CAD calls and puts) and zero-cost collars or participating forwards to protect minimum conversion rates while retaining upside — permitted only where matched to underlying payroll exposure to avoid speculative characterization.Cross-currency swaps (USD/CAD) for large notional treasury flows where the LP funds a long-dated payroll obligation against a stream of USD receivables, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Schedule I swap dealer.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — permissible only for settling payroll and contractor obligations in non-convertible or restricted third-country currencies (for example PHP, VND, or select LATAM currencies) where no onshore deliverable market exists; NDFs on CAD itself are non-standard because CAD has a fully developed deliverable onshore market, and the LP must evidence the matched underlying payroll exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM — auxiliary disbursement rail only, not treasury core. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise Canada Ltd. and Airwallex are legally permitted to operate in Ontario as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and must also register with the Bank of Canada as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, S.C. 2021, c. 23; registration regime effective November 2024). These EMIs may convert currency, hold safeguarded client float, and execute cross-border contractor and wage disbursements. However, they are not banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46): they cannot take deposit liabilities, offer CDIC-insured demand deposit accounts, provide commercial letters of credit, or serve as the sole repository for client payroll trust funds. The blueprint therefore (i) maintains the primary operating and capital escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank; (ii) uses the EMI exclusively for low-value recurring contractor disbursements below CAD 50,000 per transfer; and (iii) verifies the EMI's RPAA safeguarding arrangements and FINTRAC MSB registration status annually.
