Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec QST and provincial tax administration. Primary portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CLIC.

E-government filing portal

The non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA before making a taxable supply in Canada. Apply via CRA's online Business Registration service or Form RC1. The GST/HST account is established as part of the BN. For Quebec supplies, register separately for QST with Revenu Québec through the CLIC portal, providing the partnership agreement, proof of foreign entity existence, and a designated Quebec or Canadian representative for service. Registration is compulsory once the partnership is no longer a small supplier; voluntary registration may be elected earlier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian income tax purposes, a Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners. The partnership is, however, a registrant and a deemed person for GST/HST and QST purposes and must collect and remit indirect tax on taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election regime. The pass-through classification follows from the legal form of a limited partnership under Quebec civil law and the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: partnership fiscal-period elections under subsection 96(3), an election by a non-resident partner to file a Canadian tax return under subsection 216(4) instead of subjecting gross payments to Part XIII withholding, and GST/HST related elections such as a joint election on supplies of financial services between closely related persons. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act may apply to cross-border structures but do not allow reclassification of the partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in cumulative taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST (small-supplier threshold); a non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Green coffee beans are zero-rated as basic groceries under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when supplied in Canada and are also zero-rated as exported goods under Part V where the supplier exports them or delivers them to a carrier for export outside Canada. For QST, equivalent zero-rating applies to basic foodstuffs and qualifying exported goods. To claim the zero rate, the LP must prove that commercial possession of the coffee beans was transferred to a non-resident buyer or to the buyer's foreign carrier and that the goods were actually removed from Canada. Offshore services are not part of this goods-focused operation. Because green coffee beans are already zero-rated as basic groceries, export substantiation is still required to avoid potential reassessment if the supply is recharacterized as non-food-grade or if processing occurs before export.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian International Trade Single Window or GCA portal
  • Commercial invoice describing single-origin specialty green Arabica coffee beans with HS classification 0901.11 or 0901.12
  • Packing list and container manifest indicating the foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or ocean waybill showing the overseas consignee, port of loading in Canada, and port of discharge outside Canada
  • Buyer's payment release or inward remittance confirmation evidencing the flow of foreign currency proceeds
  • CBSA outward cargo control record or carrier's export declaration proving physical departure from Canada
  • Signed INCOTERMS delivery terms confirming transfer of risk to the overseas buyer

Canada and Quebec do not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping regime, or continuous transaction-control API mandate. Taxpayers must issue invoices containing prescribed GST/HST and QST registration numbers, the amount of tax payable, and vendor/purchaser identifiers. Electronic invoicing in EDI, PDF, or XML format is accepted if the original machine-readable data can be produced in legible form during audit. Records must be kept in Canada or be accessible electronically to CRA and Revenu Québec for a minimum of six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating for any supply not supported by contemporaneous export evidence. The consequence is reassessment at the full provincial and federal VAT rates of 14.975% plus interest. Misrepresentation or absence of reasonable care can trigger gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Tax Administration Act, a minimum 50% administrative penalty on improperly claimed rebates, and, for intentional fraud, criminal prosecution with fines or imprisonment. The normal reassessment period is four years, extending to ten years or indefinitely where a false statement or wilful default is established. Retrospective clawbacks may also revoke input tax credit entitlements and lead to denial of future zero-rating claims on subsequent export batches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, central bank registration thresholds, or blanket profit-remittance restrictions. Profit remittances may be effected freely through regulated financial institutions, subject only to FATCA/CRS reporting, anti-money-laundering verification, and standard transparency filings where a non-resident limited partner owns 25% or more of the partnership's income or capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Green coffee import proceeds and related export receipts may be retained in any major currency; the only statutory obligations are accurate record-keeping for income tax, GST/QST filing, CBSA valuation, and FINTRAC large transaction reporting where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is functionally decentralized, with FINTRAC supervising anti-money laundering registration and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) handling import/export reporting alongside customs valuation concurrent with FX flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for operating disbursements and FX execution, provided the EMI is a FINTRAC-registered money service business in Canada or a regulated EEA-EA fiduciary equivalent. Legal constraints include FINTRAC MSB registration, a Canadian agent or place of business for adequate supervision, and limitations on taking deposits. Capital contribution escrow, documentary letters of credit, and large commodity trade settlement are better served by Tier 1 Schedule I chartered banks.
