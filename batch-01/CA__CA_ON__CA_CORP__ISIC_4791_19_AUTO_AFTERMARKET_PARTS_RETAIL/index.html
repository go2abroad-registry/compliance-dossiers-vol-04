Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - GST/HST Division and Business Number Registration; primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; the Ontario Ministry of Finance separately operates the Ontario Tax Accounts portal for the Employer Health Tax (EHT) and the Ontario Business Registry for provincial corporate-registry data. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is the customs authority for import/export accounts.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Determine registration duty: a Canadian-incorporated business corporation must apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RM (customs import/export), and RP (payroll, where employees are engaged). Step 2 - Foreign-owned entities: registration is made online through Business Registration Online (BRO) where the filer holds a CRA My Business Account credential; alternatively, submit a completed Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) to the Sudbury Tax Centre, attaching certified copies of the articles of incorporation (under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Ontario Business Corporations Act), the foreign-parent registration certificate (if applicable), and the Ontario corporate information schedules administered within the T2 corporate income tax return. Step 3 - Program-account activation: the CRA issues the BN and RT account (VAT ID format, e.g., 123456789RT0001); the GST/HST registration becomes effective on the earlier of the date the first taxable supply was made in Canada or the date of the application, with retroactive registration available under subsection 242(1) of the Excise Tax Act. An application must be filed within 30 days of ceasing to be a small supplier. Step 4 - Ontario specifics: no separate Ontario GST/HST account exists because the 8% provincial HST component is collected and remitted by the CRA; the corporation must, however, register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for Employer Health Tax if Ontario payroll remuneration exceeds the CAD 1,000,000 annual exemption, and with the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) where workers are physically located in Ontario. Step 5 - Customs: an RM (import/export) program account under the same BN must be activated for the CBSA process to clear imported inventory (e.g., auto parts sourced abroad) and to file eManifest and export declarations. Step 6 - Non-resident shareholders do not require a separate Canadian tax TIN for the domestic corporation; the entity itself is the taxpayer, and non-resident shareholders/directors may require an individual CRA tax number only if they file a Canadian personal or withholding return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - CA_CORP is a Canadian-resident corporation (deemed resident under ITA subsection 250(4) by virtue of incorporation in Canada) and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through treatment is available to shareholders. Federal corporate income tax applies at the general rate of 15%, reduced to 9% on active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) up to the CAD 500,000 federal business limit; Ontario corporate income tax applies at 11.5% general rate, 3.2% for CCPC small-business income, and 10% for manufacturing and processing income. Where the corporation is controlled by non-residents it is not a CCPC, and the general federal and Ontario rates apply in full. The GST/HST is a separate value-added tax collected from customers and remitted to the CRA and does not alter the entity-level income tax classification.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; corporate status is statutory and may be displaced only through formal reorganization. Elections available to CA_CORP include: (i) voluntary registration for GST/HST while still a small supplier (ETA subsection 240(1)); (ii) election to use simplified net-tax accounting or annual GST/HST remittance frequency where the registrant qualifies as a small remitter under ETA section 237.1 and the related regulations; (iii) election under ITA subsection 85(1) for a tax-deferred rollover of property into the corporation on capital reorganization; (iv) election under ITA subsection 89(11) to treat the corporation as not being a CCPC where foreign-control facts are present; (v) the branch-tax election under ITA section 219 is limited to non-resident corporations and is not available to a domestic operating corporation; (vi) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 are mandatory and contain no taxpayer election, while treaty-based fiscal-transparency elections (e.g., Canadian administrative recognition of a US LLC's check-the-box classification) affect only cross-border investors and do not alter the Canadian corporation's own classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account (trade in goods and services, royalties, interest, dividends) and capital-account (share capital injections, shareholder loans, M&A, profit repatriation) transactions. There are no conversion ceilings, no prior approvals from the BOC or Department of Finance, and no non-resident ownership restrictions affecting an online retail treasury. Profit remittance is unrestricted: a Canadian corporation may declare and pay dividends in CAD or foreign currency without any profit remittance permit, but Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty and 15% under the Canada-China treaty) is applied by the resident payer and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency; this is a tax mechanics step, not an FX control. Practical impediments are limited to (i) FINTRAC reporting thresholds (C$10,000 for EFT and cross-border currency movements), (ii) AML source-of-funds checks when banks open treasury accounts, and (iii) the Investment Canada Act, which requires a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business to file a Notification with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada; a net-benefit review is triggered only for high-value acquisitions of control (2024 WTO-investor threshold: C$2.477 billion). None of these restraints affects current-account or ordinary treasury conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any statutory timeline. Export invoices may be denominated in CAD, USD, EUR, CNY or any freely convertible currency, and settlement can be collected in foreign accounts or converted at the corporate treasury's discretion. Administrative reporting that may touch export proceeds: (i) CBSA B13A export declaration for goods leaving Canada with a value above C$2,000 per shipment (electric courier and postal programs apply for low-value online retail parcels); (ii) GST/HST zero-rating of exported goods (the Ontario HST of 13% does not apply to qualifying exports; input tax credits remain available); (iii) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the receiving bank for cross-border transfers of C$10,000 or more; (iv) CRA reporting of foreign-source income and foreign-exchange gains; and (v) the base-erosion and anti-avoidance rules in the Income Tax Act if capital arrangements are structured to strip Canadian profits. None of these constitutes a surrender, conversion, or repatriation mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) - central monetary authority and supervisor of the retail payments framework - together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, prudential supervisor of deposit-taking institutions) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, AML/ATF authority). Foreign exchange administration is liberalised under the Currency Act and the federal Bank Act; no exchange-control or central-bank licensing regime exists for FX operations because the wartime foreign-exchange control regime was dismantled in 1951. The Department of Finance Canada retains jurisdiction over sanctions and the Investment Canada Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million gross revenue threshold for Local File and CAD 10 million for Master File based on specified transaction sizes under IC 87-2R.

Economic substance scope: Applicable to cross-border related-party transactions under ITA 247 and OECD guidelines. Canada does not apply Caribbean-style ESR (Economic Substance Regulations), but requires substantive activities and real economic presence for treaty shopping defenses and GAAR avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA section 239: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA civil penalties: gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax; compound daily interest; forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding and source deductions; courts can issue warrants for arrest, travel bans, and restitution orders; director disqualification and personal liability for civil fraud where they knowingly participate.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denying tax benefits; transfer pricing adjustment plus 10% penalty; fines and potential criminal liability if facts are misrepresented to CRA.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns are exposed to civil personal liability and gross negligence penalties; professional disqualification, travel restrictions, and asset seizures for fraud under Canadian criminal code.
AML

Statutory sanction: Criminal Money Laundering under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 500,000 for individuals / CAD 2,000,000 for corporations, imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC administrative penalties and bank freezes.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'persons acting in a management capacity' may face criminal liability; personal asset freezing, travel bans, extradition exposure; director veils pierced for wilful blindness and failure to maintain AML internal controls.
Unreported Foreign Assets and Non-Resident Withholding

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax assessments at 25% plus interest and penalties; false omission penalties under ITA 162-163; criminal liability for failing to withhold under ITA 227.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding tax under ITA 227.1; directors' personal bank accounts can be garnished; travel bans and civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty to the corporation and foreign shareholders.
