Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

An AG must have nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up in cash before registration, evidenced by a blocked-account confirmation issued by a Swiss bank; the capital remains escrowed until the company is entered in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification must be confirmed for office and storage areas by the municipal fire-prevention authority of Zug, including structural-load clearance for shelving and racking. Workplace safety obligations apply under Article 82 of the Swiss Labour Act and Suva accident-prevention instructions; no special civil-defense permit is required for low-risk retail operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV), Main Division for Value Added Tax; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT e-filing portal: SukRe (www.sukre.admin.ch); Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) and UID register managed by the Federal Statistical Office (www.uid.admin.ch). Cantonal tax authorities remain competent for profit/capital tax collection.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Switzerland that carries out taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeding CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover must obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) and register for VAT. Procedure: (1) apply for a UID via the FTA SukRe e-portal or the UID register, submitting certified constitutional documentation, power of attorney, and identification of beneficial owners; (2) file VAT registration form (e-form 0301) with the FTA Main Division for VAT, declaring the intended start date of taxable activity; (3) a non-resident entity without a Swiss branch must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (art. 67 VAT Act) who is jointly and severally liable for VAT; (4) upon approval, the FTA issues a VAT registration confirmation and the UID is extended with the suffix '-MWST' (e.g., CHE-000.000.000-MWST), which serves as the VAT identification number; (5) the entity must file quarterly or semi-annual VAT returns electronically through SukRe and maintain Swiss accounting records. Registration becomes effective from the commencement of taxable activity, and failure to register exposes the entity to assessment plus penalties.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not established in Switzerland that carries out taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeding CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover must obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) and register for VAT. Procedure: (1) apply for a UID via the FTA SukRe e-portal or the UID register, submitting certified constitutional documentation, power of attorney, and identification of beneficial owners; (2) file VAT registration form (e-form 0301) with the FTA Main Division for VAT, declaring the intended start date of taxable activity; (3) a non-resident entity without a Swiss branch must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (art. 67 VAT Act) who is jointly and severally liable for VAT; (4) upon approval, the FTA issues a VAT registration confirmation and the UID is extended with the suffix '-MWST' (e.g., CHE-000.000.000-MWST), which serves as the VAT identification number; (5) the entity must file quarterly or semi-annual VAT returns electronically through SukRe and maintain Swiss accounting records. Registration becomes effective from the commencement of taxable activity, and failure to register exposes the entity to assessment plus penalties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for foreign entities; an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration under art. 11 VAT Act where turnover is below the CHF 100,000 threshold; (ii) election to form a VAT group (art. 11 VAT Act) for closely held Swiss and foreign entities; (iii) option to tax real property under art. 22 VAT Act; (iv) tax-group election for direct federal tax purposes (art. 60-61 Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax) allowing domestic group members to be treated as a single taxpayer; (v) participation relief election under arts. 69-70 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax; (vi) recognition of a foreign entity's classification is governed exclusively by Swiss domestic tax law and DTT tie-breaker rules; the US 'check-the-box' election is not automatically recognized but may be relevant for US-Swiss treaty application. No hybrid-mismatch elections are available to AG shareholders under Swiss law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover (art. 10 para. 2 lit. a VAT Act). A foreign entity with turnover below the threshold may be exempt from registration, but a Swiss permanent establishment or a foreign entity supplying goods stored in Switzerland to Swiss consumers may nonetheless create a nexus. Voluntary registration is available under art. 11 VAT Act for corporations, cooperatives, foundations and Swiss permanent establishments of foreign businesses even if turnover does not reach CHF 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods is zero-rated with full input VAT deduction if the goods are physically dispatched or transported abroad within the statutory documentary framework (art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act). For online retail of consumer electronics, zero-rating applies where the retail sale is made to a foreign purchaser and the goods are exported from Switzerland (or from a Swiss customs warehouse) to a foreign destination. Directly related services (transport, handling, insurance costs) are also zero-rated. Services supplied to recipients with a business seat abroad are zero-rated where the service is provided to the foreign recipient (art. 23 para. 2 VAT Act). The seller must substantiate the export with a customs export declaration and transport evidence; missing proof causes the supply to be taxable at 8.1%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss e-dec / NCTS export declaration with customs clearance certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document evidencing movement out of Switzerland)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/MT103 or bank statement proving receipt of payment in foreign currency from the foreign buyer)

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime in Switzerland. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B and B2C transactions; the Swiss QR-bill and the eBill infrastructure are widely used commercial standards. From 1 January 2026, suppliers to Swiss federal public authorities must issue e-invoices in Peppol BIS 3.0 format via an approved access point. VAT returns and ancillary declarations must be filed electronically through the FTA SukRe portal using XML/Web-service API interfaces; a PDF-only submission is tolerated only for micro taxpayers. There is no digital stamping, tax authority pre-clearance, or unique transaction code requirement for invoices in the private sector.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims are vigorously pursued by the FTA. Under arts. 72 and 73 VAT Act, intentional tax evasion (including false export documentation or fictitious exports) is punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; negligent under-declaration attracts a fine of up to twice the tax; minor procedural infringements are fined up to CHF 8,000. The FTA may issue a discriminatory surcharge and publicly announce the penalty. There is a 10-year absolute limitation period and a 5-year relative limitation period for reassessment of undisclosed or misdeclared exports (art. 39 VAT Act). If the export proof bundle is incomplete or the inward remittance fails, the FTA retrospectively claws back the zero-rated treatment, imposes 8.1% VAT plus default interest, and may exclude the taxpayer from simplified export procedures and the status of approved economic operator.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no Swiss exchange control laws, no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds, and no authorization requirement for the acquisition of CHF by residents or non-residents. Switzerland operates a free capital market regime under the Federal Law on the Free Movement of Capital and the Bilateral Agreements with the EU, subject only to general anti-money laundering and sanctions screening. For an e-commerce retail entity, no prior governmental approval is required for cross-border payments, profit transfers, or dividend distribution, provided corporate vehicles are validly incorporated and tax obligations are settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Switzerland. Export receipts in CHF or foreign currencies may be retained, settled, or converted freely. There is no minimum holding period and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds. A Swiss AG may maintain foreign currency accounts without restriction, and the SNB imposes no mandatory conversion ratio. For customs and VAT purposes, statutory record-keeping of export invoices and payment evidence is required for exemption claims, but no monetary surrender obligation applies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank, with external exchange controls administered jointly with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for sanctions and due diligence matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderately feasible, subject to FINMA FinTech licensing or a full banking license. Swiss law does not ‘passport’ foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) into Switzerland. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK or EU authorized) may hold an account with a Swiss bank only as a payment service provider, but cannot independently provide a Swiss IBAN or act as a full banking counterparty for a Swiss AG without establishing a Swiss branch or acquiring a FinTech license (deposit ceiling of CHF 100 million for public deposits). For clearing rail purposes, a Swiss-registered payment institution (e.g., a FINMA-licensed Fintech) can be used for e-commerce payouts (Stripe, PayPal) but is not a replacement for a corporate account at a tier-1 commercial bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for an EMI as an auxiliary clearing node; High for a Swiss bank relationship combined with a payment service provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial capital of a Swiss AG (CHF 20,000 minimum for AG under Art. 621 OR), wire transfers must come from the shareholder's personal or corporate bank account by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA if CHF/EUR) citing payment purpose code: 'Kapitaleinlage / Einzahlung auf das Aktienkapital' (English: 'Capital contribution / payment towards share capital'). A Swiss bank account can be funded via SEPA credit transfer (European Economic Area) or SWIFT from any jurisdiction. The bank will require a formal copy of the notarised statutes (pubic deed) and the auditor's confirmation of the initial capital (if higher than CHF 20,000, a bank confirmation or the bank itself confirms to the notary). For foreign capital injection, use the bank's FX desk to convert to CHF and ensure the final debit message uses the beneficiary's name exactly matching the corporate charter. Bearer shares are prohibited for newly formed AGs since 2019; therefore, the bank is legally allowed to receive the funds without obtaining endorsement of physical share certificates. All inbound transfers must exclude sanctioned countries and pass beneficial ownership verification before funds are credited.For a consumer electronics e-commerce operator, outbound disbursements include (i) supplier payments to wholesale electronics manufacturers in China/HK/Vietnam; (ii) logistics and freight (DHL, FedEx, Swiss Post), (iii) payment processor settlement (Stripe/PayPal), (iv) royalties and service fees, and (v) dividends. Primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 for all foreign-currency wire transfers. For high-value merchandise (>USD 50,000 or equivalent), a commercial documentary letter of credit (L/C at sight) can be issued via UBS or ZKB; for repeat high-volume low-value orders, open account with SWIFT MT103 is suitable. All outgoing payments must use the correct Swiss payment purpose codes (e.g., 'Exportgutschrift' / 'Warenbezug' / 'Lizenzgebühren') and maintain underlying contracts and warehouse receipts. There is no surrender requirement and no export licensing for consumer electronics, except for items falling under Swiss export control (dual-use, cryptographic) — smartphone and personal gadgets generally require no license. SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) is available for euro payments within the EU/EEA; CHF payments use SIC / SIC5 (instant), both operated by SIX Interbank Clearing. For cross-border CHF, SWIFT is mandatory. Use FX forward within the same bank to limit currency exposure before executing MT103.FX forward contracts (USD/CHF, EUR/CHF) on a settled basis – freely permissible without fundamental rights or regulatory restriction; standard ISDA or bank's standard T&Cs. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderately feasible, subject to FINMA FinTech licensing or a full banking license. Swiss law does not ‘passport’ foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) into Switzerland. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK or EU authorized) may hold an account with a Swiss bank only as a payment service provider, but cannot independently provide a Swiss IBAN or act as a full banking counterparty for a Swiss AG without establishing a Swiss branch or acquiring a FinTech license (deposit ceiling of CHF 100 million for public deposits). For clearing rail purposes, a Swiss-registered payment institution (e.g., a FINMA-licensed Fintech) can be used for e-commerce payouts (Stripe, PayPal) but is not a replacement for a corporate account at a tier-1 commercial bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for an EMI as an auxiliary clearing node; High for a Swiss bank relationship combined with a payment service provider.
