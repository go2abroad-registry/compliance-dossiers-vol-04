Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Ontario limited partnership or to its corporate general partner; the capital of the partnership is fixed by the limited partnership agreement. No escrow account is mandated by the Limited Partnerships Act at formation; subscription monies may be held in a general partner operating or solicitor's trust account pending closing conditions. If the general partner is an Ontario corporation, shares must be issued for fair, bona fide consideration, but no minimum dollar capitalization is prescribed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincially administered tax administration; CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establish a Canadian Limited Partnership's tax identity by applying to CRA for a Business Number (BN) and Program Accounts (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll). Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) is filed with the partnership agreement and supporting identification; for non-resident partners, Form RC1 can be filed by mail/fax and may require a designated Canadian representative. Once issued, the BN serves as the taxpayer identification number for GST/HST, payroll, corporation income tax, and partnership information return (T5013) purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election for entity classification; the Ontario limited partnership is statutorily treated as a partnership for Canadian income tax purposes, filing an information return while partners bear tax. Hybrid mismatch elections may be relevant under ITA ss. 18.4–18.5 for the limited partnership's cross-border royalty-financing structure, and foreign jurisdictions may independently require an entity classification election (e.g., U.S. Form 8832) for the Canadian LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory registration arises when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act is available for (a) tangible goods exported from Canada as part of the supply, provided the goods are delivered outside Canada within a reasonable period (generally no later than 60 days after consideration becomes due) and documented export evidence is retained; and (b) services supplied to non-residents where the non-resident is not registered and is not required to be registered under the GST/HST regime, subject to anti-avoidance and situs rules. The revenue-based financing/royalty-fund's core financial services may be exempt supplies under Schedule V, Part VII, with zero-rating available only if the specific financial service is supplied to a non-resident and qualifies under the statutory exceptions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or foreign-exchange documentation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government-sponsored e-invoicing repository currently applies. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if they are complete, readable, and retained in an auditable format; GST/HST registered persons must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and no separate digital stamping or clearance pre-approval is required at the federal or Ontario level.

CRA may deny zero-rating and assess unremitted GST/HST at the applicable 13% HST rate, plus statutory interest, late-filing penalties, and a 25% penalty for knowingly making false statements under ETA s. 285.1. Gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act can reach 10% of the overstated credit or understated tax; deliberate misrepresentation may elevate the normal four-year reassessment window to a 7+ year period, and criminal prosecution applies for fraudulent export claims or false customs declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency with no general exchange controls, no restrictions on profit or royalty remittances abroad, and no central bank approval requirement for cross-border transfers. Outbound royalty and dividend payments are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, and treaty relief is available where applicable. Cross-border monetary transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 or equivalent must be reported to FINTRAC. Transactions with sanctioned countries or persons are prohibited or restricted under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Freezing Assets of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts. Exporters and royalty financing funds may retain export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely. For Canadian tax purposes, all foreign-currency income and remittances must be reported in Canadian-dollar equivalent using the applicable Bank of Canada exchange rate or CRA-annualized rate; receipts and disbursements are subject to FINTRAC large-transaction and cross-border currency reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential bank regulator and FINTRAC as the financial intelligence and anti-money laundering authority; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border disbursements and low-value royalty collections; Low for primary treasury, escrow, and lending operations. Foreign digital EMIs without a Canadian banking license may not accept Canadian deposits or carry on deposit-taking business without OSFI authorization. A fintech EMI that is registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) with FINTRAC may offer cross-border payments, FX conversion, and remittance services, but cannot provide regulated escrow or full corporate banking. For an Ontario limited partnership operating a non-dilutive royalty financing fund, the primary operating account should be held at a tier-1 OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank; EMIs may serve only as supplementary collection or disbursement gateways.

Defense evidence: Certified LP registration and Ontario limited partnership certificate; executed LP agreement; full partner register with ultimate beneficial ownership details; notarized passports and proof of residential address for each non-resident partner; tax residency certificates and foreign corporate charters; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds declarations with supporting bank statements and investment track record.

Defense evidence: Fund offering memorandum or private placement memorandum; commercial loan or royalty purchase agreements; underwriting policy and credit-risk framework; audited or reviewed financial statements; projected cash-flow waterfall; legal opinion confirming no securities or money-lending license is required for the specific revenue-based financing structure, or evidence of an applicable exemption.
Unusual cross-border wire patterns or payments originating from high-risk jurisdictions

Root cause: Unusual cross-border wire patterns or payments originating from high-risk jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Comprehensive transaction narrative; counterparty onboarding files; commercial contracts and invoices; beneficial ownership registers; sanctions screening results; FINTRAC-compliant large-transaction reports; board approval minutes; independent third-party KYC/AML review; source-of-wealth documentation for each investor and major borrower.
Undisclosed relationship between royalty-fund borrowers and fund principals, creating self-dealing or layering concerns

Root cause: Undisclosed relationship between royalty-fund borrowers and fund principals, creating self-dealing or layering concerns

Defense evidence: Independent conflict-of-interest register; related-party transactions disclosed in LP agreement and audited financial statements; fair-market valuation and transfer pricing memorandum; original borrower business plans and royalty statements; declarations of arm's length relationships; oversight committee minutes confirming independent review.
Escrow account purpose ambiguity and commingling of third-party funds

Root cause: Escrow account purpose ambiguity and commingling of third-party funds

Defense evidence: Escrow agreement or fund custody agreement; clearly defined account designation in the onboarding form; policy prohibiting commingling of client funds; independent audit trail; daily cash reconciliation; LP capital call and distribution records; escrow release conditions and signatory authority matrix.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be routed by SWIFT MT103 via a top-tier correspondent bank to the LP's Canadian-dollar operating account at a Schedule I bank. Canada does not use a statutory payment purpose code taxonomy; the bank must receive clear remittance information in SWIFT field 70, such as 'CA_LP capital contribution – Royalty Financing Fund', supported by ACH or Fedwire/CHIPS local-clearing instructions for USD-denominated contributions. The receiving bank will verify the LP's legal name, account number, and beneficial ownership documentation. FINTRAC large-transaction reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or equivalent, and the bank may request a source-of-funds package, including executed capital call notices, subscription agreements for the LP interest, and confirmed payer identity.
