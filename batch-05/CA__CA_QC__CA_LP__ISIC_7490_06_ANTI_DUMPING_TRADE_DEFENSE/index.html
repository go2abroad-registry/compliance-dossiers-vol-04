Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal Business Number (BN) administration and GST/HST program administered through Business Registration Online (BRO); Revenu Québec — provincial administration of the Québec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec payroll deductions, and Quebec corporate tax statements.

E-government filing portal

For the foreign LP, first apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1A; attach entity documentation proving foreign registration, good standing if applicable, and signing authority. Register for the GST/HST program account and, if carrying on business in Quebec, apply to Revenu Québec for a Quebec Identification Number (NEQ) and QST enrollment, using Revenu Québec's online 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' portal. Non-resident partnerships must provide a Canadian agent/representative for service, file prescribed information returns (T5013 partnership return), and, unless exempt, file partnership registration information under the Income Tax Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For the foreign LP, first apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1A; attach entity documentation proving foreign registration, good standing if applicable, and signing authority. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election comparable to the US. A limited partnership formed in Quebec is fiscally transparent by default until it elects otherwise under the Quebec Civil Association rules; for income tax, an election may be made under ITA s. 96 to treat the partnership as a 'Canadian partnership' where all partners are Canadian residents. Non-resident partners may elect, under the applicable tax treaty and Part I rules, to file and pay Canadian tax on allocated Canadian-source income rather than face Part XIII withholding. The partnership may also elect its fiscal period under ITA s. 34(1)(b) and may file a GST/HST flow-through election under ETA s. 273. Hybrid mismatch rules operate through existing allocation and deduction rules; no separate administrative election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over any consecutive four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); once exceeded, mandatory registration for both GST/HST and QST applies, unless the supply is an exempt or zero-rated supply and the registrant is a non-resident not carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of services made to a non-resident where the service is not performed in Canada, or is performed wholly outside Canada, and the supply does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For anti-dumping trade-defense consulting services delivered electronically or from Quebec to a foreign principal, 0% GST/HST and 0% QST are available provided the supplier holds documentary evidence that the recipient is a non-resident, the service is for use outside Canada, and no Canadian residence or fixed establishment consumed the service. Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated on export if the exporter retains proof of export within prescribed time limits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B3 form, Canada Customs Invoice, or electronic ACI export report, depending on the goods and carrier)
  • Bill of Lading or other shipping receipts evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT payment confirmation) establishing payment from a non-Canadian source
  • Signed engagement agreement identifying the non-resident principal
  • Statement of work and correspondence demonstrating the offshore nature of the consulting services
  • Zero-rated invoice issued with GST/HST and QST zero-rating codes

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing authorization, or digital stamping. Invoices (paper or electronic) must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and Quebec's Act respecting the QST; the CRA and Revenu Québec require returns to be electronically filed by registrants above threshold volumes, and require retention of accounting records for six years. There is no API-based pre-clearance or automatic digital certification of invoices; however, the CRA offers 'My Business Account' for e-filing returns and notices.

For zero-rated export claims, the CRA and Revenu Québec may reopen assessments within the normal assessment period or, in cases of misrepresentation attributable to negligence or fraud, at any time under subsection 298(4) ETA and corresponding provincial provisions. Where zero-rating is denied, the supplier is liable for uncollected GST/HST and QST, interest, and penalties. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract gross negligence penalties under ETA s. 285, which can amount to 15% of the unreported net tax (or higher in egregious cases), and criminal sanctions under ETA s. 327(1) for willful tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment. Revenu Québec similarly imposes penalties under the QST Act and may claw back refunds for unsubstantiated exports, including for non-resident partnerships, with joint and several exposure for partners in some circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible, free-floating CAD regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account transactions requiring no prior administrative approval. Profit and capital repatriation for a Quebec Limited Partnership is limited only by ordinary income tax, applicable Part XIII withholding on certain payments to non-residents, and AML/KYC recordkeeping obligations. There are no FX licensing requirements, conversion limits, or repatriation penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory surrender ratio, or conversion requirement for export proceeds. Revenues from anti-dumping trade-defense consulting are treated as ordinary service income and may be held in CAD or any permitted foreign currency at the entity's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada and the Exchange Fund Account; there is no standalone foreign-exchange control authority. Prudential oversight of banking institutions rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are viable for ancillary multi-currency disbursements but cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for a Quebec LP. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration as a money services business, Quebec Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) licensing for in-province activity, no access to Payments Canada Lynx settlement, inability to issue commercial letters of credit, no Quebec Bar-compliant escrow/trust capability, and elevated platform-suspension risk for partnership structures with foreign limited partners.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined Quebec provincial and Canadian federal headline rate for a limited partnership allocated to a corporate general partner; top combined marginal rate on investment income may reach 26.7% depending on provincial allocation)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on distributions to non-residents is 25% for amounts paid or credited from Canadian sources, including partnership income allocations to non-resident partners that are not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment. A limited partnership distribution of retained Canadian-source income to a non-resident limited partner is subject to 25% unless treaty relief reduces it; typical Canada-U.S. rate 15% on dividends, 5% if substantial holding; Canada-UK treaty 15% generally; Canada-France 15% generally.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Canadian transfer pricing rules (subsection 247(4) and Information Circular IC 87-2R), a penalty applies if the taxpayer fails to make or provide 'contemporaneous documentation' by the filing deadline and the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue or RMD (revenue, gross margins, or net income proxies) thresholds. The penalty is 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment, with elements: (i) the total adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; and (ii) the documentation requirement applies. The CRA requires a master file and local file based on an entity having transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1 million in gross revenue (threshold applies to the Canadian entity); a Country-by-Country report is required if the ultimate parent's annual consolidated group revenue is CAD 750 million or more. The exact local currency threshold for Quebec provincial documentation mirrors the federal approach through Revenu Québec's transfer pricing rules under section 1083.2 of the Taxation Act, requiring contemporaneous documentation where the taxpayer's revenue exceeds CAD 10 million and related-party cross-border transactions exceed CAD 2 million in a taxation year.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the UAE or Cayman as a discrete statutory regime, but the common law economic substance doctrine is embedded in the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and section 1079 of the Quebec Taxation Act. A transaction that lacks a bona fide commercial purpose and has as a main purpose tax avoidance may be recharacterized. Canadian limited partnerships engaged in anti-dumping trade defense consulting must demonstrate real commercial substance: actual office, employees with trade-remedy expertise, Canadian professional liability insurance, and active management decisions in Quebec. The CRA may also apply the 'in-doubt' rules on partnership validity under section 96 and the 'limited partner' at-risk rules under section 96.2.1 that limit loss allocations for limited partners to their at-risk amount. Revenu Québec similarly applies GAAR under Quebec law. No ESR filing is required in Quebec, but the CRA's aggressive stance on treaty-shopping and conduit arrangements through partnerships (Canada-U.S. treaty article IV and the OECD multilateral instrument) means a bare-trust or shell LP without economic substance can be denied treaty benefits and taxed at 25% on allocable Canadian income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Blindness on Partnership Allocations

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under section 239 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec section 1021 of the Taxation Act: on conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (first offence 100%; repeat 200%) and imprisonment up to five years. Gross negligence civil penalty under subsection 163(2): 125% of the tax evaded plus arrears interest at the prescribed CRA rate (currently 9% for 2025) compounded daily. Revenu Québec imposes an additional 20% penalty for Quebec income tax evasion under section 1057.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner can be held jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) of the Income Tax Act for the partnership's unremitted withholding taxes and GST/HST, if they failed to exercise due diligence or permitted the partnership to make distributions while tax liabilities remained unpaid. Quebec section 1055 permits the same. Criminal prosecution of directors is available where they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the evasion; a travel ban can be issued under section 239.1 on certification by the CRA where a person attempts to leave Canada to evade assessment or collection. Courts may pierce the LP veil in cases of fraud or if the LP is used for an illegal or defensive purpose contrary to section 35 of the Partnership Act (Quebec) and the CRA's ability to assess a limited partner on a joint and several basis for the full partnership liability if the limited partner participates in control of the business under article 2235 of the Civil Code of Quebec.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership / Treaty-Shopping

Statutory sanction: The CRA and Revenu Québec will recharacterize the sham under the GAAR (Income Tax Act section 245; Taxation Act section 1079) and deny treaty benefits under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) and the limitation-on-benefits provisions in recent Canadian treaties. Consequence: full 25% withholding on all flow-through amounts plus gross negligence penalties of 125%; the partnership may be deemed to be a corporation under section 96(5) or 96.6 of the Income Tax Act if it has a majority of non-resident members and carries on business in Canada, forcing an entity-level tax of up to 26.7% on all income without treaty relief on distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner of a sham partnership face personal assessment for the recharacterized entity-level tax under the 'look-through' rule of article 2235 Civil Code of Quebec if they signed false statutory declarations; criminal liability for making false statements under subsection 239(1) carries imprisonment of up to five years; a director may be prohibited from leaving Canada pending criminal tax proceedings where a warrant has been issued under the Criminal Code for tax fraud.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Integration through Swift Wire and Wire Transfer Rails

Statutory sanction: For a partnership, failure to comply with FINTRAC AML obligations carries administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a first occurrence and CAD 2,000,000 for a repeat for entities with gross revenue under CAD 5 million; willful non-compliance is an indictable offence with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. For laundering proceeds of crime, Criminal Code section 462.31 imposes indictable liability of up to 10 years' imprisonment; laundering property derived from designated foreign or domestic offences triggers asset forfeiture under subsection 462.37. Under Quebec's An Act respecting certain measures to combat money laundering, the LP can have its registration cancelled and fees forfeited if it fails to identify the true beneficial owner of partnership interests.

Director personal liability: A director of a corporate general partner who knowingly signs false wire transfer instructions or fails to implement an AML compliance program is personally liable for the FINTRAC penalty and may be prosecuted for the criminal offence of laundering the proceeds of crime (Criminal Code section 462.31), carrying a maximum 10-year sentence. Directors are also subject to personal prohibition orders and travel restrictions under the Criminal Code and the Canadian Victims Bill of Rights orders where the offence involved the movement of proceeds across the Canada-U.S. border.
False Anti-Dumping Trade Defense Representations

Statutory sanction: CBSA administrative monetary penalty under the Customs Act may reach CAD 25,000 per violation for false statements; conviction for smuggling or false statements under section 153 of the Customs Act carries maximum penalty of CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for five years. The CITT can bar the firm from appearing in subsequent trade remedy proceedings. In Quebec, unauthorized practice of law by a non-lawyer is punishable under the Act respecting the Barreau du Québec: fines of up to CAD 25,000 for a first offence and up to CAD 50,000 for subsequent offences, plus an injunction and punitive damages; the partnership's business license may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false trade-defense submissions to be filed are personally liable for CBSA penalties under section 109 of the Customs Act and may be prosecuted for fraud under Criminal Code section 380 (punishable by up to 14 years' imprisonment for fraud exceeding CAD 1 million). Directors of a Quebec limited partnership that holds itself out as a law firm may be held personally liable for unauthorized practice under article 2235 Civil Code of Quebec and for punitive damages.
Thin Capitalization / Excessive Interest Deduction

Statutory sanction: Interest deduction disallowed entirely, with a resulting reassessment of the limited partnership income and attribution of the disallowed amount to non-resident partners as dividends taxable at 25% withholding. Revenu Québec applies analogous thin capitalization rules under section 21.1 of the Taxation Act. CRA may impose the 10% transfer pricing penalty on the disallowed interest if contemporaneous documentation is absent. The Quebec administrative penalty for failure to report a foreign-source loan on Form TP-1081.R.C.5 is CAD 1,000 per month, and a false statement penalty of 50% of the tax avoided under section 1045.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign loan agreements they know to be sham can be assessed for the withholding tax on the deemed dividend under subsection 227.1, and may face fraud charges if they certified false financial statements to the lender or the CRA.
