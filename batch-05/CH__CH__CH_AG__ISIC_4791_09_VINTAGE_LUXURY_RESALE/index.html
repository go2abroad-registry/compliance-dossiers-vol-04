Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The AG requires minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (CO Art. 621). At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid in cash, but in no event less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, before registration (CO Art. 632). The funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a licensed Swiss bank in the company's name; the bank issues a payment confirmation and the board retains no unrestricted right of disposal until registration. Contributions in kind (Sacheinlage) require a special audit report and disclosure in the deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the cantonal fire police and Gebaeudeversicherung in accordance with VKF / AEAI standards; structural load inspection confirming floor capacity for high-value storage racks; emergency escape and extinguisher scheme; civil defense obligations under cantonal civil protection legislation; and occupational health and safety clearance under the Labour Act (ArG), including SUVA employer registration and mandatory accident insurance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), official portal www.estv.admin.ch, with the e-service portal 'MeinSwissVAT' (https://mein.swissvat.ch) for VAT registration, filing and refunds; federal/communal tax administration of the cantonal seat applies for direct taxes.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG automatically obtains its UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Identificateur d'entreprise / Identificatore d'impresa, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) upon entry in the cantonal commercial register (Handelsregister / Registre du commerce / Registro di commercio) and publication in the Swiss Official Gazette (SHAB/FOSC/FUSC); the UID, issued by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office, serves as the statutory TIN and as the VAT identification number once VAT registration is approved. VAT registration is filed with the ESTV via MeinSwissVAT using Form 'Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuer' accompanied by the articles of incorporation, commercial register excerpt and turnover projections; foreign entities without a Swiss registered seat must demonstrate Swiss taxable nexus and may be required to appoint a Swiss fiscal representative before the ESTV grants a CHE UID/VAT number, with processing typically completed within 15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections. Available mechanisms include: (i) the participation exemption (Beteiligungsabzug / déduction pour participation) automatically applied on the tax return for qualifying dividends and capital gains (minimum 10% or CHF 1 million fair value participation held for 12 months); (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) elections in certain cantons (e.g., Zurich, Geneva); (iii) VAT group election (Mehrwertsteuer-Gruppenbesteuerung) integrating closely held Swiss entities as one VAT taxpayer; (iv) advance tax rulings (Steuervorbescheid / ruling) with the cantonal tax administration and ESTV for hybrid financing and cross-border structures; (v) transitional privileged statuses (holding, domicile, auxiliary companies) which are being phased out from 2025 for multinational groups in scope of the Swiss Minimum Tax Act implementing the OECD Global Minimum Tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding VAT) in the preceding business year; no de minimis relief for foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Switzerland, except where the Swiss recipient applies the reverse-charge mechanism; small Swiss businesses below CHF 100,000 may elect voluntary VAT registration; foreign e-commerce operators selling goods into Switzerland to private consumers are registrable regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to goods physically exported from Switzerland to foreign territories when the AG proves that the goods left Swiss customs territory as a result of the supply; the supplier must be the exporter of record and the goods must be transported abroad. Services directly related to exported movable goods, freight forwarding, storage, and intermediary services supplied to foreign principals are zero-rated or fall outside Swiss VAT scope under the B2B reverse-charge principle. For online vintage luxury resale, the 0% rate requires physical shipment from Switzerland to the foreign customer's destination; supplies fulfilled from a foreign warehouse (third-party fulfilment) are not zero-rated and may create destination-country VAT obligations.

  • Customs export declaration (e-dec / SafeBorder) bearing the export registration number (Zollanmeldung / déclaration d'exportation / dichiarazione di esportazione)
  • Transport document — Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR/CIM consignment note evidencing departure from Switzerland and arrival at the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, online sales order or consignment contract and, where applicable, the vintage luxury authentication certificate establishing the secondhand good's provenance and value
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance certificate (SWIFT bank confirmation, Stripe settlement report or Escrow disbursement record) showing receipt from the foreign buyer
  • Carrier logistics report with tracking events and signed delivery acknowledgement for low-value parcel consignments
  • Insurance certificate and/or certificate of origin for high-value luxury items as value-supporting evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-authorisation or digital stamping regime exists in Switzerland. E-invoices are valid provided authenticity of origin and integrity of content are preserved via recognised electronic transmission, qualified electronic signature, or EDI/secure interchange agreement; Swiss QR-bill and eBill formats are accepted. From 1 January 2026, suppliers to the Swiss federal administration must transmit structured e-invoices through the SwissPeppol/Peppol network. VAT returns are filed quarterly or semi-annually through MeinSwissVAT; payment is due within 60 days of the reporting period. No continuous transaction-control (CTC) API obligation applies.

Swiss VAT law requires the taxpayer to retain zero-rating evidence in business records; insufficient export substantiation triggers VAT reassessment at 8.1%, default interest at 4.5% p.a. (5% as from 1 January 2025) and potential administrative fines up to CHF 800,000 for intentional tax evasion under Art. 100 MWSTG. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax fraud under Art. 101 MWSTG, sanctionable by imprisonment up to 3 years or monetary penalty, plus retrospective clawbacks with a 10-year assessment period in cases of intent. Although Swiss exchange-control rules do not impose a statutory 180-day FX-repatriation deadline, inward remittance within 180 days is the documentary benchmark accepted by the ESTV on audit for cross-border e-commerce; missing or contradictory payment evidence invites full risk-based audits, retroactive VAT clawbacks and publication of penalty decisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc for both current and capital account transactions. There is no general foreign exchange control law, no exchange licensing regime, and no restriction on dividend distributions, loan repatriation, or liquidation proceeds for domestic Aktiengesellschaften. Profit remittance is subject only to Swiss corporate income taxation and the 35% withholding tax on dividends, which may be reduced under an applicable double tax treaty. Bank transfers in CHF, EUR, USD, and other major currencies are freely permitted, subject to AML/KYC screening, sanctions screening, and counterparty due diligence by the receiving or instructing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. Exporters of vintage luxury goods, including online consignment resellers, may retain foreign currency proceeds in Swiss bank accounts or in accounts abroad without obligation to convert into CHF. The only constraints arise from sanctions, FINMA circulars on liquidity management, and internal bank policy on foreign-currency position limits.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) in conjunction with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF); foreign exchange administration is liberalized under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA), with prudential supervision by FINMA and sanctions enforcement by SECO.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium viability for offshore fintech EMI and digital banking providers. They may serve as complementary payment collection rails for Stripe, PayPal, and Escrow Pay settlement, but they cannot act as the primary treasury account for capital escrow verification under Swiss company law (CO Art. 634). A FINMA-licensed or FINMA-recognized Swiss bank must hold the registered share capital. Offshore EMIs are generally permissive as multi-currency operational conduits, but they trigger enhanced AML review, may lack direct SIC/SIC4 access, and are not suitable for large-value CHF clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital contributions are made through ordinary SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the Swiss capital escrow account. Remittance information must identify the purpose as 'Share capital contribution under Art. 632 and 633 CO' and reference the company name. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law does not prescribe a fixed de minimis threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File filing. However, Switzerland applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as interpretive authority and requires taxpayers to maintain and produce arm's-length documentation when requested by the tax administration. A Swiss constituent entity of a group with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more is subject to Country-by-Country Reporting: Swiss constituent entities of the group must file a CbC notification with the ESTV, and the ultimate parent entity, if Swiss resident, must file the full CbC report. In audit practice, material cross-border related-party transactions of any size — particularly vintage luxury resale stock purchased from or sold to related persons — must be robustly documented.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a statutory Economic Substance Regulations regime analogous to the EU or Caribbean ESR systems. Nevertheless, economic substance is essential for Swiss corporate residence, treaty entitlement, participation exemption, or withholding-tax relief. An AG whose board of directors, corporate records, business premises, bank accounts, and actual decision-making are demonstrably present in Switzerland is treated as a Swiss tax resident. A 'letter-box' AG directing the e-commerce luxury resale business from abroad will be re-characterised as managed from abroad; its Swiss residence may be denied, its foreign-related advantages revoked, and its intra-group payments reclassified as constructive dividends or hidden profit distributions subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income tax evasion and false documentation

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of omitted profit plus default interest; administrative tax-evasion penalty generally equal to the tax evaded and up to three times that amount where aggravated, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Taxation with imprisonment and fines. VAT evasion additionally triggers penalties and forfeiture of unlawfully avoided VAT.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under Swiss debt-enforcement law for wilful depletion of corporate assets during tax-enforcement proceedings; fraudulent concealment or falsification can result in criminal fines, imprisonment, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and extradition requests for international cooperation.
Shell company / sham substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Revocation of Swiss tax residence or re-attribution of taxable profits to the place of effective management; denial of treaty benefits and participation exemption; hidden distributions recharacterized and subjected to 35% WHT plus interest; closure of the company under commercial-registry procedure where the registered office no longer corresponds to substance; fines and sanctions for circuitous arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several liability under Swiss Code of Obligations Art. 754 for damage caused by the sham construction, including restitution of tax advantages with interest, civil claims by creditors, disqualification from board service, travel bans in criminal proceedings, and potential confiscation of personal benefits obtained from the shell structure.
Transfer pricing mispricing / profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment by the Swiss tax administration, disallowance of the excessive expense, reclassification of the difference as a constructive dividend, 35% withholding tax plus default interest, and administrative fine for insufficient or false documents; taxpayer may also face contested assessment through the Swiss Federal Administrative Court if documentation remains inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise non-arm's-length cross-border agreements can be held personally liable for resulting unpaid Swiss taxes if they knowingly exposed the company to tax fraud; veil piercing is available where the AG is used as a mere alter ego to drain profits overseas. Civil damages can include retroactive restitution of tax, interest, and procedural costs, and travel bans can be ordered where criminal charges for fiscal avoidance are investigated.
AML/escrow compliance failures and payment-rail abuse

Statutory sanction: If the AG acts as a financial intermediary or is otherwise subject to Swiss AMLA, violations attract regulatory sanctions, fines, and closure orders; money laundering under the Swiss Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment and a fine, and the underlying assets — including luxury goods held for resale — are subject to confiscation. Sectoral licensing may be required if escrow activity is construed as financial intermediation.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in or tolerating money-laundering structures may be personally prosecuted for money laundering or lack of due diligence, resulting in imprisonment, discovery of personal bank accounts and travel bans, asset freezing, disqualification and civil restitution of profits received; directors may also be ordered to repay the company for fines, penalties, and legal defence costs arising from their breach of duty.
