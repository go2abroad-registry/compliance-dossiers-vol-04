Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online portals. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and export reporting. Ontario corporate income tax is administered through the CRA under the federal-provincial tax administration framework.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for corporations. A Canadian corporation is generally a separate taxable person and files a T2 corporate income tax return. Certain legal forms, including some partnerships, trusts, and unlimited liability entities, may have different classification or filing consequences, but classification is determined under Canadian law and applicable treaty principles rather than by a universal elective form. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose income inclusions for specified deduction/non-inclusion and imported mismatch arrangements; they do not provide a general elective pass-through classification. A corporation may make specific statutory elections, including elections relevant to tax-deferred transfers, capital cost allowance, foreign-affiliate transactions, and GST/HST methods, but those elections do not convert an ordinary corporation into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, measured under the applicable small-supplier rules over the relevant preceding four-calendar-quarter period or in a single calendar quarter. The threshold generally applies on an associated-person and entity-group basis where required. Registration may be voluntary below the threshold. Special rules can require registration for certain non-residents, digital-economy suppliers, taxi and commercial ride services, and other prescribed activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the supplier obtains and retains evidence that the goods were exported under the applicable Excise Tax Act rules and were not consumed, used, or supplied in Canada before export. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated only where the specific statutory conditions are satisfied. The analysis is service-specific: the recipient's non-resident status must be established, the service must generally be supplied to or for the benefit of the non-resident, and exclusions must be tested, including services relating to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, services physically performed in Canada for certain purposes, personal services, litigation-related services, and services connected with use or consumption in Canada. Automated subtitle transcription, post-production, dubbing, and AI voice services supplied to a qualifying non-resident may potentially qualify, but the contract, customer location, performance, deliverables, rights of use, and Canadian use or exploitation must be documented and analyzed separately. Export zero-rating does not eliminate the requirement to register where the business makes taxable supplies and does not itself establish entitlement to an input tax refund. GST/HST registrants generally recover eligible GST/HST through input tax credits on their return; non-residents may have separate rebate mechanisms only where statutory conditions apply.

  • Canadian Export Reporting System export declaration or other prescribed CBSA export reporting record, where the goods and transaction are subject to export-reporting requirements
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, purchaser or consignee, description, quantity, value, currency, destination, and terms of sale
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier-issued transport evidence
  • Proof of delivery and proof of export, including carrier tracking, export manifest, customs exit confirmation, or foreign import documentation where available
  • Purchase order, executed contract, customer correspondence, and evidence establishing the non-resident status and foreign destination of the recipient
  • For exported services, service agreements, work orders, deliverables, time records, customer-location evidence, usage or exploitation evidence, invoices, payment records, and evidence that the service is not excluded from zero-rating
  • Foreign customs entry or import record where relevant to demonstrate importation into the destination country
  • Inward remittance or bank payment records where relevant as commercial evidence; Canada does not generally prescribe an Inward Remittance Certificate as a GST/HST zero-rating document

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance, mandatory tax-stamping, or continuous transaction-control requirement for ordinary Ontario business-to-business invoices. A GST/HST registrant must issue compliant invoices or receipts when required, showing sufficient information to support tax collection and input tax credits, including the supplier identity and BN/GST/HST account number where applicable, date, description, consideration, tax rate, and GST/HST amount or indication of tax treatment. Electronic records and invoices are permitted if they remain accessible, reliable, readable, and retained for the statutory record-retention period. CRA may require electronic filing or electronic payment for specified registrants and returns, but this is not the same as invoice pre-clearance. CBSA export and import declarations use prescribed electronic systems, including the CARM Client Portal and the Canadian Export Reporting System where applicable.

Canada has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for GST/HST export zero-rating. Export proceeds may generally remain offshore, subject to ordinary corporate tax, transfer-pricing, foreign-exchange reporting, anti-money-laundering, customs, and banking requirements. CRA may reassess incorrectly zero-rated supplies as taxable, collect the unpaid GST/HST, deny related input tax credits or rebates, and impose arrears interest and applicable penalties. Penalties may include failure-to-file or failure-to-remit penalties, gross-negligence penalties generally calculated at 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit attributable to gross negligence, and other prescribed penalties. False statements, fabricated export evidence, obstruction, or intentional tax evasion may create civil penalties and criminal exposure, including fines and imprisonment. CBSA may separately assess customs penalties, duties, interest, and administrative monetary penalties for inaccurate or unsupported export or import declarations. Records should be retained for the statutory period, generally six years from the end of the relevant tax year, or longer where an objection, appeal, reassessment, or statutory exception applies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital accounts. Corporate profits, dividends, and capital repatriation face no statutory barriers post-taxation. Profit remittance is permitted with only standard withholding tax (25% on dividends to non-residents) and mandatory reporting via CRS/FEAT for non-resident beneficial owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under Canadian law. Receipts must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) within prescribed timelines (typically 30 days for certain transactions exceeding CAD 10,000). Voluntary surrender to the Bank of Canada is available for foreign exchange control purposes but not statutorily mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
