E-government filing portal

STEP 2: Register for QST with Revenu Québec through Clic Revenu and obtain the NEQ (enterprise number) from the Registraire des entreprises before commencing Quebec business activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — For Canadian federal and Quebec provincial income tax, the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent (Income Tax Act s. 96; Quebec Taxation Act parallel): income and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners each fiscal period; the LP itself pays no income tax. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Quebec through the LP are taxed on their share of Canadian-source business income (Part I tax by filing a s. 115 return) and may face Part XIII withholding on certain Canadian-source distributions. For GST/HST and QST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate taxable 'person' and must hold its own registrations and remit GST and QST on its taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box entity classification election; LP pass-through status is mandatory for income tax and no election permits corporate taxation of the LP. Available statutory elections include: (a) GST/HST joint election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 and the corresponding QST election to zero-rate supplies between closely related registrants; (b) s. 150 ETA 'nil consideration' election among closely related persons; (c) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 permitting a non-calendar partnership year-end where allowed; (d) flexible partnership income/loss allocation under the partnership agreement subject to ITA s. 96 and Quebec reasonableness rules; (e) for US partners, a US Form 8832 check-the-box election may be relevant for US purposes only and does not modify Canadian or Quebec classification; hybrid-mismatch elections are not available to override the domestic pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold: mandatory registration arises when worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in total over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident LP that merely purchases goods in Canada and exports them without making a supply in Canada is generally not a registrant, whereas a non-resident carrying on business in Canada (e.g., storing goods or delivering to Canadian customers) must register once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (drummed and bagged penetration-grade bitumen) exported from Canada qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 (supply of property shipped or delivered outside Canada by or on behalf of the supplier, or transferred to a common carrier for export), and are correspondingly zero-rated for QST. To sustain zero-rating, the LP must prove the goods were actually exported in the ordinary course of business, typically by showing delivery to a common carrier, vessel loading at a Quebec port, customs clearance, and the foreign buyer's receipt. Auxiliary services supplied to a non-resident in connection with the exported goods (e.g., ocean freight and logistics performed outside Canada) may also be zero-rated where the conditions of the place-of-supply rules are met.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / ACI eManifest export report) for commercial goods exported from Canada where the shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or regulated
  • Ocean / through Bill of Lading, air waybill, or intermodal carrier document naming the foreign port of discharge and evidencing containerized movement from a Quebec loading point (e.g., Port of Montreal)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or telegraphic transfer advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer within the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark
  • Commercial invoice (with explicit zero-rating reference), packing list, container seal and tracking report, plus letter-of-credit or purchase-order documents matching the quantity, grade, and destination of the bitumen shipment

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance API, or digital stamping regime applies in Canada or Quebec; the CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper, PDF, and EDI invoices without government pre-approval. Mandates are limited to electronic filing and remittance: GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and Revenu Québec requires electronic filing and remittance of QST/GST returns for large Quebec businesses through Clic Revenu using the unified return. Invoice content rules under ETA ss. 169/223 and the QST Act require particulars (supplier name/BN, recipient, description, consideration, tax status, and zero-rating reference for exports), but no government digital stamp or real-time clearance API is in force.

Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard documentary-credit, bank, and CRA/Revenu Québec substantiation practice for proving genuine export proceeds. The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the four-year normal limitation period, and the limitation window is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud, wilful default, or neglect/carelessness, permitting retrospective clawback of denied zero-rating. If the export evidence fails, supplies are re-characterized as taxable at the combined 14.5% GST/QST rate with daily compound interest, plus penalties: (i) failure-to-register penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the consideration for taxable supplies; (ii) gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 and the QST Act equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax deficiency derived from false statements; and (iii) criminal sanctions under ETA s. 327 for wilful evasion, with fines of 50%–200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Exporters also face CBSA administrative monetary penalties for late, false, or inaccurate export declarations, and denial of input tax refunds (ITCs/RTDs) supporting the export chain.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital-account and current-account convertibility. All exchange controls were dismantled in 1951; no prior approval, licensing, or quota applies to inward capital injections, outward trade settlements, dividend/partnership distributions, or liquidation proceeds. Non-residents may hold CAD or foreign currency freely and repatriate profits at spot market rates. The only statutory hurdles are: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced) on taxable partnership allocations paid or credited to non-resident limited partners; (ii) the Investment Canada Act notification requirement for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, which does not apply to a passive wholesale LP below threshold review; (iii) Quebec's QST registration and filing obligations, which do not restrict conversion. The CAD floats independently; no central-bank approval is required for any individual FX transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Canada. The CA_LP may hold, net, offset, or on-lend bitumen-asphalt export receipts in any freely convertible currency without statutory ratio or deadline. Mandatory compliance duties are: (i) FINTRAC reporting of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) CBSA customs declarations and correct 10-digit HS classification (2713.20 for penetration-grade bitumen) on outbound containerized movements; (iii) bank-level sanctions screening, with the CA_LP maintaining supplier/customer evidence to prove no trade with listed jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and fiscal agent to the Minister of Finance for administration of the Exchange Fund Account. Exchange-control policy authority rests with the Department of Finance Canada and is delegated operationally to financial institutions supervised by OSFI. FINTRAC enforces AML/ATF transaction reporting, and the Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) supervises the Quebec securities and derivatives framework relevant to the CA_LP.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal Part I rate 15.0% (ITA s.123(1)(a)) plus Quebec provincial rate 11.5% (TAQ s.771), for a combined headline rate of 26.5% on taxable corporate income. The subject entity is a Quebec limited partnership (societe en commandite) that is fiscally transparent: no income tax is payable at the LP level, and each partner is taxable on its allocable share as computed in the T5013/TP-601 return. A non-resident corporate partner carrying on business through the Quebec permanent establishment is taxed at the same combined 26.5% on net taxable income attributable to the PE.
