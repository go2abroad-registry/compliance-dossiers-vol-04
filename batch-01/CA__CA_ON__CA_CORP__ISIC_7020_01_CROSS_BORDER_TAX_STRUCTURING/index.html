Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) administers federal income tax, GST/HST, and the Ontario Corporation Tax under a federal-provincial collection agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Finance retains legislative oversight. Official portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; login at canada.ca/my-cra-account).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) comprising a 9-digit base and a 2-letter program account suffix (RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RM for import/export). Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or, for non-residents, Form RC111 together with the Non-Resident GST/HST Registration application; (2) submit online through CRA My Business Account or by calling the CRA Business Registration line (1-866-588-0320 for non-residents; 1-800-267-6997 domestically); (3) open the GST/HST account (RT), payroll (RP), customs (RM), and corporate tax (RC) accounts under the same BN; (4) Ontario corporate tax registration is automatic upon filing the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return; no minimum capital requirement applies; non-resident ownership is permitted without restriction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident branch/conduit). A Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income; the combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate is 25% (15% federal + 10% Ontario), while active business income eligible for the small business deduction (up to CAD 500,000) is taxed at 12.2% (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario). Non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income (Part I) or passive income subject to Part XIII withholding; capital gains realized by corporations are subject to a two-thirds inclusion rate for dispositions after June 24, 2024. — A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) comprising a 9-digit base and a 2-letter program account suffix (RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax, RM for import/export). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident branch/conduit). A Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income; the combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate is 25% (15% federal + 10% Ontario), while active business income eligible for the small business deduction (up to CAD 500,000) is taxed at 12.2% (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario). Non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income (Part I) or passive income subject to Part XIII withholding; capital gains realized by corporations are subject to a two-thirds inclusion rate for dispositions after June 24, 2024.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election. Statutory elections available include ITA s. 216 (electing net Part I treatment for Canadian real-property rentals), s. 217 (electing to file Part I instead of Part XIII withholding on certain passive income), s. 256.1(1) (elected deemed residency of a corporation incorporated outside Canada), s. 93(2) (FDA election converting foreign affiliate surplus to taxable dividends), and s. 202 (non-resident election in respect of Canadian securities). Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed by mandatory, non-elective rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 12.7(1) (BEPS Action 2), which disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes and reverse imported mismatches. For US LLCs, CRA maintains an administrative position treating the LLC as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes unless it clearly lacks separate juridical existence under US law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148, measured on worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada, or making taxable supplies in Canada (including cross-border tax-advisory services deemed supplied in Canada under place-of-supply rules), must register for GST/HST once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is open to non-residents for input tax credit recovery, including the simplified Non-Resident GST/HST Registration regime for digital and consulting services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under s. 1 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when exported by the supplier under documentary proof of removal from Canada. Services, including international tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory, are zero-rated under s. 7 of Schedule VI where supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Electronically delivered advisory services with an offshore recipient and no Canadian situs asset nexus qualify; services connected to Canadian situs property or a Canadian permanent establishment do not. While no statutory FX repatriation deadline exists in Canada, the CRA audit benchmark expects consideration to be received within 180 days of supply; foreign receivables older than one year may be recharacterized as domestic taxable supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canada Customs Commercial Declaration submitted via CAED/ARRAS or CBSA e-manifest)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing export movement from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency settlement of the export consideration
  • Signed cross-border service agreement and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident recipient, scope of tax-structuring advice, and place-of-supply rationale
  • Evidence of the recipient's non-resident, non-registered status (e.g., foreign tax registration excerpt, non-residency certificate)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices meeting prescribed information requirements (registrant name, BN/RT suffix, invoice date, recipient name, taxable consideration, tax rate, HST payable, and zero-rated/exempt status) but may use paper, PDF, or EDI. The CRA-endorsed Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol-compliant, administered by the Digital Governance Council) is voluntary and imposes no clearance obligation.

CRA audit reviews the full evidentiary chain for each zero-rated export; missing or inconsistent documentation results in reassessment at 13% HST plus arrears interest at the quarterly prescribed rate, with a normal reassessment window of 4 years (extended to 6 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud). Deficiency penalties include Excise Tax Act s. 285 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax attempted to be hidden), s. 280(1) failure-to-collect penalty (5% of the amount plus 7% per month up to 12 months), and s. 281.1 refund/rebate misrepresentation penalty (25% of the claimed amount). Tax advisors who counsel fraudulent export zero-rating face Income Tax Act s. 163.2(4) gross-negligence penalties (greater of CAD 10,000 and 100% of fees earned); criminal tax evasion is punishable by fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years under ETA s. 329(1) and ITA s. 239(1).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions; inward equity injections, dividend remittances, royalties, interest, management fees, and loan repayments require no central-bank authorization. Actual remittance hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: 25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and specified payments (treaty-reducible, e.g., 5% under Canada–US treaty for 10% corporate shareholders; 15% otherwise), Part XIV branch tax on unremitted non-resident branch profits, thin-capitalization constraints (1.5:1 debt-to-equity under s. 18(4) Income Tax Act), and mandatory information returns for foreign affiliates and specified foreign property (Form T1134 / T1135).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; foreign-currency proceeds may be retained indefinitely in Canadian-chartered or foreign bank accounts. Any CAD conversion settles through Lynx (Payments Canada high-value payment system) without a mandated holding period or conversion percentage.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); currency administration is decentralized — OSFI supervises chartered bank prudential conduct, FINTRAC administers AML/CFT compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions lists. No approval-based foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — legally constrained. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and digital cross-border payment firms cannot take deposits or conduct banking business in Canada without a chartered bank licence under the Bank Act. A non-resident EMI may operate only indirectly as a FINTRAC-registered money services business or through an OSFI-regulated domestic partnership. Such rails are viable for ancillary FX conversion, payout orchestration, and payment acceleration, but cannot replace a Tier-1 Canadian bank account for regulatory trust and treasury-grade CAD clearing.
