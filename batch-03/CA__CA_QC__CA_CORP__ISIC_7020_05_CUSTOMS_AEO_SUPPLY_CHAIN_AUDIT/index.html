Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and federal corporate income tax, with portal 'My Business Account' at canada.ca; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST, Quebec corporate income tax, and payroll, with portal 'Mon dossier d’entreprise' at revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity: (1) determine whether a Canadian permanent establishment or taxable supply nexus exists; (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number and GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; (3) apply to Revenu Québec for a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST account via the RQ business portal or Form LM-1; (4) submit certified corporate incorporation documents, head-office address, foreign tax identification evidence, and non-resident status proof; (5) appoint a tax representative or mandatory for service of notices in Quebec if no physical establishment exists; (6) activate CRA/RQ online accounts after kiosk/agent verification and retain all registration letters.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a CBCA or QBCA business corporation is an opaque, Canadian-resident taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax (Part I of the Income Tax Act) and Quebec provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no default pass-through treatment for a standard business corporation. — For a non-resident foreign entity: (1) determine whether a Canadian permanent establishment or taxable supply nexus exists; (2) apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number and GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online; (3) apply to Revenu Québec for a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST account via the RQ business portal or Form LM-1; (4) submit certified corporate incorporation documents, head-office address, foreign tax identification evidence, and non-resident status proof; (5) appoint a tax representative or mandatory for service of notices in Quebec if no physical establishment exists; (6) activate CRA/RQ online accounts after kiosk/agent verification and retain all registration letters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a CBCA or QBCA business corporation is an opaque, Canadian-resident taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax (Part I of the Income Tax Act) and Quebec provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no default pass-through treatment for a standard business corporation.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election under federal or Quebec law; a Business Corporation is statutorily treated as an entity-level taxpayer. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are codified in Income Tax Act sections 94B to 94G and cannot be elected away. For GST/HST and QST, limited administrative elections exist for closely related corporations (e.g., nil-consideration or stream-related elections), but they affect tax remittance mechanics rather than legal entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — general small-supplier registration threshold under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act for both GST/HST and QST (worldwide taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a person ceases to be a small supplier in the first calendar quarter following the $30,000 threshold). Non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada or Quebec above this threshold must register; voluntary registration below threshold is available to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when sold and exported outside Canada in accordance with Excise Tax Act sections 22 and 23 and the parallel QST rules, including immediate export by the non-resident purchaser, delivery to a carrier for direct export, or shipment to a destination outside Canada. Services, including AEO supply-chain compliance and security audit services, are zero-rated for GST/HST and QST when supplied to a non-resident recipient and not performed in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or for consumption or use in Canada; the offshore audit engagement must be substantiated by contract, deliverable location, and the non-resident status of the client.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A/eManifest export report) for physical goods; service contracts and engagement letters for offshore supply-chain audit services
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill demonstrating export to a foreign destination
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada (carrier records, consignee acknowledgement, customs stamps)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank confirmation evidencing export proceeds received from abroad
  • Non-resident recipient status declaration/certificate and work papers documenting the offshore nature of the AEO audit services

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime applies in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST and QST registrants are required to file returns electronically and remit through CRA and Revenu Québec portals respectively. Invoices must satisfy prescribed information and record retention rules; customs/export reporting is effected electronically through the CBSA eManifest (API/portal) and, where applicable, the Canadian Export Reporting System.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and retroactively claw back zero-rated treatment on identified exports, denying input tax credits/refunds and assessing unremitted GST/HST or QST with interest. False statements or misrepresentations attract statutory penalties of 25% to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, including gross-negligence penalties; wilful evasion is subject to criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. Reassessment windows normally extend 4 years, with no limitation period in cases of fraud or misrepresentation. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but sustained failure to evidence foreign proceeds within 180 days increases the likelihood of documentary challenges to export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating G10 currency fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada abolished its foreign-exchange control regime in 1951 with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act, and the Currency Act imposes no restrictions on holding, converting, or repatriating foreign currency. Profit remittance to non-residents is unrestricted at the exchange-control level but is subject to tax compliance: Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends (reduced to 5% for a qualifying direct dividend recipient under the Canada-United States tax treaty), NR4 information reporting, and, for a Quebec corporation, the solvency test in section 38 of the Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA) before any dividend declaration. An ISIC 7020 AEO audit firm may hold, convert, and invoice in USD or EUR without Bank of Canada approval, and there is no mandatory local-reinvestment requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any timeline. For the ISIC 7020 AEO certification and security-auditing service line, there are no physical exports of goods; consequently, no CBSA export declaration is required for the professional services themselves. For any ancillary movement of goods, the Export Reporting Regulations require an electronic export declaration via the Canadian International Trade Single Window when the shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 (CAD 2,500 for non-controlled goods destined to the United States; CAD 1,000 for controlled goods). All cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank within 15 days, and the audited entity must retain currency-conversion and receipt documentation for CRA review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank responsible for monetary policy, currency issuance, and payment-system oversight; prudential regulation of banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); retail payment service providers are registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs, trusted-trader programs, and AEO mutual-recognition arrangements; in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates derivatives and securities-related banking products.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is Moderate-High for Wise (operating in Canada through Wise Canada Ltd.) as a secondary collection and low-value disbursement rail, but the European EMI licence class does not exist in Canada. The functional equivalents are FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) or foreign money services business (FMSB), plus registration as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada (regime in force since November 1, 2024). Wise Canada Ltd. is FINTRAC-registered and suitable for multicurrency supplier invoices below CAD 10,000 per transaction, but it cannot provide a regulated in-trust escrow account, commercial credit facilities, or direct access to the Payments Canada Lynx high-value clearing system; accordingly, the principal treasury account and all client AEO audit deposits must sit with a Schedule I bank to satisfy CBSA PIP/AEO trust-condition expectations and the professional standards of the Chartered Professional Accountants of Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Instruct the non-resident investor to transmit a SWIFT MT103 to the designated Schedule I bank in Quebec; populate field 50K with the investor name, field 56A with the correspondent bank (e.g., RBC New York or BMO Harris), field 59 with the corporate beneficiary name and CRA business number, and field 70 with the payment purpose message 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / EQUITY INJECTION' together with the corporate resolution number. Funds clear through the Payments Canada Lynx high-value system for same-day CAD value; the receiving bank will automatically report the electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, execute a written loan agreement, file Form T106 when the in-year aggregate transactions with the non-resident exceed CAD 1 million, and keep the debt-to-equity ratio at or below 1.5:1 to avoid disallowed interest under subsection 18(4) of the Income Tax Act; record the receipt as equity or due-to-shareholder subordinated debt so that it cannot be re-characterized as taxable consulting revenue.For cross-border vendor settlements, issue SWIFT MT103 payment orders (or MT202COV for interbank covering transfers); for documentary trade, request the issuing bank to transmit an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit via SWIFT MT700 governed by UCP600. Canadian domestic payables are routed through Payments Canada EFT or the Real-Time Rail with CRA-approved reference fields; every outbound electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC. For US-dollar payables, exploit the bank's New York or US clearing lane (for example BMO's USD window or National Bank's correspondent arrangement) to achieve next-day value. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 5 million for other transactions (T106 filing threshold).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for transactions. There is no specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions, but the CRA can challenge transactions lacking commercial substance under GAAR or transfer pricing rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239). Penalties: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (s. 227.1). Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, reassess taxes, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. Criminal charges for tax evasion if intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), penalties up to CAD 2 million for administrative violations, and criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for laundering proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines and imprisonment if they knowingly participated in or failed to prevent AML violations. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may occur.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation not provided), and charge interest on reassessed taxes. Criminal charges for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they signed false returns or knowingly failed to ensure compliance. Civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
