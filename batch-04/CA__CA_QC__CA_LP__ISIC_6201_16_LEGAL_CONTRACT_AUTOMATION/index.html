Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership; contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement and no escrow account is required. Subscription amounts may be deposited to the partnership's bank account upon formal admission as a limited partner.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for cloud data center operations; if the partnership leases an office, the landlord must supply fire safety certificate and building occupancy inspection. The partnership must ensure the workstation complies with CNESST workplace safety requirements (electrical safety, ergonomics, emergency exits).

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The General Partner of the limited partnership, acting in accordance with the partnership agreement, resolves that the partnership adopts the name; accepts the initial capital contributions subscribed; designates the registered office address and appoints the initial general partner and designated managers; opens bank accounts for deposit of capital; and authorizes the filing of the declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and all required tax registrations. The General Partner further resolves to engage counsel for compliance of the legal publicity duties and to adopt the partnership governance rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and federal income tax administration; Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec income tax administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec Clavier d'accès (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-sourced limited partnership doing business in Quebec, obtain an income tax and GST/HST Business Number (BN) from the CRA through registration by filing the appropriate non-resident partnership election and registration package (CRA Form RC158 or RC160 as applicable; online registration through CRA's Business Registration Online portal). Apply for a separate Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) with the Registraire des entreprises and register for QST with Revenu Québec using prescribed forms (e.g., Form SRZ-101, LM-1); provide partnership agreement, designation of attorney, and place of business in Quebec. Once the BN is issued, active registrations for GST/HST and QST are added; foreign entities are required to designate a tax representative in Canada if they do not have a permanent establishment but carry on commercial activity in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. An eligible limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent; income is allocated to partners and taxed at partner level. Specific elections include: (i) partnership year-end election under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act to select a non-fiscal non-calendar year end; (ii) filing of Form RC164 (Residency Election) for partnerships receiving U.S.-source income; (iii) subsections 152(4) and 96(3) elections for partnership allocations, including specified partnership income allocation; (iv) elective relief under Part IX.2 SIFT partnership rules for publicly traded foreign-source income; and (v) use of Canadian-controlled private corporation characterization elections for partner corporations, though these operate at the corporate partner level. Hybrid mismatch consequences are limited by Canadian tax treaties, the Income Tax Act foreign tax credit mechanisms, and the OECD hybrid mismatch rules implemented in sections 91.1 and 93.2 as well as various BEPS Action 2 measures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annually in worldwide taxable supplies connected with Canada for GST/HST small supplier exemption; symmetry applies for QST under An Act respecting the Québec sales tax, with a CAD 30,000 Quebec taxable supply threshold. A non-resident partnership must nevertheless register if it carries on a commercial activity in Canada or Quebec and exceeds the threshold; GST/HST and QST registration is mandatory regardless of threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies of intangible personal property to Canadian recipients who are not registered? No — generally the threshold applies unless the supply is a facility supply, registerable financial institution or telecommunication/transmission supply. Professional legal-tech SaaS supplied remotely may require mandatory registration if the non-resident makes supplies to Quebec consumers through a Canadian retail platform where threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 1-10 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, zero-rating applies to (i) supplies of goods exported directly out of Canada by the supplier, (ii) supplies of goods exported by a common carrier where possession is delivered abroad, (iii) supplies of services performed wholly outside Canada, (iv) services supplied to a non-resident non-registered person when the supply is not in relation to real or tangible property in Canada (or is in relation to outside-Canada property), and (v) certain intangible property supplies, including SaaS/cloud automation solutions, made to a non-resident non-registered recipient. For QST, Articles 1-3 of Schedule II to the Quebec Sales Tax mirror federal zero-rating, with cross-border export of professional/cloud services exempt when the recipient is outside Canada and the property or service is for use wholly outside Quebec.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency CAD/AD 600 series, through Canadian Export Reporting System for non-licensed exporters) or validated expedited export report
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing onboard/on-carrier dispatch
  • Commercial Invoice showing value, ISIC service description, and export destination
  • Foreign-exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-contract evidence proving crediting of export proceeds to the Canadian-partner foreign account
  • Proof of payment and substantiation of electronic service export (software access logs, digital contract, IP confirmation, domain or user location evidence)

No legislative real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is in force federally or in Quebec. Registrants must issue and keep electronic copies of invoices in an electronically readable format; large businesses must file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) and Revenu Québec mandates electronic transmission of QST remittances for large registrants. CRA and Revenu Québec provide API web services and secure portals for tax preparers, but there is no immediate government pre-approval of individual invoices. Quebec has adopted mandatory electronic tendering measures for public bodies, but private B2B e-invoicing remains contract-based; foreign registrants may use EDI and standard XML or PDF invoices provided they are encoded for audit traceability.

Claims of export zero-rating are subject to CRA and Revenu Québec audit. If zero-rated treatment is found ineligible, the taxpayer is assessed for unpaid GST/HST and QST, with interest under the Excise Tax Act and the QST Act plus penalties of 25% (gross negligence) of the tax amount; knowing participation in false statements can give rise to section 327 Excise Tax Act evasion fines up to $25,000 plus imprisonment and civil monetary penalties up to $5,000 for false representations. Retrospective claim adjustments are permissible in any non-statute-barred taxation period; refund claims and input tax credits claimed on export invoices may be reversed, carry interest and recoverable taxes, and lead to additional business loss for repeat infractions. Evidence must be retained for six years; failure to produce records triggers a CRA requirement and potential deemed-ineligible status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; no ministerial approval is required for inward capital contributions, outward dividends, profit remittances, or liquidation proceeds of a Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite). The sole fiscal friction is Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced usually to 5–15%) applied by the Canadian Revenue Agency (CRA) to distributions paid to non-resident limited partners. Settlement currency is unrestricted; foreign-currency accounts inside Canada are standard. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level KYC, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and direct-investment statistical reporting to Statistics Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. SaaS and legal-contract-automation service receipts (ISIC 6201/NAICS 541199) are not subject to customs declarations or exchange-control surrender. Export revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD or other convertible currencies in a Canadian foreign-currency account. For FX conversions above CAD 50,000 per transaction, banks apply standard FINTRAC transaction reporting; supporting documentation (commercial invoices, subscription agreements) is requested ad hoc.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and issuance agent; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential bank regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor; Department of Finance Canada for capital-account policy. Canada operates without a dedicated foreign-exchange control licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a payment-only rail; not viable as the primary corporate treasury account. Canada has no EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licence; foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury) must register with FINTRAC as Money Service Businesses and, within Quebec, obtain a permit from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Money-Services Businesses Act. Since November 2024, payment service providers also come under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) licensing phase-in. Recommended architecture: a Schedule I bank as the sole settlement account and clearing anchor, with an EMI used solely as a parallel disbursement rail after the EMI's FINTRAC registration certificate and RPAA transition status are verified.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner contributions must route via SWIFT MT103/OPC from the ultimate investor's bank directly to the LP's CAD master account at the chosen Schedule I bank. For reports to Statistics Canada, the remittance advice should carry the purpose code for direct investment equity/contribution and reference the entity's legal name and NEQ in the beneficiary line to avoid name-screening delays. Banks require 3-business-day pre-notification for injections above CAD 10 million and may request the executed partnership agreement, capital commitment schedule, and source-of-funds statements before crediting. Stripe payouts for the SaaS platform route via the LP's operating account in CAD, settling in two business days; the Stripe settlement currency must match the CAD operating account to prevent unnecessary FX conversion.Cross-border supplier payments for technology licences and infrastructure use SWIFT MT103 (SWIFT gpi ideally) with a USD or EUR credit to the beneficiary bank; recurring domestic disbursements clear via Payments Canada ACSS (next-day) or LVTS (real-time, for timeliness-critical payments). Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for SaaS disbursements and are excluded from the standard clearing design; vendor payments instead rely on MT103 with explicit beneficiary BIC/IBAN and the LP's treasury applying a 3-month CAD operating buffer to eliminate intraday overdraft interest. Contractor payroll to non-resident individuals clears via MT103 to local payout partners; all outbound FX conversion above CAD 1 million per month should be automatically swept into a daily forward-rate accumulator.Outright deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards (1-week to 12-month tenors) for converting USD subscription receipts into CAD at budgeted rates, executed under the bank's ISDA Master Agreement and 1998 FX Definitions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted for CAD versus illiquid emerging-market currencies (e.g., CAD/BRL, CAD/COP, CAD/INR) where CAD clearing is restricted; deliverables are used for all major pairs.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for medium-term USD liability management (notional typically CAD 5 million minimum, 1–5 year maturity) under an ISDA/CSA with bilateral credit support.Vanilla CAD/USD FX options (call/put spreads) for known M&A or distribution cash-flow contingencies, limited to premium-paid strategies to avoid leveraged exposure.Natural-hedging overlay: dual-currency CAD/USD operating accounts with daily sweeps to retain foreign revenues without forced conversion, augmented by monthly balance-sheet rebalancing forwards.
