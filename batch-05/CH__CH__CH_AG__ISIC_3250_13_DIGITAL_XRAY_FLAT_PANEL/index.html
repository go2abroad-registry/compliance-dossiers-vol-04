Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital for an Aktiengesellschaft is CHF 100,000. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up in cash into a blocked escrow account before registration, with an absolute minimum of CHF 50,000 paid-up capital. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; territorial taxation for direct taxes (Swiss-source income taxed, foreign permanent-establishment income exempt under § 52 DBG with subject-to-tax requirement); full VAT taxable-entity status for Swiss VAT purposes

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Swiss law; entity taxation follows statutory legal form (AG is a capital company). Available elections include: (i) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains (≥10% participation or market value ≥ CHF 1 million, Art. 69/70 DBG); (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) on equity surplus in safe-harbour cantons (partial); (iii) group taxation via qualified participation election (Art. 61 DBG – consolidated profit sharing up to 3-year cycle); (iv) option to apply deemed equity taxation for principal companies in Zug; (v) VAT group election (Art. 13 VAT Act) for related Swiss entities; (vi) status of 'export trader' (HEK) for certain 0% transactions. Verification of hybrid mismatch rules is required under the Swiss implementation of BEPS Action 2 (no statutory check-the-box; alignment follows financial statement classification).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to supplies of goods transported by the seller, the buyer or a third party to a foreign destination (Art. 23 Para 1 VAT Act). Services: 0% applies to services directly related to exported goods and to certain cross-border services (Art. 23 Para 2 VAT Act), including freight, customs brokerage, quality inspection related to exported medical devices (digital X-ray flat panel detectors) and to services related to hydrology/oceanology instruments if exported. Export must be proven within a documented period (customs declaration and transport evidence); recipient must be established abroad (for services). Documentary evidence (proof of export – Ausfuhrnachweis) must be obtained by the supplier and retained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Switzerland maintains no foreign exchange control regime, no central bank approval requirement and no restriction on inward investment, profit remittance, liquidation proceeds, intercompany loans or dividend distributions. A Swiss AG distributing dividends must withhold 35% Swiss federal anticipatory tax, but the foreign shareholder may claim a full or partial refund or treaty reduction under the applicable double taxation agreement; many Swiss treaties provide a 0% rate on qualifying substantial corporate participations and 5%–15% on portfolio holdings. Repatriation of trade proceeds and debt service is unrestricted. The only administrative hurdle for non-bank enterprises is the SNB statistical declaration on forms Z1/Z2 for settlement flows with a single non-resident counterparty exceeding CHF 125,000 in a calendar month.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline and no licensing requirement for holding or disbursing foreign currency export earnings. A Swiss AG exporting DR flat-panel detectors and medical-imaging hardware may freely invoice in CHF, EUR, USD, GBP or offshore CNY and retain balances in any convertible currency. Remaining compliance duties are: (i) substantiating Swiss VAT zero-rating on exports with customs and transport documentation; (ii) reporting outbound payments on SNB form Z2 when the monthly aggregate to one non-resident counterparty exceeds CHF 125,000; and (iii) maintaining full commercial documentation to support transfer pricing and customs valuation of intra-group component flows.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, acting under the National Bank Act (NBA) and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA); supplementary enforcement by FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) for banking licensing and AML supervision, and by SECO for sanctions and export controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury substitute; Medium-to-High as a complementary payment corridor. Switzerland is outside the EU/EEA passporting system, so a UK, Lithuanian or other foreign EMI licence does not permit deposit-taking or domiciliation of Swiss corporate—only FINMA licensed Swiss payment institutions or fintech banks may hold client balances under the Swiss Banking Act (Fintech Licence, Art. 1b, currently permitting non-interest-bearing public deposits up to CHF 100 million). Funds parked with an unlicensed foreign EMI would lack Swiss depositor protection, create sanctions/AML blind spots, and jeopardise the bank confirmation needed for the incorporation capital deposit. A Swiss AG may use licensed Swiss fintechs for invoice-initiation, FX execution or EUR/USD corridor payments, while keeping the principal liquidity pool and all capital confirmation functions at a fully licensed tier-1 Swiss bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The CHF 100,000 minimum share capital of the AG (at least CHF 50,000 effectively paid-in in cash under Art. 632 of the Swiss Code of Obligations) must be deposited on a dedicated capital escrow account at a Swiss bank before registration. Compliant routing: non-resident shareholder executes an SWIFT MT103 in CHF or a freely convertible currency to the bank account in the exact name of the AG, with remittance information (field 70) clearly stating 'Anzahlung auf das Aktienkapital – Einlage in das Aktienkapital' and identifying the payor and company. The receiving bank issues a capital-payment confirmation (Kapitalbestätigung/Einlagebestätigung) used by the notary and Commercial Register Office. After registration, the board releases the funds to the operational account. No SNB approval is required. Inbound receipts from a single non-resident counterparty above CHF 125,000 per calendar month must be captured in the enterprise's SNB Z1 statistical report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury substitute; Medium-to-High as a complementary payment corridor. Funds parked with an unlicensed foreign EMI would lack Swiss depositor protection, create sanctions/AML blind spots, and jeopardise the bank confirmation needed for the incorporation capital deposit. A Swiss AG may use licensed Swiss fintechs for invoice-initiation, FX execution or EUR/USD corridor payments, while keeping the principal liquidity pool and all capital confirmation functions at a fully licensed tier-1 Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory flat tax 8.5% on net accounting profit before federal tax, which yields an effective federal burden of 7.83% on pre-tax profit because the federal tax itself is deductible. Combined federal + cantonal + communal headline rate in Zug (Crypto Valley prevailing jurisdiction) is approximately 11.9%, one of the lowest in Switzerland; high-tax cantons such as Geneva and Zurich reach approximately 14.0% and 21.0% respectively.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividend distributions by the AG; Switzerland levies no cantonal or communal withholding tax on dividends; the DTA generally reduces the rate to 0% for qualifying substantial corporate shareholdings (typically 10% to 25% depending on treaty), to 5% for portfolio corporate investors, and to 15% for individuals; a refund of the excess withheld must be requested within 3 years.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / TP documentation threshold (Art. 1 TDok-V): a Swiss company must prepare a Local File when it has annual gross turnover above CHF 100 million and carries out cross-border transactions with related parties; the Master File must be prepared when the parent group has consolidated gross turnover above CHF 1 billion; simplified documentation applies for qualifying SMEs with gross revenue below CHF 10 million or where group cross-border revenue is below CHF 5 million; documentation must be submitted within 60 days of the FTA's written request and must be in German, French or English.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime. Economic substance is nevertheless indispensable and is enforced indirectly through: (i) Swiss treaty anti-abuse and PPT/LOB clauses, which require substantial business activity, premises, employees and entrepreneurial risk in Switzerland; (ii) the 2020 tax reform, which abolished the privileged treatment of domiciliary/holding companies; (iii) Art. 58a DBG anti-deferral rules targeting the transfer of taxable assets to low-tax jurisdictions; and (iv) the beneficial-ownership condition in the Swiss withholding-tax refund process. For the digital X-ray detector manufacturing AG, on-site production, clean-room assets, R&D staffing and qualified management must be documented because the FTA will test economic substance when a transfer-pricing adjustment, DTA relief claim or APA application is filed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Swiss Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 25%-250% of the evaded tax (federal, Art. 175-176 DBG), plus 4.5% per annum default interest; criminal prosecution results in a fine up to CHF 250,000 and/or imprisonment for responsible managers for up to 3 years (Art. 186 DBG); in aggravated cases with forged books the Swiss Criminal Code money-laundering and forgery provisions may add imprisonment up to 10 years; the tax shortfall remains fully recoverable.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false return are joint and several obligors for the civil tax surcharge under Art. 754(1) CO; the FTA may issue a personal liability order against the CFO/CEO when the company becomes insolvent; the criminal court may order a travel ban, confiscation of unlawfully retained gains, and the responsible foreign directors are extraditable under the bilateral extradition treaties; custodial sentences of up to 3 years apply.
