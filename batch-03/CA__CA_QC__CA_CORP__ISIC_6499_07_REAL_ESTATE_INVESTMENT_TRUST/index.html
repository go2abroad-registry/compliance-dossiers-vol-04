Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; federal registration via CRA Business Registration Online / My Business Account; Quebec GST/HST and QST registration via Revenu Québec CLIC and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec

E-government filing portal

A foreign REIT commencing Canadian commercial real property activity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account and, where applicable, payroll and import/export accounts. In Quebec, a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and a QST account must be obtained from Revenu Québec. The standard non-resident procedure is: file CRA Form RC1 (or the applicable non-resident registration application), designate a Canadian agent or branch representative if required, provide certified evidence of entity existence and treaty residence, and register for GST/HST and QST when making taxable supplies of Canadian real property in the course of a commercial activity. REITs that qualify as selected listed financial institutions are also subject to special GST/HST and QST registration and annual attribution-reporting duties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian REIT trust qualifies for flow-through treatment under Income Tax Act s.104(6); an underlying property-owning corporation, if any, remains Entity_Level_Taxation).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. REIT pass-through status is attained by continuously satisfying the s.122.1(1) 'real estate investment trust' statutory definition, not by an elective filing. Available statutory elections include: the trust distribution deduction under s.104(6); the non-resident landlord election under s.216(4) to file net Canadian rental income instead of Part XIII withholding; optional s.217 non-resident returns; the selected listed financial institution (SLFI) attribution-factor election under Canada Excise Tax Act s.225.2 and the Quebec equivalent; and Canadian treaty withholding-rate procedural elections on CRA forms RC1413/RC1414. Hybrid mismatch elections are generally unavailable or strictly limited under Canada's BEPS-Action-2-related income tax rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 small-supplier threshold of taxable supplies made in Canada during the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters; mandatory registration applies to non-resident commercial realty lessors once taxable commercial rent exceeds the threshold, with special rules for selected listed financial institutions and digital-economy suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI applies to exports of goods that are physically exported from Canada and to certain offshore services supplied to non-residents for use or consumption wholly outside Canada. For a REIT, zero-rating is relevant mainly to exported management or advisory services performed outside Canada and to prescribed financial services; Canadian-situs real property leases and property-management services performed in Quebec are either taxable or exempt as applicable.

  • Canada Border Services Agency Export Declaration (B13A or electronic Cargo/Summary Report)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export contract
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or other authorized transport document
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance / foreign-exchange repatriation evidence substantiating Canadian-dollar proceeds
  • Reconciled GST/HST zero-rated export worksheet and supporting internal records
  • For offshore services: executed service contract, non-resident destination/use certificate, and non-resident entity identification

Canada has no real-time VAT fiscal-clearance or digital-stamping system. Invoices must be issued in Quebec in French and contain the prescribed information under Excise Tax Act s.223 and the Quebec Taxation Act, including vendor name, Business Number, date, consideration, and tax payable. Electronic invoicing and EDI are accepted without government pre-clearance; no API-based clearance mandate applies, but records must be retained for six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year limitation period, extended where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud exists. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract Excise Tax Act s.285 gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the refund or credit claimed; refunds are clawed back with statutory interest, QST penalties apply under the Quebec Taxation Act, and criminal liability may arise for wilful evasion. Retroactive reassessment can recover all federal and provincial VAT amounts wrongfully zero-rated, plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime for the Canadian dollar (CAD), with no general exchange-control legislation, no capital-export licensing, and no profit-remittance exchange approvals. The sole statutory friction on remittances from a Quebec REIT is the withholding tax regime under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada): 25% on dividends/distributions to non-residents, treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% portfolio and 5% qualifying corporate under the Canada-US treaty), collected via NR4 filing and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency. Additional administrative hurdles include mandatory FATCA/CRS account reporting, T106/T1134/T1135 forms, and the submission of a source-of-funds declaration for inbound capital transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada: there is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandated conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. A Canadian-resident REIT may hold export-derived or property-sale proceeds in any currency in its Schedule I bank accounts. The only relevant compliance obligations are (i) the general requirement to report cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more to the Canada Border Services Agency/FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and (ii) the accrual recognition of unrealized foreign-exchange gains/losses under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) for tax purposes, which affects treasury cash-flow planning but not legal convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (central bank), with the Minister of Finance, Department of Finance Canada, as the statutory foreign-exchange authority under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2). Foreign-exchange controls are administered jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Canada does not license foreign fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) as banks; a non-Canadian EMI cannot hold the REIT's capital escrow, provide Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage, or serve as the primary settlement venue. A foreign EMI may process only secondary payment flows if registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and compliant with the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23), supervised by the Bank of Canada. Recommended approach: use the cross-border digital platforms of Schedule I banks (RBC Express, BMO GlobalLink, Scotiabank Global Transaction Banking) rather than a standalone offshore EMI, and route all regulatory capital contributions through the licensed Canadian banking channel.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Fully non-resident ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain without a Canadian nexus.

Root cause: Fully non-resident ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain without a Canadian nexus.

Defense evidence: Apostilled shareholder registry; notarized director passports and proof-of-address; a Canadian legal opinion confirming economic substance; an organizational chart identifying every UBO above 10%; and a banker's reference from the parent entity's financial institution.
Large one-time inbound escrow wire (e.g., CAD 10M+) from a foreign trust with opaque source-of-funds.

Root cause: Large one-time inbound escrow wire (e.g., CAD 10M+) from a foreign trust with opaque source-of-funds.

Defense evidence: Audited REIT financial statements; executed capital commitment deeds; 12-month bank statements from the originating institution showing the wire trail; notarized source-of-wealth declaration; tax residency certificates (Form NR301 for treaty claims); and a written commercial rationale memo approved by the board.
Mismatch between the REIT's registered address (Quebec) and the location of trustees, counsel, or property assets (multi-jurisdictional split).

Root cause: Mismatch between the REIT's registered address (Quebec) and the location of trustees, counsel, or property assets (multi-jurisdictional split).

Defense evidence: Complete REIT trust indenture or declaration of trust; Quebec law opinion (Civil Code arts. 1260-1298); AMF fee/eligibility letters where applicable; consolidated property portfolio schedule; and identification of all unitholders exceeding the 10% beneficial-ownership threshold.
High-volume LVTS payments inconsistent with the stated Stage-4 construction and distribution schedule.

Root cause: High-volume LVTS payments inconsistent with the stated Stage-4 construction and distribution schedule.

Defense evidence: Board-approved 12-month treasury cash-flow forecast; Stage-1 to Stage-4 drawdown schedule; executed purchase agreements and third-party appraisals; distribution resolution of the board or trustees; and CRA filings (T3 or corporate T2 with REIT election) confirming the fund-flow purpose.
Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctions-list name among directors or major unitholders.

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctions-list name among directors or major unitholders.

Defense evidence: Signed PEP questionnaire and source-of-wealth evidence; written consent to FINTRAC/OSFI enhanced due diligence; international counsel sanctions opinion; and, where applicable, a Global Affairs Canada permit under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors must route capital injections by SWIFT MT103 to the REIT's designated Canadian-dollar (CAD) or US-dollar (USD) operational account at a Schedule I bank. The remittance instruction must include the receiving bank's Canadian Clearing Code (CC) and institutional/transit number for CAD wires, or the correspondent US intermediary bank details for USD wires, with the payment purpose field stating 'equity injection – ISIC 6499 REIT capital contribution' and a remittance reference to the capital commitment agreement. FINTRAC will record all transactions over CAD 10,000; the bank requires a source-of-funds and source-of-wealth declaration for any inbound wire exceeding CAD 100,000. Do not use ambiguous purpose codes (e.g., 'consulting fees' or 'loan repayment') that could trigger Quebec Civil Code recharacterization or CRA deemed-dividend rules under subsection 15(1) of the Income Tax Act. Where subscription funds are received in trust pending deployment, the funds must be held in a segregated Capital Escrow Account under the bank's client-trust protocols consistent with National Instrument 45-106 (private placement) or 41-101 (prospectus).Outbound treasury payments should use the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border trade settlements and vendor payments for construction and property management, with the required CC/transit or correspondent routing; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 for high-value construction, notarial escrow, or cross-border equipment purchases, confirmed by the issuer's correspondent bank; (iii) the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) / ACH rail for recurring domestic dividend distributions to Canadian unitholders, using standard CPD/CCD format with the REIT's pre-notified CRA distribution codes; (iv) the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for all intra-Canada settlements exceeding CAD 1,000,000 to ensure same-day finality. All distributions to non-residents must be processed with NR4 reporting and the applicable Part XIII withholding remittance; payments above CAD 2,500 relating to goods must also be recorded in the Canada Border Services Agency trade documentation (e.g., B3-3 / CUSMA or NAFTA certificates where applicable). Designating the target rails 'Dividend_Distribution_Wire' and 'ACH' in the treasury console is acceptable provided wire transfers to foreign unitholders carry ISO 20022 remittance codes reflecting the distribution class.Outright FX forward contracts (maturity up to 24 months) with a Schedule I bank under a bilateral ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, used exclusively to hedge known CAD/USD acquisition, construction, and distribution exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for currencies with no accessible onshore deliverable market in the REIT's construction-supply chain, with CAD and USD legs settled onshore through the Canadian clearing system.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) converting USD-denominated REIT mortgage debt into CAD liabilities, executed with an authorized derivatives dealer and collateralized under the Canadian CSD clearing rules.FX swaps and rolling spot contracts for short-term liquidity bridging between the Capital Escrow Account and the Operational Account.Vanilla European-style FX call and put options, purchased on a non-leveraged basis, to cap acquisition-cost and distribution-cost uncertainty.Total return swaps (TRS) only where the reference asset is a recognized REIT portfolio asset and the swap qualifies as an effective hedging transaction; speculative TRS is prohibited by prudent treasury policy and the Quebec AMF derivatives regulations.Compliance note: all instruments must be disclosed under IFRS 9/IAS 39 hedge-accounting guidelines as adopted in Canada (or ASPE hedge accounting for private entities) and documented in the annual financial statements of the REIT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million (Local File) and CAD 10 million (Master File) in cross-border related-party transactions, subject to gross revenue thresholds of CAD 50 million and CAD 100 million respectively.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. For a REIT, the entity must meet the definition under the Income Tax Act, including asset and income tests, and if structured as a trust, must distribute at least 90% of its income to unitholders to be exempt from tax at the trust level. For a corporate REIT, it is taxed as a corporation; however, the CRA may challenge arrangements lacking genuine business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, section 239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code may result in imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they failed to exercise due diligence; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR, disallow deductions, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit; also, the entity may be deemed a 'shell' for AML purposes, leading to fines under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 2 million for corporations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the shell arrangement; travel bans and criminal charges for money laundering under the Criminal Code.
AML/Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties for non-compliance with reporting obligations may include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations; civil restitution for losses incurred by investors.
