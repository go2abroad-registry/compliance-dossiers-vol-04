UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Federal Act on Combating Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (AMLA, SR 955.0); Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance (AMLO, SR 955.01) Art. 4 (UBO threshold: any natural person who directly or indirectly holds or controls more than 25% of capital or voting rights, or who controls the legal entity by other means); Federal Act on Transparency of Legal Entities (TJPA) as implemented through amendments to the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR, SR 220), mandating filing of beneficial-owner data in a non-public federal transparency register maintained by the Swiss Federal Commercial Register Office (Geover); Swiss Criminal Code (SR 311.0) Art. 305bis and Art. 305ter (money laundering and failure to exercise due diligence in financial transactions); FINMA Anti-Money-Laundering Ordinance (SR 955.033), FINMA Circular 2024/1 'Money Laundering', and the Swiss Bankers Association / self-regulatory Due Diligence Convention (VSB/CDB) applicable to attorneys, notaries, and fiduciaries when they act as financial intermediaries. Under AMLO Art. 4, the 25% ownership threshold is the statutory triggering point for mandatory identification and verification of the ultimate beneficial owner; the Swiss transparency register window likewise uses a 25% ownership or control threshold for disclosure, with increased-risk factors (e.g., bearer-share chains, nominee arrangements, shell structures) requiring explicit documentation.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permitted for a non-regulated, low-risk OEM plush-mascot manufacturer. The Aktiengesellschaft must maintain a legally valid Swiss seat ('Sitz') stated in the articles of association (Art. 626 para. 2 item 2 Swiss Code of Obligations, SR 220), with a real, addressable location and documented administrative substance. A pure letter-box or virtual office with no local management presence is not acceptable, because the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax (Art. 50, SR 642.211) and the China–Switzerland DTA tie-breaker rule would recharacterize the place of effective management as the place where the actual administration is exercised (likely China), thereby defeating Swiss tax residency. Zug domiciliation and administrative-office service providers are lawful and customarily accepted for low-risk industrial companies, provided that the AG retains a local director resident in Switzerland, maintains Swiss books and records, and executes a documented registered-office service agreement with the domiciliation provider. For this low-risk plush-toy OEM scope, a Zug domiciliation arrangement combined with a Swiss-resident director and a service-level agreement is generally accepted by the Zug Commercial Register Office and by tax practice.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard AG minimum registered share capital: CHF 100,000 (a reduced capital floor of CHF 50,000 may be set in the articles under the 2023 company-law reform, Art. 621 para. 3 OR). At incorporation at least 20% of the nominal value of each issued share must be paid up; for the standard CHF 100,000 capital this equals a minimum paid-up amount of CHF 20,000. The paid-up cash must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank or a Swiss branch of a foreign bank and is released only after registration; the bank's blocking confirmation is a mandatory filing attachment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety inspection and acceptance (feuerpolizeiliche Gebrauchsabnahme) of the leased industrial premises; structural and statics confirmation of floor load-bearing capacity for embroidery and ultrasonic-seaming machinery; workplace-safety audit under the Federal Labour Act (ArG) and Ordinance 3 (CO 3), including CE-marked machine guarding, emergency exits, first-aid stations and ventilation; civil-defense shelter obligations apply only to larger building projects and are not triggered by a standard workshop fit-out; the final occupancy clearance (Gebrauchsbewilligung) is issued by the municipality before production commences.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard AG minimum registered share capital: CHF 100,000 (a reduced capital floor of CHF 50,000 may be set in the articles under the 2023 company-law reform, Art. 621 para. 3 OR). At incorporation at least 20% of the nominal value of each issued share must be paid up; for the standard CHF 100,000 capital this equals a minimum paid-up amount of CHF 20,000. The paid-up cash must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank or a Swiss branch of a foreign bank and is released only after registration; the bank's blocking confirmation is a mandatory filing attachment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Principal VAT Division and Direct Federal Tax Division; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch and e-services portal https://www.evat.admin.ch / https://www.gate.easymwst.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A domestic Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is registered ex officio in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at incorporation, receives the UID business identification number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx), and is automatically subject to federal, cantonal and communal income taxation, requiring periodic corporate tax returns. For VAT, the AG must register with ESTV when its annual worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 (applies to resident and non-established enterprises); a foreign entity supplying goods or services in Switzerland registers via the ESTV eVAT application (Formulary 0304 for non-established foreign enterprises), providing certified articles of association, foreign register extract, proof of Swiss place of business or substantial presence, and an estimate of expected Swiss turnover; ESTV then assigns the VAT number founded on the UID. Voluntary registration is permissible where the statutory threshold is not met but the applicant demonstrates a legitimate business interest. The entire procedure for foreign entities is completed without a local fiscal representative unless the authority requires one for improper or risk-bearing circumstances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) is a resident company subject to full entity-level corporate income taxation on worldwide income (direct federal tax at statutory 8.5%, effective 7.83%, plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; aggregate rates in representative cantons circa 12% to 21%); no pass-through or transparent entity classification is available for AGs; the taxation model is non-territorial for resident companies, subject to double taxation treaty relief, participation deduction and deemed equity interest reduction for cantonal/communal taxes; non-resident shareholders are not taxed at entity level, but dividends are subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35% (reduced under treaties) and to cantonal coupon tax where applicable.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not employ US-style check-the-box classification. For the Swiss AG, available statutory elections and hybrid-related mechanisms comprise: (i) group taxation election under the Swiss group taxation regime effective 1 January 2020, allowing domestic group companies to elect fiscal consolidation for profit tax at federal level (cantonal implementation varies); (ii) VAT group registration election under Art. 14 MWSTG, pooling closely affiliated Swiss entities as one VAT taxpayer; (iii) option to tax real property supplies under Art. 29 MWSTG to waive the exemption on real estate transfers; (iv) election to maintain simplified accounting and invoicing for small VAT payers; (v) participation relief claims, not elections, for qualifying dividend and capital gains income; (vi) hybrid mismatch compliance rules under the Swiss implementation of BEPS Action 2 (effective 1 January 2020), limiting deductions in case of hybrid financial instruments and double-deduction structures; no statutory check-the-box election exists, and entity classification is determined by mandatory Swiss legal form law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 gross annual turnover from taxable supplies of goods and services in Switzerland (worldwide for resident enterprises, Swiss-source for non-established foreign enterprises); threshold is computed on the aggregate of supplies not exempted from tax under Art. 18 MWSTG; the small-business turnover exemption applies to resident enterprises below the threshold; foreign enterprises supplying goods or services in Switzerland exceeding CHF 100,000 are mandatory VAT registrants; voluntary registration is allowed upon application and approval.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated where the supplier delivers the goods to the purchaser abroad and proves that the goods have physically left Switzerland to an external destination (Art. 23(2)(1) MWSTG, Art. 54 MWSTV). For the plush mascot OEM scope (ISIC 3240), eligibility requires transfer of power of disposal of the goods to the foreign customer, dispatch from Switzerland, and documentary proof of the export within a reasonable period; shipments via customs transit, direct export, or through a Swiss warehouse to foreign airports are accepted, but inland forwarding without proof of exit does not qualify. Services directly connected to exported goods (e.g., embroidery, ultrasonic seaming, quality control performed in Switzerland) and services supplied to foreign entrepreneurs under the customer-location B2B rule (Art. 8(1) MWSTG) are zero-rated if attributable to the foreign business; offshore disposal of intellectual property or marketing services for the foreign buyer is likewise zero-rated; the supplier must maintain intercompany or commercial agreements to demonstrate the recipient's foreign entrepreneur status.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / ECS electronic export declaration, including communication of exit notification from border customs office)
  • Commercial Invoice with full Swiss supplier details, foreign buyer details, ISIC 3240 goods description, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or International Consignment Note (CMR) evidencing physical departure from Switzerland
  • Packaging List and Combined Transport or Forwarder's Export Manifest
  • Proof of Payment — Bank SWIFT/TT transaction record or Inward Remittance Certificate confirming FX repatriation to the Swiss exporter
  • Foreign buyer identification documents (commercial register extract or foreign VAT ID) evidencing the overseas entrepreneur status

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control API reporting, and no digital stamping or crypto-signature requirement for commercial invoices. Suppliers must issue invoices substantively compliant with Art. 26 MWSTG (name and address of supplier and recipient, date of supply, description, consideration, VAT rate, and tax amount; simplified invoice rules for amounts up to CHF 400). Electronic invoicing using eBill, Swiss QR-Bill, XML/ZUGFeRD variants and PDF is legally equal to paper; mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies only to suppliers of federal administration entities. VAT returns are filed quarterly, semi-annually or annually via the ESTV portal, with payment deadlines and an 180-day extension annual filing option; records must be retained for 10 years.

ESTV audits export zero-rating through systematic documentary review and international information exchange; failure to substantiate actual physical export automatically reverses the 0% rate and reassesses the supply at 8.1% VAT, plus default interest currently at 4.5% p.a. from the date the tax was due. In cases of intent to evade tax, Art. 102 MWSTG imposes a criminal fine of up to 1.5 times the evaded tax, in addition to full retroactive recovery of the tax; negligence under Art. 96 MWSTG attracts fines up to CHF 5,000. Claims may be newly asserted (Nacherhebung) within a period of generally 5 years, extended to 10 years where tax evasion is found; criminal prosecution prescribes after 15 years. Fraudulent misuse of zero-rating for plush toy exports, including fake bills of lading, fictitious foreign customers or circular currency flows, exposes the entity to unlimited retrospective clawback of input VAT deducted and severe penalties, jointly and severally for responsible organs.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully convertible capital and current account regime. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely tradable and is not subject to general exchange controls. Dividends, interest, royalties and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable, but remain subject to anti-money-laundering (AML) verification, banking onboarding documentation, sanctions/embargo compliance, and Swiss tax formalities, including the 35% withholding tax on dividends with potential relief or partial refund. There are no statutory profit-remittance limits or capital-export licences for lawful corporate transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, no forced conversion, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in freely convertible accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to balance-of-payments statistical filings with the Swiss National Bank where applicable; tax and AML documentation must evidence the commercial reality of each export transaction.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — central bank and monetary authority; no separate foreign exchange control administration exists in Switzerland.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately feasible as a supplementary payment rail for platform fee collections and supplier disbursements, but not as a substitute for a licensed Swiss corporate account. Offshore digital EMI providers are not permitted to take deposits, grant loans, or offer principal-protected products in Switzerland unless they hold a FINMA licence; EU/EEA single-passporting does not extend to Switzerland. An offshore EMI may be used only for ancillary treasury flows and must not hold statutory share capital, dividend pools, or tax-collection accounts. Feasibility rating: Medium / legally constrained.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory corporate income tax rate assessed on post-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit). The aggregate ordinary headline rate including cantonal and communal corporate taxes ranges from approximately 11.9% in the low-tax canton of Zug to approximately 21.6% in Geneva; a typical Zug-registered manufacturing AG combining federal plus cantonal/communal taxes is subject to a fully loaded effective rate of 11.9%-15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) withheld at the source on any dividend distribution resolved by the AG's shareholders' meeting; domestic intercorporate dividends paid to a Swiss parent holding at least 10% of the nominal capital are exempt from the withholding tax under Art. 5 para. 3 VStG; preferential DTA rates reduce the 35% burden for non-resident corporate shareholders to 5% (substantial holdings in most DTAs) or 15% (portfolio holdings), with 0% for qualifying substantial shareholders under selected DTAs (e.g., Germany, France in specific structures).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return with all annexes (audited or reviewed annual financial statements, tax reconciliation schedules, participation deduction form, IP box schedules, intercompany agreements, and transfer pricing documentation) is statutorily due within 6 months after the close of the fiscal year; standard administrative practice automatically extends the filing deadline to 31 December of the calendar year following the tax period, and cantonal practice (including Zug) permits further discretionary extensions to 30 September or 31 December of the next calendar year provided a written extension request is submitted before the original deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due simultaneously with the federal return within 6 months of fiscal-year end, with the same automatic extended deadline of 31 December of the following calendar year in Zug practice; extensions must be requested electronically through the cantonal portal before the original expiry.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory simultaneous Local File and Master File in the OECD/FTA format are required only where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds CHF 900 million (Federal Transfer Pricing Documentation Ordinance and FTA Circular 2020); below the threshold, the AG must still hold adequate contemporaneous evidence (intercompany contracts, pricing policies, benchmark searches, and margin computations) and present a concise transfer pricing study to the FTA upon request within 60 days; a protected-lower ceiling of CHF 60 million group turnover grants SME administrative relief, but the substantive arm's-length duty under Art. 58 DBG continues to bind the plush-toy OEM company irrespective of turnover.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a closed Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to the Cayman/BVI ESR; nevertheless, the Swiss tax practice applies a substantive economic-activity doctrine: treaty and domestic relief (participation exemption, patent box, notional interest deduction, DTA dividend reduction, and the Art. 24a VStG retrocession provision) are granted only if the AG maintains actual physical premises in the canton, employs qualified staff who take strategic and operational decisions, holds books and records in Switzerland, and conducts genuine core business activity (design, embroidery, ultrasonic manufacturing, warehousing, and shipping of plush mascots) from Swiss soil; a letterbox company whose central administration is located abroad is treated as a foreign-controlled entity, its income is re-attributed to the foreign head office, and all treaty benefits are denied under the PPT/LOB provisions of the CH DTA network.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Abuse / Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 175, 186, and 186a DBG and Art. 56 StHG: fine up to three times the evaded tax plus statutory late-payment interest at 4.5% per annum; the administrative penalty is cumulative with an Ordnungsbusse of up to CHF 10,000 for formal default; in qualified cases, imprisonment of up to five years and monetary penalty are available against the author of the false return, and assessment periods are extended to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Under Art. 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, each director and officer becomes jointly and severally personally liable for the full tax damage caused to the company where he signed or caused the submission of the false return in breach of statutory care duties; the criminal sanction is imposed directly on the natural person irrespective of any corporate fine, a travel ban can be ordered under Art. 53 StGB when the defendant is outside Switzerland, and the FTA may pierce the corporate veil in enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities recharacterize the pricing excess as a hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschuettung): the full excess is added back to the AG's taxable income as non-deductible expense, reassessed federal and cantonal taxes are levied, the 35% Swiss withholding tax is imposed on the deemed distribution with 4.5% interest, and a special contribution is claimed on the foreign shareholder's Swiss banking channel under the Withholding Tax Act; a deliberate scheme triggers the criminal sanctions of Art. 186 DBG.

Director personal liability: Directors approving off-market intercompany prices without proper benchmarking face derivative personal liability under Art. 754 CO for the resulting withholding tax shortfall and penalties; Art. 12 DBG imposes strict personal liability on the responsible executive for uncollected withholding tax; additionally, if the inflated intercompany payments were converted abroad for the directors' private benefit, Art. 678 CO restitution and money laundering charges under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code apply.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiency through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to three years and a fine of up to CHF 500,000 for intentional laundering; the negligent form is punishable by a fine of up to CHF 250,000; organizational failure is sanctioned under Art. 305quater StGB with a fine of up to CHF 5,000,000, plus confiscation of the laundered funds and the instrumentalities, customer-restriction orders by FINMA, and closure of the AG's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Every director who knew or should have known of the laundering origin when authorizing account operations is individually criminally liable, with personal asset seizure and final execution on private assets by the Swiss Federal Debt Enforcement Office; directors named as signatories also face civil liability under Art. 754 CO and exclusion from board service in any future Swiss company, and in cross-border cases the European Arrest Warrant may be used for extradition.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The FTA and the cantonal tax authority re-litigate the assessments under the economic equivalence doctrine, deny the patent box and participation exemption, attribute the substance-related income to the foreign corporate seat or re-attribute the AG's worldwide income to the foreign head office, reassess the 35% withholding tax on all dividends paid in the last ten years with 4.5% annual default interest, and issue a formal Ordnungsbusse for abusive structuring; the tax privilege is revoked retroactively under Art. 24a VStG and the DTA limitation-on-benefits clause blocks treaty refunds.

Director personal liability: Directors serving in a shell capacity have individually rendered their own and the company's benefits to restitution claims under Art. 678 CO; piercing the corporate veil is accepted by the Federal Supreme Court when the company lacks capitalization and substance, making the shareholders and directors jointly liable for all tax arrears; criminal proceedings for qualified tax evasion can be opened against the directors personally, and a travel ban or pre-trial detention may be imposed to secure the penalty.
