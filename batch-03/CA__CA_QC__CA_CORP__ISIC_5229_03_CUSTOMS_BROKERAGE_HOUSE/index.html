Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax, portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax, portal: Revenu Québec CLIC (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/on-line-services/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register extra-provincially in Quebec by obtaining a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec within 60 days of commencing business activities. Step 2: Obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing RC4601 (Application for a Business Number) or through the CRA BizPortal; non-resident corporations must designate a Canadian agent/representative where required. Step 3: Activate the 'RT' GST/HST program account with CRA; registration is compulsory once the corporation ceases to be a small supplier, i.e., worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in the four preceding calendar quarters or in the current quarter; voluntary registration is prudent to claim input tax credits. Step 4: Register for QST with Revenu Québec by filing the online registration application in CLIC; the QST account references the federal BN plus the 'RQ' prefix; Quebec applies the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Step 5: Obtain a customs broker licence from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under Section 9(1)(a) of the Customs Act, including a corporate security bond of at least CAD 25,000, professional indemnity insurance, and designation of a licensed individual broker. Step 6: Onboard into the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) Client Portal for mandatory electronic release, accounting, and duty/tax payment through ACH or wire.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Corporation is classified as a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Part I) and the Quebec Taxation Act (Part IV); no US-style check-the-box election exists — entity classification is determined by corporate law. Available elections include: (1) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations (90% or more common ownership) to zero-rate intercorporate taxable supplies; (2) ETA s. 273 joint venture election; (3) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 227) for small registrants; (4) ITA s. 85 rollover election to transfer property into the corporation at agreed amounts; (5) ETA streamlined accounting for non-resident registrants; (6) Quebec mirror elections under the QST Act and Taxation Act; (7) capital dividend account election (ITA s. 83) for tax-paid corporate surplus distributions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) in the four preceding calendar quarters or the current quarter; ETA s. 148(1); Quebec QST applies the identical threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Customs brokerage services supplied to a non-resident in respect of goods exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 ETA (services in respect of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export), and are similarly zero-rated for QST under the Quebec Sales Tax Act where the supply is made to a non-resident and the service relates to exported goods. International freight-forwarding and transportation services tied to a destination outside Canada also qualify. The broker must charge 0% at point of sale and retain contemporaneous evidence that (i) the service recipient is a non-resident, (ii) the goods were physically exported from Canada, and (iii) the service is attributable to those goods; input tax credits (ITCs/IVRTs) on related expenses remain fully claimable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed electronically via CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CES)
  • CBSA Commercial Invoice / customs invoice showing foreign purchaser, exporter, routing, and value
  • Bill of Lading (ocean B/L, airway bill, or CMR truckwaybill endorsed for export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-transfer/SWIFT evidence of foreign-currency payment settlement)
  • CBSA CARM account statement demonstrating zero-rated accounting lines
  • ACI eManifest export control record or CBSA-approved transit documentation

Canada does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance or digital-stamping e-invoicing regime; CRA permits electronic GST/HST invoices meeting the information requirements of ETA s. 169 and ETA Regs. s. 238 provided data integrity is preserved. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI). For customs brokers, CBSA CARM mandates fully electronic release and accounting via the CARM Client Portal, EDI/API integration, automated declaration processing, electronic posting of exporter/importer statements, and ACH/wire duty-and-tax settlement; legacy B3 paper accounting is obsolete for licensed brokers.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years of the reporting period under normal reassessment rules, and without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers reassessment at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST on the gross service consideration, with statutory interest and compound arrears interest. Gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 280.1 and QSTA equivalent equal the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax evaded; false statements or omissions carry an additional 50% penalty for a second occurrence. Deliberate evasion is prosecutable under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380, with fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Licensed customs brokers face CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) up to CAD 25,000 per violation, joint and several liability for duties/taxes incorrectly released under Customs Act s. 17-18, and licence suspension or revocation for systemic non-compliance. Any foreign exchange proceeds retained beyond the customary 180-day benchmark without documented commercial justification are treated by CRA as unrepatriated export revenue, exposing the broker to transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s. 247 and potential reassessment of the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada satisfies IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls, prior-approval licensing, or profit-remittance authorisation for dividends, interest, fees, or capital repatriation. Practical remittance hurdles are tax and reporting based: (i) outbound dividends and interest to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding tax (Canadian statutory rate 25%, reduced typically to 5%-15% under an applicable income tax treaty); (ii) non-resident inter-company payments must withstand transfer-pricing scrutiny under Income Tax Act section 247, and shareholder debt must respect the thin-capitalisation ratio of 1.5:1 debt-to-equity (ITA s.18(4)) plus the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) imposed on net interest expense exceeding C$250,000 annually, with a 30% EBITDA cap; (iii) all wire transfers, large cash transactions, and cross-border electronic funds transfers above C$10,000 are FINTRAC-reportable; (iv) brokerage fees charged to Canadian clients attract GST at 5% and Quebec QST at 9.975%, while fees supplied to non-resident clients in respect of documented export movements may be zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec QST Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed timeline for converting foreign-currency receipts into Canadian dollars; exporters and licensed customs brokers may freely retain USD, EUR, CNY, or any convertible currency. For a customs brokerage house, the analogous surrender and remittance obligations are: (i) import duties and taxes collected from clients are trust funds owing to His Majesty and must be settled with the Receiver General for Canada through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system on the prescribed due date, normally the date of release where funds are cleared, or under a CBSA-approved deferred payment arrangement underwritten by a customs surety bond (Form B6-1), with maximum deferral of approximately 30 days (monthly plan) or 45 days on a semi-annual bond; (ii) no duty deferral is available to an unlicensed entity or a broker whose surety bond has lapsed; (iii) GST/HST and QST collected on brokerage fees are remitted to CRA and Revenu Québec on the normal monthly or quarterly filing cycles; (iv) office cash receipts above C$10,000 trigger a FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Report filed within 15 calendar days.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. Effective regulatory authority for cross-border banking and FX compliance is distributed among OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for bank prudential conduct, FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) for AML/ATF transaction surveillance, Payments Canada for clearing membership, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs duty and broker licence administration, and Global Affairs Canada / Innovation, Science and Economic Development (ISED) for Investment Canada Act foreign-investment administration.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (federal general rate 15% + Quebec general rate 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and deemed dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, including dividends arising from paid-up capital reduction and profit capitalization; the Quebec share of withholding is collected federally under the Canada–Quebec administration agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with related non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year (CRA administrative materiality threshold; no strict statutory threshold is codified, but absence of documentation shifts the audit burden to the taxpayer and may attract penalties).

Economic substance scope: Canada's federal and provincial tax systems do not apply named 'Economic Substance Regulations' of the UAE type; however, the GAAR (ITA s.245) and Quebec's anti-avoidance rule (Quebec Taxation Act s.1079.1) will recharacterize transactions that lack genuine economic substance or that are primarily tax-motivated. A customs brokerage house must also satisfy federal CBSA licensing requirements, demonstrating an active commercial presence, office premises, staff and actual brokerage operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax (ITA s.163(2)); criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1) for tax evasion attracts fines ranging from 50% to 200% of tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment; Revenu Québec equivalent penalties apply under the Quebec Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized false filings are jointly and severally liable for unremitted tax amounts; the corporate veil can be pierced in civil fraud claims; director assets may be seized, and criminal conviction may include imprisonment.
GAAR / Shell Company Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec invoke GAAR to deny the tax benefit, adjust taxable income, and levy interest and penalties; conduit arrangements may create deemed dividends subject to 25% Part XIII withholding; false ownership declarations trigger s.163 penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed personally if assets were transferred out of the corporation at amounts less than fair value (ITA s.160); courts may disregard separate legal personality and order direct directors' liability for unpaid corporate taxes and restitution.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) adjusts profits to arm's-length levels; s.247(3) imposes a 10% penalty on the net adjustment where documentation is missing and gross misstatement exists; QST and federal interest accrue on reassessed balances.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally assessed for transfer pricing penalties absent fraud, but they are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII or source withholding taxes; failure to document may expose directors to director-liability proceedings for taxes owed by the company.
Customs Smuggling / AML Infraction

Statutory sanction: Customs Act penalties up to CAD 25,000 per false declaration, seizure/forfeiture of goods, CBSA licence revocation; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) failures; criminal liability under the Criminal Code of Canada for smuggling and laundering, with serious fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and licensed customs brokers are exposed to personal Criminal Code prosecution; convictions may trigger asset freezing, travel bans as bail conditions, and heavy personal fines; the corporate veil is pierceable where the company is used as an instrument for customs fraud or laundering.
