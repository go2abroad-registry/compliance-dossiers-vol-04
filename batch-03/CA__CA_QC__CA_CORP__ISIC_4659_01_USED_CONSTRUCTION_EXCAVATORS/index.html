E-government filing portal

The Canadian statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for a corporation is the 9-digit Business Number (BN) supplemented by program account codes (e.g., RT0001 for GST/HST, RC0001 for corporate income tax, and a Quebec QST program code issued by Revenu Québec). Step 1: determine whether the non-resident entity is 'carrying on business' in Canada/Quebec or is making taxable supplies in Canada. Step 2: register online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) for the BN, corporate income tax account (RC), and GST/HST account (RT); non-residents without a Canadian address must file CRA Form RC391 (Application to Register a Non-Resident Business) and may designate a Canadian representative. Step 4: registration is mandatory when quarterly-measured taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in Canada (GST/HST) or in Quebec (QST), or immediately for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada that does not qualify for the small-supplier exception (Excise Tax Act, subsection 240(1.1)). Step 5: retain the BN/QST registration confirmation, foreign articles of incorporation, and evidence of the Quebec permanent establishment for subsequent filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not employ a US-style 'check-the-box' election; a corporation is a separate taxpayer by operation of law. Relevant elections available to a used-machinery trading corporation include: (i) Income Tax Act (ITA) section 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of capital property to a corporation; (ii) ITA section 22 election on the sale of accounts receivable; (iii) Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 election to deem supplies between closely related registrants to be for nil consideration (GST/HST neutrality); (iv) ETA section 227 Quick Method election for simplified GST/HST accounting, generally not attractive for exporters; (v) Quebec equivalents under the Quebec Sales Tax Act (section 1699) and the Taxation Act (section 85 parallel); and (vi) mandatory hybrid mismatch adjustment provisions under ITA sections 18.4-18.6, for which no elective carve-out exists. Treaty benefits are claimed on returns rather than by unilateral election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A sale of used hydraulic excavators or rollers is zero-rated for GST/HST under ETA section 39(1) and Schedule VI, Part V, section 1, and for QST under section 59(1) of the Quebec Sales Tax Act, where the vendor ships or delivers the goods directly to a non-resident address outside Canada and obtains proof of export 'as part of the supply' (direct export). For indirect exports, where a non-resident purchaser takes delivery in Canada, zero-rating applies only if the goods are exported within 60 days of the sale and the vendor retains the export documentation (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 14-1). Ancillary services supplied to a non-resident in respect of the exported goods (e.g., offshore inspection or freight) may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 5-7 and QST equivalents. The exporting registrant may claim input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated export, generating net GST/QST refunds through ordinary returns.

Civil penalties under the ETA include: subsection 285(1) net-tax deficiency penalty (5% of the understated amount plus 2% per month, capped at 20%); subsection 285.1(1) false statement penalty (25% of the tax that would have been evaded); and parallel QST penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act. Under ITA subsection 163(2), making a false statement on the corporate return carries a penalty of 50% of the income tax understated. Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/QST under ETA section 323 and the Quebec Sales Tax Act. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline under exchange control law; the 180-day settlement benchmark is adopted to mitigate CRA audit risk that export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days evidence non-arm's-length financing or uncollected proceeds subject to income tax recharacterization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating, fully convertible Canadian dollar with no exchange-control regime on the current or capital account. Profit remittance by a Quebec business corporation is unrestricted, subject to Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding (25% statutory, reduced to 5-15% under tax treaties, e.g., Canada-US Convention) and to standard corporate filings (T1146/T1134). Inbound acquisitions or the establishment of a new non-cultural Canadian business trigger an Investment Canada Act post-closing notification within 37 days of implementation; review thresholds generally do not apply to a wholesale used-machinery trading entity. Section 261 of the Income Tax Act permits a USD functional-currency election, materially simplifying treasury accounting for USD-dominated heavy equipment trades.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be retained offshore or repatriated freely. For tax purposes, export revenue is recognized in the corporation's functional currency (default CAD), and a s. 261 election is available where USD is the predominant operating currency. Banks and PSPs must file electronic funds transfer reports with FINTRAC for cross-border wires of CAD 10,000 or more, requiring accurate remitter/beneficiary identification, invoice references, and a clear transaction purpose code on each MT103.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and currency authority; foreign-exchange policy and administration rest with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Act; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT and cross-border EFT reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payments clearing infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada (Lynx, ACSS, Real-Time Rail).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability, with material legal constraints. Canada has no standalone electronic money institution licence; non-bank payment service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and cannot hold client escrow deposits, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide regulated bank-issued FX derivatives. Fintech rails are suitable only for operational sub-CAD 100,000 supplier disbursements and not for capital escrow, LC issuance, or treasury hedging, which must remain with a Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal Part I rate: 38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction = 15%; provincial Quebec general rate: 11.5%; combined headline rate: 26.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Federal Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on dividends (deemed or actual taxable dividends) paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada (ITA s.212(2)); no separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

T2 must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (ITA s.150(1)). Federal balance of Part I tax payable: due two months after year-end, or three months for a CCPC that is eligible for the small business deduction.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (Quebec Tax Administration Act s.1000). Provincial balance due: two months after year-end generally, or three months for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4647): mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds C$750 million. Master File: mandatory for fiscal periods beginning on or after 1 April 2022 where group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds C$1 billion and the Canadian member has cross-border related-party revenues or expenses of at least C$100 million, or cross-border related-party financing balances of at least C$25 million. Local File / contemporaneous documentation under ITA s.247(4): required where any individual related-party transaction, or the aggregate cross-border related-party transactions, exceed C$1 million; documentation must exist before the filing deadline to constitute 'reasonable efforts' and avoid the 10% penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation regime; substance is nonetheless enforced through (a) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245; penalty of 25% of the disallowed tax benefit) and its Quebec counterpart (Quebec Tax Administration Act s.1075.7), (b) the corporate residence test based on central management and control (ITA s.250), (c) the BEPS Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test for treaty benefits, and (d) the requirement for a genuine Canadian establishment with board minutes, personnel, bank accounts, and transaction records in Quebec. A mail-drop office with no operative substance in Quebec will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / wilful misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil: gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated federal tax (ITA s.163(2)) plus interest; the same penalty applies provincially under the Quebec Tax Administration Act. Criminal: ITA s.239(1) – fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or direct concealed sales face personal criminal liability for tax evasion; courts may impose travel bans and passport surrender where flight risk exists. The corporate veil may be pierced for fraud or abuse of the corporate personality under art. 317 of the Civil Code of Quebec, exposing directors to civil restitution.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA re-determination to the arm's length amount plus a 10% penalty on the net transfer pricing adjustment (ITA s.247(3)) where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; interest applies from the taxation year. Quebec applies parallel adjustment powers.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically assessed for transfer pricing penalties; however, deliberate orchestration of non-arm's length prices with intent to evade tax attracts criminal liability under s.239, personal fines, and civil delictual liability to the corporation.
