Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes (ITA s. 96): the LP itself is not subject to Part I tax or Ontario corporate tax; each partner is allocated its share of income/loss on Form T5013 and is taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source partnership income (ITA s. 115(1)(a.1)) and are subject to Part XIII withholding on specified partnership payments. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and must charge/collect HST on taxable supplies. Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; Ontario does not impose an entity-level tax on LPs, and there is no Ontario branch tax. Special deeming rules in ITA ss. 96(11)-(12) apply where all partners are non-residents (the LP can be treated as a non-resident corporation for capital gains purposes).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST specified-member election under ETA s. 273 (one general partner, typically the Canadian-nexus partner, files on behalf of the LP and claims input tax credits); (ii) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election allowing tax-deferred transfer of property to the LP; (iii) ITA s. 96(6) election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (iv) ETA s. 21 simplified (quick) accounting for qualifying small registrants with worldwide taxable supplies of CDN 400,000 or less; and (v) statutory hybrid-mismatch controls under ITA Part I (ss. 12.7, 18.6 and 20.2), which disallow deductions or impose a 15% non-resident withholding where a payment is deductible in Canada but the corresponding receipt is disregarded or untaxed in a foreign jurisdiction. OECD Pillar Two (Global Minimum Tax Act, 15% domestic top-up for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million) is not expected to bind the projected small-scale LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CDN 30,000 — a person ceases to be a 'small supplier' once its global taxable supplies exceed CDN 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148); registration is mandatory within 29 days. A non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Canada (e.g., maintaining Ontario inventory for fulfilment) must register regardless of the threshold if it makes taxable supplies in Canada; the small-supplier exception does not apply to goods imported for sale. A non-resident that is not carrying on a Canadian commercial activity may still voluntarily register to claim input tax credits (ITCs).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (3D printers, resin filaments) sold from an Ontario warehouse and shipped by the LP to a non-resident address outside Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, provided the goods are exported within a reasonable time and the LP retains documentary proof of export. Consumer parcels sent by courier or post (CN22/CN23) qualify if the foreign destination is evidenced. Digital/intangible supplies (e.g., downloadable CAD files, firmware, remote support) are zero-rated under Part V s. 10 where supplied to a non-resident and not for use in Canada. Services performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada, or in respect of Canadian real property, remain taxable even if the customer is a non-resident (Sch. VI Part V ss. 6-7). The LP must also maintain evidence of the customer's non-resident status (shipping address, IP/geo-verification and Stripe/Shopify payment records).

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (E14 / CES or ACI e-manifest) for goods over CDN 2,000; courier/postal manifests (CN22/CN23) for smaller consumer export parcels
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Ontario LP (name/BN, description, quantity, foreign destination, HST zero-rating code X)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing delivery to a foreign address
  • Foreign settlement proofs (Stripe/Shopify payout records, SWIFT/remittance statements) linking payment to the exported order

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no legal e-invoicing mandate currently exists in Canada or Ontario. The CRA and Ontario participate in the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoice framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard; adoption is optional. Legally compliant records are paper, PDF, EDI or Peppol XML invoices that satisfy ETA s. 236(1) and s. 286 (books and records must be available for inspection, generally retained for six years, and accessible from Canada). For zero-rated export sales, the supporting export documentation (customs declaration, commercial invoice, waybill) serves as the required 'supporting document' for ITC entitlement under ETA s. 169(2)(b).

Canada does not codify a statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflects the common documentary/banking convention and CRA audit practice. Export proceeds retained offshore may nonetheless be reassessed as unreported Canadian-source income or as an allocation to partners on the T5013 with interest (prescribed rate) and penalties. If the export zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA re-characterizes the supply as taxable at 13% HST, recaptures input tax credits claimed on the related inputs (ETA reassessment), and applies late-filing interest and arrears penalties. ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty equals the greater of CDN 250 and 25% of the understated net tax; criminal evasion under ETA s. 280 carries fines up to CDN 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years (indictable election). CRA may also require a non-resident registrant to post security (ETA s. 299.1) where collection is at risk. Export documentation must be retained for six years to withstand audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada operates a liberalized current-account and capital-account regime under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, and Canada Business Corporations Act. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on cross-border capital movements, and no profit-remittance hurdles for non-resident investors, apart from statutory withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 15-25%, reduced by tax treaty) and mandatory reporting for statistical and anti-money-laundering purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts. Receipts must be reported for balance-of-payments statistics to Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System, and any settlement exceeding CAD 10,000 in a single EFT transaction must be reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex may be used for high-frequency, low-value cross-border settlement and multi-currency payment routing. They do not hold Canadian banking licenses, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot provide escrow or credit facilities. A non-resident EMI that solicits Canadian clients must register as a money-services business with FINTRAC; otherwise it is prohibited from servicing Canadian entities. EMIs are viable as auxiliary clearing tools only, while the primary treasury account should remain with a Tier-1 Canadian chartered bank.

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the investor's bank to the Ontario LP's settlement account held at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level (Limited Partnership is a flow-through); combined federal-provincial general corporate rate for a corporate partner is 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on tax-deemed partnership distributions because a Limited Partnership is a flow-through entity. For an actual dividend paid by an Ontario corporation to a non-resident corporate shareholder, the statutory Part XIII rate is 25% of the gross dividend, reduced to 5% under the Canada–US Treaty for a beneficial owner holding at least 10% voting shares, and 15% for other treaty residents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA contemporaneous documentation is expected for cross-border transactions with non-arm's length non-residents where the aggregate value of transactions with each related party exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. This is an administrative threshold informing Local File and Master File maintenance. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for an ultimate parent entity whose group consolidated revenue is at least CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies economic substance through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (ITA s. 245) and judicial doctrines of sham and artificiality. An Ontario Limited Partnership must demonstrate real business activity in Canada, substantive management and decision-making, and a genuine non-tax business purpose. A hollow entity with no employees, premises, functions, assets, or operational risks in Canada may be disregarded; allocations to a non-resident limited partner can be recharacterized. Limited partners must remain passive; the general partner must have substantive operations and assume real liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest; criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) may result in fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA collection actions include garnishment of accounts, seizure and sale of assets under s. 225, and liens against partnership property.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors may be jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 160(1.1) for transfers of partnership property, or under s. 227.1 for unremitted withheld taxes. Personal assets, bank accounts, and property may be subject to restitution orders. Subsection 226(1) authorizes a travel ban preventing departure from Canada until fines and penalties are secured; criminal convictions produce permanent records and further restraint orders.
Artificial Arrangement / GAAR / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR under ITA s. 245: the tax benefit is denied, the transaction is recharacterized, and tax, interest, and penalties are recomputed. If the transaction is a reportable transaction not properly disclosed, a penalty under s. 245(8) of 10% of the tax benefit (or 50% for anti-avoidance rules) applies. The partnership may be disregarded under the sham doctrine and taxed as a deemed corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors, promoters, and parties with knowledge of the tax benefit may be personally assessed under s. 160(2) for the benefit transferred to the partnership. Personal exposure extends to civil restitution, penalties, and interest. In egregious cases, CRA may apply travel bans under s. 226 and pursue criminal charges under s. 239 for fraudulent arrangements, with imprisonment available upon conviction.
AML / Currency or Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA up to CAD 500,000 for a first violation and up to CAD 1,000,000 for subsequent violations; failure to report a suspicious transaction is an indictable offence subject to fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years. FINTRAC may issue compliance orders and revoke or suspend payment services access.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and senior officers who knew or exercised reasonable diligence should have known are personally liable under PCMLTFA s. 74(2). Money laundering convictions under Criminal Code s. 462.31 can result in imprisonment up to ten years, asset forfeiture, disgorgement, and travel restrictions under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act for non-residents. Personal bank accounts and cross-border movement are scrutinized by FINTRAC and CBSA.
