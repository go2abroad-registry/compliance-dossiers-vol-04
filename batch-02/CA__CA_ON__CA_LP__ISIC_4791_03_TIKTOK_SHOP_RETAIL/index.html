Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — including the GST/HST and Customs and Excise divisions; portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Ontario has no separate VAT agency for federal GST/HST; Ontario Provincial Tax is integrated into HST.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA before or at the time of GST/HST registration. The LP must file an RC1 form or apply electronically via CRA's Business Registration Online; provide legal entity and partnership particulars, principal place of business, commercial activity in Canada, and Canadian agent/resident representative if applicable. The CRA will enregister the LP for GST/HST under a separate account number. For non-residents, a resident representative or agent may be required under section 11 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under the Income Tax Act a Limited Partnership is a flow-through vehicle; it is not a separate taxable entity for federal income tax. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership is a separate 'person' and can be a registrant in its own right. Non-resident limited partners may be subject to Part I/XIV Canadian tax on Canadian-source income unless treaty relief applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Canadian Limited Partnership is per se pass-through and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. No general partnership classification election is available. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4, 18.5, 18.6 and 18.7 of the Income Tax Act may apply to disallow deductions where a payment is treated inconsistently by Canadian and foreign jurisdictions; no affirmative hybrid 'election' is available other than ordinary partnership agreement allocations and GST/HST joint venture elections under section 273 ETA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or in the current quarter, for the small-supplier exception; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada or soliciting orders for digital or tangible property may be required to register at the same threshold, with special mandatory registration rules for non-resident suppliers into Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI of the ETA when the goods are exported from Canada and delivered to a non-resident outside Canada, or exported by the seller or an agent in a continuous commercial shipment, or delivered by common carrier to a non-resident. For services, zero-rating applies where the service is wholly performed outside Canada or is supplied in respect of exported goods. Documentary evidence must demonstrate the actual export of the goods, including proof of domestic supply to a non-resident and export 'as soon as reasonably possible' after the sale.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Commercial Invoice and Sale Contract (including TikTok Shop platform order report)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Cargo Control Document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX counterparty credit confirmation
  • TikTok Shop payout settlement statement identifying overseas consumer payment
  • Evidence of delivery outside Canada (carrier proof of GC/Canada exit)

No Canadian fiscal clearance or real-time government stamping mandate exists. CRA does not operate a national e-invoicing clearance platform or require API-based fiscal stamping. Registered taxpayers must provide prescribed GST/HST invoice information under ETA sections 221/222, and may issue electronic invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE/XML/EDI) for registrants above the threshold. There is no PEPPOL or invoice clearance obligation.

CRA may reassess zero-rated exports as taxable at 13% HST where documentation is incomplete, within a 4-year normal reassessment period and a 10-year period for gross negligence or misrepresentation, with daily compound interest. Penalties under ETA section 285 can reach 50% of understated tax for false statements or gross negligence; separate penalties apply for failure to register, collect, file and remit, up to $250 plus stranding tax deductions. Criminal sanctions under ETA sections 327/328 include fines of up to $100,000, imprisonment of up to 5 years, and the perpetuator may be permanently debarred from zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; there is no exchange-control law, no central-bank approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, and no restriction on profit repatriation by non-resident limited partners of an Ontario Limited Partnership. The only constraints are indirect and fiscal: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes non-resident withholding tax on Canadian-source payments (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., to 0–15% for interest, royalties, and dividends under the Canada–United States Tax Convention and similar treaties); (ii) the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) requires FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and for large cash transactions; (iii) FATCA/CRS beneficial-ownership and account reporting applies to non-resident partners; and (iv) Statistics Canada may require balance-of-payments declarations for corporate international transactions above the applicable reporting threshold (commonly CAD 1 million per reporting period). None of these measures limits convertibility, timing, or amount of capital injections, trade disbursements, or exit distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio; a Canadian exporter or sell-side platform merchant may lawfully retain foreign-currency receivables in a foreign-currency account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or a FINTRAC-registered money services business, convert at any licensed financial intermediary, or deploy proceeds directly without seeking approval from the Bank of Canada or any other authority. The only applicable compliance duties are transactional and statistical: filing FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reports for wire transfers at or above CAD 10,000, reporting cross-border currency movements under the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations, and, where applicable, providing Statistics Canada with balance-of-payments survey data for international trade and financial transactions; none of these requires surrender or mandatory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy, Payments Canada for clearing infrastructure, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML and cross-border wire reporting; Canada maintains no dedicated administrative foreign-exchange-control agency comparable to a SAFE or DEA, as the Canadian dollar operates under a fully liberalized, free-floating regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for a member of the underlying social-commerce settlement system operating in Canada through a licensed, FINTRAC-registered payment entity (e.g., Wise Canada US Inc./Wise Payments Limited as an authorized money services business), and LOW for wholly offshore digital EMIs that lack a Canadian registration nexus. Legal constraints: under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and its associated transitional regulatory PPR introduced by the Bank of Canada, any payment service provider that performs retail payment functions with a meaningful connection to Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider; offshore EMIs may not lawfully provide payment accounts or settlement services to Canadian merchants without satisfying this registration and the PCMLTFA foreign-MSB registration duty. For the TikTok Shop retail model, the platform-directed payout rail (including TikTok_Shop_Payout and Wise_B2B) is acceptable as a pass-through settlement channel, but the Ontario Limited Partnership must maintain a Schedule I domestic bank account as the primary liquidity and escrow anchor, with the EMI rail limited to subsidiary disbursement, and must obtain evidence of segregation of client funds and daily reconciliation statements.
