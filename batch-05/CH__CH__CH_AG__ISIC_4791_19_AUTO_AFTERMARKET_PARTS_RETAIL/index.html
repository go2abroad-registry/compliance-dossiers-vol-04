Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni) via the SuisseTax portal and eServices; cantonal tax administration of the registered seat for profit/capital tax compliance.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the AG is statutorily a stock corporation with separate legal personality and is resident for tax purposes when its registered office and/or actual central administration are in Switzerland; resident companies are subject to unlimited taxation of worldwide profits at the federal level (8.5% effective after federal tax) plus cantonal/communal profit taxes, while income from foreign permanent establishments and foreign real property is assigned to treaty-based territorial releases; capital tax applies at cantonal level; shareholder dividends are subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification regime exists; an AG is per se an entity-level taxed corporation. Swiss tax law provides for a tax group election for VAT (group registration) and a corporate income tax group (profit and capital tax allocation) when a parent holds at least 50% of the voting rights and operates a valid group structure under Swiss law. Hybrid mismatch rules follow BEPS recommendations but no direct tax-classification election is available for the AG itself; cantons may grant innovation-oriented 'deemed equity' notional interest relief, and the participation exemption can be claimed by holding and operating companies. Swiss entity classification for foreign shareholders follows Swiss domestic law and may be scrutinized under CFC/hybrid rules of the shareholder's jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT) from supplies in Switzerland for Swiss-resident businesses; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Switzerland also must register when Swiss turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, except for suppliers of electronic services to private Swiss consumers, where the same CHF 100,000 threshold applies but a simplified non-resident electronic services registration procedure exists; voluntary registration is available for non-residents without the threshold if the supplies are primarily in Switzerland and refund needs arise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated at 0% VAT when ownership transfers to a foreign purchaser and the goods are irrevocably exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination by the supplier, the foreign customer, or a third party on behalf of either; evidence must establish exportation. Services incidental to exported goods (including freight, storage, handling, customs clearance and quality inspection) may also be zero-rated. For online aftermarket retail of automotive parts or tuning accessories, margins and wrappers used in shipping, vouchers, and drop-shipping structures must be independently documented; domestic delivery to forwarding agents in Switzerland does not automatically qualify for zero rating unless a valid customs export declaration and transport evidence identify the consignee and destination abroad.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Autoeinfuhrzollanmeldung / export declaration opRIFN) with customs acceptance date and shipment reference
  • Origin copy or electronic equivalent of the bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, post/parcel declaration, or combined transport document evidencing exit from Switzerland
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, containing the full part number, quantity, sales value, currency, consignee and country of destination
  • Proof of dispatch from Swiss business records or the party arranging export, including warehouse release order or picking list
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice confirming the foreign customer's payment in the agreed foreign currency and account route (best practice; not a standalone Swiss statutory prerequisite)

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing API, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Switzerland; the QR-bill and eBill are interoperable private-sector electronic billing standards, not fiscal conveyor systems with mandatory tax authority validation. Every VAT invoice must contain the supplier's UID, the recipient's identification if the invoice exceeds CHF 300, the invoice date, the supply description, the consideration, and the applicable VAT rate or exemption reference. Auditable accounting systems under the Swiss Code of Obligations provide an audit trail, but there is no continuous transaction control pre-clearance obligation.

The Federal Tax Administration may retroactively reassess Swiss VAT at the standard 8.1% if the export substantiation bundle is incomplete, an unregistered agent intercepts the goods in Switzerland, or the sale is deemed to have occurred domestically; the taxable amount is subject to VAT plus default interest and, in bad-faith cases, surcharges up to four times the evaded tax under VAT criminal law. Fraudulent procurement of zero-rating, fictitious export declarations, or use of forged customs documents triggers criminal proceedings under Swiss customs and VAT provisions with monetary penalties, asset seizure, potential debarment from further export, and fines for individuals and directors. Audit scrutiny focuses on shipping timestamps, destination inconsistencies, cryptocurrency or IFZA? mismatches? payment mediation via Stripe/PayPal, and incomplete copy from remittance records; all documentation must be retained for at least 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland provides unqualified current- and capital-account convertibility; CHF is fully freely floating and freely transferable with no exchange-control statute, no FX allocation system, and no restrictions on dividend distributions, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds payable to foreign shareholders beyond the standard 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax, which may be reduced or reclaimed under treaty or statutory relief rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable: Swiss law imposes no mandatory repatriation timeline, no surrender/conversion ratio, and no FX requirement for export receipts. Exporters retain complete discretion over currency and bank placement; only statistical reporting of cross-border payments to the Swiss National Bank (payment statistics surveys) and universal anti-money-laundering documentation duties under the GwG apply.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank), the independent central bank of Switzerland, responsible for monetary policy and the SIC/SIC+ payment rails, with concurrent AML/supervisory competence held by FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) over licensed banks and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium legal viability: foreign digital EMIs (e.g. UK/EU passport holders) cannot passport into Switzerland as credit institutions for CHF SIC settlement and may not hold funds for a Swiss AG without a FINMA banking licence, a Fintech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act (deposit cap CHF 100m), or a licence under the 2023 Payment Services Act. As a low-risk e-commerce merchant, CH AG may use a qualified EMI solely as an auxiliary collection/FX conversion rail for Stripe/PayPal receivables, provided gross settlement and liquidity buffers remain in Swiss domestic banks.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal: 8.5%; cantonal and communal rates vary by principal seat; representative combined effective rate in Zug: 11.9%; national average: approximately 14.6%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer); refundable/reduced to treaty rate for foreign beneficial owners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss administrative practice expects contemporaneous Local File when cross-border intercompany transactions exceed CHF 1,000,000 in a fiscal year, intangible payments exceed CHF 250,000, related-party interest-bearing loans exceed CHF 5,000,000, or when transfer prices can be challenged by audit; a Master File is expected for groups with consolidated turnover above CHF 500,000,000; CbCR filing threshold follows OECD at EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no EU-style Economic Substance Regulations, but federal anti-abuse doctrine and OECD BEPS require real entrepreneurial substance for treaty relief and corporate income tax treatment: a Swiss AG must have its registered office in Switzerland, a resident board, maintained books, active business premises, personnel who take board decisions, and bank account. Holding companies benefit form relief only if participation minimums are met and no artificial profit shifting; inadequate substance leads to denial of treaty benefits, hidden dividend recharacterisation and 35% WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Omission (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Evasion surcharge of 33% to 50% of evaded federal/cantonal tax plus default interest at 4% per annum; aggravated fraud using forged documents or sophisticated deception qualifies under Art. 186 DBG with imprisonment up to 5 years and criminal fine of up to 30% of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Board members may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes if the AG is insolvent and directors breached prudence; travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment up to 5 years for personal participation.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Structuring

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, mandatory tax reassessment under profit-adjustment provisions, cancellation of tax rulings; penalty up to 30% of underpaid tax and disgorgement refund of WHT; Swiss commercial registry may delete company for inactive shell status.

Director personal liability: Directors/beneficial owners exposed to personal liability for unlawful wealth arrangement, asset tracing, cross-border claw-back actions, Interpol notices for money laundering, and exclusion from board functions.
Failure to Withhold Swiss Verrechnungssteuer

Statutory sanction: FTA plus late interest; criminal offence of failing to withhold may be sanctioned with fine up to CHF 100,000 and full collection of underpaid WHT from company; qualified tax fraud triggers imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to the Swiss tax authorities for withheld but unremitted taxes and for the entire WHT debt if tax was intentionally avoided by decision of the board; criminal sanctions include imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering Through E-Commerce

Statutory sanction: Criminal proceedings under Swiss Penal Code (money laundering, Art. 305 bis: imprisonment up to 3 years, fine up to CHF 1,000,000); confiscation of illicit assets; permanent blacklisting from Swiss payment aggregators; civil fines for breach of AML gateway duties.

Director personal liability: Individuals knowingly involved in the scheme may be prosecuted personally; travel bans, mutual legal assistance seizures, possible extradition; no corporate veil protection for criminal proceeds.
False Transfer Pricing Documentation / Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Profit adjustment at full market value; penalty of 33% to 50% of additional tax caused; administrative fine for failure to submit documentation; conditional criminal prosecution if falsified documentation created by directors; cancellation of advantageous cantonal tax treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are personally liable in damages to the company for fiscal restitution and exposed to imprisonment for perjury-like false certification; court may order restitution plus 3 years imprisonment.
