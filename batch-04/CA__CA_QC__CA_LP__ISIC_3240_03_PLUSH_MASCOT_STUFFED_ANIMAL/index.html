Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST and federal tax administration; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial tax administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Revenu Québec Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/mon-dossier-dentreprise/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Québec limited partnership (LP) must first determine whether it carries on business in Canada/Québec and whether its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold. Registration is initiated through the business registration online portal (Business Registration Online) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA; the same application or Form LM-1-V filed with Revenu Québec opens the QST account. The LP must provide the partnership certificate, general partner identification (including Canadian residency status), and non-resident ownership details. The CRA issues a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT); Revenu Québec issues a QST registration number (RQ). For an export-only plush toy manufacturer, registration is generally required to claim zero-rating and recover input tax credits; a non-resident partner with no permanent establishment may require separate Part XIII or Part I tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A Québec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Québec income tax purposes; it is not an entity-level taxpayer and must file an annual information return (T5013 / Relevé 31). Income, gains, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners under the partnership agreement. The partnership itself is, however, a separate registrable person for GST/HST and QST purposes.

Available election forms

None. Canada and Québec do not operate a check-the-box entity classification election. Taxation as a pass-through partnership is statutory and mandatory for an LP unless it is a ‘specified’ investment vehicle with special rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.4–18.6 of the Income Tax Act may recharacterize payments but do not permit elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold) for both GST/HST and QST. A limited partnership exceeding CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies must register. Voluntary registration is permitted for exporters seeking to claim zero-rating and input tax credits; certain non-resident digital services have a nil threshold, though that is not applicable to the plush toy manufacturing export model.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% export rate when they are physically exported from Canada as soon as reasonably possible after delivery by the supplier, or by a commercial carrier on the supplier's behalf, and the supplier retains adequate evidence of export without overstaying or alteration. For custom plush mascots manufactured to foreign customer specifications, zero-rating is available on the sale of finished goods shipped to the non-resident customer. Offshore services separately qualify where performed for a non-resident and not for consumption in Canada. The LP must maintain invoices, purchase orders, proof of foreign payment, and freight/customs documentation.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA Form B13A or EDI export report) evidencing the goods were cleared for export from Canada
  • Commercial Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (e.g., ocean Bill of Lading, airway bill) showing the destination outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice substantiating repatriation of foreign currency sale proceeds to the LP's Canadian bank account within the 180-day deadline

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing platform, or digital stamping applies in Canada/Québec. Invoices must be issued for taxable supplies and contain the prescribed information under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act and section 278 of the Act respecting the Québec Sales Tax. Electronic invoicing is accepted without prior government authorization provided the recipient agrees and the content satisfies disclosure requirements. For exports, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) uses the Canadian International Trade Single Window, eManifest (ACI), and applicable export declarations (e.g., Form B13A) for customs purposes, but this is not a VAT clearance mechanism.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST/QST, interest, and penalties where the LP fails to provide timely, coherent export evidence. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include gross-negligence penalties and fines equal to 15% of any tax evaded; Quebec's Tax Administration Act imposes analogous QST penalties. If zero-rating is overturned retrospectively, the full tax becomes payable, with compounded daily interest and possibly criminal prosecution for fraudulent claims. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended to ten years where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default exists, and partners can incur director/partner liability for unremitted amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the CAD trades freely, with no exchange controls, no import/export currency licensing, no repatriation quotas, and no multi-tiered FX rates. Non-resident limited partners may inject capital, trade, and repatriate profits without government approval. The only frictions are tax compliance: Part I income tax on the LP's Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners, treaty-reduced where no permanent establishment exists; Part XIII withholding (standard 25%, treaty-reduced) applies only to passive payments such as interest, royalties, and management fees, not to active-business profit distributions. Profit remittance by a Quebec LP is structurally unblocked but requires the partnership to issue T5013 partnership information returns and NR4/T5013 supplementary slips to foreign partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. A stuffed-plush-toy OEM exporter may hold CAD or foreign currency receipts indefinitely in a Schedule I or Schedule II bank account. Only transparency filings apply: T1135 foreign income verification statement when the aggregate cost amount of foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000, and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border EFTs above CAD 10,000. No central-bank FX intervention in the CAD has occurred since 1998.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and final settlement agent; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority in Canada. Auxiliary regulators: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential licensing of Schedule I banks; FINTRAC for AML/CFT and cross-border EFT reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA); Quebec Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for provincial licensing of money-services businesses, banks, and trust companies.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM — permitted but subordinated. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partners remit equity directly to the LP's operational or capital-escrow account at a Schedule I bank in Quebec via SWIFT MT103; the USD leg settles over CHIPS/Fedwire through the bank's U.S. correspondent. Wire field 70 must read 'LP capital contribution — [partner name]'; no Canadian FX approval or license is required, but the receiving bank performs an EDD source-of-funds check and FINTRAC EFT reporting applies above CAD 10,000. Capital calls are GST/HST-neutral and are credited to partner capital accounts (not taxable income). Non-resident partners are screened against the Investment Canada Act (ICA): establishment of a new Canadian business triggers a notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED); review is required only above the CAD 1.354 billion enterprise-value threshold for WTO investors (2025), inapplicable to a plush-toy OEM. Quebec requires the LP to file changes of partners with the REQ within 60 days.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV direct wires for supplier invoices (plush fabric, accessories, packaging) and freight forwarders; same-day settlement via Payment Canada's Lynx RTGS for CAD payments or CHIPS/Fedwire for USD; SWIFT GPI end-to-end tracking (UETR) confirms beneficiary deposit. For high-value recurring OEM orders from Greater China, issue irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707), sight or 30–90 days deferred payment, with Incoterms CFR or FOB China ports; the bank's trade desk coordinates document checking (B/L, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin). Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), all payment service providers operating in Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada, with the registration window for existing PSPs closing on 2025-09-08; interaction with an unregistered offshore EMI is an AML red flag and triggers enhanced due diligence at the LP's primary bank. Strategic use: keep primary liquidity and capital at a Schedule I bank; use a single EMI only for fragmented multi-currency sub-wallets below CAD 50,000 aggregate.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Quebec limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not pay corporate income tax on income allocated to its partners. For a taxable corporate partner, the general corporate rates are 15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec, generally 26.5% combined, subject to the partner's status, income character, and applicable deductions. The federal small-business rate is 9% and the Quebec small-business rate is generally 3.2% for qualifying corporations; these rates do not apply automatically to an LP or its partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The ordinary Canadian Part XIII withholding rate on dividends paid or deemed paid to a non-resident is 25%, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty. An LP distribution is not automatically a dividend: the partnership's income is generally allocated to partners under partnership tax rules. Canadian withholding may apply to amounts allocated or paid to non-resident partners under the applicable provisions and regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Canadian transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act section 247, contemporaneous documentation is generally required for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents. The principal documentation timing rule requires preparation by the taxpayer's documentation-due date, generally the tax-return filing deadline, and production within three months after a written CRA request. Canada does not impose a general OECD Local File/Master File threshold for all taxpayers. Country-by-country reporting applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million (or the prescribed Canadian-dollar equivalent) and a Canadian-resident reporting entity or designated surrogate, subject to statutory conditions.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general regime called Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Canadian tax outcomes may nevertheless be challenged under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-ownership principles, sham doctrine, specific anti-avoidance provisions, and applicable treaty anti-abuse rules. Maintain evidence of actual functions, assets, risks, decision-making, personnel, and commercial purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file partnership information returns

Statutory sanction: Federal and Quebec late, information-return, and demand-related penalties may apply, together with interest and reassessment. The amount depends on the applicable statute, filing history, duration, and number of slips; intentional conduct may attract materially higher penalties.

Director personal liability: An LP has no directors as such. A general partner and responsible persons may face statutory liability where a provision applies, including for unremitted amounts or their own false statements or conduct. Limited partners are not automatically personally liable for partnership tax solely by reason of being partners, but liability can arise under partnership law, guarantees, or their own misconduct.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247, assess resulting tax and interest, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty generally equal to 10% of specified adjustments where the statutory threshold and conditions are met. Recharacterization and other penalties may also apply where legally available.

Director personal liability: The LP itself has no directors. A general partner or responsible person may incur liability for personal participation in a false filing or other offence. Civil liability does not arise automatically merely because an individual manages or owns an interest in the LP.
Failure to withhold or remit non-resident tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unremitted withholding, interest, and statutory penalties. Treaty relief does not excuse failure to obtain and retain sufficient evidence supporting the reduced rate.

Director personal liability: Where statutory director-liability rules apply to a corporate payer, directors may be jointly and severally liable subject to statutory defences and limitation periods. For an LP, analyze the identity of the legal payer and the general partner's duties; personal liability is not automatic for every limited partner.
Tax evasion, false statements, or tax fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution may result in fines, imprisonment, or both under applicable federal or Quebec legislation, in addition to reassessment, interest, civil penalties, and potential forfeiture or collection measures. Exact sentencing depends on the offence and facts.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, participate in, or acquiesce in an offence may be prosecuted personally. Imprisonment is not imposed merely for holding office or partnership status; travel restrictions require a specific statutory or court basis.
Misuse of limited partnership structure or undisclosed beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include loss of limited-liability protection where partnership law provides, civil claims, tax reassessment, beneficial-ownership or registry penalties, and prosecution where the conduct constitutes an offence.

Director personal liability: A limited partner's exposure depends on Quebec partnership law and the actual conduct; a general partner ordinarily bears the partnership's obligations subject to applicable law. Veil-piercing, restitution, imprisonment, or travel bans are not automatic and require a legal basis and applicable proceedings.
Anti-money-laundering or sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Sanctions and proceeds-of-crime offences may carry substantial fines, imprisonment, asset restraint or forfeiture, and other statutory consequences. FINTRAC obligations apply only where the person and activity fall within a regulated reporting-entity category.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate in or authorize an offence may be personally liable. Corporate or partnership status does not immunize personal conduct; travel bans or imprisonment require statutory authority and due process.
