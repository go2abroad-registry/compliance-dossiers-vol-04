In Ontario (CA_ON), co-administering authorities include the Ontario Ministry of Finance for the provincial component of the Harmonized Sales Tax and for provincial partner-level tax compliance, and ServiceOntario / Ontario Business Registry for limited partnership statutory registration, annual returns, and the required agent for service of process.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario limited partnership ('LP') must complete the following registration cascade: (1) Register the LP under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16 through the Ontario Business Registry, obtaining an Ontario LP registration number; no statutory minimum capital is required, but an Ontario head office and an Ontario-resident attorney/agent for service of process are mandatory; (2) Apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number ('BN') by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts), enclosing the LP declaration, register of general and limited partners, and formation documents of each non-resident partner; (3) Open the GST/HST program account (BN program code 'RT') within one month of the day the LP ceases to qualify as a 'small supplier'; voluntary registration is available to the exporter before the threshold is met in order to claim input tax credits on UHT plant construction and packaging equipment; (4) File annually Form T5013 and T5013SUM (Partnership Information Return) with the CRA within five months after fiscal year-end, allocating Canadian-source business income among general and limited partners; (5) Each non-resident partner is separately assessable under Part I on its allocable share of Ontario business income; treaty relief forms (e.g., Form NR301-NR303) are supplementary documentation and do not replace the LP-level registration obligations. Payment rails (SWIFT LC / TT Wire) must be documented to produce remittance evidence for the partners and the CRA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no material exchange controls, no advance approvals for most outward or inward remittances, and no restrictions on profit distribution, interest, dividends, or capital repatriation. Non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on certain Canadian-source passive income and distributions characterized as interest, rent, or royalty, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. Active business income allocated to a Canadian limited partnership is generally taxed at the partner level, and a non-resident partner may be subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source business income; profit remittance itself is legally unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules, no prescribed mandatory conversion timelines, and no requirement to repatriate export receipts to Canada. Export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts at Canadian authorized banks. Reporting obligations apply only for cross-border currency transactions involving large cash amounts under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including a CAD 10,000 or equivalent threshold for transaction reporting to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; the foreign exchange function is administered under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act, with banking regulatory oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a centralized foreign exchange control authority or exchange permit regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Offshore digital E-money institutions and non-bank payment service providers are not licensed as banks in Canada and cannot accept deposits in the legal sense. Cross-border EMIs may operate in a limited manner under the Retail Payment Activities Act once licensed by the Bank of Canada, or under provincial money services business regulations, but they generally face restrictions on deposit-taking, payment clearing settlement, and correspondent banking access. Tier-1 bank accounts are strongly preferred for capital escrow and trade finance, with EMIs acceptable only for small operational disbursements after a successful Bank of Canada retail payment registration and FINTRAC MSB registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder or limited partner capital contributions should be routed through the correspondent banking network to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian operating account using a SWIFT MT103 with a clearly stated payment purpose code, typically referencing 'Business Investment / Capital Contribution' and the invoice or capital call reference number. The remitting foreign bank must include the Ontario LP's registered legal name and Canadian bank account details. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving bank will report the transaction under FINTRAC rules; the bank may also require a copy of the capital call notice, the limited partnership agreement, a board resolution or general partner resolution authorizing the contribution, and proof of tax identification numbers. No exchange control approval is required in Canada, but the bank's compliance may require a source of funds declaration and a sworn statement confirming the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime.Trade disbursements for imported packaging materials, UHT processing machinery, and oat-based ingredients should be executed through standard SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for non-documentary payments and through Commercial Letters of Credit for medium-to-large trade settlements where vendor performance risk mitigation is necessary. Tier-1 Canadian banks can issue irrevocable confirmed letters of credit, and all trade settlement flows must comply with the bank's Customer Due Diligence requirements, including a sanctions check on the beneficiary bank and an end-use declaration. Outgoing international transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 may be reported to FINTRAC, and the bank must be provided with the underlying purchase order or proforma invoice to support the 'payment against goods' purpose.
