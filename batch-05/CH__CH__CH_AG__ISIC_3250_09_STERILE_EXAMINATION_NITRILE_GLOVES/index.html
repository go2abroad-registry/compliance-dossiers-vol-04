Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; UID register portal: www.uid.admin.ch; customs and export declarations via the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (EZV / Office fédéral de la douane).

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is registered in the Cantonal Commercial Register, and the statutory identification number is automatically assigned by the Federal Statistical Office as the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID), formatted CH-xxx. For VAT purposes, the AG must electronically register with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV) under Art. 64 of the Swiss VAT Act by submitting the UID and business activity data; ESTV then confirms the UID as the VAT registration number. A non-resident foreign enterprise must apply directly to ESTV through a Swiss tax representative, obtain its own UID, and register for VAT when its taxable supplies in Switzerland exceed the statutory turnover threshold; the representative serves as the addressee for VAT assessments, correspondence and refund procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box regime. The CH_AG is a per se taxable corporation under Swiss law and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available elections relevant to the AG include: (i) VAT group treatment (Gruppenbesteuerung) under Art. 13 para. 3 of the Swiss VAT Act for related entities under common direction; (ii) option to tax otherwise exempt supplies under Art. 22 of the Swiss VAT Act; (iii) saldo taxation (flat-rate reduction of input VAT) under Art. 37 of the Swiss VAT Act for smaller taxpayers; and (iv) Swiss corporate participation relief for dividends and qualifying capital gains. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid rules apply to prevent double non-taxation involving foreign transparent or opaque entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7.7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual net taxable turnover arising from Swiss supplies (Art. 21 para. 1 of the Swiss VAT Act). Resident suppliers assess total Swiss taxable turnover; non-resident suppliers must register when turnover generated from taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000. Registration is also mandatory for exporters claiming zero-rating or seeking input VAT recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods, including powder-free sterile nitrile examination gloves, qualify for the 0% export VAT rate under Art. 23 para. 1 of the Swiss VAT Act if they are physically transported from Swiss customs territory to a foreign destination and the supplier obtains an accepted export customs declaration plus transport evidence. Services supplied to a foreign business recipient with a place of supply abroad are outside Swiss VAT or taxed at 0% where documented; the supplier must prove the recipient is established abroad and the service is actually rendered to that recipient. The zero rate does not apply to Swiss domestic deliveries or where the exported goods merely move to a Swiss warehouse.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Ausfuhrzollanmeldung lodged electronically in e-dec with official export confirmation
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Swiss customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or TT credit advice showing receipt of foreign sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order or letter of credit

No continuous transaction control (CTC), no real-time fiscal clearance, and no fiscal stamping requirements exist in Switzerland. E-invoices are valid under Art. 62 of the Swiss VAT Act if the authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are guaranteed from issuance through storage. The QR-bill / SIX electronic invoice payment slip is the standard domestic payment format. Export declarations must be filed electronically through the e-dec customs system; no API-based pre-clearance or digital fiscal stamp is required.

Swiss export zero-rating is subject to intensified audit by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and customs authorities through automatic cross-checks of export declarations, transport documents and bank flows. Switzerland does not impose foreign-exchange controls, but the 180-day benchmark is applied in this dossier as the conditional evidence window for finalizing bank credit advice and export substantiation. If the exporter cannot substantiate the export or the foreign nature of the sale within the relevant record-keeping period, the 0% rate is retrospectively replaced by standard VAT at 7.7% (or 2.5% if applicable) on the gross sales price, and the tax is clawed back with default interest and a late-payment surcharge. Deliberate false declarations constitute VAT fraud under the Swiss VAT Act and persons criminally prosecuted may face fines up to four times the tax evaded plus imprisonment-related penalties; the limitation period for clandestine tax evasion is extended to ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely tradable, and there are no exchange controls or restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan interest, or liquidation proceeds. Foreign direct investment and portfolio flows are fully liberalized, though banking relationships remain subject to Anti-Money Laundering (AML) verification and internal bank risk policies. No mandatory approval is required for profit remittance, provided corporate documentation (audited financial statements, tax certificates, and board resolutions) demonstrates legitimate underlying commercial activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation requirement for export receipts. Exporters are free to hold proceeds in foreign currency or convert to CHF at market rates through licensed banks or supervised financial intermediaries. This aligns with Switzerland's open capital account and the absence of IMF Article VIII restrictions; however, proceeds must be economically substantiated in trade documents for banking compliance and transfer-pricing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and State Secretariat for International Financial Affairs (SIF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with significant legal constraints. Switzerland authorizes fintech companies and electronic money institutions (EMIs) under the 'Fintech License' (banking license waiver per Art. 1b Banking Act) for deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million, and supervised by FINMA. However, cross-border payment service providers must be locally authorized or passport in under a mutual recognition agreement; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., non-Swiss licensed EMIs from the EU) may operate in Switzerland subject to FINMA's regulatory perimeter and can only execute payment transactions without taking deposits. Due to higher AML scrutiny and administrative burden, traditional tier-1 banks remain the preferred clearing and settlement rails for trade finance and escrow in medical PPE transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital contributions should be effected via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers identifying the purpose code (judicial or contractual payment, e.g., 'Contribution to share capital' under FINMA classification) and the depositor's relationship. For Swiss companies, the injection is credited to a corporate account after legal issuance of shares and registration in the Commercial Register; verification includes the notarized subscription statement, bank confirmation, and proof of payment from the shareholder's account. An AG must also undergo an ordinary audit if it is a listed company, a financial intermediary, or if shareholders holding at least a 10% equity interest request one. Companies not meeting the ordinary audit threshold generally undergo a limited audit, and small companies with fewer than 10 FTE may be exempted by unanimous shareholders' resolution unless an ordinary audit is requested.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax assessed on after-tax profit (approximately 7.83% pre-tax equivalent); combined federal plus cantonal/communal headline for an industrial stock corporation (AG) typically ranges from 11.9% to 21.6% depending on the registered seat.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed by the AG, imposed under Art. 1-4 VStG; the tax is generally refundable to Swiss residents and reduced, waived or refunded to foreign shareholders only under an applicable double tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the fiscal year-end under Art. 124 DBG; extension may be granted by the cantonal tax administration, generally not exceeding 12 months after the balance sheet date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Swiss TP documentation (Local File and Master File) applies where the group records consolidated annual revenue above CHF 80,000,000 or where the Swiss taxpayer's cross-border intercompany transactions exceed CHF 5,000,000 in the tax year per Swiss TP Circular No. 49 of 2021; a Country-by-Country Report and notification are compulsory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds CHF 900,000,000; documentation must be submitted in a Swiss national language or English within 30 days of an audit demand.

Economic substance scope: Swiss law abolished the privileged holding/mixed and domiciliary statuses as of 2020 (STAF); the AG must now demonstrate genuine economic substance under OECD asset-risk-control principles: own or leased dipping production facilities, qualified operational and QA staff, active management decisions taken in Switzerland, and the Swissmedic distribution authorization for sterile medical devices. While EU-style Economic Substance Regulations do not apply, the Swiss administrative practice and the OECD Multilateral Instrument substance requirements condition the availability of treaty benefits and Swiss WHT refunds; a letterbox AG without real manufacturing activity can be denied treaty relief, reclassified by foreign tax authorities, and subjected to the 15% Global Minimum Tax top-up under the Swiss Global Minimum Tax Act of 2024.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Suppression of Swiss-Source Profit

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with default interest of 4-5% per annum; administrative fine up to 1x the evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG, and up to 3x the evaded tax in aggravated cases; publication of the conviction in serious cases and revocation of export-related authorizations by the competent authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers are personally prosecuted under Art. 181 DBG as responsible legal representatives; they face restitution of the evaded tax, monetary fines, travel bans, asset seizure and disqualification from serving as board members.
Aggravated Tax Fraud / Criminal Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 3 years under Art. 186 DBG; where coordinated with Swiss Criminal Code forgery or fraud provisions, custodial sanctions can extend to 10 years; fines up to 3x the evaded tax plus forfeiture of the economic benefit.

Director personal liability: The CEO, CFO and any signatory director are treated as principals or co-perpetrators and face custodial sentences, pre-trial detention, travel bans and extradition risk under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and withholding tax refunds; full taxation of the entire industrial margin at ordinary federal/cantonal rates; 15% Pillar-Two top-up tax; liquidation proceedings for formal statutory defects under Art. 731b OR; administrative fines and reclassification of the tax seat.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unlawful distributions and restitution under Art. 754/758 OR, face travel bans in administrative criminal investigations, and be barred from future board appointments.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / CbC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Discretionary arm's-length assessment with an imputed EBIT margin, default interest of 4-5%, procedural fines for non-cooperation, withdrawal of APA protection and direct exposure to economic double taxation without competent-authority relief; CbC non-filing is prosecuted as a federal tax administrative offence.

Director personal liability: Individual monetary fines against responsible officers; the knowingly false certification of TP documentation constitutes criminal tax fraud and triggers personal investigation, asset attachment and travel restrictions.
AML / Financial Crime in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA): fines up to CHF 500,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for intentional violations; confiscation of the funds and equipment; removal of the company's directors from the register of beneficial owners; the bank relationship is subject to immediate termination.

Director personal liability: Board members and financial officers are prosecuted personally without the protection of the corporate veil; they face imprisonment, travel bans, disgorgement of gains and joint civil liability for damages under Art. 754 OR.
Unlicensed Medical Device Manufacturing / Distribution

Statutory sanction: TPA Art. 86: imprisonment up to 5 years or a monetary penalty for intentional placement on the market of unauthorized medical devices; seizure of the product batches; withdrawal of export certificates; import/export bans; any imposed fines are not tax-deductible.

Director personal liability: The board and the responsible quality officer are criminally liable under the TPA and under the organizational fault provision of Art. 102 of the Swiss Criminal Code; individuals face personal fines, corrective cost assessments and prohibition from managing regulated medical device companies.
