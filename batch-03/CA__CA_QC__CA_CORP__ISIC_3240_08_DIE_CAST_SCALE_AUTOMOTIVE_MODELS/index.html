Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no exchange controls, no prior approvals for foreign investment, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, or capital for non-resident shareholders, provided all applicable federal and Quebec corporate and withholding tax returns are filed and paid. Dividends paid by a Quebec-resident corporation to non-residents are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax, reduced by treaty where applicable. Capital inflows and outflows must comply with FINTRAC large-transaction reporting thresholds and the Investment Canada Act if the acquisition of control of a Canadian business is involved.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory percentage, and no obligation to surrender export receipts or convert them into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, and may be used directly for foreign supplier payments, subject only to the exporter's internal tax and financial reporting policies, GST/HST zero-rating documentary requirements, and applicable FINTRAC reports for transactions at or above C$10,000.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks; FINTRAC administers AML/CFT obligations; in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises financial services and money-services businesses.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign fintech or electronic-money institutions (EMIs) without a Canadian registration are not permitted to provide banking or money-transfer services to a Canadian business as a principal regulated entity. They may operate only through a registered Canadian money-services business (MSB) or through a partnership with a schedule I bank. As cross-border virtual issuers, many foreign EMIs will not provide CAD-denominated accounts to non-resident corporate clients, and Quebec requires money-services businesses using an establishment in Quebec to obtain an AMF licence. For the current low-risk die-cast collectibles business, a foreign EMI may be used solely as an auxiliary disbursement rail for small international supplier payments, but the primary operating and escrow account must remain at a tier-1 domestic bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        UBO ambiguity created by foreign corporate shareholders or trust structures
        
Root cause: UBO ambiguity created by foreign corporate shareholders or trust structures

        
Defense evidence: Notarised corporate register excerpt, official certificates of incumbency and good standing, a full UBO chain traced to individuals, passports, utility bills and identity documents for all persons with 25% or more ownership, and a board resolution identifying the reporting UBO for each corporate shareholder.

      

      
      

        Unusual inbound wire volumes or frequent matching inbound/outbound transfers without underlying trade documentation
        
Root cause: Unusual inbound wire volumes or frequent matching inbound/outbound transfers without underlying trade documentation

        
Defense evidence: Signed commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, shipping contracts, purchase orders from overseas suppliers, die-casting and tampo-printing production manifests, and bank trail explanations linking each wire to a specific trade transaction.

      

      
      

        High-risk counterparty or jurisdiction in the supplier or distributor network
        
Root cause: High-risk counterparty or jurisdiction in the supplier or distributor network

        
Defense evidence: Sanctions screening clearance reports from a commercial screening tool, counterparty beneficial-ownership certificates, independent correspondence showing the business purpose, and a written AML risk assessment with EDD file containing evidence of the counterparty's commercial registration and trade history.

      

      
      

        Resale of finished products via marketplaces and card processors with settlement mismatches
        
Root cause: Resale of finished products via marketplaces and card processors with settlement mismatches

        
Defense evidence: Marketplace settlement reports, PayPal merchant statements, e-commerce tax certificates, and reconciled CSVs showing gross sales, transaction fees, FX conversion rates, and net remittances attributed to each customer order.

      

      
      

        Naming mismatch between corporate registration, invoicing entity, and bank account title
        
Root cause: Naming mismatch between corporate registration, invoicing entity, and bank account title

        
Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation from Corporations Canada or Registraire des entreprises du Québec, GST/HST registration certificate from CRA, a formal name booking confirmation, and a board resolution confirming the exact operating and invoicing legal name.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity and shareholder-loan injections should be routed by SWIFT wire to the bank account in the name of the Canadian operating company. The wire must clearly specify the sender as the shareholder or shareholder entity, the beneficiary as the Quebec corporation, and include a purpose code reflecting 'Direct Investment in Canada' or 'Intra-company Financing'. The bank will require a board resolution approving the capital contribution or loan, a signed subscription agreement, and, if applicable, a loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate and repayment schedule. Transactions equal to or above C$10,000 are reported to FINTRAC as large cash or wire transactions; the bank may also require a source-of-wealth questionnaire for the investor. No CFIUS-type foreign investment approval is required for this scale-model manufacturing company, but Investment Canada Act notification thresholds remain applicable if the acquirer is a non-resident investor seeking to acquire control of a Canadian business.Outbound supplier payments and trade settlements should be executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 as direct wire transfers for invoice settlement, or through a commercial documentary letter of credit (L/C) for larger or contractually governed purchase orders from Chinese die-casting tooling suppliers and tampo-printing vendors. The standard rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for straightforward payment against commercial invoice, with the invoice number and contract reference in the payment narrative; (2) SWIFT MT700/Irrevocable Documentary Credit issued by the Canadian bank in favour of the overseas supplier, covering cost, insurance, and freight (CIF) shipments with presentation of a clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin; and (3) PayPal as a low-value prompt-payment rail for sample orders, pre-production prototypes, and marketplace refunds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
26.5% combined general non-CCPC rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); qualified manufacturing and processing profits attract the federal M&P rate of 13%, yielding a combined headline rate of 24.5% before Quebec M&P credits and deductions.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% (flat federal Part XIII withholding tax on any dividend paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident recipient; Quebec imposes no separate dividend withholding tax).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) ITA and s.80 Penal Fines? Quebec: fine between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to five years; disgorgement and compound interest to CRA/Revenu Québec; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2) ITA and corresponding provincial provision.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct underreporting face personal civil restitution, CRA asset seizure, prohibition order, travel bans, and imprisonment; where a director personally benefits from diverted corporate cash, the corporate veil is pierced and personal liability attaches to all federal and provincial taxes, penalties, and interest.

      

      
      

        Non-Filing or Failure to Register a Quebec Permanent Establishment
        
Statutory sanction: Reassessment on attributable net income at the general combined rate; late-filing penalties (5% + 1%/month to 12%) on both federal and provincial tax; involuntary dissolution risk for provincial registration; non-resident deemed-payment assessments relating to amounts withheld on payments to third-party agents.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and QST under comparable Canadian provisions; for source withholding defaults the CRA issues s.227.1(1) director’s liability notice, authorizing seizure of personal bank accounts, freezing, and, upon ex parte court proceeding, director travel restrictions.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell IP Parking
        
Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) ITA recharacterization of the actual transaction to arm’s length; s.247(3) adjustment of gross income; s.247(4)- penalty of 10% of the amount of adjustment if documentation inadequately prepared; denial of deductions for royalties acquired with an anti-avoidance purpose; gross negligence penalty 50%; provincial parallel penalty under Quebec Taxation Act.

        
Director personal liability: A director who causes the company to enter into TP arrangements without contemporaneous records can be assessed the TP penalty directly as a remittance officer; if the adjusted tax is not paid, CRA may pursue director liability using an LL? / de facto director determination, seize personal property, and impose a court-ordered travel ban where economic interest is hidden offshore.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Mouthwash
        
Statutory sanction: FINTRAC record-keeping penalty for corporations up to CAD 500,000 per administrative monetary penalty (AMP); repeated/aggravated offences up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for criminally negligent money laundering under the Criminal Code; federal tax evasion penalties as above; Quebec separate AMP for missing registry filings.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use the shell to launder sale proceeds face criminal charges as principals, personal fines under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, confiscation of personal assets derived from the offence, and extradition/travel restrictions; nominee directors are not shielded merely because they acted at the instruction of the foreign beneficial owner.
