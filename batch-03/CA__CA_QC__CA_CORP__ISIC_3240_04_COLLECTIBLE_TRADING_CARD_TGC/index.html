Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for QST (Quebec Sales Tax), Quebec corporate income tax, and business registration; Portals: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca) and Revenu Québec CléInfo Business Portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a business corporation federally (Corporations Canada) or in Quebec (Registraire des entreprises), or register the foreign entity as an extra-provincial branch. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST account through CRA Form RC1 or the online Non-Resident GST/HST Registration application. Step 3: Register for QST with Revenu Québec via CléInfo using Form LM-3 or the online business registration service where the corporation carries on taxable commercial activity in Quebec. Step 4: Open federal and Quebec corporate income tax accounts (CRA RT account and Revenu Québec RP account). Issuance time is typically 15 to 20 business days. Non-resident suppliers of cross-border supplies may use the simplified CRA e-commerce GST/HST registration framework.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections include: GST/HST closely related persons election under Excise Tax Act s.156, GST/HST and QST joint venture elections (ETA s.273; QSTA s.439.9), corporate amalgamation and winding-up rollover elections (ITA s.87 and s.88), non-resident deemed-disposition elections, and hybrid-mismatch compliance provisions under ITA ss.94.5 to 94.8. Foreign entity classification follows Canadian domestic character rules (e.g., a foreign LLC is generally treated as a corporation unless a clear flow-through character is established under its home jurisdiction law).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold under GST/HST (ETA s.148) and QST (QSTA s.29.2); a registrant is required once taxable worldwide revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers selling to Canadian consumers apply the same CAD 30,000 threshold under the 2021 e-commerce alignment rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Collectible trading cards, being tangible personal property, are zero-rated under s.22 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, and correspondingly exempt under s.106 of the QSTA, where the supplier physically exports the goods or delivers them to a common carrier for export from Canada. Holographic cold-foil printing and publishing services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated under Part V, s.9 of Schedule VI to the ETA if the benefit of the service is consumed outside Canada. Full input tax credits (ITCs) and QST refunds are available to the manufacturer/publisher on inputs used in zero-rated export supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA Export Report (G13 CAED, ACI eManifest, or approved customs broker filing)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document showing dispatch from Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange settlement evidence (bank or payment-rail receipt from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (HS code 9504.40, destination country, declared value, Canadian origin)
  • Executed zero-rated supply agreement / purchase order from the non-resident customer

No federal or Quebec real-time e-invoicing, API fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate applies to the manufacture, printing, or publishing of collectible trading cards. Electronic invoices are legally recognized under the Quebec Act respecting the legal framework of information technology (RLRQ c. C-1.1) with the recipient's consent. CRA and Revenu Québec require six years of books and records retention. Sector-specific certified sales-recording software requirements in Quebec apply only to designated service businesses (e.g., meal services), not to exporting card manufacturers or publishers.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary audit. Absent an export report, shipping evidence, or foreign-currency settlement proof, CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year period, or indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default exists, and may claw back input tax credits and QST refunds with arrears interest. Gross-negligence penalties of 25% of the assessed amount apply under ETA s.285 and QSTA s.1034. Wilful evasion is prosecutable criminally under ETA s.327 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act, s.169, with fines up to 200% of the amount at issue and imprisonment of up to five years; false customs declarations additionally attract CBSA monetary penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a freely floating exchange rate regime with no foreign exchange control legislation; no exchange control permit is required for capital or current account transactions. Capital account is fully convertible, including foreign direct investment, equity injections, dividends, and repatriation of proceeds. Profit remittance by way of dividends to non-resident shareholders is subject to Part XIII federal withholding tax (default 25% under the Income Tax Act, reduced to 5%-15% under applicable tax treaties) and to Quebec dividend withholding tax where applicable under the Taxation Act (CQLR c. I-3); no central bank approval or administrative licence is required for remittance. The only practical hurdles are banking-provided source-of-funds verification and FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., EFT reports for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender or conversion requirement on export proceeds; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts, hold foreign currency accounts at Canadian financial institutions, and convert at commercial rates. Filing obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting (large cross-border currency transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, electronic funds transfer reports) and, for statistical purposes only, Statistics Canada's export/import reporting. No exchange control certificate is needed for commercial trade settlements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); currency issuance governed by the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary Canadian bank account denominated in CAD (or USD if the operating account is a dual-currency account); the payment narrative must state 'share capital contribution' or 'subscription for shares' and reference the share allotment resolution. No exchange control approval is required in Canada; however, the bank may request the executed unanimous shareholders agreement, the directors' resolution authorizing issuance of shares, and the Quebec registraire enterprise update for director and share capital changes. Statistics Canada foreign direct investment reporting is limited to enterprise-level surveys and is not a clearance requirement. FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; the remitting foreign bank must complete an equivalent local report for the outbound leg. Funds should be received only after the account is fully activated and the bank's compliance has cleared the 'source of funds' questionnaire.Clearing rails for supplier payments and trade settlements include: (i) SWIFT MT103 for high-value direct supplier wire transfers (e.g., Korean and US holographic foil printers); (ii) commercial letters of credit issued subject to UCP 600, confirming through correspondent banks for large production runs; (iii) Payments Canada real-time rail (RTR) and the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic same-day CAD payments; (iv) Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for routine CAD cheques and debits; and (v) card network settlements via Stripe and Authorize.Net for e-commerce consumer sales. All international payments exceeding CAD 10,000 must generate FINTRAC EFT reports; documentary compliance requires invoices, customs declarations (B13A export declaration for goods leaving Canada), and if letters of credit are used, full presentation of shipping documents.
