Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is a separate taxable person resident in Canada under the Income Tax Act; it computes and pays corporate income tax on worldwide income and is a 'registrant' for GST/HST/QST; shareholders are not taxed on corporate earnings until distribution. No pass-through election is available and no prescribed tax-exempt zone applies to the bonded-trade activity.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory classifications/elections include: (a) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status for the small-business deduction (Income Tax Act ss.89/125, s.256 grouping rules); (b) non-resident corporation elections under ITA s.216 for Canadian-source insurance income; (c) hybrid-mismatch conformity elections under ITA ss.18.2/18.3 (D/NI and deducted-non-included recharacterization) applicable from 2023; and (d) provincial elections for QST input-tax-refund allocation rules administered by Revenu Québec. As a general rule, a Business Corporation engaged in bonded warehouse wholesale trade will remain entity-level taxable with no flow-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 measured as world-wide taxable revenues in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold). Registration is mandatory as soon as the threshold is exceeded; a supplier importing goods into Canada is taxable on import regardless of the threshold; QST registration follows the same CAD 30,000 threshold under the Quebec Act respecting the Quebec sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act ss.22-23 applies to tangible goods (including age-dated Scotch and Bordeaux wines in bonded storage) that the supplier exports from Canada by commercial courier/common carrier or by its own conveyance, delivering the goods to a destination outside Canada. Bonded-warehouse removals for export qualify provided the goods are physically exported under CBSA customs control and supported by contemporaneous export evidence. Services supplied to non-residents (e.g., storage, handling and logistics services performed for non-resident principals) qualify for zero-rating under ETA ss.7/9 when the service is not performed for consumption in Canada and the supplier retains written evidence identifying the non-resident recipient. The supplier must be a GST/HST/QST registrant and report the zero-rated supply on the applicable return to trigger input tax credit refunds.

  • CBSA customs export declaration (Canadian export entry/eExport submission) identifying the outbound movement and bonded-warehouse removal
  • Bill of lading, air waybill or road manifest issued by the commercial carrier evidencing delivery outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract naming the non-resident purchaser and the foreign destination
  • Proof of settlement/inward remittance records (SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or foreign-exchange conversion statement) matching the foreign consideration
  • Bonded warehouse removal order and excise duty certificate under the Excise Act, 2001 confirming duty suspension and export release
  • GST/QST reconciliation worksheet linking each exported case/lot to the zero-rated line reported on the registrant's return

Canada does not operate a real-time CTC fiscal-clearance or digital-stamping system for B2B invoices. Mandated electronic invoicing applies to federal B2G procurement: large suppliers must send e-invoices compliant with Peppol BIS 3.0 through certified service providers. Revenu Québec has announced a phased rollout of mandatory B2B e-invoicing (pilot from 2026, phased compliance obligations under regulatory development). Alcoholic beverages under bonded warehouse mode must clear CBSA's single-window border systems and follow Excise Act, 2001 duty-remittance reporting to CRA, including electronic submission of customs entries (eManifest/eExport) for export movements.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating where documentary proof is deficient or where the supply is found to have been consumed in Canada. For fraud, misrepresentation or neglect, the normal four-year limitation period is replaced by an unlimited reassessment window, and retroactive clawbacks include net GST/QST, legislated interest and penalties. Penalties: Excise Tax Act s.280 (greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax that would have been payable but for the false statement), s.281/s.282 offences on summary conviction with fines up to C$250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; parallel QST penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act; CBSA administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act for false export declarations; and potential suspension or revocation of excise bonded-warehouse licences under the Excise Act, 2001. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day export-repatriation rule; the 180-day benchmark is retained as a prudential documentary-review standard by which CRA may test the genuineness of the export and the commercial arm's-length character of deferred trade receivables.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible. Canada has maintained no exchange-control restrictions since the repeal of the Exchange Control Act, so neither government approval nor central-bank permits are required for current-account or capital-account operations. Inbound equity contributions, intercompany loans, supplier prepayments, and outbound profit repatriation may move freely. The only practical hurdles for a non-resident-owned Quebec business corporation are: (i) federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, and similar amounts paid to non-residents at the statutory 25% rate, generally reduced by an applicable income tax treaty (for dividends usually 15%, or 5% where the beneficial owner holds at least 10% of the voting shares; for arm's-length interest 0% or a reduced treaty rate); (ii) reporting obligations under Income Tax Act section 233.3 (Form T106) for reportable cross-border transactions with non-residents, together with transfer-pricing documentation; (iii) Quebec corporate registry obligations, including updating the Registraire des entreprises for director and beneficial-ownership changes; and (iv) standard bank and FINTRAC due-diligence screening of the source and purpose of funds. No convertibility ceiling, surrender requirement, or repatriation timeline is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. A Quebec bonded-spirits and wine merchant may lawfully retain receipts in CAD, USD, EUR, or GBP in domestic or foreign-currency accounts, and may hold balances offshore provided the related income is disclosed to the CRA. Non-FX compliance duties remain: exports must be supported by CBSA export declarations; excise-duty relief on bonded removals must be evidenced under the Excise Act, 2001 and the applicable CRA licence or warehouse authorization; and the proceeds of export sales, if involving a non-resident related party or reportable transaction, may trigger Form T106 or other information-return disclosure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange control institution in Canada. Regulatory responsibility is distributed as follows: the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally registered banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers excise licensing and duty security under the Excise Act, 2001; and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) controls customs bonded-warehouse procedures.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used as secondary collection or disbursement channels, but they are not an adequate primary clearing solution for a Quebec bonded-spirits and wine merchant. Legal constraints: any EMI with a Canadian nexus or collecting Canadian-origin funds must satisfy FINTRAC's money-services-business (MSB) registration, and payment-service providers may require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which the Bank of Canada administers. Offshore EMIs generally cannot: (i) hold the excise-duty security or CBSA bonded-warehouse guarantee that the CRA or CBSA requires from a licensed excise holder; (ii) issue or confirm commercial letters of credit under UCP-600; (iii) act as the depository for CRA excise-duty payments; or (iv) provide the administered corporate cash-pooling and treasury-control structure expected of a Canadian operating company. The recommended design is a Schedule I bank as the master treasury and settlement account, with a fintech EMI layered on top for buyer collections or merchant payouts, subject to full PCMLTFA compliance, record-keeping, and audit-rights protections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing uses a SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'Contribution to Capital' or 'Equity Injection', showing the Quebec corporate legal name and business number in the beneficiary line. The remitter must appear as the true beneficial remitter in the SWIFT COVER/ordering details to avoid sanctions-screening delays. High-value CAD transfers settle through Lynx (the Canadian high-value RTGS); USD-denominated capital should be sent via Fedwire or CHIPS to the Canadian bank's US correspondent and converted to CAD only when required. Where an inbound equity transfer exceeds CAD 1,000,000, counsel should confirm whether a Statistics Canada direct-investment survey filing is required. The capital must be credited to issued share capital and kept separate from trade receivables. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan instead of equity, the terms must be arm's-length and bear interest, and any interest paid to a non-resident will be subject to Part XIII withholding unless a treaty waiver or exemption is established. The receiving bank will require source-of-funds evidence (the parent's audited financial statements), the shareholder resolution, and a beneficial-owner declaration before treasury deployment.Supplier settlements for bonded Scotch whisky and Bordeaux wine should be executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 (gpi) in GBP and EUR through the Canadian bank's correspondent network. For initial large orders (commonly above USD 100,000 per supplier) or when documentary assurance is requested, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued under UCP-600, with presentation of bills of lading, commercial invoices, and cargo insurance certificates; a documentary-collections (D/P) fallback is acceptable. Excise-duty payments to the CRA are cleared through the CRA 'My Payment' channel or a participating financial institution and are debited from the dedicated bonded-duty sub-account. CBSA bonded-transfer security is posted by the bank or a surety in favour of the Receiver General. Non-resident disbursements for distributor commissions, freight, and insurance should follow the same SWIFT rail with permissible purpose codes, with each payment screened against Canadian sanctions lists. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used as secondary collection or disbursement channels, but they are not an adequate primary clearing solution for a Quebec bonded-spirits and wine merchant. Legal constraints: any EMI with a Canadian nexus or collecting Canadian-origin funds must satisfy FINTRAC's money-services-business (MSB) registration, and payment-service providers may require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which the Bank of Canada administers. Offshore EMIs generally cannot: (i) hold the excise-duty security or CBSA bonded-warehouse guarantee that the CRA or CBSA requires from a licensed excise holder; (ii) issue or confirm commercial letters of credit under UCP-600; (iii) act as the depository for CRA excise-duty payments; or (iv) provide the administered corporate cash-pooling and treasury-control structure expected of a Canadian operating company. The recommended design is a Schedule I bank as the master treasury and settlement account, with a fintech EMI layered on top for buyer collections or merchant payouts, subject to full PCMLTFA compliance, record-keeping, and audit-rights protections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a mandatory OECD-style Local File/Master File threshold. However, contemporaneous arm's length documentation must be prepared and retained for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions. Form T106 must be filed when the total value of reportable transactions with a single non-resident non-arm's length person exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Country-by-country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the Caribbean/OECD-EU form. The Income Tax Act general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) allows CRA to disregard transactions lacking genuine economic substance, and DTA benefits are denied for treaty-shopping arrangements. A bonded wine/spirits trading entity must maintain demonstrable commercial substance: real employees, physical warehouse risk, inventory custody, and active management decisions. Absent these indicia, CRA and Revenu Quebec may recharacterize arrangements and impose withholding taxes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Income Tax Act and Excise Act, 2001

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of federal tax evaded; criminal conviction on indictment carries a fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Excise Act, 2001 evasion of duty adds separate penalties and forfeiture of bonded goods.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and excise duties under ITA s. 227.1 and Excise Act, 2001. Directors who direct or authorize evasion face personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, civil restitution, and travel bans in tax or extradition proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under GAAR (ITA s. 245), denial of treaty benefits, disallowance of intercompany deductions, recharacterization of dividends as Part XIII taxable payments, and Quebec anti-avoidance reassessments under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the sham structure can be personally assessed for the corporate tax liability where the corporation is insolvent. Courts may pierce the corporate veil in fraud or abuse cases, ordering civil restitution, travel bans, and extradition for sentencing in egregious cross-border tax fraud.
Money Laundering - PCMLTFA and Criminal Code

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation and CAD 100,000 for an individual per violation. Criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 is punishable by imprisonment up to ten years, and bonded inventory may be seized and forfeited with restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate or benefit from money laundering face imprisonment, criminal fines, personal restraint and freezing orders, and travel bans. Civil forfeiture of assets derived from the proceeds of crime is applied directly to the directors' personal holdings where they controlled the laundering scheme.
Failure to File / False Aggressive Tax Planning

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties at federal and Quebec level; gross negligence penalty of 10% of understated tax; transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward transfer pricing adjustment where documentation is absent or inadequate; possible criminal prosecution for making false statements under ITA s. 239.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid source deductions and unremitted GST/QST. Tax authorities can lift the corporate veil to collect from directors who participated in the non-filing or knowingly allowed fraudulent returns; in high-value cases, travel bans, international criminal prosecution, and imprisonment apply.
