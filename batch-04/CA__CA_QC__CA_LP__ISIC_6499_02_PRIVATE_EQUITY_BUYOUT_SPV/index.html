Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Capital is determined solely by the limited partnership agreement (LPA) and may be contributed in cash, property, or services under arts. 2238-2239 CCQ.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy certificate from the local municipality for the registered office; fire safety inspection by the Service de securite incendie; no RBQ structural load modifications; CNESST workplace clearance not triggered absent employees, though voluntary compliance is recommended if administrative staff are retained.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Capital is determined solely by the limited partnership agreement (LPA) and may be contributed in cash, property, or services under arts. 2238-2239 CCQ.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec income tax, under the single-desk arrangement for businesses established in Quebec. Portals: CRA 'My Business Account' and Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier Entreprises'.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must: (1) file a declaration of registration at the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ identifying the extra-provincial LP; (2) apply through Revenu Québec's single desk (Form LM-101-V or Mon dossier Entreprises) to open the QST account (NEQ + suffix) and the federal GST/HST account; (3) submit the LP agreement, proof of foreign existence, manager identity and banking details; (4) file and remit electronically once registered. For a non-resident, the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies, not only Canadian turnover.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must: (1) file a declaration of registration at the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ identifying the extra-provincial LP; (2) apply through Revenu Québec's single desk (Form LM-101-V or Mon dossier Entreprises) to open the QST account (NEQ + suffix) and the federal GST/HST account; (3) submit the LP agreement, proof of foreign existence, manager identity and banking details; (4) file and remit electronically once registered. For a non-resident, the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies, not only Canadian turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Available elections for a PE buyout LP: (a) ETA s. 150 closely-related-persons election (GST/HST/QST neutralization of inter-entity supplies with subsidiary holdcos); (b) ITA s. 96(3) and s. 97(2) elections for deemed dispositions and partnership-property rollovers; (c) hybrid-mismatch outcomes are governed by ITA ss. 17.1, 18.2 and 18.3 (BEPS Action 2), with grandfathering relief but no affirmative classification election; (d) ITA s. 253.1 deems a non-resident partner to carry on the partnership's Canadian business through the same permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier ceiling (ETA s. 148 and Quebec Taxation Act); a non-resident partnership exceeding worldwide turnover of CAD 30,000 must register and charge GST/HST/QST on taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI when tangible personal property is exported as soon as possible after supply and documentary proof is retained. Services: zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST, unless the service relates to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at performance. The LP's core financial services are generally exempt (Sch. V, Pt. VII); zero-rating applies mainly to management/consulting fees, M&A advisory fees and disposal of portfolio securities when the recipient is non-resident and the benefit is consumed outside Canada.

  • Commercial invoice and executed acquisition/sale agreement identifying the foreign purchaser and export delivery terms
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/house), air waybill or truck/rail eManifest (ACI/CUSCAR) proving physical departure from Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government clearance API in Canada or Quebec; Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing remains voluntary for B2G only. Mandatory duties: issue invoices showing GST/HST and QST registration numbers, retain books/records for six years, and file/remit GST/HST/QST electronically via CRA or Revenu Québec portals.

On disallowance, the LP is reassessed for uncollected GST/HST and QST on the full selling price plus arrears interest; false export claims attract a 25% penalty (ETA s. 285) and gross negligence a 50% penalty (ITA s. 163(2)/QTA); wilful evasion is prosecutable criminally (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239) with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. Standard assessment limitation is four years, extended to eight years for non-residents that fail to register or file, and unlimited for fraud or wilful misrepresentation, permitting retrospective clawback of export refunds and input-tax credits; partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's indirect tax debts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a G10 free-floating and fully convertible currency; current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under the repealed Exchange Control Act, and no prior central-bank approval, profit-remittance permit, registration fee, or capital-repatriation tax applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Residual constraints: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25%, treaty-reducible) on Canadian-source amounts allocated to non-resident partners, with interest to arm's-length non-residents generally exempt under subsection 212(1)(b) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) Investment Canada Act net-benefit and national-security review for certain non-Canadian acquisitions or inward investments; (iii) FINTRAC and PCMLTFA reporting for international EFTs and cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more; (iv) Canadian sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada and OSFI, which operate as compliance checkpoints rather than conversion restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for receipts of any kind; the Quebec LP may hold, invoice, and disburse CAD, USD, and EUR balances freely. The only applicable obligations are the general cross-border reporting rules under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: declaration of importation or exportation of currency or monetary instruments equal to or above CAD 10,000, and FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange policy, clearing oversight, and compliance administration are coordinated by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), Payments Canada (Lynx), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury; ancillary use only. Offshore digital EMI providers (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Airwallex) operating without Canadian registration are treated as unauthorized money-service businesses for Canadian regulatory purposes and cannot provide Canadian IBANs, Lynx access, CDIC-insured deposit accounts, or cross-border lending and escrow rails. A Quebec LP may use a FINTRAC-registered and, where applicable, AMF-licensed money-service business solely for low-value operating disbursements such as travel and subscriptions; capital escrow, syndicated-loan drawdowns, acquisition wires, and FX hedging must be executed through Schedule I bank accounts. Federally regulated Canadian banks further apply de-risking policies that may suspend or reject wires issued from unlicensed or offshore EMIs.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound committed capital must be received through a Canadian Schedule I bank account. Recommended protocol: (i) the remitter wires via SWIFT MT103 using the exact legal name of the limited partnership, the Canadian account number, and a purpose narrative stating 'Capital contribution to Quebec limited partnership for private equity acquisition', with the bank classifying the flow as direct or portfolio investment; (ii) the ultimate investor's legal name must appear as the remitter; (iii) CAD-denominated legs clear irrevocably via Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS, formerly LVTS) using the partnership's Canadian transit and institution numbers; (iv) USD legs clear via the bank's US correspondent through Fedwire or CHIPS; (v) EUR legs clear via SEPA or instant SEPA; (vi) any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is FINTRAC-reportable, requiring a large-value transaction form and an updated source-of-funds statement; and (vii) for syndicated-lending commitments, the capital-call notice must be submitted to the bank's compliance desk in advance and the drawdown must be accompanied by a countersigned drawdown certificate and a copy of the executed facility agreement.Outbound acquisition consideration settles via SWIFT MT103 from the partnership's operational account to the seller or target bank account, with a purpose narrative stating 'Acquisition of shares or assets'; cover payments use MT202 COV; CAD legs settle domestically through Lynx; USD through Fedwire or CHIPS; and EUR through SEPA. For syndicated-loan construction in the buyout context, the facility agent disburses against a compliant drawdown notice via SWIFT MT103 with a 'syndicated loan drawdown' reference, and any required FX conversion is executed with the corporate dealing desk before release. Escrowed acquisition funds are released only on joint instruction of the general partner and the escrow compliance officer. Quebec Annual Declaration (Declaration Annuelle) under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, confirming the partnership's identity, head office, general partners, and registry data. Within 60 days after the partnership's fiscal year-end; if the fiscal year-end is changed, the declaration must be updated within 60 days of the new year-end. $27 USD Administrative late fees may be imposed under the REQ fee tariff; continued non-compliance after a show-cause notice can lead to the registered partnership being struck from the enterprise register, causing loss of legal capacity to carry on business.
Federal Partnership Information Return (T5013) and T5013 slips issued to each limited and general partner. Five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end; for a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is May 31 of the following calendar year. $0 USD Late filing penalties under the Income Tax Act, generally calculated on a per-day basis up to CAD 2,500 for information returns, plus interest; gross negligence penalties may apply for false statements or omissions.
Quebec Partnership Information Return (TP-600) with related declarations for each partner's Quebec-source income. Five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end; a return may be required even with no Quebec income if the partnership carries on business in Quebec or has a Quebec establishment. $0 USD Interest and penalties under the Tax Administration Act, including late-filing penalties and failure-to-declare penalties; the general partner may be held liable where wilful omissions or false declarations occur.
Annual register audit and maintenance confirmation covering the register of partners, capital account ledger, minute book, and underlying books of account. Best practice within 90 days after fiscal year-end and before the T5013 and TP-600 filing deadlines; the limited partnership agreement may stipulate a specific date. $0 USD No direct government filing penalty; however, failure to maintain and produce records may expose the general partner to costs and evidentiary liability under the Civil Code of Quebec and may trigger audit rights under credit or investor agreements.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Quebec law does not impose a statutory independent audit on a limited partnership solely on the basis of size. A compulsory audit arises if the LP is a reporting issuer under the Quebec Securities Act, if it is registered with the Autorite des marches financiers as an investment fund or dealer/adviser, if the limited partnership agreement or a unanimous agreement requires an audit, or if creditors or investors impose an audit covenant. A private equity buyout SPV that is not publicly accountable and is not a registered securities firm will not have a mandatory statutory audit.
