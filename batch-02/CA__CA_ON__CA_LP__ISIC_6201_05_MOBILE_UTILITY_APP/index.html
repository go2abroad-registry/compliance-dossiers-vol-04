Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account. For a limited partnership, the general partner typically registers on behalf of the partnership using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts). Online registration is available through Business Registration Online; the CRA will issue a BN/RT account upon completion. Supporting documentation may include the foreign entity's constitutional documents, partnership agreement, and evidence of Canadian place of business or resident agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act, s.96. No entity-level election is available for Canadian tax purposes; partners file Schedule T5013. Partnerships may select a non-calendar taxation year with CRA permission, and joint elections are available respecting partnership fiscal periods under ITA s.249.1(9). Hybrid mismatch and treaty-based elections are limited to eligibility under applicable tax treaties and the OECD Multilateral Instrument; no standalone federal hybrid election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated taxable supplies, excluding exempt supplies; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for non-residents carrying on business in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies when goods are exported in a continuous freight movement or in a prescribed manner outside Canada, or supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada. Offshore services are zero-rated when performed for a non-resident client and either the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or the recipient is not resident in Canada and the supply does not relate to Canadian real estate or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The supplier must retain documentary evidence of export within the two-year record retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13 or CBSA authorized electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or outbound air waybill evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing foreign currency funds received

Canada does not have a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoicing mandate. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices provided the invoices contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (supplier name, BN, date, description, GST/HST amount or rate). Electronic records must be retained in an accessible, legible format. The CRA does not operate a centralized clearance API for standard GST/HST invoices.

CRA may reassess previously allowed GST/HST zero-ratings and recover refunds with interest if the documentary evidence is insufficient or the export does not meet the ETA requirements. Penalties include the ETA s.280 penalty for failure to provide information, penalties under ETA s.285.1 for false statements, gross-negligence penalties under Income Tax Act s.163.2, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Repeated or fraudulent export zero-rating claims can trigger large clawback assessments, interest, and penalties of up to 50% of the unreported or misclaimed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current account and capital account. No government authorization, exchange permit, or central bank license is required for: (i) inbound capital subscription into the Ontario LP; (ii) outbound trade settlements; (iii) conversion of USD Apple App Store and Google Play settlement proceeds into CAD; or (iv) remittance of distributable cash to non-resident partners. The only statutory frictions are tax and transparency measures: (a) distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding of 25%, reduced to 15% (general) and 5% (corporate partners owning 10%+) under the Canada-US tax treaty, supported by an NR301 or NR302 declaration filed with the CRA; (b) every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC (customer action is not blocked, only recorded); (c) a 25% Part XIII withholding is applied to patent-copyright-royalty-type flows, which are immaterial in a freemium utility-app model where the LP receives aggregated net settlement from Apple and Google, not end-user payments. Practical convertibility hurdles are banking-system frictions rather than statutory controls: source-of-funds diligence on subscription proceeds, beneficial-ownership disclosure for all partners holding 25% or more, and the need to maintain a USD sub-account to avoid forced FX conversion of App Store USD settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, holding period, or surrender obligation on export proceeds, app-store remittances, or foreign-currency receivables. The LP is free to retain USD, settle forward contracts, or sweep USD into CAD at its sole discretion. The only binding obligations are regulatory record-keeping and reporting: (i) both inbound and outbound wire/EFT transactions of CAD 10,000+ must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank under PCMLTFA; (ii) the LP must keep books and records under ITA section 230 evidencing the reconciliation of Apple/Google gross settlements, platform fees (15% or 30% commission), FX conversions, and partnership distributions; (iii) if the LP receives cash (physical currency) of CAD 10,000+, the LP itself files a FINTRAC large-cash-transaction report. No conversion ratio applies to USD proceeds from Apple App Store Connect or Google Play Console.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). FX policy, licensing, and systemic oversight are co-administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and cross-border EFT surveillance, and by the Department of Finance for international capital movements. Canada operates under a fully liberalized floating exchange-rate regime; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no exchange-control approval authority now exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary rail, Low as the primary treasury relationship. An Ontario LP may lawfully use a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business or non-bank EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or a regulated EU/UK EMI) for small cross-border vendor disbursements and low-value USD-CAD conversion, subject to the constraint that such entities cannot take deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot access Payments Canada LVTS or US Fedwire CHIPS settlement in the LP's name. For a freemium app treasury, Apple App Store Connect and Google Play seller agreements generally require settlement to a bank account in the legal entity's own name at a recognized bank; EMIs may be rejected as the settlement destination. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as the master clearing node; EMI used only for micro-vendor payouts below CAD 5,000 per transaction and ring-fenced with daily limits, while all capital, app-store settlements, and FX hedging flow through the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for capital injection into the Ontario LP from US and non-resident partners: (i) Partners wire in their base currency, typically USD, via Fedwire or SWIFT to the Schedule I bank's US correspondent with the bank's SWIFT BIC and the LP's unique beneficiary account number, referencing 'SUBSCRIPTION CA_LP_[PARTNER_NAME]' in field 70; (ii) CAD wires from Canadian partners settle same-day through Payments Canada LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) using an MT103; (iii) the bank will request the purpose/payment code at onboarding: for equity-like partnership subscriptions, use SWIFT purpose code CBL (capital/business-related) or the bank's 'EQUI' mapping, appended with the subscription-agreement identifier; for app-store operating remittances from Apple/Google, use 'CRED/SALE' with a reference to the payout statement; (iv) every EFT of CAD 10,000 or more triggers the bank's FINTRAC EFTR filing, which is automatic and non-blocking; (v) inbound USD is credited to the USD clearing sub-account and can be (a) swept to CAD via the bank's auto-FX conversion at a hedged rate, or (b) held and converted at a future date per a booked forward. No prior federal or provincial government approval is required; the LP must simply ensure the incoming wire beneficiary name matches the exact registered legal name to avoid SWIFT rejection.Clearing-rail selection for outbound payments: (i) Small operational trade payouts and cloud-infrastructure invoices from AWS and Google Cloud under CAD 1,000,000 are settled via SWIFT MT103 messages routed through Payments Canada LVTS (for CAD) or US CHIPS/Fedwire (for USD), with field 70 referencing the invoice number and field 50k the LP's account; these inbound-clearing vendors accept only MT103/ACH, so the LP should fund the payment from the USD sub-account or use a pre-booked FX forward; (ii) larger procurement contracts for hardware/equipment or a major software-development agreement above CAD 1,000,000 use a Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 via the Schedule I bank's trade desk (SWIFT MT700 series), with document requirements of commercial invoice, clean transport document or electronic delivery certificate, certificate of origin, and beneficiary draft; payment is executed by MT103 on presentation of compliant documents; (iii) Apple App Store and Google Play Billing settlements arrive inbound as aggregator ACH or SWIFT credits to the LP's CAD or USD account and require no LC; Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as the master clearing node; EMI used only for micro-vendor payouts below CAD 5,000 per transaction and ring-fenced with daily limits, while all capital, app-store settlements, and FX hedging flow through the Tier-1 bank.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

N/A — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes; the partnership itself pays no federal or Ontario income tax. Each partner's share of income is computed as if earned directly by the partner and taxed at the partner's applicable corporate or personal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII rate applies to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation under ITA section 212(2). However, an Ontario limited partnership distribution is not a dividend; it is a return of capital, income allocation, or repayment of debt. Non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their net Canadian-source business income, not on distributions. A distribution funded by retained Part I-taxed earnings should generally be free of further withholding for a non-resident partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for transactions between the Canadian entity and a non-resident non-arm's-length person, per ITA subsection 247(4). A T106 information return must be filed when the aggregate value of such transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Country-by-country (CbC) reporting applies for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime applicable to Ontario limited partnerships. Instead, CRA relies on judicial substance-over-form, sham, agency, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under ITA section 245. A limited partnership that merely holds app-store rights with no real activity in Canada may be recharacterized, its partners' treaty benefits denied, and its limited liability protection lost if limited partners participate in management or control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Revenue Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax, plus daily arrears interest. Criminal prosecution under ITA sections 238(1)/239(1) may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years, in addition to disqualification from managing a limited partnership.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts under ITA section 227.1. A court may impose travel bans, restitution orders, and personal penalties where the general partner knowingly directed revenue concealment; limited partners who exercised management control also face unlimited personal liability.
Shell Company / Hollow Limited Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the structure under GAAR, deny treaty withholding reductions, and impose a 50% gross negligence penalty on the resulting tax deficiency. The Ontario Limited Partnerships Act strips limited partner limited liability where a limited partner takes part in control of the business, making such partner personally liable for partnership obligations.

Director personal liability: The general partner remains fully liable for all partnership debts, including tax amounts, penalties, and interest. Directors who sign false T5013 or T106 filings may be subject to director liability for unremitted tax and to criminal liability if they knowingly participate in a sham arrangement.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / T106 Omissions

Statutory sanction: CRA may increase income to arm's-length levels, deny deductions for notional payments, and impose a 10% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment amount. Part XIII withholding may be assessed on deemed royalties or interest payments, with interest and daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be liable for Part XIII withholding if the partnership fails to withhold on deemed cross-border payments. Personal exposure includes director liability for payroll-like remittances where applicable and, in cases of deliberate false documentation, the same criminal sanctions as tax evasion.
AML / FINTRAC and Anti-Money-Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties up to CAD 1,500,000 for administrative monetary penalties on corporations, with additional orders for non-compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Conviction for laundering proceeds of crime can result in imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally liable for failure to comply with FINTRAC obligations when they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the contravention. A conspiracy or money-laundering conviction can trigger personal criminal liability, freezing orders, travel restrictions, and substantial restitution awards.
