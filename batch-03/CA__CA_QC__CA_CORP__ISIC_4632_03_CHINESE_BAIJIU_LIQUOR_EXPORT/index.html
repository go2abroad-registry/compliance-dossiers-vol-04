Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Collections and Verification Branch, GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; Revenu Québec, for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax. Federal online portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/business.html; Quebec portal: Revenu Québec S2 (s2.revenuquebec.ca) and Registraire des entreprises at registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is taxed on worldwide income as a separate person under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and under the Taxation Act (Québec); there is no flow-through eligibility. The entity is also a separate GST/HST and QST registrant. Territorial scope: a Canadian-incorporated resident corporation taxes its worldwide income; inbound non-resident shareholders are not taxed unless there are dividends, interest, rents, royalties or a permanent establishment. Goods-related scope: the imported Chinese baijiu becomes taxable in Canada upon importation unless the corporation is a GST/HST registrant and reclaims the GST/QST paid at import as an input tax credit. Export sale from Québec to a foreign buyer is zero-rated, not exempt, and the entity must still include export supplies in its GST/HST Net Tax Calculation and file periodic returns.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification system. Available statutory elections include: (i) the section 216 election for a non-resident recipient of rents and royalties to file an income tax return on that income; (ii) the subsection 219(2) branch tax election for non-resident corporations to defer the Part XIV branch tax on permanent establishment profits in treaty situations; (iii) the Excise Tax Act section 167 joint election for a deemed nil supply of all or substantially all of a business, and the section 273 joint venture election for GST/HST; (iv) the QST associated-corporations election under the Quebec Taxation Act for eligible related entities; (v) the voluntary registration election for non-resident small suppliers under subsection 240(1.1) of the Excise Tax Act; and (vi) mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 to 18.6 disallow deductions in structured arrangements, which operate without any elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in each calendar quarter or total taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceeding CAD 30,000 triggers mandatory GST/HST and QST registration (the 'small supplier' threshold). There is no monetary threshold for: (a) a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada; (b) a person importing liquor into Canada or clearing it under a customs duty deferral; (c) a person with a permanent establishment in Quebec. Zero-rated export supplies are counted when applying the CAD 30,000 threshold, and voluntary registration may be requested immediately upon incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods (including Chinese baijiu) made by a GST/HST registrant is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are exported from Canada and are delivered to a non-resident purchaser, or are shipped directly to a destination outside Canada in accordance with the Export of Goods Regulations; the exporter must prove that ownership and risk of loss passed to the foreign buyer and that the goods actually left Canada before being consumed, used or resold in Canada. The same zero-rating applies to the supply of freight, transportation and insurance services reasonably attributable to the export leg. For QST purposes, exports to a destination outside Quebec are similarly zero-rated. Where the corporation holds a valid excise licence, it may obtain a remission or rebate of excise duty on spirits exported out of Canada, and the GST/QST paid at importation is fully creditable as an input tax credit provided the alcohol is subsequently exported rather than sold in the domestic Quebec market.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list citing exact HS classification (2208.90.90 for baijiu), proof of foreign buyer, INCOFERM terms, per-unit and total selling price, and excise warehousing movement reference

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control system. GST/HST and QST invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format containing the prescribed disclosure elements (supplier name and BN, registrant number, date, tax-inclusive amount, tax rate). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework adopts the Peppol BIS 3.0 / PINT-A-NL standard on a voluntary basis via accredited service providers. For exported spirituous goods, a mandatory Customs Export Declaration must be transmitted electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the Canadian QP/EDI portal, and the excise duty electronic release or removal authorization must be obtained from CRA using their Interactive Services and Quebec's S2 portal for the QST return.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/HST and QST within four years of the filing date; the period is extended to ten years for omissions or misrepresentations by the taxpayer, and there is no limitation period for fraud. False zero-rating claims are subject to retroactive ITC clawbacks and recharacterization of exports as taxable domestic supplies, with gross negligence penalties of 25% of the unreported GST/HST and QST and 50% of any federal excise duty avoided, plus compound daily interest. CBSA additionally imposes Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) fines of up to CAD 25,000 per controlled export transaction for false declarations, and the Customs Act authorizes criminal prosecution with fines of an amount determined by the court and imprisonment up to five years. The Excise Act, 2001 provides for civil penalties equal to the full excise duty evaded and seizure of alcoholic products. Revenu Québec may issue assessments without time limit for QST where a business files fraudulently and can apply penalties up to 40% of the tax evaded under the Tax Administration Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account under IMF Article VIII. No exchange control authorization is required for profit repatriation, dividend payment, or export proceeds conversion. The sole hurdles are fiscal rather than regulatory: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (subject to the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement), transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and the back-to-back payment rules targeting treaty-shopping. CNY, USD, and CAD clearing is market-convertible through the North American interbank and CIPS-correspondent rails without conversion quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or forced conversion ratio for export receipts. High-proof baijiu exporters may retain, net, or convert proceeds in any freely convertible currency. All funds remain subject, however, to CRS/FATCA account reporting, section 233.1(1) affiliate disclosure, FINTRAC wire and currency reporting above CAD 10,000, and the requirement that any foreign currency retained offshore be economically justified under the transfer-pricing rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange policy and financial integrity supervision exercised jointly under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) Rules, and OSFI oversight. Cross-border currency and transaction reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium, as a supplementary overlay only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Revolut, Currencycloud) may legally provide multi-currency virtual IBANs and cheaper conversion for ancillary disbursements, but they cannot serve as the sole repository of export proceeds. The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires Canadian registration or a licensed agency partner, and the PCMLTFA correspondent-banking rules restrict flow-through of funds that bypass a Canadian Schedule I bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contribution and loan funding from PRC shareholders should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103/MT202 through a correspondent bank—via CIPS (RMB) or CHIPS/Fedwire (USD)—into the National Bank of Canada or BMO operating account, quoting purpose code 'Investment / Capital Contribution' and referencing the Federal corporation number and Quebec registration (NEQ).
