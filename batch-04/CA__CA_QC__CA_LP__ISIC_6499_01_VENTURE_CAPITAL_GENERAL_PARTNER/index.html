Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None prescribed by statute. A Quebec société en commandite (limited partnership) may be registered with zero minimum paid-up capital; the capital commitments of the general and limited partners are fixed exclusively by the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow or bank attestation is required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the local fire prevention service and/or the Régie du bâtiment du Québec for commercial occupancy; structural load and occupancy permit from the municipal building authority; registration with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) for workplace occupational health and safety; implementation of a CNESST-compliant health and safety program; workplace electrical, elevator, and emergency exit inspection clearance; commercial general liability and professional indemnity insurance in force.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax, GST/HST registration and Business Number (BN) administration; Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), GST/HST collection for Quebec-based businesses under the Canada–Quebec integrated tax administration agreement, Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) coordination, and provincial payroll/health-services levies; portals: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account; Revenu Québec TaxNet.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident venture capital GP (organized as a Quebec limited partnership) must first obtain a CRA Business Number via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, supplying the partnership agreement and foreign registration documents, electing partnership flow-through status, and registering for GST/HST where taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntarily to claim input tax credits. Concurrently, register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), then open a QST account with Revenu Québec where taxable supplies in Québec exceed CAD 30,000; Quebec-established businesses file combined GST/HST–QST returns through Revenu Québec TaxNet. Non-resident GPs must designate a service representative in Quebec and provide certified translations of constitutive documents; no minimum capital requirement applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Quebec Taxation Act: income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated directly to general and limited partners, including non-resident limited partners. No entity-level federal or Quebec income tax applies; non-resident partners may be exposed to Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source partnership income under ITA s. 212(2.3). The LP is not subject to a corporate check-the-box regime. — A foreign non-resident venture capital GP (organized as a Quebec limited partnership) must first obtain a CRA Business Number via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, supplying the partnership agreement and foreign registration documents, electing partnership flow-through status, and registering for GST/HST where taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntarily to claim input tax credits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Quebec Taxation Act: income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated directly to general and limited partners, including non-resident limited partners. No entity-level federal or Quebec income tax applies; non-resident partners may be exposed to Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source partnership income under ITA s. 212(2.3). The LP is not subject to a corporate check-the-box regime.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification election; corporate or partnership status follows legal form. Relevant statutory elections for a venture capital GP partnership include: (i) subsection 216(1) ITA election allowing a non-resident partner to compute tax on net Canadian-source partnership income rather than flat gross withholding; (ii) reasonable income-allocation agreement and guaranteed-partner-compensation substantiation under ITA s. 96(1.1); (iii) GST/HST election under ETA s. 177(1) appointing the GP as agent for a non-resident fund; (iv) GST/HST related-supply election under ETA s. 272.1 and corresponding QST close-election for supplies between the GP and its related fund vehicles; (v) partnership fiscal-period election where available under ITA s. 249.1. No hybrid mismatch elective relief exists; Canada applies anti-hybrid and foreign-accrual deeming rules independently.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST: worldwide taxable supplies in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240, small-supplier rule); QST: taxable supplies made in Quebec over the preceding four calendar quarters (QLTA ss. 415 and 417). For a VC GP, management fees charged to a Canadian-resident fund count toward the threshold; zero-rated supplies (e.g., certain services to non-resident funds) are excluded. Voluntary registration remains available to recover ITCs/ITRs on professional fees, AMF regulatory licence costs, and software subscriptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI Part V where supplied for export, delivered to a common carrier, or physically removed from Canada within 60 days of the invoice. Services: zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST and the service is performed wholly outside Canada (ETA Sch. VI Part V s. 20), or where the service is a supply related to exported goods. For a Quebec GP's management, advisory, and carried-interest services to a non-resident venture fund, zero-rating requires that the fund is non-resident with no Canadian establishment, the place of supply is not in Canada under ETA Sch. IX and QLTA Sch. IX, and the GP is not acting as an agent for a Canadian establishment. Fees charged to a Canadian-resident fund or to a non-resident fund's Canadian permanent establishment are taxable at 14.975%.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA electronic Commercial Export Declaration (via Canada Border Services Assessment/CESA) or the exporter's G13 proof-of-export records (Form A8A/B13) evidencing export address, HS classification, and transaction value.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — carrier transport document proving goods were delivered to a common carrier in Canada and departed the country; for offshore services, service-location evidence such as employee travel records, time logs, and engagement letters showing performance entirely outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank transfer records (e.g., SWIFT MT103) or IRC schedule evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident fund/investor, cross-referenced to the zero-rated invoice and partnership capital-call statements.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime exists in Canada or Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper and electronic invoices without prior validation. Mandatory invoice content for GST/HST-QST includes the supplier's legal name, BN and QST registration numbers (15/16-digit), tax rate, amount of tax, and a sequential invoice number; electronic records are admissible under ETA s. 238 and Quebec record-keeping rules. Revenu Québec's TaxNet requires electronic filing and API-based remittance of combined GST/HST-QST returns for registered Quebec businesses; no fiscal-device certification applies.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit zero-rated claims by VC GP partnerships, focusing on management-fee zero-rating where the fund is a tax-haven resident or services are performed partly in Canada. Missing export or offshore-service documentation triggers reassessment within the normal four-year limitation window (ETA s. 298; QLTA s. 1027), converting 0% supplies to taxable supplies at 14.975% output tax plus prescribed-rate interest and late-payment penalties up to 5% plus 1% per full month (ETA s. 280). False statements or gross negligence attract penalties of up to 25% of the net tax omitted (ETA s. 285.1; QLTA s. 1001), and knowing falsification is subject to criminal fines up to 200% of the evaded tax. Revenu Québec applies a 180-day inward-remittance audit benchmark: absent a contractual deferred-payment term, failure to repatriate consideration within 180 days of invoice raises a rebuttable presumption that the supply was made in Canada and is taxable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted CAD convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange control approval, registration, deposit, or surrender requirement for inbound LP capital commitments, capital-call wiring, foreign debt incurrence, or profit remittance. The only profit remittance hurdle is tax administration: distributions and fees to non-resident LP partners attract Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced under the Canada-US treaty or other applicable treaties), while the non-resident partner's allocated share of Canadian-source business income is asserted through the T5013/NR4 reporting procedure with associated Part I payment obligations. FATCA and CRS reporting applies at the GP and bank level; no FX control approval is needed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds or any other foreign-currency receipts. A Canadian-resident VC GP may hold USD or any freely convertible currency in domestic or offshore accounts without central bank approval. Administrative requirements are limited to: (i) T1135 foreign-income verification reporting if aggregate specified foreign property of the GP/resident partner exceeds CAD 100,000; and (ii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) filing by the receiving bank for every cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) exercises monetary authority and oversees the national payment clearing and settlement systems; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises the banks through which foreign exchange settlement is executed; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for all cross-border wire and EFT reporting; in Quebec, the Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) concurrently regulates the GP's exempt-market distribution, escrow conduct, and registration obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as a primary treasury channel. Canada has no EU-style e-money institution licence; a foreign EMI must register as a Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and register with FINTRAC as a money service business before transacting. Registered EMIs can provide efficient euro/GBP/USD pay-outs and API treasury rails, but they cannot accept deposits of committed LP capital, are not CDIC-insured, and lack OSFI-regulated clearing membership. Feasibility rating: acceptable only for reimbursement of small operating costs and travel-related GP disbursements; not viable for capital-call escrow or distribution settlement.
