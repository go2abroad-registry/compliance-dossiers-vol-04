Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime; the LP is mandatorily fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax under Income Tax Act s.96 and the Quebec Taxation Act. Available elections include a partnership fiscal period election under ITA s.249.1 and a partner-level phased income election. The LP may also elect GST/HST group treatment under ETA s.156 and the QST equivalent, and may consider the hybrid-mismatch election rules in ITA ss.18.4-18.6 and related Quebec provisions to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable worldwide supplies for GST/HST, and CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in Quebec for QST. Financial services supplied by the fund are exempt supplies, so a partnership whose only taxable turnover is exempt financial services may remain unregistered despite exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration may still be sought to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported from Canada under a customs export declaration. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient and not supplied to, or consumed by, any person in Canada. For a royalty-financing fund, revenue-based financing or royalty transactions with non-resident digital brands will generally constitute exempt financial supplies unless structured as exported services under Schedule VI Part V s.7 of the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST regulation; the taxpayer must substantiate the non-resident status, foreign use, and place of supply, including a written contract identifying the recipient and the service description.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Export Report / Form B13A) for physical goods; for pure services, an export declaration is not applicable
  • Bill of Lading or other contract of carriage evidencing physical removal from Canada; for services, the signed financing royalty agreement and evidence of delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/FX wire confirmation documenting the non-resident's payment
  • Recipient non-resident status certificate or equivalent tax residence evidence if relied upon for zero-rating
  • Detailed GST/HST/QST-compliant invoice showing the supply description, rate basis and zero-rated nature

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API approval or digital stamping exists in Canada at the federal or Quebec level. Electronic invoices are permitted if accessible in a legible, readable format and contain the mandatory prescribed particulars (seller identity, BN, date, description, tax amounts). The federal and Quebec e-invoicing framework is voluntary and follows PEPPOL-BIS 3.0 interoperability without mandatory pre-clearance.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated supply at the combined 14.975% rate where contemporaneous documentary proof is deficient, adding statutory interest and penalties for late reporting. Gross negligence carries a civil penalty of up to 50% of the tax evaded under the Income Tax Act and parallel Quebec rules; fraudulent zero-rating or refund schemes are indictable offenses with fines up to 200% of the tax attempted to be avoided and imprisonment. Refunds may be clawed back plus interest if documentation is manufactured or the underlying export never occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with fully free convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign exchange controls or mandatory approvals for inward capital contributions, profit remittances, royalty payments, or financing-fund distributions by a non-resident LP. Profit remittance is governed principally by tax compliance: Part XIII withholding at source (e.g. 25% Canadian statutory withholding on dividends, interest, rents, or royalties to non-residents, subject to treaty reduction under the Canada-US Tax Treaty where applicable), rather than exchange-control restrictions. Québec also applies its own provincial withholding and reporting obligations under the Taxation Act (Quebec).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no surrender rule, statutory timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations are limited to general AML/CFT rules: financial institutions must report electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and the Canada Border Services Agency collects trade data with underlying paperwork preserved. No central bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Department of Finance (Canada/FINTRAC) administers no separate foreign exchange control agency, and OSFI supervises federally regulated banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability for cross-border use in Canada. Canada does not recognize the European 'EMI' or electronic-money-institution vertical as a bank-regulated category. A foreign EMI cannot take deposits in Canada without becoming a bank; it may operate only as a registered money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC if it carries on remittance or foreign-exchange dealing in Canada. Digital EMIs can be practical only as an ancillary payment-rail provider behind a tier-1 Canadian bank account, not as the primary clearing or custody bank for an LP royalty-financing fund. Québec's financial-licensing regime under the AMF may further constrain unlicensed fintech inbound activity.

Canada does not use numeric central-bank purpose codes for FX-control purposes, but the bank will demand a clear economic-purpose string such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / ROYALTY FINANCING FUND / LP INVESTMENT' and may file a FINTRAC EFT report for any amount equal to or above CAD 10,000. To satisfy bank verification, include the signed LP certificate, source-of-funds evidence, capital account statement, and confirmation that the inbound transfer is a contribution to partnership equity and not a loan requiring Part XIII withholding. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Federal-Quebec general corporate rate (Federal 15% Part I + Quebec 11.5%); the Limited Partnership (CA_LP) is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96, so the headline rate applies only at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII (ITA s.212(2)) for dividends paid by a Canadian resident to a non-resident recipient; treaty-reduced rates apply (e.g., 5% under the Canada-US DTA for a US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares; 15% otherwise). Distributions by the LP to non-resident partners that are not attributable to a Canadian business carried on in Canada are also subject to Part XIII at 25%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due by May 31 of the following year). A Canadian partnership with non-resident partners or with gross revenue of CAD 2,000,000 or more, or assets of CAD 5,000,000 or more, is required to file.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 6 months after the corporate partner's taxation year-end; quarterly instalments of estimated Part I tax are due on the last day of each quarter for the current-year tax obligation; instalment relief is available where the partnership has already remitted on behalf of the partner.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Part XIII withholding must be remitted no later than the 15th day of the month following the month in which the amount was paid or credited to the non-resident partner; the NR4 Summary and slips are due on or before March 31 of the calendar year following the year of payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in transactions with any single non-arm's-length non-resident, or CAD 5,000,000 in aggregate with all non-arm's-length non-residents: exceeding either threshold triggers (a) mandatory T106 information return filing and (b) contemporaneous Local File documentation under CRA policy; a Master File is required for MNE groups with worldwide gross revenue of CAD 1,000,000,000 or more. The same thresholds apply for Quebec provincial reassessments.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not maintain a standalone codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. Economic substance is enforced through (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245 and Quebec Taxation Act s.1079.1), (2) the 'misuse or abuse' jurisprudence, (3) sham and agency doctrines in partnership law, and (4) the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in DTA applications. A Quebec LP engaged in revenue-based financing for digital brands must demonstrate substantive management and control in Quebec, real employees, localized books and records, and arm's length capitalization; otherwise it may be recharacterized as a conduit or shell and lose access to treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (willful circumvention of Canadian income tax)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; unabated interest on all arrears; seizure and sale of property under ITA s.224; Quebec adds parallel penalties under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who 'assented to or acquiesced in' the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part I and Part XIII amounts under ITA s.227.1; courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution, impose travel bans and passport surrender, and imprison directors for up to 2 years for tax evasion or up to 5 years for fraud under Criminal Code s.380.
Transfer Pricing Violation (ITA s.247)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s.247(2) adding the adjustment amount to the LP's income; penalty under ITA s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment where documentation is incomplete or the taxpayer fails the reasonable-efforts defense; interest under ITA s.161; T106 late-filing penalties and CAD 25,000 fines for record destruction; Quebec reassessment and penalties follow the same adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors are assessable under ITA s.227.1 for any withholding that becomes unremitted after the deductions are disallowed; in aggravated cases criminal tax evasion charges, travel bans, and passport surrender follow; the LP's separate legal personality may be disregarded where it is found to be a sham or mere conduit.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,500,000 for entities per violation (higher amounts may apply to large money services businesses based on gross revenue); criminal indictment carries imprisonment for up to 5 years; FINTRAC may suspend or refuse Quebec registration; Revenu Quebec and the AMF (Autorite des marches financiers) may impose separate provincial sanctions.

Director personal liability: Every director, officer, or partner who 'directs, authorizes, assents to, acquiesces in, or participates in' the AML offense is personally guilty of an offense and liable to fines and imprisonment under the PCMLTFA; travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and civil restitution orders may issue under proceeds-of-crime legislation; the corporate veil is pierced when the LP functions as the alter ego of its controllers.
Shell Company / Sham and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under ITA s.245 and Quebec Taxation Act s.1079.1 denying the entire tax benefit; gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understated where misrepresentation is established; the non-resident partner may be deemed to carry on business through a permanent establishment subject to Part I tax on a net basis; treaty benefits are denied under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are personally exposed under ITA s.227.1 for all unremitted Part XIII and Part I taxes; criminal fraud charges under Criminal Code s.380 may be laid for sham conduit arrangements; courts may order civil restitution, impose travel bans, require passport surrender, and pierce the corporate veil where the LP is an instrumentality of its founders.
