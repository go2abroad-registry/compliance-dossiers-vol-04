Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Administration fédérale des contributions (AFC) — official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; e-service access via eService / SUISA for VAT registrations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity performing taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT with the FTA using Form 08 (VAT registration application) via the FTA eService portal; the entity, or its fiscal representative if not resident in Switzerland, submits the application and receives a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr./UID-CH), effective no later than the start of the taxable activity. For corporate income tax, a non-resident entity is generally taxable only if it has a Swiss permanent establishment; registration for UID-CH (Business Identification Number) is also obligatory under the Federal Business Identification Law. The AG, as a Swiss resident company, is automatically registered with the cantonal tax authority and the Federal Register of Commerce; its UID-CH number serves as the unified tax identifier for federal/cantonal tax declarations and VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with worldwide income for resident AG; Swiss income tax is levied at federal, cantonal, and communal levels on the legal entity as a separate taxpayer. A Swiss AG's worldwide net profits and reserves are generally taxable, with exception-based territorial carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. The AG is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a check-the-box entity classification regime analogous to US Treasury Reg. 301.7701-3. However, specific elections exist: (i) VAT grouping election (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) to treat several related entities as one taxable person; (ii) election for partial taxation or participation relief on dividends and capital gains; (iii) election for privileged status of holding companies under qualified cantonal tax regimes only for closed historical cases; Switzerland has enacted hybrid mismatch rules (as from 1 January 2020) disallowing deductions in respect of certain hybrid financial instruments and reverse hybrid structures; no standalone entity classification election is otherwise available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic businesses and entrepreneurs: mandatory registration if annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies) exceeds CHF 250,000. Non-established foreign entities: mandatory registration regardless of turnover, as soon as the foreign entity carries out taxable supplies in Switzerland; optionally, certain non-entrepreneurial or exempt activities do not count. Newly established businesses may opt for VAT liability from the start of business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Switzerland to destinations outside Switzerland are zero-rated (0% with full input VAT deduction) provided the goods are physically dispatched to a foreign destination and the export is duly declared to Swiss Customs (Customs export declaration / Ausfuhranmeldung). The supplier must retain and present proof of export. Services relating to movable property or services supplied to a recipient abroad may be zero-rated if the place of supply is deemed to be abroad and the recipient is described as a non-resident entrepreneur or final consumer; for services in the B2B area the benefit criterion or other statutory place-of-supply rules apply. Supporting documentation must evidence foreign receipt or benefit and payment flows.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted and processed by the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (FCA)
  • Transport document proving physical removal from Switzerland: bill of lading, CMR consignment note, airway bill, or Swiss customs receipt
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and proforma invoice matching the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt showing receipt of payment from the foreign counterparty
  • Packing list and, where relevant, export licences/material certificates for medical devices

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or CTC (continuous transaction controls) requirement; no government-backed electronic invoicing API or digital stamping obligation exists for VAT. Electronic invoices are legally accepted provided they satisfy Swiss VAT invoice requirements. The Swiss E-Bill (eBill) and Swiss QR-bill are common in B2B/B2C payment and invoicing; the Peppol network is used on a voluntary basis for e-invoicing. An invoice must contain: name/address of supplier and recipient, UID-CH of supplier, date, nature of supply, quantity, consideration, tax rate and tax amount. No mandatory prior clearance by the tax authority is required.

The Federal Tax Administration and Customs Authority conduct targeted audits on export zero-rating claims. Where the required proof of export is incomplete or falsified, the FTA retroactively reassesses VAT at 8.1% plus interest (default interest currently up to 4.5% annually) and applies penalties. Under the Swiss VAT Act (Art. 96–97, 102), intentional or negligent tax evasion is punishable by a fine up to three times the evaded tax, and in severe cases criminal fines up to CHF 20,000 or imprisonment may be imposed. For customs declarations, false documents or omissions trigger administrative penalties under the Customs Act of up to CHF 10,000 per offence plus repayment of any customs advantages. Repeated deliberate abuses may lead to clawback of input VAT deductions, loss of zero-rating status, and publication of final administrative assessments.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Switzerland imposes no quota, prior-approval, or repatriation regime on foreign-exchange payments. The Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital and CO Art. 4 guarantee unrestricted cross-border settlement; residual emergency powers under the Federal Act on the National Bank have never been activated for ordinary corporate flows. Profit remittance and dividend payments are exempt from FX control but are subject to a statutory 35% withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which may be reduced or rebated under applicable double-taxation treaties. Royalties, intercompany interest, and trade payables require transfer-pricing documentation (OECD-aligned, Swiss VPDT guidelines) but no monetary license. Invoicing in CHF, EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency is permitted without SNB certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland does not prescribe any timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. A Swiss medical-device exporter may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts onshore or offshore, apply netting arrangements, and freely fund foreign disbursement accounts. Mandatory administrative duties remain: (i) electronic customs declarations via the Swiss Customs Administration (NCTS/Chrome) for each outbound shipment of titanium orthopedic implants, supporting trade statistics, export controls, and Swissmedic/FDA/CE document trails; (ii) SNB statistical reporting on cross-border receivables, payables, and direct-investment positions where annual thresholds (generally CHF 10 million) are exceeded, under Art. 66 National Bank Act; (iii) declaration of foreign accounts and intangibles in the Swiss tax return. No surrender-based sanctions attach to uncollected or offshore-held proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque Nationale Suisse), headquartered in Bern and Zurich; financial-market conduct supervision vested in FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority); trade and export-control competence held by SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) and the Swiss Customs Administration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal corporate income tax imposed on profit after federal tax (pre-tax incidence approximately 7.83%); the Canton Zug aggregate with cantonal and communal profit tax and federal tax is approximately 15% in ordinary cases. The pipeline schema codes Switzerland as a single national jurisdiction, but Swiss direct tax is collected and administered at the confederation, canton, and commune level; the CH_AG is therefore a taxpayer of the Canton Zug plus its commune of seat.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends distributed by the CH AG; under most Swiss DTAs the rate is reduced to 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting capital and 15% for portfolio investors; certain treaty protocols provide 0% on qualifying substantial holdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 10,000,000 - annual gross cross-border controlled transactions exceeding CHF 10 million trigger the preparation of a Swiss Local File and the group Master File under Swiss Federal Tax Administration practice; safe harbour thresholds are CHF 1,000,000 for intercompany goods transactions (porous titanium joint implants) and CHF 250,000 for intra-group services; the documentation is not filed with the tax return but must be produced within 30-60 days upon written official request.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply generic Economic Substance Regulations in the Gulf-style sense; however, treaty benefits require material substance under LOB/PPT clauses, and the STAF patent box and R&D super-deduction are enforceable only if the qualifying R&D activity, management decisions, and operational control of the titanium implant production genuinely take place in Switzerland. The Swiss doctrine of abuse of law (Rechtsmissbrauch) and the economic-substance-first approach allow the tax authorities to reattribute profits of a Zug AG that is a bare contractor, mailbox, or conduit: no employees, no production lines, and no engineering R&D in Switzerland defeats all income-based incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Criminal Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Simple tax evasion (art. 174-175 DBG) attracts a fine of one to three times the evaded federal, cantonal, and communal taxes plus default interest; qualified tax fraud involving forged documents (art. 186 DBG) carries imprisonment up to five years and a fine of up to CHF 30,000 in addition to the tax; Swiss anticipatory tax evasion (art. 66 VStG) carries imprisonment up to three years; the assessment limitation period is extended from five to ten years in evasion cases.

Director personal liability: Signing directors are prosecuted as co-perpetrators and face criminal fines and imprisonment; article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations makes each board member civilly liable for the full amount of taxes, penalties, and interest caused by breach of fiduciary duty; the authorities can issue travel bans, seize personal bank accounts in Switzerland, and request extradition through mutual legal assistance where profits are parked abroad.
