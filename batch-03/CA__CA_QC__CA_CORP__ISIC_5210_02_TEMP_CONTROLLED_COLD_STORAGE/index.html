Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — GST/HST, federal corporate income tax and payroll; Revenu Québec (Agence du revenu du Québec) — Quebec Corporate Income Tax, QST, and Quebec payroll; Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec CLICS (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, including through an enterprise in Quebec, must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or registering online through Business Registration Online (BRO). For Quebec, the same registration triggers the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and QST number through Revenu Québec if the business makes taxable supplies in Quebec. Foreign entities not resident in Canada that are required to file a Canadian income tax return must also obtain a BN for corporate income tax purposes and may need to register for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act if they are engaged in a commercial activity in Canada and are not a 'small supplier' or if they are non-residents who exceed the registration threshold or request to register voluntarily. Revenu Québec registration is completed via the 'Registration Form for an Enterprise Doing Business in Quebec' (LM-101-V) or electronically in CLICS, and requires a Quebec business address, the federal BN, and a representative to be designated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian federal or Quebec tax law. Canadian corporations are automatically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act and the Taxation Act (Quebec). Foreign entity classification is determined under CRA administrative policy by reference to the legal characteristics of the entity and its jurisdiction of formation. Available elections relevant to corporate reorganisation and tax planning include the s. 85 income tax rollover election for transfers of property to a corporation, the s. 86 share exchange election, the s. 93(1) election on dividends from a foreign affiliate, the s. 256(9) designation to deem a person to be related, and the Quebec provincial corresponding elections under the Taxation Act. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed through the general anti-avoidance rule and the hybrid mismatch rules in s. 12.6(1) and related provisions enacted under the Budget Implementation Act, but there is no elective mechanism to override entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST; non-resident persons carrying on a commercial activity in Canada are subject to registration if they exceed the threshold, provided they have a permanent establishment or make supplies in Canada; a non-resident may also voluntarily register. For QST, Revenu Québec applies the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for businesses making taxable supplies in Quebec; once registered, GST/HST and QST numbers are mandatory on invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the GST/HST and QST regimes for qualifying exports of tangible personal property from Canada when the goods are delivered or shipped outside Canada to a non-resident person, or to a carrier for export before the goods are used in Canada. For supplies of services, including warehousing and logistics services provided in connection with goods destined for export, a 0% rate may apply if the supply is made to a non-resident person and the service is not rendered in respect of Canadian real property, or if the service relates directly to tangible personal property that is exported in the course of the supply. The service must be contemporaneously documented and the goods must actually leave Canada within a reasonable period to support zero-rating; cold-chain storage provided before export may be zero-rated where the warehousing service is supplied to a non-resident customer and the goods are for delivery outside Canada, though the storage itself must be clearly tied to an export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A Form) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and purchase order from the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or sufficient foreign exchange / payment evidence (wire transfer, SWIFT or bank statement) showing settlement in foreign currency
  • Warehouse receipt or cold-chain inventory records showing the goods were held in Canada in transit and subsequently removed for export

Canada and Quebec do not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic documents provided they meet the prescribed information requirements under the Excise Tax Act and the QST regime, including seller identification, registration numbers, description, tax rates, and total amounts. The federal government has launched voluntary electronic invoicing interoperability pilots (e.g., Pan-Canadian e-invoicing specification) but no statutory API-based clearance obligation applies as of the current tax year. Revenu Québec does not operate a live fiscal data reporting portal; tax returns and registrations are filed through CLICS.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to stringent CRA and Revenu Québec audit review. The normal reassessment period for GST/HST and QST is four years from the filing date, but is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect attributable to the taxpayer. Where export documentation is incomplete, the zero-rated treatment is denied, resulting in a clawback of the tax amount that should have been collected, plus interest, and penalties under s. 280 and s. 285 of the Excise Tax Act (i.e., the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated tax in respect of false statements or omissions) and under s. 79.1 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act. Gross-negligence penalties may apply without a separate fraud determination, and criminal prosecution may be initiated for wilful evasion under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act or s. 96.1 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act. Although Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, CRA guidance and transfer-pricing audit practice expect that export proceeds are repatriated in a commercially reasonable period; a delay exceeding 180 days may be treated as a shareholder benefit, an indirect loan, or an unpaid balance under s. 15(2) of the Income Tax Act and the Quebec equivalent, leading to an additional tax assessment on the amount outstanding.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully convertible; Canada maintains no general exchange-control legislation and accepts IMF Article VIII obligations for both current and capital account liberalization. Profit remittance is lawful through dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or loan repayments; the material hurdle is fiscal: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, typically treaty-reduced), possible Quebec/Federal transfer-pricing adjustments, and anti-avoidance rules (e.g., deemed shareholder benefits, thin capitalization under subsection 18(4)).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline in Canada. A Quebec cold-chain warehousing entity may retain USD-denominated receivables in foreign-currency bank accounts, convert when commercially advantageous, or apply natural-currency matching for supplier obligations without Bank of Canada approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with residual exchange-control authority held by the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act; OSFI supervises Schedule I chartered banks; the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) licenses and supervises Quebec-regulated financial institutions. Payments Canada operates the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), and the emerging Real-Time Rail (RTR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium. A foreign digital EMI cannot operate a deposit-taking or clearing system in Canada without a federal Schedule I banking charter or provincial credit union licence; such EMIs may at most register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC for foreign-exchange dealing and remittance and, as of the Retail Payment Activities Act regime, be authorized but non-deposit-taking payment service providers. Their rails are acceptable for API-driven disbursement routing and FX execution, but capital escrow and high-value trade settlement should remain domiciled at a domestic Schedule I bank.
