Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV) — Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; VAT registration portal: ESTV eVAT (https://www.evat.admin.ch)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office or permanent establishment must register for Swiss VAT using the eVAT portal if it generates taxable turnover in Switzerland and does not benefit from the CHF 100,000 annual turnover threshold available to Swiss-resident businesses. A foreign company with a branch or permanent establishment must obtain a Swiss Value Added Tax Number (MWST/TVA no.) through the local cantonal tax authority if the PE itself conducts taxable supplies. A straight-forward application involves: (i) preparing statutory identification documents, (ii) declaring anticipated Swiss turnover, (iii) appointing a Swiss resident tax representative if no Swiss place of business exists, (iv) receiving the VAT identification number, and (v) filing VAT returns quarterly or annually in Swiss francs. For ordinary income tax, a Swiss AG incorporated under Art. 620 et seq. of the Swiss Code of Obligations obtains a company identification number (UID number) automatically upon commercial register entry; tax registration for corporate income tax is effected by the cantonal tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election. Swiss tax law is based on entity-level taxation under federal, cantonal, and communal rates. Available elective mechanisms relevant to a CH_AG include: (i) VAT group election (Art. 12 VAT Act), (ii) election to maintain a single economic unit for VAT across Swiss group companies, (iii) Swiss capital contribution reserve ('agio') election preserving tax-free distribution of paid-in capital, (iv) election for fair market value step-up in the case of a qualified corporate reorganisation/restructuring, (v) election to apply the flat-rate tax credit method for foreign permanent establishment profits, and (vi) statutory reserve allocation on a deemed liquidation basis. Hybrid mismatch elections (e.g. under EU ATAD) are not directly applicable outside the EU; Switzerland generally avoids hybrid mismatch treatment in its own domestic law and dual-residence tie-breaker rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) on both current and capital account under Art. 27 and 28 of the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments; capital flows are freely admitted, and there are no authorization, licensing, or repatriation restrictions applicable to profit remittances, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. The only friction is a 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends and qualifying interest distributions, which may be reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty. Non-resident repatriation in hard currency is unrestricted, and bank debits are processed in any freely tradable currency. Payment transactions are not subject to exchange control but payments must be made through SNB-regulated banks if routed via Swiss-based accounts; offshore settlement is unblocked for Swiss resident entities, provided anti-money laundering reporting duties are fulfilled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export receipts; Swiss law does not require exporters to surrender or convert foreign currency proceeds. Exporters are free to hold, invest, or convert receipts at commercial bank discretion. SNB reporting obligations apply only for statistical balance-of-payments purposes under the SNB's Reporting Regulation, which requires periodic reporting of external assets, liabilities, and payment transactions above a threshold (typically CHF 10 million per annum for permitted payment data); no central repatriation or concentration into a state account is required.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) established by the Swiss Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank; operational authority in accordance with the Federal Foreign Exchange and Balance of Payments Reporting Ordinance (VW-VO).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for non-bank fintech EMIs domiciled in Switzerland with FINMA licensing under the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA); Low for foreign digital EMI-only providers without a Swiss banking or FinIA license. Swiss regulators require any entity maintaining current accounts or executing payment services for third parties to hold a banking license (if accepting deposits) or FinIA license (if offering payment services). Foreign EMIs may operate in Switzerland solely on a cross-border pass-through basis without local licensing if services are initiated abroad and no solicitation occurs within Switzerland; however, Swiss-domiciled trading entities are often not permitted to receive trade settlement payments onto an unlicensed foreign EMI account because Swiss banks apply strict 'clear title' and KYC standards. For transactional efficiency, a Swiss-licensed bank account is strongly preferred; a UBS or ZKB account provides FPS (Swiss Faster Payments) and SEPA participation, which foreign EMIs cannot reliably offer for CHF-based trade settlements.

Root cause: Country-of-origin sanctions risk on goods or counterparties

Defense evidence: Deliver a per-product country-of-origin analysis with harmonized-system (HS) codes, a detailed supply-chain audit for US, EU, and Chinese goods to ensure no OFAC/SECO/Swiss SECO embargo violations, and a counterparty screening report confirming the absence of sanctioned parties on FINMA and SECO lists.
Payment clearing uncertainty due to the absence of an operational Swiss IBAN

Root cause: Payment clearing uncertainty due to the absence of an operational Swiss IBAN

Defense evidence: Obtain a Swiss IBAN with the bank in the same currency (CHF and EUR) and demonstrate the continuity of the account through a bank letter confirming that methods SWIFT, SEPA, and FPS (Faster Payments) are available to process receipts and disbursements without third-party risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be routed from the parent or investor's bank to the Swiss company's capital escrow account via SWIFT MT103, using the ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPITAL' (or `SUPP` for supplemental contribution) in field 70. The transfer must be earmarked as equity against a capital contribution resolution of the board of directors; the Swiss Commercial Register entry showing the paid-up share capital must be updated before the funds are converted into an operating account. The confirmation must be supported by a bank certificate (Kontobestätigung) and, if the contribution exceeds CHF 1 million, a Swiss statutory auditor's confirmation. For inbound payments from non-EU jurisdictions, include the remitter's AML identification details (passport or LEI), and state the underlying contract reference in field 72. The Swiss National Bank must be notified only when the aggregate quarterly cross-border reportable data exceed the SNB reporting thresholds; no prior authorization is required.Trade settlements for the purchase of IVD reagents should be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in the settlement currency (USD, EUR, or CHF), or via a commercial documentary letter of credit for larger or first-time suppliers in high-risk jurisdictions. Photocopies of the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and certificate of analysis must be transmitted through the SWIFT secure messaging channel when using a 'Documents against Payment' instruction. Useful corridors are: (1) SEPA instant and standard credit transfers for EU suppliers in EUR; (2) SWIFT MT103 for UK, Swiss, and US suppliers; (3) commercial LC (UCP600) for suppliers in Asia or other emerging markets. All outbound disbursements must contain the purpose code `GOOD` or `S1034` (purchase of goods) in the SWIFT instruction and, for amounts above the SNB reporting threshold (usually €10,000 or equivalent per individual cross-border payment and CHF 10 million cumulative), the bank will report to the SNB for statistical balance-of-payments purposes.
