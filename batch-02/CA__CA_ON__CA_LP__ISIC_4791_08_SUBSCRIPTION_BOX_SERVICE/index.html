Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under the Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96, a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: no entity-level federal or Ontario provincial income tax; income/losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who are taxed in their own capacity. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income (business carried on in Canada, rentals, and dispositions of taxable Canadian property), subject to a treaty override; resident partners are taxed on worldwide income. Ontario parallels this in its Taxation Act, 2007. For GST/HST, the LP is a distinct 'person' and registrant that must account for and remit HST on its own supplies and may claim input tax credits (ITCs).

Available election forms

1) US federal check-the-box: a Canadian LP is not a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2; an eligible entity election on Form 8832 may be filed to choose partnership/corporate classification for US tax, subject to the deemed-corporation anti-avoidance rule for foreign-owned eligible entities under § 301.7701-3(e) and the Canadian 'fiscally transparent' analysis. 2) Canadian partnership transparency is mandatory — no 'elective fiscal transparency' exists; however, non-resident partners may elect under ITA s. 216(1) to compute Canadian rental income/liability on a net basis instead of the 25% gross withholding (Part XIII) basis. 3) GST/HST related-party elections: ETA s. 156 election to deem a nil consideration for taxable supplies between closely related registrants; ETA s. 273 joint-venture election allowing a participant to act as the GST/HST agent for a joint venture. 4) Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA Division E? actually 'Hybrid mismatch rules' in subsections 18.18–18.31, enacted in 2022 via Bill C-19? — for safety, 'the hybrid mismatch rules introduced under the BEPS Action 2 package (effective for 2022+)') apply to deny deductions for amounts that are non-taxable in the recipient's jurisdiction; Canadian LPs remain transparent, so hybrid transparency mismatches with the US should be monitored.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); a non-resident (including an LP) carrying on business in Canada that makes taxable supplies in Canada must register once its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or over four consecutive calendar quarters (plus CAD 30,000 for each related person/associated registrant); voluntary registration permitted. For foreign vendors of 'specified digital supplies' a lighter registration system exists, but physical subscription boxes are not digital supplies and require the standard BN/RT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (subscription box merchandise): zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the supplier delivers tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada — for a physical box business, the decisive test is 'delivery to an address outside Canada' using the supplier's or recipient's freight/courier arrangement; domestic delivery to a Canadian address is taxable at 13% HST and cannot be zero-rated merely because the recipient is a non-resident or the payment originates abroad. Services: services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 unless they relate to real property in Canada or tangible personal property in Canada; subscription-administration services rendered to foreign affiliates may qualify. The exporter must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence that the goods were actually shipped abroad, that the recipient is a non-resident, and that the consideration was received.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CERS/B13A Canadian Export Reporting System declaration (required for controlled/government goods or exports above the prescribed value; for low-value courier exports, the courier's electronic manifest may substitute)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international waybill (courier AWB, ocean B/L, truck CMR) showing original load and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of FX receipt (bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice, payment settlement records, Stripe/Authorize.Net export settlement statements) evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial sales invoice and purchase order or subscription agreement identifying the non-resident customer and the foreign delivery address
  • Proof of delivery confirmation (courier proof of delivery with foreign address, date-stamped) and insurance/freight invoices

Standard electronic records, POS and invoicing software are acceptable, provided records can be produced to CRA upon audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, fully convertible G10 currency. The capital account has remained open since the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; there are no central bank approvals, no foreign-investment licensing, and no profit-remittance barriers. Under the Income Tax Act, non-resident limited partners carrying on business through the Ontario LP are taxable on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income (Form NR6 and optional s. 216 return), but partnership distributions representing return of capital or tax-free surplus incur no Part XIII withholding; dividends paid by a Canadian corporation would be subject to 25% Part XIII tax, subject to treaty reduction (e.g., Canada-US Treaty). Practical hurdles are limited to FINTRAC EFT reporting for wires of CAD 10,000 or more, CBSA Form K-73 for physical currency movements above CAD 10,000, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting when a non-resident acquires an LP interest representing direct investment (10% or more). None of these constitute exchange controls or remittance barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement on export proceeds, and there is no prescribed timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Subscription-box export revenue may remain in USD, CNY, EUR, or a multicurrency account at a Canadian Schedule I bank without Bank of Canada approval. Mandatory obligations are limited to: (i) reporting any inbound or outbound EFT of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC; (ii) reporting physical currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more at the border (CBSA); and (iii) maintaining books and records sufficient to support the GST/HST and income tax filings of the LP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority and, since the 2024 phased commencement of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), the supervisory regulator for retail payment service providers. Canada, however, has no statutory foreign exchange control administration. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/CFT reporting on cross-border electronic funds transfers, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) enforces physical cross-border currency reporting. Prudential liquidity and derivative conduct rules are administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as an auxiliary treasury rail; MEDIUM as a primary account. Canada has no statutory 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence comparable to the EU/UK regime. Foreign digital payment firms (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury, Relay) may lawfully operate in Canada only by (i) registering as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA if they deal in foreign exchange, remit funds, or process EFTs, and (ii) registering with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA as a Retail Payment Service Provider when executing, clearing, or settling payment transactions for Canadian users as a business. EMIs cannot accept deposits (Bank Act ss. 544-545), so transaction balances must be custodied with a Canadian Schedule I/II bank. Legal constraints: (a) foreign EMI balances are contractual payment floats, not banking deposits, and will not satisfy a bank's or tax authority's expectation for partnership capital contribution evidence; (b) an EMI's access to Canadian clearing requires a principal-agent arrangement with a regulated domestic bank; (c) the LP's subscription revenue must ultimately settle to a Canadian banking institution to establish clean audit trails for the CRA and FINTRAC. Recommended deployment: outbound supplier disbursements (Wise Business / Airwallex) and inbound collection aggregation for non-Canadian buyers; never as the sole repository for LP capital or for deferred subscription reserve funds.

Recommended deployment: outbound supplier disbursements (Wise Business / Airwallex) and inbound collection aggregation for non-Canadian buyers; never as the sole repository for LP capital or for deferred subscription reserve funds.
