Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Quebec business corporation. Shares may be issued for consideration authorized by the corporation, subject to the articles, directors' approval, and the Business Corporations Act. Quebec does not generally require incorporation capital to be deposited into an escrow account. The corporation should maintain evidence of subscription, payment, and share issuance in its minute book. Banking institutions may impose their own funding and beneficial-owner verification requirements.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, obtain municipal building and occupancy approvals and fire-prevention acceptance, including review of exits, fire alarms, sprinklers, emergency lighting, storage heights, hazardous materials, and fire access. Structural floors, racking, docks, mezzanines, and lifting equipment should be reviewed by qualified professionals and maintained within certified load limits. Workplace compliance requires CNESST occupational-health and safety procedures, training, incident reporting, machine and forklift controls, personal protective equipment, emergency plans, and inspections. Additional approvals may apply to pressure equipment, fuel systems, refrigeration systems, hazardous products, or dangerous-goods transport.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and federal corporate income tax administration (portal: CRA My Business Account / GST/HST NETFILE / Business Registration Online); Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec provincial corporate income tax administration (portal: Revenu Québec Cliquez / Trans@ction; Registraire des entreprises du Québec for the enterprise number NEQ).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity does not require a Canadian incorporation to obtain a tax id. The foreign supplier that carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or apply online via CRA Business Registration Online to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN); add the GST/HST program account (RT-suffix) to the BN. (2) For Quebec Sales Tax, register separately with Revenu Québec using the NEQ assigned at Quebec business registration, or request a QST account (e.g., account number with a 'TQ' identifier) if the person has a Quebec establishment or makes taxable supplies in Quebec above the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold. (3) Under the July 1, 2021 GST/HST e-commerce framework, a non-resident seller fulfilling Canadian orders through a Canadian cross-dock or fulfillment warehouse is deemed to make supplies in Canada and must register unless the small supplier exception applies; if a distribution platform is used, the platform is the deemed supplier and must register and collect regardless of the seller's revenue. QST rules align with the GST framework via the Quebec Taxation Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act; corporate income is taxed at the entity level and any distribution to shareholders is separately taxed; no flow-through or check-the-box classification is available.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections: (1) Excise Tax Act (GST/HST): section 156 election to simplify invoicing between a supplier and a related corporation for direct payment supplies; section 150 election for closely related corporations to elect taxable treatment on intercompany supplies to preserve input tax credits; section 273 election for a member to file GST/HST on behalf of a partnership. (2) Income Tax Act: section 216 election for a non-resident owner of Canadian real property to file a net-basis Part I return rather than Part XIII gross withholding; section 219 branch tax rules for non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada; hybrid mismatch limitation under section 18.6 denies deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements with no opt-out election except as prescribed. (3) Quebec: analogous QST elections under the Quebec Taxation Act for closely related persons and consolidated filing elections. (4) For cross-border e-commerce fulfillment, the corporation may elect simplified accounting methods for associated persons under the GST/HST rules; no election can convert an entity-level taxpayer into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST and CAD 30,000 for QST (small supplier threshold) measured by taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or the preceding four calendar quarters (s. 148(1) Excise Tax Act; analogous Quebec rule). A non-resident e-commerce seller using a Canadian warehouse or cross-dock facility is deemed to make supplies in Canada and must register once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; however, for sales made through a distribution platform, the platform is the deemed supplier and bears the registration and collection obligation regardless of the seller's revenue. Certain non-resident digital-service suppliers may be required to register under the simplified non-resident registration framework, but the small supplier threshold generally applies to goods-fulfillment scenarios.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 20 Excise Tax Act when the goods are shipped or delivered to a destination outside Canada by or on behalf of the supplier before use or consumption in Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under section 7, Schedule VI unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is rendered to an individual present in Canada. Consequently, warehousing, cross-docking, pick-pack, and order-fulfillment services performed on goods physically located in Quebec are taxable at the combined 14.975% rate (GST + QST) even when charged to an overseas e-commerce principal; international freight transportation of goods from Canada to an outside destination is separately zero-rated under section 24 of Schedule VI. To claim zero-rating on the export of goods, the Canadian entity must make a supply that includes the export, retain contemporaneous evidence of export, and ensure the goods are not further manufactured, altered, or used in Canada before export. Input tax credits (ITCs) and Quebec input tax refunds (ITRs) are available on related expenses (inbound logistics, utilities, labour, occupancy) to the extent the expenses support commercial activities, including zero-rated supplies; a net refund arises when ITCs/ITRs exceed output tax.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, digital stamping, or continuous control transaction framework is in force in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST is self-assessed and remitted via CRA Form GST34 (GST/HST Return) through GST/HST NETFILE; QST is self-assessed and remitted to Revenu Québec via its electronic filing channels. Tax invoices must contain prescribed data elements (supplier GST/HST and QST registration numbers, date of supply, description, tax amount or rate). Pan-Canadian e-invoicing based on the PEPPOL standard is voluntary (Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Council initiative) and is being adopted for B2G transactions; Quebec has introduced similar B2G e-invoicing pilots. Non-residents may use a simplified registration process, but all electronic records and source documents must meet CRA and Revenu Québec record-retention requirements.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, which is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, neglect, or wilful default. If the exporter cannot produce the required documents, the zero-rating is reversed, output GST/HST at 5% and QST at 9.975% are reassessed, and arrears interest is charged. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: (i) failure to register or file penalties under s. 280 (greater of 5% of the amount owing and $100, plus 1% per month to 12 months); (ii) false statements or omissions under s. 285(1) – 50% of the net tax sought to be evaded (minimum $100); (iii) gross-negligence penalty under s. 285.1 – up to 50% of the understated net tax; (iv) criminal tax evasion under s. 327 – on summary conviction a fine up to $10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months, and on indictment a fine up to $100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Analogous penalties apply under the Quebec Taxation Act through Revenu Québec, including refusal or clawback of input tax refunds (ITRs) and monetary penalties of up to 50% of the tax evaded. Retrospective clawbacks may also disallow input tax credits/refunds on related expenses where the underlying zero-rating is denied, and directors may be liable for unremitted GST/HST and QST under the statutory director-liability provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada grants unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital-account transactions under OECD capital-movement liberalisation norms. Profit remittance, dividends, management fees, and repayment of shareholder loans may flow freely without prior Bank of Canada approval; the only macro-level frictions are Quebec’s source-withholding obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and the arm's-length transfer-pricing requirements for cross-border related-party payments. No bona fide onerous foreign-exchange conversion is imposed on the operational CA_CORP treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. CA_CORP may lawfully retain foreign currency receivables offshore or in domestic USD/CAD accounts. Export records remain subject to CBSA import/export reporting, GST/HST zero-rating documentary support, and FINTRAC’s EFT over $10,000 CAD threshold reporting; no exchange-control approval certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, with prudential banking supervision delegated to OSFI and AML/ATF surveillance to FINTRAC; Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration authority because of its free-floating currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintechs such as Airwallex, OFX, and CurrencyCloud are viable only as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs); they do not hold a Canadian Schedule I banking licence and cannot provide bank-grade escrow protection under CDIC. Legal constraint: core cross-border settlement must ultimately clear through a Canadian bank or a regulated correspondent bank. MSB rails are permissible as an operational efficiency layer for small ACH/Wire disbursements, but prudential capital-admission and controlled FX treasury functions should remain in an OSFI-regulated bank.
