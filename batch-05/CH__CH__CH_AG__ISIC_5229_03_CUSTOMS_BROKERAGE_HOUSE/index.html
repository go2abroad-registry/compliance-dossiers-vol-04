Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or registered service address is permissible for the seat of a Swiss AG if a board member or a duly authorized person with signatory authority is reachable in Switzerland and all corporate records are available at the address. However, for a licensed customs brokerage house under the Swiss Customs Act (ZG/SR 631.0) and FOCBS supervision, a purely virtual office without verifiable operating presence is likely insufficient. A physical Swiss operating address or a demonstrable agent arrangement is expected for customs declarations, bonded transit records and AEO-type licensing. Virtual office arrangements therefore carry material substance risk for customs brokerage and should be supplemented by a real operational site or controlled licensed warehouse/agent facility.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug / Greffe du registre du commerce du canton de Zoug), under the supervisory control of the Swiss Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt, EHRA) within the Federal Office of Justice.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations, SR 220), Arts. 620-763 governing the Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA), in particular Arts. 621 (minimum share capital), 628 (Sacheinlage / contribution in kind), 632 (paid-up capital), 643 (legal personality through registration), 718 (Swiss-resident board member), and 727a (opting out of the auditor); together with the Swiss Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV, SR 221.411), Arts. 14 and 18-20.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially authenticated Deed of Incorporation and Articles of Association (Statuten / Statuts), together with the electronic Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung im Handelsregister) filed pursuant to HRegV Art. 18.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Aktiengesellschaft must have a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (CO Art. 621); at least CHF 50,000 of the paid-up capital must be actually paid in before registration, subject also to the rule that no less than 20% of the nominal value of each share is paid (CO Art. 632). For cash contributions the paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened at a Swiss bank in the name of the company in formation, and the bank must confirm in writing to the Commercial Register Office that the funds are at the free disposal of the Board of Directors; the account is released automatically upon registration. In-kind contributions (Sacheinlage) must be the subject of a special audit report under CO Art. 628 and must be described in the notarised deed of incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV) — VAT Main Department; eTax/eService customer portal (www.estv.admin.ch); central business register UID via ZEFIX.

E-government filing portal

A foreign customs-brokerage entity without Swiss residence must apply through FTA eService using Form 'Registration of a legal entity / non-resident business', provide certified constitutional documents and appointment of fiscal representative if required, and obtain a Swiss UID/MWST-VAT number before invoicing taxable Swiss supplies. Registration is mandatory once the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold is met or for import transactions where VAT deferral is sought under a customs warehouse/bonded transit procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss-resident AG; taxed on worldwide business profits at federal/cantonal/communal level; no pass-through eligibility; bonded transit facilities do not create income-tax-exempt-zone status). — A foreign customs-brokerage entity without Swiss residence must apply through FTA eService using Form 'Registration of a legal entity / non-resident business', provide certified constitutional documents and appointment of fiscal representative if required, and obtain a Swiss UID/MWST-VAT number before invoicing taxable Swiss supplies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss-resident AG; taxed on worldwide business profits at federal/cantonal/communal level; no pass-through eligibility; bonded transit facilities do not create income-tax-exempt-zone status).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections relevant to an AG: election to continue hidden reserves on transfer of assets or on migration of tax residence (Art. 61 DBG), group relief/consolidation upon qualifying intra-group transfer, partial taxation of qualifying dividend income, and anti-hybrid election/neutralization rules under Swiss implementation of BEPS (no elective pass-through is available).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding VAT); foreign service providers must register when Swiss-scope services exceed CHF 100,000 per year; import VAT, customs duties and security are payable irrespective of this threshold, with bonded transit deferral available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% Swiss VAT applies to exports of goods where the goods leave Switzerland and the exporter holds customs evidence of export; to services directly and verifiably connected with exported or transiting goods (including customs brokerage, handling and international freight); and to services supplied to a foreign-resident principal that benefit exclusively overseas. Eligibility requires a written contract, stated export nature, and contemporaneous documentary trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration and export licence)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent Air Waybill / CMR international transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign-currency export proceeds receipt)

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or continuous transaction control. Invoices must satisfy Art. 26 VAT Act content and retention requirements; electronic invoicing (QR-bill/eBill) is accepted. No taxpayer-facing clearance API is mandated.

FTA export audits require complete provenance and export documentation; missing or contradictory evidence leads to immediate reassessment at 8.1% plus 4.5% default interest and penalty taxes. Culpable false declarations are treated as VAT evasion: fines up to the tax evaded, imprisonment for serious cases, loss of input VAT deduction, and retrospective collection within the statutory limitation period (up to 10 years for evasion).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility of CHF on both current and capital account; no exchange-control permit is required for foreign direct investment, capital injections, profit remittances, dividends, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. A customs brokerage house may hold USD/EUR receivables and client duty funds without conversion mandates. The only material restrictions arise from AML/CFT due-diligence duties (GwG Art. 3-9), not from currency regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation-timeline, or minimum-conversion-ratio rules for export receipts in Switzerland. Proceeds from customs brokerage fees or bonded-transit services may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency, subject solely to Swiss accounting and tax documentation duties (OR Art. 957-963b) and to underlying customs-clearance evidence.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse) under the National Bank Act (NBG), with FINMA supervising financial intermediaries under the Banking Act (BkA) and Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-Low as primary rail. FINMA-licensed fintechs (BkA Art. 1b; public-deposit ceiling CHF 100m) may support low-value disbursements, but cannot issue customs guarantees, hold customs-duty settlements, or provide BAZG e-dec debit mandates. Offshore EMIs without FINMA authorisation are not viable as the sole treasury channel for a licensed customs broker; supplementary use is permissible under FINMA Circular 2016/07.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% (Swiss federal statutory rate on profit after deduction of the direct federal tax itself; equivalent to 4.25% of pre-tax commercial profit; combined federal, cantonal and communal headline rates for an ordinary Zug-resident operating company generally range from 11.5% to 21.1%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) withheld at source on dividend and profit-share distributions made by the Swiss AG to both resident and non-resident shareholders; relief is available only by treaty or qualified domestic-law refund procedure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax period is the previous calendar or commercial fiscal year. The return must be filed within six months of issuance by the competent cantonal tax authority; for a 31 December year-end, cantonal practice generally requires lodgement by 30 June of the following tax year. Extended filing deadlines are available only with the tax authority's prior concession and do not suspend default interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Swiss administrative practice and the OECD TP framework adopted by the ESTV, a Local File-equivalent transfer pricing documentation set is expected where the Swiss customs-brokerage entity's annual controlled cross-border transactions exceed CHF 5,000,000 or where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds CHF 50,000,000; a Master File-style group documentation package is expected where consolidated worldwide turnover exceeds CHF 1,000,000,000. Even below these thresholds, the canton of Zug routinely requests contemporaneous pricing evidence for high-margin customs and logistics service arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and imposes no annual ESR reporting obligation. Swiss law nevertheless enforces an economic-substance standard through case law, anti-abuse doctrines, and the Swiss withholding-tax beneficial-ownership rule. A customs-brokerage AG claiming treaty benefits must operate with locally employed licensed customs brokers, an actual Swiss office, locally taken business decisions, and segregated ERP/customs records. A letter-box company that merely books brokerage margin while foreign related parties perform all functions risks recharacterization as a sham or as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Direct Federal Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax fraud and aggravated omission of income are sanctioned under Articles 175 and 186 of the Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act with imprisonment or monetary penalties, and the amount of the fine may reach up to three times the tax evaded in serious cases. The tax administration may also re-open the final assessment for up to ten years and impose default interest. False customs declarations under the Swiss Customs Act may trigger confiscation of assets and fines up to CHF 2,000,000.

Director personal liability: Under Swiss administrative criminal law, the directing manager, chief executive officer, and licensed customs broker are personally liable where the tax offence is committed in a corporate capacity and cannot be effectively assigned to a natural person. Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Articles 144 and 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, ordering the responsible director to restitute the evaded tax and related withholding tax. Travel bans, asset freezes, professional disqualification, and custodial sentences of up to five years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Pricing and Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax authority reclassifies the excess amount as a hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung), denies the expense deduction for Swiss tax purposes, increases taxable profit, and imposes 35% Swiss withholding tax on the deemed distribution, with default interest at approximately 5% per annum. Treaty reduction of the withholding tax is disallowed if the TRC, beneficial-ownership declaration, or contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is deficient.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the mispriced invoices are jointly and severally liable under Swiss withholding-tax law if the company fails to remit the tax on the deemed distribution. The CFO or managing director can be prosecuted as an accessory to tax evasion and ordered to compensate the full tax shortfall. Corporate relief or indemnification does not exclude individual criminal liability.
Letter-Box Entity Lacking Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority disregards the Swiss entity for tax purposes and reallocates profits to the foreign principal under the substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules; treaty benefits are denied under the principal-purpose test. A 35% withholding tax is assessed on undeclared profit-shifting plus default interest, and the commercial register may strike the company for non-complying registered seat requirements under Article 12 of the Swiss Code of Obligations.

Director personal liability: The nominal Swiss director who certifies false substance evidence faces criminal liability for falsifying documents and assisting tax evasion, removal by the commercial registry, a five-year ban on serving as a director, civil restitution claims for the full tax shortfall, and personal enforcement measures including travel bans and asset freezing.
AML Violations in Customs-Brokerage Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Regulatory enforcement may include FINMA administrative orders, monetary sanctions, asset confiscation, and revocation of the Swiss customs brokerage licence under the Swiss Customs Act. Criminal prosecution under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act and Swiss Criminal Code may lead to custodial sentences of up to five years, fines, and permanent exclusion from regulated financial and customs intermediation activities.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers, licensed customs brokers, and board members can be prosecuted in their individual capacity because corporate limited liability does not shield wilful AML misconduct. Directors face criminal fines, imprisonment, civil damages for client losses, withdrawal of their personal brokerage licence, travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from the board of any Swiss regulated entity.
