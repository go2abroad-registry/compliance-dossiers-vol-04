Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), under the Ministère de la Justice du Québec; online services at servicesenligne.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca

Governing statute

Civil Code of Quebec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), arts. 2236-2246 (limited partnership); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1), arts. 1-111; Charter of the French Language (CQLR c C-11) for LP designation rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Registration of a Limited Partnership (Déclaration d'immatriculation - société en commandite), filed under art. 2236 C.C.Q. and art. 12 et seq. of the ALPE

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No mandatory paid-up capital. Under art. 2245 C.C.Q., a limited partner's liability shield requires an actual contribution to the partnership fund, but no minimum amount, escrow account, or notarized capital statement is imposed by the ALPE.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety clearance (visa de sécurité incendie) and occupancy permit under the Quebec Construction Code and Safety Code; structural load review by a licensed engineer where spectator/tournament seating is installed; CNESST registration, first-aid equipment, and a written workplace health-and-safety program for employees; accessibility compliance for public venues under the Act to secure handicapped persons in the exercise of their rights.

Paid-in capital rule: No mandatory paid-up capital. Under art. 2245 C.C.Q., a limited partner's liability shield requires an actual contribution to the partnership fund, but no minimum amount, escrow account, or notarized capital statement is imposed by the ALPE.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — canada.ca — for federal GST/HST and federal income tax; Revenu Québec — revenuquebec.ca — for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec provincial income tax. CRA applies the federal Excise Tax Act and the Income Tax Act; Revenu Québec applies the Tax Administration Act (Quebec) and the Taxation Act (Quebec).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or written Form RC1. It then registers for a federal GST/HST account once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured over four consecutive calendar quarters. For Quebec, the LP must file Form RQ-0001 with Revenu Québec to obtain a QST registration number and a provincial-qualification business number. The LP files a T5013 partnership information return identifying all partners, including non-resident partners; a Canadian resident representative or agent should be designated for service of process and filings. Quebec payroll and health-services-fund accounts are also required if the LP employs Canadian staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act or the Quebec Taxation Act. Each partner reports its allocable share of partnership income or loss. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their share of Canadian-source partnership business income; separate Canadian-source amounts (such as management fees, royalties, or certain investment returns) may be subject to Part XIII withholding, subject to applicable tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; LP pass-through treatment is mandatory, not elective. For GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership itself registers and accounts as a registrant in the partnership name. Applicable elections and mechanisms include the hybrid-mismatch rules under Part I of the Income Tax Act (effective 1 July 2022 per BEPS Action 2) which may disallow deductions for payments subject to inconsistent tax treatment in the foreign partner's residence jurisdiction; partnership income-allocation agreements that reflect the partners' true economic interests under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; and the election to treat the partnership as a separate person for certain foreign-accrual and offshore-fund computations under subsection 96(3).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters; the QST threshold in Quebec is identically CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies. Esports platform subscriptions, tournament entry fees, and digital matchmaking services are taxable supplies of digital services, so mandatory registration is triggered once the threshold is exceeded. Registration is also required earlier where the LP voluntarily registers or carries on a commercial activity in Quebec through a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) goods exported by the vendor pursuant to Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, and (ii) digital services and intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident recipient under Schedule VI, Part V, provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and provided the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/QST. An esports community platform charging entry fees, subscriptions, and tournament platform fees to foreign users may zero-rate those supplies where the service is enjoyed wholly outside Canada; QST zero-rating mirrors the GST treatment under Quebec rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13 or EDI electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Signed platform terms or contract confirming the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore place of enjoyment
  • Zero-rated invoice, compliant with Quebec French-language rules, bearing the LP's GST/HST and QST registration numbers

Canada and Quebec impose no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no government API clearance regime for e-commerce or software platforms. Electronic invoices and payment records, including Stripe and PayPal settlement files, are accepted for audit purposes if they meet CRA's Books and Records Requirements and Revenu Québec's record-keeping rules. Every invoice issued by a Quebec enterprise must be drawn up in French or accompanied by an approved French version and must show the GST/HST registration number, the QST registration number, the rates applied, and, for zero-rated exports, the statutory basis of the zero-rating.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, and without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If export zero-rating is denied, the LP becomes liable for the uncollected GST/QST together with arrears interest calculated at the prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec rates. Statutory penalties include a minimum penalty of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax understated for knowingly false statements, rising to 50% for gross negligence and on repeat offences; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion or intentional filing of false returns is available under the offences provisions of the Excise Tax Act and the Québec Tax Administration Act, carrying substantial fines and imprisonment of up to five years on summary conviction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital account. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital contributions, subject to corporate law solvency tests, tax compliance, and anti-money-laundering reporting. Non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership may repatriate distributable profits in any freely convertible currency, provided that the LP complies with Part VII of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, where applicable, the Special Economic Measures Act sanctions regime. Payments in excess of C$10,000 crossing the Canadian border must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Exporters and service providers such as the esports platform may retain all export receipts in the currency of invoicing, convert freely at commercial bank rates, or deploy them for treasury purposes. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) may require full reporting of foreign revenue for income tax; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) requires reporting of large cross-border wire transfers of C$10,000 or more. No mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administration is also subject to the regulatory oversight of FINTRAC and the Department of Finance Canada for sanctions and cross-border reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for purely offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) other than Canadian chartered banks. A foreign EMI may not hold or operate as a full banking entity in Canada without a federal or provincial banking licence; however, it may provide payment processing services on a software-as-a-service basis if it registers with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and complies with FINTRAC as a foreign money services business. For direct treasury clearing and high-volume tournament payouts, a tier-1 Canadian bank account remains necessary; integration with Stripe and PayPal should be made through the bank's merchant acquiring and payout gateway.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate income tax rate in Quebec, comprising 11.5% federal general rate plus 11.5% Quebec provincial rate (both effective 2023 onward; federal rate reduced where taxable income is allocated to a province and subject to the Quebec abatement).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends remitted by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; not applicable to the LP itself, but applies when a corporate partner distributes profits to foreign investors.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013: due no later than March 31 of the following calendar year for a December 31 year-end, extended to May 1 where the partnership is required to file electronically and all partners are individuals; RL-15: due no later than February 28 of the following calendar year; Quebec corporate partners must include the LP allocation in their corporate T2/CO-17 returns due six months after each corporate fiscal year-end.

RL-15 Box 1 / Box 2 Québec allocation of the partnership’s business income (loss) and investment income (loss) of each Québec-resident or non-resident partner carrying on business in Québec. The LP must issue RL-15 slips for each partner, including non-residents with Québec-source income. Non-resident partners may be required to file a Quebec income return to report Québec-source business income; the RL-15 is an information slip and does not satisfy the partner’s filing obligation. Late filing of T5013: CAD 25–100 per day for each partner, up to 12 months, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 7,500 for partnerships with 1–250 partners (increased for larger partnerships); late filing of RL-15: CAD 25 per day per slip, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 25,000; additional penalties for repeat non-compliance and for failure to file information returns in prescribed electronic format; failure to remit non-resident withholding tax: 10% late penalty plus daily interest at the prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec rate (currently approximately 5% for CRA, plus Revenu Québec’s rate of about 6% for corporate accounts).
T5013 Box 124 / RL-15 Box 10 Current-year capital cost allowance (depreciation) claimed by the partnership on servers, software, hardware, and platform intangibles. Non-resident partners need the information to compute their own depreciation recapture or capital gains on disposition; the LP claims CCA at entity level and allocates net income after CCA to partners. Late filing of T5013: CAD 25–100 per day for each partner, up to 12 months, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 7,500 for partnerships with 1–250 partners (increased for larger partnerships); late filing of RL-15: CAD 25 per day per slip, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 25,000; additional penalties for repeat non-compliance and for failure to file information returns in prescribed electronic format; failure to remit non-resident withholding tax: 10% late penalty plus daily interest at the prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec rate (currently approximately 5% for CRA, plus Revenu Québec’s rate of about 6% for corporate accounts).
T5013 Box 119 / RL-15 Box 7 Interests paid or payable by the partnership to partners or third parties on capital contributions; relevant for withholding obligations. Interest paid by an LP to a non-resident lender is subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless reduced under the Canada-U.S. or Canada-other treaty; the LP must file NR4 returns and remit withholding. Late filing of T5013: CAD 25–100 per day for each partner, up to 12 months, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 7,500 for partnerships with 1–250 partners (increased for larger partnerships); late filing of RL-15: CAD 25 per day per slip, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 25,000; additional penalties for repeat non-compliance and for failure to file information returns in prescribed electronic format; failure to remit non-resident withholding tax: 10% late penalty plus daily interest at the prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec rate (currently approximately 5% for CRA, plus Revenu Québec’s rate of about 6% for corporate accounts).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer-pricing documentation (Local File/Master File under CRA’s administrative guidance) is required where the transaction value exceeds CAD 5 million (gross revenues for the year) or the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds CAD 5 million for a taxation year. Quebec does not impose a separate TP documentation threshold but adopts federal TP rules under the Taxation Act.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) do not apply in Canada in the form used in the UAE or other Gulf jurisdictions. Canada instead applies statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) and the specified partnership/limited partnership ‘business limit’ restrictions, which require genuine activity, real risk-bearing partners, and commercial purpose. If the LP is a Manitoba-qualified limited partnership under Quebec law, its limited partners lose limited liability if they take part in control of the business; for tax purposes, all partners must reflect economic substance of the platform operations, and CRA may recharacterize allocations without real economic risk as nothing more than profit-sharing arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Mischaracterization of partnership income and non-resident allocations

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the allocation under GAAR, impute income to the Canadian partners, disallow expenses, and impose a 10% gross-negligence penalty on amounts misreported; Revenu Québec may similarly reassess under the Taxation Act, with penalties up to CAD 5,000 for non-filing or false statements, plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be held personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and the corporation’s unpaid tax liabilities under ITA s.227.1; willful participation in a partnership-veil tax scheme may lead to civil restitution and exclusion from future CRA compliance programs; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment.
Failure to withhold Part XIII tax on royalties/interest/dividends paid to non-residents

Statutory sanction: Liability for the full unreported 25% withholding tax, interest on arrears, a 10% late-remittance penalty, and penalties for failure to file NR4 information returns; Revenu Québec may separately assess provincial penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general-partner corporation are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted tax under ITA s.227.1; gross negligence or deliberate non-withholding exposes board members to significant personal enforcement and CRA’s director-liability procedures.
Transfer pricing non-arm’s length services and intangibles

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust transfer pricing under s.247(2), impose transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) when the adjustment exceeds the threshold and the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts; 5% GAAR penalty possible; reassessments are not statute-barred where misrepresentation is proven.

Director personal liability: Transfer-pricing penalty assessed on the partnership or corporate partner is normally not automatically recoverable from directors absent gross negligence or deliberate avoidance; willful participation can trigger criminal liability under the Income Tax Act or Criminal Code for tax evasion, including imprisonment.
Failing to file T5013/RL-15 and income tax returns for the LPs

Statutory sanction: Minimum CAD 100 and daily penalties up to CAD 7,500 (T5013), CAD 25,000 (RL-15), federal/Quebec interest, and the potential loss of the right to claim business expenses; if the LP is later deemed to be a corporation for tax purposes under the Canadian anti-hybrid rules, exposure increases to corporate income tax rates plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who authored the repeated omissions are personally liable under federal and Quebec tax laws for the amounts owing; civil travel bans may issue for significant outstanding tax liabilities; evidence of deliberate concealment supports fraud prosecution and imprisonment.
AML and Banking red flags on tournament payout flows

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation for money-services-business failures; banks may freeze accounts under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; tax authorities can treat unreported prize fees as unreported income.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the general-partner corporation may be subject to personal FINTRAC penalties only where they directed the violations; criminal money-laundering charges can include up to 10 years’ imprisonment and asset forfeiture.
