Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — sole federal administrator of GST/HST, payroll, and partnership information returns. Registration and account portals: CRA 'My Business Account', 'Represent a Client', and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca. Ontario is a participating HST province; the provincial component is collected by the CRA on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance, so no separate provincial sales-tax registration exists. The limited partnership must first be registered with ServiceOntario (Ontario Business Registry) under the Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Ontario LP: file the Limited Partnership Declaration with ServiceOntario to obtain the provincial LP registration number; maintain an Ontario registered office and a resident general partner. (2) Obtain the federal Business Number (BN): submit CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' (or apply through BRO) in the partnership's name; CRA issues a 9-digit BN and opens program accounts, including RT (GST/HST) and RP (payroll) if employees exist. (3) Mandatory GST/HST registration: zero-rated exported BPO services are 'taxable supplies' for threshold purposes, so the LP must register once worldwide taxable supplies (including associated persons) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a commercial call-center operation will exceed this threshold immediately, and registration is mandatory effective from the earliest supply. (4) Partnership filings: file the T5013 Partnership Information Return within 5 months after fiscal year-end and issue T5013 slips to each limited partner; identify non-resident partners and their foreign tax identification numbers. (5) Banking and FX: open a Canadian bank account, activate GST/HST NETFILE, and preserve SWIFT MT103/wire-transfer evidence for export substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax under Income Tax Act, s. 96 (the partnership is not a taxpayer; each limited partner includes its allocable share of income/loss). For GST/HST, Excise Tax Act, s. 272.2(1) deems the partnership to be a separate person able to register, collect HST, and claim input tax credits. Territorial Scope: the LP carries on business in Canada through the Ontario call center, establishing Canadian nexus and a permanent establishment for all partners, including non-residents. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none applies in Ontario or federally.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box system; the LP's pass-through status is statutory. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 249.1 partnership fiscal-period election (non-calendar year-end, subject to partner stub-period accrual); (ii) ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to file a net-basis Part I return for certain rental/timber income rather than Part XIII gross withholding; (iii) GST/HST annual-filing election for registrants with annual taxable supplies up to CAD 1.5 million, and the Quick Method accounting election for registrants with annual taxable supplies up to CAD 400,000; (iv) hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 19.2, 20.1, 21.2 and related) operate automatically without elective carve-outs — a foreign partner's separate check-the-box election (e.g., U.S. IRS Form 8832 treating the LP as an association taxable as a corporation) does not alter the Canadian tax identity but may trigger hybrid-mismatch recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA small-supplier threshold): registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies of the LP and associated persons exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated export supplies count as taxable supplies for this test. A remote BPO/call-center operation will exceed the threshold immediately. Non-resident providers carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold; no separate Ontario threshold exists because Ontario uses the CRA-administered HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, a supply of a service is zero-rated where (i) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, (ii) the recipient is not registered under subsection 240(1) of the ETA at the time the service is performed, and (iii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or a service rendered to an individual in Canada. A remote BPO call center physically operating in Ontario that supplies helpdesk, tele-sales, and back-office services to a foreign client (e.g., a U.S. corporation) qualifies for 0% HST as an exported service, provided the foreign client is not GST/HST-registered and the services are not rendered to individuals located in Canada; any Canadian-facing call volumes would be taxable at 13%. The LP may claim full input tax credits (ETA s. 169) on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports, and should obtain positive non-residency confirmation from the client.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A) — applicable only where incidental physical goods (e.g., thin-client hardware) are exported; not required for pure service exports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — required only for incidental physical shipments; replaced by service delivery logs and call detail records (CDR) for BPO services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 — bank evidence of FX repatriation from the non-resident payer, matched to the zero-rated invoice
  • Executed Master Services Agreement / Statement of Work identifying the non-resident client and the offshore BPO services
  • Non-resident status proof — W-8BEN-E (U.S. recipient), foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign tax identification number, or OECD CRS self-certification

None — Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no mandatory electronic-invoicing API, and no digital stamping or tax-certificate mandate. CRA has adopted the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard for optional B2G e-invoicing (Government of Canada procurement), and e-invoices are valid if they contain the prescribed GST/HST invoice information and are retained in accordance with ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230. Registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE and remit electronically; books, records, and export substantiation must be retained for six years.

CRA regularly audits exported-service zero-rating claims on a contemporaneous-document basis. If substantiation fails, CRA assesses 13% HST on the full consideration, denies related input tax credits, and charges arrears interest. Penalties include the gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax amount (ETA s. 280(1)) for false statements made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; tax-evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA may reassess within four years of the return's due date (ETA s. 298), with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Retrospective clawback can also extend to previously allowed input tax credits and to HST deemed collectible from the non-resident client where the supplier cannot prove the recipient's non-resident, non-registered status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under both current and capital accounts. Ontario limited partnerships may freely receive and remit funds in any currency without prior government approval. Profit remittances, dividends, and partnership distributions to non-resident partners are permitted unconditionally, subject only to tax withholding (e.g., Part XIII tax) and standard anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds. There are no capital account restrictions, licensing gates, or notification obligations for cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation requirement. Export proceeds from BPO services may be held in foreign currency, converted to CAD at any licensed financial institution, or used directly for offshore disbursements. Reporting to Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) applies solely for tax purposes; FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or more) must be observed for cash or cross-border wire transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canada's central bank); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the AML/CTF supervisor; no dedicated foreign exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian-licensed money services businesses (MSBs) and payment service providers registered with FINTRAC, such as Wise Canada and Payoneer, can support cross-border collections and vendor disbursements. However, offshore EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration or a commensurate restricted-authority license cannot maintain deposit-like accounts or provide escrow/services that are legally reserved for Schedule I/II banks. Wise B2B and similar platforms are viable for settlement rail support but cannot act as the primary treasury bank for the LP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and capital contributions to the Ontario LP should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity's bank. The wire narrative must explicitly state 'LP capital contribution' and reference the LP's name and Ontario registration number. For amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting will be triggered automatically. No central bank permission is required; the bank may request a capital utilization plan and a certificate from the general partner confirming the contribution is classified as partnership capital and not a loan (to avoid tax recharacterization). All outbound payments must align with the underlying BPO contracts; cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian-licensed money services businesses (MSBs) and payment service providers registered with FINTRAC, such as Wise Canada and Payoneer, can support cross-border collections and vendor disbursements. However, offshore EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration or a commensurate restricted-authority license cannot maintain deposit-like accounts or provide escrow/services that are legally reserved for Schedule I/II banks. Wise B2B and similar platforms are viable for settlement rail support but cannot act as the primary treasury bank for the LP.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% combined headline corporate tax rate for a non-CCPC corporate partner of an Ontario Limited Partnership (federal Part I general rate 15.0% plus Ontario general corporate income tax rate 10.0%). The Ontario LP itself is fiscally transparent under Subdivision k of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is not an entity-level taxpayer; income is allocated to partners and taxed at each partner level.
