Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca/taxes-business; and Revenu Québec (revenuquebec.ca) as the taxing authority for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial corporate income tax, and Quebec enterprise registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or an equivalent Quebec statute and obtain a certificate of incorporation. It then applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or, for non-residents, Form RC1. The BN is the Canadian tax identification number and is supplemented with program accounts for GST/HST and corporate income tax where required. In Quebec, the entity must also register with the Registre des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and then open a QST account with Revenu Québec. Non-resident entities must designate a representative in Canada for service of notices and documents, and may file electronically only through an authorized representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or an equivalent Quebec statute and obtain a certificate of incorporation. It then applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or, for non-residents, Form RC1. The BN is the Canadian tax identification number and is supplemented with program accounts for GST/HST and corporate income tax where required. In Quebec, the entity must also register with the Registre des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and then open a QST account with Revenu Québec. Non-resident entities must designate a representative in Canada for service of notices and documents, and may file electronically only through an authorized representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Business Corporation is mandatorily taxed as a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, and consolidated tax returns are unavailable. Statutory elections that may be relevant include the functional-currency election under s.261 of the Income Tax Act, the s.93(2) deemed-dividend election on distributions from a foreign affiliate, and the s.216(1) election for non-resident net rental or investment income. Hybrid-mismatch rules under ss.18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act operate automatically as anti-avoidance provisions and cannot be avoided by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, and no reasonable expectation of exceeding CAD 30,000 in the next quarter, under the small-supplier exemption in s.148 of the Excise Tax Act and the parallel Quebec Taxation Act rule. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded and may be voluntary for eligible zero-rated or exempt suppliers seeking input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods are zero-rated when transferred to a non-resident for export and physically exported from Canada within a reasonable period, usually 60 days, as evidenced by an export declaration or equivalent customs documentation. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident, are not performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and are not rendered through a Canadian permanent establishment. Financial services supplied to non-residents under Part IX of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act are zero-rated when the recipient is non-resident and the service is not sufficiently connected to Canada; royalty and licence income from intangible property is zero-rated when the non-resident licensee does not exploit the right in Canada. A revenue-based financing fund must map each payment stream to the correct classification because pure financing components such as interest or loan fees may be exempt rather than zero-rated when the activity is a domestic financial service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration or equivalent proof of export for goods)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange settlement evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance, or digital-stamping regime applies at either CRA or Revenu Québec. Electronic invoicing is permitted, but invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec regulations, including the supplier's GST/HST and QST registration numbers, and must be retained for six years. Invoices issued in Quebec must also satisfy the language requirements of the Charter of the French language.

CRA and Revenu Québec may claw back zero-rating and reassess at the full combined rate of 14.975% with arrears interest and penalties if the supply is later determined to be consumed in Canada or if the export documentation does not satisfy the statutory evidence requirements. Civil penalties under ss.280 and 285 of the Excise Tax Act and parallel Quebec provisions apply to false statements, omissions, or failure to provide information, calculated as a percentage of the net tax otherwise payable. Criminal prosecution under s.327 of the Excise Tax Act and s.346 of the Quebec Taxation Act is available for deliberate evasion, carrying fines up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment; the normal four-year reassessment period is waived where misrepresentation, carelessness, neglect, or wilful default is shown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Canada has a free-floating exchange rate regime with no exchange-control quotas, no prior-approval requirements for capital movements, no forced conversion rules and no profit-remittance restrictions. Dividends, royalty payments, loan repayments, capital withdrawals and revenue-sharing distributions may be remitted freely, subject only to applicable withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and to ordinary AML/KYC requirements. For enhanced-risk treasury structures such as royalty-based financing, each cross-border wire must be accompanied by complete beneficial-ownership information, a clear purpose-of-payment description and a documented source-of-funds trail to satisfy FINTRAC reporting and bank compliance requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory repatriation or conversion of export receipts into Canadian dollars. Exporters and financing vehicles may retain foreign-currency earnings abroad, clear them through correspondent accounts, or convert them on the interbank market at freely determined rates. Export documentation is required for customs purposes through the Canadian Export Reporting System (Caesar) and related CBSA declarations, but neither the Bank of Canada nor any other regulator mandates a surrender deadline or a conversion ratio for export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; no dedicated central foreign-exchange administration exists in Canada. Prudential oversight of federally regulated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary treasury, Low for primary clearing. Canadian law does not recognise the European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. An offshore EMI cannot directly hold Canadian-dollar clearing accounts or access Payment Canada rails unless it is acting through a regulated Schedule I bank, Schedule II bank, or a federally regulated Canadian credit union. Registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC may provide remittance and payment services, but they may not accept deposits or operate as a full clearing bank. Offshore EMIs are therefore viable as secondary collection and distribution channels for foreign-currency flows, but the primary treasury infrastructure for a Quebec royalty-financing fund should sit with a Tier 1 Canadian commercial bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Use of 'fund', 'royalty' or 'financing' in the registered corporate name, combined with ISIC 6499, creates a preliminary classification as an unlicensed investment fund or securities dealer.

Root cause: Use of 'fund', 'royalty' or 'financing' in the registered corporate name, combined with ISIC 6499, creates a preliminary classification as an unlicensed investment fund or securities dealer.

Defense evidence: Constating articles and certificate of status from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec or Corporations Canada; a formal Quebec legal opinion confirming that the revenue-based royalty financing is structured as a commercial lending / revenue purchase arrangement and does not constitute the sale of securities; the ISIC/NAICS classification certificate; corporate governance documents showing no public solicitation, no pooling of investor capital and no discretionary investment management; and template revenue-financing agreements evidencing bilateral commercial contracts.
High-frequency cross-border MT103 wires from numerous e-commerce or digital-brand merchants can be treated as unexplained or structured third-party cash flows.

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border MT103 wires from numerous e-commerce or digital-brand merchants can be treated as unexplained or structured third-party cash flows.

Defense evidence: Master revenue-financing agreement; merchant onboarding KYC files; executed revenue-share schedules; underlying platform settlement reports from marketplaces such as Shopify, Amazon or Stripe; merchant-level invoicing and tax documentation (GST/HST/QST as applicable); a treasury reconciliation ledger matching every incoming payment to a specific receivable; and transaction-monitoring narratives explaining expected volumes and seasonal volatility.
Non-resident beneficial owners and a multi-tier offshore holding structure raise concerns over concealment of control and source of funds.

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial owners and a multi-tier offshore holding structure raise concerns over concealment of control and source of funds.

Defense evidence: Fully populated beneficial-ownership register under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec BCA equivalent; audited corporate chart showing each 25%-or-more beneficial owner; certified passports and proof of residential address for all key owners; professional source-of-wealth and source-of-funds reports prepared by an independent accountant; board resolutions authorizing the banking relationship; and executed declarations confirming the absence of politically exposed persons and prohibited jurisdictions.
Large inbound capital injections from related offshore entities may appear as suspicious funding without a clear debt or equity label.

Root cause: Large inbound capital injections from related offshore entities may appear as suspicious funding without a clear debt or equity label.

Defense evidence: SWIFT MT103 details with explicit remittance instructions such as 'shareholder loan' or 'equity capital contribution'; signed shareholder loan agreement or subscription agreement; board minutes approving the capital injection; remitting bank's customer due-diligence file and reference letter; tax treaty analysis addressing interest withholding or dividend withholding; Investment Canada Act notification or exemption assessment; and a consolidated group liquidity and repatriation policy.
Sanctions or adverse-media flags against digital-brand merchants or e-commerce counterparties can cause account restriction or delayed onboarding.

Root cause: Sanctions or adverse-media flags against digital-brand merchants or e-commerce counterparties can cause account restriction or delayed onboarding.

Defense evidence: Pre-onboarding and ongoing negative-media screening records; sanctions screening results against Canadian, OFAC, UN and EU lists; merchant government-issued business and tax identifiers; contractual covenants prohibiting sales of sanctioned goods; e-commerce platform verification records; and an enhanced monitoring policy with periodic re-screening and immediate escalation procedures.
Unexpected accumulation of cleared funds in the operating account before disbursement to brand owners may be interpreted as money laundering or layering.

Root cause: Unexpected accumulation of cleared funds in the operating account before disbursement to brand owners may be interpreted as money laundering or layering.

Defense evidence: Treasury cash-flow forecast; signed pipeline of committed and pending royalty-financing disbursements; aged receivables ledger; financing agreement drawdown schedules; audited financial statements demonstrating matching liabilities and fund flows; a client money/escrow ledger if segregated account is used; and the bank's agreed transaction-monitoring baseline and periodic compliance validation reports.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed using SWIFT MT103 for account-to-account beneficiary wires into the Canadian corporate's CAD or USD account. The wire instruction must include the beneficiary's legal name exactly as registered, the Canadian bank's SWIFT/BIC, the beneficiary account number, the end-to-end transaction reference and a clear purpose-of-payment narrative such as 'Shareholder Loan – Royalty Financing Fund', 'Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Intercompany Working Capital Loan'. USD wires are cleared through Fedwire/CHIPS via the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; CAD wires are cleared through the Automated Clearing Settlement System. Compliance verification will include a source-of-funds package comprising the signed financing or subscription agreement, board resolutions, financial statements, and the beneficial-ownership register. If the in-bound capital constitutes acquisition of control of a Canadian business by a non-Canadian investor, an assessment under the Investment Canada Act must be made, with filing above relevant thresholds. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit statutory CAD threshold for Local File/Master File; however, CRA expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 (Form T106 filing threshold). Country-by-Country Report (Form RC5105) is required for groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a numbered ESR regime; instead, economic substance is enforced through CRA's general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA), the Quebec GAAR, and the principles of the OECD Model Commentary. For a royalty-financing fund, substance is measured by genuine board control, active strategic and operational management in Quebec, real office/banking functions, transfer-pricing documentation, and the actual exercise of IP and financing rights. Thin capitalization (s.18(4) and s.212(8) ITA) and CFC rules (s.91) prevent abusive debt-push-down and diverted income structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: s.239(1) ITA: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine up to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalties (s.163.2) add up to 50% of understatement or overstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate or acquiesce are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings under s.227.1 ITA, may be personally directed to pay restitution, and face travel bans and asset freezes pending criminal proceedings. Under Quebec law, the Civil Code permits veil-piercing.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s.247 ITA authorizes reassessment to arm's-length amounts, and a penalty of 10% of the downward income adjustment applies if the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts. Interest compounds on the reassessed liability. Quebec applies parallel reassessment under its own anti-avoidance rules.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable unless they knowingly certified false returns or directed the mispricing. In that event, personal penalties under s.235.1 and potential piercing of the corporate veil for fraud can attach.
Sham / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245 ITA) retroactively re-characterizes the transaction, with a 25% penalty on the resulting tax avoidance; Quebec GAAR mirrors the federal rule. The CRA may also assert a permanent establishment elsewhere and reallocate income.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders risk personal liability to creditors when the corporate structure is a mere sham. CRA can pierce the veil and hold shareholders jointly liable for tax debts, and Quebec's oppression remedy can strip limited-liability protection.
Part XIII Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: s.227(8) ITA makes the payer liable for the full unremitted amount; s.227(9) imposes a penalty of 10% (or 20% for intentional failure) and daily interest on the deficiency. Repeat failures may escalate to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding under s.227.1 ITA, regardless of intent, unless they can prove due diligence. This exposure is not dischargeable in bankruptcy and can trigger personal asset seizure.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Medium-Risk Financing Fund)

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation; criminal money-laundering offences under the Criminal Code carry imprisonment up to 10 years. Unregistered MSBs face cease-and-desist orders and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, or induced the offense are party to it, personally open to incarceration and fines. FINTRAC may also levy corporate penalties that directors must indemnify unless due diligence is proven.
