Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); GST/HST filing and registration portal: My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and CRA Program Accounts) via the CRA's Non-Resident Registration service; the partnership is assigned a nine-digit Business Number with an RT (GST/HST) program account; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold; supporting documents include the partnership agreement, proof of provincial registration in Ontario, and information on the Canadian representative if required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and CRA Program Accounts) via the CRA's Non-Resident Registration service; the partnership is assigned a nine-digit Business Number with an RT (GST/HST) program account; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold; supporting documents include the partnership agreement, proof of provincial registration in Ontario, and information on the Canadian representative if required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not use check-the-box classification; limited partnerships are generally fiscally transparent with certain Canadian and non-resident partners subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source income; available elections include the GST/HST quick method election for small businesses, annual filing election for non-resident registrants, and provincial attestation elections; hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act may deny deductions in cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies per calendar quarter or per four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers are also required to register if making taxable supplies in Canada in the ordinary course of business even below threshold unless small supplier rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada in accordance with Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, including goods exported within a prescribed timeframe, and to certain services rendered to non-residents where the services are performed outside Canada or are supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is for consumption outside Canada; voice streaming services delivered electronically to non-resident customers may be zero-rated if the supply is not made in Canada and satisfies section 142.1 deeming rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation showing settlement from non-resident customer
  • Contract or purchase order confirming the non-resident status and foreign destination of the supply

E-invoicing is systemically accepted but not mandatory at the federal level; no real-time tax clearance or fiscal stamping regime applies to GST/HST; invoices must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act; CRA requires electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with an authorized representative, and third-party data reporting applies to digital platform operators under the GST/HST framework for online marketplace supplies.

CRA may deny zero-rating on audit if documentary proof is incomplete; penalties apply under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act for false statements or omissions causing a misrepresentation, and section 285 may impose arrears interest; willful evasion may result in fines and prosecution; retrospective reassessments generally cover a four-year period, extendable to ten years in cases of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating, fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII; both current-account and capital-account transactions may be effectuated in any currency without prior approval, and no restriction exists on opening foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada. The only material remittance/leakage hurdle for the Ontario LP is tax-driven rather than FX-driven: distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (generally 25%, reduced by treaty to 5-15% where the partner qualifies), and the remitting bank applies standard reporting obligations but no convertibility or profit-remittance license requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None - Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion timeline, or repatriation quota for export receipts. Digital services proceeds received by the LP from Apple (Apple IAP) and Google (Google Play Billing) in USD, EUR, GBP and other settlement currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the LP's treasury discretion. Any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC within 24 hours, and the LP must recognise revenue at the Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rate for Canadian tax and IFRS purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; Canada has no centralized Foreign Exchange Administration because the FX regime is fully liberalized. Prudential regulation of banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT monitoring lies with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as primary treasury rail, MEDIUM as supplementary corridor. Legal constraints: an EEA/UK/HK-licensed EMI (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currenxie) is not a Schedule I or II bank and cannot lawfully hold Canadian 'deposits' for the LP without an OSFI authorization, and active solicitation of Canadian clients would trigger a FINTRAC Money Service Business registration requirement in Canada. Apple and Google developer settlement rules generally require a bank account in the LP's legal name, so using an EMI as the beneficiary account for app-store payouts is operationally fragile. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario general provincial rate 11.5%; a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay entity-level income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident; distributions from a Limited Partnership are not dividends; a non-resident partner is generally subject to Part I net tax on allocated Canadian-source income and, where the distribution includes interest, royalty, or other Part XIII-type payments, a 25% withholding component may arise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the last day of the fifth month after the partnership fiscal period-end, which for a calendar-year straddling period is normally May 31 following the calendar year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due two months after the taxation year-end for a non-CCPC, and six months after the year-end for a CCPC eligible for the federal small business deduction; Ontario tax calendar follows the federal filing date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit master/local-file threshold under Canadian law; however, Form T106 (Report on Non-Arm's-Length Transactions) must be filed when the aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year; CRA expects contemporaneous TP documentation, commonly aligned to OECD TP Guidelines, based on the size, complexity, and risk profile of the transactions.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime exists in Canada; rather, Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 of the Income Tax Act and substance-over-form judicial doctrines. A Canadian LP operating a voice-streaming app must demonstrate genuine operational substance — real employees, Canadian management decisions, actual premises, and economic ownership of IP — to prevent recharacterization or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to Report Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Criminal Code and Income Tax Act s.239: fine not less than C$10,000 and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and other statutory obligations; CRA can obtain travel bans, civil asset freezes, and director's liability assessments for failure to withhold.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment increasing taxable income or reducing losses; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward TP adjustment, subject to a C$5,000 minimum; interest accrues from the date the reassessment would have applied.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable to creditors for TP penalties if the LP is insolvent; corporate directors can be assessed for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax and other amounts if they knowingly allow a sham TP arrangement.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment that recharacterizes all Canadian-source revenue as taxable; denial of treaty benefits; possible application of s.163 gross-negligence penalties; CRA may also levy GST/HST on undeclared taxable supplies.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who execute or assent to a sham arrangement can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, penalties, and interest; travel bans and enforcement proceedings against personal assets may follow.
AML / Unlicensed Money-Services Offences

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for corporations; money-laundering offences under Criminal Code s.462.57 carry fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; unregistered money-services business may face cease-and-desist and criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors of the general partner are personally liable for AML failures under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; personal fines, prohibition orders, and travel bans can be imposed alongside criminal prosecution.
