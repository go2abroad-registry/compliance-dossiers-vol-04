Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — business registration and tax account portal: My Business Account (canada.ca/taxes/my-business-account) and Business Registration Online (BRO)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a taxable presence in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN), plus a GST/HST registration number (RT suffix), by applying through CRA's Business Registration Online, filing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), or submitting a written request to the Calgary Tax Services Office. Non-resident corporations must provide certificate of corporate status, registered office details, director and officer information, and a Canadian contact point. GST/HST registration is mandatory before a person makes a taxable supply in Canada unless eligible for the small-supplier exception. Ontario provincial accounts (e.g., WCB, employer health tax, corporate income tax notices) may be coordinated through the same CRA one-window registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (federal and provincial corporate income tax apply at the entity level; residents taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations taxed on Canadian-source income, with branch profits tax and Part XIII withholding implications)

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include the Income Tax Act section 216 election (net rental income from Canadian real property), section 219 branch tax election for non-resident insurers, and GST/HST election to simplify registration and reporting. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and Budget 2022 measures apply to deny inappropriate double deductions; no entity classification election is available for corporations. A non-resident may elect under subsection 115(1) or use treaty-based PE thresholds subject to tax rulings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide gross annual taxable revenues (excluding the sale of capital property and financial services); a non-resident with a permanent establishment or making an 'exploitative supply' in Canada may still have registration obligations where the small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate the supply of goods that are exported from Canada by the supplier or on its behalf within 60 days of delivery under Excise Tax Act section 21 or 22, provided proof of export is retained. Zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied to a non-resident (sections 9 and 23) where the service is performed exclusively outside Canada or relates to exported goods, and to transportation and logistics services ancillary to exported goods. The supplier must be a registrant, the recipient must be a non-resident, and the property or service must not be acquired for consumption in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency CCI or equivalent electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill / sea waybill) evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment showing consideration received from the non-resident recipient
  • Written contract or purchase order identifying the goods/services, destination, and parties
  • Proof of delivery to a common carrier and transportation documents
  • Commercial invoice and packing sl
  • Cargo control documents or carrier-generated proof of export (e.g., PARS / RPSP)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is permitted if reliable and retrievable; CRA accepts standard electronic invoices (e.g., XML, PDF) with no pre-clearance requirement. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) for most registrants, and certain large businesses and non-resident registrants must use electronic payment remittance. No specific Canadian e-invoice clearance API is mandatory.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within statutory four-year assessment periods (or without limitation where misrepresentation or fraud exists), clawing back input tax credits and converting zero-rated export status to taxable supplies if documentary proof is insufficient. False or misleading statements attract penalties under Excise Tax Act subsection 280(1): the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax claimed, plus administrative interest. Gross negligence penalties can apply under subsection 285.1 and Income Tax Act criminal prosecution may be pursued for wilful evasion. Exporters engaging in sham 'NRST' bills or recycling documentation face retrospective disqualification and seizure of credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD enjoys fully free convertibility on both current and capital account. Canada imposes no exchange controls on capital inflows, dividend remittances, interest payments, or management fees, and does not require prior central-bank licensing of cross-border transfers. The capital account's only binding constraints for a consulting corporation are the Part XIII withholding tax regime (25% standard, reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-US Income Tax Treaty and applicable Canada-EU treaty instruments), the Investment Canada Act notification regime (not triggered at this entity's size), and Customer Due Diligence/beneficial-ownership verification (Ontario ISC Register of Individuals with Significant Control) administered by the chartered bank as the regulated reporting entity. Profit repatriation is therefore a tax-and-verification exercise, not a convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: not applicable. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no period within which export/service receipts must be repatriated or sold. Service receivables may be accumulated and retained indefinitely in USD/EUR multi-currency accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The only binding statutory reporting duties are: (i) FINTRAC cross-border funds-transfer reports (EWR/EFT) filed by the financial institution for any single transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (the corporate client is not required to self-file but must be prepared to disclose the underlying purpose code and supporting contract); (ii) Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations (CAD 10,000 threshold) if physical cash or bearer instruments are carried into or out of Canada; and (iii) Statistics Canada International Transactions in Services survey / Balance of Payments reporting for annual cross-border service receipts exceeding CAD 2 million (applicable only if consulting revenue materially exceeds this de minimis threshold).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) as the monetary authority issuing the Canadian dollar (CAD). Exchange-control and cross-border cash oversight is shared with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its regulations; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions resides with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (supplementary rail only; not a replacement for the Schedule I primary account). Legal constraints: A Canadian business corporation may lawfully receive client collections via a digital EMI if (a) the EMI is registered with FINTRAC as a money-services business in Canada (e.g., Wise Canada) or, if unregistered, onboarding would fail FINTRAC's risk-based approach; (b) the EMI's sponsor bank is a recognized Canadian/US/EU bank so that CAD settlement occurs through the ACSS/Lynx clearing rails; (c) the corporate entity files Form T1135 for any offshore wallet balance exceeding CAD 100,000 and complies with FATCA/CRS attribution; and (d) revenue recognition under IFRS is unaffected. An offshore-only EMI domiciled in a non-treaty or grey-listed jurisdiction is not permitted as the primary settlement account for CAD-denominated client payments because such routing would flunk FINTRAC's terrorism-financing red-flag guidance and the bank's own source-of-funds validation. Recommend pairing a Wise Business or Payoneer USD/EUR collection wallet with daily sweeping to the Tier-1 CAD operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended protocol: (i) initiation via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the non-resident sponsor's bank; (ii) beneficiary account at a Canadian Schedule I bank in CAD, USD or EUR; (iii) purpose-code (SWIFT field 70) drafting per Canadian banking practice: 'SHARE/LOAN/SUB' for shareholder loan, 'SHARE/EQUITY/INJ' for share-subscription paid-in capital, 'SHARE/ADVANCE' for round-trip funding. Shareholder loans must be evidenced by a written demand-loan agreement with a five-year term and CRA-prescribed interest rate to avoid a deemed shareholder benefit under ITA subsection 15(2); Part XIII withholding applies on interest and Form T106 must be filed. Equity injections must be supported by a corporate minute book resolution, issuance filings with Corporations Canada/Ontario Registry, and updated ISC significant-control records. Statistics Canada Balance of Payments investor-position reporting applies only when direct investment from the non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million; below that threshold no BOP filing is required. FINTRAC EWR/EFT reporting is automatically triggered at CAD 10,000 per transfer, so the CA_CORP must maintain the supporting loan/equity file for five years after funds clear. Same-day CAD settlement is via Lynx/LVTS for wires above CAD 1 million or urgent transfers; ACSS retail batch for smaller amounts.For consulting-marketplace disbursements (US/EU subcontractors, FDA user-fees, EU Notified Body fees), the clearing-rail set is: intra-Canada CAD settlement via ACSS (retail batch) or Lynx/LVTS for urgent same-day wires; USD settlement via Fedwire/CHIPS through the Canadian bank's US correspondent branch (e.g., BMO Harris or RBC's US entity); EUR disbursements via SEPA Instant / SEPA Credit Transfer through the bank's EU correspondent with SWIFT MT103 receipts. For documentary-trade scenarios (capitalized laboratory equipment for clinical evidence), an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued as SWIFT MT700 with a 30-90-day deferred-payment term and a CAD/USD discount line at an OSFI-regulated bank would be the prescribed rail, although it is not required for the core consulting workflow. Credit-card settlement is integrated via Stripe/Adyen and reconciled daily to the same treasury ledger. Recommended treasury stack: primary CAD operational account at a Tier-1 bank, US collection wallet via Wise Business, EUR collection via SEPA, and daily zero-balance sweeping of all wallets into the primary CAD account.Outright FX Forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) against firm pipeline invoices; non-deliverable variants available via OSFI-regulated dealers; ISDA CSA margining not required under the commercial end-user exemption when gross notional is below the CAD 3-billion clearing threshold.Zero-cost collars and participating forwards for locked, invoice-based revenue cash flows.FX Swaps and FX swaptions used to manage cross-currency working-capital peaks (bank counterparty only).Currency basis swap (EUR/USD) at notional of USD 1 million or above, capturable in a multi-currency account at the Canadian bank; permitted as prudent hedging for EUR receivables.Exotic NDFs (e.g., TRY, ZAR, USD crosses) executed strictly on the bank's off-balance-sheet book, non-speculative, hard-linked to contractual receivables with tenor capped at 12 months.Interest-rate swaps and FRAs are NOT permissible without an approved derivatives policy and an executed ISDA 2002 Master Agreement at the entity level.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (supplementary rail only; not a replacement for the Schedule I primary account). Legal constraints: A Canadian business corporation may lawfully receive client collections via a digital EMI if (a) the EMI is registered with FINTRAC as a money-services business in Canada (e.g., Wise Canada) or, if unregistered, onboarding would fail FINTRAC's risk-based approach; (b) the EMI's sponsor bank is a recognized Canadian/US/EU bank so that CAD settlement occurs through the ACSS/Lynx clearing rails; (c) the corporate entity files Form T1135 for any offshore wallet balance exceeding CAD 100,000 and complies with FATCA/CRS attribution; and (d) revenue recognition under IFRS is unaffected. An offshore-only EMI domiciled in a non-treaty or grey-listed jurisdiction is not permitted as the primary settlement account for CAD-denominated client payments because such routing would flunk FINTRAC's terrorism-financing red-flag guidance and the bank's own source-of-funds validation. Recommend pairing a Wise Business or Payoneer USD/EUR collection wallet with daily sweeping to the Tier-1 CAD operational account.
