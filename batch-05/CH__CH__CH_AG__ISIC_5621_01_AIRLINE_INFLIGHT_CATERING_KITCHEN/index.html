Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC), via the official web portal www.estv.admin.ch and the e-service portal www.estv.admin.ch/ev-vat. Cantonal tax authorities remain competent for direct cantonal and communal income taxes, with corporate registration coordinated through the Central Business Register (Zefix/UIDW).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or owning a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft must first obtain the company's UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / Business Identification Number) from the Swiss Central Business Register (Zefix/UIDW) upon company incorporation or branch registration. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a separate VAT registration application with the ESTV using Form MWST/Formulaire TVA (available online through e-Services), indicating intention to pursue taxable activities in Switzerland, unless the annual worldwide turnover is below the statutory CHF 100,000 threshold; in that case registration is voluntary or mandatory in limited enumerated cases. The ESTV will issue a permanent VAT number (MWST-Nr./TVA-Nr.) that is distinct from the UID. Foreign entities without a Swiss registered office or permanent establishment can register for VAT only when the tax liability derives from Swiss supplies exceeding the threshold and are then appointed a Swiss VAT representative or, in certain cross-border cases, use the simplified KGA (Kompensationsgruppe/Zahlungsempfänger) procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide for a U.S.-style check-the-box classification regime. All capital companies such as the AG are per se entity-level taxable persons for Swiss corporate income tax, wealth tax, and VAT. The AG may elect: (i) VAT group membership under Article 12a of the Swiss VAT Act for closely linked persons, allowing consolidated VAT liability; (ii) use of the qualified participation deduction (Beteiligungsabzug) for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income; (iii) the Swiss notional interest deduction for excess equity only where relevant under cantonal special regimes; (iv) group income-tax allocation under cantonal tax laws (Steuerrelevante Gruppenbesteuerung) in limited, non-statutory administrative practice; and (v) disclosure-based accounting elections for federal and cantonal tax depreciation. Hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted under Swiss substantive tax law; Switzerland has adopted the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules through controlled foreign company legislation and the 2022 federal direct tax reforms, but an AG may not elect its tax character as pass-through or entity-level beyond the default statutory classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding exempt income and certain arts/charities); a reduced threshold of CHF 10,000 applies to supplies from foreign of the use of rights on Swiss immovable property or for certain import-related services; the general threshold under Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act is CHF 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 23 of the Swiss VAT Act, a supply of goods or services is zero-rated if the supplier proves that the goods have been delivered outside Switzerland or that the services are performed outside Swiss territory; specific rules apply to aircraft ground handling and inflight catering: supplies of meals, beverages, and inflight equipment delivered directly into an aircraft for remuneration are deemed supplies of goods eligible for zero-rating only when the aircraft is operated by an international airline on a flight abroad, and the operator provides an export proof or an international-flight certificate. Zero-rating also applies to value-added services to movable tangible property in Switzerland that is then exported, but inflight catering consumed in international air transport is generally characterized as a supply of goods and may be zero-rated with the valid export certificate, including the airline’s end-consumption outside the customs territory. Domestic flights remain taxable at the ordinary or reduced rate. The taxpayer must retain evidence of physical export and of the transport contract, typically an International Air Transport Association (IATA) cargo or catering delivery receipt.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (via e-dec/e-dec web) validated by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS)
  • Proof of Export for VAT purposes (Form 177.070 / Export-proof certificate or equivalent customs-validated electronic export record)
  • Airline Catering Delivery Receipt / Handling Agent Certificate confirming delivery into the international aircraft
  • International Air Transport Association (IATA) structured airway bill or airline contract reference with flight-schedule evidence showing an international destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (blue form or bank SWIFT/MT103 record) demonstrating the export consideration was credited to a Swiss bank account

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping exists under Swiss VAT law. Swiss invoices must comply with the formal requirements of Art. 26 VAT Act (name and full address of supplier, VAT ID, content type, date, amount). Since 2020, the standardized QR-bill replaces legacy payment slips for bank payments in domestic B2B/B2C where requested, but it does not require e-procurement clearance. Swiss tax authorities require e-audit capable data export in Swiss eCH-01199 or DTA XML format for taxpayers subject to electronic recordkeeping; no government API clearance is imposed before invoice issuance. Airline inflight catering operators dealing with Swiss airports may be contractually required to use CASS (Cargo Account Settlement System) and IATA-compliant electronic data interchange, but this is commercial/aviation regulatory practice rather than a statutory tax clearance mandate.

Instances of unsupported zero-rating are treated as tax evasion or attempted tax evasion under Articles 69 and 70 of the Swiss VAT Act. If an export zero-rating is disallowed, the supplier is retroactively liable for the previously omitted VAT, plus (i) default interest at the statutory rate of 4.5% per annum from the due date until payment; (ii) a procedural fine of up to CHF 250,000 for intentional evasion; (iii) an additional administrative penalty of up to three times the evaded tax in cases of fraud under cantonal or federal criminal law; and (iv) prosecution under the Swiss Criminal Code, including imprisonment in severe cases. Where the inflight catering supplier cannot prove physical export within the 180-day repatriation and documentary window, the VAT authority may reverse credited input VAT and impose an infrastructure-determined increase of tax of up to 2.5% of the tax amount for unjustified refund. Swiss customs audit scrutiny is heightened for bonded airport operators: retrospective clawbacks are not time-limited if documents are forged or where the exporter assists in or realizes an unjustified VAT refund, with statutory limitation periods of 10 years for fraud and 5 years for simple negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Swiss franc floats freely and is fully transferable across borders. Profit remittance in the form of dividends, branch profits, royalties, and interest is unrestricted and not subject to prior exchange-control approval. Practical frictions arise solely from anti-money-laundering onboarding obligations and bank account documentation requirements rather than statutory conversion restrictions. Invoices, contracts, and intercompany transactions may be denominated in CHF, EUR, USD, or any agreed currency without SNB consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandated surrender or conversion of export proceeds is applicable in Switzerland. Swiss exporters, including airport in-flight caterers, may retain and deploy foreign-currency receipts freely. There are no statutory timelines, no minimum conversion percentages, and no central-bank purchase obligations. Export proceeds may be settled through normal banking channels, credited to a Swiss foreign-currency account, or converted to CHF at a commercially negotiated rate with no government intervention.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF) within the Federal Department of Finance; no separate foreign exchange administration authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMI banking in Switzerland. Cross-border digital EMI accounts not authorized under the Swiss Banking Act generally cannot take deposits or provide payment accounts. A Swiss FinTech license under Article 1b of the Banking Act permits limited deposit-taking (up to CHF 1 million, non-interest-bearing, no use for cash settlement) but does not grant the full corporate treasury, FX, or credit facilities required for an airport in-flight catering kitchen handling USD/EUR aviation payrolls and trade flows. An offshore EMI may provide ancillary payment services only if acting through a properly licensed Swiss agent or if the client relationship is structured outside Swiss banking territory; even then, FATCA, Swiss-QI, and FINMA outsourcing rules render the arrangement practically unattractive. A full Swiss banking license or a partnership with a tier-1 Swiss bank remains the only robust compliance structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound CHF capital injections must be executed via the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system using EUR-denominated SEPA corridors or SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP transfers. The reimbursement must reference the statutory capital-contribution purpose in the payment memo: 'Einlage / Apport en capital / Conferimento di capitale', accompanied by a shareholder meeting resolution, capital increase notarization, confirmation from the commercial register after publication in the Swiss Official Gazette, and a bank-side KYC completion certificate. Inbound foreign-currency funds should be denominated in EUR or USD; it is advisable to route through the paying shareholder's own fully identified bank account to preserve a traceable source of funds and avoid third-party-payment red flags. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax 8.5% levied on after-tax profit (pre-tax equivalent 7.83%); combined cantonal and communal component ranges from approx. 6.0% to 12.0% points; aggregate combined headline in Canton Zug approx. 14.6%, Canton Zurich approx. 19.7%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer), payable by the distributing Swiss AG on dividends and other distributions on share capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the close of the commercial fiscal year (e.g., 30 June of the following year for a calendar-year AG); the cantonal tax administration may grant extensions upon reasoned written request before expiry; federal and cantonal assessments are issued separately but always from the same filed return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The distributing AG must file the return and pay the 35% Swiss WHT within 30 days after the resolution to distribute dividends (or other taxable distributions); the annual recap statement follows by 28 February of the subsequent calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and CbC Report: required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated gross revenue reaches CHF 900 million or foreign equivalent, active from the first day of the fiscal year following the threshold. Local File: mandatory for a Swiss AG when intra-group transactions (sales, purchases, services, intellectual property, guarantees, financing) exceed CHF 1 million in the fiscal year; for group loans/guarantees the threshold is CHF 1 million aggregate principal; the FTA's TP Guidance (2021) also allows the cantonal tax administration to demand full Local File documentation regardless of the threshold in audit circumstances. The filing is in German/French/Italian or English upon request.

Economic substance scope: Swiss law does not impose a discrete 'Economic Substance Regulations (ESR)' statute of the type applied in UAE/offshore hubs; however, the following substance requirements bind the CH AG: (1) the MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) applied within Swiss DTAs — treaty benefits are refused if one of the main purposes is to obtain DTA relief, absent genuine business purpose and substance; (2) the Swiss anti-abuse doctrine (Steuerumgehung) which re-characterizes letterbox transactions; (3) the federal and cantonal tax administrations demand for the inflight catering AG: permanent airport leasehold or bonded kitchen premises, active board meetings in Switzerland, production equipment, licensed personnel, and actual principal risk-taking for food safety and aviation compliance; absence of these features can void holding/participation relief and NID retroactively.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Swiss Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the evaded federal and cantonal tax plus arrears interest of 3.5% p.a. (federal); administrative fine up to 3x the evaded federal tax (Art. 175 DBG); cantonal surcharges mirror the federal fine; the offense is a misdemeanor and the company itself is penalized.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and authorized signatories are co-addressed as persons conducting the company's tax affairs; fines may be imposed directly on them; under Swiss Code of Obligations (CO Art. 754) they are civilly liable for restitution of damages incurred by the company or the tax authority; directors may face a travel ban and withholding of passports where examination of assets or cross-border escape is feared.
Swiss Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 186 DBG) or fine; confiscation of the evaded tax amount and assets; simultaneous administrative re-assessment of the full tax difference plus 3.5% interest and late payment surcharges; adversarial civil courts cannot override the criminal orders.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment applies directly to the responsible CEO/CFO, not the legal entity; personal fine up to CHF 30,000 in addition to the company penalty; the corporate veil is pierced for restitution (CO Art. 754); Swiss criminal courts routinely order pre-trial detention and travel bans for non-resident directors to secure the tax claim.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under the PPT: withholding tax must be re-collected at 35% on all dividends; participation relief and NID are retroactively removed; discretionary assessment with 10-year limitation period in case of intent; administrative penalty up to 3x evaded tax and publication of the sanction decision.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintained the letterbox structure are personally liable for the re-assessed tax when the company lacks assets (CO Art. 754); the FTA may order them to provide security for the tax claim; criminal proceedings for private-sector corruption (SCC 322 note) in the aviation channel can lead to imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: FTA/cantonal tax authority determines the taxable profit by discretionary estimation, adds the undeclared profit, charges 3.5% p.a. interest, and imposes a penalty of up to 3x the additional tax if negligence or intent is shown; re-characterization may convert payments into hidden profit distributions subject to 35% WHT; TP documentation orders carry procedural fines up to CHF 10,000 per missing document.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for selecting and documenting the arm's length method (CO Art. 754); the civil burden shifts against the director if documentation is absent; in egregious cases the Swiss criminal courts convict the director of tax fraud for intentionally manipulating TP records; travel bans and exit denial apply to secure the penal process.
AML / Banking Compliance Breach in an Aviation-Bonded Environment

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG Art. 39): fine up to CHF 500,000 for intentional violation and up to CHF 150,000 for negligence; under the Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years for aggravating cases); in addition, the involved funds are subject to forfeiture; the airport concession license and food safety certificate may be revoked by the airport operator and BAZG (Federal Office for Customs and Border Security).

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and board members are individually liable for failure to establish adequate CDD/EDD procedures; personal fine and imprisonment are imposed directly on natural persons; the corporate veil does not shield directors in criminal AML proceedings; travel bans and pre-trial detention are standard tools in any Swiss felonious proceeding of this kind.
