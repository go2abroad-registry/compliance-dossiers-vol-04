Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a CA_CORP under federal/provincial corporate law is taxed as a separate person under ITA s. 142(1); resident corporation pays Part I tax on worldwide income. Territorial scope attaches only to non-resident corporations (Canadian-source income under Part I plus branch tax under ITA s. 219). No check-the-box/partnership pass-through election exists; Canada has no general tax-exempt zone and no S-corporation analogue.

Available election forms

Income Tax Act elections: s. 85 (rollover of property on incorporation), s. 83 (capital dividend), s. 88 (wind-up rollover), s. 216 (non-resident rental income), s. 219 and s. 219.1 (branch-tax relief), s. 18.6 (EIFEL group-ratio election for interest limitation), s. 245/247 (GAAR and transfer-pricing contemporaneous documentation). GST/HST statutory elections under ETA: s. 150 (designate branches), s. 156 (non-application to internal asset transfers), s. 211 (closely related corporations), s. 272 (co-ventures and agent-principal supplies), s. 273 (listed financial institution attribution). No check-the-box for hybrid entities, but hybrid-mismatch anti-avoidance under ITA s. 18.6/18.2 disallows double-dip interest; foreign-trust transparency election under ITA s. 94.3 (limited to designated foreign trusts).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold: registration becomes mandatory at the end of a calendar quarter once taxable (including zero-rated, but excluding exempt) supplies in Canada exceed C$30,000 over the current and preceding four quarters. Financial-service fees from trust administration are excluded for the threshold. A non-resident with a Canadian permanent establishment or supply of real property in Canada is registrable even below the threshold; voluntary registration is available under ETA s. 240(4) for persons engaged in commercial activity, with security at the Minister's discretion.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 zero-rates a supply of tangible personal property exported by the supplier (or non-resident recipient/taxpayer's agent) to a destination outside Canada, requiring proof that export occurs within a reasonable time and that the goods are not for use in Canada. Services: Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident if performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident not in-canada at the time, provided the service does not relate to Canadian real property/goods and is NOT a financial service. Critical carve-out for this scope: fiduciary administration of an irrevocable offshore spendthrift trust is a financial service, so trust-management fees to non-resident settlors/beneficiaries are exempt (no GST/HST, no ITC entitlement), not zero-rated; Schedule VII provides only a narrow zero-rating window for specific financial services supplied by financial institutions to non-residents. The corporate trustee may zero-rate only ancillary bookkeeping/corporate-services fees that are genuinely separate from the financial-service mandate, and must apportion ITCs (ETA ss. 141.04, 141.05) between exempt and zero-rated activities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD (ISO 4217: CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no prior-authorization requirement, no exchange-control licensing, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or trust income payout to non-residents. Repatriation of investment capital, loan repayments, and fiduciary distributions may be executed freely, subject to (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act (Canada) withholding for Canadian-source amounts remitted to non-residents (default 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US treaty), and (ii) FINTRAC large-transfer reporting for wire and cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more. Canada satisfies OECD Code of Liberalisation obligations; the IMF AREAER reports no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions or on capital transactions, other than routine statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists under Canadian law. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no deadline for repatriation, and no requirement to convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. Exporters and fiduciaries may retain proceeds in any convertible currency. If such proceeds are held at an OSFI-regulated financial institution in Canada, the institution must apply standard EDD procedures and report suspicious or large transactions (threshold CAD 10,000) to FINTRAC. No exchange-rate settlement or forced sale regime applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions and FX risk is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision and cross-border transaction reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration; the Canadian dollar operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary fiduciary deposit functions. Canadian fintech platforms (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho) are not authorized to accept trust deposits as Schedule I banks and cannot provide true escrow/trust-clearing services. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Airwallex, Intergiro) are legally constrained because holding client trust money on deposit constitutes 'deposit-taking' under the Bank Act (Canada) and the Ontario Loan and Trust Corporations Act, which requires either a Schedule I bank licence, a federal/ provincial trust company licence, or an explicit statutory exemption. EMIs may be used solely as auxiliary disbursement or expense-payment rails, not as the primary liquidity clearing layer. Additionally, the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada) introduces mandatory registration and settlement-fund safeguarding rules applicable to payment service providers, reinforcing that EMIs cannot provide fiduciary-grade custody of discretionary trust assets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 for international wires or via correspondent-bank internal transfers for CAD/USD. Canada does not enforce statutory purpose codes; however, the bank's wire instructions must clearly state the economic purpose (e.g., 'Trust capital contribution — discretionary trust settlement') and the beneficiary account must be either the Trust Fiduciary Settlement Account or the Capital Escrow Account. Settlement of CAD wires occurs through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system (successor to the former LVTS); USD wires settle through U.S. correspondent rails (Fedwire/CHIPS). For any inbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, the bank must report to FINTRAC, and the client must submit: (i) signed trust resolution authorizing receipt, (ii) source-of-funds evidence matching the amount, (iii) beneficiary/trust ownership confirmation, and (iv) a completed bank-specific incoming-funds questionnaire. There is no exchange-control approval step; only standard commercial KYC/EDD verification applies.Outbound trade settlements and fiduciary distributions are executed via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires, or via Payments Canada ACSS/ACH for domestic disbursements. For commercial trade payables, irrevocable documentary credits (Commercial Letters of Credit, UCP 600) can be issued by Schedule I banks, with CAD funds cleared through Lynx and USD funds cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent Fedwire/CHIPS account. Large beneficiary distributions should be pre-notified to the bank using Form T3-related supporting schedules (trust distribution schedule) and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding documentation for non-resident payees. The trust's operating account must maintain sufficient intraday liquidity in Lynx to avoid settlement failure; prudent practice is to stagger large distributions over value dates and confirm the receiving bank's ABA/SWIFT routing in advance.Deliverable FX forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) executed under the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions with a licensed Schedule I or foreign bank counterparty, subject to OSFI margin and collateral requirements.Vanilla FX call/put options (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) purchased for hedging trust-distribution currency exposure, executed under ISDA with CRD-approved counterparties.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) transacted under an ISDA Master Agreement with a jurisdiction-qualifying Ontario credit-support annex (CSA), used to match trust asset and liability currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) only for ring-fenced cross-currency legs involving a non-convertible or restricted currency (e.g., CAD/CNH or CAD/INR); CAD itself is fully deliverable and a CAD-based NDF would carry increased pricing and legal-enforceability risk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary fiduciary deposit functions. Canadian fintech platforms (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho) are not authorized to accept trust deposits as Schedule I banks and cannot provide true escrow/trust-clearing services. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Airwallex, Intergiro) are legally constrained because holding client trust money on deposit constitutes 'deposit-taking' under the Bank Act (Canada) and the Ontario Loan and Trust Corporations Act, which requires either a Schedule I bank licence, a federal/ provincial trust company licence, or an explicit statutory exemption. EMIs may be used solely as auxiliary disbursement or expense-payment rails, not as the primary liquidity clearing layer. Additionally, the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada) introduces mandatory registration and settlement-fund safeguarding rules applicable to payment service providers, reinforcing that EMIs cannot provide fiduciary-grade custody of discretionary trust assets.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and anti-treaty-shopping provisions require economic substance for transactions to be respected. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; imprisonment possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; CRA may reassess and impose gross negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML violations; travel bans and seizure of personal assets; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML failures if they were aware or negligent; travel bans and personal fines; potential imprisonment for up to 5 years.
