E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first register as an extra-provincial company with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a NEQ, or use a federal incorporation with provincial registration. Subsequently, apply online or via Form RC1 to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN). Upon the BN being issued, the entity must open the applicable CRA program accounts, including the GST/HST account (RM prefix) and corporate income tax account (RC prefix). For Quebec sales tax, the registrant must use Revenu Québec's online registration service to obtain a QST account number (LM prefix). Non-resident corporations without Canadian incorporation must file Form RC4696 (Non-Resident Registration) and may need to request a lease-based Quebec establishment determination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian or Quebec domestic law. A Canadian business corporation is always taxed at the entity level and may not be treated as a pass-through. Relevant elections include: (1) joint election under subsection 156(1) ETA for closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate supplies as made for nil consideration; (2) the section 158.1 ETA election for exempt supplies between closely related corporations; (3) corresponding QST elections under the Quebec Tax Administration Act and the Excise Tax Act (Québec) rules; (4) for hybrid mismatch planning, the entity's classification under foreign law (e.g., IRC 7701) may be examined, but this does not create a Canadian domestic pass-through status; (5) an election may be made to apportion federal and Quebec income under provincial allocation rules where a permanent establishment exists in Quebec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (for GST) over the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; QST follows the same threshold for supplies made in Quebec. Cross-border digital services suppliers may be required to register under the simplified non-resident registration without threshold in certain circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part III of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the exporter keeps satisfactory evidence of physical export from Canada and the supply is made to a non-resident recipient for removal. Services (including digital streaming or video-matching services) supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI, where the service is not performed in Canada or is performed in Canada but delivered to a non-resident who is not in Canada, and the supply is not a supply of real estate, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or certain specified services (e.g., services relating to Canadian real property or personal property). For a video-chat social app, zero-rating applies if the place of supply is outside Canada and the non-resident recipient receives the service or in respect of a business that is carried on exclusively outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) filed through the Canada Border Services Agency's Canadian Export Information System (CAED) / CARM portal, including the export permit number and harmonized system code
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the physical departure and destination of goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank settlement/payment evidence demonstrating the receipt of foreign consideration for the exported goods or services

Canada and Quebec do not mandate real-time government-side clearance or digital stamping of standard B2B invoices. Registrants must retain and issue invoices containing prescribed data under the Excise Tax Act (CTA) and Quebec's Tax Administration Act, including supplier's registration number, amount of consideration, and tax amount, unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or the Related Affairs (DSAB) system; QST returns are filed through Revenu Québec TransID. For non-resident suppliers of digital services, CRA and Revenu Québec provide a simplified non-resident registration and filing gateway; there is no API-based invoice clearance approval mechanism.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated treatment if the registered exporter or service supplier fails to produce sufficient proof of export within the reassessment period, which is four years after a reporting period unless misrepresentation or fraud extends it to 10 years. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec's Tax Administration Act include false statement penalties of up to 25% of the tax sought to be avoided, interest on unpaid tax, and administrative monetary penalties for failure to retain records. Wilful evasion carries criminal liability, fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment. Clawbacks are retroactive, and the taxpayer must pay both the tax and penalties, with no statutory grace period for late-filed export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts and Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control authorization is required for profit remittance, intercompany loans, or user-payout settlement. Controlling acquisitions by non-Canadian investors are screened under the Investment Canada Act. Outbound dividends are exposed to Part XIII federal withholding tax (25% statutory; treaty-reduced to 5% for a qualified 10% shareholder under the Canada-United States Convention) plus Quebec's complementary levy on dividends paid to non-residents by a corporation domiciled in Quebec.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not operate an export-proceeds surrender regime. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to foreign-currency receipts generated by software, telephony, or data-processing services. Repository banks must file FINTRAC Cross-Border Wire Transfer Reports (CBR) for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but the treasury retains full discretion over currency denomination, reinvestment, and conversion timing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign-exchange control powers are statutorily allocated to the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with OSFI (prudential supervision of banks) and FINTRAC (reporting supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act) completing the regulatory perimeter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury anchor; permissible only as a payout-aggregation overlay. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., a UK or EU-authorised electronic-money institution) must be registered with FINTRAC as a foreign money services business before dealing in Canada and, after September 8, 2025, must hold a Bank of Canada payment-service-provider registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act if it executes payment functions in Canada. The EMI is not an OSFI-regulated deposit-taker and balances are not CDIC-insured. Consequently, the Schedule I bank account remains the depository and tax-basis ledger, while the EMI can reconcile App Store net settlements and fractional payout wire legs.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); Canadian-controlled private corporations on active business income may benefit from a reduced combined rate of 12.2% through the Small Business Deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Federal Part XIII statutory rate 25%; Quebec additional statutory withholding on dividends paid to non-residents 12%; combined statutory 37%. Tax treaties may reduce the combined rate (e.g., Canada–US: 15% general, 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and contemporaneous Local File documentation are mandatory where aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1 million; a Master File is required for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue of CAD 760 million or more (Country-by-Country Reporting threshold).

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations in Canada/Quebec; however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA), business purpose test, and beneficial ownership analysis; a Quebec video-matching corporation must maintain real office premises, local board decisions, and operational substance to avoid treaty shopping denial or sham recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Section 239 ITA criminal sanctions: fine up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s. 163(2); Quebec criminal and civil penalties under Taxation Act ss. 1020–1029.1.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as parties to the offence, subject to personal fines, restitution orders, and imprisonment; CRA may issue third-party demand and enforce director liability under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions, plus travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) ITA penalty: 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000); CRA may reassess outside the normal reassessment window; outstanding tax and interest remain collectable.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not liable for the TP penalty unless they cause or participate in a false entry; however, they may be pursued for unpaid corporate tax under CRA civil collection if the corporation is dissolved or made insolvent.
Shell Company and Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: CRA will invoke GAAR to deny treaty benefits and re-characterise transactions; reassess tax, interest, and penalties; evidence of willfulness triggers tax evasion prosecution and fines of up to 200% of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Fiscally, courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a sham; directors face civil restitution, asset seizures, forced compliance travel bans, and imprisonment under s. 239 ITA on criminal conviction.
AML / FINTRAC Violations in Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for entities (or CAD 500,000 for individuals); unregistered MSB fines of up to CAD 500,000 and/or six months' imprisonment on summary conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be held personally liable for directing or acquiescing in MSB non-compliance; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment, prohibition from managing corporations, and cross-border travel restrictions under enforcement orders.
