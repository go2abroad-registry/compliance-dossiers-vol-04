Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Quebec business corporation is a Canadian-resident taxpayer subject to full worldwide taxation under ITA Part I (T2) and the Quebec Taxation Act Part I (CO-17). It is not a pass-through entity. Because ultimate shareholders are non-residents, the corporation is a non-Canadian-controlled private corporation (non-CCPC) and cannot access the Small Business Deduction. Dividends paid to foreign shareholders are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced to 5-15%). The shares are not taxable Canadian property because the business is an operating service exporter, so no section 116 clearance is required on a future share disposition.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections for this exporter include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for non-calendar GST/QST filing periods; (ii) ETA s. 22/23 closely related corporation election to deem intra-group supplies made for no consideration, simplifying cross-charges; (iii) ETA s. 25 joint venture election if an unincorporated offshore project is structured; (iv) ETA s. 179 reverse-charge election, generally inapplicable to a zero-rated exporter but relevant if the corporation ever imports Canadian-taxable services; (v) foreign affiliate elections under ITA ss. 93(1), 113(1) and Regulation 5902 if an offshore delivery subsidiary is inserted; (vi) hybrid mismatch measures under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.6 - the corporation may file the s. 18.4(1) election for a qualifying foreign branch to align taxation of certain branch income; (vii) the default combined GST/QST return election with Revenu Québec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (including zero-rated export revenues) in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory registration for GST and QST under ETA s. 148 and QST Act s. 210; voluntary registration is advisable before reaching this threshold to recover input tax credits on startup costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of custom BIM/CAD engineering drafting services to a non-resident client qualifies for 0% GST/QST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 and the equivalent QST Act provision where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of supply; (ii) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST/QST; (iii) the service is supplied for use exclusively outside Canada and is not performed in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iv) the BIM deliverables are transmitted electronically offshore. The contract must characterize the delivery as custom engineering services rather than a licence of standard software, because a pure software licence would remain taxable. Contemporaneous non-resident status evidence must be obtained and retained; absent such evidence, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at the full 14.975% rate and the supplier becomes liable for uncollected tax.

  • Executed export service agreement expressly identifying the non-resident client, the BIM deliverable scope, the offshore transmission method (portal, FTP, email), and a governing clause confirming performance outside Canada and no connection to Canadian real or movable property
  • Commercial invoice or XML/PDF e-invoice stating '0% GST/QST - export zero-rated supply under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 / QST Act s. 52.0.1'
  • Non-resident status evidence: foreign trade register extract, foreign VAT/GST/TIN certificate, or a statutory declaration from the client confirming non-residency and absence of Canadian GST/HST registration
  • Digital delivery proof: file transfer logs, project-server access logs, IP origin logs, delivery tickets, or signed project handover certificates showing offshore receipt and use
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or Wise business statement demonstrating receipt of foreign currency settlement from the non-resident client
  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) - required only for incidental physical media or hardware prototypes exported; not required for pure digital CAD/BIM service exports

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no tax-authority API approval for invoices in Canada or Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices (XML, PDF, EDI, and increasingly PEPPOL) provided they contain the prescribed disclosure data. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of GST/QST returns and specified information slips, but not per-invoice pre-clearance. All invoices, credit notes, and export evidence must be retained for six years; each invoice must state: '0% GST/QST - exported service zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 / QST Act s. 52.0.1'.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the standard four-year window (extended to ten years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, and unlimited for fraud). If zero-rating is denied, the exporter is assessed for uncollected GST/QST at the combined 14.975% rate plus compounded daily interest, and any input tax credits or refunds previously paid are clawed back. Specific penalties include: (i) failure to register or collect - ETA s. 280, up to CAD 10,000 plus 6% annual interest; (ii) false statements or gross negligence - ETA s. 285, penalty from 50% to 100% of the understated tax, fines up to CAD 50,000 and/or up to two years imprisonment on summary conviction; (iii) parallel penalties under ITA ss. 162/163 and the Quebec Taxation Act, with referral to the CRA Criminal Investigations Program and s. 380 Criminal Code fraud charges carrying up to 14 years imprisonment for deliberately fraudulent export invoicing; (iv) transfer-pricing reassessment under ITA s. 247(3) if export prices deviate from the arm's length range, with associated penalties. The exporter must produce the complete documentation bundle within 30 days of a written audit demand; failure to produce contemporaneous non-resident evidence is treated as an admission that the supply was taxable in Canada.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is a fully convertible, freely floating fiat currency. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, confirming the absence of restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, and the capital account is de jure open. Inbound equity subscription, shareholder loans, and outbound dividend, interest, royalty, and service payments require no central-bank license, no advance registration, and no currency rationing. Residual obligations are reporting, not permission: (i) FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer (IEFT) reports for every cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) declaration of physically carried currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) ICA notification/review for non-Canadian capital injections establishing or acquiring a Canadian business; and (iv) screening against Canadian consolidated sanctions lists. Profit remittance is unimpeded, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced; e.g., 10% or 15% dividends, 10% interest, and 10% royalties under the Canada-China tax agreement) and to transfer-pricing documentation for related-party service and licence fees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951 and imposes no statutory timeline, no surrender ratio, and no mandatory conversion on export receipts. CA_CORP may invoice its USD or CAD clients in any freely negotiated currency, retain the proceeds in the export account, convert at interbank market rate through any Canadian bank or FINTRAC-registered MSB, and repatriate the balance to non-resident shareholders subject only to corporate-law solvency rules and Part XIII withholding. The sole compliance obligations are: (i) the bank's automatic FINTRAC IEFT report for every cross-border transfer above CAD 10,000; (ii) GST/HST zero-rating records for exported engineering/BIM services supplied to non-residents (Schedule VI, section 9 of the Excise Tax Act), requiring retention of the export contract, invoice, and proof of payment; and (iii) Canadian balance-of-payments classification of the proceeds as computer and information services for Statistics Canada survey purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa, Ontario, is the central monetary authority and exclusive issuer of the Canadian dollar. Canada does not operate a foreign-exchange administration commission or a SAFE-style exchange-control office. Prudential regulation of authorized banks and foreign bank branches is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Cross-border fund movement reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its Regulations. Foreign investment entry controls are administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act (ICA). Sanctions licensing and enforcement are co-administered by Global Affairs Canada, FINTRAC, and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act. Domestic clearing and settlement infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada (Real-Time Rail and the former Large Value Transfer System).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as a supplementary disbursement rail; Low as a replacement for the primary deposit relationship. Wise Inc. (Wise, formerly TransferWise) is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and offers Wise Business multi-currency accounts with CAD, USD, EUR, GBP, and CNY balances, local-currency clearing (Payments Canada EFT, US ACH, SEPA, Faster Payments), and mid-market FX conversion. A Quebec-registered corporation may lawfully use Wise to collect small foreign invoices and to pay foreign subcontractors, subject to four treasury constraints: (i) funds held at Wise are custodial/prepaid obligations, not insured deposits under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation Act, so the monthly float must be capped at the lower of CAD 25,000 or 10% of operating liquidity with a daily sweep back to the Schedule I bank; (ii) every cross-border transfer above CAD 10,000 will be reported by Wise to FINTRAC as an IEFT, creating a parallel data trail that must reconcile with the bank ledger; (iii) Wise cannot execute cheques, letters of credit, payroll tax remittances to CRA or Revenu Quebec, or Part XIII withholding processes, so statutory payments must remain on the bank rail; and (iv) Airwallex, Nium, and Payoneer are also FINTRAC-registered MSBs and viable for niche corridors, but Wise is the only EMI with mature Canadian routing, transparent pricing, and tested compatibility with the target payment rails specified as Wire_Transfer and Wise.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate for fiscal years ending in 2024 (15% federal Part I general rate + 11.5% Quebec general corporate rate under the Taxation Act (Quebec)). A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) with active business income benefits from a reduced combined rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII gross tax imposed on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to any non-resident shareholder (Income Tax Act s. 212(2)). The rate is treaty-reduced: 5% for a US beneficial owner that is a company holding at least 10% of the voting stock and 15% for other US portfolio shareholders (Canada-US DTA Article X); 10% for substantial Canadian-China corporate shareholding and 15% for portfolio dividends (Canada-China DTA Article X).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a related non-resident entity triggers the mandatory filing of Form T106 (Related-Party Transactions). Canada adopts the OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation principles; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared and produced to the CRA within three months of a written request. Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting applies to MNE groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least approximately CAD 1,000,000,000 (the CAD equivalent of EUR 750 million), governed by ITA sections 233.7 to 233.74 and the Canada Revenue Agency CbC filing portal.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Bermuda; instead, the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) denies tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose. Corporate residence is a common-law factual determination based on the location of the corporation's central management and control, per the De Beers / Stikeman principles, and the CRA may deem a Quebec-registered shell to be managed from abroad solely to access a treaty or to avoid domestic tax. Anti-treaty-shopping protections appear in Article XXIX-A of the Canada-US DTA (Limitation on Benefits), and the GAAR applies to all artificial structures, including those used by BIM/CAD engineering service providers where the principal purpose is to strip profits through non-arm's length fees paid to offshore or related entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements (Offshore Billing Scheme)

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross negligence penalty on the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2); Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 1052); 25%-50% penalties for false statements (ITA s. 163(3)); reassessments reopened beyond the normal reassessment period upon misrepresentation; interest compounded daily. Criminal: conviction under ITA s. 239(1)(d) for wilful evasion carries a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to two years; where the Revenue fraud exceeds CAD 1,000,000, prosecution under Criminal Code s. 380 can result in imprisonment of up to fourteen years.

Director personal liability: Quebec Civil Code art. 317 allows the corporate veil to be pierced where the corporation is used for a fraudulent or unlawful purpose; directors or de facto officers who knowingly signed false returns face immediate personal civil liability for the unremitted tax, penalties, and interest; the CRA and Revenu Quebec may issue personal assessments against directors under ITA s. 227.1 and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 34; in egregious evasion, directors incur criminal exposure and can be prohibited from departing Canada under ITA s. 226.1 when the tax debt exceeds CAD 50,000.
Sham / Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of the tax benefit with reassessment of the full amount and interest (ITA s. 245); a penalty of 25% of the avoided tax under s. 245(4)(a), rising to 50% where the arrangement includes a misrepresentation designed to mislead; denial of treaty benefits under Article XXIX-A of the Canada-US DTA; nullification of Quebec deductions; publication of audit findings in CRA's voluntary disclosures context does not apply to fraudulent schemes; civil fraud damages may be asserted by the tax authority.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permitted the corporation to function as a shell face personal liability for taxes recovered under the veil-piercing doctrine (Civil Code art. 317, 'fraude a la loi'); the CRA may assess directors for the corporation's Part XIII and source deduction liabilities under ITA s. 227.1; travel bans under s. 226.1 apply where a personal assessment remains unpaid; in the case of intentional misrepresentation to extend the reassessment window, directors may be personally criminally liable for conspiracy to defraud the revenue.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income at arm's-length prices plus daily compounded interest; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount where reasonably adequate documentation has not been prepared contemporaneously and retained (ITA s. 247(3)); penalty of CAD 1,000 per month, up to a maximum of CAD 24,000, for failure to respond to a CRA information demand (ITA s. 247(4)); additional 50% gross-negligence penalty where the TP adjustment arises from intentional mispricing; T106 late-filing penalty of CAD 100 for each day of non-compliance up to 25 days, with daily increases after that.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a T2 or CO-17 or any schedule containing false or misleading transfer-pricing information expose themselves to criminal liability under ITA s. 239 and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 1054; the CRA can separately pursue directors under ITA s. 227.1 for any portion of the corporation's unpaid Part XIII withholdings linked to the arrangement; directors may be named personally in Quebec courts on a 'faute' theory, giving rise to civil restitution orders and exclusion from serving as officers of any Quebec corporation.
Non-Filing, Unremitted Withholdings, and Part XIII Violation

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties (5% plus 1% monthly surcharge up to 12 months on federal unpaid balance; same structure under Quebec); 10% penalty for failure to remit source deductions, and 20% for repeated unreasonableness (ITA s. 227(8) and (9); Quebec L.T.A. ss. 1067-1071); interest compounded daily; Part XIII failure to withhold renders the payer (Quebec entity) liable for the full 25% of the dividend gross amount as a debt due to the Crown; criminal prosecution of the corporation under ITA s. 239; in aggravated cases, deregistration under the Canada Business Corporations Act and Quebec Business Corporations Act for failure to comply with tax statutes.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholdings under ITA s. 227.1 and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 34, provided they knew or ought to have known of the failure and did not exercise the 'diligence standard' (due diligence defense); a director who participated in a dividend declaration while knowing the corporation was insolvent faces personal recovery of the entire unpaid withholding plus interest; departure prohibitions under ITA s. 226.1 block international travel for directors with outstanding personal assessments exceeding CAD 50,000; intentional failure can lead to imprisonment for up to two years.
