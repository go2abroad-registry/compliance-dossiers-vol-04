Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST and provincial corporate tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account, Revenu Québec Business Portal.

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign entity must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing form RC1 or through the Online Business Registration portal, providing articles of incorporation and non-resident director information. For GST/HST, register under the BN program for VAT/TIN purposes. For QST, register with Revenu Québec using form RQ-0001 (or online) to obtain a Quebec Registration Number (QST ID). Import/export and payroll sub-accounts must be opened simultaneously where applicable. The 15-digit TIN comprises the 9-digit BN with two-letter program identifier (RT, RN, RM).

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods (intraocular acrylic lenses) exported from Canada are zero-rated under Sections 1 and 2 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act provided the export is made by way of sale and the goods are exported in accordance with the CRA's prescribed evidence of export, including delivery to a carrier for transport outside Canada or export by the foreign purchaser. Services directly performed on exported goods or on goods temporarily imported for finishing may be zero-rated if the goods are subsequently exported.

False declarations or fraudulent zero-rating expose registrants to penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 25% of erroneous tax, minimum CAD 250), interest under section 280.2, and gross negligence penalties under section 285.1 (up to $4,000 or 25% of net tax). Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada operates an open capital account with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; there is no foreign-exchange approval regime, no licensing of FX transactions, and no requirement for Bank of Canada approval before crediting or remitting foreign currency. The Canadian dollar is a free float under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act; official intervention occurs only exceptionally for monetary-stability reasons. Profit remittance from a Quebec corporate subsidiary to foreign shareholders is unrestricted from an FX-control standpoint, but is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate; reduced to 15% generally under the Canada-US treaty, 5% on certain US portfolio dividends where the beneficial owner is a company owning at least 10% of voting shares, and 10-15% under other applicable tax treaties), as well as CRA and Revenu Québec compliance. Dividend remittances and capital repatriation also trigger FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTRs) where the amount is C$10,000 or more. Foreign-currency revenues (USD, EUR, CNH) from exports of intraocular acrylic lenses may be held freely, but the Canadian corporate treasury must translate foreign-currency balances into Canadian dollars for tax purposes under the Income Tax Act and Income Tax Regulations (section 261, functional-currency rules and section 39 foreign-exchange gain/loss rules). An elective Canadian-dollar functional-currency reporting regime is available for qualifying corporations. No capital-control hurdles exist for a share subscription or intercompany loan denominated in a foreign currency.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or holding timeline applies to Canadian exporters. A Quebec company exporting intraocular acrylic lenses may retain 100% of export proceeds in any convertible currency (USD, EUR, CNH, GBP) in domestic or foreign bank accounts, and may time the conversion to CAD according to commercial needs without central-bank authorization. The only binding requirements are: (i) FINTRAC EFTR reporting by the financial institution for every international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more; (ii) Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) obligations for cash receipts/deposits of C$10,000 or more; (iii) Canadian Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) for all merchandise exports, including the declared HS classification for ophthalmic/medical devices under HS Chapter 90; (iv) Income Tax Act record-keeping and year-end translation of foreign-currency balances; and (v) five-year retention of underlying trade documentation (invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and bank advices) under the PCMLTFA and its Regulations.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and central bank; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential bank regulator and derivatives margining overseer; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — AML/CTF supervisor for banks, MSBs, and reporting repository; Global Affairs Canada — administrator of economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
FEASIBILITY RATING: MEDIUM — CONDITIONAL. Canadian-incorporated Fintech Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, OFX, Payoneer, dLocal) may be used for low-value, high-frequency cross-border payables and receivables, typically under C$50,000 per transaction, provided they are FINTRAC-registered and maintain sub-accounts at Schedule I banks. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) chartered under the UK FCA, EU PSD2/EMD2, or Hong Kong/HKMA regimes are NOT permitted to take deposits from Canadian residents or to operate as a bank in Canada without an OSFI license or an authorized foreign bank branch under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); they may act only as a pass-through FX platform and must not hold client funds overnight. OSFI has announced a fintech/limited-purpose bank charter concept, but full deposit-taking authorization for digital-only banks is not yet operationalized. AML constraint: the high-risk medical-device manufacturing classification (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112) will trigger enhanced EDD and a reputational-file review by any MSB before facility approval. Therefore, the main clearing and liquidity infrastructure should remain with a tier-1 Schedule I bank; EMIs may be added for supplier disbursement efficiency, but never for capital injections or profit-repatriation flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Non-resident shareholders should route the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 direct to the Quebec corporate operating account (preferably to RBC or National Bank of Canada as the primary receiving bank). The wire must include an explicit purpose statement in Field 70, for example 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION / SHARE CAPITAL / CA_CORP / ARTICLES DATED [YYYY-MM-DD]' — generic references such as 'Investments' or 'Goods' may trigger enhanced EDD. FINTRAC requires the bank to file an EFTR for any international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more; the company is not the reporter but must cooperate with the bank's source-of-funds attestation. If the contribution is in a foreign currency (USD or EUR), the receiving bank will either convert at the spot rate or credit a foreign-currency clearing sub-account at the bank's New York or London correspondent (typically via JPMorgan, BNY Mellon, or Citibank for US-dollar clearing). Under the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28), a non-Canadian shareholder beginning a new Canadian business must file a mandatory notification with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) after incorporation, while an acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business above the prescribed enterprise-value threshold (adjusted annually; approximately C$1.3 billion for WTO-treaty investors in 2024-2025) requires prior review and approval — a greenfield capital injection into a newly incorporated Quebec business corporation by a WTO investor is notifiable, not reviewable unless it involves a cultural business. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: FEASIBILITY RATING: MEDIUM — CONDITIONAL. Canadian-incorporated Fintech Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, OFX, Payoneer, dLocal) may be used for low-value, high-frequency cross-border payables and receivables, typically under C$50,000 per transaction, provided they are FINTRAC-registered and maintain sub-accounts at Schedule I banks. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) chartered under the UK FCA, EU PSD2/EMD2, or Hong Kong/HKMA regimes are NOT permitted to take deposits from Canadian residents or to operate as a bank in Canada without an OSFI license or an authorized foreign bank branch under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); they may act only as a pass-through FX platform and must not hold client funds overnight. OSFI has announced a fintech/limited-purpose bank charter concept, but full deposit-taking authorization for digital-only banks is not yet operationalized. AML constraint: the high-risk medical-device manufacturing classification (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112) will trigger enhanced EDD and a reputational-file review by any MSB before facility approval. Therefore, the main clearing and liquidity infrastructure should remain with a tier-1 Schedule I bank; EMIs may be added for supplier disbursement efficiency, but never for capital injections or profit-repatriation flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
26.5% combined general corporate income tax rate (15% federal portion + 11.5% Quebec provincial portion, subject to annual indexation)

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on all taxable dividends paid to non-residents of Canada, unless reduced under a Canada income tax treaty; typical treaty rates: 15% general, 5% for corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of the voting stock

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue for the mandatory Master File and Local File obligations for taxation years beginning after 2018; all reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions in excess of CAD 5 million (aggregate gross proceeds) require a prescribed T106 information return and contemporaneous documentation under section 247

      
Economic substance scope: Canada adopts an economic substance doctrine embodied in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, and Quebec enforces analogous anti-abuse provisions; DTA benefits may be denied where the Canadian entity lacks real economic substance, employment, and substantive activities; OECD-style substance requirements are applied contextually for treaty shopping and hybrid mismatch arrangements

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act; fine of 50% of tax evaded up to 200% of the amount, plus imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years; Revenu Québec levies an additional penalty equal to 50% of the evaded amount and orders full restitution; civil reassessment with interest compounded daily

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for corporate income tax, depreciation recovery, and any penalties when they caused the false declaration; Quebec authorities may issue a travel ban and a civil recovery order against directors, and a 5-year penal sentence may be served personally

      

      
      

        Shell Company
        
Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment nullifying the tax benefit; Parliament grants CRA power to deny treaty relief and impose interest and penalties of up to 50% of the tax benefit under section 245(5); Quebec may apply its own anti-avoidance penalty of up to 30% of the avoided provincial tax; invalid tax planning also exposes the entity to cessation of licences under the Quebec Manufacturing Licensing Act

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitated a shell corporation to defraud the CRA and creditors are personally liable for the full amount of corporate tax plus penalties, may be stripped of travel privileges by court order, and can receive imprisonment of up to 2 years less a day in Quebec for fraud; in certain criminal proceedings involving cross-border financing, a fine up to CAD 500,000 may apply

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under section 247(3): 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest; penalty for failure to maintain documentation is CAD 1,000 per month up to CAD 12,000 for each failure; CRA also levies a foreign reporting penalty of CAD 2,500 per month for non-compliance with Form T1134; double-dip interest not amortizable; reassessment may trigger Quebec penalties of up to 20% of unpaid provincial tax

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed transfer pricing misinformation are liable for the corporate penalty, may face administrative monetary penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act, and have their personal assets seized to satisfy the debt; a director or executive can be subject to an order to be prohibited from leaving Canada pending final reassessment

      

      
      

        AML / Money Laundering
        
Statutory sanction: Offence under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Criminal Code; fines up to CAD 500,000 for each offence for corporations; monetary penalties under FINTRAC direction up to CAD 1,000,000 for failures in compliance programs; the provincial Minister may suspend or revoke any required health and medical device manufacturing licence

        
Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are liable for failing to implement a PCMLTFA compliance program; each director may be criminally convicted and imprisoned for up to 10 years; personal forfeiture orders, freezing orders, and travel bans are available; further, the director may be civilly restitutionary for all funds laundered through the corporate vehicle
