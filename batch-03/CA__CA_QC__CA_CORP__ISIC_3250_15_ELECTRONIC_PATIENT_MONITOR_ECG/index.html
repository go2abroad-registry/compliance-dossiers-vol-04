Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada/Quebec do not use a check-the-box regime; a legal corporation is automatically a 'taxable Canadian corporation' for federal Part I and Quebec corporate income tax purposes. Elections may nevertheless be available under section 216(4) of the Income Tax Act for non-resident owners of Canadian rental/receivable income to elect to file a net-basis return; section 216.1 election for cross-border aviation; section 48 deemed-disposition election on exiting Canadian tax residence; and section 277 alternative audit elections. Hybrid mismatch (BEPS Action 2) rules set out in sections 18.2–18.4 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec equivalent provisions impose matching adjustments but do not offer elective entity classification mechanisms. Partnerships and certain investment entities may make permitted elections (e.g., section 186.2 dividend allowance; section 96(10) partnership fiscal conformity) but these do not alter the default entity-level taxation of a business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD – the small supplier threshold under the Excise Tax Act (s. 240(1.1) and Sched. VI) and the Quebec Taxation Act (s. 424, RQ, c. I-3); taxable supplies include zero-rated exports, and once worldwide turnover exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, the supplier must register for GST/HST and QST within 30 days of the day on which the threshold was exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated supply (0% GST/HST and QST) is available for goods exported from Canada under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act and Part V, Schedule VI (Exported Goods), and under the Quebec Taxation Act analogous provisions. The supplier must establish that (a) the goods are supplied in Canada under an agreement of sale for export, (b) the goods are not further processed, altered, or used in Canada prior to export, and (c) they are delivered to a common carrier or shipped to a non-resident consignee within a reasonable time (CRA generally requires immediate shipment and no change in ownership after export). For services, zero-rating under Part V, Schedule VII applies where the service is supplied to a non-resident and is performed wholly outside Canada or is performed in Canada in respect of exported goods. For ECG patient monitor hardware, the standard procedure is zero-rating by invoice, with supporting evidence held in the permanent export file; input tax credits on manufacturing inputs are then recoverable. The small supplier election may be filed to voluntarily register and claim input tax credits on pre-export purchases.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic Canadian Export Reporting System / ERS report) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency for exports valued over CAD 2,000 and/or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the common carrier, evidencing export from Canada and delivery to the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List, including HS classification (ISIC 3250/NAICS 334510), country of origin, and value declarations
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer within the prescribed repatriation period
  • Customs export authorization / release documents, and any government export permit applicable to medical electronic devices

No federal or Quebec real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API-based electronic VAT filing, or digital tax stamping is currently in force. E-invoicing is permitted and legally valid if the invoice contains all statutory particulars under the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations (SOR/91-45) and Quebec Regulation respecting the application of the Taxation Act (RRQ, c. I-3, a. 456, r. 1). Canada has announced a federal 'modern e-invoice' framework using the PEPPOL standard, but it remains optional / in pilot stage. There is no pre-clearance of invoices with CRA or Revenu Québec before issuance; invoice data must be retained and presented upon audit.

CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinize zero-rating claims through document-matching audits, export-trail reviews, and cross-border data exchanges. If zero-rating is disallowed, the supplier is reassessed at the full 5% GST and 9.975% QST on the export invoice, plus interest compounded daily under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act. Civil penalties apply under s. 280 (failure to collect), s. 285 (falsifying evidence), and s. 285.1 (gross negligence) of the Excise Tax Act, generally 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; false statements in an application or refund claim carry an additional 20% penalty. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act and s. 239 of the Income Tax Act can result in fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment up to five years. In Quebec, the same conduct may attract penalties under sections 461–463 of the Quebec Taxation Act, and directors' liability can be asserted under s. 323 of the Excise Tax Act and Quebec equivalent provisions. Retrospective clawback is available for up to four years (ordinary assessment) and without limitation in cases of fraud or misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible for both current and capital account purposes. Canada maintains no exchange control legislation restricting the remittance of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or equity distributions to non-residents. Repatriation is effected at freely negotiated market rates, subject only to corporate law solvency tests, applicable withholding taxes under the Canada-Quebec tax framework (where relevant), and normal AML/KYC verification at the remitting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain export receipts in CAD or foreign currency, invoice in any acceptable currency, and settle through domestic chartered banks without any central bank approval or surrender obligation. Normal trade documentation and CBSA customs declarations apply for statistical and tariff purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange control administration is exercised jointly under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administered by FINTRAC, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank conduct. There is no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High supplementary viability for cross-border collections and disbursements, but medium as a primary liquidity rail. Digital EMIs such as Wise and Payoneer can provide foreign-currency receipt and payout concentration accounts, yet they must register as Canadian Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and comply with provincial licensing (Quebec, when applicable). They do not substitute for a Schedule I bank settlement account for statutory capital escrow, ISIC-3250 trade operations, or commercial letters of credit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Opacity in ownership chain or mismatched ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) declarations on the FINTRAC MSB/account-opening form

Defense evidence: Provide initial/updated certificate of incorporation (Quebec or federal), shareholder register, trust/ownership structure chart, UBO declaration form, certified passports of all directors and UBOs, proof of residential address, and a CRS/FATCA self-certification for each account holder.
Sanctions/PEPs screening hit or incomplete politically exposed persons (PEP) resolution

Root cause: Sanctions/PEPs screening hit or incomplete politically exposed persons (PEP) resolution

Defense evidence: Supply a sanctions screening consent letter, a FINTRAC PEP questionnaire with clear relationship/disposition details, documented source of wealth and source of funds reports, and an organizational nexus memorandum explaining any geographic or sector contacts with high-risk jurisdictions.
Insufficient source-of-funds evidence for initial capital deposits or intercompany inbound wires

Root cause: Insufficient source-of-funds evidence for initial capital deposits or intercompany inbound wires

Defense evidence: Provide executed capital subscription agreements, board resolution for capital injection, bank statements of the investor for the prior 3-6 months, wire trace/purpose code explanation, business plan with projected cash flows, and invoices/contracts supporting the transaction chain.
Industry licensing / regulatory classification of medical device manufacturer (ISIC 3250)

Root cause: Industry licensing / regulatory classification of medical device manufacturer (ISIC 3250)

Defense evidence: Submit Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (or MDEL application confirmation), ISO 13485 certificate, product classification file, third-party quality audits, and a detailed trade compliance manual to demonstrate that the company's core business is regulated, not a shell/alternative-remittance operation.
Incomplete or expired corporate authorization for foreign directors or signatories

Root cause: Incomplete or expired corporate authorization for foreign directors or signatories

Defense evidence: Deliver the updated register of directors, by-laws/BDC articles, notarized board/minutes/consents, corporate power-of-attorney form, and any provincial registration certificate (Quebec, Canada) ensuring the person opening the account has actual capacity. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injections are effected through SWIFT MT103 transfers to the company's CAD/USD capital account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The payment field must state a precise purpose code, commonly '6010 – Equity injection' or 'Capital contribution from non-resident parent' under the Canadian banking purpose-code taxonomy; for distributions it is '7010 – Dividend / profit remittance'. The receiving bank will require the originating bank's OFAC-compliant wire, a board resolution authorizing the capital injection, and a signed UBO declaration. No prior Bank of Canada approval is required, but the bank may request the transaction to be booked in an 'Escrowed Capital' sub-account pending verification of the CSO/B2B subscription agreement and anti-money-laundering source-of-funds due diligence. For transactions in excess of CAD 100,000, the bank may also ask for an independent confirmation of the wire's purpose via the Comfort Letter from the foreign investor.Outbound trade settlements from the Quebec manufacturing and EPC operations are cleared via SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for routine payments and via commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) for high-value import/export of electro-medical components and finished monitoring devices. The Canadian importer/exporter should maintain a dedicated trade finance account with documentary collection (D/P, D/A) capabilities. Each disbursement must be accompanied by a customs invoice, pro forma invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and a certified country-of-origin certificate (when applicable), with matching transaction references to the commercial contract. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined: 15% Federal Part I general corporate rate plus 11.5% Quebec general corporate income tax rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders (Income Tax Act s. 212); treaty-reduced rates commonly apply, e.g., Canada–United States DTA: 5% when shareholder controls at least 10% of voting stock, 15% for portfolio dividends; Canada–most treaty partners: 10% to 15% depending on ownership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (s. 247(4) ITA and Quebec s. 25.40) above which contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File and Master File) is required; transactions below this threshold still require reasonable written policy, but the statutory penalty threshold is CAD 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not have dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA and Quebec s. 1060) denies tax benefits arising from transactions lacking bona fide business purpose, real economic substance, and arm's-length risk allocation. Transfer-pricing audits also rely on the OECD substance-over-form doctrine and the 'commercial rationality' test to challenge circular payments, offshore 'round-tripping', and interposed companies with no active business or employees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years (s. 238 and s. 239 ITA); Quebec imposes corresponding sanction up to double the amount evaded (Quebec Taxation Act s. 121.1); CRA may reassess beyond the normal three-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud exists.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax withholdings under s. 227.1; directors who knowingly participate in evasion can be personally prosecuted, subjected to civil restitution orders, travel bans pending charges, and prison sentences; veil-piercing applies where corporate form is used as a shield for deliberate fraud.
Shell Company and Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA may ignore the shell under the sham doctrine, deny treaty benefits under Lack-of-Beneficial-Ownership principles, apply GAAR to nullify the tax reduction, and levy a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the misstatement; Quebec may impose an administrative penalty of CAD 1,000 for false declarations per return.

Director personal liability: Directors who conceal the true control of the corporate group risk personal liability for resulting corporate tax debts if the company is stripped of assets; directors may receive prohibition on departure through court orders and may be convicted of fraud under the Criminal Code for using incorporated shells to defraud the government.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Low-Risk Tier)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, a corporation can be fined up to CAD 500,000 per administrative violation; directors/offences causing awareness can be treated as separate offences subject to imprisonment up to 10 years; Quebec does not provide separate civil freedom regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 if they knowingly fail to establish an AML compliance program; upon criminal conviction, directors are subject to imprisonment up to 10 years and travel bans; confiscation orders can attach to personal assets where funds were intentionally routed through the company to conceal proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess a 10% penalty on the amount of net transfer-pricing adjustment where the taxpayer has failed to make reasonable efforts to determine arm's-length prices (s. 247(3) ITA); double taxation adjustments and indirect GST/HST penalties may stack; Quebec may impose 10% penalty on additional provincial tax arising from transfer-pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily liable for transfer-pricing penalties, but if the corporation is stripped of assets or declared a pauper business, personal liability can arise under s. 227.1 for unpaid money withheld (payroll, GST); liability may extend to director’s personal income tax return under s. 91 if they personally benefitted from the transfer-pricing scheme; civil restitution orders and director travel bans are granted in serious multinational evasion cases.
