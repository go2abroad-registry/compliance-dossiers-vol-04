Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax, with Quebec administered by Revenu Quebec (RQ) under the federal-provincial single-administration agreement; commercial registry: Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ). Portals: CRA My Business Account (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and RQ Mon dossier d'entreprise (www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A Quebec societe en commandite (limited partnership) must first obtain a 9-digit NEQ/BN from REQ. GST/HST (RT program account) and QST (RM program account) are then activated in a single-window filing via RQ or CRA Form RC1. A non-resident general partner must file the LP registration certificate and proof of authority, may be required to post security under ETA s. 297, should appoint a Canadian fiscal representative, and must register within 30 days after the LP ceases to be a small supplier; non-resident registrants file GST/QST returns on prescribed forms (e.g., RC151 series) quarterly or monthly.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - the LP is fiscally transparent for federal income tax (ITA s. 96) and generally for Quebec income tax; however, under Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 615.1, a societe en commandite that leases or holds Quebec immovable property (including a warehouse leasehold) is deemed an entity-level taxpayer unless all partners are 'reporting entities'. For GST/QST purposes the LP is a separate registrant person liable on its taxable supplies. — A Quebec societe en commandite (limited partnership) must first obtain a 9-digit NEQ/BN from REQ. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the LP is fiscally transparent for federal income tax (ITA s. 96) and generally for Quebec income tax; however, under Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 615.1, a societe en commandite that leases or holds Quebec immovable property (including a warehouse leasehold) is deemed an entity-level taxpayer unless all partners are 'reporting entities'. For GST/QST purposes the LP is a separate registrant person liable on its taxable supplies.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96.1; (ii) Quebec declaration/election preserving flow-through treatment where all LP members are reporting entities (TA-QC s. 615.1); (iii) joint closely-related-entities election under ETA s. 156 for GST/QST; (iv) hybrid-mismatch neutralizing elections under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 and Quebec counterparts; (v) rollover election for property contributed to the partnership under ITA s. 97(2).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (worldwide taxable supplies of the LP and any associates, tested per calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive quarters; ETA s. 123(1) 'small supplier' and Quebec TVQ Act s. 14). The LP must register for GST and QST within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register even below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Core warehouse and fulfillment services on goods physically located in Quebec are TAXABLE at GST+QST because the Part V, Schedule VI, s. 7 ETA zero-rating for services to non-residents is unavailable for services in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed. Zero-rating applies only to: (i) goods sold by the LP and exported without use in Canada within 60 days, supported by satisfactory export evidence; (ii) services performed in respect of property located outside Canada; (iii) cross-dock transshipment of in-bond (bonded/sufferance warehouse) goods re-exported without release into the Canadian market, with import GST relieved under Schedule VII s. 17 where goods are exported by the importer unused within 90 days. An advance GST/HST ruling is recommended for any in-transit storage arrangement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A export declaration, or B3-3 coding form for in-bond re-export movements)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent carrier transport document evidencing the physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank FX statements proving foreign-currency settlement by the overseas principal

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital fiscal signature regime exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework administered by the CRA-certified Canadian Peppol Authority. Invoices in foreign currency are permitted; GST/QST is converted at the Bank of Canada daily rate on the invoice date. Mandatory record retention: six years (ETA s. 286(1); Quebec TA s. 37).

CRA and Revenu Quebec may deny zero-rating, disallow input tax credits, and reassess outside the four-year normal limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Civil penalties include 25% of tax understated or falsely claimed (ETA s. 285), gross-negligence penalties of 25%-50% (ITA s. 163(2); Quebec TA s. 1049.3), daily compound arrears interest at prescribed rates, and parallel QST penalties; wilful evasion is indictable with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years (ETA s. 327), and CBSA may seize bonded goods for unpaid duties. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the internal documentary-control benchmark for assembling export evidence and settlement records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both the current and capital accounts; there are no exchange controls restricting the repatriation of capital or the remittance of profits for a Quebec limited partnership. The operative statutory hurdle is not FX convertibility but the Canadian Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% on distributions of income to non-resident limited partners, subject to treaty relief under the Canada-US Tax Convention or an applicable bilateral tax treaty. An additional administrative filter is the Canada Revenue Agency requirement that non-resident partners file a partnership information return (TP-698 / T5013) and that the LP maintain a registered Quebec establishment (établissement) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to lawfully operate and distribute funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; the Quebec 3PL LP and any associated e-commerce fulfilment entities may freely retain foreign currency earned from overseas order fulfilment. However, all cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or the equivalent in foreign currency) must be reported by the reporting entity (the bank or registered money-services business) to FINTRAC, and balance-of-payments data on export/import trade in goods must be reported to Statistics Canada under the Trade Returns Regulations and the Customs Act framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border funds reporting; foreign-exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with the Bank of Canada Act governing monetary policy and the payment and settlement system.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident limited partners owning greater than 25% of the LP and forming opaque multi-layer ownership structures

Root cause: Non-resident limited partners owning greater than 25% of the LP and forming opaque multi-layer ownership structures

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the limited partnership agreement; exhaustive register of partners with full legal name, date of birth, jurisdiction of tax residence, and taxpayer identification number; an ownership organigram through every intermediate entity to each ultimate beneficial owner; source-of-funds documentation comprising bank statements, capital commitment letters, and notarized capital contribution declarations; and PEP self-certification questionnaires or a non-PEP statutory declaration.
Sanctions exposure, high-risk jurisdiction tie-ins, or jurisdiction-hopping in originating and destination wiring corridors

Root cause: Sanctions exposure, high-risk jurisdiction tie-ins, or jurisdiction-hopping in originating and destination wiring corridors

Defense evidence: Screening clearance reports against the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List, the United Nations Act schedule, and international sanctions lists; PEP and adverse-media search logs; a formal customer risk-rating rationale demonstrating low-risk category under FINTRAC guidance; a geographic exposure memorandum mapping all counterparty jurisdictions; and a documented suspicious-transaction-report protocol for any residual alerts.
Unexplained intra-entity funding inflows inconsistent with economic substance of the ISIC 5210 / NAICS 493110 cross-border 3PL operation

Root cause: Unexplained intra-entity funding inflows inconsistent with economic substance of the ISIC 5210 / NAICS 493110 cross-border 3PL operation

Defense evidence: Business-plan package covering the Quebec warehouse operation, sublease or lease agreement for the fulfilment facility, contract set with overseas e-commerce marketplace counterparties, inventory-management and order-fulfilment reports, payroll and operating-expense run-rate statements, and the Canada Revenue Agency business number and tax-registration confirmations or applicable treaty-residency certificates.
Failure to establish Quebec domicile, absence of a valid Canadian tax identification number, or incomplete partnership registration

Root cause: Failure to establish Quebec domicile, absence of a valid Canadian tax identification number, or incomplete partnership registration

Defense evidence: Quebec enterprise registration certificate issued by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ); Canada Revenue Agency business number and account-program confirmation; partnership information return filing confirmation (TP-698 for Quebec fiscal purposes); and the bank-preferred statutory declaration of domicile together with the certificate of the general partner's good standing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from overseas limited partners or parent entities should be effected by SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to the Quebec LP's Schedule I bank operational account, with the beneficiary legal name and account number stated exactly as registered, the remitter referencing the LP's Quebec NEQ and the specific capital-commitment internal reference, and the purpose-of-payment code mapped to 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' (or the intermediary bank's equivalent corporate-purpose classification for Bank of Canada balance-of-payments reporting). USD/CAD conversion should be executed by the bank's treasury desk via a spot contract or same-day value trade to avoid embedded spread leakage; any inbound EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is reported to FINTRAC by the receiving bank, and the LP should instruct all remitters to use the full legal entity description to prevent a frozen-funds compliance hold.Cross-border trade settlements to overseas 3PL vendors, freight forwarders, and e-commerce counterparties should be executed through SWIFT MT103 conformant wires using the International Bank Account Number and the bank identifier code of the beneficiary; same-day Canadian-dollar domestic clearing should use the Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the evolving High-Value Payment System, while batch lower-value payroll and disbursement flows may be cleared through the Retail System (ACH) and, where commercially available, the Real-Time Rail for sub-threshold instant payments. Late filing constitutes a penal offence under RLRQ, c. P-44.1; a legal person is liable to a fine of C$300 to C$2,000 (C$100 to C$1,000 for a natural person). The REQ may note the default in the public register and, after two consecutive years of non-filing, publish a notice of intention to cancel the enterprise registration; cancellation takes effect 30 days after the notice.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory independent audit is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership solely because of its form. A compulsory audit arises only where the partnership is a reporting issuer under securities legislation, where the partnership is registered as a tax shelter under Income Tax Act section 237.1 and audited financial statements are required, or where the partnership agreement, lender, or investor covenant expressly demands an audit. A standard e-commerce cross-dock 3PL LP with no public securities and no tax-shelter status has no mandatory audit obligation.
