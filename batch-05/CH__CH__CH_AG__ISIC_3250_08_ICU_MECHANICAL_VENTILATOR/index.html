Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of the registered office (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug if Zug is selected as the seat); the Swiss Federal Commercial Register (eGRIS) acts as the central public database of all cantonal commercial registries.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / OR), SR 220, Articles 620 et seq. (incorporation and governance of the Aktiengesellschaft / AG); Articles 626–635 (formation deed and registered capital); Article 637 (contributions in kind); Articles 698 and 716b (shareholder and board competences).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised deed of incorporation (Errichtungsurkunde) and commercial register application form (Anmeldung zur Handelsregistereintragung) filed by the notary; registration becomes effective upon publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC / SHAB).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 129 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The share capital of an Aktiengesellschaft must be at least CHF 100,000. At formation, at least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up, but the aggregate paid-up capital may not be less than CHF 50,000. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the company's name; the bank confirmation of payment is submitted to the notary and the Commercial Register. The blocked funds are released only upon the company's registration.

Funding & unlock path: Before production may start, the company must obtain the cantonal fire safety certification, structural load approval, workplace occupational safety inspection, SUVA accident-prevention certification, and electrical installation approval under the Low Voltage Ordinance. The production plant and all technical devices must be suitable for operation in an ICU device manufacturing environment, including emergency lighting, emergency exits, and fire-safety segregation of test laboratories.

Paid-in capital rule: The share capital of an Aktiengesellschaft must be at least CHF 100,000. At formation, at least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up, but the aggregate paid-up capital may not be less than CHF 50,000. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the company's name; the bank confirmation of payment is submitted to the notary and the Commercial Register. The blocked funds are released only upon the company's registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch and e-services via www.e-service.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first be registered in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister / Registre du commerce) at the cantonal level, obtaining the Swiss Identification Number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer UID). For VAT purposes, the UID is used as the VAT ID; the entity must apply for VAT registration with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV) if taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000 per year (excluding VAT). The application is submitted electronically via the e-service portal or by paper form, providing the UID, business purpose, and expected turnover. The ESTV confirms the VAT registration and assigns a VAT number in the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST/IVA/IVA. Non-resident entities without a Swiss registered office but with taxable operations in Switzerland must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative for VAT and direct tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a check-the-box election system. However, Swiss tax law provides specific elective regimes that may be exercised by an AG: (i) VAT group election (Art. 21 VAT Act) allowing affiliated entities to be treated as one taxable person; (ii) participation deduction election (Art. 69 Federal Act on Direct Taxation) for qualifying dividends and capital gains on qualifying participations, which is applied upon meeting thresholds and subject to confirmation; (iii) possible cantonal tax status electives (e.g., mixed holding or principal company status) that must be formally requested and approved; (iv) Swiss-Hybrid Mismatch Rules (implementing OECD BEPS actions) do not provide elective elections but allow for a modified taxation approach if mismatches are identified; the taxpayer must notify the tax administration of any hybrid arrangement triggering a mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual global taxable turnover (excluding VAT); for non-profit/charitable activities CHF 150,000; for certain smaller businesses a CHF 150,000 threshold applies from 1 January 2025 under the revised VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to a destination outside Switzerland are subject to VAT at 0% if the goods are physically transferred to a foreign country and the exporter retains documentary evidence of export (Art. 23(2) VAT Act). Services supplied in connection with exported goods (e.g., transport, insurance) and certain international services (e.g., services abroad, international freight) are likewise zero-rated if provided to a foreign recipient and the recipient provides a declaration of business status. For medical devices such as ICU mechanical ventilators, the exporter must ensure the goods leave Switzerland within a reasonable time (typically 30 days) and must hold corresponding proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss customs export document duly stamped by the Swiss Federal Customs Administration)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill, CMR road consignment note, or other transport document indicating the destination outside Switzerland)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign currency payment received from the overseas customer, or bank statement verifying the receipt of proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Sales Contract or Order Confirmation
  • Export Permit (if required under Swiss medical device or dual-use export controls)

Switzerland does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for business-to-government (B2G) transactions under the Confederation's procurement rules using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format. For B2B transactions, e-invoicing is permitted and encouraged; however, the acceptance of electronic invoices must be agreed contractually. Taxpayers must preserve all invoices and underlying books in a format that is readable and auditable by the tax administration, with a retention period of 10 years. No API-based fiscal clearance mechanism is in force.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration conducts risk-based audits on export zero-rating claims, focusing on completeness of export evidence and timely remittance of proceeds. If the evidence is insufficient or funds are not repatriated within the 180-day window, the VAT at 8.1% is retrospectively payable from the date of the original supply, plus statutory default interest (5% per annum). In cases of fraudulent claims or willful tax evasion, fines are imposed under the Federal Act on Direct Taxation and the VAT Act; administrative penalties can reach 3 times the evaded tax (or up to 5 times in serious cases) and criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years. Additionally, the clawback extends to the entire supply chain and the exporter's fiscal representative is jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility for the Swiss franc (CHF). No exchange controls, no central bank approval for inward/outward transfers, and no legal restrictions on profit or capital repatriation for a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. Dividends and capital redemptions are freely remittable, subject to the 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) which is reclaimable by treaty-eligible recipients; this is a tax consideration, not an FX restriction. CHF settlement works through SIC/SIC4 and CLS. Practical banking friction arises from post-merger consolidation (UBS/Credit Suisse), banks' elevated KYC documentation demands, and strict privacy/AML balancing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. The manufacturer may retain 100% of export receivables in foreign currency, maintain multi-currency accounts, convert to CHF at any time, and request conversion via spot or forward transactions. There is no mandatory timeline or conversion ratio. Statutory reporting obligations exist for balance-of-payments statistics: Swiss companies generally report cross-border payment flows above CHF 10 million aggregate per calendar year or individual transactions above CHF 25,000, subject to SNB threshold-based reporting schedules.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is MEDIUM. FINMA-licensed FinTech companies may take public deposits up to CHF 100 million on a non-interest bearing basis, but a full banking licence is required to provide broad corporate treasury and cross-border financing services. Offshore EMI licences (FCA-UK or EU PSD2) are not passportable into Switzerland; they can only engage on a reverse-solicitation basis without holding Swiss client funds. Zug 'Crypto Valley' licensed banks (SEBA Bank AG, Sygnum Bank AG) provide viable tokenised FX/liquidity rails but are less suitable for heavy government LC confirmation and medical-device trade finance. Therefore, for a Mechanical Ventilator exporter relying on LC and SWIFT wire rails, a domestic commercial bank remains the operational treasury anchor; fintechs are supplementary FX and payment-innovation rails.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax levied on post-tax profit (effective federal burden on pre-tax profit: 7.83%); combined federal + cantonal + communal effective statutory burden approximately 14.6% in the preferred Zug base and 11.9%–21.0% across German/French cantons

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is deducted at source by the AG on all dividends (including hidden distributions and repurchases) paid to shareholders; Swiss-resident corporate shareholders receive a full refund; non-resident shareholders receive a refund of the excess over the applicable DTA rate

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when annual cross-border related-party transactions (goods, services, financing) exceed CHF 5,000,000; Master File: mandatory when the group's consolidated global turnover exceeds CHF 850,000,000 (ESTV TP documentation circular, OECD-aligned 2020/2023); the ICU ventilator manufacturer must also retain a benchmarking study for its RoHS/export prices to related distributors in Europe and Asia

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a functional substance doctrine though not an ESR-style licensing code: treaty benefits, patent box relief, notional interest deduction and tax rulings all require real economic activity, payroll, premises and management in Switzerland; a pure shell AG owning the ventilator IP while all decision-making occurs abroad can be denied DTA protection, have the patent box revoked with retrospective tax, and be treated as tax-transparent under the Swiss practice on ‘Basisgesellschaften’ (Art. 21 DBG / treaty abuse para. 2); the Swiss QDMTT and OECD minimum tax further require substance for intra-group allocation of profits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fiscal delinquency (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge up to 3 times the evaded federal tax plus cantonal tax surcharge; procedural fines up to CHF 1,000; late interest at 3% p.a.; in aggravated cases (forgery of titles), a criminal conviction for tax fraud (Art. 186 DBG) with imprisonment up to 3 years or fine

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are subject to personal criminal fines and imprisonment; the veil is pierced (Durchgriff) only on proof of abuse (asset commingling, undercapitalization), exposing board members to unlimited civil restitution for unpaid company taxes; a travel ban is possible under StPO Art. 224–225 during a criminal tax investigation
Non-remittance of Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: Claim against the AG for 35% of the gross distribution, plus 3% interest and a penalty up to 3 times the tax if omission was willful; liquidation proceedings may be initiated against the company

Director personal liability: Under Art. 12 VStG the board members and actual managers are personally liable for the unpaid WHT when the company is unable to pay; in criminal cases, custodial sentences up to 3 years apply and the responsible CFO is barred from future board mandates by registration authorities
Artificial shell / substance-less base company (Basisgesellschaft)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and reclassification of the AG as a sheltered vehicle: full 35% WHT on deemed distributions, participation and patent box relief withdrawn retroactively, plus late interest and evasion surcharge up to 3 times

Director personal liability: Directors of the omitted substance entity face criminal liability for false declarations; personal assets can be attached for the unpaid balance where funds were diverted to the parent; a Swiss exit ban and extradition request can be issued in cases of organized tax fraud
Transfer pricing understatement of profit

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment corrected to arm's-length profits under Art. 58 DBG, with late payment interest of 3%; documentation failure: fine up to CHF 10,000 and the burden of proof shifts against the taxpayer; deliberate sham pricing attracts the 3x evasion surcharge

Director personal liability: The CFO and CEO who knowingly signed the intercompany contracts become personally liable for the resulting underpaid tax under the Swiss Code of Obligations tightening (Art. 754 OR); criminal conviction for tax fraud; travel bans in the criminal phase
