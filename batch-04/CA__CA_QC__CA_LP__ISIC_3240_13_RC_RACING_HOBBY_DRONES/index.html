Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax and GST/HST; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec income tax, QST, and payroll source deductions; business registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ). Portals: CRA My Business Account and RQ Mon dossier d'entreprise / CléNumérique.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain an 11-digit NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) via the immatriculation declaration for sociétés en commandite. Step 2: after NEQ issuance, apply for the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 by mail/fax (or Business Registration Online where the agent holds a Canadian credential); request GST/HST program account (BN + 'RT') and, if applicable, payroll program account (RP). Step 3: apply to Revenu Québec for the QST registration using Form LM-1-V (or online under Mon dossier d'entreprise with the NEQ and an authorization mandate); RQ issues a QST number linked to the NEQ in the 'TQ' series. Step 4: file the partnership information return T5013 (CRA) and CO-17 (RQ) annually, allocating income to partners. Step 5: non-resident partners must provide their foreign TINs and residence declarations to the LP; the LP must withhold and remit the 25% Part XIII tax (treaty-reduced if applicable) on non-resident partners' shares of Quebec business income unless the partner files a Part I return for the year. Step 6: maintain a designated partnership representative for CRA/RQ correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: no entity-level federal or Quebec income tax; each partner includes its allocable share of partnership income or loss in its own return. For GST/HST/QST, however, the partnership is itself a separate person and registrant and must charge, collect and remit GST and QST on taxable supplies and claim input tax credits (ITCs/RTDs). Canada does not provide check-the-box classification; the LP classification is statutory (ITA s. 96 and Quebec Taxation Act arts. 596 et seq.). Territorial scope: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income derived through the LP.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available elections for an LP include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1 and Quebec parallel) to adopt a non-calendar fiscal year; (ii) ETA s. 273 partnership/member election to treat the partner as supplier/recipient for GST/HST/QST purposes (ITC attribution); (iii) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-resident partners to compute Canadian tax on a net basis on specified Canadian-source income instead of gross Part XIII withholding, with a Quebec mirror under the Taxation Act; (iv) hybrid mismatch regime under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (BEPS Action 2) and Quebec conforming rules: these are disallowance and relief rules, not elective classifications, but an LP that is a hybrid entity must apply them to cross-border payments between partners and foreign transparent entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST (ETA s. 148) and QST (Taxation Act): registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies of the person and its associated persons exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration must be completed within 29 days of the quarter end. For the FPV drone retail business via PayPal/Stripe, the LP should register voluntarily before reaching the threshold in order to claim ITCs/RTDs on drones, carbon-fiber materials, brushless motors, freight, and warehousing inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when a supplier sells tangible personal property (FPV racing drones, spare parts) to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported as part of the supply, i.e., shipped by the supplier from Canada to a destination outside Canada and exported as soon as is reasonable after the sale (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1; QST mirror in the Quebec Taxation Act). Services supplied to non-residents, such as remote tuning or consulting, may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 7 provided the service is not performed on goods located in Canada and the recipient is outside Canada. The sale must be reported in the GST/QST net tax calculation as zero-rated, preserving entitlement to ITCs/RTDs on all Canadian input costs including carbon-fiber components, brushless motors, milling costs, warehouse, and freight. Zero-rating is denied if the goods are sold to a Canadian resident, delivered in Canada, or accompanied by insufficient export evidence.

  • Commercial invoice showing seller BN/RT and QST numbers, Incoterms, destination country, and 0% export indication
  • CBSA e-manifest or export record (a formal CAED export declaration is required only for controlled or permit goods; hobby drones are generally CAED-exempt, but the courier or forwarder's export manifest must identify the shipment as a Canadian-origin export)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note showing pickup in Canada and delivery outside Canada (e.g., DHL, FedEx, UPS)
  • Foreign customs entry or delivery proof (e.g., US CBP entry summary, postal or courier tracking showing delivered status abroad)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or PayPal/Stripe settlement statements evidencing receipt of the foreign consideration
  • Purchase order, contract, and correspondence identifying the non-resident buyer's registered address
  • T5013 partnership return and GST/QST working papers retained for the six-year CRA/RQ record period

Canada and Quebec do not impose mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Requirements: (i) every GST/HST/QST invoice must state the supplier's BN and GST/HST registration number (and QST number where applicable), the invoice date, the description of goods, the consideration, and the amount of tax payable or a 0% export indication; (ii) invoices issued by a Quebec supplier must be drawn up in French under the Charter of the French Language, whether paper or electronic; (iii) e-invoices are accepted if kept in a readable format for six years and exchanged with the recipient's consent; (iv) CRA and RQ accept XML/EDI remittance, GST/HST NETFILE, and RQ e-Services; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) and PEPPOL interoperability are being adopted for B2B e-invoicing but are not yet mandatory; (v) no certified fiscal-device or API-clearance requirement applies to e-commerce sales processed via Stripe or PayPal, but gateway receipts must display the registration numbers and applicable taxes when GST/QST is collected.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline on export proceeds; the 180-day convention reflects the CRA/RQ audit-evidence window, under which payment should be received and the corresponding inward remittance document obtained within approximately six months of shipment to substantiate zero-rating. On audit, CRA and Revenu Québec may disallow zero-rating for deficient documentation and reassess the supplies as taxable at the combined 14.975% rate, claw back the export-related ITCs/RTDs previously claimed, and charge prescribed daily compound interest (2025 prescribed rate approximately 8%) plus penalties: (i) false statement or omission penalty under ETA s. 280.1 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 5% of the net tax sought to be evaded, with an equivalent QST penalty under the Quebec Tax Administration Act; (ii) gross-negligence fines and imprisonment for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded; (iii) unlimited reassessment periods where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud or wilful default; (iv) Revenu Québec may suspend or revoke the LP's QST registration; and (v) the general partner(s) of the LP are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/QST, interest, and penalties under ETA s. 323 and the parallel Quebec provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a regime of full current account convertibility administered by the Bank of Canada with no quantitative restrictions on repatriation of profits earned by limited partnerships. Capital account transactions remain subject to prior approval thresholds for portfolio investments exceeding CAD 5 million per counterparty; operational FX needs for trade finance and hedging fall outside these controls. Profit remittance is unrestricted once tax filings are complete and beneficial ownership is verified, subject only to standard AML screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists for surrender of export proceeds. Receipts from international sales of FPV drone components and accessories are freely convertible and repatriable without mandated conversion ratios or timelines. Export declarations under the Export and Import Permits Act are required only where controlled items are involved; retail hobby-grade drone transactions trigger no such obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
