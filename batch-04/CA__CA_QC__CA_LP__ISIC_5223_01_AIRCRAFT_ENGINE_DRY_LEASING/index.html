Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA section 96 and the Quebec Taxation Act, so no entity-level corporate income tax is imposed; the Partnership is separately registered and liable for GST/HST and QST in respect of supplies made in the course of its commercial activities; non-resident limited partners are taxable on their allocated share of Canadian-source business income and must file or elect under the applicable treaty provisions; no tax-exempt zone regime applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canadian law; entity classification follows the legal form: an LP is a transparent partnership unless it is a public SIFT partnership taxed as a corporation. Available elections include: (i) a fiscal-period election for the partnership (ITA section 249.2) to adopt a common year for all members; (ii) an election under ITA section 216(4) permitting a non-resident partner to file a Canadian return on net taxable Canadian business income; and (iii) elective and mandatory neutralization rules under the hybrid-mismatch provisions (ITA sections 18.2 and 18.4) which operate without a general opt-out; no partnership classification election is available for indirect tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the small-supplier threshold measured on taxable supplies made worldwide (in a calendar quarter and the preceding three calendar quarters) for both GST/HST and QST; aircraft-engine leasing receivables will normally exceed this threshold, making registration mandatory; a non-resident lessor with a Quebec permanent establishment is also subject to mandatory registration irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A dry lease of a turbofan aircraft engine qualifies as a zero-rated supply of goods when the engine is, under the terms of the lease, exported from Canada and used primarily outside Canada (ETA section 23(1)(c)); the engine must be made available to a non-resident lessee for use principally on international routes or in a non-Canadian engine pool. An alternative ground is the zero-rating for aircraft and aircraft-related supplies under Schedule VI Part VII of the ETA where the supply is made to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST. Quebec TVQ applies a parallel export test: the movable property must be physically removed from Quebec or used primarily outside Quebec by a person who is not a Quebec resident, subject to the LTVQ zero-rating provisions. The supply must not involve any significant domestic use in Canada; routine ferry flights, Canadian stopovers, or installation on a Canadian-registered aircraft will defeat zero-rating.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A/CAED or successor electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System or the CBSA GCA portal), including the engine serial number, value, and HS classification
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing carriage from Canada to the foreign lessee or foreign engine pool, naming the non-resident lessee as consignee
  • Commercial invoice with GST/QST registration numbers and a clear zero-rating statement referencing ETA section 23(1)(c) and the corresponding QST provision
  • Signed dry-lease agreement containing the export covenant, primary-use-outside-Canada clause, and engine return/redelivery terms
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance: SWIFT MT103, escrow release statement, or bank credit advice demonstrating remittance of lease rent from the non-resident lessee
  • Where applicable, ATA Carnet, temporary export permit, or CBSA/customs authorization for engines exported for overhaul, repair, or exchange prior to redelivery to the foreign lessee

No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no digital stamping or tax-authority API validation exists in Canada or Quebec. Suppliers must issue compliant tax invoices containing the GST/QST registration number, transaction date, description, and tax amounts (ETA section 221 and Quebec fiscal law); electronic invoices in EDI/XML or PEPPOL formats are permitted with recipient consent; GST/HST and QST returns and remittances must be filed electronically by larger registrants, and Revenu Québec mandates electronic filing for QST registrants meeting the prescribed turnover threshold.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit export zero-rating claims under the Export Monitoring and Post-Processing Verification programs; if the engine is not physically exported or is used primarily in Canada, the zero-rated treatment is reversed and the lease is reassessed at 14.5% GST/QST plus arrears interest; input tax credits/input tax refunds are clawed back with a four-year normal reassessment period, extended to ten years in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Civil penalties apply up to 25% of the tax or refund misstated, and the GAAR (ITA section 245 / Quebec Tax Administration Act) may recharacterize artificial structures, including circular engine-exchange and escrow arrangements; criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act can result in fines and imprisonment for willful evasion or false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1952, covering both current-account and capital-account transactions with no exchange-control restrictions. There are no statutory convertibility limits, no profit-remittance permits, and no treasury-style investment licensing for a passive aircraft-engine leasing LP. The only genuine deterrents are tax-driven: Part XIII withholding tax on outbound rents, interest, dividends, and royalties (generally reduced under the Canada-US treaty) and compliance with Form T106 and SEMA/OFAC-screening rules where the Quebec LP is foreign-controlled. The CAD floats freely; the LP may hold and settle in CAD, USD, EUR, or any other currency without central-bank consent and may fund its operations through inbound sponsor equity or non-resident partner advances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, surrender percentages, repatriation windows, or holding-period rules on export proceeds, aircraft-engine lease rentals, or trade receivables. A Quebec LP may retain USD earnings onshore or offshore indefinitely. The only operational layer is AML/ATF: banks report to FINTRAC all electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour window and must screen each transaction against the Special Economic Measures Act and equivalent UN, OFAC, and EU sanctions lists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) issues the Canadian dollar as legal tender under the Currency Act and conducts monetary policy; prudential regulation of federally chartered deposit-taking institutions rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML supervision with FINTRAC. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange-control board, no central-bank FX licensing, and no prior-approval requirement for cross-border capital flows; the Bank of Canada may intervene only in exceptional disorderly-market conditions. Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value settlement system for CAD finality.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium but constrained. Canada does not issue EU-style EMI licences. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully provide multi-currency conversion and payment-initiation services only after FINTRAC registration as Money Services Businesses and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act is fully in force, authorization as PSPs under Payments Canada supervision. They cannot hold or segregate aircraft-engine security deposits as escrow agent for a Quebec LP without a Canadian trust-company or Schedule I banking licence; such deposits must sit at a CDIC-member Canadian bank or licensed trust company to satisfy Civil Code of Quebec obligations and aviation counterparty credit requirements. Recommended structure: a Canadian Tier-1 bank as settlement anchor and escrow holder, with the fintech/EMI used only as a secondary low-value disbursement corridor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control permit is required. Sponsor equity should be routed by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 with MT103 cover, in CAD or USD, directly to the LP's operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, with a descriptive payment narrative stating 'capital contribution — Quebec limited partnership — aircraft-engine leasing'. USD wires from a US tax-equity sponsor should clear through the Canadian bank's US affiliate (BMO Harris, TD Bank NA, CIBC US) for same-day intra-bank availability; CAD legs settle through Payments Canada's Lynx system with Bank of Canada finality. Escrow security deposits must be wired to the segregated escrow account with the explicit reference 'security deposit — aircraft-engine lease agreement'.
