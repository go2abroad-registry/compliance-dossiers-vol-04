Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership (Civil Code of Québec, arts. 2242-2252). Contributions may be in money, property or services as agreed in the partnership contract; no mandatory escrow, capital deposit or bank guarantee is required for a non-licensed mobile software activity conducted under NAICS 511210.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: occupancy and safety compliance certificated against the Quebec Building Code and Safety Code (Régie du bâtiment du Québec, RBQ), including smoke detection, fire extinguishers and unobstructed exit paths. Structural clearance: RBQ or municipal inspection required if office load or occupancy exceeds design parameters. Occupational clearance: registration of the establishment with the CNESST, formation of a health and safety program for workplaces with 20 or more workers, first-aid kit provisioning, and a workplace injury prevention notice under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. S-2.1).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership (Civil Code of Québec, arts. 2242-2252). Contributions may be in money, property or services as agreed in the partnership contract; no mandatory escrow, capital deposit or bank guarantee is required for a non-licensed mobile software activity conducted under NAICS 511210.

Category: None — mobile utility application development and distribution is an unlicensed digital service activity in Quebec; the only possible physical requirement is a standard municipal commercial occupancy permit (certificat d'occupation) or zoning clearance for an office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - 'My Business Account' (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) for federal GST/HST and income tax registration; Revenu Québec - 'CLIC' portal (revenuquebec.ca) for QST, source deductions, and provincial income tax. The Registraire des entreprises du Québec (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca) issues the provincial enterprise registration number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership with non-resident limited partners: (1) determine whether the LP carries on business or has an establishment in Canada/Quebec; (2) file the limited partnership declaration with the Registraire des entreprises and obtain the NEQ; (3) apply to CRA via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account, naming the general partner or designated agent as authorized representative; (4) register the QST account with Revenu Québec online or via Form LM-3-V; (5) appoint an agent for service in Quebec if so required. No statutory minimum capital is required; non-resident ownership is permitted.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax; each partner reports its proportionate share. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership itself is a registrant/person capable of collecting tax and claiming input tax credits. — For a Quebec limited partnership with non-resident limited partners: (1) determine whether the LP carries on business or has an establishment in Canada/Quebec; (2) file the limited partnership declaration with the Registraire des entreprises and obtain the NEQ; (3) apply to CRA via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account, naming the general partner or designated agent as authorized representative; (4) register the QST account with Revenu Québec online or via Form LM-3-V; (5) appoint an agent for service in Quebec if so required. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax; each partner reports its proportionate share. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership itself is a registrant/person capable of collecting tax and claiming input tax credits.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Available elections include: (i) alternative fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4); (ii) voluntary GST/HST/QST registration election for small suppliers; (iii) partnership agent election for administrative filings. Hybrid mismatch provisions (ITA ss. 12.1-12.16) reclassify certain dual-status arrangements but cannot elect a limited partnership into a taxable entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold), measured on worldwide taxable supplies over the current calendar quarter and immediately preceding four calendar quarters; the threshold does not apply where the supply is deemed by law to be made by an online distribution platform operator (Apple/Google).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of software (intangible personal property) and related digital services made to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Canada under Schedule VI Part V (GST/HST) and the Quebec Sales Tax Act. For online app store sales, the marketplace operator may be deemed supplier; direct sales to business or consumers are zero-rated only if the LP retains contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-residence, the app download jurisdiction is not Canada, the user's location and payment instrument are non-Canadian, and the place of use is outside Canada. Supplies deemed made in Canada are taxable at 14.975% combined.

  • App Store Connect/Google Play Console analytical sales and settlement reports identifying storefront, transaction currency, and Apple/Google commission
  • Contracts or platform terms with Apple/Google showing the LP as principal or supplier of the app and the platform as distribution agent
  • Digital sales ledger recording each transaction's IP country, device country, bank card country, and storefront code
  • Bank statements and remittance advices showing the subscription/boxed compensation from Apple/Google received in Canada
  • GST/QST export reconciliation worksheet listing the zero-rated supplies and the corresponding Schedule VI remission basis
  • Where tangible media is involved, a customs export declaration and bill of lading; for pure downloads this is not applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper and electronic invoices; registered suppliers must show their BN, tax rate, and tax amount. E-invoices may be issued without prior authorization, but records must be retained for at least six years and a complete audit trail must be available in an electronically readable form.

CRA/Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the normal limitation period in cases of neglect or fraud. Falsely zero-rating domestic supplies triggers retroactive GST/QST collection, interest, and penalties: ETA s. 285 gross negligence penalty up to 25% of the tax that should have been collected; ITA s. 163(2) false statements penalty up to 50% of the related income tax understated. Revenu Québec imposes equivalent penalties under the Tax Administration Act, and criminal prosecution applies in egregious cases of tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payments, royalties, loan repayments, and capital repatriation are unrestricted from a foreign exchange standpoint; however, non-resident recipients may be subject to withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and any applicable treaty. No ministerial or central bank pre-approval is required for cross-border transfers of funds into or out of Quebec.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for receipt or surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may retain payments in foreign currency, maintain foreign-denominated bank accounts domestically or offshore, and are not required to convert such receipts into Canadian dollars. FINTRAC large transaction reporting applies to cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000, but no exchange conversion requirement exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada; federal foreign exchange regulation is administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with prudential supervision of Schedule I banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/CFT reporting supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). There is no dedicated Canadian foreign exchange control administration equivalent to an exchange authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) licensed in the EU or UK may offer FX conversion, payment aggregation, and card-wallet settlement in CAD through a sponsored partner arrangement with a Canadian Schedule I bank, but it cannot lawfully take retail deposits or provide full current-account banking services for a Quebec limited partnership. Regulatory constraints include the Bank Act deposit-taking prohibition, Quebec-specific collection and money-services licensing under provincial law, FINTRAC MSB registration, and OSFI guidance on outsourcing. A cross-border EMI may be used solely as an ancillary payment rail, not as the primary account-holding bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions are routed from the remitting partner’s bank via SWIFT MT103 to the LP’s CAD settlement account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire should include the partnership’s REQ registration number and the unique reference corresponding to the subscription agreement. Under Statistics Canada’s international transactions reporting, use the applicable balance-of-payments code for direct investment capital injection or other investment liabilities; Canadian banks will apply the appropriate code, such as 'D5 — Other investment — liabilities — capital contributions' or 'P6 — Capital injection into a resident enterprise'. For receipts equal to or above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC large cash transaction reporting rules do not apply to wire transfers, but the bank will apply its own transaction-monitoring thresholds and may request a purpose-of-payment declaration.Outbound trade payments are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 for vendor invoices, software licensing fees, and cloud infrastructure costs. For high-value supply contracts, a commercial letter of credit — typically an irrevocable, confirmed LC drawn under UCP600 — provides safer clearing and protects against documentary non-compliance. Domestic Canadian disbursements use the national EFT clearing system, while cross-border USD payments move through Fedwire via correspondent banks. In-app purchase commission flows to Apple and Google are cleared through the platform-side payment processors and settled back to the LP’s operating account without a separate FX conversion.Deliverable FX forward contracts (including CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) with authorized Canadian Schedule I or II banksCross-currency swaps (XCCY swaps) to manage structural balance-sheet FX exposure between CAD and operational foreign currenciesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to prohibited or restricted-convertibility jurisdictions where domestic forward hedging is impractical
