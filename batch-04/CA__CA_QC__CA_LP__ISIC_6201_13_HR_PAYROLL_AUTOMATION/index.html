Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Quebec limited partnership. A limited partner's contribution must consist of money or property; services are not a valid contribution under article 2236 CcQ. No statutory escrow account is required for cash or property contributions, but the general partner should record unpaid subscriptions as receivables and obtain written acknowledgement of receipt.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a municipal certificate of occupancy for the registered office. Install fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and clear evacuation routes in accordance with the Quebec Fire Safety Code. Register with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) as an employer, maintain a workplace first-aid program, and confirm structural load limits before storing heavy IT equipment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online and My Business Account portals. Provincial: Revenu Québec, clic Revenu portal, and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ registry). GST/HST is administered federally under the Excise Tax Act; in Québec the GST is collected by Revenu Québec on behalf of CRA, and the Québec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) is administered by Revenu Québec under the Act respecting the Québec sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership (or extra-provincial foreign LP) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises to obtain the Québec enterprise number (NEQ). Step 2: Register with CRA through Business Registration Online for a Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll deductions), RC (Part I income tax where applicable), and a T5013 partnership-information-return account. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec for QST, QPIP, and health-services-fund (HSF) program accounts linked to the NEQ, and file the partnership declaration via clic Revenu. Step 4: For non-resident partners, complete CRA Forms NR601/NR604 and implement Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source partnership allocations, or confirm the partner's Part I return obligations. Step 5: Maintain separate BN program accounts for any partner that itself is a registrant; the LP's GST/HST and QST registrations stand in the partnership's own name and NEQ.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s. 96 and the Québec Taxation Act, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it computes income, gains, losses, and credits as a notional taxpayer but is not itself taxable; allocations flow to partners under the partnership agreement. If all partners are resident in Canada throughout the year, the LP is a 'Canadian partnership'; otherwise it is a 'non-Canadian partnership' (ITA s. 102), triggering special T5013 reporting, Part XIII withholding for non-resident partners, and potential deemed permanent-establishment attribution. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s. 96 and the Québec Taxation Act, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it computes income, gains, losses, and credits as a notional taxpayer but is not itself taxable; allocations flow to partners under the partnership agreement. If all partners are resident in Canada throughout the year, the LP is a 'Canadian partnership'; otherwise it is a 'non-Canadian partnership' (ITA s. 102), triggering special T5013 reporting, Part XIII withholding for non-resident partners, and potential deemed permanent-establishment attribution. For GST/QST purposes the partnership is deemed a distinct person (Excise Tax Act s. 272.1 and QST mirror) and registers, collects, and remits in its own right.

Available election forms

Canada has no elective check-the-box regime; classification follows legal form under the Civil Code of Québec and, for foreign entities, CRA administrative practice. Available elections: (i) joint nil-consideration election for closely related registered persons (Excise Tax Act s. 156, mirrored for QST) for intra-group GST/QST neutrality; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election on the T5013 return (ITA s. 96(3); Québec mirror); (iii) for non-resident partners, the ITA s. 216 election to file a Part I return on net Canadian investment/rental income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (iv) hybrid-mismatch ordering and deeming rules enacted under the 2019-2021 federal budgets operate mandatorily and cannot be elected away; parties should structure governing-law classification to avoid tripping the mismatch rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control licensing, no repatriation permit, and no conversion quota for a Quebec limited partnership (LP). Inbound capital contributions and outbound profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are freely executable via SWIFT. The only material hurdles are regulatory and tax compliance: (i) financial institutions must report to FINTRAC all international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the LP must maintain accurate beneficial-ownership registers to satisfy KYC; (iii) payments of a Canadian-source nature to non-resident partners may trigger Part XIII withholding tax or Part I taxation, subject to income-tax treaty relief (e.g., Canada-US Treaty); (iv) capital account transactions are subject to Investment Canada Act (ICA) notification/review only if the non-resident investor acquires control of a Canadian business above applicable thresholds, which is not expected for passive LP contributions; (v) aggregate foreign property held by the LP above CAD 100,000 must be reported to the CRA on Form T1135.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation deadline, or foreign-currency holding limit on export proceeds. A Canadian LP may retain export receipts in any freely convertible currency. Nevertheless, if export-related payments are received via international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, the LP's bank must report the transaction to FINTRAC, and the LP must produce underlying commercial documentation (invoices, contracts, shipping or service-delivery evidence) upon request. For the payroll automation software scope, overseas subscription/license receipts denominated in USD, EUR, or GBP are treated as service export proceeds and are freely disposable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority, currency issuer, and supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML and cross-border EFT surveillance by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Quebec-level money-services-business registration and supervision by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for ancillary payout or receive-only rails; Low for primary capital custody. Legal constraints: Under the Bank Act (Canada), an offshore EMI is not authorized to take deposits or maintain CAD demand accounts in Canada; it must clear through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a trust company. The RPAA requires a payment service provider carrying on business in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada; an offshore EMI may face FINTRAC MSB registration obligations for performing cross-border EFTs, and Quebec's AMF may require registration under the Money-Services Businesses Act. Therefore the LP should use (i) a Tier-1 Canadian bank for operating/capital accounts, and (ii) a licensed Canadian payment processor or a partnered EMI (e.g., Wise Platform via a Canadian railing bank) exclusively for mass payroll disbursements. The LP itself, as a B2B software vendor not holding client funds or executing EFTs, does not require RPAA registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the injection via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident partner's bank to the LP's USD or CAD account held at a Tier-1 Canadian bank, with field 70 (Remittance Information) clearly stating 'LP capital contribution' and referencing the executed contribution resolution of the general partner. The bank will expect: (i) the certified partnership agreement showing the partner's entitlement and the capital call; (ii) source-of-funds documentation for the remitting partner; (iii) confirmation that the contribution is equity, not a loan, unless a shareholder/partner loan is separately syndicated; (iv) for amounts exceeding relevant thresholds, an Investment Canada Act brief confirming exemption or notification status; and (v) a 'specified foreign property' reporting note for CRA Form T1135 if the LP holds foreign funds or receivables. For large cross-border transfers, the bank may require an advance notification and a FINTRAC-validated purpose narrative; use the correct 'Investment in Canadian enterprise' economic code in the bank's internal purpose map (e.g., Statistics Canada Balance of Payments classification of direct investment) to avoid false ML-typology flags.For payroll automation and SaaS-associated outflows: (i) contractor, licence-fee, and vendor payments in foreign currency settle via SWIFT MT103, with adequate commercial invoices and any required Part XIII withholding (e.g., Canadian royalties to non-residents) documented in the payment narrative; Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (federal 11.5% + Quebec 13%; adjusted for provincial abatement) for general corporate income; for a limited partnership, income is flow-through to partners and taxed at each partner's applicable marginal rate (federal and Quebec combined).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividend-like distributions from a corporation: 25% federal Part XIII; treaty reduced rates. For LP distributions to non-resident partners constituting business income, no separate dividend WHT; income taxed under Part I at progressive rates, with Part XIII withholding if amounts represent fees, interest, rental, or deemed partnership distribution taxable under Part XIII.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation if the gross value of a non-arm's-length transaction exceeds CAD $1 million (goods, services, intangibles) or CAD $5 million (loans); Master File / CBCR thresholds: consolidated group revenue over CAD $1 billion (Master File) and CAD $850 million (CBCR) threshold. Quebec does not impose separate thresholds; federal s.247 applies.

Economic substance scope: Canada imposes a general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA) denying benefits from 'offensive' transactions without economic substance. CRA also attacks shell structures under SAAR (anti-avoidance for treaty shopping) and transfer pricing recharacterization. The Quebec LP must be actively managed in Quebec, have a real general partner with entrepreneurial capacity, maintain adequate economic substance (employees, premises, bank accounts, decision-making records), and avoid artificial diversion of Canadian SaaS profits to tax-haven partners. CRA may reallocate income to persons whose activities are not adequately compensated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 100% of unremitted tax; Quebec also imposes civil penalties under Tax Administration Act (up to CAD $5,000 plus double deficiency).

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be assessed for unpaid taxes remitted from employees (payroll source deductions); under s.227.1 ITA, they are personally liable if wages were 20 days overdue; CRA may seize personal assets, impose travel bans through court orders, and seek criminal indictments for officers who 'knowingly participate' in evasion.
Transfer Pricing Penalty and Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) adjustment; 10% penalty on net adjustment (maximum potential); 5% to 10% of gross adjustment where documentation absent; CRA can recharacterize transaction and impose interest on underpaid instalments; legal defense costs non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors can be liable under s.247(8) and equitable fraud if they directed an artificial arrangement, especially if they signed a false compliance certificate; personal restitution in cases of deliberate undervaluation of Canadian services.
Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies tax benefit (Part IVA income inclusion); s.245 penalty up to 25% of denied benefit for affected transactions; Quebec also invokes anti-avoidance penalties; CRA may reassess and impose gross-negligence penalty plus interest.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise if the LP general partner is insolvent and fails to exercise due care in business decisions; courts could pierce the veil where LP is engaged as a sham; directors may be subject to a civil restitution order and travel restrictions while a tax fraud investigation is ongoing.
AML / Money Laundering and FINTRAC (Proceeds of Crime)

Statutory sanction: Unregistered MSB: administrative monetary penalty up to CAD $500,000 for corporation and CAD $100,000 for individual; criminal money laundering (section 462.31 Criminal Code): up to 10 years imprisonment; FINTRAC may impose compliance penalties up to CAD $10 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they directed or acquiesced in laundering; personal financial penalties under PCMLTFA; travel bans can be imposed to prevent flight; directors also exposed to charges of 'structuring' payroll payments to evade reporting.
Source Deduction and Payroll Liabilities

Statutory sanction: Under s.227 ITA: failure to withhold is a debt to Crown equal to withholdable amounts; 10% penalty (up to 20%) for repeated failure; directors liable personally for unpaid source deductions; Quebec penalties up to 25% of unremitted amounts for wilful omission.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for source deductions and GST/QST under s.323; travel bans may be issued if director leaves Canada with outstanding liability exceeding CAD $50,000; criminal charges for unlawful deduction from wages and conversion are possible.
Non-Resident WHT Failure

Statutory sanction: Unremitted Part XIII tax be assessable against the partnership as agent for non-resident persons, plus interest, penalties, 50% gross negligence if deliberate; NR4 non-filing: CAD $2,500 summary penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be personally liable for failure to withhold (fiduciary duty); travel bans via CRA collection if debt unpaid; personal restitution if the LP is wound up with unpaid withholding.
GST/HST and QST Compliance

Statutory sanction: Unassessed GST/HST: s.323 director liability; failure to register penalties up to 10% of unreported net tax; QST penalty under Quebec Excise Act, similar director liability; interest compounded daily on unreported tax.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid GST/HST/QST where the corporation/LP has not paid, if they were directors at time liability arose, with no requirement of fault; includes QST amounts from digital services; enforcement via seizure of personal assets, travel bans and bankruptcy remedies.
Cross-Border E-Commerce / Data Privacy (PIPEDA & Copyright)

Statutory sanction: PIPEDA damage awards (administrative penalties up to CAD $100,000 in private-sector actions; higher for Quebec public-sector); Copyright Act: statutory damages up to CAD $150,000 per work; also Criminal Code offences for unauthorized renewable use.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally responsible for data breach under Quebec Law 25 (CAD $100 million max fine or 3% global revenue); personal tort liability where they directed unlawful handling of personal data; criminal liability for inducing breaches.
