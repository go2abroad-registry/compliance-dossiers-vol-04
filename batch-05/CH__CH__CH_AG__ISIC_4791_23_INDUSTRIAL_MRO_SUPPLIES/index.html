Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Monbijoustrasse 40, 3003 Bern; e-portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT e-filing via 'SimplyTax' portal; business identification number verification via www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss resident Aktiengesellschaft must obtain a Swiss Business Identifier (UID; format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) from the commercial register, and be automatically issued the UID; VAT registration with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration must be filed through Form 301 / e-portal when the company's taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 net per year, or voluntarily where only non-taxable turnover is generated. A foreign entity without a Swiss place of business that has Swiss taxable supplies must also apply for UID/VAT registration directly with ESTV, appoint a Swiss fiscal representative if required, and provide articles of association, foreign tax registration evidence, and expected Swiss turnover. For the liable Swiss export business, registration must be completed before incurring Swiss VAT obligations; issuance of the UID-MWST / UID-IVA certificate is immediate upon approved filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss AGs are opaque corporate taxpayers subject to federal, cantonal, and municipal income tax (aggregate ordinary rate approximately 11.9% to 21%; holding/mixed-status regimes provide partial relief). No check-the-box election exists under Swiss law. Available elections include: VAT group consolidation (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) for closely-linked legal persons; simplified percentage-rating election for partial input VAT deduction; accommodation or low-value asset disposal flat-rate options; hybrid mismatch neutralisation rules effective 1 January 2020 limiting deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; and qualifying group taxation election for direct federal tax purposes where a parent holds at least 99% of voting capital shares.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover: for Swiss-resident suppliers, the small-business exemption applies where domestic taxable turnover (net of input tax) remains below CHF 100,000; for foreign suppliers without a Swiss place of business, mandatory registration is triggered where global taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 and the supplies are otherwise taxable in Switzerland (services used or enjoyed in CH, or distance-selling of goods into Switzerland).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports under Art. 23 of the Swiss VAT Act applies to physical goods that are exported from Switzerland to an overseas recipient or to a customs warehouse, including goods sold through the electronic MRO catalog where the supplier or a logistics carrier physically dispatches the goods abroad. Services directly connected to the exported goods (international transport, freight forwarding, handling) and services supplied to an entrepreneur abroad who uses them outside Switzerland are generally outside the scope of Swiss VAT. The zero rate is claimable only if an official customs export proof demonstrates that the goods irrevocably left Switzerland, with commercial terms, destination and risk transfer consistent with export status.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged via e-dec/NCTS or equivalent electronic export notification, showing the exporter, consignee, goods classification, net value, and customs clearance stamp
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical export
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank credit statement confirming settlement of foreign-currency proceeds (where export payment is received in FX)
  • Transport insurance documents, delivery receipts, and Incoterms-based proof of foreign destination

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based BIS reporting system. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 format; Swiss QR-billing (QR invoice) is mandatory for structured paper invoices. Federal procurement requires electronic invoices (B2G). VAT returns and Annexes A/B/C must be filed electronically via the ESTV 'SimplyTax' web portal; invoices and supporting export documents must be retained for 10 years for fiscal control.

ESTV applies strict documentary scrutiny to zero-rated export supplies; export vouchers must be retained for 10 years. Missing or false export substantiation triggers retrospective VAT assessment with a 10-year limitation period, default interest (normally 4% per annum), and penalties: intentional tax evasion carries a fine of up to 400% of the evaded tax, negligent evasion up to 100%; fabrication of export documents or circular/fictitious export schemes constitutes criminal tax fraud. Additionally, input VAT deductions are clawed back and affected invoices are invalidated. Switzerland does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for VAT zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark is used as documentary repatriation diligence and for internal audit evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully convertible hard-currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no mandatory centralised approval for cross-border payments, and no profit remittance restrictions for the current account or for direct investment income. Dividends and branch profits of a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) may be freely remitted abroad, subject only to the standard 35% Swiss federal withholding tax on dividends if the payment is deemed to have a Swiss source; the withholding tax can be reduced or avoided where a tax treaty or the Swiss participation relief applies. Capital repatriation on liquidation or capital redemption likewise faces no convertibility hurdle. The only practical constraints arise from AML/KYC documentation, the Swiss stamp duty on Swiss securities account turnover, and the SNB's Balance of Payments statistical reporting triggers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or forced conversion obligation exists. Swiss exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in any currency and hold them offshore or onshore. There is no timeline within which receipts must be converted into CHF, and no mandatory conversion ratio. The only compliance requirement is statistical: a Swiss-resident enterprise is required to report to the Swiss National Bank, under the SNB's Ordinance on the Collection of Statistical Data on Payments with Foreign Countries and on Claims and Liabilities to Foreign Countries, if cross-border payment turnover with foreign counterparties exceeds CHF 10 million annually. Reporting is aggregated by category and is not a permission process. Export documents must comply with the Federal Customs Administration's electronic export declaration system (NCTS) for Swiss exports; customs documents are not tied to FX conversion.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), Amtshaus Börsenstrasse, Zurich; together with the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) and the Federal Customs Administration (FCA) for trade payment statistics and export declarations; exchange controls are administered by the SNB within the framework of Switzerland's liberal capital account regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for a regulated Swiss operational treasury account. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g. neobank or electronic money institution licensed in the EU/UK) do not generally hold a Swiss banking licence under Art. 1 of the Swiss Banking Act and cannot legally provide deposit-taking or payment services to a Swiss AG as a substitute for a licensed Swiss bank. A CHF settlement account must reside with a Swiss-regulated bank or a FINMA-licensed FinTech bank; however, a Swiss AG may use offshore EMIs as auxiliary disbursement rails for non-CHF supplier payments provided (i) the account is clearly sub-contracted and not the primary treasury account, (ii) the EMI does not hold funds in Switzerland on the Swiss entity's behalf in an unauthorised manner, and (iii) the head office bank grants a written crypto/liquidity-neutral consent. FINMA's FinTech licence allows deposit-taking up to CHF 1 million without interest, but it remains a Swiss-regulated entity, not an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For any capital contribution from the non-resident parent to the Swiss AG, use a SWIFT MT103 in CHF/EUR/USD to the Swiss AG's preferred Tier-1 bank account. The wire must contain the business purpose narration precisely: 'Non-resident parent direct investment capital contribution to Swiss subsidiary; BOP direction: inbound; purpose code D/CAP (Direct Investment Equity Capital)' to avoid misclassification by the Swiss National Bank's statistics. The amount must be credited to a designated capital reserve account or a free capital account and posted in the AG's books against a contribution reserve, with the bank receiving the SWIFT confirmation. If the bank queries the source of wealth, provide the same evidence bundle as in the AML blockage defence. The transaction is reportable to the SNB in the quarterly balance-of-payments report if the entity exceeds the reporting threshold. There is no mandatory conversion or repatriation requirement; the funds can remain in EUR until the company's treasury policy converts to CHF via a standard market rate spot or forward transaction.Supplier settlements for industrial MRO supplies must be routed through the primary cleared rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments to EU and global suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT700 for irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) where purchases exceed CHF 500,000 or require customs-backed security; (iii) SEPA CT in EUR when the supplier is EU/EEA-based, using the Swiss SEPA channel through a Swiss correspondent; (iv) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for urgent CHF domestic payments; and (v) the RTGS/CHATS US-dollar correspondent for USD settlements after FX conversion. Every outward payment must carry a valid foreign-trade purpose code: goods purchases (PUR), industrial supplies (MRO), or service fees (SRV), as required by the SNB statistical ordinance. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% of net profit (effective federal rate approximately 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal and communal headline rate ranges from approximately 11.9% to 21.0% depending on the canton.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Swiss AG, withheld at source; treaty relief for qualifying non-resident shareholders is implemented mainly by refund after withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual tax return is due within the canton's prescribed period after the statutory fiscal year end; for a calendar-year taxpayer this is normally 30 June of the following year. Extensions are commonly granted to 30 September or 31 December upon request.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1a / Box 1: Net profit before tax (Reingewinn / bénéfice net) Enter the net profit per the Swiss statutory annual financial statements prepared under the Swiss Code of Obligations, before appropriations to reserves and before dividend distributions. The Swiss AG is the taxpayer and files the return. A non-resident shareholder does not report the Swiss AG's profit; only a foreign company with a Swiss permanent establishment is required to file a separate Swiss return for the PE-attributable profit. Late or incomplete filing triggers a reminder and an administrative default penalty (Ordnungsbusse) of up to CHF 10,000 under federal direct tax law, plus cantonal procedural surcharges. Late-payment interest accrues at statutory rates of approximately 3% to 5% per annum. Knowingly or negligently filing false returns gives rise to an evasion penalty of up to three times the evaded tax, in addition to reassessment and interest.
Line 1b / Box 2: Tax adjustments for non-deductible expenses and hidden distributions Add back non-deductible expense items such as corporate income taxes, excessive management and royalty fees, non-depreciated disallowed construction costs, private portions, and hidden value transfers to shareholders. Adjustments are made in the Swiss AG's return. Non-resident shareholders should not amend the AG's filing; any constructive dividend resulting from excessive payments is taken into account for Swiss withholding tax purposes. Late or incomplete filing triggers a reminder and an administrative default penalty (Ordnungsbusse) of up to CHF 10,000 under federal direct tax law, plus cantonal procedural surcharges. These reliefs are available only to the Swiss resident AG and are not transferable to a foreign parent. The foreign parent's domestic tax treatment of Swiss-source dividends is governed by its residence country. Late or incomplete filing triggers a reminder and an administrative default penalty (Ordnungsbusse) of up to CHF 10,000 under federal direct tax law, plus cantonal procedural surcharges. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss transfer pricing documentation duty under Art. 174a DBG applies when annual gross turnover reaches at least CHF 10 million and the taxpayer has cross-border transactions with related parties. In practice, a Local File is expected for related-party transactions exceeding approximately CHF 1 million; a Master File is generally expected for group consolidated revenue above CHF 100 million under Federal Tax Administration guidance.
