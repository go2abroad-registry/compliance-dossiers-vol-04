Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax authority and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; co-administration with Revenu Quebec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec corporate income tax and provincial payroll obligations under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement. The CRA Business Number (BN) is the master federal tax identifier; the QST account (BN suffix 'RM') is the Quebec value-added tax identifier.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Quebec must (1) obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec; (2) apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) with the RC (Part I corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST) and RP (payroll) program accounts through BRO or Form RC1 (online issuance is immediate); (3) register for QST with Revenu Quebec (same BN with 'RM' program code; online via 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' or Form LM-1-V) before commencing taxable supplies in Quebec; and (4) if the corporation is a non-resident registrant, appoint a representative for service in Canada under Excise Tax Act section 299. GST/HST and QST registration becomes mandatory immediately once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold; below the threshold voluntary registration is recommended to recover input tax credits (ITCs) on media inventory, SaaS tools and agency operating costs. CRA and Revenu Quebec exchange registration data automatically, so a single BN application satisfies both authorities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate legal taxpayer: Part I federal corporate income tax plus Quebec Part I corporate tax are assessed on the corporation itself (Income Tax Act, section 15; Quebec Taxation Act), with no pass-through of income to shareholders. Dividend distributions trigger shareholder-level taxation with integration relief (dividend tax credit / refundable dividend tax on hand). Provincial allocation follows the permanent establishment formula via the T2 return; residency is governed by central management and control; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax, potentially treaty-reduced.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime; legal-form corporations are automatically taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections include: (a) the GST/HST joint election for closely related corporations (Excise Tax Act, section 156) and its mirror QST election, which deems qualifying intercompany supplies to be made for nil consideration; (b) the capital dividend account election (Form T2054, ITA section 83(2)); (c) the section 216 election replacing Part XIII withholding with a net Canadian return for certain rental and royalty-type income; (d) associated-corporation elections and waivers governing the small business deduction (ITA sections 125 and 256); and (e) hybrid-arrangement limitation rules in ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6 (BEPS Action 2), which disallow double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of annual worldwide taxable supplies, net of capital-property sales (the GST/HST and QST 'small supplier' threshold). Registration is mandatory immediately upon exceeding the threshold; a foreign supplier making taxable supplies in Canada in the ordinary course must register, and a non-resident may register voluntarily to claim ITCs. Quebec applies the same $30,000 threshold for QST; there is no separate provincial monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported services applies under Excise Tax Act section 23 and mirror QST provisions: a supply of digital advertising, media-buying arbitrage and affiliate lead-generation services is 0%-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST or QST at the time of supply, unless the service relates to (i) real property situated in Canada, (ii) tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or (iii) an individual who benefits from the service while physically present in Canada. Services directed to foreign markets (foreign-media advertising, offshore affiliate traffic, foreign campaign management) are additionally covered by the advertising-services export rule in ETA section 23.1 where distribution occurs through non-Canadian media. For QST, the supply likewise falls outside Quebec tax when the recipient's business is foreign and the service is not consumed in Quebec. The supplier must obtain written non-resident evidence (residency certificates, foreign VAT registration numbers, address and OFAC verification) and an executed export-service contract to substantiate the 0% claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Form B13A proves physical exit for any tangible media or promotional collateral bundled with the service; pure digital service exports are substantiated by a service-export evidence dossier (contract, invoices, campaign delivery logs and technical performance data)
  • Bill of Lading - required where physical materials or promotional goods are shipped internationally; for digital-only deliverables, substitute the carriage contract, delivery receipts and server/access logs
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or Mercury-ACH/Wise transaction record evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident client within the repatriation window

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting gateway, or continuous-transaction-control digital stamping regime exists at the CRA or Revenu Quebec level; Canada operates a self-assessment VAT model with ex-post audit. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding $1.5M CAD, and all QST returns are filed electronically through Revenu Quebec's secure business portal. Invoices may be issued in any medium provided they contain the BN, supply date, description, consideration and the applicable GST/HST/QST amount or a zero-rating indicator. E-invoicing interoperability is voluntary (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is the emerging public-sector standard); records must be maintained in an electronically retrievable format under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act and section 230 of the Income Tax Act.

Zero-rating claims for exported services attract joint CRA/Revenu Quebec audit scrutiny under export-substantiation programs. If non-resident status or the offshore-consumption test fails, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Canada and the registrant must remit the uncollected GST/HST/QST (14.975% in Quebec) plus: (a) daily compound interest (statutory base rate plus 2% federally; equivalent provincial daily rate for QST); (b) a 10% negligence penalty on unremitted tax under ETA section 280 and a 10% penalty on overclaimed rebates or refunds under ETA section 285; and (c) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ITA section 239 and ETA section 326, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for repeated or reckless misconduct. Reassessment windows extend from the normal four-year limitation period to ten years (and indefinitely for fraud or wilful misrepresentation), permitting full retrospective clawback of zero-rated turnovers and any input tax credits previously recovered on the underlying media inventory and operating costs.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized capital and current account regime under a floating Canadian dollar. There is no Foreign Exchange Control Act constraining capital inflows, profit remittance, or dividend distributions; non-resident taxation is addressed through Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding rather than exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-receipt surrender or mandatory CAD conversion timeline exists. Export proceeds may be retained in any permitted currency; only general reporting obligations apply, including FINTRAC electronic funds-transfer reports above CAD 10,000 and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments survey filings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential bank regulation is delegated to OSFI, and FINTRAC supervises AML/CFT obligations for banks and money-services businesses.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (15% federal Part I rate plus 11.5% Quebec general rate); Quebec's 3% digital advertising services tax may apply separately to qualifying large digital advertising intermediaries.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s. 212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to any non-resident shareholder; no deduction is allowed to the Canadian payer.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; balance of Part I tax is due no later than 2 months after fiscal year-end, extended to 3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the SBD with taxable capital below CAD 15,000,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due date is the same as the federal return: 6 months after fiscal year-end; Quebec balance due generally 2 months after fiscal year-end, with the 3-month extension for an eligible CCPC mirroring federal rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 filing required when transactions with any one non-resident non-arm's length person exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate FMV; Schedule 10 Local File is triggered when gross revenue is at least CAD 20,000,000 and specified related-party transaction values exceed CAD 5,000,000; Schedule 25 Master File and Schedule 91 Country-by-Country Report apply to MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000; contemporaneous documentation is nonetheless administratively required under ITA s. 247(4) for all material related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: No standalone decree-based ESR regime applies in Canada or Quebec; however, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245 and Quebec Taxation Act s. 1079.2), the sham doctrine, and treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions. A Quebec affiliate media-buying vehicle must maintain real employees, office premises, contractual control over media planning and risk exposure, and substantive decision-making; otherwise the CRA or Revenu Quebec may disregard the intermediary, recharacterize transactions, or deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fictitious Advertising and Lead-Gen Invoices

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 14 years; civil reassessment with gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s. 163(2); prescribed interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, signed, or acquiesced in false filings are jointly and severally liable for withholdings and penalties under s. 227.1; individual criminal exposure includes fines, imprisonment, restitution orders, seizure of assets, and potential travel restrictions while criminal proceedings are pending.
Shell Company and Sham Intermediary Structure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the arm's length standard of ITA s. 247(2), transfer pricing adjustment penalty of 10% of the net adjustment when documentation is absent or unreliable under s. 247(3), secondary adjustments deemed as shareholder benefit or dividend, denial of deductions, and application of GAAR with a 25% penalty on tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors can be assessed for penalties connected with wilful participation in sham arrangements; personal civil liability may include restitution of corporate tax liabilities where the corporation is stripped or insolvent, and criminal charges under s. 239 for participating in the false arrangement.
AML and Unregistered Money Services Activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 500,000 per violation for entities; Criminal Code s. 462.31 laundering offense punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; asset restraint and forfeiture orders; additional state penalties in Quebec under the Act respecting the prevention of money-laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate unregistered money movement face criminal liability, imprisonment, personal fines, FINTRAC compliance orders, asset freezes, travel bans under bail conditions, and exclusion from serving as officers of any federally regulated corporation.
Unreported Part XIII Withholdings and Deficient T106 Reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full Part XIII withholding tax against the Canadian payer under ITA s. 215, plus penalties under s. 227(8) of 10% of the unreported amount where failure is willful, daily compounding interest, late-filing penalties for information returns under s. 162(7), and transfer pricing penalties where intercompany charges are non-arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII withholdings even after corporate dissolution; criminal prosecution under s. 239 can result in imprisonment up to 14 years, personal restitution orders, civil fraud awards, and travel bans imposed by the court.
