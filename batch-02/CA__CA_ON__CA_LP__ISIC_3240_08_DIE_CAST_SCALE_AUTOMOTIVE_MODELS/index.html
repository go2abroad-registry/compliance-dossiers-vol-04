Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Digital portals: 'My Business Account' (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and the Business Number program at canada.ca. Customs export reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Single Window Initiative (SWI). Ontario HST is collected by CRA under the federal-provincial Harmonized Sales Tax framework; the Ontario Ministry of Finance performs audit referral and revenue-allocation functions only and operates no separate VAT registration for manufacturing LPs.

E-government filing portal

Canada has no separate unified TIN for partnerships; the Ontario limited partnership is identified for federal taxes by a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts. Procurement sequence for a foreign-owned LP: (1) complete Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership registration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario Business Registry); (2) file CRA Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts') signed by the general partner, attaching the LP agreement and partner schedules — non-resident partners must disclose jurisdiction of incorporation/residence and foreign registration numbers; (3) CRA issues the BN with program accounts: RT (GST/HST) once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, RC (income tax information return) where applicable, and optional RP (payroll); (4) register the same BN in the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for customs export declarations; (5) non-resident partners are not separately given a Canadian TIN unless they carry on business in Canada on their own account; CRA tracks Part XIII withholding and reporting through the LP's BN. GST/HST registration under ETA s.240 must be filed within 30 days of the LP ceasing to be a small supplier, with the RT0001 account effective retroactive to the date the threshold was exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal income tax the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: income and losses are computed at the partnership level under ITA s.96 and allocated to partners, each of whom bears tax on their allocable share; the LP itself pays no federal or Ontario income tax. For GST/HST the same LP is statutorily deemed a separate taxable person (ETA s.272.1) and must register and charge tax in its own name. Territorial scope: the LP carries on business in Ontario through its die-casting plant; non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source allocated income unless the partnership itself creates a permanent establishment triggering broader attribution. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal income tax the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: income and losses are computed at the partnership level under ITA s.96 and allocated to partners, each of whom bears tax on their allocable share; the LP itself pays no federal or Ontario income tax. For GST/HST the same LP is statutorily deemed a separate taxable person (ETA s.272.1) and must register and charge tax in its own name. Territorial scope: the LP carries on business in Ontario through its die-casting plant; non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source allocated income unless the partnership itself creates a permanent establishment triggering broader attribution. Tax-exempt zone treatment is not available for a manufacturing-export operation in Ontario.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under the Income Tax Act; pass-through status is mandatory for the Ontario LP. Available statutory elections relevant to the structure: (a) joint election under ITA s.96(3) between the LP and a partner to accelerate or defer the inclusion of the partner's allocated partnership income into the partner's taxation year; (b) voluntary registration election by a small supplier under ETA s.240(2.1), permitting immediate input tax credit recovery before the CAD 30,000 threshold is reached; (c) Quick Method remittance election under ETA s.170 for registrants with annual taxable supplies of CAD 400,000 or less; (d) hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3) are mandatory and non-elective — the LP cannot elect out of deduction denial or deferral where hybrid financial instruments or hybrid transfers exist between the LP, a non-resident corporate general partner, or foreign limited partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA s.148 'small supplier' threshold). The LP ceases to be a small supplier when the aggregate worldwide value of taxable supplies made in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceeds CAD 30,000 (excluding capital-property sales and financial services); registration is then mandatory within 30 days. A non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada through the Ontario plant or a Canadian agent is subject to the same threshold. Because the LP exports die-cast scale models at 0% HST, registration is recommended from day one to recover input tax credits on tooling, zinc alloy, tampo inks, packaging, and logistics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of tangible die-cast collectibles is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.20 where the LP transfers ownership and exports the goods as part of the same supply — meaning the buyer is a non-resident, delivery occurs outside Canada (FOB Ontario, DDP US distribution center, or direct-to-consumer postal export), and risk passes to the foreign consignee. The LP must hold 'satisfactory evidence' of export under ETA s.169(b): physical movement out of Canada and documented transfer of risk. Services: related stamping, design, and freight services supplied directly to the non-resident customer are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 where the direct beneficiary is a non-resident and the services are not consumed in Canada. Domestic sales to Canadian hobby shops or collectors are taxable at 13% HST; goods sold from a Canadian warehouse to a Canadian marketplace intermediary are taxable at 13%, not zero-rated.

There is no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no digital stamping obligation, and no mandatory API-based electronic invoicing in Canada at the federal or Ontario level. Compliance is documentary and self-assessed: every GST/HST invoice must state the prescribed particulars of ETA s.169 (supplier name and BN, date, description, tax rate, tax payable; simplified invoices permitted below CAD 150). Returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or MyBA. CRA operates a voluntary PEPPOL-based 'Canada E-Invoice' interoperability pilot for B2B invoices; adoption is encouraged for exporters but not legally required. For exports, the LP must transmit shipment data through the CBSA Single Window Initiative (SWI) — the CERS electronic export report (B13A equivalent) — before exportation, with advanced cargo data under ACE for US-bound goods and generally the e-export declaration due within 48 hours for non-US shipments; each CERS report number must be preserved in the export evidence file.

Zero-rated exports are a priority CRA compliance area under the GST/HST Export Verification Program. On audit, if the export file is incomplete or contradicted — goods resold in Canada, forged B13A/BOL documents, or PayPal proceeds originating from a Canadian address — CRA recharacterizes the sale as a domestic 13% HST taxable supply and assesses the full 13% HST not remitted, plus compound daily interest from the filing deadline. Additional exposures: (a) ETA s.285 penalty for a knowingly or grossly negligently false statement in a return or claim — the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the net tax understated; (b) ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any federal income tax or refund misstatement traceable to the same false export invoices; (c) criminal liability under ETA s.329 and ITA s.239 — fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent export schemes; (d) CRA may deny refunds and input tax credits, reassess prior years, and in egregious cases restrict or cancel registration. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful neglect is established, exposing historical years to retrospective clawback of refunds previously paid. Although Canada imposes no fixed statutory FX repatriation ceiling, export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days attract transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA s.247 and may be imputed as a deemed benefit or shareholder loan where a partner or foreign affiliate delayed payment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has no general exchange-control statute and no restrictions on inward or outward capital movements, including equity subscriptions, loan repayments, trade settlement, or profit remittance. Profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject only to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate, typically treaty-reduced to 5-15%) and, where applicable, GST/HST on specific payments; the partnership should also verify that the incoming investment is not subject to Investment Canada Act review, which for a low-asset die-cast distribution business will not be triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion obligations on export receipts. All proceeds from export sales of die-cast scale models may be held, converted, or remitted freely in any currency; there is no statutory timeline for collection and no minimum percentage required to be converted into Canadian dollars.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Department of Finance Canada on exchange-rate and capital-movement policy; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border funds-monitoring and AML reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium-High as an ancillary disbursement rail, Low as a primary banking substitute. Canadian-registered Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Service Providers (e.g., Wise Canada, Payoneer, PayPal) function as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses and, from 2024, are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada; they can hold CAD balances and execute cross-border MT103-comparable transfers but cannot provide escrow segregation, true trade finance, or committed credit facilities. Offshore EMIs without Canadian MSB registration may not lawfully provide payment services to Ontario-resident entities and should be avoided for the capital escrow function.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not itself subject to Canadian income tax. Taxable income and losses are allocated to partners. For a Canadian corporation, the federal general corporate rate is 15% and the general Ontario corporate rate is 11.5%, producing a combined general rate of 26.5%. Ontario small-business rates may reduce the combined rate to 12.2% for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations on eligible active business income within the applicable business limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard Canadian Part XIII withholding tax rate on dividends paid or deemed paid to a non-resident is 25%. An LP distribution is not automatically a dividend; its treatment depends on the nature of the underlying amount, the recipient's status, the partnership agreement, and the applicable statutory and treaty rules. A Canadian corporate partner may receive partnership allocations without dividend characterization, but subsequent dividends paid by that corporation to a non-resident are generally subject to 25% withholding before treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general monetary threshold below which contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is automatically unnecessary for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Canadian taxpayers must make reasonable efforts to determine arm's-length pricing and maintain contemporaneous documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act. Form T106 reporting generally applies to reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents where the aggregate value exceeds CAD 1,000,000, subject to the statutory rules. A BEPS Master File and Local File may be required for qualifying multinational enterprise groups under Canadian reporting rules; Country-by-Country reporting generally applies at the group threshold of EUR 750 million consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate United Arab Emirates-style or Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable solely to an LP. Substance is nevertheless relevant through the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty principal-purpose tests, beneficial-ownership requirements, sham-transaction doctrine, transfer-pricing rules, foreign-affiliate rules, partnership allocation rules, thin-capitalization rules, and common-law characterization. The LP should maintain genuine Canadian commercial activity where claimed, including appropriately documented management, employees or contractors, manufacturing capability, inventory control, customer and supplier contracts, bank accounts, books and records, decision-making, and risk assumption. A limited partnership should not be used as a conduit for circular payments, artificial allocations, treaty shopping, or diversion of manufacturing profits without functions, assets, and risks supporting the allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment of tax, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the relevant understated tax or overstated credit, repeated-failure penalties, and prosecution under the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act. Criminal tax evasion can result in fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years, with aggravated offences carrying more severe consequences under applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, officers, and persons who authorized or participated in the conduct may face prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, civil recovery, director-liability assessments for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST, and asset seizure. Limited-partner status does not protect a person who manages the business, commits fraud, gives instructions, or acts outside the limited-partner role.
Unremitted Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for the full unremitted amount, arrears interest, late-remittance penalties, failure-to-deduct penalties, information-return penalties, and gross-negligence penalties. CRA may deny treaty-reduced rates and assess the statutory 25% rate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed under the Income Tax Act director-liability rules where the corporation fails to remit source deductions and statutory conditions are met. General partners and controlling officers may face equivalent recovery exposure where the partnership or related payer is legally responsible. Civil restitution, prosecution, and imprisonment are possible for deliberate withholding fraud; a travel ban is not an automatic tax penalty but may arise from a court order, immigration consequence, or criminal process.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income or deductions under section 247, impose a transfer-pricing penalty generally equal to 10% of the applicable adjustment where statutory conditions are met, deny unsupported deductions, reassess withholding, and apply interest. Failure to provide records can lead to adverse inferences and additional penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face civil recovery where intentional misconduct or breach of fiduciary duty causes loss. Criminal exposure may arise if records or returns are knowingly falsified. Ordinary transfer-pricing reassessment does not automatically pierce the corporate veil or create imprisonment, but deliberate evasion can produce personal criminal liability.
Shell Company, Sham Partnership, or Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard or recharacterize arrangements under the general anti-avoidance rule, deny treaty benefits, reassess income and withholding, impose interest and penalties, and refer suspicious structures for enforcement. Corporate and partnership records may be compelled and audited.

Director personal liability: Persons who knowingly direct sham arrangements may face civil restitution, oppression or fiduciary-duty claims, tax prosecution, fines, imprisonment, asset tracing, and beneficial-ownership enforcement. A travel restriction is not automatic but may be imposed by a court in connection with criminal proceedings or failure to comply with court orders.
AML, Sanctions, and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC obligations may include client identification, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, recordkeeping, suspicious-transaction reporting, and large-cash or electronic-funds reporting where applicable. Administrative monetary penalties, transaction blocking, account termination, seizure, forfeiture, and criminal prosecution can apply. Proceeds-of-crime and terrorism-financing offences may carry substantial fines and imprisonment, including life imprisonment for the most serious offences.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, compliance officers, and authorized signatories may face personal criminal liability where they knowingly participate, facilitate, counsel, or deliberately ignore unlawful transactions. Civil restitution, forfeiture, asset restraint, director disqualification, and court-ordered travel restrictions may apply. Non-resident persons may also face Canadian immigration and extradition consequences.
Customs, Import Valuation, and GST/HST Fraud

Statutory sanction: The Canada Border Services Agency may reassess duties and GST, impose administrative monetary penalties, seize goods, issue compliance orders, and pursue prosecution. CRA may deny input tax credits, assess GST/HST, interest, and penalties, and reassess related income-tax deductions.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and directors may face personal criminal liability for knowingly false customs declarations or tax fraud. Civil recovery and forfeiture may follow. Travel bans are not automatic but may arise from criminal proceedings, warrants, or court orders.
Corporate and Partnership Record Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may issue information demands, deny deductions or losses, apply penalties for missing information returns, reassess on the basis of available evidence, and extend the normal reassessment period where misrepresentation or fraud is present. Ontario corporate-law penalties may apply to entities required to maintain corporate records or beneficial-ownership information.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face statutory compliance orders, civil claims, personal assessments for source deductions, and criminal liability for knowingly destroying or falsifying records. Veil piercing is exceptional and fact-specific; it is more likely where the entity is used as an instrument of fraud, sham, or deliberate statutory evasion.
