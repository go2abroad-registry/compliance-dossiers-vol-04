Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership (Civil Code of Quebec arts. 2236-2256); capital is fixed contractually in the limited partnership agreement. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account (federal GST/HST, income tax, import/export); Revenu Quebec – Clic Revenu and Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (Quebec QST, employer contributions, provincial registration)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership seeking to operate in Quebec must first file a Partnership Declaration (Déclaration de société en nom collectif / société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec and obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Thereafter, the partnership applies to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using a program account for GST/HST and, if applicable, payroll or import/export accounts. Separately, the partnership registers with Revenu Quebec for QST and other Quebec program accounts. Non-resident partners without a Canadian establishment may require a non-resident identification number through CRA forms and a Quebec attorney (mandataire) for service of process for the provincial registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have an IRS-style check-the-box election; legal form is determinative under the Quebec Civil Code and the Income Tax Act. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent and files an information return while allocating income, losses, and credits to partners. Available statutory elections include voluntary GST/HST registration for a small supplier, the ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident partner to file a Canadian income tax return on net Canadian-source income, and partnership allocation elections under prescribed partnership agreements. Hybrid-mismatch rules are mandatory and are not elective.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies made in Canada within the preceding four calendar quarters (GST/HST 'small supplier' threshold) and CAD 30,000 for QST purposes for persons carrying on commercial activity in Quebec; voluntary registration is permitted below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rated when the trust exports tangible personal property from Canada under GST/HST schedules, provided the goods leave Canada and are accompanied by an appropriate export declaration. Services supplied by an ESOP trust to a non-resident employer or non-resident trust generally qualify for 0% GST/HST when the service is not consumed in Canada, is not rendered in connection with Canadian real property, and is not performed in respect of tangible personal property physically in Canada at the time of performance. If the trust provides services through a Canadian establishment to a Quebec resident, 5% GST plus 9.5% QST apply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API-based transaction clearance, and no digital stamping regime exists federally or in Quebec. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised floating-currency regime with no exchange-control legislation; the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act contain no restrictions on convertibility for either current account or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible, and non-resident profit remittances from a Quebec Limited Partnership require no Bank of Canada authorisation or FX allocation. The principal legal hurdles are tax-based rather than exchange-control: payments such as dividends, interest, royalties, or partnership distributions to non-resident partners attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) (generally 25% statutory, potentially treaty-reduced to as low as 5-15%), requiring CRA Form NR 301 or NR 601 preparation and timely remittance. Additionally, any international electronic funds transfer (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the Canadian financial institution to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), but no prior government approval is required for the underlying conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed with effect from 1 December 1951, and Canada has never re-imposed any statutory timeline for repatriation, mandatory surrender quotas, or forced conversion ratios on export receipts or any other foreign-currency proceeds. A Quebec LP administering an ESOP may retain disposition proceeds from foreign equity in offshore or Canadian foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) indefinitely without conversion to CAD, subject only to CRA reporting of deemed dispositions, attribution rules, and the accounting record-keeping obligations under the Financial Administration Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act. FINTRAC still requires the bank to file an incoming EFT report for any international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more into such accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and issuer of Canadian dollars; prudential regulation of chartered banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision, client identification and EFT reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); securities and derivatives oversight in Quebec by the Autorite des marches financiers (AMF); domestic clearing and settlement oversight by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium with direct legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the European 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) or FinTech EMI-issuer licence as a distinct bank category; an offshore EMI (e.g., FCA-authorized in the UK or e-money licensed in an EU state) cannot passport into Canada or hold client deposits in Quebec unless separately authorised as a bank under the Bank Act (Canada) or, for payment processing only, registered as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) supervised by the Bank of Canada. The ESOP administration LP may use such offshore EMIs solely as a payment-technology or card-dispersion vendor, but settlement must be hosted at a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank for fiduciary protection, CAD clearing via Payments Canada rails, and FINTRAC reporting. Overall feasibility for the primary treasury account is Low; for ancillary employee-benefit disbursement wallets it is Medium, provided the contractual chain preserves the Canadian bank as settlement counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners, sponsors, or parent investors should route capital injections by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from their home bank to the Quebec LP's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using purpose code INVE (investment) or FUND (inter-company funding) in the ISO 20022 purpose field. The remitter must identify, on the payment reference line, the LP's REQ registration number and CRA Business Number, and the bank must receive an executed capital subscription agreement and escrow instruction letter before crediting. For any single international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, the Canadian receiving bank files a FINTRAC incoming international EFT report; funds are then placed in the Capital Escrow Account and capitalised by a general-partner resolution; for regulatory clarity the inflow should not be titled as a shareholder loan but as contributed capital, with a contemporaneous capital-account allocation schedule maintained in accordance with Quebec tax and partnership accounting rules.Domestic employee-benefit and payroll disbursements settle in CAD via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for high-value payments; cross-border payouts to non-resident beneficiaries, foreign trustees, or venders of shares are executed via SWIFT MT103 through a correspondent banking network (e.g., via RBC, TD, BMO, or National Bank US clearing correspondents) with the beneficiary bank BIC and beneficiary account number provided. Trade settlement in connection with foreign share acquisitions may additionally utilise commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 issued by the Canadian bank; all outgoing international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC, and any Part XIII withholding tax due on distributions to non-residents is deducted, remitted through the CRA, and documented via Forms NR 601 and NR4. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Limited Partnership level (fiscally transparent flow-through entity; no separate entity-level income tax). A corporate partner bears combined federal and Quebec general corporate tax of 26.5% (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%) on its allocated share of Canadian-source active business income; individual partners bear graduated federal and Quebec marginal rates (top combined marginal rate approximately 54%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate of 25% on Canadian-source dividends paid to non-resident beneficial owners. In the LP context, distributions of allocated active business profits to a non-resident partner without a Canadian permanent establishment are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding; however, amounts characterized as Canadian-source dividends, interest, rents, or royalties distributed from the LP's investment portfolio are subject to the 25% statutory withholding at source.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 must be filed within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period end (e.g., a December 31 fiscal period end is due by May 31 of the following year); T5013 slips must be furnished to each partner no later than the last day of the first calendar month following the CRA filing deadline (or March 31 in most cases).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation): mandatory where controlled cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 5,000,000 in the fiscal year. Master File: mandatory where the partnership or its controlling group generates annual gross revenue above CAD 50,000,000 and the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 500,000,000; Country-by-Country Report (CBCR) filing applies at CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), which denies tax benefits from an avoidance transaction lacking genuine commercial substance or a bona fide business purpose, and through the reasonable expectation of profit (REOP) doctrine. For the ESOP/EOT trust, statutory substance conditions under s.510.2 ITA must be continuously satisfied: the trust must be resident in Canada, maintain an updated roll of employee beneficiaries, hold shares exclusively for employee compensation, restrict employer non-arm's-length financing, and conduct real trust activity (option grants, distributions, record-keeping); an empty trust will be recharacterized as a sham and lose the EOT exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Willful Non-Payment / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: on summary conviction, a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax attempted to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may reassess beyond the normal statute-barred period (s.152(4)(a)(A)) and assess arrears interest under s.161.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for the entity's unremitted withholdings and source deductions under s.227.1 ITA; criminal liability attaches to directors who directed, authorized, assented to, or acquiesced in the evasion; the CRA enforces director's liability assessments against personal assets, garnishes wages and bank accounts, and certified debts above CAD 10,000 may trigger passport denial and travel restrictions under the Federal-Provincial Collection Agreement.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping – GAAR Redetermination

Statutory sanction: s.245 GAAR denies the treaty or domestic tax benefit and recharacterizes the entire arrangement; gross-negligence penalty under s.163.2 of 50% of the misstated amount; the 2024 and 2025 federal budgets legislated a stand-alone 25% GAAR penalty on undisclosed or abusive transactions; the CRA may deem the LP to have a Canadian permanent establishment and reassess all related years; foreign trust opaque status may be revoked.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false declarations or knowingly participates in the treaty-shopping scheme is jointly liable for the 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163.2; directors who made, or assented to, the misrepresentation may be subject to civil fraud claims; a director acting as trustee who misappropriates the ESOP trust's share proceeds faces criminal breach of trust under Criminal Code s.336 (14 years) and fraud under s.380 (14 years).
