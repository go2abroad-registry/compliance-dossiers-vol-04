Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), federal portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca). Because a permanent establishment is located in Quebec, Revenu Québec (RQ) administers both the federal GST and the provincial QST under a joint administration agreement; RQ's registration and filing portal is CLIC / Mon dossier d'entreprise (revenuquebec.ca). The Quebec enterprise identifier (NEQ) is issued by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. Customs and export reporting are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) platform.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 - Register the extra-provincial Limited Partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) and comply with Quebec's mandatory partner declaration register (Registre des associés) under Bill 32/PL32, which requires public disclosure of non-resident partners. STEP 2 - Obtain the federal 15-character Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, opening the RT (GST/HST) program account; the partnership registers under its own name as a 'person' for excise tax purposes and must disclose each partner's BN or SIN. STEP 3 - Register with Revenu Québec for the QST and Quebec-administered GST by filing the combined registration form (LM-1-V) through CLIC or the RQ portal, obtaining the QST number. STEP 4 - Non-resident partners must each obtain a BN and file Canadian Part I returns (Section 115), potentially elect under ITA 216 for investment income, and address Part XIII withholding on distributions. Electronic registration is generally effective within one to ten business days; foreign-entity identity verification may lengthen processing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Quebec LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes: there is no federal or Quebec entity-level tax; each partner reports its distributive share of income or loss on its own return. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. The partnership is, however, a separate 'person' for GST/HST and QST, and is itself the registered taxpayer for indirect taxes. Territorial scope is source-based for non-residents and worldwide for resident partners. Canada-maintained no tax-exempt free-zone regime applies; export-oriented manufacturers instead rely on zero-rated exports, ITC recovery, and import relief under CUSMA for US-origin inputs.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; partnership flow-through taxation is mandatory. Available elections and statutory mechanisms: (1) ITA 97(2) election to transfer capital property into the LP on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (2) ITA 96(3) election allowing a corporate partner to compute its partnership income as if the LP were a separate Canadian resident taxpayer, aligning fiscal periods; (3) ETA election on filing-frequency (annual, semi-annual, quarterly or monthly) based on revenue thresholds, filed through Revenu Québec; (4) OECD Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements rules (ITA ss.18.4 to 18.6, effective July 1, 2022) apply automatically with no elective relief, denying deductions or including income where a non-resident partner's jurisdiction characterizes the LP as a taxable entity; (5) GST/QST input-tax-credit allocation decisions, made in the partnership's registration profile, for the formulaic versus actual ITC allocation methods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - The 'small supplier' threshold: a partnership whose taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000 may remain unregistered. Registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, even if all sales are zero-rated exports. A manufacturer of ICU mechanical ventilators with foreign government LC and wire payment rails will almost certainly exceed CAD 30,000 in the first reference period and must register for GST/HST and QST from inception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods actually exported from Canada by the vendor or at the direction of the purchaser qualify for 0% GST/QST under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and the corresponding QST provisions, provided the vendor holds documentary evidence of export. ICU mechanical ventilators exported to foreign governments and hospitals (HS 9019.20.00) fall squarely within the exported-goods zero-rating rule. Related services performed directly in respect of exported goods (e.g., installation, commissioning, warranty service performed outside Canada) and certain consultancy services supplied to non-resident buyers are outside the scope of or zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V items 5 to 7. Caution: a domestic sale of a ventilator to a Canadian hospital is taxable at the full 5% GST and 9.975% effective QST because ICU ventilators are not enumerated in the zero-rated medical-device list of Schedule VI Part II; only exports attract 0%.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration - B13A electronic filing confirmation via CBSA CARM/eManifest, including the G7 Customs Declaration data, HS 9019.20.00 classification, and Canadian exporter reference number
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill - ocean, air, or truck cargo control document showing shipment origin in Canada and foreign destination, with the exporter as shipper or the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Commercial Invoice - describing the ICU mechanical ventilator, serial numbers, unit value in CAD, foreign buyer identity, Incoterms, and country of origin
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder export certificate - continuous carrier certificate, warehouse receipt, or third-party logistics statement confirming that the goods physically left Canada
  • Bank inward-remittance records - SWIFT MT103 or wire transfer statement evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident buyer; this is the functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, which Canada does not issue as a statutory customs document

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate comparable to Latin American or EU clearance models. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Interoperability Framework (PCEIP) and gradually adopted B2G/B2B formats are acceptable but not mandatory. Mandatory digital channels are limited to: (1) e-filing of GST/HST and QST returns and remittances via Revenu Québec's CLIC portal; and (2) electronic transmission of customs export declarations through the CBSA CARM/eManifest platform (G7 Customs Declaration and B13A data set equivalents). Invoices may be issued in paper or PDF form, but electronic records must meet CRA/RQ record-retention rules, and proof of export must be obtained within two years of the filing due date to sustain zero-rating.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day inward FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter reflects the standard trade-finance benchmark, while the binding legal obligation is the six-year books-and-records retention period (ETA s.286, ITA s.230). If the exporter fails to obtain acceptable export evidence within two years of the return due date, CRA and Revenu Québec will claw back the zero-rating, assess output GST at 5% and QST at 9.975% effective, and charge compounded daily interest at prescribed rates. Normal reassessment is limited to four years, but the window is unlimited for misrepresentation, neglect, or willful default. Penalties include the ETA s.280 failure-to-report penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 1% of unreported net tax) and the ETA s.285 gross-negligence penalty of 25% of tax or rebate denied. Quebec tax legislation mirrors these penalties for QST. Fraudulent export claims can additionally trigger criminal liability under Part IX of the ETA, fines, imprisonment, and debarment from the CBSA trusted-trader programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under Canada's liberalized current-account and capital-account regime. No exchange-control permits are required for capital injections, profit repatriation, or trade settlements. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally pass-through; foreign partners are taxed on Canadian-source income and may remit net earnings without currency restrictions. Cross-border wire transfers above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting, not exchange control. The principal administrative friction is bank-enhanced due diligence for a non-resident-owned, pass-through entity, not convertibility or remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds. Exporters and limited partnerships may hold export receivables in CAD or foreign-currency accounts at authorized Canadian banks. Export invoices, transport documents, and settlement records must be retained to support Canada Revenue Agency audit readiness and transaction-reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including FINTRAC large-currency-transaction and cross-border electronic-funds-transfer reports for amounts at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and, for Quebec money-services businesses, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF); Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as primary treasury rail; High only for low-value disbursement and utility payments. A foreign digital EMI is not a Canadian chartered bank and cannot accept Canadian retail deposits or hold regulatory capital under the Bank Act. To operate in Quebec, it must register as a money-services business with FINTRAC and obtain a licence from the AMF under Quebec's Money-Services Businesses Act. Such providers can facilitate cross-border disbursements and receivable collection, but they are inappropriate for capital escrow, government-backed LCs, or the high-value clearing needs of a medical-device manufacturer requiring regulatory confidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated operational account at a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank. The wire must cite ISO 20022 External Purpose Code 'CAPC' (capital payment) or the bank-specific 'Corporation Advances - Capital' classification, and the beneficiary field must include the exact legal name of the limited partnership and its Quebec NEQ registration number. The sender should identify the remitting party as the limited partner making the contribution, with a reference to the capital account contribution resolution. Before execution, the bank requires an advance KYC package, proof of partnership existence, beneficial-ownership identification, and confirmation of the source of funds to prevent wire-hold. The receiving bank will verify the MT103 routing through the beneficiary BIC and, for Canadian-dollar crediting, use the High Value Clearing System (HVCS) managed by Payments Canada. For USD inbound funds, correspondent clearing via New York is normal; no FX conversion is required if the account accommodates USD. Funds received should be booked to the LP's capital account within ten business days, supported by a board or general-partner resolution.Supplier and component payments are cleared through the SWIFT MT103 rail for cross-border trade, with ISO 2022 purpose code 'TRAD-Goods' and a full invoice reference. For larger, structured transactions, a confirmed irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP600 may be raised by the Canadian bank on behalf of the LP, payable against presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, and full-set bills of lading. For domestic Canadian disbursements, including purchases from registered suppliers and government procurement, use Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) or the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) where applicable, with electronic payment references tied to purchase orders. For government contracts, the Canadian buyer frequently settles by electronic funds transfer via the Federal Government's procurement platform; the LP must maintain an operational account that can receive government EFT and, if required, process LCs with the bank as advising/confirming bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services, royalties, and interest transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidelines).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. For treaty benefits, the principal purpose test (PPT) under the MLI may deny benefits if the transaction lacks genuine economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) and Quebec Taxation Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the Income Tax Act (s. 227.1) and Quebec Tax Act; travel bans and seizure of personal assets are possible.
Shell Company (Artificial Transactions)

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) allows the CRA to recharacterize transactions; penalties of 25% of the tax benefit, plus interest. Quebec may impose similar penalties under its GAAR provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for the tax benefit denied, and may face injunctions and asset freezes.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal charges for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years; they may also be subject to civil forfeiture of assets and travel restrictions.
