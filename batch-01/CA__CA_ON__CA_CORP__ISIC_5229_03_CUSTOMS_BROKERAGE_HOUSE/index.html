Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax authority; GST/HST registration and administration via CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance administers provincial corporate taxes and relies on CRA for HST collection under the harmonized system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated customs broker carrying on business in Ontario must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), either electronically through the CRA portal or by mail, before exceeding the CAD 30,000 GST/HST registration threshold or upon commencement of Canadian commercial activity. The CRA will issue a BN and a separate GST/HST program account with an 'RT' suffix. The broker must also obtain a Canadian registered business address and may be required to designate a Canadian fiscal agent or representative. The CBSA customs broker licence is a separate statutory licensing process administered by the Canada Border Services Agency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian or provincially incorporated business corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act and Ontario Corporations Tax Act. Canadian-resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. No pass-through or partnership-like status is available for this corporate entity.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or hybrid-pass-through election comparable to the U.S. regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for supplies between closely related corporations; (ii) ETA sections 271-273 elections for GST/HST group registration; (iii) Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident rental income on a net basis; (iv) Income Tax Act section 219 branch tax election for non-resident corporations; and (v) certain Ontario allocation elections on the provincial corporate return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - a person is considered a small supplier and is not required to register for GST/HST unless total taxable supplies (before expenses) over the most recent four consecutive calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000. Non-resident customs brokers carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold; registration is mandatory once the threshold is surpassed or if the broker chooses to register voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service made to a non-resident person is generally zero-rated unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada or is a personal service rendered in Canada. A licensed customs brokerage house may therefore apply 0% GST/HST on brokerage and logistics services supplied to a non-resident principal for goods exported from Canada, provided the recipient is non-resident, the service is not tied to Canadian real property, and sufficient export evidence is retained. Supplies of goods literally exported by the supplier are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I, subject to CRA export documentation rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A via CAED/eExport or CARM equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-exchange receipt record showing payment from the non-resident principal

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically (via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI). For customs brokerage, the CBSA requires electronic submission of commercial transaction data through the Single Window Initiative, the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal, and the Automated Customs Information System (ACIS); brokers use authorized digital interfaces for release, accounting, and export declarations.

The CRA and CBSA may jointly audit customs brokers' export documentation and zero-rating claims. If zero-rating is claimed without a valid export declaration, transport document, or proof of payment, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 13% HST, add statutory interest, and impose penalties under the Excise Tax Act. Penalties include a 5% late-filing penalty plus 3% for successive months of delinquency, and for false statements or gross negligence, penalties of the greater of $250 and up to 50% of the net tax amount that would otherwise be assessed. In cases of fraudulent export zero-rating, the CRA may claw back refunds and credits, extend the normal four-year reassessment period indefinitely, and pursue criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully freely convertible under both current account and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no exchange control legislation or restrictions on the conversion, transfer, or repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, or service fees. Profit remittances by non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless reduced by treaty (e.g., under Canada-US treaty, 5% for substantial shareholdings, 15% for other dividends). No advance approval is required for wire transfers or dividend distributions, but cross-border transactions exceeding $10,000 CAD trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations. There are no restrictions on foreign currency bank accounts in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may retain funds in foreign currency accounts or convert as per commercial discretion. All export transaction records must be maintained under the Income Tax Act and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requirements; trade documents must support the underlying customs broker transaction for GST/HST and customs bond purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF; no dedicated foreign exchange control authority exists in Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium for wholly offshore digital EMI models. Canada does not recognize the European Economic Area (EEA) 'Electronic Money Institution' passport. Any fintech or EMI offering Canadian CAD accounts, payment services, or cross-border clearing must be licensed either as a bank under the Bank Act (federally via OSFI) or as a regulated payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once fully in force, with FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) for foreign exchange dealing and remittance services. Use of unlicensed offshore EMIs for principal operational accounts is legally disfavored and can trigger heightened FINTRAC scrutiny, loss of deposit insurance (CDIC) coverage, and branch onboarding denials. Hybrid structures with a Canadian-regulated fintech subsidiary or partnership with a Schedule I bank are viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections to a Canadian operational account are executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202, Fedwire (USD), or EFT/ACH within the Canadian clearing system (ACSS / Lynx). Recommended purpose codes: for equity subscription use 'INVESTMENT IN CANADIAN ENTITY - SHARE CAPITAL' with reference to the federal/provincial corporate registry number; for loan advances use 'INTERCOMPANY LONG-TERM LOAN' accompanied by a written loan agreement, interest rate schedule, and Form T106 (Foreign Reporting) where applicable. Verification requires source of funds declaration, confirmatory account details (bank name, institution number, transit number, SWIFT code), and a completed client information gathering questionnaire. Funds derived from non-resident shareholders above CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC; loans above CDN $100,000 require contemporaneous transfer documentation and board approval minutes.Outbound trade settlements for customs brokers include payments to CBSA (via the Commercial Payment Program or CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management module), payments to foreign customs authorities, subcontractor fees, freight forwarding disbursements, and import/export duty remittances. For customs revenue payments, use CBSA's approved financial institution rails only. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-provincial general corporate rate (federal general rate 15% plus Ontario general corporate rate 11.5%); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on active business income up to C$500,000 pay 12.2% combined (federal 9% plus Ontario 3.2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on gross dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (ITA s.212(2)); treaty relief commonly reduces the rate to 5% for a company that is the beneficial owner of at least 10% of the voting shares and to 15% for portfolio investors

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a taxation year constitute the CRA contemporaneous documentation benchmark (Information Circular IC 87-2R); ultimate parents of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million must file a Country-by-Country Report using Form RC5869

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is tested through the OECD BEPS minimum standard for preferential regimes, the central-management-and-control residence test, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245). A customs brokerage captive or a shell agent corporation lacking actual employees, physical assets, and de facto management control in Canada is exposed to treaty-benefit denial, reassessment of allocations, and transfer pricing penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and customs misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment of up to five years; Customs Act s.122: seizure and forfeiture of goods and penalties up to 200% of the value of the goods; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under ITA s.163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors may be convicted personally as parties to the offence; joint and several liability applies for unremitted employee or withholding taxes under ITA s.227.1; civil restitution is enforceable against personal assets; passport surrender, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to five years per count may be imposed
Transfer pricing mispricing and shell arrangement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result; 10% penalty on the adjusted net amount; GAAR recharacterization under s.245; denial of provincial allocation; prescribed-rate interest compounded daily

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or documentation are exposed to third-party penalties under ITA s.163.2 and criminal prosecution for evasion; personal assets may be traced where the corporate entity is a mere façade; travel bans and imprisonment attach on conviction
