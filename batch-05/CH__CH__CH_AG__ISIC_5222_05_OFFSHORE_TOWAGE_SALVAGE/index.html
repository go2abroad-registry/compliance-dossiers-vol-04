Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC); official portal www.estv.admin.ch, with eVAT e-filing platform and e-services portal; UID registry administered by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (BFS) at www.uid.admin.ch; customs export procedure administered by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS/BAZG) through e-dec and the TARGO/Plate platform; cantonal tax administrations (Steuerämter) serve as collecting agents for cantonal and communal direct taxes under the federal harmonisation regime.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) incorporate in the commercial register (Handelsregister/Zefix) with minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 and a paid-up minimum of CHF 50,000; (2) obtain the statutory Swiss Business Identifier (UID – Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) which is automatically issued by the Federal Statistical Office upon commercial register entry and is used as the cantonal corporate income tax number, federal tax number and VAT identification number (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx); (3) for a foreign entity without a Swiss registered seat, obtain a UID by direct application through the ESTV eVAT registration portal or through the competent cantonal tax authority where the entity has a Swiss establishment; (4) register for VAT within 30 days of commencement of tax liability under Article 20 of the VAT Act, with mandatory appointment of a Swiss fiscal representative (Vertreter) for taxable persons not domiciled, resident or seated in Switzerland; the registration request is submitted electronically via eVAT and is effective from the date of registration; after approval the UID is activated for VAT purposes and the taxpayer is enrolled in the ESTV VAT register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the AG is a distinct legal taxpayer subject to Swiss corporate income tax at federal, cantonal and communal levels on its worldwide net profit and paid-in capital; profits attributable to foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt under the territorial carve-out; the participation exemption and notional interest deduction only reduce the tax base; the AG is not a pass-through or transparent entity for Swiss tax purposes and cannot elect flow-through treatment; cross-border hybrid mismatch results are recharacterised under anti-hybrid rules introduced by the Federal Act on Tax Reform and AHV Financing (TRAF) effective 1 January 2020.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a check-the-box election system. Available statutory elections applicable to an AG include: (a) VAT group taxation election under Article 13(2) VAT Act allowing legally, economically and organisationally integrated entities to form a single taxable person (Groupe TVA / Unternehmensgruppe); (b) election of the participation exemption at the cantonal level with requisite documentary evidence; (c) optional remuneration-neutral liquidation-style accounting for the corporate income tax; (d) the election for a foreign permanent establishment to be taxed in Switzerland on a treaty-exempt basis subject to the relevant double tax treaty; and (e) under hybrid mismatch rules no elective relief is available, the consequences being imposed by law (deduction denial or inclusion of income) without taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable worldwide turnover for persons resident, domiciled or seated in Switzerland (Article 21(1) VAT Act); non-Swiss entities supplying taxable services in Switzerland are subject to mandatory registration without any turnover threshold pursuant to Article 21(3) VAT Act unless the Federal Tax Administration has granted an exemption under Article 21(5); voluntary registration is available under Article 21(4) where turnover is below the threshold; current federal rates applicable as of 1 January 2024 are 8.1% standard, 2.6% reduced, and 3.8% special accommodation rate; import VAT is collected by customs on importation into Swiss territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed for (i) export supplies of goods physically transported from Switzerland abroad under Article 52 VAT Act; (ii) transport and towage services supplied directly in connection with exported goods, including deep-sea towage and salvage services ancillary to such export, under Article 23(2)(4) VAT Act; and (iii) services performed or utilised outside Swiss territory, such as offshore towage and maritime salvage on the high seas, which are treated as outside the scope of Swiss VAT or zero-rated where the B2B place-of-supply rule locates the supply at the recipient's establishment abroad (Article 23(2)(6) VAT Act). To qualify, the invoice must be addressed to a foreign recipient, the service must be performed offshore or connected to goods leaving Switzerland, and the proceeds must be received from abroad; salvage or towage performed within Swiss territorial waters or for a Swiss-resident customer is taxable at 8.1%.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / déclaration d'exportation) lodged through e-dec and validated by FOCBS/BAZG with unique movement reference number (MRN) and export tariff line
  • Ocean bill of lading (Konnossement) or equivalent multimodal waybill / salvage service report evidencing cross-border carriage or offshore performance coordinates
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT credit advice (Bankgutschrift) demonstrating receipt of foreign currency proceeds by the Swiss exporting entity from the foreign counterparty

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping or digital seal system is operated by ESTV. Switzerland applies the QR-Bill (QR-Rechnung) payment standard for CHF-denominated invoices as of 1 January 2020, requiring a QR code and IBAN; the QR-Bill complements conventional e-invoicing formats (XML, ZUGFeRD/Swiss companion) and is accepted as evidence of remittance. With effect from 1 January 2026, Swiss Bundesrat ordinance requires domestic businesses to be able to receive B2B e-invoices in a structured electronic format, with SMEs required by 1 January 2027; exporters must retain electronic customs declarations via e-dec and may submit them via the digital TARGO/Plate platform; there is no requirement for customs declarations or invoices to pass fiscal clearance through ESTV before export.

Federal Tax Administration audits verify export documents, bills of lading, customs declarations, and inward remittance evidence; records must be retained for 10 years. If the export zero-rating conditions are not substantiated or payment is not received within the 180-day period, ESTV retrospectively disallows the 0% rate and reassesses the supply at 8.1% VAT with default interest and late-payment interest. Standard assessment limitation is 5 years under Article 43 VAT Act, extended to 10 years in cases of deliberate evasion or tax fraud. Simple VAT violations carry fines up to CHF 25,000 under Article 182 VAT Act; aggravated VAT fraud under Article 183 VAT Act carries imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to CHF 30,000, with joint liability of the company and corporate officers. Fraudulent zero-rating claims also trigger anti-money-laundering reporting obligations and potential revocation of the Swiss fiscal representative mandate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Swiss franc on both current account and capital account under Article 1 of the Foreign Exchange Ordinance; no prior administrative authorization, central-bank approval, or exchange-control licence is required for cross-border payments, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, loan servicing, or settlement of maritime salvage and towage claims. The Swiss franc is freely floating and is not subject to any surrender or conversion quota. Practical frictions are limited to (i) the statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax on Swiss-source dividends, which can be reduced to a rate as low as 0% or partially refunded under an applicable double-taxation treaty where the beneficial owner qualifies, and (ii) the mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation imposed on Swiss banks for individual cross-border transactions equal to or exceeding CHF 25 million per calendar month, which is a statistical disclosure duty rather than a restriction on convertibility. For a Swiss AG active in deep-sea towage and salvage, there is no exchange-control barrier to invoicing in CHF, EUR, or USD, nor to maintaining foreign-currency accounts at a FINMA-regulated bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export earnings, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to bring foreign-currency export proceeds onshore. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (CH_AG) engaged in offshore towage and salvage may lawfully retain receivables denominated in CHF, EUR, or USD in domestic or foreign-currency accounts for an indefinite period. The only formalities are: (i) the company's annual financial statements must reflect the resulting currency exposure in accordance with Swiss GAAP FER; (ii) cross-border transactions at or above CHF 25 million in a calendar month must be reported by the instructing Swiss bank to the SNB in the balance-of-payments statistics; and (iii) proceeds received from salvage arbitration (e.g., Lloyd's Open Form awards) must be cleared through a Swiss-regulated payment channel to satisfy the enhanced due-diligence expectations of FINMA Circular 2016/07. There is no obligation to hedge, no conversion requirement at the time of receipt, and no penalty for holding export proceeds in a third currency.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank, Berne head office), acting with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF); exchange-control and payment-policy supervision are exercised through the Foreign Exchange Ordinance (Devisenverordnung / Ordonnance sur les opérations de change, SR 941.131), while prudential and anti-money-laundering supervision of banks is exercised by FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) under the Banking Act of 8 November 1934 (BankG / SR 952.0) and the Anti-Money Laundering Act of 10 October 1997 (GwG / SR 955.0).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Domestic FINMA-licensed fintechs and neobanks, such as Yapeal, Swissquote, and neon, are rated Medium for treasury sub-functions (day-to-day liquidity, low-value SEPA payments, FX execution) and can be integrated as secondary accounts, but they do not provide documentary-credit capacity, escrow-settlement infrastructure, or Lloyd's salvage disbursement rails, and are therefore not viable as the primary banking relationship for a Medium-Risk-Enhanced ISIC 5222 entity. Foreign unauthorised e-money institutions and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Intergiro) are rated Low and are legally unattractive for a Swiss AG: FINMA treats unlicensed deposit-taking and payment intermediation as a regulatory offence, exposure to non-FINMA EMIs triggers enhanced due-diligence scrutiny under GwG Articles 3 and 8, and the absence of a Swiss settlement account (SIC membership or Swiss correspondent network) prevents compliant handling of LOF salvage awards and escrow disbursements. A hybrid stack is acceptable and common: Tier-1 Swiss bank for governance, escrow and clearing, plus one FINMA-licensed fintech for FX conversion and worker disbursements, provided the board's treasury policy explicitly limits both aggregate float and per-transaction limits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital and shareholder-loan injections must be routed exclusively through a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank using SWIFT GPI (Global Payments Innovation) MT103 messages with explicit and non-manipulable purpose coding (e.g., 'CAPT - Equity Capital Contribution', 'Equity financing of salvage operations', or the ISO 20022 purpose 'MINI/CAPR') and full remitter identification including legal name, address, LEI, and tax identifier. The statutory capital of the CH_AG of at least CHF 100,000 (with minimum CHF 50,000 paid-in at incorporation) must be deposited into a dedicated capital-escrow account (Libor-/Einzahlungskonto) in the name of the company at the onboarding Tier-1 bank, evidenced for the commercial register by a bank confirmation (Bankbescheinigung) certifying that the funds are at the free disposal of the board. Shareholding documentation, a resolution of the extraordinary general meeting, the notarised subscription statement, source-of-funds declarations, and the UBO register extract must accompany the wire; in addition, the Swiss instructing bank will report the transaction to the SNB in the balance-of-payments statistics if the monthly cumulative cross-border flow equals or exceeds CHF 25 million. No exchange-control permission, SNB licence, or prior authorisation is required; the only admissible funding instruments are telegraphic transfer, SEPA credit transfer, Fedwire/CHIPS, and SWIFT MT103/202 cover, each with a matching purpose code and reconciled against the ledger as consolidated equity or subordinated shareholder loan.Outbound disbursements for towage, salvage, wreck removal, fuel, port charges, and crew wages must be cleared through the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-customer transfers for operational payments and salvage-award disbursements to shipowners, insurers, and P&I clubs, with CHF clearing across SIX Interbank Clearing (SIC), EUR clearing via SEPA and T2 RTGS, and USD clearing via Fedwire or CHIPS through the Swiss bank's correspondent network; (ii) SWIFT MT700/MT710 documentary credits (commercial letters of credit) under UCP 600 for the reimbursement of tug-company hire and emergency-salvage commitments, issued or confirmed by the Tier-1 Swiss bank; (iii) the Lloyds Standard Form of Salvage Agreement (LOF) wire protocol, under which salvage awards agreed through the Lloyds Salvage Arbitration Branch or negotiated settlements are paid by escrow release instructions requiring dual board authorisation and a compliance attestation of the underlying award; and (iv) escrow disbursement instructions for multimillion-claim settlements, released only against a jointly signed SWIFT MT103 plus a written award notice and the bank's final sanctions screen. Aggregate outbound wires in a single currency equivalent to or above CHF 25 million per calendar month attract the same SNB statistical reporting duty, and every outbound USD wire must carry a correct SWIFT mandatory field 70 (remittance information) referencing the contract number and the salvage-master's report to prevent downgrade or interdict by a correspondent bank.Outright OTC FX forwards in EUR/CHF and USD/CHF with a FINMA-regulated Swiss counterparty, executed under a validated ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex and strictly within board-approved notional limits (no leverage above the underlying towage/salvage exposure).FX swaps and roll-over swaps in USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, and EUR/USD used to fund daily operational liquidity mismatches without taking open speculative positions.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) aligned with long-term ship-finance debt and charter-hire liabilities, converting USD or EUR debt service into CHF at fixed or floating rates.Plain vanilla FX options (calls and puts) and zero-cost collars (risk reversals) covering salvage-award receipts and tug-charter disbursements for a horizon not exceeding 24 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on illiquid emerging-market currencies (e.g., NGN, BDT, IDR, EGP) that may arise from wreck-removal or port-state claims in developing jurisdictions, entered into with the Swiss bank's emerging-markets desk and subject to SECO/OFAC/EU sanctions screening of both the counterparty and the reference currency.Interest rate swaps (IRS) associated with the underlying vessel-financing facility, considered permissible treasury tools when used strictly for liability management of the CH_AG's maritime loan book, and excluded from propriety trading by a written treasury policy approved by the board.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Domestic FINMA-licensed fintechs and neobanks, such as Yapeal, Swissquote, and neon, are rated Medium for treasury sub-functions (day-to-day liquidity, low-value SEPA payments, FX execution) and can be integrated as secondary accounts, but they do not provide documentary-credit capacity, escrow-settlement infrastructure, or Lloyd's salvage disbursement rails, and are therefore not viable as the primary banking relationship for a Medium-Risk-Enhanced ISIC 5222 entity. Foreign unauthorised e-money institutions and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Intergiro) are rated Low and are legally unattractive for a Swiss AG: FINMA treats unlicensed deposit-taking and payment intermediation as a regulatory offence, exposure to non-FINMA EMIs triggers enhanced due-diligence scrutiny under GwG Articles 3 and 8, and the absence of a Swiss settlement account (SIC membership or Swiss correspondent network) prevents compliant handling of LOF salvage awards and escrow disbursements. A hybrid stack is acceptable and common: Tier-1 Swiss bank for governance, escrow and clearing, plus one FINMA-licensed fintech for FX conversion and worker disbursements, provided the board's treasury policy explicitly limits both aggregate float and per-transaction limits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax (imposed on profit after tax; combined Zug cantonal/municipal effective rate approx. 11.5%–12.0% on pre-tax profit)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on distributions from a Swiss AG; Swiss-resident shareholders receive a full credit or refund; foreign beneficial owners may claim a refund down to the applicable DTA rate, generally 0%–15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within six months after the financial year-end; for a 31 December year-end the filing deadline in Canton Zug is 30 June of the following year; written extension requests may be granted only prior to the deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 30 days after the end of the calendar quarter in which a taxable distribution (including hidden distributions) was made (Art. 11 VStG).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory country-by-country reporting for Swiss-parented MNE groups or constituent entities of a foreign group with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million; Switzerland imposes no fixed statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold at the taxpayer level, but the tax administration may order documentation on a risk basis; for maritime towage and salvage entities a contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer-pricing study is strongly advised where intercompany turnover exceeds CHF 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies an economic-substance doctrine through the Federal Act on Maritime Navigation — Swiss flag registration, statutory seat, and effective general management in Switzerland are preconditions for the direct-tax shipping exemption; under the OECD Inclusive Framework and modern LOB/PPT clauses in Swiss DTAs, a shell AG holding passive towage contracts for a foreign parent is denied treaty relief, the 35% WHT is reimposed, and the maritime tax privilege is retroactively revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Reassessment through the extended limitation period (10 years for wilful concealment); penalty of one-third of the evaded federal and cantonal tax, increased to two-thirds for forged documents (Art. 175 DBG); tax fraud (Art. 186 DBG) carries imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to CHF 10,000; the vessel may be subject to maritime arrest to secure the tax claim.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or tolerated the false declaration are criminally liable as organs (Art. 12 DBG), expose themselves to personal imprisonment and fines, and are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax under Art. 754 OR; Swiss tax-criminal procedure permits exit bans for defendants domiciled abroad and seizure of private bank accounts.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Swiss tax residency and treaty benefits; reassessment of revenue in the hands of the foreign principal; retroactive application of the 35% WHT on all distributions; exchange of information under the Swiss Mutual Assistance Act; deregistration of the Swiss-flag vessel and revocation of the navigation licence.

Director personal liability: The board may be sued for the unpaid WHT and taxes for breach of corporate purpose (Art. 717 OR); directors who knowingly enabled the artificial structure face criminal sanctions for false attestations, can be struck from the commercial register with a prohibition on future mandates, and may be subjected to exit restrictions and extradition risks under the Swiss Criminal Procedure Code.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment with reclassification of the shortfall as a hidden dividend subject to 35% WHT plus default interest; an intentional mispricing penalty of up to one-third of the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); the cantonal tax authority may issue a documentation order with daily fines for late delivery.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length contracts are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and WHT under Art. 754 OR; in cases of falsified invoices, directors face tax-fraud imprisonment and a personal fine; the foreign parent is indirectly exposed through Swiss enforcement proceedings.
