Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office arrangements are generally not acceptable as the registered office in Quebec for a limited partnership; a limited partnership is not a legal person, but the declaration of registration must disclose an establishment in Quebec with a physical address where records and documents are kept and where notices may be served. The general partner must maintain a physical establishment. A virtual office providing only a mailing address will be rejected. A serviced office with a physical presence (dedicated desk/meeting room, mail handling, and personnel) may be accepted only if the arrangement is genuine and the establishment is open during normal business hours. For an e-commerce jewelry/watches business with no physical storefront, the registered office may be the general partner's business office, an accountant's office, or a law firm's office with a physical suite in Quebec.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow/blocked capital account is prescribed for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are fixed freely in the partnership agreement (money, property, or industry), must be delivered as agreed, and the general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations while limited partners' exposure is capped at their contribution provided they do not take part in management (arts. 2236–2255 CQLR c CCQ-1991).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal GST/HST) - www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; Revenu Québec - www.revenuquebec.ca (Québec Sales Tax, QST/TVQ)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership (or foreign LP making an extra-provincial declaration) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Québec enterprise number (NEQ) if the LP has an establishment in Québec. Step 2: Apply to the Canada Revenue Agency using Business Registration Online or Form RC1 to obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST account; the BN is the federal tax identification number. Step 3: Open a QST account with Revenu Québec using the NEQ and file the required registration; registration for GST/HST and QST is mandatory if annual taxable supplies exceed the applicable CAD 30,000 threshold, and may be mandatory for a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada regardless of the small-supplier threshold. Step 4: Appoint an authorized Canadian representative if no Canadian resident partner exists; non-resident ownership is permitted and no minimum capital is required for the LP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent: a Canadian Limited Partnership is not the taxpayer for federal income tax or QST in its own capacity; income and loss are allocated to and reported by the partners in their own tax returns, with the LP filing an information return if applicable)

Available election forms

None under Canadian federal or Québec provincial law: Canada has no check-the-box election for partnerships, and the LP’s fiscal transparency is mandatory. Limited statutory elections exist only in narrow administrative contexts, such as a partnership fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1 where all partners consent and CRA accepts a business purpose. Hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4(3) and (4) operate mechanically to deny deductions in deduction/non-inclusion arrangements; no hybrid-mismatch election or remedial designation is available. Québec’s Taxation Act and Revenu Québec practice generally align with the federal pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies. For GST/HST, the small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies; for QST, the threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in Québec. A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada is generally required to register even if the CAD 30,000 threshold is not met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, tangible personal propertysuch as jewelry, watches, and fashion accessories is zero-rated for GST/HST if it is exported by the supplier as part of the supply, delivered by the supplier to an address outside Canada, or made available to a carrier for physical export as part of the supply. For QST, an equivalent zero-rating applies when goods are transferred outside Québec. The LP must retain contemporaneous documentary proof of physical exportation, such as carrier tracking, waybills, customs declarations, and confirmation that title and risk passed outside Canada. A mere customer declaration or foreign billing address is not sufficient without objective export evidence.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration or Summary Report (including G13 export declaration where mandatory for goods exceeding CAD 2,500 or controlled goods)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract showing the foreign consignee
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical dispatch from Canada and destination outside Canada
  • Carrier manifest or postal/courier tracking report confirming exportation
  • Foreign exchange/bank remittance receipt or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds

No mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime exists in Canada or Québec. GST/HST and QST invoices must contain the prescribed statutory information; CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices provided they are legible and can be produced on audit. Revenu Québec requires invoices issued by a Québec establishment to be drafted in French. There are no API-based clearance requirements, digital fiscal stamping, or real-time pre-clearance reporting obligations.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating is treated as a false statement or tax evasion. CRA may reassess outside the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established. Reassessed GST/HST and QST amounts attract arrears interest and civil penalties, including a 25% false-statement penalty under the Excise Tax Act and equivalent civil penalties under Québec’s tax administration laws; evasion penalties, criminal fines, and imprisonment may apply in serious cases. Refunds attributable to zero-rating are retrospectively clawed back if the export documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, because the supply is then re-characterized as taxable in Canada from the original tax period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency; no exchange-control permits are required for cross-border payments or capital injections. Profit repatriation from a Quebec limited partnership is generally unrestricted, provided that applicable Canadian withholding taxes on non-resident partners are addressed and that the LP's beneficial-ownership and tax-residency records are complete. For a Canadian-resident LP earning active business income, distributions to non-resident limited partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding or income-tax treaty reduction; the limited partnership itself is a pass-through entity for Canadian tax purposes. Practical convertibility hurdles are limited to bank AML/KYC screening, FATCA/CRS reporting obligations, and FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. No repatriation timeline is imposed by the Bank of Canada or Revenu Quebec. Cross-border electronic funds transfers in excess of C$10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC, and supporting export documentation should be retained by the bank to evidence the commercial nature of transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision; Canada has no dedicated central-bank exchange-control approval authority. Anti-money laundering supervision over cross-border wire transfers is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not recognize the EU-style 'electronic money institution' licence, so an offshore EMI cannot act as a regulated deposit-taking or escrow institution in Quebec. The EMI may be used only as a collection- or disbursement-rail if it is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For escrow-grade capital injection and trade settlement, a Schedule I chartered bank account is mandatory. Stripe and PayPal are viable as commercial settlement rails for e-commerce, but they must be layered on top of a Canadian bank account and must not be used to disguise third-party settlement funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be effected by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the remitter's bank to the Quebec LP's operating or escrow account. The wire must include full originator and beneficiary legal names, the LP account number, and a precise remittance narrative referencing 'Capital contribution to [Quebec LP name] under limited partnership agreement dated [date]'. Canada does not impose a statutory purpose code at the SWIFT level, so where the bank's platform requires a purpose menu, select the category indicating 'corporate treasury / equity injection' and attach a PDF of the LP agreement authorizing the contribution. All inbound transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; the bank may request copies of the partnership agreement, proof of identity of the remitting partner, and source-of-funds evidence. Funds originating from an offshore EMI should be declined unless the EMI is a registered Canadian MSB and the ultimate funder is identifiable.Trade settlements to foreign jewellery and watch suppliers should be executed via SWIFT MT103 wires with a UETR reference, using the clearing bank's real-time foreign exchange rate or a pre-agreed forward rate. For large or first-time transactions with overseas manufacturers, a documentary commercial letter of credit is recommended to protect both parties; the bank should issue an irrevocable LC at the request of the Quebec LP after reviewing the underlying sale contract, proforma invoice, and shipping terms. For recurring e-commerce refunds and supplier deposits, Stripe and PayPal commercial payouts are suitable only for low-value, high-volume transactions and must be fully reconciled against settlement reports.
