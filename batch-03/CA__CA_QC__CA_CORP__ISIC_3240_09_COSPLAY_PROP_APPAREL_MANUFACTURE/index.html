Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal Income Tax Act administration, GST/HST policy owner, and single-window Business Registration Online portal; Revenu Québec - direct administration of GST/HST in Quebec under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement, administration of the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act, and employer payroll remittances. Corporate registration is completed through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar). Principal portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec CLIC Revenu (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec-based business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA and the 10-digit Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, the latter being mandatory within 60 days of commencing commercial activity in Quebec; (3) register for GST/HST and QST through Revenu Québec's CLIC Revenu business registration service (Quebec administers both federal GST and provincial QST for Quebec-based registrants), which issues a GST/HST account number (BN with 'RT' program) and a distinct QST account number; (4) register for Quebec corporate income tax (CO-17 series) and any employer/worker-compensation accounts. For foreign (non-resident) suppliers: a non-resident that carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Quebec must obtain a CRA BN using Form RC1 or RC1A (Business Registration Online), then register with Revenu Québec for QST where the non-resident is not a small supplier, imports goods, or supplies intangible personal property or digital services to Quebec consumers under the special deemed-registration rules. Non-residents may use a Canadian representative for service of documents but remain personally liable for registration and remittance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and under Title III of the Quebec Taxation Act; it has no domestic pass-through or disregarded-entity status. Canada has no check-the-box regime. For U.S. federal tax purposes, a Canadian business corporation is a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. section 301.7701-2(b)(8) and therefore cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. The entity is subject to tax at the corporate level on worldwide income with territorial sourcing rules for non-residents.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (a) GST/HST/QST: voluntary registration while below the small-supplier threshold, annual filing election, quick method of accounting, simplified input tax credit method, group registration election for qualifying closely related corporations, and joint returns under the coordinated Quebec GST/QST registration system; (b) Income Tax Act: section 85 rollover election for tax-deferred property transfers into the corporation, amalgamation and winding-up elections under section 87/88, section 256 ownership-control elections, section 261 functional-currency reporting election, and various capital cost allowance pool elections. Anti-hybrid: Canadian domestic hybrid-mismatch rules implementing BEPS Action 2 restrict double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; there is no election to convert the Canadian corporation into a hybrid or flow-through entity, and any U.S. LLC or other hybrid entity owned by the corporation remains subject to the Canadian hybrid-mismatch provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST and QST 'small supplier' threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of tax) determined on a trailing four-calendar-quarter basis. Registration is not mandatory below the threshold, but voluntary registration is available and is substantively required for any supplier wishing to claim input tax credits on export inputs. Non-resident digital suppliers of intangible personal property or services to Quebec consumers are subject to special deemed-registration rules once their total taxable supplies in Quebec exceed CAD 30,000, irrespective of physical presence. A supplier zero-rating exported goods may also be required to register to import goods without paying the GST/QST on the importation and to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I, subsection 1 of the Excise Tax Act (GST) and the corresponding QST provision. Manufactured cosplay prop apparel, theatrical costume robes, and synthetic wigs exported by the supplier to a non-resident buyer qualify for 0% GST/QST provided: (i) the goods are supplied in the course of the supplier's commercial activity; (ii) the goods are exported by the supplier without use in Canada, generally within 60 days of delivery, as part of the ordinary commercial practice; and (iii) the supplier retains full proof of export for six years. Classification under HTS Chapter 61/62 (apparel), Chapter 65 (wigs), or Chapter 95 (festive/entertainment articles) does not affect zero-rating. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated only where the service recipient is a non-resident and the service is not performed on tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance (unless the property is subsequently exported under qualifying conditions) and does not relate to real property situated in Canada. Input tax credits (ITCs / RTDs) on manufacturing inputs remain fully claimable. No export certificate or permit is required for textile costumes or wigs unless the goods contain endangered species, controlled cultural property, or restricted military materials.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA G13 export declaration or ACI eManifest transmission to the Canada Border Services Agency; where the destination is the United States, retain the USMCA/CUSMA Exporter's Certificate of Origin when preferential tariff treatment is claimed.
  • Bill of Lading - through bill of lading, air waybill, courier consignment note, or Canada Post CN22/CN23 customs declaration naming the non-resident consignee and foreign destination, with the Canadian exporter identified as shipper or principal party in interest.

Canada and Quebec do not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) fiscal clearance regime, mandatory digital stamping, or pre-approval e-invoicing requirement. Both GST/HST and QST operate on a self-assessment periodic return basis (monthly, quarterly, or annual remittance through CRA or Revenu Québec electronic filing). Canada has adopted the PEPPOL e-invoicing standard and operates a pan-Canadian e-invoice trust framework (voluntary B2B/B2G exchange infrastructure, operational 2025). Quebec registrants must retain invoice data satisfying GST/HST and QST disclosure requirements (vendor legal name and registration number, date, description, amount, tax status, and reference) for six years. Point-of-sale electronic sales recording systems used in Quebec must meet Revenu Québec's certified billing-software specifications where applicable; there is no obligation to affix a digital stamp and no market-wide clearance approval.

CRA and Revenu Québec routinely audit export zero-rating substantiation. If proof of export is absent, incomplete, or inconsistent with invoice, consignee, payment, or freight-forwarding records, the zero-rating is denied, the supplier is reassessed for unremitted GST/QST on the full selling price, and statutory arrears interest accrues from the original filing due date. Civil penalties: 50% of the understated net tax for false statements or omissions under section 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST provision; gross-negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax may also be asserted. Criminal prosecution for evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act / section 239 of the Income Tax Act may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Reassessments are generally barred after four years, but the period extends to ten years where misrepresentation is attributable to carelessness, negligence, or wilful default, with no time limit in cases of fraud. Participation in circular cross-border payment arrangements or invoice-carousel schemes may additionally trigger reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and denial of all related input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange controls. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and legally exchangeable. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, and royalties are freely transferable subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% standard, reducible by treaty) and any sanctions/AML verification. No exchange rate approval or central-bank permit is required for inward investment or outward remittance, but transactions are reportable to FINTRAC for cross-border transfers over CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters are not required to surrender export receipts to the central bank, nor obliged to convert or repatriate trade proceeds within a statutory deadline. Receipts may be held in foreign currency with Canadian authorized banks or offshore accounts, subject to record-keeping and tax reporting under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority, with foreign exchange administration coordinated by the Department of Finance under the Investment Canada Act and FINTRAC/OSFI oversight under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate feasibility but legally restricted as a full banking substitute. Electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment service providers such as Stripe or Shopify Payments are not authorized 'banks' under the Bank Act and cannot accept deposit liabilities. They may operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs)/Payment Service Providers registered with FINTRAC, and can support e-commerce settlement. For treasury liquidity and clearing, a chartered bank account remains mandatory; an EMI account can be held as a collection/ancillary rail for low-balance operational flows, subject to FINTRAC reporting and the corporation's banking resolution.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec general corporate headline rate is 26.5% (federal 15% plus Quebec 11.5%). A qualified Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) carrying on active business income is subject to a combined reduced headline rate of 12.2% on the first C$500,000 of active business income (federal small business rate 9% plus Quebec SME rate 3.2%).
