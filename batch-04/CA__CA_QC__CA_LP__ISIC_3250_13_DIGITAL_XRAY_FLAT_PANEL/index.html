Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital and no mandatory escrow account apply to a Québec limited partnership. Capital contributions, contribution timing, withdrawal rights, and partner accounts must be governed by the limited partnership agreement. The general partner should document each contribution and maintain accounting records.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, occupancy, and fire-prevention approvals; maintain fire detection, suppression, evacuation, emergency-response, and hazardous-material controls. Structural and electrical work must comply with the Québec Construction Code and applicable Régie du bâtiment du Québec requirements. Register with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail where required, implement workplace hazard assessments and training, and address X-ray or other radiation-emitting equipment under the Radiation Emitting Devices Act, applicable regulations, and Health Canada safety codes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), portal https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (Business Registration Online, GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account). Provincial (Quebec): Revenu Québec, portal https://www.revenuquebec.ca (Clic Revenu). Export and customs declarations are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec limited partnership must: (1) register the partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, adding GST/HST (RT) and payroll (RP) accounts as applicable; (3) register with Revenu Québec for the QST account using the prescribed registration application or the Clic Revenu online service when making taxable supplies in Quebec or exceeding the C$30,000 threshold; (4) file partnership information returns (T5013 with CRA and CO-17 with Revenu Québec) and issue allocation schedules to each partner; non-resident partners must each obtain Canadian tax identification (individual or corporate BN) and are subject to Canadian withholding. Canada issues no separate TIN to the partnership itself; the LP is fiscally transparent and the partners are the statutory taxpayers.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The Income Tax Act (Canada) (ss. 96-103) and the Taxation Act (Quebec) treat a limited partnership as fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the limited partnership agreement; no entity-level income tax is imposed on the LP. Entity-level taxation arises only in anti-avoidance contexts, including ITA s. 94.1 (a Canadian investment fund predominantly financed by non-residents may be deemed a corporation), the SIFT partnership rules for publicly traded specified investment flow-throughs, and Quebec-specific special rules; a manufacturer of digital X-ray flat-panel detectors is not ordinarily caught. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Income Tax Act (Canada) (ss. 96-103) and the Taxation Act (Quebec) treat a limited partnership as fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the limited partnership agreement; no entity-level income tax is imposed on the LP. Entity-level taxation arises only in anti-avoidance contexts, including ITA s. 94.1 (a Canadian investment fund predominantly financed by non-residents may be deemed a corporation), the SIFT partnership rules for publicly traded specified investment flow-throughs, and Quebec-specific special rules; a manufacturer of digital X-ray flat-panel detectors is not ordinarily caught. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income is taxable in Canada; foreign-source income is allocated to partners and may be shielded by foreign tax credits in their home jurisdictions.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch regime under federal or Quebec law. Available statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1 (non-calendar year-end subject to anti-deferral rules); (ii) joint election under ITA s. 94.3(19) that may recharacterize interests in grandfathered foreign-affiliate structures; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 and 94.1-94.3) contain filing-based elections limiting or applying deeming consequences; (iv) non-resident withholding elections under Regulation 105 and Part XIII, including treaty-based waiver applications; (v) QST registrant elections on fiscal year-end and reporting frequency, and input tax credit allocation elections; (vi) for US partners, the LP may be classified as a foreign partnership under US check-the-box, but the Canadian side disregards that election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of taxable supplies in Canada (GST) and C$30,000 of taxable supplies in Quebec (QST) over the trailing four calendar quarters; mandatory registration upon exceeding either threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; registration is at the partnership level and covers all permanent establishments in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act, a sale of goods, including digital X-ray flat-panel detectors, is zero-rated where the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada or exports them as soon as is reasonable in the circumstances, and retains satisfactory evidence of export (CRA Memorandum 4-5-1). Under section 25.1, services supplied to a non-resident not present in Canada are zero-rated where the service is for the non-resident's consumption outside Canada or relates to exported goods; installation, commissioning, and software-update services bundled with exported detectors must be contractually identified as exported services to preserve the 0% rate. The QST mirrors federal zero-rating for qualifying exports. Zero-rating is claimed at the invoice level without prior CRA or Revenu Québec authorization, but the burden of proof rests on the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or electronic declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including the correct 10-digit HS code, declared value, and ultimate destination country;
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill: authenticated transport document evidencing physical movement to a foreign destination (ocean bill of lading, multimodal waybill, or truck manifest) cross-referencing the commercial invoice and export declaration;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment proof: SWIFT MT103, telegraphic-transfer credit advice, or foreign-exchange conversion record evidencing receipt of the sales proceeds in a convertible currency from the non-resident buyer within the 180-day repatriation window; although not a domestic statutory condition in Canada, it is the primary audit evidence supporting the zero-rating claim.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API validation, or digital stamping requirement for invoices; the CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, and PEPPOL-based invoices. Quebec has announced a phased mandatory electronic invoicing regime for B2B/B2G transactions using the PEPPOL BIS format and certified PEPPOL access points, expected to commence between 2026 and 2029 based on taxpayer turnover, with the largest taxpayers first; until the applicable commencement date, electronic invoicing is voluntary and conventional invoicing remains compliant. GST/HST and QST returns must generally be filed electronically (NETFILE and Clic Revenu) once prescribed turnover or filing-frequency thresholds are exceeded.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated export within the normal four-year limitation period, which becomes unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, willful default, or fraud is established. If zero-rating is denied, the unpaid GST (5%) and QST (9.975%) are clawed back with daily compound interest plus penalties: 25% of the understated net tax for false statements or omissions, rising to 50% for gross negligence, and failure-to-file penalties of 1% per month capped at 12% of net tax (minimum C$250); Quebec applies parallel penalties. Fraudulent export schemes are criminal offences under the Excise Tax Act and Criminal Code, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment; CRA may also seize refunds, require security, and assert liability against general partners of the LP, whose exposure is unlimited.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange-control approval gates for debt, equity, trade, or profit remittance. Non-resident limited partners may repatriate partnership distributions freely; however, any Canadian-source taxable income allocated to a non-resident partner must be reported to the CRA and may be subject to withholding under Part XIII or Part I filing obligations, and all cross-border payments are screened for sanctions and AML compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Canadian exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at domestic or regulated foreign banks; no surrender to the Bank of Canada is required. Reporting requirements arise under FINTRAC for cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and under the CRA for specified foreign property reporting where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority, but Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration under the Bank Act or Currency Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises chartered banks, FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT reporting, and the Minister of Finance sets the overarching legislative perimeter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Canadian law distinguishes chartered banks from money-service businesses (MSBs); an offshore digital EMI is not a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act and cannot provide Canadian clearing codes, ACSS electronic funds transfer, or LVTS settlement. To process cross-border payments in Canada, an EMI must register as an MSB with FINTRAC, obtain a Quebec money-services business licence from the AMF if operating in Quebec, and generally maintain a settlement account with a Schedule I bank. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot securely receive export trade proceeds from a Quebec LP in CAD or hold corporate escrow funds without creating regulatory and concentration risk.
