Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Quebec limited partnership. The initial capital contribution is freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be made in money, property, or services. No statutory escrow account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the competent municipal fire service is required for any warehouse space storing lithium-ion batteries. Structural load and layout must satisfy municipal occupancy bylaws. Occupational health and safety clearance is addressed through self-assessment of emergency exits, electrical installations, and storage practices under the Act respecting occupational health and safety; CNESST employer registration is mandatory once employees are hired.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Quebec limited partnership. The initial capital contribution is freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be made in money, property, or services. No statutory escrow account is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account and GST/HST Internet Filing Service; Provincial: Revenu Québec — e-services portal for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) registration and filing.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a nine-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1; Step 2: Activate the GST/HST program account (RT) through the same registration, selecting the Canadian activity as 'online retail of portable power equipment'; Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec for a QST account using the online business registration service or Form LM-1, providing the federal BN and Quebec establishment address; Step 4: If the limited partnership has no physical Quebec presence, appoint a responsible natural person located in Quebec or Canada as the authorized contact/agent for tax administration; Step 5: Retain the partnership resolution and constituent LLP/LP declaration to evidence the entity's authority to bind the partnership for GST/QST filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent under federal and Quebec law: income, gains, losses, and credits flow to the partners. For GST/HST and QST administration, the partnership itself may be registered as a registrant in its own capacity, but it is not a taxpayer for income tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. A limited partnership cannot elect to be treated as a corporation; pass-through status under section 96 of the Income Tax Act and the Taxation Act (Quebec) is mandatory. No hybrid-mismatch entity election is available internally. For cross-border treaty purposes, the LP remains transparent and may be treated as an intermediate flow-through entity under applicable double-taxation conventions, subject to the specific foreign tax law characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters for federal GST/HST small-supplier relief; the Quebec QST registration threshold is likewise CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Quebec over the same four-quarter lookback period. The threshold applies to all revenue channels, including online marketplace sales, unless the marketplace itself is the deemed supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST/HST rate applies to tangible personal property exported from Canada when the supplier or its delivering agent ships the goods outside Canada under an export sale, possession and risk of loss pass outside Canada, and the goods are not acquired for consumption/use in Canada. Portable solar generators and panels sold online to foreign customers qualify as zero-rated exports when physically shipped abroad, including courier fulfilment. QST zero-rating follows the same destination principle under Quebec rules. Offshore services qualify only if performed entirely outside Canada or directly incidental to an exported supply; remote digital assistance must be tested separately for place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A) or the applicable authorized export report filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration/AES system for goods leaving Canada by commercial shipment.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / final carrier invoice showing the goods departed Canada and were delivered to the foreign destination address.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign payment bank credit evidence linking the customer's remittance to the corresponding export invoice (retained as audit corroboration of the cross-border commercial settlement).

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory government API clearance, or digital tax stamping is in force for GST/HST or QST. CRA and Revenu Québec accept standard electronic invoices in accessible formats (PDF/XML); taxpayers must preserve complete transactional records in legible electronic form. Businesses may voluntarily use PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing, but there is no mandatory clearance before invoice issuance for standard B2B or B2C exports.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, and indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established. If the export documentation is incomplete or the transaction is recharacterized as a domestic supply, the full GST/HST and QST become payable on the selling price, with compound interest and late-filing penalties. Deliberately false export declarations may attract gross-negligence penalties and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec taxation statutes, with retrospective clawback of any input tax credits claimed on purchases attributable to the export supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both capital and current accounts with no general exchange-control legislation. Profit remittances by non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% unless reduced by treaty) and Canadian partnership reporting requirements, but no central bank approval is required for conversion or transfer of funds. Quebec imposes additional provincial registration and reporting obligations, not FX approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export proceeds surrender rules, no statutory conversion timelines, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts within Canada or abroad, subject to FINTRAC transaction reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or equivalent) and deemed tax residency provisions for controlled foreign corporations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange transactions are monitored indirectly through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Regulated Canadian EMIs and payment service providers such as Wise (TransferWise), Payoneer, and Nuvei offer multi-currency settlement for cross-border ecommerce disbursements, but they are not substitutes for tier-1 banking in Quebec. The Canadian Retail Payment Activities Act requires registration for payment service providers handling Canadian CAD clearing; foreign EMIs without Canadian registration may only support non-CAD corridors with limited AML counterparty coverage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require inbound partner equity contributions to be routed via SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or MT202 COV (cover payment) to the partnership's Canadian operational account. For statistical and tax clarity, the remittance information field must reference 'Contribution under Limited Partnership Agreement [date]' and the purpose code 'Direct Investment in Canada' (BOP Code 100/300). The bank will require a completed Beneficiary Ownership and Source of Funds declaration, plus, for amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting triggers.
