Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; tax transparency is mandatory for limited partnerships. Available elections and positions: (i) selection of fiscal treatment of non-resident partners under Income Tax Act s.115(4) with Form NR3010 to compute taxable income on a net basis; (ii) GST/HST partnership election under ETA ss.272.1 and 274 to treat the partnership as a separate registrant and permit quarterly/annual attribution choices; (iii) QST counterpart election under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; (iv) election to keep the partnership's GST/HST fiscal year aligned with the calendar year or fiscal year of partners; (v) election under ETA s.156 to treat closely-related corporations (where the general partner is a corporation) as a single registrant, subject to Quebec QST rules; (vi) non-resident partner deemed supply election under ETA s.273 in narrow agency scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a non-resident or resident supplier is a small supplier and exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration unless taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the previous four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.240(1)); identical CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold applies for QST under section 419 of the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; e-commerce sellers almost always exceed this threshold and become statutorily obliged to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible outdoor camping equipment (tents, sleeping bags, lanterns, stoves, and related gear under HS Chapter 6306 / 9401 / 7321, ISIC 4791) are zero-rated for GST/HST when sold to a non-resident purchaser and delivered by the supplier or at the supplier's direction to a destination outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1); the same goods are zero-rated for QST under Schedule III of the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax. Zero-rating requires proof that the goods physically left Canada within a reasonable commercial time (CRA administrative practice targets export within 60 days of shipment; absence of timely export voids zero-rating and converts the supply to taxable at 14.975%). Supplies of related offshore services, including freight, insurance, and fulfilment-centric logistics supplied to a non-resident for goods exported, also qualify as zero-rated where the services are acquired for consumption outside Canada. Sales to Canadian consumers via the LP's online storefront attract full GST/HST and QST, with deemed collection obligations on marketplace facilitators where applicable under the July 2021 digital economy rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA B13A form (or CAED electronic export reporting) where goods value exceeds CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods, with Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) unique transaction ID
  • Commercial invoice with ISIC 4791 activity code, precise HS classification of camping equipment, unit value, currency, and foreign destination address
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent courier waybill (DHL, FedEx, UPS, Canada Post) showing release of goods from Canada and delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: wire transfer record, Stripe or PayPal payout statement, payment processor settlement report, or letter of credit evidencing payment in foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Secondary forensic support: carrier delivery confirmation, foreign customs entry record from the destination country, freight forwarder certificate, and exporter's internal inventory and shipping log demonstrating physical cross-border movement

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices exists at the federal or Quebec level; CRA and Revenu Quebec require that invoices be issued for all taxable supplies and retained in paper or electronic form for at least six years with prescribed content (ETA Regulations, SOR/91-238: BN, QST number, date, description, amount, and tax components); Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary; Stripe, PayPal, and other PSP settlement reports must be preserved as source records; Quebec imposes civil and penal fines for false or omitted invoice data and for failure to provide an 'info on the invoice' where the recipient is a registrant; a Quebec tax invoice must display the QST registration number and, when GST applies, the GST registration number.

Canada has no per-se statutory 180-day currency repatriation deadline for export receipts, but CRA and Revenu Quebec apply a 180-day documentary and cash settlement window as an audit benchmark; consistent failure to document remittance within this period triggers Option 10 scrutiny, reassessment of the zero-rating, and reclassification of the export as a taxable domestic supply with arrears interest on the 14.975% combined tax. Retrospective assessments may be issued within four years of the original notice of assessment, and this window is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established (ETA s.298). False export claims attract: (i) a penalty of 25% of understated net tax for false statements (ETA s.285); (ii) a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ETA s.285.1, mirroring Income Tax Act s.163(2)); (iii) criminal prosecution on indictment with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years (ETA s.327); (iv) QST equivalents under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax, including penalty of 50% of the evaded QST and penal proceedings by Revenu Quebec. Genuine input tax credits claimed by the LP can be retroactively recovered, interest compounded daily at prescribed rates, and deliberate non-repatriation of export proceeds may be attacked as a deemed benefit to partners under Income Tax Act s.15(2) with attendant 5% monthly penalty exposure on non-arm's length shareholder loans and additional transfer-pricing adjustments under s.247.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible, freely floating currency and Canada maintains Article VIII status under the IMF Articles of Agreement. There is no distinction between capital and current account convertibility for compliance purposes; no prior authorization, central bank permit, or repatriation license is required for inward or outward cross-border transfers. Profit remittance hurdles are purely fiscal: the Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent, and each non-resident limited partner is deemed to carry on the partnership's Quebec business and must file and pay federal Part I tax (T1 or T2) on its allocable share of Canadian-source income, subject to treaty permanent-establishment thresholds. Quebec levies parallel income tax under the Taxation Act (RL-15 reporting), and distributions of already-taxed partnership earnings are freely wireable abroad without further exchange-control intervention. Interest on loans advanced by non-resident partners is Canadian-source and subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (reducible under treaty, with NR4/RL-16 slips), requiring a fully documented treasury and intercompany loan structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no haircut on export receipts derived from cross-border internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment. Proceeds of such sales may be retained indefinitely in CAD, USD, or any other convertible currency in the CA_LP's operating account at a Schedule I bank or a Desjardins caisse. Settlement of CAD/USD conversions occurs through the liquid interbank market, typically via CLS bank or Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) participant counterparties, without any central-bank reporting or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Government of Canada; it maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime but is not an exchange-control authority. The Minister of Finance retains residual powers under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act remnants of the former exchange-control framework, which was abolished with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. Day-to-day prudential supervision of Schedule I Canadian chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) record keeping, verification, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations, and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises Quebec-registered money-services businesses and cooperatives such as Desjardins.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High with dual federal and Quebec registration. Non-resident fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut-style platforms) are permissible for operational disbursements, supplier settlements, and payroll sub-accounts only where they hold a FINTRAC money-services-business registration under the PCMLTFA and, for Quebec operations, an AMF registration under the Act respecting money-services businesses (c. S-8.3). Such EMIs are not Schedule I banks, cannot take deposits legally, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot serve as the principal liquidity concentration account or a capital escrow depository for a Quebec LP. Payment service providers (Stripe/PayPal) used by the e-commerce merchant are further captured by the emerging Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regulatory perimeter once its PSP registration framework is fully operational. A sound blueprint therefore maintains the primary CA_LP treasury account at a tier-1 Quebec bank, conceding fintech EMIs a permitted but subsidiary role in disbursement, FX conversion, and cross-border vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscriptions from non-resident limited partners are executed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers (with MT202 COV cover instructions where an intermediary correspondent processes the USD leg) into the CA_LP's designated CAD/USD operational account at the Quebec branch. The remitter must state a clear purpose narrative, e.g., 'Capital contribution to Quebec limited partnership — online retail of outdoor camping equipment (ISIC 4791)', together with a copy of the registered partnership agreement, REQ extract, register of partners, and source-of-funds pack. Partner loans instead follow an intercompany lending protocol requiring an executed loan agreement, arm's-length interest, a T106 filing where applicable, and a standing NR4/RL-16 withholding mechanism for interest payments. No licensing, exchange-control permit, or central-bank approval is required for the inbound credit; however, FINTRAC regulation obliges the bank to record all incoming electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and to retain UBO documentation. The proceeds, once cleared, may be swept into the partnership's FX dealing line for conversion to CAD at the bank's treasury desk.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for CAD/USD supplier settlements, requiring exact beneficiary details and an invoice-level supporting pack; (ii) Commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) for high-value inventory purchases from Asia or other overseas suppliers, issued by the tier-1 bank against commercial invoice, packing list, full set of ocean bills of lading, inspection certificates, and Incoterms evidence; (iii) domestic Canadian rails (ACSS/LVTS in CAD and Fedwire/FedACH through US correspondents for USD) for North American vendor payments; (iv) card network settlement rails (Visa/Mastercard) through Stripe/PayPal, with funds periodically swept into the operational account after daily gross settlement, refunds, and chargeback reserves are reconciled; and (v) pre-authorized debits and internal book transfers for general administrative overhead in Quebec. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High with dual federal and Quebec registration. Non-resident fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut-style platforms) are permissible for operational disbursements, supplier settlements, and payroll sub-accounts only where they hold a FINTRAC money-services-business registration under the PCMLTFA and, for Quebec operations, an AMF registration under the Act respecting money-services businesses (c. S-8.3). Such EMIs are not Schedule I banks, cannot take deposits legally, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot serve as the principal liquidity concentration account or a capital escrow depository for a Quebec LP. Payment service providers (Stripe/PayPal) used by the e-commerce merchant are further captured by the emerging Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regulatory perimeter once its PSP registration framework is fully operational. A sound blueprint therefore maintains the primary CA_LP treasury account at a tier-1 Quebec bank, conceding fintech EMIs a permitted but subsidiary role in disbursement, FX conversion, and cross-border vendor payments.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal–Quebec general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% provincial); the LP is a fiscally transparent partnership, so the rate applies at the partner level, not at the entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. LP cash distributions are generally not subject to this dividend withholding because the partnership is fiscally transparent; non-resident partners are instead taxed under Part I on their allocated Canadian-source business income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (T106 filing threshold). Schedule 25 Master File: required where Canadian gross revenue ≥ CAD 100 million (foreign-headquartered MNE group) or global consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 1 billion (Canadian-headquartered group). Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in Canada; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s. 245; Quebec Taxation Act s. 1079.1), the sham doctrine, and Income Tax Act s. 103, which permits the CRA to reallocate partnership income where an allocation arrangement is artificial or tax-motivated and not reasonably attributable to a partner's capital, services, or risk. The LP must document each partner's economic contribution and the commercial purpose of the e-commerce structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2)); criminal fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (ITA s. 239). Quebec penalties under Taxation Act s. 1049 and Criminal Code s. 380 for fraud exceed CAD 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize, participate in, or acquiesce to the evasion are personally liable for fines and restitution; criminal proceedings trigger travel bans and asset freezes, and the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent acts.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million and 10% of gross revenue (ITA s. 247(3)); denial of the deduction and interest on reassessment; Quebec penalty of 10% of the adjustment (Taxation Act s. 1079.3).

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner's directors who sign false certificates, omit controlled transactions, or knowingly cause the partnership to understate income may face criminal tax evasion charges under ITA s. 239; civil recovery actions can pierce the veil where the LP is used as an instrument of fraud.
Artificial Partnership Allocations / GAAR

Statutory sanction: CRA reallocation of income to the correct partner under ITA s. 103(2); gross-negligence penalty up to 50% of the reallocated tax plus arrears interest; GAAR may nullify the entire tax benefit (ITA s. 245).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are liable for the reallocated tax where they are the designated taxable partner; deliberate misrepresentation in the allocation converts the issue into criminal tax evasion with imprisonment exposure and travel restrictions.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million (individuals) and CAD 10 million (entities); Criminal Code s. 462.31 provides forfeiture of proceeds of crime and imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly launder proceeds, or who omit required AML controls while exercising direction, are criminally liable; travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure orders apply, and the limited liability shield is void for illegal conduct.
Failure to File Partnership Information Returns

Statutory sanction: Federal penalties: CAD 25 per day (min CAD 100, max CAD 2,500), or CAD 100 per day (min CAD 500, max CAD 12,500) for gross negligence. Quebec: 5% late-filing penalty plus 1% per month on any Quebec tax payable, and additional fines for each missing RL-15 slip.

Director personal liability: The designated partner (general partner) and, if corporate, its directors are jointly and severally liable for penalties attributable to their direction or acquiescence in the filing default; director liability also arises for unremitted withheld amounts under ITA s. 227.1.
