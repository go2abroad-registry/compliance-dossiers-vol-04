Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority administering the GST/HST, the Income Tax Act, and the Business Number program; registration and compliance portal: Business Registration Online and CRA My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/business.html. Ontario Ministry of Finance participates in the HST which is collected federally by the CRA under the Ontario Harmonized Sales Tax agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery handles the underlying limited partnership registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership seeking a Canadian tax identity must: (1) register or extra-provincially register the limited partnership with the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery (Online Registration / CIPO system) and obtain the Ontario registration number; (2) apply for a distinct federal Business Number (BN) for the partnership itself — a partnership holds its own BN/GST-HST account — using CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or online through Business Registration Online; (3) register for the GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) by opening the RT account, which is mandatory once the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over any trailing four consecutive quarters, and may otherwise be made voluntarily to claim input tax credits; (4) appoint a general partner with a Canadian address for service or file Form RC59 (Authorizing or Cancelling a Representative) to appoint a tax representative, and provide CRA with the foreign address, original partnership agreement, foreign tax identification numbers, and a certificate of existence for the non-resident entity; (5) obtain the BN confirmation and the GST/HST registration effective date, then file periodic GST/HST returns (Form GST34) electronically via NETFILE or TELEFILE.

For GST/HST the partnership is separately treated as a registrant person under ETA s.272.1, while income tax remains pass-through. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under s.96 of the Income Tax Act; the partnership is not a taxable entity, and all income, gains, losses, and credits flow through and are allocated to the partners according to the partnership agreement. For GST/HST the partnership is separately treated as a registrant person under ETA s.272.1, while income tax remains pass-through. Territorial scope is federal Canada-wide taxation plus Ontario provincial income allocation where the LP carries on business in Ontario or pays an Ontario-resident partner.

Available election forms

Canada applies statutory fiscal transparency rather than US-style check-the-box; no entity-classification election exists for a Canadian domestic limited partnership. Relevant elections include: (a) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.24(1); (b) partnership rollover election under ITA s.97(2) for contributions of eligible property; (c) hybrid mismatch elections under ITA ss.18.2 and 18.21 for tiered transparent/hybrid arrangements involving foreign partnerships, LLCs, and trusts; (d) GST/HST joint election under ETA s.156 to treat certain supplies between a registrant and a qualifying non-resident as made for nil consideration; and (e) the GST/HST quick method and net-tax elections where the annual taxable-revenue thresholds are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once its worldwide revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. Zero-rated export revenues count toward the threshold while exempt-supply revenues do not. A non-resident AI stock-photo marketplace must apply the place-of-supply rules in ETA s.142.1 and s.143.2 to determine whether digital services, image licences, AI-generated visual assets, and subscription access are made in Canada and therefore subject to Ontario HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may claim the 0% export rate when: (i) it supplies goods that are exported from Canada by or on behalf of the supplier under Part V of Schedule VI, ETA s.1; and (ii) it supplies intangible personal property — stock-image licences, AI-model outputs, search and content APIs, hosted digital libraries, and subscription access rights — to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and does not acquire the supply for use in Canada, under Schedule VI s.23 and s.23.1. The place-of-supply rule in s.143.2 attributes digital/intangible supplies to the recipient's jurisdiction only where the customer is a non-resident, the exploitation of the intellectual property occurs outside Canada, and no Canadian use is contemplated. Royalties received from foreign licensees for AI-training datasets and algorithmic visual outputs qualify for zero-rating. Physical export of storage media containing image libraries qualifies under s.1 only with contemporaneous proof of direct export and non-resident destination.

  • CBSA Form B13A — Canada Customs Export Declaration or eManifest Canada export cargo report (for physical media and goods)
  • Commercial invoice and signed licence or sale contract evidencing transfer to a non-resident customer and exclusion of Canadian exploitation rights
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or courier consignment note showing the outward export route (for tangible deliveries)
  • Inward foreign-remittance proof: SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice, bank deposit slip, or payment-rail settlement report showing CAD-converted receipt from the non-resident buyer
  • CRA Form GST189 — General Application for Refund (for non-resident refund claims of GST/HST paid on Canadian inputs)
  • Customer residence evidence and offshore use proof: certificate of incorporation, foreign VAT/GST registration number, registered-office record, server logs, IP-address geolocation records, and digital delivery logs demonstrating consumption of licensed visual assets outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, pre-clearance API, or nationwide mandatory e-invoicing regime exists in Canada as of the current compliance cycle. CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing (NETFILE/XML or TELEFILE) for all registrants with annual taxable revenue exceeding CAD 1 million, and non-resident registrants must file electronically. CRA does not prescribe a mandatory invoice format but requires prescribed invoice content and supporting books and records under ETA s.286, including supplier name, Business Number, tax amount, and recipient details, with records kept in Canada or accessible electronically upon request; PEPPOL-compliant B2B/B2G e-invoices may be exchanged voluntarily but carry no tax-clearance effect.

CRA audit focus is on proof of export, non-resident customer status, and actual foreign use; failure to substantiate zero-rating, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day foreign-exchange deadline, triggers reassessment at 13% HST plus compounded daily prescribed-rate interest and can extend to a 4-year normal reassessment period or an unlimited 10-year period for misrepresentation. Civil penalties include: ETA s.280 failure to collect or remit tax at 10% of the unremitted amount; ETA s.285 false statements or wilful evasion at 15% of the net tax sought to be evaded; ETA s.280.1 gross-negligence penalty at 25% of the misstated amount; and criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 or ITA s.239 with fines of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax evasion. Retroactive clawbacks may also disallow input tax credits claimed on related Canadian inputs, and each partner of the LP is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST debts, penalties, and interest under ETA s.272.1(3).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without prior approval requirements for profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital repatriation to non-resident limited partners. The only practical hurdles are (a) FINTRAC mandatory reporting of cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (b) Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (25% statutory rate, treaty-reducible), (c) CRS/FATCA due-diligence and information reporting, and (d) bank-level sanctions and PEP screening; none of these constitute a convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory repatriation deadline applies to export proceeds in Canada. Receivables arising from international licensing and stock-photo royalty sales may be retained in the original invoice currency, and exporters are free to settle, convert, or hedge at commercial discretion; only the FINTRAC cross-border reporting threshold applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration; convertibility is governed by the market-based regime under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the 'electronic money institution' (EMI) licence class; non-resident digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) cannot serve as the primary deposit-taking treasury account for a Canadian Ontario LP unless they hold an OSFI-authorized foreign bank branch or are registered as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. They are viable only as ancillary settlement and payout connectors for marketplace disbursements, subject to counterparty credit limits and the LP maintaining its principal operating account with a Schedule I bank.

Turnover: An Ontario limited partnership is not required by statute to undergo an independent audit solely because of size or legal form. An audit becomes mandatory if the LP is a reporting issuer under securities legislation, if required by the partnership agreement, or if imposed by lender or investor contractual covenants. No size-based audit threshold is imposed on LPs by the Limited Partnerships Act.
