Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Ontario under the Canada-Ontario Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Finance administers the Employer Health Tax (EHT).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; a foreign-incorporated entity must provide certified constitutional documents, a certificate of corporate status, and an Ontario extra-provincial licence issued by ServiceOntario. Step 2: activate the RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll) accounts through the BRO as required. Step 3: file the Ontario Initial Return once the CBCA corporation is extra-provincially registered. Step 4: register for Ontario EHT with the Ontario Ministry of Finance when annual Ontario payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (exemption threshold for private-sector employers). Step 5: if non-resident directors are appointed, file the consent to act and jurisdiction acknowledgment under OBCA s. 118(4). Online issuance generally completes within 1–5 business days; foreign applicants may be routed to the CRA International Tax Services Office for identity verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act s. 2. Federal Part I general corporate rate is 15%; the small-business rate is 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. Ontario corporate income tax is 10% general and 3.2% small business, collected by the CRA as a single harmonized provincial return. Canada taxes resident corporations on worldwide income; no pass-through or S-corporation election exists.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; an entity incorporated under the CBCA or OBCA is a per se corporation. Available Canadian statutory elections include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate supplies as nil consideration for GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 179 election for drop-shipment transactions to preserve zero-rating for goods ultimately supplied to non-residents; (iii) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of property to the corporation; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident recipient of Canadian rents or royalties to file on a net taxable basis; (v) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election; and (vi) ITA s. 39(5) foreign-exchange capital-loss election. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3) contain ordering rules but do not provide an elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold, computed on worldwide taxable supplies of the corporation and its associated entities over the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters. A zero-rated exporter may voluntarily register for GST/HST to claim full input tax credits and recover HST embedded in Canadian costs. Under the 2021 specified-digital-services regime, non-resident digital suppliers are likewise required to register once their Canadian consumer revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the prior twelve months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VPN subscription service supplied to a non-resident individual or enterprise is zero-rated under ETA s. 21.5 (supply of a service to a non-resident) and/or ETA s. 25(1)(c) (export of intangible personal property) where the recipient is not registered for GST/HST, the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the digital service is delivered by electronic transmission to the foreign user. CRA administrative guidance confirms that cloud-based network security, encryption, and connectivity services supplied to non-residents qualify for the 0% rate. The supplier must maintain non-resident status evidence, including a CRA Form GST116 self-certification or equivalent foreign tax identification declaration, foreign billing address, foreign IP geolocation, and payment-origin data.

  • Zero-rated GST/HST invoice showing the supplier's BN/RT registration number, customer identity, service description, supply date, and the zero-rating basis under ETA s. 21.5 or s. 25
  • Non-resident status certificate (CRA Form GST116) or the customer's written self-certification with a foreign tax identification number
  • Executed service agreement and end-user licence terms evidencing electronic delivery to the non-resident recipient
  • Payment service provider settlement records (Stripe / Paymentwall) evidencing foreign currency denomination and foreign billing origin
  • Technical delivery logs (IP geolocation, server endpoints, authentication events) available for CRA audit substantiation
  • Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading are not required for pure digital exports; such CBSA documentation applies only if tangible software media or security hardware is physically exported

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API clearance, or digital-stamping mandate applies to GST/HST in Canada. Invoices must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including the supplier's BN/GST/HST registration number, the date, the recipient's identity, a description of the service, the consideration, and the applicable tax treatment. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol PINT/Factur-X standards is being rolled out voluntarily for B2B transactions; the federal government accepts Peppol-based e-invoices for B2G purchases but issuance remains elective.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline; transfer-pricing rules under ITA s. 247 nonetheless require that arm's-length consideration be received within a commercially reasonable period, normally by fiscal year-end, with contemporaneous documentation. If the zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA may reassess the supply at 13% HST, deny input tax credits, and charge prescribed arrears interest. Gross-negligence penalties apply under ETA s. 280(1) at 25% of net tax and under ITA s. 163(2) at 50% of the misstatement; fraudulent conduct is subject to criminal prosecution under ETA s. 281. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(3); ITA s. 152(4)), permitting retrospective clawback of refunds and credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under a floating exchange rate regime. There are no general restrictions on foreign exchange transactions or profit remittances by non-residents. Dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repatriation to non-residents are permitted, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% treaty-reduced) and compliance with FINTRAC reporting for transactions equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. The CAD/USD and CAD/EUR markets are deep and freely tradable; no surrender, approval, or invoicing restrictions apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts without surrender obligations. Reporting is limited to: (i) FINTRAC large electronic funds transfer reports for wire transfers over CAD 10,000; (ii) Statistics Canada trade and direct investment surveys; and (iii) Export and Import Permits Act requirements for controlled/dual-use goods and services, which are not typically applicable to VPN/encryption software unless listed under Canada's Export Control List.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canadian law permits the use of fintech payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) as agent/vendor payment channels, but any entity providing payment or FX services to Canadian residents must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Offshore EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration cannot lawfully maintain bank-like accounts for Canadian corporations; however, global EMIs such as Wise and Payoneer may be used for disbursement and collection under their Canadian MSB licenses, subject to enhanced onboarding restrictions for VPN/anonymity service providers. Legal constraints include enhanced due diligence on beneficial ownership, source of funds, and prohibition on accepting cryptocurrency or unverified third-party payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections by non-resident shareholders are effected via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to the Canadian operating account, using the correct beneficiary bank identifier (bank code, institution number, transit, and SWIFT/BIC). The bank will require a purpose/beneficiary notification (e.g., 'Equity Contribution', 'Shareholder Loan'), and the receipt must be reconciled against corporate records. Same-day tracking is available via SWIFT GPI. For investments exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank must file a FINTRAC large electronic funds transfer report. Statistics Canada may require completion of foreign direct investment survey forms. No prior exchange-control approval is required; CAD proceeds may be converted to USD/EUR in the spot market without restriction. To mitigate AML review, shareholders should provide a board resolution, subscription agreement, and source-of-funds evidence.Outbound trade disbursements (e.g., software licensing, cloud infrastructure rents, subcontractor fees) are cleared via SWIFT MT103/202 for cross-border payments and EFT/ACH for domestic payments. For large or recurring vendor payments, MT103 with ISO 20022 structured fields (including UETR) provides end-to-end traceability and fee transparency. Commercial letters of credit are not typical for SaaS/network services but may be used for hardware or infrastructure purchases; standby LCs may support contractual guarantees. All outbound wires equal to or above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting. Payments to sanctioned or restricted parties are prohibited; banks will screen counterparties using SDN/sanctions filters.
