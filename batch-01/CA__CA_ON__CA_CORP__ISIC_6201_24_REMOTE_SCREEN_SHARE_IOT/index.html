Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca; Ontario Ministry of Finance (Ontario corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Canada–Ontario Tax Collection Agreement)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the BRO portal or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The registrant must open a GST/HST program account by filing through BRO or Form RC1 before the due date of the first GST/HST remittance; non-resident suppliers of digital services may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration (Form RC51 and the non-resident digital services registration procedure). No separate Ontario corporate income tax registration is required because Ontario corporate income tax is collected federally by the CRA; a separate Ontario Employer Health Tax account with the Ontario Ministry of Finance may be required if the corporation's Ontario payroll exceeds the statutory exemption threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a provincially or federally incorporated business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I federal income tax and Ontario corporate income tax; no look-through or partnership pass-through treatment applies

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 216 election by a non-resident to file a Part I return on Canadian rental or timber income; (ii) s. 142.6 deemed disposition elections on foreign affiliate disposition of Canadian resource property; (iii) Part XIII bifurcation and branch-return elections under s. 219 for non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 negligible-value election between registrants; (v) annual GST/HST rebate filings claiming input tax credits attributable to exports; and (vi) hybrid-mismatch alignment elections and disclosures required under Canada's BEPS Action 2 rules for structured financial arrangements, applicable where the Canadian corporation interacts with offshore hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold. A non-resident supplier of specified digital services (including remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software) must register for GST/HST and charge tax when its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the trailing four calendar quarters; registration is voluntary below the threshold, and the simplified non-resident registration applies to digital services supplied through online platforms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and most services are zero-rated (0% HST) under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software: (i) supplies of intangible personal property (software/IP rights) to a non-resident recipient who is not a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under s. 7 of Schedule VI when the supply is made for use wholly outside Canada; (ii) supplies of services rendered to non-residents are zero-rated under s. 5.1 of Schedule VI where the service is not for consumption in Canada; (iii) physical export of embedded IoT devices requires documentary proof of export within a reasonable time. Zero-rating eligibility must be evidenced through contracts, invoices, IP geolocation logs for remote-screen sessions, customer non-residency declarations, and third-party platform (Stripe/Paddle) settlement records.

  • If physical IoT devices are shipped out of Canada: Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A/G13) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or via a licensed customs broker
  • Commercial invoice and bill of sale identifying the recipient as a person outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Stripe/Paddle settlement records and bank inward remittance certificate (account credit advice) evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Signed master subscription or license agreement stating the provision of the remote screen-share/IoT telemetry service and the foreign place of use
  • IP-geolocation and server-access logs demonstrating that software delivery and technical support occurred outside Canada (for intangible/service zero-rating claims)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or pre-validation of invoices exists in Canada/Ontario. The CRA operates a post-audit document-retention model. Businesses may issue invoices in PDF or structured electronic formats; there is no countrywide mandatory e-invoicing platform. Ontario-based registrants should retain machine-readable audit trails (transaction logs, IP geolocation, payment-gateway raw data from Stripe/Paddle) to substantiate place-of-supply determinations; participation in the voluntary Peppol BIS 3.0 network (Canada Digital Exchange / PAN-Canadian Peppol) is optional. Quebec's mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime does not apply to this Ontario entity unless it separately registers in Quebec.

The CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal 4-year limitation period, extended to 7 years where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. If export zero-rating is denied, the CRA will: (i) assess GST/HST at 13% Ontario HST on the full export value; (ii) disallow related input tax credits; (iii) charge daily compound interest arrears; (iv) impose a failure-to-remit penalty of 6% plus 1% per full month on unremitted amounts under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act; (v) impose a false-statement penalty of up to CAD 25,000 plus a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated net tax under ss. 285 and 285.1; and (vi) for intentional evasion, expose the corporation to summary conviction fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act, in addition to criminal fraud exposure under s. 380 of the Criminal Code (Canada).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized free-floating exchange-control regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; there is no exchange licensing, no approval queue, no conversion band, and no requirement to obtain central-bank permits for inbound equity, intercompany loans, export proceeds, or dividend remittances. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible into USD, EUR, GBP, and all major currencies through any Schedule I bank or licensed dealer. Profit remittance (dividends and branch remittances) is unrestricted at the exchange-control level but triggers Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, generally reduced to 5-15% under an applicable tax treaty such as the Canada-US Tax Convention) together with CRA NR4/T5 reporting. For this Ontario-based remote-screen-share and IoT telemetry software exporter, USD subscription proceeds may be freely retained in a USD corporate account, converted on the spot market, or swapped via forward contracts without pre-authorization. The only mandatory compliance hurdle is reporting: cash or electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more require FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reports or Electronic Funds Transfer Reports, and the entity must respond to Statistics Canada's Canadian Balance of International Payments survey if statistically selected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Board was abolished in 1951 and the CAD has floated freely since 1970. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriating export proceeds, no compulsory conversion ratio (effective required conversion ratio is 0%), and no restriction on retaining USD or other foreign-currency receipts in domestic operating accounts, USD clearing accounts, or offshore accounts of a Canadian bank. Export receipts invoiced in USD by this software entity may be swept into a USD-denominated corporate account, converted at management discretion, or hedged with derivatives without any Bank of Canada reporting or surrender obligation, provided FINTRAC reporting thresholds and corporate income-tax recognition rules are independently observed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange regulatory authority is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential oversight of banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border funds monitoring and AML/CFT enforcement under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act for residual exchange-control legal authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rated Medium as a supplementary collection and contractor-disbursement rail, not as a primary treasury settlement host. Canada has no EU-style Electronic Money Institution licensing category under the Bank Act; offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or as agents/partners of licensed retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada for payment service providers active in Canada as of the 2024-2025 regime. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI cannot hold a direct settlement account at the Bank of Canada and cannot access the Lynx RTGS system; (ii) local CAD direct-deposit rails are available through partner-bank arrangements (e.g., transit-institution-account numbers at Schedule I or provincial trust partners), permitting inbound collections and outbound contractor payouts, but ledger balances held on an EMI platform are not CDIC-insured; (iii) sustained use of an EMI for settlement of export invoices will elevate FINTRAC monitoring, especially where aggregated flows exceed CAD 10,000 per boundary event or diverge from the underlying Stripe/Paddle SaaS invoice ledger. Recommended treasury posture: retain Stripe and Paddle for merchant acquiring and use a FINTRAC-registered MSB/EMI only for non-material cross-border contractor disbursements; maintain all corporate receivables, tax-basis cash, capital escrow balances, and FX hedging commitments at a domestic Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription and shareholder-loan proceeds enter via SWIFT MT103 (with the ordering institution and remitter in fields 50K/52A matching the identified shareholder) and clear through the receiving Schedule I bank's Lynx RTGS feed for same-day CAD availability; USD inflows clear via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's US affiliate or correspondent before credit to a USD corporate account. For CBCA/ONBCA corporations, the inbound transfer must be supported by (i) an approved directors' resolution authorizing the share issuance or intercompany advance, (ii) a capital-contribution or loan agreement expressly stating 'general corporate funds / software-development working capital', and (iii) an updated share register and CRA notice of changes. Recommended SWIFT purpose text in field 70 is 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR COMMON SHARES' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN'; banks may map such text to reason codes 'CA-REM' for equity and 'CA-INT' for intercompany balances. Any single receipt or apparently connected series of receipts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 triggers a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report by the receiving bank. A Capital Escrow Account maintained at the primary Schedule I bank is permissible to quarantine non-resident subscription proceeds until share certificates are issued, the corporate income-tax folio is validated, and any conditions contained in the Ontario business documents are satisfied.Outbound settlements run over the correspondent banking mesh: SWIFT MT103 for supplier payables, intercompany charges, royalties, and dividend remittances; MT202/COVER for bank-to-bank nostro funding in USD and EUR. For hardware procurement related to IoT telemetry kit assembly, an irrevocable sight Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) or documentary collection (MT400) advised through the bank's trade finance desk is the compliant clearing rail and protects against supplier-performance risk. Canadian-dollar disbursements to domestic vendors clear via Lynx (RTGS) for same-day finality; USD disbursements clear via the bank's US clearing affiliate (BMO Harris, TD Bank US, RBC US Bank, CIBC's US correspondent) over Fedwire or CHIPS. FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to each outgoing transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Outbound dividend and royalty remittances are released only after Part XIII withholding tax is remitted to the CRA (form B1/R14 with the associated NR4/T5 filings), and the SWIFT remittance field must identify the economic nature as 'DIVIDEND', 'ROYALTY', 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN REPAYMENT', or 'SUPPLIER PAYMENT' to satisfy both the paying bank's compliance review and the receiving bank's reason-code mapping.Deliverable OTC CAD/USD and CAD/EUR FX forwards executed with the primary Schedule I bank's treasury desk under a standard ISDA 2002 or Canadian Master Agreement, credit-facility-backed and exempt from mandatory clearing for end-users.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing CAD/USD, available although rarely necessary given full CAD convertibility; useful only where the Canadian entity lacks a domestic credit facility and the hedge must be booked offshore.FX swaps (CAD/USD spot-forward combinations) for aligning USD subscription-receipt inflows against USD-denominated cloud-infrastructure and hardware payables without creating residual open exposure.Vanilla European-style currency options (CAD/USD collars, strangles, and purchased puts/calls) for protecting forecast export margin against adverse CAD appreciation while preserving upside participation.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) on intercompany loans denominated in USD or EUR where the Ontario entity borrows from a foreign affiliate and requires explicit currency and interest-rate risk matching under IFRS 9 hedge accounting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rated Medium as a supplementary collection and contractor-disbursement rail, not as a primary treasury settlement host. Canada has no EU-style Electronic Money Institution licensing category under the Bank Act; offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or as agents/partners of licensed retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada for payment service providers active in Canada as of the 2024-2025 regime. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI cannot hold a direct settlement account at the Bank of Canada and cannot access the Lynx RTGS system; (ii) local CAD direct-deposit rails are available through partner-bank arrangements (e.g., transit-institution-account numbers at Schedule I or provincial trust partners), permitting inbound collections and outbound contractor payouts, but ledger balances held on an EMI platform are not CDIC-insured; (iii) sustained use of an EMI for settlement of export invoices will elevate FINTRAC monitoring, especially where aggregated flows exceed CAD 10,000 per boundary event or diverge from the underlying Stripe/Paddle SaaS invoice ledger. Recommended treasury posture: retain Stripe and Paddle for merchant acquiring and use a FINTRAC-registered MSB/EMI only for non-material cross-border contractor disbursements; maintain all corporate receivables, tax-basis cash, capital escrow balances, and FX hedging commitments at a domestic Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Ontario); 12.2% combined reduced rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty reduction (e.g., 5% for a US or UK corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% voting stock; 15% for portfolio investments).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de minimis monetary exemption for Local File preparation: controlled cross-border transactions require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation in all material cases. Country-by-Country Report and Master File alignment become mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 900 million (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not adopt UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and transfer-pricing anti-recharacterization rules. A remote-support software corporation must demonstrate real employees, actual board oversight, ownership of relevant IP, and commercial risk assumption in Ontario; a letterbox subsidiary with no Canadian activity will be attacked as abusive.
