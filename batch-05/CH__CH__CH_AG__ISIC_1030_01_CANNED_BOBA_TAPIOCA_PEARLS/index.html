Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible for current-account trade and capital-account movements. No prior approval, licence, or golden-document is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder-loan repayment, or capital repatriation. Practical frictions: (i) 35% Swiss withholding tax applies to dividends and certain interest, recoverable or reduced under double-tax treaties; (ii) banks screen every cross-border wire under the CDB 20 due diligence convention; (iii) SNB statistical reporting obligations arise when monthly cross-border payments exceed CHF 10 million or when aggregate foreign assets or foreign liabilities exceed CHF 10 million at calendar year-end; and (iv) unilateral EU/US/UK sanctions screening is automatic. For a CHF 0.5–5 million boba-ingredients manufacturer, effective convertibility is high with no interbank availability restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion window, and no minimum repatriation ratio for export proceeds in USD, EUR, or third currencies. The exporter may retain 100% of foreign currency in USD/EUR-denominated accounts with Swiss banks, hold proceeds offshore, or fully hedge them; only SNB balance-of-payments statistical thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank/Banque nationale suisse) acting as the monetary and payments-system authority; FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) supervises banks and AML/CFT conduct; SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) administers economic sanctions; there is no separate foreign-exchange administration because Switzerland maintains a no-exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as primary treasury bank; Medium as a supplementary disbursement rail only. Switzerland has no standalone EMI licensing regime; fintechs operating under Art. 1b Banking Act (innovation-technology licence, public deposit cap of CHF 100 million) are mostly crypto/fintech-focused (e.g., Sygnum, SEBA) and unsuitable for low-risk tapioca trade flows. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud-type) are not Swiss-domiciled, have no SIX clearing membership, and create CDB 20 counterparty-risk exposure; they may be used for sub-10% monthly payment volumes with a documented treasury policy and full UBO/source-of-funds disclosure to the primary Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct corporate tax rate; combined federal/cantonal/communal headline rate for a Zug AG is approximately 11.9% (Zug) up to approximately 21.0% in higher-tax cantons.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) on dividends and distribution of Swiss AG profits, including constructive dividends arising from non-arm's-length transactions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within 180 days after the fiscal year-end, unless an extension is granted by the cantonal tax authority. Extension to 31 December of the following year is common if requested before the deadline and if the taxpayer submits preliminary financial data.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Identification and Corporate Data Legal entity name, commercial register number, Swiss UID (IDE/UID), registered seat address, board members, authorized representative, fiscal year start/end, legal form AG, status of non-resident ownership. Non-resident AG shareholders must ensure a Swiss tax representative is appointed, and the Swiss UID and commercial register details are current.
