Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca/business-registration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for the import/export RM program account via the CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) client portal.

E-government filing portal

The Ontario limited partnership must first be created or extra-provincially registered under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act by filing a Limited Partnership Declaration with the Ontario Business Registry, obtaining an Ontario Business Identification Number. For tax purposes the LP is not an income-tax taxpayer (fiscally transparent), but it is a separate 'person' for GST/HST under Excise Tax Act s. 272.1. Procedure: (1) file the Ontario declaration; (2) apply to CRA through BRO for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) including the RT (GST/HST) program account and, if applicable, the RM (import/export) program account, using Form RC1 or BRO online; (3) apply before making taxable supplies in Canada once the small-supplier threshold of CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters is exceeded, because registration is then mandatory; (4) where all general partners are non-residents, appoint a resident representative for service of documents under ETA s. 277(1); (5) activate the CBSA RM account and exporter identifier through CARM; (6) after each fiscal year-end, file the T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners within five months of the fiscal year-end per ITA ss. 96 and 229.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; classification is statutorily fixed. An Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96(2) for income tax while treated as a separate GST/HST registrant. Available elections include: (a) a treaty-based hybrid entity election under Article IV(6) of the Canada-United States Tax Convention (and analogous treaty provisions) to synchronize treatment of a fiscally transparent entity as a resident of one treaty state where the other state treats it as a corporation; (b) the GST/HST Quick Method election for small suppliers (ETA s. 238(1), Form GST74) where annual taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 400,000; (c) the GST/HST streamlined accounting election (Form GST70) for qualifying businesses with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 200,000; (d) an election to file GST/HST annually where net tax does not exceed CAD 3,000 per year; and (e) internal fiscal period elections for partnerships under ITA s. 249.1 where permitted. Note that ETA s. 156 closely related nil-consideration elections and GST/HST group registration are generally unavailable to a partnership, so the dealer should consider interposing a Canadian holding corporation to access group relief. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) require review where non-resident partners classify LP allocations inconsistently across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - mandatory GST/HST registration arises when the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in aggregate over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148); registration is also mandatory without regard to the threshold where the LP carries on business in Canada, solicits orders, or has a permanent establishment in Canada, and voluntary registration is available below the threshold but binds the registrant for at least one year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of surplus commercial aircraft rotables and avionics are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5 where the LP ships or transports the goods to a destination outside Canada, or exports them as baggage, or delivers them in Canada to a common carrier for continuous export to a foreign destination, provided the LP retains contemporaneous proof of export. Services performed on the parts for a non-resident recipient, or for a resident recipient where the parts are thereafter exported, may qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7. Supplies delivered to the purchaser in Canada, installed or consumed in Canada, or not evidenced by export documentation are taxable at 13% Ontario HST. The LP must be a GST/HST registrant to claim zero-rating and corresponding input tax credits on acquisition costs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or the electronic CARM export reporting message for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 where an export declaration is required)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier showing the ultimate destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or escrow-account statement evidencing the foreign-currency receipt and settlement from the non-resident buyer)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government-approved digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing. Once registered, the LP must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through a CRA-approved service provider, generally on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis depending on revenue and the elections made. Import and export accounting is handled electronically through the CBSA CARM portal (Canadian Single Window), and a prescribed B3-style declaration is required for importations. Invoices must contain the prescribed information under ETA s. 221(1), including the 9-digit BN and RT program account, date of supply, description, and total HST charged. Books and records must be retained for six years under ETA s. 286. Voluntary e-invoicing interoperable with PEPPOL is permitted but not required.

CRA may reassess within four years from the date of assessment, extended to seven years where the LP made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (ETA s. 298). Unsubstantiated zero-rated claims are recharacterized as taxable supplies at 13% HST, related input tax credits are denied, and interest at the prescribed rate is charged. Penalties include ETA s. 280 (gross negligence: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded), ETA s. 285 (false statements or omissions: 5% of the misreported amount plus 1% per month up to 12%, applied repeatedly), and criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) and ETA s. 327(1) with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Export substantiation documents must be preserved for six years under ETA s. 286.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without prior exchange-control approval. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a reserve currency that trades in deep, open markets, and non-resident investors may repatriate capital, dividends, and partnership distributions without statutory conversion hurdles. Profit remittances by a Canadian limited partnership are not subject to non-resident withholding at the partnership level due to the pass-through classification; however, non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive payments (e.g., dividends, interest, royalties) and may be taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income. Banking counterparties must still verify sources of funds for AML/ATF and sanctions compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists under Canadian law to surrender export receipts or repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within a specified timeline. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency offshore or convert to CAD at commercial rates. The only applicable obligations are regulatory reporting thresholds: currency flows exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC by financial entities and money services businesses, and the Canada Border Services Agency and Statistics Canada impose trade data reporting on export transactions. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to surplus commercial aircraft parts exports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority and lender of last resort); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (federal prudential banking regulator); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (anti-money laundering and terrorist financing supervisory body)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) and digital-only banks are not authorized to accept deposits or operate as banks in Canada absent an OSFI-regulated bank license. They may function as a foreign money services business (MSB) if registered with FINTRAC, but settlement ultimately requires clearing through a Canadian chartered bank. A licensed Canadian fintech PSP or a foreign EMI with a strong correspondent relationship to a Schedule I Canadian bank can provide cross-border collection and disbursement support, but it is not a substitute for a domestic operational/commercial bank account. For aviation parts trading with escrow requirements, a bank-anchored escrow arrangement is strongly preferred to avoid legal characterization as unauthorized deposit-taking.
