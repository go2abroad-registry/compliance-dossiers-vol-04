E-government filing portal

Foreign entity obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts (GST/HST: RT0001, Corporate Income Tax: RC0001, Import/Export: RM0001) through CRA Business Registration Online using the Non-Resident pathway. Required filings: certified copy of foreign incorporation certificate, Canadian registered office address or agent/attorney for service (Power of Attorney), officer/director identification for verification, and NAICS 423460 activity description. Available election forms

Available elections for a non-resident corporation under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and Excise Tax Act (ETA): (i) ITA s. 216 election to file net-basis tax on Canadian-source rental income; (ii) ITA s. 85 tax-deferred rollover for transfer of property to a Canadian corporation; (iii) ITA s. 1502 election deeming the non-resident corporation resident in Canada to access treaty relief; (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 227) for small registrants; (v) ETA s. 179 election for drop-shipments by non-resident non-registrants; (vi) Hybrid-mismatch rules are mandatory (non-elective) under ITA ss. 18.3, 18.4, and 18.5, disallowing deductions for excessive interest or expenses arising from hybrid arbitrage arrangements involving deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplied acetate eyeglass frames under Section 1, Part V, Schedule VI, ETA: goods exported by the supplier must (i) leave Canada within 60 days of delivery (CRA may accept longer periods on written justification); (ii) be supported by documentary proof of export; and (iii) be delivered to a consignee outside Canada. Offshore services (e.g., sourcing, logistics coordination for foreign wholesalers) zero-rate under Section 7, Part V, Schedule VI ETA where the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed primarily (over 50%) in Canada. Local warehousing and inspection services performed in Ontario attract 13% HST unless separately structured. A CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B13A) is mandatory for commercial exports over CAD 2,000 or where export controls apply; low-value shipments under CAD 2,000 rely on carrier waybills plus commercial invoice substantiation. Wholesale resale of imported frames also qualifies for zero-rating when title transfers overseas or goods are shipped directly from Canada, subject to drop-shipment rules under ETA s. 179.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API approval for invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary; accepted formats include EDI, PDF, XML, and PEPPOL BIS 3.0 under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework. Mandatory e-filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with taxable supplies above CAD 1,500,000 using GST/HST NETFILE; e-payment is required through CRA My Payment for electronic fund transfers. Ontario does not issue a separate provincial return or fiscal clearance; HST administration is fully integrated with CRA under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA).

CRA treats export zero-rating claims by non-resident sellers as high-risk when the exporter lacks a substantive Canadian establishment, goods are re-entered or destroyed, receivables remain outstanding beyond the 180-day benchmark without documented commercial justification, or the transaction involves a non-arm's length offshore intermediary. Penalties: (i) gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1(2) equal to the greater of CAD 500 and 15% of the incorrectly refunded/claimed amounts; (ii) false statement offence under ETA s. 327(1) with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on summary conviction or 8 years on indictment; (iii) compounding prescribed daily interest under ETA s. 280 and ITA s. 161(1) applied on reassessment; (iv) extension of the normal 4-year reassessment limitation period indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established; (v) Ontario's harmonized HST exposes the full 13% provincial component to the same clawback. All export documentation must be retained for 6 years under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230; failure to produce evidence on audit triggers deeming of a taxable supply at 13% HST with no offsetting ITC.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions. No prior authorization is required for inward or outward remittances, foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, or dividend distribution by non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is subject only to standard withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Part XIII on eligible dividends, interest, royalties) and may be reduced by an applicable tax treaty. There are no exchange control reviews, central bank filing requirements, or waiting periods for commercial payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or surrender requirements for export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may hold proceeds in foreign currency accounts at domestic Schedule I banks or Canadian branches of foreign banks without restriction. Reporting obligations may arise solely through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export documentation (Form B13A) for customs statistics and through standard banking record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administrative agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Canada has no formal EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensing regime comparable to the EU; non-bank payment service providers are regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which mandates registration with the Bank of Canada and compliance with operational, safeguarding, and incident-reporting obligations. Offshore digital EMIs may provide cash management or payment-prioritization services, but they cannot open domestic Canadian bank accounts or clear CAD-denominated payments without a Canadian registration or an agent-bank arrangement with a licensed deposit-taking institution. Corporate treasury should treat offshore EMIs as supplementary billing/commercial-card rails, not as primary clearing conduits for statutory capital injections or trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Injected capital should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's corporate bank account to the Ontario corporation's segregated CAD-denominated Operational or Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I bank. The payment must clearly reference the purpose code 'Capital contribution for share subscription' (SWIFT purpose code 6100 or bank-specific remittance code), accompanied by a board resolution and an internal corporate remittance instruction naming the subscriber, share class, and amount. Banks will verify the source of funds and may request an audited financial statement of the remitter to substantiate the injection. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Canadian federal and Ontario provincial general corporate income tax rate: 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%). For Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) claiming the Small Business Deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income: combined federal 9% + Ontario 3.2% = 12.2%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A 25% branch tax also applies under ITA s. 219 to profits remitted by an Ontario permanent establishment of a non-resident corporation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than 6 months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period (ITA s. 150). The balance-due day for the tax payment is 2 months after fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months after fiscal year-end for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction. Ontario applies the same integrated deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ITA s. 247(3) penalty threshold: the 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies only where the mispricing adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue for the year. Reasonable contemporaneous documentation (CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R, TPM-06R) is mandatory once any cross-border related-party transaction exists. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies where the multinational group has consolidated Canadian revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Ontario and Canada do not maintain UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, economic substance is a legally operative requirement through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the business-purpose and sham doctrines, and the CRA audit position that form without commercial reality may be recharacterized. A wholesale distributor of acetate ophthalmic frames is expected to maintain a physical office or warehouse in Ontario, real board governance, a Canadian bank account, executed distribution contracts, and payroll substance. Artificial interposition of a shell intermediary, conduit financing, or treaty-shopping structure will be attacked under GAAR, limitation-on-benefits provisions in Canadian tax treaties, anti-hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.6), and continuous Ontario extra-provincial licensing requirements that mandate an attorney for service and registered address.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1) - summary conviction: fine of CAD 2,000 to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; indictment: fine of CAD 10,000 to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated under ITA s. 163(2); CRA reinstated reassessments with compounded daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding without proof of intent. Where criminal evasion is proven, individual directors may be convicted, and travel bans or departure-clearance restrictions may be imposed during CRA criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) re-adjustments of price and allocations; 10% penalty on the gross adjustment exceeding the greater of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; recharacterization of interest, royalties, and dividends under ITA s. 247(1); statutory interest and a 3-year reassessment extension in misrepresentation cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the T2 while knowingly participating in a transfer-pricing scheme can be charged for false statements under ITA s. 239; civil restitution from personal assets; fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may obtain court orders freezing personal and corporate bank accounts and seizing proceeds transferred abroad.
Shell Company and Artificial Structure Without Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (ITA s. 245) denies the tax benefit and imposes penalties of up to 25% of the benefit under ITA s. 245(4); the CRA may recharacterize the entire transaction and assess gross negligence penalties; Ontario may revoke extra-provincial registration; the shell entity may be subject to money-laundering offences involving criminal organization sanctions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil of a shell is pierced where the arrangement is fraudulent or a sham; Ontario courts may award equitable compensation and disgorgement against directors. Director penalties under s. 227.1 can reach 25% of unremitted amounts; travel bans and passport surrender may be imposed as bail conditions; imprisonment of up to 5 years is possible.
Dividend, Interest, and Royalty Repatriation Evasion

Statutory sanction: CRA assessment of the unpaid Part XIII tax; penalty of 10% on withheld amounts not remitted under ITA s. 227(2); compounded daily interest; late-filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month; treaty-limitation-on-benefits may be applied to deny relief where the recipient lacks substance or beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Joint and several personal liability under ITA s. 227.1 for up to 25% of the unremitted withholding amount, plus interest and penalties; directors may be restrained from leaving Canada through CRA judicial application; civil seizure of personal real estate and investment portfolios; criminal sanctions for knowing concealment.
AML, FINTRAC, and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code fines without statutory limit; Special Economic Measures Act and Freezing Assets of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act: fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for wilful sanctions violations.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors are personally liable where they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the breach; personal fines and imprisonment up to 10 years; asset freezes and travel bans by order of the Minister of Foreign Affairs; US secondary exposure may attach where USD clearing is used for prohibited counterparties, placing directors at risk of OFAC enforcement.
