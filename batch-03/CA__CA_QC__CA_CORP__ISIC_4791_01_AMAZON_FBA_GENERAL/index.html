Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer taxed under ITA Part I on worldwide income, with a federal general corporate rate of 15% (9% small-business rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000 if the corporation is a Canadian-controlled private corporation) and a Quebec general corporate rate of 11.5% (3.2% reduced rate for income eligible for the small-business deduction); combined headline rate approximates 26.5%. There is no look-through or transparent classification for federal or Quebec income-tax purposes. If the corporation were instead a non-resident corporation, it would be taxed only on Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(3)) and be exposed to an additional 25% Part XIV branch tax on its after-tax Canadian business income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Available statutory elections affecting entity or regime classification: (i) Non-Resident-Owned Investment Corporation (NRO) election under ITA s. 133, which substitutes Part I tax with a 25% Part XIV tax for passive investment holding (review when FBA treasury or IP-licensing functions are separated); (ii) functional-currency reporting election under ITA s. 261 (eligible corporations may report Canadian tax results in U.S. dollars); (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 218) and the closely related corporation election (ETA s. 156) permitting combined net-tax accounting; (iv) joint venture election (ETA s. 273 and Quebec parallel) designating a single JV operator as the sole GST/QST registrant; (v) small-business deduction business-limit allocation agreement among associated CCPCs (ITA s. 125(3)); and (vi) various rebate and simplified-accounting elections for non-resident registrants. Hybrid mismatch election mechanisms are not available; Canada applies OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5) and the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when worldwide taxable supplies (including supplies of associated persons) exceed CAD 30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in any single calendar quarter (ETA ss. 148(1), 240); the identical CAD 30,000 QST threshold applies under the QSTA. Non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada are also required to register. For Amazon FBA general retail, the threshold is quickly exceeded once Canadian consumer sales commence, so immediate registration and collection of GST plus QST is expected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) goods — a registrant's supply of tangible personal property delivered in Canada to a carrier for export, or exported as soon as is reasonably possible after the consideration becomes due, provided the registrant keeps conclusive proof of export (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1-2; parallel QST provisions); (b) services — a supply made to a non-resident person where the service is not in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and the service is not performed for an individual present in Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7); and (c) digital retail services delivered remotely to non-residents. For an Amazon FBA operation, inventory physically located in Quebec and sold to Canadian end consumers is taxable at the combined 14.975% rate; only sales delivered to foreign destinations, supported by international transport documentation, or services rendered to offshore buyers qualify for zero-rating. Zero-rating is denied if the goods return to Canada, are consumed in Canada, or if the exporter cannot prove the goods left Canada within a reasonable period.

  • Canada Export Declaration (B13A) filed electronically through the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000; courier low-value shipments may be evidenced by carrier manifest data
  • Signed commercial invoice or Amazon payment settlement report identifying the Canadian seller, the CA GST/HST and QST registration numbers, the Quebec ship-from fulfillment center, and the foreign consignee address
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note showing the Canadian shipper, foreign consignee, and terminal-exit scan; CBSA eManifest acknowledgment where applicable
  • Outbound customs broker certificate or CBSA export acceptance report confirming actual export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/MT103 credit advice, or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency settlement of the exported sale
  • Amazon FBA inventory movement report and carrier pickup scan records demonstrating physical removal of the goods from the Quebec fulfillment center

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice clearance exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices must disclose the supplier's name and BN or QST registration number, the date, a description of the supply, and the tax amount shown separately (ETA s. 223(1); parallel Quebec rule under the Tax Administration Act); simplified invoices are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Initiative (Peppol/BIS 3.0, operated under the Council of the Federation with CAN/XIS network access) is voluntary for B2B transactions and not yet mandated. Revenu Québec accepts standard EDI/XML and CLICK portal submissions without government pre-clearance of each invoice. Regardless of format, all underlying transaction, logistics, and payment records must be retained for CRA and Revenu Québec audit windows.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298; Quebec Tax Administration Act s. 1018), extended to ten years where misrepresentation involves neglect or carelessness, and with no time limit for fraud. If conclusive proof of export is not substantiated, the full combined 14.975% GST plus QST is assessed on the invoice amount, input tax credits and any refunds are clawed back, and statutory interest at prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec quarterly rates accrues from the original filing date. Civil penalties include a 6% late-remittance penalty plus 1% per full month on the unremitted amount, false-statement penalties of 5% of the amount with an additional 5% for repeated defaults within five years, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax (ETA ss. 280(1), 281.1(1)-(2); ITA s. 163(2); Quebec TAA s. 1059). Criminal prosecution for export-invoice fraud or evasion can result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239; Quebec TAA s. 1056). The 180-day repatriation benchmark is not a codified statutory deadline in Canada but is treated as best practice for documentary substantiation and currency-control risk; all export proceeds should be settled and recorded in the corporate accounts at the applicable CRA exchange rate within that window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current-account and capital-account items are fully convertible for the Canadian dollar; no prior approval, licensing, quota or repatriation restriction applies to profit remittance, dividend distribution or capital return by CA_CORP (Quebec business corporation). FBA marketplace proceeds received in USD/EUR/CNH may be converted at market rates and remitted freely, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% dividends to a US parent), deemed-dividend anti-avoidance rules, Investment Canada Act notification thresholds, and beneficial-ownership disclosure to the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canada abolished exchange-control surrender and repatriation requirements in 1951; an ISIC 4791 marketplace exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency and hold USD/EUR/CNH-operating accounts without mandatory conversion ratios or statutory timelines. Only transparency obligations apply: financial entities file FINTRAC EFT reports for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and travellers crossing the border with cash or bearer instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must declare to CBSA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Canada's central bank. Canada operates a clean floating exchange-rate regime under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Accounts framework; there is no standalone foreign-exchange-control administration agency. Prudential banking supervision is exercised by OSFI, AML/CFT and money-services-business registration by FINTRAC, cross-border currency declaration by CBSA, and payment clearing-lattice oversight by Payments Canada (Lynx and ACSS).
