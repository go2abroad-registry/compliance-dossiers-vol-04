Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: 'My Business Account' (MyBA) and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; provincial registry counterpart: Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery (ServiceOntario), Companies and Personal Property Security Branch, for filing Form 3 under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Register the Limited Partnership in Ontario under the Limited Partnerships Act via ServiceOntario Form 3 (Declaration), including name search and filing fee; obtain Ontario LP registration number. STEP 2: Apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) for the LP using Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC348, by telephone, or at a Service Canada location; the LP's 9-digit BN is its statutory tax identifier. STEP 3: Activate CRA program accounts: RT (GST/HST) for taxable supplies of used construction excavators; RP (payroll) if the LP employs Ontario staff; the LP does not open an RC corporate income tax account because it is fiscally transparent, but it registers for the T5013 Partnership Information Return program. STEP 4: Non-resident partners must each obtain a Canadian tax identifier (BN or social insurance-based reference) as needed for Part I installment obligations on their allocable Canadian-source business income; the LP performs any required Part XIII withholding on specified payments. STEP 5: GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the applicable small-supplier threshold or when the LP carries on commercial activity in Canada and issues taxable invoices; the RT account effective date governs collection obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (income tax transparency under ITA s.96; the LP is not a taxable entity and is not subject to entity-level federal or Ontario corporate income tax; partners include their allocable share of Canadian-source income and are taxed directly). For GST/HST purposes the LP is a separate 'person' under ETA s.123 and must register, collect, and remit HST and claim input tax credits (ITCs); Ontario HST is a single 13% composite tax administered federally. Non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their Canadian-source allocable share unless a treaty reduces nexus, and CRA applies federal/territorial allocation rules based on permanent establishment presence.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. Available statutory elections relevant to a used-equipment trading LP: (1) ITA s.97(2) tax-deferred rollover of capital property contributed to the partnership; (2) ITA s.96 partnership allocation elections where the partnership agreement is amended and continuous-dealer rules are respected; (3) ETA s.167(2) election to treat a sale of the business or a part thereof as a going concern at nil GST/HST upon disposal of the used-machinery inventory; (4) IHT/ETA non-resident disclosure elections and s.115/116 clearance certificate options for dispositions of taxable Canadian property; (5) hybrid-mismatch compliance elections and filings under ITA ss.18.2, 18.3 and s.212.3 LIFE (prescribed Form RC5600 series) where an offshore partner introduces debt into a Canadian LP; (6) voluntary small-supplier GST/HST registration election where the LP opts to register before reaching the threshold, subject to restricted pre-registration ITC recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 50,000 (effective 2025 small-supplier threshold, replacing the legacy CAD 30,000; measured by worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated and exempt supplies, of the LP and associated registrants in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of used construction excavators physically exported from Canada as part of the supply, under ETA s.1 Part V(VI) (property exported by the supplier) and for pre-export services such as repair and refurbishment under ETA s.5 Part V(VI). Conditions: (a) the LP must evidence that title and risk passed to a non-resident buyer; (b) the excavators must be exported and delivered outside Canada within a reasonable period, generally 60 days from the invoice/supply date per CRA Policy P-051R2; (c) contemporaneous documentary proof of export must be retained; (d) a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant, or the LP itself, must ship the goods; (e) correct HS classification for used 360-degree revolving excavators is HS 8429.52 (HS 8429.51 if not 360-degree). Canada has no margin scheme for used goods, so the 13% HST is collectible unless export is fully documented.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A, CAED/G13 EDI transmission) for goods valued over CAD 2,000 where no US-bound or low-value exemption applies; retained even when an exemption is claimed
  • Commercial Invoice signed by the LP stating HS 8429.52, machine serial number/VIN, description, preferred trade terms (Incoterms), and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or Through-Waybill (ocean, air, or highway) naming the LP as shipper/consignor and a consignee with a non-Canadian address
  • Carrier or Freight Forwarder Certificate of Export certifying receipt of the machinery and departure from Canadian territory with dates and routing
  • CBSA/CBP cross-border exit evidence, including ACE/AES import reference documents for US-bound excavators or foreign customs entry documents
  • SWIFT wire transfer or inward remittance confirmation from the remitting Canadian bank evidencing settlement by the non-resident buyer, including letter-of-credit drawdown records when the Swift_LC rail is used
  • Bank-issued inward remittance certificate or equivalent confirmation matching the invoice amount and export shipment record
  • Freight invoice and marine/cargo insurance certificate with declared foreign end-destination

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no API-based pre-clearance mandate is in force in Canada as of 2025; electronic invoices are accepted if they are legible, accurate, and contain the prescribed information (vendor BN, date, description, total consideration, and HST amount). Voluntary participation is available under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol-based). Mandatory electronic filing: GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue over CAD 1.5 million must file returns and remit via GST/HST NETFILE (CAD 100 penalty per unfiled return); monthly/quarterly remittance frequency and accelerated remittance thresholds apply based on taxable supply volumes.

Canada imposes no statutory fixed 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard commercial and letter-of-credit practice for used heavy-machinery export sales, while zero-rating itself is substantiated by physical export within 60 days of supply, not by payment timing. CRA may reassess, claw back zero-rating, deny ITCs, and require full 13% HST plus interest if the export evidence bundle is incomplete; the normal reassessment period is four years, unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties include ETA s.285(1) gross-negligence/false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided; ETA s.327 evasion penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years; and ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any understated tax. CRA and CBSA conduct joint audits of high-risk equipment exporters, and repeated false export claims are prosecuted as tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction, and the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, no capital-import/export licensing, and no restrictions on equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend/profit remittances, or trade settlement flows for resident or non-resident persons. Non-resident limited partners of the Ontario LP face zero FX-convertibility hurdles in contributing capital or receiving partnership distributions. The practical friction points are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at a statutory 15% on income allocations and certain distributions, treaty-reduced for most jurisdictions (e.g., Canada-US treaty rates of 0-15% depending on income type and treaty entitlement); (ii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) reporting by banks and MSBs for every wire at or above CAD $10,000; and (iii) CRS/FATCA classification and beneficial-ownership documentation on all foreign partners. Used-machinery sales proceeds may be invoiced and settled in CAD, USD, EUR, RMB, or any freely chosen currency without central bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds derived from used construction excavators or rollers. Exporters may retain proceeds indefinitely in foreign currency, convert at market rates, hedge via derivatives, or repatriate without Bank of Canada or FINTRAC approval. The relevant compliance obligations are: (i) CBSA export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (G7 e-Export) for any good exported above CAD $2,000; (ii) commercial invoice and bill of sale preserving VIN, chassis, or serial numbers; (iii) six-year record retention under the Income Tax Act; (iv) FINTRAC threshold reporting for each EFT at or above CAD $10,000 executed by the LP's bank or MSB; (v) GST/HST zero-rating on qualified exports with documentary proof under Excise Tax Act s. 21/s. 117; and (vi) no obligation to sell export earnings to a domestic bank or government-designated dealer.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada administers no exchange-control statute; the Bank of Canada's FX market activity is limited to monetary-policy intervention and does not constitute a licensing or approval regime for private capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

RATING: MEDIUM (secondary rail only). Foreign digital EMIs that are not licensed as Canadian deposit-taking institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Mercury, Payoneer) cannot legally maintain CAD deposit accounts for a Canadian Ontario LP and are not viable as the primary treasury spine. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) operating in Canada (Wise Business, Airwallex, OFX, and provincial-licensed MSBs) are viable for auxiliary multi-currency wallets, competitive FX conversion, and low-value cross-border vendor payouts, subject to: (i) FINTRAC MSB registration under the PCMLTFA; (ii) CAD $10,000 EFT reporting per transaction; (iii) absence of commercial LC, escrow, and documentary trade capabilities; and (iv) aggregate limits designed to keep principal liquidity within a Tier-1 Schedule I bank. An optimal treasury blueprint routes all capital calls, LC-backed trade settlements, and large FX forwards through the bank, while MSB rails handle sub-CAD 100,000 supplier disbursements in USD/EUR.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital inflows are executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) from the remitting limited partner's bank to the Ontario LP's Schedule I beneficiary account, cleared through the bank's USD/CAD correspondent (e.g., J.P. Morgan New York or the Canadian bank's New York agency). Field 59 must state the exact registered Ontario LP legal name; Field 50K must show the contributing partner; Field 72/77B remittance narrative must recite: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA LP - [REGISTERED LEGAL NAME] - CAPITAL CALL NO. [X]'; ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity injection) or bank-proprietary 'C' code is required. Verification duties: the receiving bank files a FINTRAC EFT report for any inflow equal to or above CAD $10,000, performs a pre-credit sanctions/UBO re-screen on the contributor, validates CRS/FATCA classification, and credits only against the LP's executed capital-call register. Absent a valid purpose code, a notarized capital-call confirmation, and the UBO chart, the bank may suspend the credit as high-risk pending enhanced due diligence.Outbound trade settlements use: (i) SWIFT MT103 corporate wire for open-account supplier payments (dominant rail); (ii) SWIFT MT700 irrevocable commercial letters of credit (sight or usance with 60-120 day tenors) where the used-machinery seller requires documentary conditional payment; (iii) documentary collections via MT400/MT410 where the vendor accepts presentation of a bill of lading; and (iv) Payments Canada Lynx (high-value CAD payment system) or ACSS/E-next for domestic CAD legs below/above threshold (Lynx is mandatory for CAD $500,000 and higher). Compliance steps: match bank beneficiary to the commercial invoice and LC; quantify invoice with serial/VIN numbers against Incoterms 2020 (EXW/FCA/DAP); file FINTRAC EFT reports for each payment at or above CAD $10,000; conduct SEMA/SDIPA/OFAC pre-screen of counterparty before release; and archive the purchase agreement, bill of sale, shipping document, customs declaration, and SWIFT confirmation as the AML/trade audit package.Vanilla OTC FX Forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD; tenors up to 24 months): permitted for hedging trade receivables and payables without per-deal regulatory approval, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank master FX line.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): permitted exclusively for restricted/non-deliverable currency pairs (e.g., CAD/CNH or CAD against offshore-controlled currencies) where physical CAD delivery to the counterparty jurisdiction is legally unavailable; CAD itself remains fully deliverable in the interbank forward market.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (USD/CAD fixed-for-floating): permitted for term-funding or capital-commitment FX/interest-rate exposure of the LP, subject to OSFI margin rules, ISDA/CSA collateral documentation, and credit-support annexes.OTC FX Options and Zero-Cost Collars (USD/CAD): permitted for hedging firm trade commitments and auction-tender exposure, subject to the bank's derivative eligibility assessment and the partnership's documented risk-management policy under IFRS 9 / IAS 21 hedge accounting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: RATING: MEDIUM (secondary rail only). Foreign digital EMIs that are not licensed as Canadian deposit-taking institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Mercury, Payoneer) cannot legally maintain CAD deposit accounts for a Canadian Ontario LP and are not viable as the primary treasury spine. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) operating in Canada (Wise Business, Airwallex, OFX, and provincial-licensed MSBs) are viable for auxiliary multi-currency wallets, competitive FX conversion, and low-value cross-border vendor payouts, subject to: (i) FINTRAC MSB registration under the PCMLTFA; (ii) CAD $10,000 EFT reporting per transaction; (iii) absence of commercial LC, escrow, and documentary trade capabilities; and (iv) aggregate limits designed to keep principal liquidity within a Tier-1 Schedule I bank. An optimal treasury blueprint routes all capital calls, LC-backed trade settlements, and large FX forwards through the bank, while MSB rails handle sub-CAD 100,000 supplier disbursements in USD/EUR.
