Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; shares may be issued for any lawful consideration. If par value shares are adopted, the consideration received must be at least equal to the aggregate par value, which is fully credited to stated capital. No escrow account or capital deposit is required for a business corporation incorporated in Quebec.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: if the registered office is leased, obtain a fire safety attestation from the competent municipal fire service under the Building Safety Act and applicable municipal by-laws. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST, provincial corporate income tax and related registration obligations. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec CLIC (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

Canada does not issue a standalone VAT TIN. The statutory corporate tax identifier is the CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit root with separate program accounts, such as RT for GST/HST and RP for payroll. A foreign-incorporated entity may apply for a BN by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or by using CRA's online business registration service. After incorporation under the Quebec Business Corporations Act, or after extra-provincial registration in Quebec of a federal corporation, the entity obtains a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises and registers with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial corporate tax through the CLIC portal. A non-resident corporation engaged solely in passive equity holding and receipt of dividends or capital gains is generally not required to register for GST/HST or QST, but if it is resident in Canada by incorporation or central management and control it must file Canadian and Quebec corporate income tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or elective transparency regime exists in Canada. A Quebec business corporation is per se a taxable entity and cannot elect partnership status or disregarded-entity status. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act, including section 18.4 and related provisions, are mandatory anti-avoidance rules that deny deduction/non-recognition mismatches; they are not elective mechanisms. Ancillary elections such as capital-dividend account or foreign-affiliate surplus elections do not alter entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, being the GST/HST small-supplier threshold; Quebec QST uses a matching CAD 30,000 threshold. Dividends, interest, and proceeds from disposition of shares are exempt financial services under Schedule V, Part VII of the Excise Tax Act and do not count as taxable supplies, so a pure equity holding SPV normally has no mandatory GST/HST or QST registration unless it separately makes taxable commercial supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec QST rules that mirror it, tangible personal property exported from Canada is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods under Schedule VI, Part V and retains documentary proof of export, including customs declarations, transport documents, and evidence that possession passed outside Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may also qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V if the statutory conditions are met. However, a pure equity holding SPV's dividend, interest, and capital-gain income constitutes exempt financial services under Schedule V, Part VII, not zero-rated export supplies, and the SPV cannot claim input tax credits for expenses attributable to exempt activities unless it also carries on separately taxable commercial activity.

  • Canada Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration/CAED system, including the exporter's proof number, for reportable commercial shipments)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Manifest evidencing physical export from Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank payment advice evidencing receipt of the foreign export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and supply contract stating Incoterms, destination, and transfer of title to the non-resident buyer
  • For exported or offshore services: dated service agreement, statement of work or completion certificate, and written confirmation that the service recipient is not resident in Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, no continuous transaction control clearance platform, no API tax clearance requirement, and no digital stamping mandate. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices in verifiable formats such as XML, PDF, or EDI. The federal government is voluntarily promoting the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard based on Peppol, but adoption is not mandatory for a passive equity holding SPV.

CRA and Revenu Québec treat export zero-rating claims supported by the documented evidence bundle as a high-risk audit area. If the documentation is deficient or false, the supply will be re-characterized as taxable in Canada, GST/QST will be assessed on the full consideration, the corresponding input tax credit or rebate will be clawed back, and compound daily interest will accrue from the due date of the return. Civil penalties include a false-statement penalty under the Excise Tax Act of 25% of the understated net tax or overstated refund, a gross-negligence penalty under Income Tax Act section 163(2) of 50% of the understated income tax for knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence making a false statement, and criminal fines and imprisonment under Income Tax Act section 239 and Excise Tax Act section 285 for wilful evasion. The normal reassessment limitation period is generally four years for GST/HST, and no limitation period applies where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud, wilful default, neglect, or carelessness, permitting retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada complies with IMF Article VIII and maintains no capital-control approval regime: inbound equity subscriptions, dividends, intercompany loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds are freely remittable in CAD or foreign currency without central-bank consent. The only profit-remittance friction is fiscal: dividends paid by a Quebec-resident corporation to a non-resident attract federal Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; treaty-reduced to 15% portfolio / 5% for a beneficial owner corporation holding at least 10% of voting shares under most Canadian treaties) plus Quebec's parallel 12% withholding under Section 1042 of the Quebec Taxation Act, with Revenu Québec generally administering treaty relief consistent with the applicable convention. Administrative compliance requires NR4 information returns and CRA remittance of Part XIII withholding by the 15th day of the following month. The Investment Canada Act net-benefit review applies to acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above monetary thresholds, but does not restrict the SPV's treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. A Quebec-resident entity may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts indefinitely in onshore or offshore accounts, subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (Electronic Funds Transfer Report and Large Cash Transaction Report at CAD 10,000) and standard Canadian tax reporting (T1134/T1135 for foreign affiliates and foreign property).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority issuing CAD and manager of the Official International Reserves as fiscal agent of the Minister of Finance under the Bank of Canada Act and Currency Act. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency. OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises Schedule I banks prudentially, while FINTRAC enforces the AML/CFT and cross-border transaction reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as primary clearing bank; Conditional as auxiliary payment rail. Canada has no 'Electronic Money Institution' licensing class; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Mercury) holding only foreign EMI or money-transmitter authorizations cannot operate as Schedule I deposit-taking banks, cannot clear CAD through Lynx, and cannot hold escrowed subscription proceeds for a Quebec holding company. Legal constraints: (i) any provider servicing Canadian residents must be registered with FINTRAC as a money services business under PCMLTFA and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act, 2021 regime came into force (September/November 2024), registered as a payment service provider; (ii) an unregistered foreign EMI is non-compliant for soliciting Canadian business; (iii) a foreign EMI account cannot satisfy a 'capital escrow' undertaking demanded by the parent or downstream lenders. Use such rails only for discretionary expense disbursements; never for structured equity or debt-covenant legs.

Federal CBCA annual return (if the SPV is a Canada Business Corporations Act company) Within 60 days after the anniversary date of the filing of articles of incorporation $29 USD The Director may issue a notice of default and, if the default is not cured, administratively dissolve the corporation; no separate late-filing fee is imposed.
Federal T2 corporation income tax return Within 6 months after the end of each taxation year (e.g., June 30 following a December 31 year-end) $0 USD CRA late-filing penalties apply as a percentage of any unpaid balance plus monthly interest accrual; a zero-income equity-holding SPV still must file to avoid administrative assessments and loss of attribute carry-forwards.
Quebec corporation income tax return (CO-17) Within 6 months after the end of the taxation year, generally matching the federal T2 deadline $0 USD Revenu Québec imposes interest and parallel late-filing penalties; failure to file may lead to administrative assessments.
