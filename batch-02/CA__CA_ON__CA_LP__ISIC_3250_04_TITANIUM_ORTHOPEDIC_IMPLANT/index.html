Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA), through Business Registration Online ('BRO') and My Business Account portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario HST administration; Ontario Business Registry for statutory limited partnership registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign party establishing a Canadian connection of an Ontario limited partnership obtains tax identification in the following order: (1) file the Ontario Limited Partnership Declaration with the Ontario Business Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.9, and obtain an Ontario internal registration number; (2) cause the general partner or an authorized Canadian representative to apply to the CRA using Form RC101 through Business Registration Online for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN), selecting the GST/HST program account ('RT'); (3) where no Canadian-resident general partner exists, the non-resident partner/agent must provide proof of authority, notarized entity documents, and a Canadian address for CRA correspondence; CRA may issue a non-resident BN/program account; (4) after issuance of the BN and RT program account, the taxpayer can log into My Business Account or Represent a Client for GST/HST filings, T5013 partnership information return obligations, and provincial HST remittance; (5) no separate Ontario tax ID is required for HST because Ontario uses the federal RT account for HST administration, but other Ontario registrations may be required for payroll, tobacco, or specific sector levies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal income tax law. An Ontario LP is statutorily a fiscally transparent partnership under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, and neither the LP nor its partners may elect corporate taxation. The only qualifying elections in this context are administrative: registered persons under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act may elect to be a GST/HST-related group or close-connection for intercompany supply simplifications, and certain partner-level elections may be made under the Income Tax Act only for matters such as aligning fiscal periods or resource-property allocation; none of these alters the default pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000: an Ontario LP is a small supplier, and generally exempt from mandatory registration, unless its total worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies (including associated persons) exceed CAD $30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Canada above the threshold, or soliciting sales and delivering taxable goods in Canada, are statutorily required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier as part of the supply are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Titanium orthopedic hip-joint prosthetics manufactured and sold by the Ontario LP for delivery outside Canada qualify as zero-rated exports even if the buyer is an affiliated foreign distributor, provided title and risk pass before export and physical export occurs within a reasonable commercial time. Services to non-residents are zero-rated only when not performed for or in relation to Canadian real property, Canadian tangible personal property, or use/consumption in Canada; engineering/technical support services executed offshore for a foreign consignee generally meet this test. The exporter may claim input tax credits for manufacturing inputs attributable to zero-rated export sales, giving rise to a cash refund if ITCs exceed output tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A Canadian Export Declaration, transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System / CAED); low-value shipments under the administrative exception require a transportation manifest and shipping invoice in lieu of B13A
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (issued by the carrier) proving delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/wire-transfer advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign consignee

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing authorization for GST/HST invoices. Taxpayers must issue and retain readable GST/HST invoices containing the supplier's name and BN, a description, consideration, date, and tax amount; CRA accepts electronic records, EDI/PDF/e-invoices, if they meet the documentation standard in the Excise Tax Act. The CBSA's Canadian Export Declaration (B13A/CAED) is a customs-security declaration, not a tax-invoice clearance system.

CRA will scrutinize the chain from manufacturing through customs and foreign receipt; insufficient or inconsistent export documentation will cause the sale to be recharacterized as a taxable Canadian supply and the 13% HST assessed on the full consideration, with corresponding denial of ITCs. Civil penalties for false statements or gross negligence include penalties up to 50% of the understated net tax, CRA can disregard transactions and apply willful default reassessment time limits, and criminal prosecution is possible for intentionally fraudulent zero-rating claims. The taxpayer must preserve all underlying customs, shipping, purchase, manufacturing and FX settlement records for at least six years after the relevant taxation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating national currency with virtually unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. There are no exchange control permits for profit remittances, capital injections, or dividend distributions by an Ontario limited partnership. The Bank of Canada does not require exchange approval; the practical hurdle is the tax characterization and substance review of remittances by the Canada Revenue Agency plus FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds. Export receipts may remain in foreign currency offshore; however, they must be included in the partnership's Canadian tax income and reported on the appropriate T-series form or financial statements for Ontario corporate tax administration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; prudential foreign exchange and bank licensing additionally supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and cross-border transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Marginal as a core treasury vehicle; Low as a primary operating bank. Offshore digital EMIs are not regulated as deposit-taking banks in Canada and cannot lawfully hold core operational or escrow funds for the LP. They may be used only for ancillary supplier payments if they are registered as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC and remain subject to client due diligence; most Schedule I Canadian banks will reject inbound/outbound payments from unlicensed EMIs because of correspondent-account de-risking. The primary clearing and liquidity relationship must be held at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from non-resident partners are routed via SWIFT MT103 to the receiving Schedule I bank with the LP's account number, beneficiary legal name, and institution SWIFT/BIC. The payment must reference a clear purpose code, for example 'EQUI' or 'EQUITY INJECTION', and must state 'FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT' in the beneficiary line. The bank will complete FINTRAC large-transaction reporting if the amount exceeds C$10,000, and verification is performed through SWIFT gpi tracking and value-date confirmation.Supplier disbursements are cleared via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for routine purchase-price settlements and via commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) for bulk titanium acquisition, capital equipment, and heavy-value supply contracts. All payments must include a purpose text such as 'PURCHASE OF MEDICAL DEVICE RAW MATERIALS AND COMPONENTS', reference the invoice and purchase order numbers, and be supported by underlying documentation including a bill of lading, inspection certificates, Incoterms declaration, and pro forma invoice to satisfy bank AML and Canada Revenue Agency transfer-pricing audit requirements.Outright FX forward (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) executed with a Canadian Schedule I dealer under an ISDA 2002/CSA master agreement and supported by underlying trade receiptsFX option strategies (vanilla call/put and zero-cost collars) over freely convertible currencies to cap titanium procurement costs and protect U.S.-dollar receivablesCross-currency swap (USD/CAD) to align U.S.-dollar capital injections and long-term trade receivables with Canadian-dollar operating liabilitiesFX swap or swapfix (forward value-date replacement) for treasury liquidity smoothing without speculative open positionNon-deliverable forwards limited to genuinely non-deliverable currencies such as the Chinese renminbi, where the Canadian bank can net-settle offshore; a CAD NDF is not a permissible or useful hedging instrument because the Canadian dollar is fully deliverable
