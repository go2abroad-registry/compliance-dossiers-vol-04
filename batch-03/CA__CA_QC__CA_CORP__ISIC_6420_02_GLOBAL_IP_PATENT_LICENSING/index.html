Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec QST and provincial income tax. Online portals: CRA My Business Account, Revenu Québec CLIC / My Business Account, and Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity incorporates a Quebec or federal business corporation, obtains a federal incorporation number from Corporations Canada, obtains a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises, obtains a CRA Business Number (BN15) via Business Registration Online (form RC1 or non-resident RC372E where necessary), and registers for GST/HST. For Quebec, register for QST with Revenu Québec using the combined GST/HST-QST registration form or Form LM-1/VO; provide executed trademark/patent assignment and sub-license agreements to evidence the taxable supply. No minimum capital is required; non-resident shareholders are permissible.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity incorporates a Quebec or federal business corporation, obtains a federal incorporation number from Corporations Canada, obtains a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises, obtains a CRA Business Number (BN15) via Business Registration Online (form RC1 or non-resident RC372E where necessary), and registers for GST/HST. For Quebec, register for QST with Revenu Québec using the combined GST/HST-QST registration form or Form LM-1/VO; provide executed trademark/patent assignment and sub-license agreements to evidence the taxable supply. No minimum capital is required; non-resident shareholders are permissible.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. Canadian and Quebec law classify a corporation by statute; a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act. Available elections include: (i) the GST/HST closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat intercorporate supplies as nil for GST/HST; (ii) GST/HST group registration under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act; and (iii) the corresponding Quebec QST election for closely related registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and non-elective under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act and must be analyzed for cross-border patent royalty flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada/Quebec in any single calendar quarter of any current or previous four-consecutive-quarter period; GST/HST and QST registration become mandatory once the C$30,000 threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are removed from Canada and documentary proof of export is retained. For patents and trademarks, a supply of a right to use intangible personal property is zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and who does not acquire the right for consumption or use in Canada; if the licensed IP rights are used wholly outside Canada, the royalty is zero-rated. Quebec QST mirrors the federal treatment for supplies made in Quebec to non-residents where the IP is not used or enjoyed in Quebec. Ancillary services supporting the cross-border patent sub-license may also be zero-rated when performed for a non-resident and not used in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration where required under the Customs Act / Export Control List
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or equivalent carrier document evidencing physical export movement where goods are involved
  • Commercial invoice or royalty invoice detailing patent/trademark rights, territory, consideration, and licensee
  • Executed patent/trademark sub-license agreement and evidence of chain of title
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

Canada and Quebec do not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. No national continuous transaction control mandate is in force. Invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST and QST data under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec taxation rules. Electronic invoicing is permitted if the invoice is legible and accessible; there is no government API clearance requirement. Large registrants must file GST/HST and QST returns electronically and remit payments through CRA and Revenu Québec portals.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess denied zero-rated supplies, claw back input tax credits or refunds, and apply interest from the original due date. False statements or omissions regarding export eligibility can trigger penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act and gross-negligence penalties under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, with equivalent Quebec penalties. Repeated or fraudulent export zero-rating claims may lead to criminal prosecution, director and officer liability, and confiscation of refunds plus substantial monetary penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, AML controls, tax rules, securities and investment laws, and transaction-specific reporting. There is no general exchange-control approval requirement for foreign investment, intercompany funding, royalty receipts, or dividend remittances. Payments to non-residents can trigger Canadian withholding tax, commonly 25% on Canadian-source royalties unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty and supported by required beneficial-ownership and treaty documentation. Dividends may also be subject to withholding, generally 25% before treaty relief. Related-party royalties and financing must be supportable under Canadian transfer-pricing rules; treaty entitlement, beneficial ownership, anti-avoidance rules, and Quebec tax considerations require transaction-specific review. A Canadian corporation should retain contracts, invoices, tax forms, and evidence of the commercial purpose and valuation of each cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general mandatory export-proceeds surrender, forced-conversion ratio, or statutory deadline requiring exporters to convert or repatriate foreign-currency receipts. A Quebec corporation may retain receipts in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, accounting and tax reporting, sanctions, and any contractual or transaction-specific obligations. Banks may request invoices, license agreements, customs or shipping records where relevant, and payment-purpose information.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration requiring routine approval of ordinary cross-border payments or currency conversion. The federal Department of Finance, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) have separate tax, AML, sanctions, prudential, and reporting roles.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment services; low as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank or for complex cross-border royalty treasury. Canada does not use a general EU-style electronic-money-institution authorization category. A foreign payment provider may need registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act and related regulations if it performs covered retail payment activities in Canada; applicable FINTRAC registration, AML obligations, sanctions screening, safeguarding, and bank-partner requirements must also be assessed. An offshore EMI account does not remove Canadian corporate, tax, recordkeeping, or reporting obligations and may not be accepted for capital subscriptions, escrow, or high-value intercompany royalty flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Receive subscriptions, shareholder loans, and parent-company funding into a Canadian-dollar or appropriate foreign-currency corporate account through SWIFT or another bank-approved cross-border wire rail. Use the exact legal name of the Canadian corporation and its account details; identify the sender, ultimate funding source, currency, transaction purpose, and relationship between payer and recipient. Canada has no universal statutory FX purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires; provide a clear payment narrative and any bank-requested purpose or transaction codes. Before release, provide subscription or loan documents, board resolutions, beneficial-owner information, and source-of-funds evidence. Reconcile the incoming amount to the corporate ledger and capitalization or debt records. Do not route funds through personal or unrelated third-party accounts. Escrow or blocked accounts are transaction-specific and must be arranged with the bank.Settle royalties, licence fees, goods, and services by bank wire, commonly SWIFT MT103 for cross-border customer credit transfers, or by commercial letter of credit where the transaction and counterparty risk justify it. Match each payment to the executed contract, invoice, calculation statement, and supporting delivery or IP-use evidence. Confirm beneficiary name, bank, account or IBAN where applicable, SWIFT/BIC, intermediary-bank instructions, currency, fees, and payment reference. Screen counterparties, beneficial owners, banks, and jurisdictions for Canadian sanctions and AML concerns. Determine Canadian withholding tax and treaty eligibility before paying non-resident royalties or other potentially subject payments; remit and report withholding when required. Retain payment records, agreements, invoices, tax filings, and transfer-pricing support. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 2 million aggregate transaction value requiring Form T106 filing and contemporaneous TP documentation

Economic substance scope: Canada has no stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the offshore sense; however, GAAR and TP rules require economic substance, real decision-making, and meaningful business activity for a resident IP Holding Company to obtain treaty benefits and avoid reassessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment plus interest and penalties (Income Tax Act s. 238, 239)

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable; civil restitution and travel bans may be imposed; imprisonment potentially applicable
Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of tax benefit, full reassessment, interest, and a 10% penalty on the denied benefit; Canadian court orders may require corporate reorganisation

Director personal liability: Directors may become personally liable for corporate tax remittances and penalties if they knowingly participated in a sham; civil fraud remedies apply
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment up to 10 years under the Criminal Code and PCMLTFA

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted and personally fined or imprisoned; court-imposed travel bans and civil restitution orders may be issued
