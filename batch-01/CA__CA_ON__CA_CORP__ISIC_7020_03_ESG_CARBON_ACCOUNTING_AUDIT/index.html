Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal portals: 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration; Ontario corporate income tax and the Ontario provincial HST component are administered by the CRA under the Canada-Ontario Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement, with the Ontario Ministry of Finance retaining policy authority over the 8% provincial HST rate and Ontario corporate tax rules.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian business corporation (inc. or corp.) obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA, either instantly through Business Registration Online (BRO) after Articles of Incorporation are issued, or via paper Form RC1 (processing time 15-25 business days). The BN is the single statutory tax identifier activating program accounts for GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and customs (RM). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or establishing a Canadian permanent establishment must file Form RC1 within 15 days of commencing business and must appoint a Canadian representative for service of documents. No separate Ontario TIN is required for corporate income tax or HST; a separate Ontario Employer Health Tax account with the Ministry of Finance is required only if Ontario payroll remuneration exceeds CAD 1,000,000 annually. The ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage mandate (ISIC 7020) requires no additional tax licence prior to BN activation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Canadian business corporation; the entity is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act plus Ontario corporate tax. Available elections relevant to this cross-border structure: (i) ITA s.85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of property into the corporation; (ii) ITA s.216 election for a non-resident to report Canadian rental income on a net basis; (iii) Excise Tax Act s.156(2) election to treat supplies between closely related registrants as nil consideration; (iv) ETA s.273 joint venture election; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18.4-18.6 operate as mandatory adjustment rules with no elective opt-out; (vi) single-member/greenhouse-gas credit registry transfers do not qualify for any special entity-level election under the ETA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; the same threshold generally applies to non-residents supplying services into Canada, except that a non-resident with a permanent establishment in Canada is not eligible for the small-supplier exemption and must register regardless of revenue level. ESG carbon audit advisory and offset brokerage services are taxable supplies; voluntary registration before the threshold is permitted and may be advisable to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (i) section 1 zero-rates tangible goods exported by the supplier; (ii) section 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident, including Scope 1-3 carbon footprint auditing and ESG advisory, provided the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and is not acting as a Canadian agent; (iii) carbon offset credits transferred from a foreign registry to a non-resident buyer qualify as zero-rated intangible personal property exports when the registry delivery occurs outside Canada. Interprovincial supplies of offset credits within Canada remain subject to 13% HST unless separately zero-rated. The supplier must retain contemporaneous documentation demonstrating the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore place of benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or ACI e-manifest for goods consignments)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods and exit from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire or SWIFT confirmation showing foreign-source payment into the Canadian entity's account)
  • Executed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, scope of services, and absence of Canadian situs
  • GST/HST Schedule VI Part V technical analysis memorandum documenting the zero-rating position for audit file retention

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA API clearance obligation is currently in force for Ontario taxpayers. CRA accepts electronic invoices and books provided they are legible and retained for six years pursuant to ITA s.230 and ETA s.286. Voluntary interoperability standards apply under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) for B2B e-invoicing; the Government of Canada has announced but not yet enacted a digital reporting regime for GST/HST covering large registrants. Revenu Quebec has proposed phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing from 2027 for large suppliers, which is not applicable to an Ontario-based entity absent interprovincial Quebec operations.

Canada imposes no express statutory 180-day currency repatriation deadline for zero-rated service exports; the 180-day benchmark reflects CRA audit practice of applying commercially reasonable collection periods, and a receivable outstanding beyond 180 days may trigger additional scrutiny of the export narrative. For exported goods, CRA generally requires evidence of export within a reasonable time after the supply, failing which the zero-rating is reversed. Penalties and clawback risks under the Excise Tax Act include: s.285(1) misstatement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the net tax; s.280 failure-to-remit interest at 6% per annum compounding daily; s.281.1 late-filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per full month; s.298 reassessment period of four years for a registrant, extended indefinitely where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established; s.245 ITA general anti-avoidance rule may re-characterize artificial export-zero-rating structures; criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 / ETA s.327 for tax evasion carries fines of 50%-250% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member state. The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible for current international transactions and capital account inflows/outflows, with no exchange-control licensing, capital repatriation authorization, or central-bank approval gate. Non-resident shareholders of an Ontario business corporation may freely receive dividends, redemption proceeds, and liquidation surpluses, subject only to non-resident Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and, where applicable, Section 219 branch tax on Canadian-source business income remitted abroad. Ontario and federal tax filings (CRA) are required for dividend distributions, but there are no currency convertibility impediments. The principal remittance hurdles are fiscal (withholding tax, treaty eligibility, FATCA and CRS reporting), not regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds or foreign currency receipts. A Canadian exporter and cross-border service provider (including ESG carbon audit and carbon offset brokerage) may retain funds in CAD, USD, or any other freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada approval. Statutory compliance is limited to CRA record-keeping obligations requiring exporter invoices, bank receipts, and supporting contracts to be retained for six years, and to PCMLTFA anti-money laundering duties: inbound international electronic funds transfers equal to or greater than CAD 10,000 received in a single SWIFT or wire instruction are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the receiving financial institution. There is no surrender certificate or conversion ratio to file.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation and supervision of Canadian banks is separately vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (SC 1991, c. 46). Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration; exchange rate policy and monetary conditions are administered by the Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance Canada under a floating-rate framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability with strict legal caveats. Foreign digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar) may lawfully operate in Canada only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and may not hold deposits or provide a primary operating account equivalent to a Schedule I bank. Usage of a duly registered FINTRAC/MSB fintech is a workable supplementary rail for receiving multi-currency inflows from US and EU clients and for making cross-border disbursements to offset suppliers, provided the platform accepts the fully documented corporate KYC package and the treasury conducts daily reconciliation between EMI balances and the primary bank account. An unregistered offshore EMI, or an entity that purports to accept deposits without Canadian authorization, is a regulatory unacceptable counterparty and must be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital injections from non-resident investors are routed through SWIFT MT103 with an ISO 20022 external purpose code such as EQUI or INVE for equity subscription and LOAN or INTC for shareholder advances. The CAD leg settles through Payments Canada Lynx (the high-value clearing system), while USD-denominated injections settle via the bank's US correspondent on CHIPS or Fedwire and, if the operating account is CAD-denominated, are spot-converted by the receiving bank at a pre-agreed commercial rate. The treasury must pre-clear the receiving branch with a beneficiary reference, the defined purpose code, source-of-funds documentation, and the corporate Powertto verify that the remittance is booked without delay under the Corporations Returns Act threshold reporting to Statistics Canada where applicable.Outbound trade settlements for ESG audit fees, carbon offset purchases, and consulting disbursements are executed over SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with full remittance information for ordinary supplier payments. Larger offset-supplier commitments are supported by a Commercial Letter of Credit in the ICC UCP 600 / eUCP v2.0 framework, issued through the bank's trade finance desk using SWIFT MT700 and MT707 messages, with documents presented conforming to the LC terms. Domestic CAD disbursements above the low-value threshold settle via Payments Canada Lynx, USD outflows settle through the bank's correspondent network (CHIPS) into the beneficiary's US bank, and recurring payment files are transmitted host-to-host from the company's enterprise resource planning system to the bank's cash management platform for same-day value dating.Onshore deliverable CAD/USD FX Forwards with maturities up to 24 months executed under a bank FX master agreement and subject to OSFI-supervised credit limitsNon-deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on non-convertible offset-origination currencies (e.g., CNY, BRL, IDR, KRW) used solely to hedge carbon credit purchase price exposure in emerging marketsCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Canadian-law credit support annex for long-term foreign currency working capitalVanilla FX Options in CAD/USD (calls and puts) purchased through the corporate banking FX desk to establish a guaranteed maximum cost of USD disbursements

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with strict legal caveats. Foreign digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar) may lawfully operate in Canada only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and may not hold deposits or provide a primary operating account equivalent to a Schedule I bank. Usage of a duly registered FINTRAC/MSB fintech is a workable supplementary rail for receiving multi-currency inflows from US and EU clients and for making cross-border disbursements to offset suppliers, provided the platform accepts the fully documented corporate KYC package and the treasury conducts daily reconciliation between EMI balances and the primary bank account. An unregistered offshore EMI, or an entity that purports to accept deposits without Canadian authorization, is a regulatory unacceptable counterparty and must be avoided.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is mandatory when reportable non-arm's-length transactions with the same non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate fair market value per taxation year; Country-by-Country reporting is required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds USD 750,000,000 (approximately CAD 1 billion); contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation should be prepared at all materiality levels to satisfy reasonable-efforts standards

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to Cayman/Bermuda, but CRA applies statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), transfer-pricing recharacterization, and treaty residence rules requiring genuine management and commercial activity. A carbon-offset brokerage with foreign non-arm's-length affiliates must demonstrate substantive decision-making, real employees, office premises, and pre-tax business purpose to avoid denial of tax benefits and treaty access

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: summary conviction offence carries a fine of CAD 5,000 to CAD 50,000 plus a penalty of 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment carries a fine and imprisonment up to 5 years; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, sign, or acquiesce in wilful evasion may be prosecuted personally; courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution for unpaid taxes, and travel bans or imprisonment can follow an indictable conviction
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may redetermine taxable income under ITA s.247(2); a 10% penalty applies under s.247(7) on the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to use arm's-length arrangements; gross negligence penalties under s.163(2) may also apply

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate transfer-pricing penalties, but they become personally liable if they sign false returns, provide false information, or use the corporation as a mere facade for a shamtax scheme; criminal prosecution or civil fraud damages may follow
GAAR / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: ITA s.245(2) denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the transaction; deduction/credit claims are disallowed retroactively, and taxpayers may face the 50% gross negligence penalty where there is knowing or grossly negligent participation; provincial reassessment barriers are lost for misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or maintain a shell to commit abuse may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of the denied benefit if corporate assets are depleted; travel restrictions may be sought in serious cross-border avoidance cases involving evidence flight
Money Laundering / Unregistered Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code money laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 14 years; PCMLTFA registration, record-keeping, and reporting violations attract administrative monetary penalties and criminal fines; sanitization of proceeds enables forfeiture proceedings and international asset freezes

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in laundering or unregistered money-services activity face personal criminal liability, civil forfeiture of personal assets, passport hold/travel bans, and imprisonment
