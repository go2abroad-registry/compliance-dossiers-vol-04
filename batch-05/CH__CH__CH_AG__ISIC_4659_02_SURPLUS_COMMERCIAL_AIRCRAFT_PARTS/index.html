Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC; official portal https://www.estv.admin.ch); cantonal tax administrations (e.g., Zug or Zurich) are separately competent for cantonal corporate income and capital taxes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office must first appoint a Swiss fiscal agent or establish a branch registered in the Swiss commercial registry. The entity then applies for a central business identification number (UID) through the Federal Statistical Office UID register or directly files the FTA VAT registration application with the ESTV eService portal using Form 2025 for foreign legal entities. The application must attach incorporation documents, director identification, power of attorney, Swiss representative mandate, and projected turnover statements. The UID (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) issued by the Federal Statistical Office constitutes the statutory VAT identification number for all federal tax dealings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Aktiengesellschaft CH_AG is a Swiss-resident corporate taxpayer; subject to federal corporate income tax, cantonal/communal profit and capital tax; worldwide income is in principle taxable, with Swiss law territorial carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate).

Available election forms

Swiss law has no check-the-box election. Elective mechanisms available under Swiss federal and cantonal law include VA group taxation (Art. 11 MWSTG), the participation deduction election under Art. 69/70 StHG for qualifying intercorporate dividends, cantonal tax consolidation (e.g., 100% subsidiaries in Zug/Zurich), and explicit filing elections in transfer-pricing and functional documentation. Hybrid mismatch rules under Art. 58a StHG apply automatically to neutralise double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover from Swiss-taxable activities (excluding VAT-exempt supplies and zero-rated export sales); a non-resident supplier that only exports goods or supplies offshore B2B services to foreign recipients has no Swiss VAT registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports, including certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics, are zero-rated under Art. 23 para. 2 no. 1 MWSTG when physical export is verified. Cross-border services, such as MRO services, logistics, brokerage services and digital services to a foreign airline, are zero-rated under Art. 23 para. 2 no. 2 MWSTG when the recipient is outside Switzerland. Zero-rating requires prompt removal from Swiss customs territory and full documentary substantiation by the taxpayer.

  • Swiss customs export declaration submitted electronically through the e-dec / AES system with movement reference number
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and transport evidence identifying ISIC 4659 aircraft parts and Incoterm
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of the foreign currency payment
  • Copy of title transfer document or equipment purchase agreement
  • Destination-country customs admission document or customs clearance certificate where available

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API revenue-authorisation, or digital stamping regime is currently in force. Swiss VAT law permits electronic invoicing, and QR-bill is the statutory payment slip standard since 30 June 2020; invoices must include compulsory VAT data under Art. 26 MWSTG and the issuer must retain original invoice and export evidence for commercial and VAT audit purposes.

The FTA may conduct risk-based audits of aviation parts exporters. If zero-rating evidence fails, the full 8.1% VAT is retroactively assessed with default interest at 4% per annum, the corresponding input VAT is disallowed, and administrative fines apply; wilful tax evasion attracts up to three times the evaded tax and imprisonment, increased in aggravated cases, with a limitation period of 10 years; negligent under-reporting triggers lower fines and a 5-year period. Sham export documentation is referred to customs criminal enforcement authorities for potential further sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments and the Swiss Debt Enforcement Act. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, trade payments, and capital movements are freely transferable without exchange-control approval. The only practical hurdles are corporate-law distributable-profit requirements, Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividends unless reduced by a double-tax treaty, FINMA anti-money-laundering compliance screening, and SNB statistical reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency, credited to a Swiss or foreign bank account, and used directly for trade disbursements. The only reporting obligations are statistical in nature under the Swiss National Bank Ordinance on the collection of data on cross-border financial relations, plus standard anti-money-laundering documentation for cash-like instruments.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque Nationale Suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMIs and offshore digital payment institutions are viable only as supplementary payment rails, not as primary treasury accounts under Swiss law. Switzerland does not recognise EEA or UK EMI passporting; any EMI or fintech operating with Swiss nexus must obtain appropriate FINMA authorisation as a bank, fintech licensee under Article 1b Banking Act, or registered payment service provider under the Anti-Money Laundering Act. For a medium-risk aviation spares trading company, an offshore digital EMI is rated Low for primary settlement and Medium for ancillary collections, subject to FINMA-compliant KYC and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory rate (combined cantonal/communal effective rate varies by canton, typically 12-21%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory withholding tax on dividends; reduced to 0% under Swiss participation exemption (≥10% shareholding, ≥1 year holding) for qualifying corporate shareholders; treaty relief for non-residents (typically 0-15% depending on DTA).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months of fiscal year-end; extensions granted upon written request (typically up to 12 months).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Net profit before tax (commercial profit per financial statements) Non-resident companies with Swiss permanent establishment (PE) must report PE-attributable profit; otherwise, only Swiss-source income (e.g., real estate, permanent establishment) is taxable. Late filing: administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 (per canton); late payment: default interest at 4% per annum; intentional non-filing may lead to criminal prosecution.
Box 2 Add-back of non-deductible expenses (e.g., taxes, excessive director fees, hidden profit distributions) Same adjustments apply to PE profit; ensure arm's length pricing for intercompany transactions. Late filing: administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 (per canton); late payment: default interest at 4% per annum; intentional non-filing may lead to criminal prosecution.

Late filing: administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 (per canton); late payment: default interest at 4% per annum; intentional non-filing may lead to criminal prosecution.
Box 4 Taxable profit (after adjustments and deductions) Compute Swiss taxable income; apply federal and cantonal/communal rates. Late filing: administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 (per canton); late payment: default interest at 4% per annum; intentional non-filing may lead to criminal prosecution.
Box 5 Tax credits (e.g., foreign tax credit, withholding tax credits) Claim foreign tax credit for taxes paid abroad on Swiss-source income; attach Form 823 for treaty relief. Late filing: administrative fine up to CHF 10,000 (per canton); late payment: default interest at 4% per annum; intentional non-filing may lead to criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory if related-party transactions exceed CHF 5 million (per year); Master File mandatory if group consolidated turnover exceeds CHF 900 million. Simplified documentation for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) with transactions below CHF 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but requires genuine economic substance for tax residency and to benefit from treaty relief. Anti-abuse rules (e.g., General Anti-Avoidance Rule, substance-over-form) apply; a shell company without substance may be denied treaty benefits and could be subject to recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CHF 250,000 (or 3x the evaded tax) and imprisonment up to 3 years; civil penalty of up to 3x the evaded tax; interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly cause the company to evade tax; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; imprisonment for responsible officers.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residency, recharacterization of income, retroactive taxation, and penalties up to 30% of underpaid tax; potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they sign false declarations; travel bans and extradition risks in cross-border cases.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to CHF 500,000 for individuals and CHF 5 million for companies; imprisonment up to 5 years; confiscation of assets; revocation of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for AML breaches; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment; civil liability for damages.
