Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal GST/HST, customs, and income tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec QST and provincial tax administration. Portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (canada.ca) and Revenu Québec 'clicSÉQUR' (revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership establishing or carrying on business in Quebec must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online service, including separate GST/HST account registration when taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. Subsequently, the partnership must register with Revenu Québec for a QST number and, where applicable, for provincial payroll and employer deductions. In the Quebec public records context, the LP may obtain a 'numéro d'entreprise du Québec' (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises; the NEQ may be combined with CRA BN for coordinated filing. For a free trade zone bonded depot, the operator must also have a 'licensed bonded warehouse' approval from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and may need a customs bonded warehouse license, but the core tax ID remains the BN and QST number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada. A limited partnership is mandatory pass-through for income tax purposes under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; it files an information return (Form T5013/T5013 Summary) and allocates income or losses to partners. Quebec limited partnerships (sociétés en commandite) are similarly transparent for Quebec income tax. Relevant elections include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under s. 96(8) of the Income Tax Act, (ii) joint elections under Excise Tax Act s. 156 for closely related persons to treat supplies as made for nominal consideration, and (iii) the 'actual use and benefit' ratio election for financial institutions under ETA s. 188.1. Canada has hybrid mismatch ordering rules in Income Tax Act ss. 18.6 and 18.7, and corresponding Quebec measures, but an LP cannot elect to alter its domestic tax classification; foreign-check-the-box status of a Canadian LP for U.S. or other purposes has no effect on Canadian CRA treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST and CAD 30,000 for QST over any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or the current quarter if a business has not existed for four quarters). Non-residents carrying on a taxable business in Canada are equally subject to the small supplier threshold but must register if they make taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the day the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to a GST/HST and QST registrant where the supply of goods constitutes an 'export' under Excise Tax Act s. 105, i.e., goods are sold and delivered outside Canada, or are exported by the purchaser within prescribed time limits. For services, zero-rating applies where the service is supplied to a non-resident who is not a GST registrant and the service is not performed in Canada (ETA ss. 7 and 23), or is supplied in respect of real or tangible personal property situated outside Canada. A bonded depot operator in a free trade zone may zero-rate goods sold to a foreign buyer, provided the goods are physically removed from the zone and exported, with title passing outside Canada; storage and handling services relating exclusively to exported goods may also be zero-rated. A registrant must retain sufficient evidence within two months (or shorter stipulated period) after delivery to prove the goods were exported. If not satisfied, CRA treats the supply as taxable and may issue an assessment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CAERS electronic export report with CBSA confirmation code)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing place of discharge outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other banking/FX remittance evidence confirming payment from the foreign purchaser
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing goods, value, and export destination
  • CEA Section 95 export reporting documentation and customs entry/warehouse release documents from the bonded depot

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists under Canadian GST/QST law. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed data and retain books and records in legible paper or electronic form at their establishment in Canada. For exports and bonded depot operators, CBSA requires electronic export declarations filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) and the eManifest portal; customs entries may carry a unique confirmation code, and CBSA may require supplemental electronic documentation for goods moving from a free trade zone or bonded depot.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit export zero-rating claims rigorously, particularly in free trade zone and bonded warehouse structures. A claim found to be unsupported by export documentation is recharacterized as taxable, resulting in GST/QST assessment, interest from the due date, and recovery of any input tax credits claimed on the associated warehousing and logistics costs. Gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 285 may reach 25% of the tax (or 100% for repeat offenders in Quebec) plus civil interest. Knowingly making false export claims, forging export documents, or willful omission is a criminal offence under ETA s. 327 and s. 327.1, carrying fines up to CAD 200,000 (or CAD 25,000 on summary conviction) and imprisonment up to 5 years. The normal reassessment period for a registered person is 4 years, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation, and is unlimited for fraud or willful default. Quebec QST carries parallel penalties under the Taxation Act and the Excise Tax Act framework, and records must be retained for at least 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the Canadian dollar is freely floating with no exchange-control permits, no central-bank authorization for inward equity subscriptions, loan repayments, royalties, or partnership profit distributions, and no approval for opening foreign-currency accounts. Profit remittances by a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident limited partners trigger only Canadian Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally treaty-reduced, for example 15 percent for a U.S. resident corporate or partnership member under the Canada-United States tax treaty) and corresponding Quebec non-resident withholding under the Taxation Act (Quebec); no repatriation quotas, surrender requisites, licensing, or FX rationing hurdles exist. The practical frictions are bank-level source-of-funds diligence, the submission of the relevant Canada Revenue Agency forms and the Quebec NR301 partnership declaration, and treaty-residency certifications when treaty relief is claimed; dividends, interest, and distribution payments are fully convertible and remittable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation deadline, or statutory conversion ratio for export revenues. The Quebec bonded-depot operator may retain export proceeds in any convertible currency in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts or in offshore maintaining accounts, provided the underlying receipts are booked in the Canadian entity. The applicable obligation is documentary rather than surrender-based: exports from the free trade zone require electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED, Form A8) for goods valued above CAD 2,000, with reporting generally due within 15 days after clearance, and CBSA eManifest consignment data applies to shipments transiting bonded corridors; bonded warehouse operations additionally require duty-suspended inventory reconciliation, CBSA periodic physical examinations, and annual licensing renewal. Treasury policy may nevertheless pair export receivables with import payables and use spot or forward markets to manage net exposure, given the absence of any regulatory conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the federal monetary authority and lender of last resort; foreign exchange and trade compliance functions are shared among the Department of Finance Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), the Canada Revenue Agency, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; prudential supervision of Schedule I banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Bank of Canada operates the oversight framework for payment clearing and settlement systems including Lynx and the Retail Payment Activities Act regime administered by the Bank of Canada.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Canada-Quebec general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec); not directly imposed on the Limited Partnership as a flow-through entity, but allocated to corporate partners resident in Quebec

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding applies to dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; pure distributions of partnership profits to non-resident limited partners are not categorically subject to Part XIII dividend WHT, but may be subject to tax under Part I or treaty protection depending on the partner’s status and permanent establishment

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 CAD aggregated annual value of cross-border transactions with non-arm’s length parties triggers mandatory Form T106 reporting; contemporaneous local-file/master-file documentation is expected for all intercompany transactions above C$1M; country-by-country reporting applies for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750M or more

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but substance is mandatory under GAAR (Income Tax Act s. 245) and treaty abuse rules; bonded warehouse license and free-trade-zone operations require physical premises, security bond, inventory control, customs supervision, and active management in Quebec

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239: fines up to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus interest and reassessment

Director personal liability: General partners and directors may be jointly and severally liable for unreported employee source deductions and GST/HST; travel bans and asset seizure are possible after a conviction
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR permits CRA to ignore the transaction, re-determine tax, impose 100% of the tax benefit plus interest, and apply the general anti-avoidance penalty; customs authorities may revoke the bonded-warehouse licence

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the limited-liability shield if the limited partner participates in control or management; directors and officers can be held personally liable for resulting tax and penalties
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Quebec and federal AML enforcement: criminal fines up to C$2,000,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, asset confiscation, and customs forfeiture of the bonded goods

Director personal liability: Officers, partners, and compliance officers are personally liable under the Criminal Code and customs legislation; travel bans and administrative monetary penalties may be imposed
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII

Statutory sanction: Unremitted amount becomes immediately collectible with 10% per annum interest and a minimum 20% penalty for failure to collect; criminal prosecution for wilful non-remission

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts under federal and Quebec law; travel bans are possible for persistent non-compliance
