Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Business Corporation incorporated federally or in Ontario is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through status is available for a corporation; territorial scope includes worldwide income for resident corporations with foreign tax credit relief.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Canadian tax law; a corporation is always entity-level taxed. Relevant administrative elections include the GST/HST joint venture election, the closely related corporation deemed-supply election for GST/HST, the functional currency reporting election for Canadian income tax, and the section 216(4) election for non-residents deriving Canadian rental income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD — a non-resident supplier carrying on business in Canada without a permanent establishment in Canada is generally required to register for GST/HST if its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, unless exempt or zero-rated supplies limit the obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Canadian supplier may zero-rate exports if the goods are delivered by the supplier to an address outside Canada by commercial carrier or mail, or if the supply is exempt under the export rules for services provided to a non-resident person where the service is performed wholly outside Canada or meets the 'in respect of exported tangible personal property' exception; the supplier must keep proof that the goods left Canada and the purchaser is a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency ACI/export report or form B13A where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation substantiating collection of the sale proceeds in a foreign currency

No Canadian mandate for real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing; registered suppliers must file GST/HST returns electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, retain full audit trails, and may use optional Peppol-based e-invoicing; CRA e-services do not impose transactional API clearance or real-time tax stamping for standard export invoices.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period and apply the gross-negligence or false-statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act, plus interest and possible criminal prosecution for knowing fraud; improperly claimed zero-ratings are clawed back with penalty and interest calculated from the original filing date, and willful misrepresentation may extend the limitation period indefinitely.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no exchange-control legislation, no licensing of FX transactions, and no limitation on corporate dividend distributions or capital repatriation, provided Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% for dividends/interest/royalties) is deducted and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Ontario solvency tests under the Ontario Business Corporations Act must be met for capital reductions or distributions. No CUSMA or Bank Act capital-movement restrictions apply to a fully Canadian-owned or foreign-owned non-bank operating corporation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export-proceeds surrender rule, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained offshore or onshore in any freely convertible currency. Export declarations are mandatory for each shipment valued above CAD 2,000, filed electronically with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS). All electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC; the bank will require a purpose code and underlying commercial documentation for the FX component of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange transactional regulation is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); prudential regulation of banks and payments is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. EU/UK-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) do not hold passporting rights into Canada and cannot maintain a deposit-taking business onshore without registration as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and, if acting as a payment service provider, registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Offshore digital EMIs may be used only as ancillary collection rails for small distributor prepayments below CAD 10,000; they are not viable for capital escrow, trade settlement, or LC operations, and funds placed with an unregistered EMI are not CDIC-insured.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal/Ontario general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); 12.2% combined rate for active business income of a qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on the first CAD 500,000 of taxable income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (federal Part XIII withholding tax on gross dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, absent treaty relief).

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. Final taxes payable are due two months after fiscal year-end (three months for eligible CCPCs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length parties in a taxation year is the reporting threshold for Form T106. There is no statutorily fixed minimum amount for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; all controlled cross-border transactions require reasonable contemporaneous documentation to avoid the s.247(3) penalty. Country-by-country reporting is mandatory when the consolidated group revenue reaches CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the CRA may disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial substance under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the transfer-pricing anti-abuse provisions of s.247. For a food wholesale distributor, real economic substance is demonstrated through active local management, warehouse and inventory presence, employment, purchasing contracts, and decision-making discretion in Ontario.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction, a fine equal to 50%–100% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; upon indictment, a fine equal to 100%–200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years. In addition, the gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the understated tax, plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or orchestrate evasion are personally liable to criminal prosecution and imprisonment. The CRA can pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a sham or a facade for personal enrichment, allowing collection against personal assets. Travel bans may be imposed pending sentencing or extradition in serious cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Transaction / GAAR (Income Tax Act s.245)

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit and impose a 15% GAAR penalty (s.245(3)) plus interest. Alternatively, transfer pricing reassessment under s.247(2) may be applied, attracting the s.247(3) penalty equal to the lesser of 10% of the gross adjustment and 50% of the transfer-pricing adjustment, plus potential T106 late filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Where the corporation is a mere shell used to evade tax, courts may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to restore diverted funds. Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and unpaid withholding taxes; personal travel bans and civil restitution orders may be issued in civil or collection proceedings.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax (Part XIII) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable under ITA s.227(8) for the full unremitted Part XIII amount, plus penalties and interest under s.227(9). The amount may be denied as an income deduction under s.215(2), and the failure may constitute an offence punishable on summary conviction or indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for the entire unremitted withholding tax, including interest and penalties, regardless of corporate liquidation. Personal assets may be seized to satisfy the director liability; serious or repeated non-compliance may trigger travel bans and criminal prosecution.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Compliance (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA can reach CAD 100,000 for a first violation and CAD 500,000 for a subsequent violation. On indictment, a corporation may be fined at the discretion of the court and responsible individuals may be imprisoned for up to 5 years for non-compliance with a FINTRAC demand.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who 'direct, authorize, or acquiesce' in a violation are personally liable for the administrative monetary penalties and are subject to prosecution. In cross-border cases, travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed pending criminal proceedings, and directors may be barred from serving as corporate fiduciaries in Canada.
