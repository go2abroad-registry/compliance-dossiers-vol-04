Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); for Quebec-specific QST and provincial obligations, Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST with the CRA via Business Registration Online. Because the LP is established in Quebec, it must also register with Revenu Québec for a QST account and with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Business Registry) for a Quebec enterprise number. The application must include the partnership's legal name, registered office in Quebec, governing instrument, identity and residency of all partners (general and limited), estimated annual taxable supplies in Canada/Quebec, and commencement date of commercial activity. The CRA/Revenu Québec will issue the BN/QST number upon verification of the entity's constitution; a representative for service of process in Quebec is generally required if no office is maintained there.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada. A Quebec limited partnership is statutorily transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and the Quebec Taxation Act: partnership income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. Relevant elections include: (i) an election under subsection 97(2) ITA on the contribution of property to partnership capital; (ii) an election under subsection 216(1) ITA for a non-resident partner to be taxed on net Canadian rental income instead of gross Part XIII withholding; and (iii) a designation as a ‘Canadian partnership’ under subsection 102(1) ITA, which is effectively a statutory status rather than an elective mechanism. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 and 18.5 ITA apply imperatively; no elective carve-out for a Quebec LP exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada / Quebec in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the small-supplier threshold). Registration is mandatory once this threshold is exceeded, including where the supplies are zero-rated exports; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted for commercial activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property (e.g., silicone fidget products) is zero-rated under section 1, Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the goods are supplied in Canada to a foreign customer and are delivered by the vendor or on the vendor's behalf to a destination outside Canada. Equivalent zero-rating applies under Schedule III to the Quebec Sales Tax Act (QST). The LP must be a GST/QST registrant, the goods must physically leave Canada within a reasonable time, and the vendor must maintain documentary evidence of export, including the foreign purchaser's identity, the destination, and the carrier's confirmation of international shipment. For services supplied to non-residents and performed wholly outside Canada, separate zero-rating provisions apply under paragraph 7(a), Part V, Schedule VI.

  • CBSA commercial export declaration (Form B13A or approved electronic export reporting via CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System/EDI) where the export is subject to reporting thresholds or permit control
  • Commercial invoice, packing slip, and transport documents (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill) clearly showing the LP as shipper, the foreign consignee, and direct shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer records and foreign exchange remittance confirmations evidencing that the export proceeds were received from the overseas buyer

Canada does not currently impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control regime. GST/HST registrants with taxable sales above CAD 1,000,000 are required to file returns electronically through the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE; Revenu Québec similarly requires electronic filing for QST registrants above certain thresholds. E-invoicing is permitted by mutual consent between suppliers and buyers, and Canada is moving toward Peppol BIS interoperability for voluntary e-invoice exchange; no digital clearance mandate applies today.

If CRA or Revenu Québec determines that the zero-rating claim was not substantiated, or that the export was fictitious or fraudulent, the tax authority will reassess the supply at the standard rate (GST + QST) with arrears interest and penalties. Under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, knowingly making a false statement in a return or rebate application can trigger penalties of CAD 250 plus 25% of the claimed amount; gross negligence penalties under subsection 285(1) ITA may reach 50% of the understated tax. Revenu Québec imposes parallel penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act. Failing to repatriate export proceeds within the documented foreign exchange control timeline may also trigger income inclusion and penalty exposure under the ITA. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 ITA can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Retroactive reassessments extend to four years for GST/QST and income tax; the period is extended to seven or ten years, or indefinitely, in the case of neglect, misrepresentation, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility under IMF Article VIII for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No Canadian or Quebec statutory restriction prevents a resident limited partnership from converting CAD into foreign currency or from remitting profits, capital, or distributions abroad. The practical remittance hurdle is fiscal rather than exchange-control: non-resident limited partners receiving Canadian-source partnership income are subject to federal Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% default, reduced under applicable tax treaties) and parallel Quebec withholding obligations administered by Revenu Québec, requiring issuance of Form NR4 and Relevé 19. Banks will treat profit remittance as freely convertible once the LP evidences withholding compliance and provides updated CRS/FATCA self-certifications.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain receipts in any foreign currency in domestic or offshore bank accounts. The only related obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting for cross-border physical currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more, and (ii) normal commercial invoicing and tax valuation standards under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. The mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds is 0%.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator of federally chartered banks; Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) as the Quebec financial-market and money-services-business regulator; FINTRAC as the financial intelligence unit for cross-border transaction and currency reporting. Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange administration body equivalent to SAFE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Cross-border digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are viable for platform payouts, foreign-currency collections, and low-value disbursements, but they are not licensed deposit-taking banks in Canada and cannot serve as the primary escrow or treasury account. Any non-resident EMI providing money-service functions into Canada must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, obtain a licence under the Act respecting money-services businesses administered by the AMF. The LP may therefore use an EMI as a secondary collections conduit, but capital escrow, commingled treasury, and large-value trade settlement must sit with a CDIC-insured domestic Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions flow from non-resident partners to the LP's escrow or operating accounts at BMO or National Bank via SWIFT MT103 for single transfers, with MT202 cover for larger aggregated investor flows. The remitter reference must state 'LP capital contribution - inward direct investment' and the receiving bank must hold a copy of the LP agreement and capital-call notice before value date. CAD funds settle same or next day through LVTS or Lynx RTGS; USD and EUR funds clear via SWIFT into designated currency clearing accounts and are converted to CAD under a same-day spot FX contract when needed. Canada imposes no formal purpose-code registry, but the bank's internal transaction code should reflect an equity contribution to a resident partnership, and the LP must supply source-of-funds evidence for any instruction above CAD 100,000. No investment-declaration requirement under Quebec law constitutes a capital control; the transaction is reportable only for FINTRAC large-value threshold, sanctions screening, and CRS/FATCA consistency.Three clearing rails support trade and supplier settlements. (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for raw silicone resin, packaging, and logistics payables, with the bank instruction noting 'payment for compression-moulded silicone fidget components - NAICS 339930'. (2) Commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600, made payable against presentation of a clean-on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms 2020 (FOB or CIF). (3) Automated platform clearing for the retail channel: Amazon ACH in USD, Shopify and Etsy settlement via Wise/Payoneer converted into CAD, and PayPal Platform Pay sweeps into the Canadian operating account. CAD outflows use LVTS/Lynx; USD outflows use correspondent-bank clearing through CHIPS and Fedwire. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% aggregate federal/Quebec general corporate rate for active business income of a corporate partner (federal 15.0% + Quebec 11.5%). The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a separate income taxpayer; income is allocated to partners and taxed at each partner's applicable federal and provincial rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable to the limited partnership itself, which makes distributions, not dividends. If a Quebec/Canadian corporation later pays dividends to non-resident shareholders from repatriated profits, the federal Part XIII withholding tax is 25%; treaty relief may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on the applicable income tax treaty and the shareholder's ownership percentage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate annual value of covered non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers the contemporaneous documentation and reasonable efforts requirements under Income Tax Act subsection 247(4). CRA also expects OECD-aligned master file and local file documentation for larger cross-border operations; country-by-country reporting and CBCR notification obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of approximately C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate administrative 'Economic Substance Regulations'; instead, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 ITA), the sham doctrine, and substance-over-form interpretation impose an economic substance requirement for transactions to be respected. A Quebec limited partnership must have real commercial substance: Canadian employees, physical production, management decisions, bank accounts, and economic risk. A fiscally transparent partnership with a non-resident partner in a no/low-tax jurisdiction and a purported foreign sole decision-maker may be disregarded for tax purposes or treated as creating a Canadian permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Non-Filing / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of misreported federal tax under Income Tax Act section 163, plus accrued interest and late-filing penalties; on criminal indictment under section 239, fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Revenu Québec may impose parallel provincial penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate general partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/QST under section 323 and section 227.1; the general partner's personal assets are exposed. A limited partner who participates in control of the partnership loses the limited-liability shield, potentially reaching the partner's personal assets; CRA may obtain civil court judgments, asset freezes, and travel restrictions for significant tax debts.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess profits to the arm's-length price and impose a gross-negligence transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where documentation and reasonable efforts standards are not satisfied; daily information-return penalties and interest accrue; Quebec may also adjust provincial allocation if federal profit adjustment is made.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal liability for knowingly acquiescing in false transfer-pricing entries or fraudulent GST/HST benefits; general partners are exposed to contractual and statutory claims for the partnership's tax liability; severe non-cooperation may trigger stays under CRA enforcement and departure prevention orders.
Artificial Tax Avoidance / GAAR / Shell Partnership

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, CRA will deny or recharacterize the tax benefit, reassess within extended limitation periods, charge interest, and apply the 10% transfer-pricing penalty if the scheme also involves non-arm's-length cross-border pricing; Revenu Québec applies its GAAR equivalent

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the limited partnership is a facade or sham; directors and controlling shareholders may be personally assessed for tax liabilities under Quebec civil law, including restitutionary orders and disgorgement of distributed partnership income; judicial sanctions may include orders prohibiting foreign travel.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Repatriated Amounts

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the unwithheld Part XIII tax and all interest; section 227 penalties and non-resident withholding garnishments can be imposed; the partnership may lose deductibility of the underlying expense if withholding and reporting obligations are not satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unwithheld Part XIII amounts if they knew or should have known of the payment; Quebec courts may order civil restitution and asset freezes; travel bans are available under CRA enforcement where the personal tax debt is material.
AML / FINTRAC and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: An unregistered money services business is subject to FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 and possible criminal fines; false information in partner registers attracts corporate and penal consequences under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined for failure to report suspicious transactions of significant value; Quebec limited partners who knowingly engage in laundering proceeds from unlawful conduct may lose limited liability and face criminal charges, civil forfeiture, and imprisonment.
