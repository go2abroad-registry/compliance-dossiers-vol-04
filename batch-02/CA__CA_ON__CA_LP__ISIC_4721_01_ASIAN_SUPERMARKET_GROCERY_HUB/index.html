Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority for GST/HST and Canadian income tax administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Business Registry — provincial tax and business registration. Portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership, the operative statutory tax ID for GST/HST is the CRA Business Number (BN). Apply online through CRA My Business Account or by Form RC1 (Request for Business Number); CRA may require the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration to be completed first through the Ontario Business Registry. The application must identify the partnership, the general partner, and the limited partners. CRA will issue a 9-digit base BN plus a program-account suffix such as 123456789 RT0001, which must be displayed on invoices, included in HST returns, and stated on customs/export documentation. The CRA BN functions as the federal TIN and GST/HST registration number for the LP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent by statutory design and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation for Canadian income tax purposes. Available elections include partnership rollover elections under the Income Tax Act (e.g., ITA s.97(2) for property contributed to a partnership and s.98(3) for transfers of partnership property to a corporation). Hybrid mismatch rules operate automatically by statute and are not elective; no domestic opt-out is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 CAD. A person is a small supplier until worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. A physical retail grocery location in Ontario also creates continuing Canadian nexus; even below the threshold, the LP may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V. The registrant must retain evidence that the goods were physically removed from Canada, normally by (a) the supplier exporting the goods itself, (b) the supplier shipping via a common carrier or freight forwarder for export, or (c) delivering the goods to a non-resident purchaser who exports them within a commercially reasonable time. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated as offshore services unless they relate to Canadian real property, tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of supply, or services to an individual present in Canada. Export zero-rating allows full input tax credits for HST paid on purchases supporting the export. Basic groceries sold domestically may also be zero-rated, but export zero-rating specifically requires documented physical export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Form B13A or equivalent electronic custom-terms data/CUS-IFP for commercial exports)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or truck) showing movement from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or foreign-exchange record evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale showing the non-resident consignee and Incoterms
  • Packing list and carrier/freight-forwarder certificate of departure from Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction control e-invoicing regime applies in Canada for grocery retail. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts showing the supplier's BN and the HST charged. Electronic documents are acceptable; voluntary B2B e-invoicing may use the Pan-Canadian Peppol network. POS receipts issued through Interac or credit-card terminals satisfy the point-of-sale invoice obligation, but detailed invoices must be available to CRA on request.

CRA treats export zero-rating as a high-risk review area. If the required export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent, CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess HST at 13% in Ontario, disallow or claw back related input tax credits, charge arrears interest, and impose civil false-statement or gross-negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act. Criminal prosecution is possible for intentional evasion. The ordinary CRA assessment period is four years, but it is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, wilful default, or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no general exchange controls under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Bank Act. Capital injections by non-resident limited partners, foreign-currency account maintenance, and repatriation of partnership distributions require no Bank of Canada or OSFI prior approval. Profit and capital remittances to non-resident partners are nevertheless subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at the statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced to 15% or less under an applicable tax treaty (for example, the Canada-United States Convention), and to comprehensive OFSI-style sanctions screening. The proposed grocery-retail limited partnership will not trigger Investment Canada Act review given the WTO enterprise-value thresholds are unlikely to be exceeded.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada maintains no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender mechanism for export proceeds. As a net importer of Asian condiments and vegetables, the LP is under no obligation to convert foreign-currency receipts into CAD. The entity may lawfully hold USD, CNY/CNH, JPY, HKD, EUR, and other foreign-currency operating accounts at Schedule I banks and may use intercompany or receivable proceeds to settle offshore supplier invoices without forced conversion. The only applicable statutory reporting duty is FINTRAC's large electronic fund transfer reporting for any wire or electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more in a single 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction reporting and AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada and enforcement by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Ancillary feasibility only. Wise Payments Canada Inc., Airwallex (Canada) Limited, and similar entities are FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs), not Schedule I or Schedule II banks. They cannot provide Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) insured operating accounts, Interac Direct Payment merchant clearing, credit-card acquiring settlement, or commercial documentary-credit issuance. Legally, operating a deposit-taking business without a Bank Act authorization is prohibited; accordingly, an Ontario-domiciled EMI/MSB relationship is viable solely as a supplementary disbursement rail for low-value supplier payments and efficient FX conversion (e.g., paying small Chinese or Southeast Asian vendors). The primary treasury architecture must be anchored at a chartered Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited-partner capital contributions must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's home-country bank directly into the LP's CAD Capital Escrow Account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire instruction must carry the LP's full registered legal name, the account number, the receiver bank's SWIFT/BIC, and the remittance narrative: 'CA_LP - CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [partner legal name]'. No exchange-control approval is required for CAD, USD, CNY/CNH, JPY, HKD, or EUR. Where the contribution is denominated in a foreign currency, the Canadian bank will convert at a spot rate quoted under its standard FX terms, and the economic terms must be documented in a confirmation ticket. Each electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is reported by the bank to FINTRAC, and the originator must provide source-of-funds evidence on request. Escrow release conditions should include receipt of the wire, issuance of a partnership capital-account credit memorandum, updating of the LP's capital ledger, and a legal opinion confirming valid Ontario registration and the general partner's authority to open the account and accept capital.Supplier payments for imported Asian condiments and vegetables clear through four primary rails: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 direct high-value wire settlements to Asian beneficiary banks (e.g., HSBC Hong Kong, DBS Singapore, Standard Chartered Shanghai); (b) commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700 to the supplier's confirming bank, with settlement on presentation of compliant transport, invoice, and inspection documents (MT754/MT756 followed by MT202); (c) documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) for established suppliers to reduce letter-of-credit costs; and (d) cross-border CAD/USD ACH using the Canadian account's US routing-number service for high-frequency, low-value payments to US-based wholesalers. For Chinese suppliers, the bank may route through cross-border RMB (CNH) clearing via CIPS or an offshore CNH account, subject to applicable Canadian economic sanctions, Global Affairs Canada export/import permits, and CBSA customs documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 gross revenue threshold for the taxpayer and all non-resident related parties

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to transactions lacking economic substance. The partnership must have genuine business purpose and commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they participated in or acquiesced to the evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of tax benefits, and penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham; possible injunctions and dissolution orders.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities; criminal penalties including imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they failed to establish adequate compliance programs; travel bans and asset seizures may occur.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of the understated tax, and interest on unpaid amounts; potential double taxation

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing; travel bans and criminal prosecution for tax evasion may apply.
