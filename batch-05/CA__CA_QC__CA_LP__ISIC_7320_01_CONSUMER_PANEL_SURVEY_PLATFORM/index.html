Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital or statutory escrow requirement applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Limited partners must, however, contribute the capital agreed in the partnership agreement to enjoy limited liability under Civil Code of Québec art. 2240; contributions may be held in escrow only if the partnership agreement imposes that condition.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain from the building owner the fire safety attestation and ensure compliance with the Quebec Fire Safety Code (CQLR c B-1.1, r. 8), including maintained extinguishers, unobstructed exits, emergency lighting, and an evacuation plan for the office floor. Structural load inspection: rely on the landlord's building occupancy certificate; the office lease must confirm that ordinary office loading is structurally permitted. Occupational clearance: no CNESST-specific clearance certificate is required for a low-risk office environment, but the employer must register with CNESST if employees are hired, implement a basic health and safety orientation, provide ergonomic equipment, and keep a first-aid kit in accordance with CNESST standards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA, Agence du revenu du Canada) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec via Mon dossier d'entreprise / CLIC portal; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for the compulsory Quebec enterprise number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint the Canadian general partner and file the limited partnership declaration with REQ under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises to obtain the NEQ. 2) Register the partnership for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the GST/HST program account (RT) and payroll account (RC) if applicable; the BN is issued to the partnership as a separate person for GST/HST purposes. 3) Register with Revenu Québec for the QST using the NEQ, obtaining a 10-digit QST registration number (TQ account); use the single-window registration or Form LM-1-V. 4) File the federal partnership information return (T5013) and the Quebec partnership return (TP-600) with RL-15 slips when a corporate or non-resident partner exists or partnership gross revenue exceeds CAD 2,000,000. 5) Enroll in CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CLIC for electronic filing, assessments, and audit correspondence. Typical turnaround is 24–72 hours for BN and QST issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax under ITA section 96, with each partner reporting its allocable share directly; no entity-level income tax. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is a separate person under ETA section 177(1) and is the registrant/collector on taxable supplies. Territorial scope: income allocated to Canadian-resident partners is fully subject to Canadian and Quebec tax; income allocated to non-resident partners from active Canadian-source service business is taxed through a non-resident Part I filing (T1159) and is not subject to Part XIII withholding, unless the allocation represents passive payments such as management fees. No tax-exempt zone applies. Quebec may impose the health services fund (FSS) payroll tax if the partnership employs Quebec personnel.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a federal check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Relevant elections for a Quebec limited partnership: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA section 240 and the corresponding QST registration; (b) election under ETA section 156 to deem supplies between closely related registrants to be for nil consideration, mirrored in QSTA provisions for Quebec; (c) partnership registration and deemed-person rules under ETA section 177 (with QSTA equivalent) permitting the partnership itself to register and file rather than the general partner; (d) income tax rollover elections under ITA section 97(2) for transfer of property to the partnership on a tax-deferred basis; (e) hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 18.2–18.6 may disallow deductions on payments where the counterparty is a hybrid entity; a US member may file Form 8832 for US check-the-box purposes, but that election has no direct Canadian tax consequence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148(1); identical CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST under the Quebec Sales Tax Act; tested on taxable supplies made in a single calendar quarter and in the preceding four calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of market research, polling, and data analytics services to non-residents qualify for 0% GST/HST under Part V, Schedule VI, ETA, section 7, and for 0% QST under Schedule III, QSTA, section 30, provided the service is not supplied to an individual present in Canada and does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or intangible personal property exploited principally in Canada. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and of offshore service delivery/consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) plus Bill of Lading or air waybill, required only where tangible survey materials or incentive goods are shipped from Canada; pure service exports to non-residents require no customs export declaration.
  • Export commercial invoice and master services agreement evidencing supply to a non-resident counterparty, supported by platform IP-location logs and user-metadata records demonstrating consumption outside Canada, together with a non-resident status file (W-8BEN-E for US clients, foreign certificate of incorporation, or tax residency certificate).
  • Inward remittance confirmation, SWIFT/Wise transaction record, or PayPal Mass Payout settlement ledger proving receipt of funds from the foreign payer within the 180-day settlement covenant, plus partnership books and T5013/RL-15 allocation schedules substantiating the zero-rated revenue.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API government stamping, or QR-code fiscal invoice regime exists in Canada. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing standard is voluntary; CRA and Revenu Québec accept EDI and XML e-invoices. Suppliers must retain issue records and electronically file information returns (T4A and RL-1/RL-15) when applicable to panelist incentive payments exceeding regulatory slip thresholds; zero-rated export invoices must be coded as such with supporting documentation retained.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment window under ETA section 298 (extended to eight years for misrepresentation or non-resident-related cases; QSTA section 991 mirrors). If contemporaneous export documentation is inadequate, the full 5% GST and 9.975% QST are retrospectively clawed back to the supply date with associated input tax credit disallowances, prescribed quarterly interest, and penalties: ETA section 280 false statement penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the related tax; ETA section 285 gross negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be avoided; QSTA equivalent penalties under sections 991–993; and Criminal Code section 380 fraud liability (imprisonment up to 14 years) where the zero-rating claim is made with intent to deceive.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no general exchange controls, no surrender requirements for export proceeds, and no restrictions on profit remittance for non-resident partners of a Quebec Limited Partnership. Distribution payments to foreign partners are freely transferable abroad, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 15% unless reduced by tax treaty) and to reporting of cross-border currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000 under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law does not mandate conversion, surrender, or repatriation of export/foreign-currency proceeds. However, survey platform payments received from non-resident clients that are booked as export revenue may be subject to mandatory reporting under the Statistics Act for Balance of Payments compilation, and any cross-border movement of physical currency, bearer instruments, or monetary instruments over CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is exercised under the Currency Act and the Minister of Finance mandate, with operational compliance oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Wise (formerly TransferWise) Canada and PayPal Canada are registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC, not banks or Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) in the Canadian legal framework. They may be used as complementary payment rails for cross-border consumer panel incentives, mass payouts, and low-value disbursements, but they cannot serve as the sole operating bank for the limited partnership. Legal constraints: no deposit-taking authority; customer funds must be held in segregated trust accounts at a Canadian FDIC-insured bank; unregistered offshore EMIs are prohibited from soliciting Canadian clients and are subject to enforcement by FINTRAC and provincial securities regulators.

They may be used as complementary payment rails for cross-border consumer panel incentives, mass payouts, and low-value disbursements, but they cannot serve as the sole operating bank for the limited partnership. Legal constraints: no deposit-taking authority; customer funds must be held in segregated trust accounts at a Canadian FDIC-insured bank; unregistered offshore EMIs are prohibited from soliciting Canadian clients and are subject to enforcement by FINTRAC and provincial securities regulators.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Fiscally transparent regime: the Quebec Limited Partnership (LP) is not a taxpayer under ITA s. 96 and s. 750 of the Quebec Taxation Act (QTA); income is allocated to partners and taxed at partner level. For comparative context, a Quebec-resident corporate partner would face a combined statutory general corporate income tax rate of 26.5% (15% federal Part I general corporate rate + 11.5% Quebec corporate rate), while an individual Quebec-resident partner faces a combined top marginal rate of approximately 53.31%. A non-resident corporate partner earning Canadian-source business income through the LP is taxed at the 15% federal Part I rate plus 25% Part XIV branch tax Absent treaty relief, for an aggregate non-treaty effective burden of 36.25%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII rate of 25% applies to 'dividends' paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident recipients (ITA s. 212(2)). Critical: a Quebec LP is fiscally transparent; distributions of profit to partners are NOT dividends and are NOT subject to Part XIII withholding. Instead, the non-resident partner is taxable directly under Part I on its allocated Canadian-source business income; a non-resident corporate partner is further subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% on after-tax income (reducible under a tax treaty). Quebec does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax on partnership distributions to partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ITA s. 237.1: Form T106 must be filed for any non-arm's length cross-border transaction if the aggregate value in a transaction category with one non-resident exceeds C$1,000,000 per fiscal year (single category threshold aggregated across all transactions with that person). CRA requires contemporaneous 'complete and accurate documentation' for those transactions; documentation must be prepared in English or French and delivered within 3 months of a written demand from CRA. Additionally, for the LP's multinational group (including a foreign parent or sister entities), Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory only when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds the annual CbCR threshold (currently approximately C$757 million); a master file and local file are not statutorily separate filings in Canada, but CRA audits treat the T106, organizational charts, and TP policy as the effective master/local file substitute.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applied in MENA jurisdictions do not operate in Canada. Instead, Canada applies: (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule of ITA s. 245 (mirrored by Quebec under ss. 1079.6 – 1079.9 QTA), which permits CRA to disregard any avoidance transaction lacking a bona fide commercial purpose and genuine economic substance; (2) the OECD/G20 BEPS aggressive tax planning disclosures and Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test, which can deny treaty benefits to partnership structures created primarily to reduce withholding tax; and (3) the Quebec civil law doctrine under Book 8 of the Civil Code of Quebec (arts. 2236–2245) requiring that a limited partnership possess a real commercial business and that limited partners refrain from taking part in management. The LP must maintain in Quebec its own office, employees, customer contracts, payment infrastructure accounts (including PayPal/Wise settlement accounts), board decision-logic, and accounting records; otherwise CRA may disregard the partnership and allocate its income directly to the non-resident partners, invalidating the intended pass-through treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations (Federal ITA s. 239(1), Quebec QTA s. 1763)

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross-negligence penalty (ITA s. 163(2)) on the understated tax, plus daily compound interest and a separate 100% penalty for wilful evasion under s. 239(1) (civil increase of the tax sought to be evaded); criminal: on summary conviction a fine of 50%–100% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; on indictment a fine of 100%–200% and imprisonment for up to 5 years. In Quebec, a false or deceptive return is punishable under s. 1763 QTA by fines up to double the tax sought to be avoided and imprisonment up to 24 months.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner who knowingly participated in the scheme are personally subject to criminal prosecution; the Criminal Code (other than the ITA) may additionally impose penalties for fraud to the use of an electronic platform with up to 14 years of imprisonment; civil fraud suits by minority partners or creditors can pierce the veil where the LP is a mere alter ego.
Failure to Withhold Payroll, Part XIII, and Non-Resident Allocations

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(8): a 10% penalty on every amount that was required to be withheld but was not remitted, plus daily interest; failure to remit is a due-and-payable debt of the LP; criminal sanction under s. 227(9) of imprisonment of up to 2 years and fines where the failure was knowingly made; Revenu Québec applies the same scheme with additional penalties for late remittance of source deductions (s. 549 QTA).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally, jointly, and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 and QTA s. 1013.1 for the entire unremitted withholding, regardless of whether the LP is insolvent and even after the LP has been wound up; no insurance indemnity covers the director for such a debt; CRA routinely obtains writs of seizure and can initiate bankruptcy against the corporate general partner, forcing personal settlement by directors.
Unregistered Money Service Business / AML Violations (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to C$1,000,000 for an individual and C$2,000,000 for a corporation for serious MSB violations; non-registration itself carries AMPs of up to C$250,000 (individual) and C$500,000 (entity) depending on the table; criminal violations for laundering proceeds or terror financing on conviction are punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and unlimited fines; FINTRAC may also enter a compliance agreement or freeze the platform's settlement accounts during audit.

Director personal liability: S. 73.1 PCMLTFA: directors, officers, and partners who 'authorize, permit or acquiesce in' an MSB non-registration or AML failure are personally liable for the AMP; additionally, FINTRAC can seek injunctions, and criminal charges can result in travel bans/conditions attached to the sentence, preventing cross-border movement of directors during prosecution.
