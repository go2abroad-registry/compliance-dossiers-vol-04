Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni ATC); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; online VAT services and CH-LOGIN are available through the ESTV eService portal. VAT is exclusively federal and is administered by ESTV; corporate income tax is administered federally and by cantonal/communal tax authorities according to the AG's place of effective management and registered seat.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the competent cantonal register office. Step 2: Upon registration, the AG automatically receives a permanent Swiss company identifier (UID - Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / IDE / IDI) in the format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx, which serves as the general Swiss tax identifier. Step 3: For VAT, submit the registration form to ESTV through the VAT eService portal; ESTV activates the UID with the suffix 'MWST' and issues the final Swiss VAT number in the format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx MWST. Step 4: A foreign non-resident entity without a Swiss seat or Swiss permanent establishment that makes taxable supplies in Switzerland must appoint a Swiss fiscal/tax representative before registering; the representative files the VAT registration with ESTV, receives the Swiss VAT ID, and assumes joint liability for VAT. Registration is mandatory once Swiss taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold and voluntary below it; the registration application should be filed before the taxable activity is continued without VAT compliance. No separate TIN is issued beyond the UID; all tax communications, invoices and customs declarations reference the UID/VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election, pass-through election, or elective hybrid classification for an AG. An Aktiengesellschaft is legally and fiscally classified as a resident corporation subject to Swiss corporate income tax at entity level on its worldwide net profits, with double-tax relief and participation relief where applicable. Available elections and administrative mechanisms include: (i) voluntary VAT registration when turnover is below CHF 100,000; (ii) VAT group taxation (Organschaft) for controlled Swiss entities; (iii) the Swiss SME flat-rate VAT method (Saldosteuersatz / taxe forfaitaire) for qualifying businesses; (iv) Swiss tax ruling applications to confirm participation relief, financing structures or export/VAT treatment; and (v) foreign tax credit/relief claims under Swiss double tax treaties. There is no territorial tax election and no tax-exempt-zone regime for an ordinary commercial drone retail AG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 net VAT-exclusive turnover from taxable supplies in Switzerland per business year. Once this threshold is exceeded, VAT registration is mandatory; below it, voluntary registration is available. For foreign-established businesses, the threshold applies with respect to taxable supplies made in Switzerland, and registration requires a Swiss fiscal representative where the foreign supplier has no Swiss seat or permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: The sale of FPV racing drones, carbon-fiber frames, brushless motors, spare parts and related drone hardware qualifies for Swiss export exemption (0% VAT with input VAT deduction) if the goods are physically removed from the Swiss customs territory and exported to a foreign customer, either directly by the Swiss AG or through a forwarding agent/carrier commissioned by the supplier. The supply to the export vehicle or the final foreign recipient must be supported by proof that the goods left Switzerland. Services: B2B services for foreign-established customers (engineering, firmware/software, assembly support, repair services) are normally regarded as supplied outside Switzerland under the Swiss place-of-supply rules and therefore outside Swiss VAT, with input VAT deduction for the Swiss AG; B2C services are outside Swiss VAT only where the applicable place-of-supply rules so provide. The Swiss AG must document the customer's foreign status, the contract, the delivery/performance chain, and the export event. Input VAT attributable to exports is recoverable through the periodic Swiss VAT return, and resulting input VAT surplus is refunded by ESTV.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Swiss customs export declaration, NCTS/e-dec customs transit record, or official electronic export summary evidencing exit of the FPV drones from Swiss customs territory
  • Transport Document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or forwarder's delivery order, signed by the carrier/forwarder and confirming delivery to the foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Payment Proof: bank statement, SWIFT MT103, PayPal or Stripe settlement report, or inward remittance certificate showing receipt of export proceeds in CHF or foreign currency from the foreign buyer

Switzerland does not currently operate a mandatory real-time tax-clearance system, government fiscal API pre-approval, continuous transaction reporting, or digital stamping of invoices. B2B and B2C invoices may be issued on paper or electronically if the recipient can access them and the statutory invoice content is complete. Swiss VAT law requires the invoice to identify the supplier and recipient, describe the supply, state the date, the consideration, the applicable VAT rate and the VAT amount, and show the supplier's Swiss UID/VAT number. Structured electronic invoices such as Swiss eBill, EDI and XML invoices are accepted on a bilateral basis; the Swiss QR-bill is used for payment initiation but is not a fiscal clearance instrument. Taxpayers must preserve all invoices, books and records in machine-readable/auditable form for ESTV inspection; no central government e-invoicing platform is mandatory for commercial retail supplies.

ESTV may audit export zero-rating claims, payment-rail settlement reports, customs declarations and delivery records. If export substantiation is missing, incomplete or inconsistent, the export exemption is reversed and the Swiss AG is assessed for output VAT at 8.1% on the sales consideration, together with default interest. Simple VAT evasion is punishable by a fine up to the amount of the tax evaded; aggravated intentional VAT fraud involving forged export documents, phantom deliveries or circular cross-border transactions is punishable by fines up to four times the tax evaded and criminal tax proceedings. The Swiss AG's directors and, for non-established sellers, the Swiss fiscal representative may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT and penalties. Records and export proofs must be retained for at least 10 years; in cases of fraud, ESTV may retrospectively reopen assessments and claw back the VAT advantage beyond the ordinary limitation period, plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland provides full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital account under the federal free-movement-of-capital principle and OECD liberalisation commitments. No prior government approval is required for profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan servicing, or capital repatriation by a Swiss AG. The only practical friction is the domestic 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend and interest payments, which is reducible or eliminated at source under applicable double taxation treaties (0% to 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders, 0% for treaty-eligible interest) upon submission of a tax-domicile certificate (préavis de domicile / Veranlagungsbestätigung). Conversion between CHF, USD, EUR, and offshore CNH occurs at freely market-determined rates; SNB policy interventions, if any, do not constitute convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for export receipts generated by ISIC 3240 FPV racing drone sales. A Swiss exporter may retain USD, EUR, CNY/CNH, or other foreign-currency balances indefinitely and select its own conversion schedule. The sole compliance duty is statistical reporting under the SNB balance-of-payments reporting ordinance: direct reporting is required for enterprises whose annual cross-border payments or foreign assets/liabilities exceed approximately CHF 10 million (as periodically revised), and individual export transactions may be sampled below that threshold. Failure to report is an administrative offence under the Swiss National Bank Act but does not restrict the exporter's free use of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank; French: Banque nationale suisse), Berne and Zurich, is the central monetary and foreign-exchange authority of Switzerland. No separate foreign-exchange control administration exists; the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) regulates banks and financial institutions, and the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF) coordinates international financial-market policy.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal corporate income tax on after-tax net profit (effective 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal plus cantonal and municipal rates range from approx. 11.9% (Obwalden) and 14.6% (Zug) to approx. 21.1% (Zurich City), depending on tax jurisdiction of the registered seat.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on all dividends and profit distributions paid by the AG, regardless of beneficiary residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed within six months after the end of the business year (art. 125/126 DBG and cantonal practice); calendar-year AGs generally receive a filing deadline of 30 June of the following year; extensions of up to six months are available on justified written application; tax returns filed late accrue interest from the original due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Swiss Master File and Local File documentation is required where the multinational group reaches consolidated gross revenue of CHF 50 million; simplified contemporaneous documentation applies to intra-group transactions of CHF 10 million or more; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more with mandatory filing via the ESTV.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate an OECD-style ESR register, but economic substance is enforced through (i) the global minimum tax (Pillar Two) effective 1 January 2024, applying a 15% top-up tax to groups with annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more; (ii) the BEPS Multilateral Instrument and treaty-benefit anti-abuse clauses requiring real business activity for DTA relief; and (iii) settled case law requiring permanent establishment, qualified staffing, premises and actual decision-taking in Switzerland for participation relief and WHT refunds; an AG holding only passive rights or using nominee directors without local operations risks denial of treaty benefits, administrative assistance to the foreign tax administration and reclassification of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the evaded federal tax plus a fine of 25% to 50% of the evaded amount; cantonal fines up to 200% of the evaded tax; the assessment may be reopened for ten years (objective Nachsteuer); criminal interest accrues at 5% p.a.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or authorised the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax with their personal assets under art. 759 CO; criminal convictions trigger personal fines and exclusion from board positions; pre-trial travel bans and asset freezes are routinely ordered.
Qualified tax fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years (art. 186 DBG) in addition to restitution of the evaded tax and accrued interest; the criminal court may order forfeiture of the proceeds of crime; administrative assessments are reopened without time limit in connection with the criminal act.

Director personal liability: The acting director or CFO is prosecuted personally; the company is jointly liable for fines under art. 177 DBG for offences committed in the course of business; travel bans, confiscation orders and disqualification from serving as directors apply to the natural persons involved.
Dividend stripping / withholding tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Unpaid withholding tax is collected from the AG together with interest at 5% p.a.; a penalty of up to 100% of the undeclared WHT is imposed; deliberate concealment may double the administrative fine; the claim provokes liquidation and bankruptcy proceedings if unpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the withholding tax if the distribution is made after they know or should have known that the tax was not declared; criminal liability for the responsible officer carries up to three years imprisonment; the board is disqualified from executing further distributions.
Transfer pricing manipulation / base erosion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and adjustment of taxable profit under art. 58 DBG as a constructive dividend, triggering the 35% withholding tax unless a DTA limits the rate; interest and penalties on the adjustment; Pillar Two top-up tax at 15% applies to in-scope groups; a concealed adjustment may be prosecuted as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Board members approving or signing defective transfer pricing documentation face personal liability for the adjusted tax under art. 759 CO; falsification of financial statements (art. 163 CO) carries imprisonment of up to three years; travel bans and disgorgement against the directors personally follow the criminal judgement.
AML shell-company abuse and money laundering

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under art. 260bis StGB is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and a fine; aggravated cases impose longer sentences; FINMA may impose administrative enforcement measures, license withdrawals and publish sanction decisions; failure to report suspicious transactions to the Money Laundering Reporting Office (MROS) triggers administrative fines and automatic dissolution procedures; Swiss/EU sanction violations run in parallel.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer are prosecuted as natural persons under the CDB 20 rules with personal fines up to CHF 500,000; nominee directors are individually incriminated under art. 260bis StGB independent of the company; the court may order travel bans, asset freezes, personal restitution orders and a de facto insolvency of the entity, with the board disqualified from managing any company for up to five years.
