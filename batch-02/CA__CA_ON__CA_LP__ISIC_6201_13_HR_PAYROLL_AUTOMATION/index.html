Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax and GST/HST administration; CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance — provincial tax and Ontario-specific obligations (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance). For Ontario HST, CRA administers the federal GST and the Ontario provincial HST component together.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', or by applying online through CRA's Registration portal / My Business Account. The partnership must register for GST/HST (RT account) if it is not a small supplier or if it carries on business in Canada and supplies taxable supplies. The LP must also register for a CRA payroll account (RP) if it employs Canadian workers, and an import/export account (RM) if engaged in commercial cross-border activities. Ontario registration may be required separately for Ontario Employer Health Tax if the LP has Ontario payrolls. Non-resident general and limited partners file Canadian income tax returns as applicable; the partnership itself files an information return (T5013) to report allocation of income and loss to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Income Tax Act purposes: it is not a taxable entity in its own right; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners according to the partnership agreement. However, the LP is a separate registrant for GST/HST in respect of its commercial activities, and non-resident partners may have Canadian tax filing and Part XIII or Part I withholding obligations. Ontario does not impose a separate corporate income tax on the LP itself; Ontario tax attributes flow through to partners.

Available election forms

None (Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' partnership classification election; partnership flow-through treatment is non-elective under the Income Tax Act). Available elections impacting cross-border LPs include: (i) Income Tax Act s.216(1) election permitting a non-resident partner with Canadian rental income to compute Part I tax on a net basis; (ii) treaty-based election to reduce non-resident withholding tax; (iii) GST/HST small supplier voluntary registration election to charge and claim input tax credits; and (iv) administrative elections dealing with fiscal year-end alignment for flow-through reporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated taxable supplies) in either the preceding four calendar quarters or the current quarter; if the LP exceeds this 'small supplier' threshold, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Registration may also be required on a lower or nil threshold where the non-resident LP is carrying on business in Canada or specifically makes taxable supplies of real property in Canada, and voluntary registration is available when not required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is available for (i) goods exported from Canada where evidence of export is produced and the goods are supplied to a non-resident, and (ii) certain services supplied to non-residents, including services performed wholly outside Canada and services, such as payroll-SaaS digital platform services, supplied to a non-resident where the supply does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. To claim zero-rating, the supplier must have documentation sufficient to prove the supply qualifies; otherwise the supply is taxable at 13% HST in Ontario.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Export Declaration / Canada Border Services Agency export filing);
  • Bill of Lading (through ocean or air waybill, or highway carrier manifest where applicable);
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank remittance / payment receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident; not a statutory certificate in Canada but accepted as supporting evidence of payment)

Canada does not currently mandate a continuous-transaction-control (CTC) or real-time fiscal clearance regime with government pre-approval of every invoice. No digital stamping or mandatory electronic invoice clearance API exists. GST/HST registrants must, however, issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name, BN, date, description, tax rate, tax amount), retain valid supporting export documentation, and file GST/HST returns electronically when above CRA thresholds. Large and intermediate registrants generally transmit returns via CRA's secure online services, including API/XML-enabled filing for high-volume taxpayers, and electronic payment is required for prescribed large-dollar remittances.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims closely, particularly for intangible and digital services. If the supporting documentation is inadequate or the export condition is not met, GST/HST assessed at 13% may be clawed back retroactively. Penalties include: interest on arrears under s.280; failure to collect/remit penalties under s.280.1; false statements or omissions penalty under s.285 (not less than $100 and up to 25% of the amount of tax sought to be avoided); gross-negligence penalty under s.285.1 (up to 50% of the understated tax); and criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and potential imprisonment. Extended reassessment periods apply where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is a fully convertible currency jurisdiction for both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign exchange controls restricting the conversion of CAD to foreign currency, and no prior central bank approval is required for inbound capital contributions, profit repatriation, or cross-border dividend/partnership distributions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax compliance, notably Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (e.g., on dividends, interest, rent or royalties) and potential withholding under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act (Canada) for payments to a non-resident partner. Capital repatriation by non-resident partners is generally unfettered, but must satisfy bank source-of-funds documentation and any constraints arising under the Investment Canada Act for entities exercising control of a Canadian business. In short, capital and current account transactions are freely exchangeable without mandated regulatory approval, but international transfers crossing C$10,000 in a 24-hour period trigger FINTRAC reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Canada. An exporter is not required to convert foreign exchange receipts into Canadian dollars, nor is there a prescribed timeline for bringing export proceeds into Canada. For goods exports, CBSA export declarations under the Customs Act serve statistical and compliance purposes; for services exports, Statistics Canada's quarterly survey or corporate reporting may apply where thresholds are met. The Bank Act and OSFI guidance do not mandate any minimum conversion ratio for export revenue. Accordingly, the Ontario limited partnership may retain foreign currency proceeds in a Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank account and may choose the timing and method of remittance without exposing itself to exchange-control penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banc du Canada); in the context of transactional reporting and anti-money-laundering compliance, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Canadian law does not recognise foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) as deposit-taking institutions, and an offshore EMI (e.g., a UK or EU e-money licence holder) cannot provide a CDIC-insured Canadian-dollar account or clear payments through the Canadian Payments Association (CPA). Effectively, an offshore EMI acts only as a payment intermediary, not as a banking account. For the Ontario limited partnership, this is acceptable only for low-value disbursement collections, not for holding operational or capital funds. If the platform stores client funds, the LP must treat those obligations carefully under provincial (Ontario) trust accounting rules, and the provider should be registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) if it engages in cross-border remittance for foreign clients. A stronger legal approach is to use a Schedule I bank for treasury and to whitelabel a licensed Canadian MSB for payroll collection/disbursement, thereby avoiding unlicensed deposit-taking in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for capital contributions should use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary being the CA_ON limited partnership's CAD account at a Schedule I bank. The payment narrative (Field 70) should clearly state: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP; ISIC 6201; OPERATIONS AND PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT; NON-RESIDENT INVESTMENT'. The receiving bank will require a copy of the partnership registration, the executed capital call or subscription agreement, identification of the remitter/beneficial owner, and a source-of-funds declaration. For CAD settlements over C$25,000, use LVTS; for amounts below that, standard ACSS wire works. The bank may also apply a 'purpose code' for the Foreign Investment Statistics questionnaire — the correct classification is generally 'FDI – investments in Canadian partnership' (Query code QTD) and not a goods-trade code. Funds should be received in a dedicated capital-escrow account and later transferred to the operational account using an internal journal entry; this preserves the capital-trail for the LP's T5013 allocation and avoids anti-money-laundering false positives. If any wire equals or exceeds C$10,000, the financial institution must issue an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) to FINTRAC; such reporting is automatic on the bank side and requires no client mandate.Outbound treasury disbursements for SaaS operations (licensing fees, hosting costs, subcontracting, international payroll disbursement) should be executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires, with the LP's counterparty reference in Field 71. For domestic payroll in Canada, use the CPA's ACSS network (direct deposit) for CAD payroll; for cross-border payroll to non-Canadian employees, route through a global payroll ERP connector to the same Schedule I bank's global ACH or correspondent network. If the LP purchases on-premise hardware or enters into a licence resale arrangement under which a commercial letter of credit is required, use a documentary letter of credit issued under UCP 600 by the Canadian bank, with required documents being invoice, transport document (if any), and acceptance certificate. Each outbound transaction must be matched to an underlying invoice, contract, or payroll run; the bank's sanctions screening will inspect the counterparty bank, country, and contractual documents. A stronger legal approach is to use a Schedule I bank for treasury and to whitelabel a licensed Canadian MSB for payroll collection/disbursement, thereby avoiding unlicensed deposit-taking in Canada.
