Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax (Part I of the Income Tax Act) and GST/HST; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec corporate income tax; Registraire des entreprises du Québec for the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Federal registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; Quebec portal: revenuquebec.ca (SNAP electronic filing).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the federal/Quebec incorporation number plus, for Quebec, the NEQ. Step 2: Register with CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO, Form RC1, or by mail/fax; non-resident corporations use Form RC1A and must name a Canadian representative for service. Step 3: Electronically open program accounts under the BN: RM (federal corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RD (direct deposit). Step 4: Register with Revenu Québec via the combined LM-1 form or SNAP to obtain the QST account, the Quebec corporate income tax account, and the employer contributions account. Online BN issuance is immediate; Quebec accounts are normally issued within 5 to 10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer: a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed under Part I on worldwide income, and a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source income with an additional Part XIV branch tax. No passthrough or S-corporation analogue exists in Canada. Territorial scope: worldwide for resident corporations; no general tax-exempt or free-zone regime applies in Quebec to resin statue manufacturing and export sales.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections: (i) section 216 Income Tax Act (ITA) election for a non-resident's net rental income; (ii) section 219 ITA branch-tax election for non-resident corporations; (iii) subsection 256.3(1) amalgamation election; (iv) section 88(2) winding-up elections; (v) discretionary late/revoked election relief under subsection 220(2.1) ITA; (vi) hybrid-mismatch limitation: foreign-affiliate-dumping anti-hybrid rules (Parts VI.1 and XII.2 of the ITA) with no elective bifurcation available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold), increased to CAD 50,000 effective 22 March 2025 under federal Budget 2024; QST: small-supplier threshold aligned with the federal GST threshold. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; registration may be voluntary below the threshold. Attribution includes taxable supplies of associated and closely-related persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Resin statue garage kits are tangible personal property (TPP); exports qualify for zero-rating under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI, Part V, section 20 where the supplier ships or transfers possession of the goods so that they are exported as part of a continuous journey to a place outside Canada within a reasonable time (with deemed export under ETA subsection 20(3) for deliveries to a common carrier, customs broker, or freight forwarder). Ancillary services supplied to a non-resident client (e.g., design or remote quality-control consulting) may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 unless performed in Canada in respect of Canadian real estate or goods. Domestic Canadian sales, including pre-order deposits where delivery occurs in Canada, are subject to GST 5% and QST 9.5%; only export shipments evidenced by a proper documentation trail carry the 0% rate and allow recovery/refund of related input tax credits.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CAED/CCI electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice and itemized packing list
  • Through Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX receipt proving repatriation of export sale proceeds
  • Freight forwarder/courier manifest or Canada Post international shipment record
  • Destination-country customs entry or overseas consignee signed proof-of-delivery

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or approval-before-issue mandate for GST; CRA accepts paper, PDF, and EDI invoices. Revenu Québec has announced and is phasing in a mandatory continuous-transaction-control (CTC) e-invoicing/e-reporting regime for QST, with the initial compliance wave for large registrants (Quebec payroll above approximately CAD 15 million) targeted for the 2026-2027 fiscal period and full rollout for all QST registrants expected by the 2029-2030 fiscal period; transmission will use structured e-documents (UBL/Peppol-compatible) via API, not government stamping. For the subject SME, no mandatory e-invoicing applies before the final rollout phase.

CRA audits zero-rating claims through the GST/HST export-review program and will deny the 0% rate and assess uncollected GST (5%) plus QST (9.5%) where the documentation bundle is incomplete, implausible, or inconsistent with the shipment record. Penalties and interest accrue under the ETA: arrears interest at prescribed rates; ETA subsection 285.1(1) false-statement penalty of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax, whichever is greater; ETA subsection 280(1) gross-negligence penalty of 25% for a first occurrence and 50% for subsequent occurrences. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Under the Income Tax Act, a bogus export claim can trigger the subsection 163(2) 50% gross-negligence penalty and, for fraud, criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) with fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Note: Canadian law imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflects the standard documentary benchmark used by CRA and Canadian banks to evidence the completion of an export sale and is embedded in the compliance bundle above.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Canada maintains no exchange controls or central-bank approval requirements for current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance via dividends is permitted subject to Part XIII withholding tax at a standard rate of 25%, reduced by applicable income tax treaties; banks require evidence of a legally declared dividend, surplus computations, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting where withdrawals exceed CAD 10,000. No prior authorization is required, but proof of source of funds must be maintained to satisfy anti-money-laundering (AML) due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in Canadian or offshore accounts, but must report specified foreign property (including offshore currency deposits) via Form T1135 if the aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000 at any time during the tax year. For corporate accounting, receipts must be converted to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; foreign exchange gains and losses are recognized in taxable income. No CRA or Bank of Canada approval is required for holding export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is MODERATE; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) can serve as ancillary rails for preorder collections in EUR/USD, but are not CDIC-insured banks and cannot function as primary liquidity repositories. They are subject to FINTRAC reporting on cross-border flows and may trigger enhanced due diligence when used for supplier settlements. Recommended position: sweep EMI balances daily to the primary Canadian Tier-1 account; refrain from using EMIs for PF or statutory filing evidence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for other transactions (per non-resident related party).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. Tax treaty benefits require beneficial ownership and genuine economic activity. CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including GST/HST and payroll) if they fail to exercise due diligence. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company (Artificial Transactions)

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits; penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit. CRA may re-assess and impose interest. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the artificial arrangement. Courts may pierce the corporate veil to recover taxes.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, criminal fines up to CAD 2 million, and imprisonment for individuals. For repeat offenders, penalties escalate.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices and impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete) plus interest. For gross negligence, penalties up to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in transfer pricing mispricing. Civil restitution and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
