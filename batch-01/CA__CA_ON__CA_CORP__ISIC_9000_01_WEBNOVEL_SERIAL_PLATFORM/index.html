Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and the CRA Non-Resident Simplified GST/HST Registration portal. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance, which separately administers the Employer Health Tax for employers with permanent establishments in Ontario.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian Business Corporation, incorporation is completed by filing articles with Corporations Canada (federal) or ServiceOntario (Ontario); the incorporating authority transmits incorporation data to the CRA, which issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN). The corporation must then activate the RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll) program accounts, plus the RZ (customs) account if importing or exporting goods. A non-resident corporation that is not incorporated in Canada must apply for a BN using Form RC1 and, if carrying on business in Canada or making taxable digital supplies to Canadian consumers, register for GST/HST through the CRA Non-Resident Simplified Registration portal before exceeding the small-supplier threshold; the BN serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime for domestic corporations; a Canadian Business Corporation is taxed at the entity level under Income Tax Act s. 2(1) and the Ontario Corporations Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections include: Excise Tax Act s. 156 (election for supplies between registrants to be deemed made for consideration), s. 167 (election for taxable supplies on a sale of a business), and s. 150 (election for supplies between closely related corporations); for non-residents, Income Tax Act s. 216 and s. 217 elections permit elective Canadian tax returns for certain passive and employment income, while foreign subsidiary entity classification for CFC/FAPI purposes is governed by Income Tax Act s. 91-95.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold, Excise Tax Act s. 123(2)); for non-resident digital suppliers and digital platform operators under the GST/HST digital economy measures effective 1 July 2021, registration is mandatory when taxable supplies of digital services or intangible personal property to Canadian consumers exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold; no separate Ontario registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of intangible personal property, including web-novel serial content, e-books, and reading-app subscriptions, to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under Excise Tax Act s. 25(1) where the supply is not for use or enjoyment in Canada and the recipient is not registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply. Under the place-of-supply rule in Part III of Schedule IX, a digital supply is made in Canada if the recipient's usual place of residence is in Canada; supplies to consumers with non-Canadian billing addresses and geolocation therefore qualify for the 0% rate, with input tax credits fully recoverable. The registrant must retain reasonable evidence of foreign destination, including platform settlement data, payment-rail billing addresses, and IP/geolocation logs.

  • Customs export declaration (B13A) — applicable only if physical media or promotional goods accompany the digital supply; not required for pure digital transmissions
  • Commercial invoice and subscription/licence contract identifying the non-resident recipient and the terms of supply
  • Digital delivery logs and IP-address/geolocation evidence proving receipt outside Canada
  • Apple App Store and Google Play sales and settlement reports evidencing consumer transactions and platform commission deductions
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX settlement records confirming export proceeds remitted to the Canadian corporation

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls at the federal or Ontario level. Registrants must issue invoices containing the prescribed GST/HST information under Excise Tax Act s. 223, with simplified invoices permitted for supplies of CAD 30 or less, and must retain records for six years. Digital platform operators must maintain transactional records of Canadian consumer supplies and make those records available for CRA audit upon request.

CRA may audit zero-rated export claims under its GST/HST verification programs; the standard reassessment period is four years (Excise Tax Act s. 298(1)), extended to ten years in cases of misrepresentation or fraud (s. 298(2)). Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but a 180-day substantiation window is the general CRA audit benchmark for export-proceeds evidence. Penalties include a 25% gross-negligence penalty on the additional tax under ETA s. 285, civil penalties under Income Tax Act s. 163.2 of 50% of the related tax, prescribed interest under ETA s. 280, joint and several director liability under ETA s. 323, and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 with fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full, unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital account. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed and exchange controls abolished in 1951. No prior approval from the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance is required for inward equity investment, foreign ownership of the share capital of an Ontario business corporation, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of invested capital. Dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25 percent, generally reduced to 5 percent under the Canada–United States income tax treaty and other applicable treaties, with withholding fully creditable against the recipient's home jurisdiction tax. Interest payments to arm's-length non-resident lenders are Free of withholding; thin capitalization rules under subsection 18(4) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) apply where shareholder debt to equity exceeds 1.5 to 1. There are no restrictions on holding foreign currency bank accounts or on invoicing export transactions in foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts to the Bank of Canada or any exchange control authority. Exporters of digital content, including web fiction serialization subscription services, may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts held at Schedule I Canadian banks. The only reporting requirements are the International Merchandise Trade statistical declarations to Statistics Canada for physical goods and the FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction and Electronic Funds Transfer reporting thresholds of C$10,000; neither imposes any API to surrender, scale down, or forcibly convert export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Department of Finance Canada for monetary and external payments policy; prudential banking supervision and foreign exchange advisory administration are exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary liquidity management and payment disbursement, but Low as the primary or statutory treasury architecture. Canada does not recognize the European EMI licence; cross-border digital payment institutions such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are regulated in Canada only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and in Ontario as remittance businesses under the Ontario Money Remittance and Money Services Business regulatory framework. Such entities are not banks under the Bank Act, are not members of CDIC, cannot take deposits in the legal sense, and cannot issue guarantees acceptable to large commercial counterparties. Consequently, the core corporate operating account, capital escrow, and dividend clearing architecture must reside with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank under OSFI supervision, with EMI rails restricted to small-ticket author royalty payouts or foreign-in-app-purchase settlement concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident parent is routed by SWIFT MT103 over a standard correspondent banking network into the Ontario entity's Schedule I bank account. The payment must be denominated in CAD or converted on arrival at the Canadian beneficiary bank's spot rate without any Bank of Canada approval. The remittance instruction must include the following compliance protocol: field 50 K naming the foreign parent precisely; field 56 A with the remitting bank's correspondent BIC; field 59 with the Ontario corporation's name and account number; field 70 stating 'CA__CA_ON__CA_CORP__ISIC_9000_01_WEBNOVEL_SERIAL_PLATFORM SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' and referring to the corporate registry number; and field 71 A for charges. FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting applies to wire transfers of C$10,000 or more. The Canadian receiving bank will apply a CIP/KYC screen matched to the onboarded beneficial ownership diagram; no exchange control permit is required. Legal capital rules in Ontario permit freely transferable shares without ministerial authorization, and restrictions apply only to extraordinary share repurchase or redemption arrangements, which must be supported by cancellation of shares and a special resolution.Royalty and trade disbursements to foreign authors, translators, and licensing partners are executed through two parallel clearing rails: first, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for bank-to-bank settlement of monthly author royalties, which settles directly into the foreign payee's local bank via the correspondent network and the payee's receiving BIC. Second, for small-balance micro-payouts to individual authors below C$1,000, the platform uses regulated MSB rails such as EMTN or the selected EMI API, subject to FINTRAC registration and transaction threshold reporting. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for digital subscription services and are reserved only for future merchandising, print edition, or metadata licensing where physical goods or documentary trade financing is required. Consequently, the core corporate operating account, capital escrow, and dividend clearing architecture must reside with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank under OSFI supervision, with EMI rails restricted to small-ticket author royalty payouts or foreign-in-app-purchase settlement concentration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (15% federal Part I rate under ITA s. 123(1)(b) plus 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding under Part XIII ITA on dividends paid to non-residents; the Canada-US treaty reduces the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for dividends paid to a company owning at least 10% of the voting stock; equivalent reductions apply under the Canada-UK and other OECD DTAs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed when reportable transactions with a single non-arm's length non-resident exceed CAD 1 million in a taxation year; comprehensive Local File and contemporaneous documentation are required once aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 5 million; Master File and Country-by-Country reporting apply at consolidated group revenue of CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, corporate residence under the common law central-management-and-control test, GAAR (ITA s. 245), treaty shopping doctrines, and CRA anti-avoidance guidance require genuine Ontario board activity, local decision-making, employees, and IP management; a scripted Ontario shell with offshore control risks residency reassessment and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): fine equal to 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years on indictment; civil reassessment plus a 50% gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding under ITA s. 227.1; courts may impose travel bans, extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties, and restitution orders against signing directors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer pricing adjustment with cascading interest; a 10% penalty on the net governmental adjustment per ITA s. 247(3) where gross negligence exists; secondary Part XIII withholding assessments on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false statutory declarations or cause non-arm's length shifting may face criminal liability for counselling tax evasion; CRA may name directors as personally liable for the corporation's unpaid withholding arising from the transfer pricing adjustment under ITA s. 227.1.
Shell Company and Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-determination of corporate residence under the mind-and-management test; full Canadian taxation on worldwide income; Part XIII re-classification of distributions as dividends; GAAR avoidance penalty under ITA s. 245 and litigation costs; deregistration risk under the Ontario Business Corporations Act for maintained shell status.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed for false declarations under the OBCA and personally liable for unremitted HST or payroll where they directed the shell's affairs; CRA practice permits assessment of the de facto director resident abroad.
GST/HST and Digital Revenue Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unremitted HST, a 6% penalty on late remittances, daily interest, and potential prosecution under the Excise Tax Act for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Under Excise Tax Act s. 323, every director of the corporation is jointly and severally liable for HST amounts not remitted if the corporation fails to pay after enforcement; a due-diligence defence is the only escape.
