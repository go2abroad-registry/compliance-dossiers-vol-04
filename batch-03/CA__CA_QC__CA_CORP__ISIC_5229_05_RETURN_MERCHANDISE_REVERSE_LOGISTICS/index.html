Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange-rate regime with no material restrictions on current-account or capital-account convertibility. Profit remittance through dividends is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty; no exchange-control approvals are required for cross-border inflows or outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

N/A - no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in Canadian or offshore accounts and settle trade obligations freely. Transactions at or above C$10,000 trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations for financial institutions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank regulation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex may operate in Canada as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC and may support cross-border payment flows through licensed partner banks; they are not chartered banks, cannot accept deposits, and must also comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act. Settlement on domestic Canadian rails requires an indirect or sponsored banking relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File mandatory for transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in gross value per transacting related party per year (for aggregate intercompany transactions exceeding CAD 5,000,000, full documentation is required; for service transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000, the simplified 'services' documentation may apply under CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R); transfer pricing penalties may apply regardless of threshold if adjustments exceed CAD 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: For federal corporate tax, the economic substance doctrine is applied through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act: transactions lacking genuine economic substance and structured primarily to obtain a tax benefit are subject to recharacterization. For Quebec, Revenu Québec applies its own GAAR provision similar to the federal rule. Although Canada does not impose 'economic substance regulations' in the OECD-style ESR format for regular commercial corporations, the CRA and Revenu Québec require that a reverse logistics entity have substantive employees, premises, inventory control, and decision-making functions in Canada to avoid deemed permanent establishment and to substantiate TP documentation. For a Quebec corporation relying on treaty benefits, the Limitation of Benefits provision in tax treaties (where applicable, e.g., Canada-U.S. treaty LOB) and principal-purpose test deny treaty benefits unless the entity has real economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Return / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Federal: gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated federal tax (subsection 163(2)) plus 25% Quebec penalty; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 239 ITA: fine of 50-200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to two years; Revenu Québec civil penalties up to 50% of the deficient Quebec tax plus interest and refund disgorgement.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source withholdings (GST/QST and payroll) under section 227.1 ITA and Quebec Taxation Act similar provisions; for wilful evasion, the corporate veil is pierced, directors may be personally assessed for unpaid corporate tax arising from fraudulent preference claims, and travel bans are imposed under the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) arrest warrants; provincial director disqualification under the Quebec Business Corporations Act; imprisonment for up to two years on criminal counts.
Transfer Pricing Penalty – Inadequate Documentation or Non-Arm's Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the amount of adjustment when the adjusted net income exceeds CAD 5,000,000 or when documentation inadequacy is established (subsection 247(3)); interest and additional taxes accrue on the reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not liable for transfer pricing penalties absent fraud or wilful blindness; however, the CRA may apply the joint and several liability rule for transactions that redound to the benefit of the non-arm's length person (subsection 160 ITA) and asset transfer rules may pierce the veil; court-imposed compliance orders and director travel restrictions may be sought in aggravated cases.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA GAAR reassessment recharacterizes the transaction and denies the treaty benefit; Quebec GAAR similar; the entity may be deemed to have a permanent establishment elsewhere; withholding-tax deficiencies (25% non-resident dividend tax, plus interest and penalties of up to 50%) may be assessed; the CRA's administrative policy may also deny GST/QST input tax credits on intercompany invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate officers may be treated as promoters of an avoidance transaction and be liable for a penalty equal to the corporate tax benefit (section 331.1 and 331.2 ITA for advisors/promoters); in Quebec, an officer who knowingly participates in the sham may be civilly liable to creditors under the Civil Code of Quebec (articles 316-317), assets may be frozen, and travel bans under the Income Tax Act for non-cooperation are possible.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for an individual; criminal money-laundering offence under the Criminal Code of Canada: imprisonment up to 14 years; proceeds of crime forfeiture order; provincial securities or licensing penalties under Quebec's AML regime if the company falls under a designated business sector.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate or fail to implement an AML compliance program under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act face personal fines and imprisonment; Quebec courts may issue disclosure and seizure orders; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent conveyances, and travel bans are issued under a criminal arrest warrant for non-appearance.
Unremitted Source Deductions / GST-QST

Statutory sanction: Directors' liability: each director is jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions (employee income tax, CPP, EI, QPIP, QPP) and for GST/HST and QST collected; CRA penalty: 10% of the unremitted amount plus 10% per year interest; failure to remit trust amounts can constitute a criminal offence under section 244 ITA; Quebec penalty under the Act respecting the ministère du Revenu: additional 15% of unreported QST for third infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot escape liability even after resignation if the liability arose during their tenure; the CRA/Revenu Québec assesses personal liability and registers a lien on personal property; travel bans and jail time only in criminal non-remittance cases; the director due diligence defence requires proof of reasonable care and professional reliance.
