Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the canton of registered office; coordination via the Federal Commercial Register platform (Zefix/RegOf) operated by the Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property (IPI) with final publication by the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC/SHAB).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Articles 620-763 (Stock Company law, as revised effective 1 January 2023), together with Articles 626-631a OR on incorporation procedure, Article 640 OR on minimum capital, and the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Deed of Incorporation containing the Articles of Association (Statuten) together with the registration application (Anmeldung) filing submitted to the Cantonal Commercial Register Office.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% (minimum CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up at incorporation. The paid-in funds must be placed in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank and evidenced by an unconditional banker's confirmation; no state escrow account is required under Swiss law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: mandatory inspection and fire-prevention clearance by the cantonal fire authority (Feuerpolizei/Kantonaler Gebäudeversicherung) covering premises, alarm systems, and emergency exits. Structural load inspection: verification certificate from the cantonal building inspectorate confirming that the warehouse and office floors meet the load-bearing standards for storage racking and parcel sortation equipment. Workplace clearance: health-and-safety verification by SUVA and the cantonal labor inspectorate for office, picking, and delivery staff, including first-aid provision, accident-prevention training, and ergonomic assessments.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% (minimum CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up at incorporation. The paid-in funds must be placed in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank and evidenced by an unconditional banker's confirmation; no state escrow account is required under Swiss law.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/SFTA) – Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer (Main VAT Division), via the Swiss Federal Tax Administration portal (www.estv.admin.ch) and the Swiss Business Register (Zefix/RegOf/UID Register) operated by the Federal Statistical Office and the Federal Commercial Register Office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not resident in Switzerland but carries out taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT/UIDS after exceeding CHF 100,000 worldwide turnover from taxable activities, unless exempt. The foreign entity must appoint a Swiss-based tax representative to register with the SFTA, obtain a Swiss business identification number (UID) from the Federal Commercial Register, and file a registration form via the Swiss GST portal. For income tax purposes, a Swiss AG is registered with the cantonal tax authority of its seat and receives a cantonal tax number; non-resident shareholders would not need a Swiss TIN solely for shareholding. The statutory VAT and UID procedures are administered through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and the Federal Commercial Register Office.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity that is not resident in Switzerland but carries out taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT/UIDS after exceeding CHF 100,000 worldwide turnover from taxable activities, unless exempt. The foreign entity must appoint a Swiss-based tax representative to register with the SFTA, obtain a Swiss business identification number (UID) from the Federal Commercial Register, and file a registration form via the Swiss GST portal. For income tax purposes, a Swiss AG is registered with the cantonal tax authority of its seat and receives a cantonal tax number; non-resident shareholders would not need a Swiss TIN solely for shareholding. The statutory VAT and UID procedures are administered through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and the Federal Commercial Register Office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections similar to the United States. A Swiss AG is mandatorily taxed at entity level at the federal, cantonal, and communal levels. The group consolidation option under Swiss corporate tax law (Steuerkonsolidierung) permits profit-transfer agreements among group companies, but this is an intercorporate consolidation mechanism rather than an entity classification election. For VAT, an election to tax real estate and to waive VAT exemption is available for immovable property transactions under Art. 22 Swiss VAT Act; foreign parent entities may use hybrid mismatch rules under OECD/G20 BEPS recommendations, but they must be aligned with domestic anti-abuse provisions implemented in Swiss tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of worldwide taxable turnover from supplies of goods and services, excluding capital investment income, unless the entity is a foreign business making exclusively VAT-exempt supplies in Switzerland, in which case no registration is due; special thresholds apply to radio and television broadcasting services and to distance selling.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by a Swiss supplier to a foreign consignee or to a foreign country are zero-rated for Swiss VAT (Art. 23 VAT Act), provided the export is evidenced and the goods physically leave Switzerland. Services related to movable tangible property, services relating to immovable property, and other B2B services supplied to foreigners are zero-rated where they are used abroad and meet conditions in Art. 8 VAT Act. The supplier must be able to demonstrate with supporting documentation that the export occurred and that the recipient is established abroad.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration via Swiss Customs e-dec)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill as transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of payment from abroad

Switzerland does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal API, or digital stamping regime for ordinary B2B or B2C VAT invoices. Swiss VAT invoices must contain mandatory data: supplier name and address, UID number, buyer name and address, date of supply, nature and quantity of the goods/services, consideration, and the applicable VAT rate. E-invoicing using Swiss QR-bills or the Swiss payment standards is recognised. No government pre-clearance or real-time validation API is imposed; however, all invoices and accounting records must be retained and be accessible to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration upon audit.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or submission of false documentary evidence constitutes tax evasion under the Swiss federal tax law, leading to fines equal to the tax evaded (plus penalty), interest, claim enhancement, and potential criminal imprisonment for wilful fraud. The SFTA may retroactively adjust VAT assessments within the limitation period of 10 years for intentional tax evasion, and 5 years for gross negligence, and will claw back any erroneously zero-rated VAT plus statutory interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Swiss law imposes no general exchange-control restrictions on capital inflows, profit remittance, dividend distributions, liquidation proceeds, or external financing. Prior approval by the SNB or FINMA is not required for transfers to or from non-resident accounts, and counterparty emerging-market capital controls are the only practical friction. Residual legal constraints arise solely from binding international sanctions implemented by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) and from FINMA's withholding-tax and operational rules. For a Cash-on-Delivery e-commerce model, collection proceeds may be accumulated offshore and repatriated in freely usable currency at any time, provided each flow is reconciled with the underlying commercial transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland has no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. Exporters are not required to repatriate foreign-currency earnings within a fixed period, convert them into CHF, or hold them in Switzerland. Receipts may be maintained in FX accounts with Swiss or foreign banks. Nevertheless, under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) and FINMA Ordinance (AMLO-FINMA), banks must identify the economic background of any cash-related or unusual flows; cash-equivalent COD collections passing through third-party logistics agents must be contractually attributable to the Swiss AG, and transactions of CHF 15,000 or more in cash or near-cash must be treated as client-identification triggers.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank, Bundesplatz 1, Bern), which conducts monetary policy and operates the SIC payment and settlement system; prudential supervision of banks and financial intermediaries is exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA, Laupenstrasse 27, Bern).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a secondary treasury execution rail; Low as a substitute for the statutory banking relationship. Airwallex is a globally licensed payments platform (e.g., UK FCA EMI authorisation and other EEA/common-law licences) rather than a FINMA-authorised Swiss bank. The AG may open an Airwallex account for low-value cross-border disbursements to logistics partners and supplier payouts, but: (i) the statutory share-capital escrow deposit must be held with a Swiss bank authorised by FINMA and evidenced by the Art. 633 CO Bankbestaetigung; (ii) Airwallex cannot issue the notarial confirmation required for commercial-register filing; (iii) primary correspondent liquidity should remain with a Tier-1 Swiss bank; and (iv) all AMLA documentation - UBO declarations, source-of-wealth evidence, and transaction monitoring - must still be supported by the Swiss ring-fenced banking relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Swiss federal direct tax is 8.5% on corporate profit after tax, equal to 7.83% on a pre-tax basis. Cantons and municipalities levy separate corporate income taxes in a range of approximately 4.2% to 14.5%, producing aggregate combined headline rates from about 11.9% to 21.6% depending on the canton of residence.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impot anticipe) on all dividend and constructive distributions made by the Swiss AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return is due within six months after fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year AG the cantonal deadline is commonly 30 June of the following year, with extensions available where complete financial statements have been prepared and professional representation is used.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter; ordinary commercial interest and royalties outside taxable instruments do not require a quarterly return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 10,000,000 in aggregate gross related-party transactions in a tax year is the practical Swiss threshold that triggers the expectation of contemporaneous OECD transfer pricing documentation, including a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File. Below this threshold, the Swiss tax authority may still request a benchmarking study, functional analysis or TP policy when cross-border flow from emerging-market logistics or COD settlement creates audit risk.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Swiss GAAR under Art. 210 DBG and OECD treaty anti-avoidance rules require genuine economic substance. A Swiss AG in mail-order retail with cash-on-delivery must maintain real premises, employees, board meetings, decision-making functions, bank accounts and cash flow control in Switzerland. The foreign emerging-market logistics hub must be a distinct and substantiated function, not an artificial arrangement to shift profit; otherwise the Swiss authorities may recharacterise the structure, deny treaty relief and impose 35% Swiss withholding tax on constructive distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of federal and cantonal taxes, penalty interest of about 5% per annum, and administrative penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax; qualified tax fraud with falsified documents may impose criminal penalties including imprisonment for responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under Swiss corporate law for damages caused by wilful or negligent management; in tax penal proceedings the responsible officer can be individually fined, and where hidden profits are distributed to non-resident shareholders the director may be liable for the AG's unpaid tax and withholding tax.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of Swiss anti-abuse law under Art. 210 DBG, reallocation of income to the actual controlling owner, denial of DTA benefits, potential liquidation proceedings under commercial registry law, and penalty assessments for wilful failure to report substance changes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to restore withdrawn capital, are jointly liable for the company's tax debts in formal liquidation, and can face travel restrictions or entry bans through Swiss debt enforcement if unpaid tax liability is linked to concealment of assets abroad.
Transfer Pricing / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax authority makes an arm's-length adjustment and recharacterises the excess as a hidden profit distribution; the excess is subject to Swiss 35% withholding tax and can also be taxed as a non-deductible expense, plus late payment interest and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the withheld tax that should have been collected, and may face civil restitution for breach of fiduciary duties and criminal charges for disloyalty to the company.
AML and Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Swiss AML Act duties apply to financial intermediaries; implicated logistics or payment providers that intentionally circumvent KYC/STR obligations face criminal fines and imprisonment. The proceeds from structured or concealed CoD money flows may be confiscated under the Swiss Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use unlicensed payment rails or direct the splitting of cash flows to conceal taxable revenue are individually exposed to criminal conviction, confiscation, civil damages, and asset-freezing or travel bans during criminal investigation.
