Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (federal Business Number and GST/HST); Revenu Québec – revenuquebec.ca (Quebec Sales Tax, Quebec corporate income tax, and NEQ registration through Registraire des entreprises du Québec).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are not automatically assigned a TIN; registration is required. Step 1: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA’s Business Registration Online service or by filing Form RC1; a non-resident corporation must include its registered foreign address and authorized domestic representative. Step 2: Register for the federal GST/HST programme account as part of the same BN application, or add it later. Step 3: Register the corporation with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Numéro d’entreprise du Québec (NEQ). Step 4: Register with Revenu Québec for the Quebec Sales Tax account (QST) using the CliC-SÉUR Affaires portal or Form LM-1; if the corporation has a permanent establishment in Quebec, also open a provincial tax account for Quebec corporate income tax. Step 5: Use the BN and QST number on all tax returns, invoices and customs/export documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists in Canada; a Business Corporation is automatically liable to Part I federal corporate income tax and Quebec corporate tax. In cross-border structures, characterization is settled under the Income Tax Act (ss. 18.2 and 18.21) and the Quebec Taxation Act, which incorporate hybrid-mismatch response rules. Elective relief includes ITA s. 216 (non-resident electing to file a net-basis return for Canadian rental/royalty income) and the QST Act's election to self-assess on certain imported services; none of these alter the default corporate entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident with a permanent establishment or carrying on business in Canada/Quebec triggers mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 of the Excise Tax Act (and the analogous QST rules), a service supplied to a non-resident person is zero-rated if the service is not in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or intangible personal property used in Canada, and the non-resident is not an individual present in Canada when the service is performed. For goods, zero-rating applies when the goods are exported in the course of the supply and evidence of export is maintained. A registered agent/offshore secretarial service targeting non-resident clients qualifies where the service recipient is legally non-resident and the performance/place-of-supply indicators point outside Canada; supporting documentation must evidence the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore nature of the service.

  • Canadian customs export declaration (B13A or electronic export report, in force) for tangible exports
  • Original or electronic bill of lading/air waybill/carrier certificate evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Bank or payment-rail evidence of inward remittance of export proceeds (foreign-currency receipts)
  • Signed supply contract, non-resident status certificate (e.g., W-8/tax residency certificate), and service performance record evidencing zero-rated offshore service supply

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous-control e-invoicing currently applies to B2B supplies in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoices are recognized evidence if complete and preserved. Registered suppliers must file GST/HST and QST returns through CRA/Revenu Québec prescribed electronic channels once taxable turnover exceeds the relevant filing threshold; all registered persons are required to use the applicable taxpayer portal for returns and remittances. Canada is developing a PEPPOL-compliant national e-invoicing framework for business-to-government transactions; adoption remains voluntary for private-sector B2B invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years from the end of the reporting period; the period is open indefinitely for misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default. If the exporter/registrant fails to substantiate the zero-rating, the 0% rate is reversed and the unremitted GST/HST/QST is assessed, together with daily compounded interest and civil penalties. For knowingly false statements or gross negligence, penalties under s.280 Excise Tax Act (up to 25% of the tax) and Quebec equivalents apply; criminal evasion can subject an officer or corporation to fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Export documentation must be retained for six years after the end of the tax year to which it relates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD, ISO 4217) is freely convertible and floating; since the 1951 termination of the Foreign Exchange Control Board, Canada has imposed no capital-account or current-account exchange controls, and profit remittance is unrestricted. The practical compliance constraints on a Quebec-registered entity distributing profits to non-resident shareholders are: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) withholding at 25% gross (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%/15% under the Canada-U.S. Convention), with NR4 informational filing and remittance under s. 215(1); (ii) the solvency-test requirement before any distribution under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act (CQLR c. S-31.1); and (iii) FINTRAC reporting for any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Exported corporate-secretarial and registered-agent services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated for GST and QST (ETA, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7), preserving the cross-border revenue model; no exchange-control licence or Central Bank approval is required for payments into or out of the entity's Canadian and foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory repatriation, or statutory conversion ratio for export proceeds or cross-border service consideration. Exporters and service providers are free to retain foreign currency in multi-currency accounts with a Schedule I bank, and translation of receipts into CAD for income tax and GST/QST purposes uses the Bank of Canada published daily nominal exchange rate (ITA s. 261; ETA s. 160). The only binding obligations are AML/ATF-related: Canadian banks must report to FINTRAC every international electronic funds transfer (outbound and inbound) of CAD 10,000 or more, and the company must retain underlying client agreements, detailed invoices, and treasury instructions to evidence the commercial purpose of each receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa, in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control ministry; monetary authority and financial-system stability oversight reside in the Bank of Canada, while banking and AML rule-making is grounded in the Bank Act (SC 1991, c. 46), the Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement rail; LOW as the primary treasury or capital-escrow account. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK Electronic Money Institution licence; accepting deposit liabilities requires authorization under the Bank Act and CDIC membership, while an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot lawfully take Canadian deposits. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, NIUM and similar) may operate only as registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, and since the 2024 extension of the foreign-MSB registration duty, an offshore platform directing services at Canada must register or restrict its service geography. An EMI account is therefore acceptable for low-value disbursements to overseas registries and legalization agents but is not viable for client statutory monies or equity-injection custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject equity by SWIFT MT103 from their home bank to the company's Schedule I operating account through the bank's correspondent network (e.g., a U.S. clearer for USD or direct CAD via the bank's NOSTRO). Field discipline is decisive: Field 50K/F must identify the ordering customer, Field 59 the Quebec operating account, and payment details must state 'Shareholder equity contribution — [QBCA/Canada Business Corporations Act registration number], Investment Canada Act new-business notification on file'; mixed or promotional language is rejected by the bank's sanctions engine. The contribution must be recorded in the minute book as share capital or contributed surplus, supported by a subscription resolution within 30 days of receipt. The company must file the Investment Canada Act new-Canadian-business notification within 30 days (services being non-cultural and normally WTO-investor notification-class), maintain the FINTRAC beneficial-ownership record of the shareholder, and, if the contribution is instead structured as a shareholder loan, execute a formal loan agreement and observe Part XIII withholding on any interest. No NR4 arises on a pure equity wire. Quebec corporate-transparency registry declarations must be updated to reflect the ultimate shareholder as required by provincial law.Given the ISIC 8299 service model, trade disbursements consist of statutory fees remitted to Corporations Canada or the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, foreign-registry and apostille/legalization agent fees, client fee-sharing payments, shareholder distributions, and Part XIII tax remittances. Domestic high-value and tax-critical payments clear in Canadian dollars with finality over the Bank of Canada Lynx high-value system via the Schedule I bank, while low-value registrar payments use Interac e-Transfer and bulk ACH-style clearing. Cross-border outflows are executed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers and, where correspondent cover is required, MT202 COV; USD payments settle over Fedwire/CHIPS through the Canadian bank's U.S. clearing affiliate. Commercial documentary Letters of Credit under UCP600/eUCP and standby LCs are available through the same banks but are a contingency rail only, since no physical-goods trade is expected; they would be opened only when escrowed client deliverables require documentary payment protection. Every outbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more triggers a FINTRAC EFTR by the issuing bank, so the treasury file should always pair the instruction with the underlying invoice or REQ/CRA payment voucher.Outright deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/major-trade currencies) with a Schedule I bank dealer under ISDA 2002 / Canadian Master Agreement termsFX swaps (short-dated fixed-for-fixed, same-currency pairs) used only to cover operational CAD/USD liquidity gapsCross-currency swaps (CCS) and cross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term balance-sheet and intercompany-loan hedgingVanilla FX options (purchased puts/calls) limited to hedges of committed non-CAD receivables, never for speculative short positionsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only in third-currency pairs where the delivery currency is regulated or inconvertible (e.g., certain EMEA/Asia emerging-market pairs); NDFs are unnecessary and generally unavailable against CAD because CAD is a fully deliverable currency

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement rail; LOW as the primary treasury or capital-escrow account. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK Electronic Money Institution licence; accepting deposit liabilities requires authorization under the Bank Act and CDIC membership, while an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot lawfully take Canadian deposits. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, NIUM and similar) may operate only as registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, and since the 2024 extension of the foreign-MSB registration duty, an offshore platform directing services at Canada must register or restrict its service geography. An EMI account is therefore acceptable for low-value disbursements to overseas registries and legalization agents but is not viable for client statutory monies or equity-injection custody.
