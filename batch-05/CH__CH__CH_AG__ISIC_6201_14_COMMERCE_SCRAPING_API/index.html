Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (SFTA) / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV), Portal: www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply for a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nummer) using SFTA Form 754 (registration for VAT purposes). If the entity is not domiciled in Switzerland but performs taxable activities in Switzerland, it must appoint a domestic tax representative or fiscal establishment. The application requires a copy of the commercial register excerpt, statutes, board resolution, and proof of anticipated turnover. Upon registration, the entity receives a Swiss UID number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which also serves as the tax identification number for all Swiss taxes. For entities not registered in the Swiss commercial register, a separate VAT ID is assigned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a 'check-the-box' election. An AG (Aktiengesellschaft) is automatically a separate taxable entity for federal, cantonal, and communal corporate income taxes and is subject to Swiss withholding tax on distributions. For VAT, an election for VAT grouping is available under Art. 21 Swiss VAT Act (Ortsbezogene Zusammenschlüsse, Gruppenbesteuerung). Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized; Switzerland applies domestic anti-hybrid rules consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2 only to the extent explicitly implemented.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual global turnover (Art. 10 para. 2 VAT Act) for domestic entrepreneurs; for foreign entrepreneurs, registration is required when Swiss-source taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per year and the foreign business is not already exempt; voluntary registration is possible for smaller turnover. Special reduced threshold of CHF 25,000 applies to non-profit sports and charitable events.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) under Art. 18 and Art. 23 Swiss VAT Act applies to: (1) supplies of movable goods exported from Switzerland to a foreign country, provided the goods are physically transported out of Switzerland by the supplier, the buyer, or a third party; (2) supplies of services if the recipient is based abroad and the service is effectively used abroad, with limited exclusions for immovable property, goods-related services, and passenger transport; (3) transport services and other services directly related to exported goods. Advance rulings are available for ambiguous cases; the zero rate must be substantiated with documentary evidence within 60 days of delivery, though an extension may be granted.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Switzerland does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system (no CFDI/PEPPOL-style clearance mandate). However, the Swiss VAT Act requires definitive e-invoicing for audit trail purposes using Swiss QR-bill (ISO 20022) when invoicing by electronic means. For business-to-government (B2G) transactions, e-invoicing is mandatory for federal procurement since 2020. No API-based fiscal stamping is required by the SFTA; instead, taxpayers must retain original electronic records and provide access on request.

Swiss tax authorities impose a severe clawback regime for fraudulent export zero-rating claims. If the SFTA determines that export documentation was falsified or the goods did not leave Switzerland, the zero rating is retroactively reversed and VAT at the standard rate (8.1%) becomes due, plus interest. An additional penalty of up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 70-74 VAT Act) may be levied, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Art. 186-187 Swiss Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law can result in fines up to CHF 100,000 or imprisonment for individuals. The statute of limitations for prosecution is 15 years for fraudulent claims, and ordinary assessments have a 10-year limitation period for wilful concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full capital and current account convertibility. The Swiss franc (CHF) is a freely convertible global reserve currency with no exchange controls. Foreign investors may freely inject, repatriate, and remit profits subject to standard Swiss corporate and tax laws. Profit remittance hurdles are essentially nil from a currency perspective; however, Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividend distributions applies, reduced under applicable double taxation treaties upon proper filings. Capital gains from the sale of participation interests are generally non-taxable for foreign investors holding less than 5% of the company's capital, while intercompany loans must respect thin capitalization, interest rate arm's length, and substance requirements to avoid classification as hidden equity distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion regime for export receipts. Exporters are free to retain, deploy, or convert all proceeds in any currency, and there is no prescribed repatriation timeline. The only relevant obligations are commercial bookkeeping and tax reporting in CHF or functional currency, plus transparency and anti-money-laundering filing requirements for large or suspicious cross-border transactions under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA).

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) in conjunction with the Federal Department of Finance and the State Secretariat for International Financial Affairs (SIF); no dedicated foreign exchange administration authority exists in Switzerland.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Cross-border EMI viability is Low-Medium. A Switzerland-licensed fintech company (FINMA 'FinTech' or 'Banking Innovator' license) or a full banking license is required for deposit-taking and payment intermediation. Offshore digital EMI licenses (e.g., UK, Lithuanian, or Singapore EMIs) do not carry any Swiss passporting rights and cannot provide regulated account-opening or payment collection services directly into Switzerland. They may be used solely as unregulated payment-off-ramp service providers with contractual disclaimers, subject to Swiss AMLA requirements; in practice, a Swiss AG should engage only FINMA-supervised payment institutions or licensed banks to maintain treasury credibility and avoid anti-money-laundering red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the Swiss AG is executed by international wire transfer over SWIFT using a compliant equity-contribution purpose code (e.g., 'CAPITAL' or 'TREATY/GENA' dependent on corridor) with a reference to the Swiss Commercial Register number, accompanied by the corporate board resolution and bank KYC pack. The receiving Swiss bank will typically request a copy of the cash-issuance resolution, the notarial deed if the share capital increase is registered, and the source-of-funds documentation for the foreign sender. CHF capital contributions up to CHF 20,000 are duty-exempt; amounts above that incur a 1% Swiss stamp duty, although contributions resulting in at least a 10% equity stake of a newly formed AG qualify for the participation relief/exemption where applicable. Wires must reference the client-specific escrow account if funds are held pending registration or conversion.Outbound trade disbursements for data-pipeline licensing, subcontractor settlements, and procurement are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct invoice payments and SWIFT MT202 COV bank-to-bank transfers where intermediary clearing is required. Commercial letters of credit are permissible and standard for high-value international trade contracts but are less relevant for SaaS/data-API licensing; payment factories may use MT101 bulk instructions for repetitive vendor payment runs. The Swiss SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) rail settles all domestic and CHF-denominated transactions in real-time; EUR payments are cleared through SEPA or EURO SIC; USD payments require a correspondent bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal direct tax: 8.5% of net profit after tax (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax income; Art. 68 DBG). Combined federal + cantonal + municipal headline rates range from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to 21.1% (Zurich); Geneva approximately 13.99%; nationwide average approximately 15%. The applicable headline rate depends on the AG's registered seat.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% on gross dividend distributions (Art. 1 and 4 VStG) levied at the Swiss payor level; the tax is economically borne by the shareholder and is refunded, in whole or in part, only upon a documented claim or under a treaty-stipulated at-source relief procedure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Cantonal law generally requires submission within 3 to 6 months after fiscal year-end; extensions to 30 September (occasionally to 31 December) of the following calendar year are routinely granted for commercial taxpayers; missing a final deadline triggers default assessment (Ermessensveranlagung).

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT return and payment due within 60 days after the end of each quarter or calendar year; extension only with prior FTA authorisation; repeated defaults trigger a fixed reminder procedure.

Box A1 – Jahresgewinn / annual net profit Net profit per commercial balance sheet (Art. 958 OR) before tax allocation; taken directly from the audited income statement. The AG is resident at its registered seat or place of effective management (Art. 50 DBG) and is taxed on worldwide income; the foreign parent's residence does not alter this line. If the foreign parent effectively manages the AG from abroad, the AG's seat may be reclassified, triggering deemed migration and exit-tax consequences. Box A2 – Add-backs (nicht abziehbare Kosten) Add back non-deductible expenses: federal and cantonal income taxes; excess interest on hybrid debt; 50% of supervisory board compensation; excessive management fees; hidden profit distributions. For foreign-owner groups, service fees, royalties and interest paid to the parent are deductible only if supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence; unsupported items are recharacterised as hidden distribution and attract 35% withholding tax. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box A3 – Tax-exempt income and participation relief Deduct qualifying participation income: dividends and capital gains from qualifying holdings of at least 10% of paid-up capital held for one year or more; deduct foreign PE results and foreign real estate income under applicable DTA provisions. Participation relief applies only to the Swiss resident entity and is independent of the beneficial owner's residence; the foreign shareholder separately claims DTA credits in its home jurisdiction. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box A4 – Taxable profit before loss offset Commercial net profit plus add-backs minus exemptions and participation relief; constitutes the base for federal, cantonal and municipal income tax. A non-resident company with a Swiss permanent establishment uses the same reconciliation but only for Swiss-source attributable income; a Swiss resident AG with foreign shareholders must report worldwide turnover without a territorial limitation. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box A5 – Loss carryforward utilisation Offset prior-year losses within the 7-year carryforward window (Art. 67 DBG; Art. 25 StHG); no carryback; no group or fiscal-transparency relief. Losses of the foreign shareholder cannot be transferred to the AG; Swiss law does not permit group relief even where the foreign parent consolidates the AG's results. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box B – Taxable capital (cantonal capital tax) Cantonal and municipal capital tax base: paid-in share capital plus disclosed and hidden reserves, reduced by participation relief within capital tax and thin-capitalisation safe harbours (commonly 6:1 debt-to-equity ratio). No federal capital tax; shareholder loans to the AG are reclassified as taxable capital where the ratio of related-party debt to equity exceeds Swiss FTA benchmarks or interest is not at arm's length. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box C – Tax due Federal direct tax: taxable profit times 8.5% computed on the net-of-tax basis per Art. 68 DBG; cantonal/municipal tax: taxable profit times cantonal base rate times communal multiplier. The AG is separately assessed; non-resident shareholders receive no Swiss tax credit for the AG's corporate income tax except under an applicable DTA. Late filing: per-cantonal surcharge of up to 10% of assessed tax plus default interest (Art. 132-136 DBG); procedural non-compliance: fine up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 174 DBG); intentional evasion: fine equal to the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); aggravated evasion through false documents: fine between 100% and 300% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 176 DBG); repeated non-compliance may lead to director disqualification.
Box 200 – Taxable supplies at 8.1% Domestic B2B and B2C recurring subscription and metered API revenue taxed at the standard Swiss VAT rate of 8.1%. When invoicing foreign-resident business customers, the place-of-supply rule (Art. 23 MWSTG) places the supply abroad: zero-rated, no Swiss VAT; the invoice must state the foreign recipient and show no Swiss tax. Warning then fine up to CHF 8,000 for late or incomplete returns (Art. 103 MWSTG); false declaration or concealment of turnover: fine up to CHF 20,000 plus retroactive tax and delay interest; VAT evasion: fine up to four times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to one year (Art. 105 MWSTG); responsible directors are jointly subject to criminal process under Art. 104 and 106 MWSTG.
Box 210 – Supplies at 0% (export) Zero-rated exports of digital services to foreign B2B customers supported by evidence of the recipient's foreign administration (residence certificate, VAT registration number, commercial documentation). No Swiss VAT liability on exported digital services; the foreign customer applies reverse-charge in the destination jurisdiction. Warning then fine up to CHF 8,000 for late or incomplete returns (Art. 103 MWSTG); false declaration or concealment of turnover: fine up to CHF 20,000 plus retroactive tax and delay interest; VAT evasion: fine up to four times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to one year (Art. 105 MWSTG); responsible directors are jointly subject to criminal process under Art. 104 and 106 MWSTG.
Box 400 – Input tax deduction Input VAT on Swiss hosting, infrastructure, legal and advisory costs; pro-rata recovery where mixed use occurs; no deduction for entertainment expenses. Service charges invoiced to the AG by the foreign parent without Swiss VAT may still support input deduction; if the supplying parent is foreign, the AG must apply the reverse-charge mechanism (Bezugsteuer) where required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Swiss Federal Tax Administration expects a Local File for cross-border controlled transactions (services, goods, financing, royalties) where cumulative related-party volume exceeds CHF 5,000,000 per fiscal year; a Master File applies where the foreign parent group has consolidated turnover of at least CHF 900,000,000 (2019 FTA transfer pricing documentation guidance); secondary adjustments are routinely applied where hidden distributions are found.
