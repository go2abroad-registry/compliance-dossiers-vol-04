Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST and federal payroll; Revenu Québec for Québec corporate income tax, Québec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), Québec payroll source deductions and employee health contributions. E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Québec Business Corporations Act and obtain registration with the RÉQ, which issues the Quebec enterprise registration number. 2) Obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) program accounts: a newly incorporated federal corporation is automatically issued a BN by Corporations Canada; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must apply for BN registration directly to CRA using Form RC9A or via the CRA non-resident account opening procedure. 3) Register with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V (Request for Registration) for QST, Québec corporate income tax, and source deductions; the QST number and employer number are issued separately. 4) For defense tactical hardware, register under the Controlled Goods Program, obtain any required Global Affairs Canada export permit under the Export and Import Permits Act, and set up the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (GCA) profile for electronic export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box elective entity classification; a CBCA/Québec BCA corporation is mandatorily taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act and the Québec Taxation Act. Relevant elections available to the corporate taxpayer include: the GST/HST closely related corporation election under subsection 156(2) of the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST election to make intercorporate supplies non-taxable; the section 85 rollover election for transfers of eligible property to the corporation; the amalgamation election under section 87; and prescribed elections for foreign affiliate surplus within the hybrid mismatch framework. Hybrid mismatch outcomes under the OECD BEPS Action 2 provisions are applied automatically and cannot be opted out of; characterization of any hybrid foreign entity is determined by reference to its governing law attributes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act and the equivalent QST rule under the Act respecting the Québec sales tax: a person (including a non-resident carrying on business in Canada) must register for GST/HST and QST when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters. Supplies of real property and activities connected with a permanent establishment in Canada may require immediate registration without reference to the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property (armored all-terrain tactical vehicles) is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods by or on behalf of the supplier and the goods are shipped, in a continuous export journey, to a destination outside Canada. Supplies to a foreign state or a foreign state agency are zero-rated where the goods are delivered to that state abroad and are not for consumption or use in Canada. Related engineering and after-sales services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and non-registered recipient, provided the services are not performed in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The exporter must hold a valid Global Affairs Canada export permit for goods on the Export Control List, comply with the Controlled Goods Program where applicable, and maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence of export in its books and records.

  • CBSA B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System, GCA, showing the exporter reference and Canadian export number)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and technical product description identifying the armored all-terrain military tactical vehicles, consignee and destination
  • Through ocean bill of lading or international air waybill evidencing the continuous export journey to the foreign consignee
  • End-User Certificate, Destination Control Statement, and the Global Affairs Canada export permit issued under the Export and Import Permits Act
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice or correspondent bank confirmation) evidencing settlement of the export invoice in convertible foreign currency or Canadian dollars

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-by-transaction API clearance mandate applies to B2B invoices in Canada or Québec. Electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns through CRA and Revenu Québec portals is mandatory for registrants above the applicable filing thresholds. Pan-Canadian adoption of the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing framework is voluntary as of the 2024-2025 implementation window. Invoices may be issued electronically in standard EDI/PDF format, and no state-operated clearance token is affixed to export invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST and QST where the export supporting documentation is incomplete, inconsistent or fraudulent. The normal reassessment limitation period is four years under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, but the period is unlimited where the exporter has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud. Civil gross-negligence penalties of up to 25% of the tax in question apply, together with prescribed interest, late-remittance penalties and possible prosecution for wilful evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. Invalidated defense export permits expose the exporter to retrospective clawback of input tax credits and input tax refunds, automatic recharacterization of zero-rated exports as taxable supplies, and recovery of unpaid GST/HST, QST, interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions; there are no general restrictions on current account convertibility, dividend distributions, or profit repatriation. Non-resident investors may freely remit dividends, interest, and proceeds from the sale of a Canadian corporation, subject to standard corporate tax compliance, thin capitalization rules, and withholding tax requirements under the Canada-Quebec Revenue Agency and the Canada Revenue Agency. The only material hurdles are (i) the need to file annual returns under the Corporations Act (Quebec) and the Canada Business Corporations Act, (ii) arm's length transfer pricing documentation for cross-border related-party transactions, and (iii) anti-money laundering obligations triggering enhanced due diligence for defense-sector businesses. Capital account transactions are equally unrestricted; however, transactions involving military or strategic goods require approval under exports permits from Global Affairs Canada and may be subject to financial reporting thresholds under FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All foreign currency export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts, converted, or hedged at the corporate treasury's discretion. Nevertheless, any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or greater, or the equivalent in another currency, must be reported to FINTRAC as an electronic funds transfer or cross-border currency movement. In addition, contracts for the export of all-terrain military tactical vehicles must comply with the Export and Import Permits Act, and proceeds supported by the corresponding permit and invoice documentation must be retained for audit and tax purposes. There is no requirement to repatriate proceeds within a fixed timeline; however, taxable foreign exchange gains and losses must be computed in accordance with the Income Tax Act and included in the taxable income of the Quebec corporation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank and monetary authority), Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated bank supervision, and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border currency transaction reporting and AML enforcement

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not prescribe a legislative de minimis threshold for Local File preparation; however, CRA administrative policy requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for non-arm's length cross-border transactions where the aggregate annual value exceeds CA$1,000,000. The Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of CA$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and Quebec's GAAR (Taxation Act s.1079.6) deny the benefit of transactions that lack genuine economic substance and the implementation made for non-tax purposes. A Quebec corporation acting as a shell, without employees, premises or operational nexus in the manufacturing of armored tactical vehicles, will be disregarded; income and liabilities will be reattributed to the real taxpayer.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Federal Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment of up to 2 years; Quebec Taxation Act: similar fine plus imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil penalties apply for repeated false statements (hence 20% plus interest).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized or acquiesced to evasion are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax shortfalls (ss.160 and 227.1 ITA); can face criminal imprisonment, civil restitution, and Canadian and international travel restrictions when an arrest warrant or tax lien is active.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upon reassessment, Canada may impose a penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, if the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5,000,000 or 10% of the corporation's gross revenue. Interest and adjusted tax are immediately collectible.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized non-arm's length transactions can be assessed personally for amounts the corporation fails to remit; courts may impose civil penalties and travel bans when corporate assets are insufficient and the directors are beneficiaries of the shifted profit.
GAAR / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA disallows the tax benefit under s.245 ITA; Quebec may apply its parallel GAAR; the corporation can be de-registered; export permits under the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) may be revoked or suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors are civilly liable for misrepresentations of economic substance; fines up to CA$25,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years may follow for egregious sham arrangements; travel bans may be imposed under criminal asset forfeiture statutes.
AML / Sanctions / Defense Export Violations
