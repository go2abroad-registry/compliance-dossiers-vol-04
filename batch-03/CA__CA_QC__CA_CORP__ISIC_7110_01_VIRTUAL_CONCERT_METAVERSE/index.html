Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll (Business Number program; canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; CRA Business Registration Online); Revenu Quebec (revenuquebec.ca; CLIENT system) for Quebec corporate income tax, QST (TVQ), and GST/HST collection within Quebec under the single-administration agreement. The Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ) issues the 10-digit Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) as the provincial registration gateway.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the 10-digit NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec. Step 2: Register the corporation through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or the Revenu Quebec single-window to obtain the CRA 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll if employees are hired); in Quebec, the QST registration number is issued under the NEQ. Step 3: If the corporation is controlled by non-residents, file CRA Form RC4601 (non-resident corporation information return), enroll for treaty-reduced Part XIII withholding arrangements, and address Section 116 certificate requirements for any Canadian asset dispositions. Step 4: Activate electronic filing in CRA My Business Account and Revenu Quebec CLIENT2 for T2, GST/HST, and QST returns, and confirm no separate commodity or digital-services licensing tax applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (C-corporation analogue): a Canadian-resident Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act at the federal 15% general corporate rate (after provincial abatement and general rate reduction) plus Quebec corporate income tax at 11.5% under the Taxation Act; no pass-through or fiscal transparency is available. If the company is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), the federal small business deduction applies to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income and Quebec applies its reduced SME rate, but classification remains entity-level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (C-corporation analogue): a Canadian-resident Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act at the federal 15% general corporate rate (after provincial abatement and general rate reduction) plus Quebec corporate income tax at 11.5% under the Taxation Act; no pass-through or fiscal transparency is available. If the company is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), the federal small business deduction applies to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income and Quebec applies its reduced SME rate, but classification remains entity-level. Territorial scope: worldwide income with foreign tax credits for resident corporations; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Quebec is taxed only on Canadian-source income and must file a Part I return.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; corporate classification is statutory. Available elections include: GST/HST grouping election under Excise Tax Act s. 273 (closely related corporations may elect to treat intercompany supplies as zero-rated), simplified non-resident registration under ETA s. 242.1 for suppliers of digital services into Canada, the QST mirror grouping election under LTVQ s. 227.1, and the Quebec administrative election to file GST/HST through Revenu Quebec. For hybrid mismatch outcomes, Income Tax Act Part XVI.1 (ss. 18.2, 18.3, 18.4) contains mandatory floating/highway rules denying or deferring deductions on hybrid arrangements and requires the filing of prescribed designations where Canadian entities interact with foreign hybrid entities; no US-style check-the-box elections or IIR/UTPR elections arise at registration stage.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15.47375%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST); a Quebec-resident corporation operating virtual concerts will in practice register immediately because event revenue will exceed the threshold. Mandatory registration with no threshold applies, effective July 1, 2021 for GST/HST and January 1, 2019/July 1, 2021 for QST, to non-resident suppliers of digital services and to digital platform operators making supplies to Canadian/Quebec consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Virtual-concert access and digital ticket tokens supplied by the Quebec corporation are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the place of supply is outside Canada: for B2C digital services, ETA Sch. IX Part 4 deems the supply made in Canada only where the recipient's usual place of residence is in Canada, so sales to foreign purchasers qualify for 0% GST/QST under Sch. VI Part V s. 7 (services to non-residents) and s. 10 (intangible personal property to non-residential unregistered persons). Supplies to Canadian-resident consumers are taxable at the 15.47375% combined rate, and the corporation must collect and remit based on onboarding data (cardholder country, USDC wallet jurisdiction, billing address, IP geolocation). Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline (the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951); the 180-day benchmark in the task schema is treated only as voluntary treasury best practice for audit credibility. Input tax credits on expenses attributable to zero-rated exports remain fully recoverable under ETA s. 169, giving an effective cash refund of GST/QST paid on inputs.

  • Pure digital supply (no customs declaration required): retain (i) executed event terms and ticketing contract identifying the non-resident recipient, (ii) zero-rated commercial invoice referencing ETA Sch. VI Part V, (iii) non-residency evidence (IP geolocation, billing address, payment-rail country data, foreign VAT ID or CRA Form RC2701 declaration), (iv) settlement records (Stripe payout reports and USDC wallet hashes with block-explorer references), and (v) metaverse/CDN delivery-access logs proving consumption outside Canada.
  • If physical goods are shipped (merchandise bundles or commemorative tickets): file the Canadian Export Reporting System declaration (formerly G13) and retain the Customs export declaration, bill of lading or air waybill, commercial invoice, and proof of foreign payment to satisfy ETA s. 18(b) export evidence rules.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API clearance obligation exists in Canada or Quebec. Suppliers must issue invoices with prescribed particulars (ETA s. 223; LTVQ art. 25) including supplier name, GST/QST registration numbers, date, description, consideration, and tax amount; electronic invoices in XML/EDI/Peppol format are accepted voluntarily, and simplified invoices are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. A voluntary Peppol network is being adopted for procure-to-pay interoperability but is not a fiscal clearance mandate.

Zero-rating claims are audited with heightened scrutiny where revenue is settled through third-party processors or crypto on-ramps; if the substantiation bundle does not prove non-residency, CRA or Revenu Quebec reverses the 0% rate and reassesses GST/QST with compound interest. Penalties include ETA s. 280 (25% of the amount for a false statement; greater of CAD 5,000 and 50% where the person knew or was grossly negligent), ETA s. 285 (50% gross-negligence penalty), and Quebec Tax Administration Act arts. 59-60 mirroring penalties; criminal exposure arises under ETA s. 327 (fine up to 200% of tax evaded and/or up to five years' imprisonment) and ITA s. 239. The normal reassessment window is four years, but is unlimited where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is established (ETA s. 298(2); Quebec Tax Administration Act art. 1010).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation. Inward and outward fund transfers are limited only by cross-border wire reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) — $10,000 CAD or equivalent, per FINTRAC. The Investment Canada Act applies to net benefit review for controlling FDI above statutory thresholds, but does not restrict currency convertibility. Dividend remittance payments are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (potentially reduced by Canada–US or other tax treaties), but no foreign exchange approval is required. The Canadian dollar floats on global markets; the Bank of Canada does not set or maintain an official parity. Cash payments and advance payments for digital event tickets may be subject to Part XV retailer point-of-sale reporting under the PCMLTFA Regulation, but do not constitute FX limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory FX surrender or mandatory conversion obligations for export receipts. All export proceeds, including proceeds from virtual concert ticket sales and tokenized digital performance rights invoiced in USD or any convertible currency, may be retained abroad, held in foreign-currency bank accounts with Canadian Schedule I banks, or converted to CAD at the seller's discretion and market rate. Nothing in the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, or Canadian foreign exchange regulatory framework mandates repatriation timelines or conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange monitoring via Statistics Canada and Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMI / electronic money institution (e.g., Payoneer, Wise, Revolut Business) accounts may be opened remotely for a Canadian corporation, but such EMIs are generally not licensed as banks in Canada under the Bank Act and are not members of the Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). Holding material operational liquidity in an unregistered foreign EMI is disfavoured by Canadian banks and federally regulated trust companies because (i) the entity is not a 'bank' for Canadian AML/CFT purposes; (ii) any Canadian cash-out/transaction activity may cause captive AML obligations on the EMI that would require FINTRAC registration as a Canadian MSB under the PCMLTFA; (iii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), if applicable, imposes a licensing regime on participant settlement service providers operating in Canada; (iv) foreign EMIs are frequently rejected for corporate treasury accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks due to elevated adverse interaction risk, especially when the EMI is used as an intermediary for ticket disbursements. Legal constraint: the Canadian entity is expected to open a bank account directly with a regulated Canadian financial institution to maintain clean audit trails for revenue from virtual concert operations and payments to global artists.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended purpose: 'BOAJ' or 'Equity Investment (CRO)' in the MT103 field 20; Canadian banks accept cross-border equity contributions without specific FX authorization, but require an underlying board resolution and a signed subscription agreement. For amounts above CAD 250,000, a capital-account log must be retained showing source from the foreign corporate entity's ledger; wire informational fields must not mention 'blockchain', 'token sale', 'NFT', or 'metaverse' without attaching the proper contract reference. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMI / electronic money institution (e.g., Payoneer, Wise, Revolut Business) accounts may be opened remotely for a Canadian corporation, but such EMIs are generally not licensed as banks in Canada under the Bank Act and are not members of the Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). Holding material operational liquidity in an unregistered foreign EMI is disfavoured by Canadian banks and federally regulated trust companies because (i) the entity is not a 'bank' for Canadian AML/CFT purposes; (ii) any Canadian cash-out/transaction activity may cause captive AML obligations on the EMI that would require FINTRAC registration as a Canadian MSB under the PCMLTFA; (iii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), if applicable, imposes a licensing regime on participant settlement service providers operating in Canada; (iv) foreign EMIs are frequently rejected for corporate treasury accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks due to elevated adverse interaction risk, especially when the EMI is used as an intermediary for ticket disbursements. Legal constraint: the Canadian entity is expected to open a bank account directly with a regulated Canadian financial institution to maintain clean audit trails for revenue from virtual concert operations and payments to global artists.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined 26.5% (Federal basic rate 38% minus 10% provincial abatement minus 13% general rate reduction = 15%; Quebec general corporate rate 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII gross withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (T106 Corporation T106 Information Return) mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of all transactions with a non-resident related party exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. In addition, CRA expects contemporaneous Local File documentation for intercompany services if headcount/transaction volume is significant; Canada-CbC reporting applies for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) such as those in the UAE; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) and the Canadian treaty permanent establishment concept require substantial business activity. A corporation with only a registered office and no employees/functional assets in Quebec will have its place of effective management located elsewhere; CRA can view the entity as a shell, disallow treaty benefits, and assert GAAR. Intercompany payments must be supported by real services, actual technology IP ownership, and personnel; otherwise deductions are recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50%–200% of tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. CRA civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for corporate penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the filing of false returns; jail term may be imposed on the director/shareholder as primary offender. Courts pierce the veil when the corporation is a sham.
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245 ITA) denies the tax benefit; treaty-reduced WHT denied; CRA recharacterizes the transaction and imposes Part XIII WHT on unrepatriated deemed dividends. Administrative penalties of up to CAD 5,000 per omission.

Director personal liability: Directors liable under s.160 (transaction with creditor) for the amount of assets transferred out of the corporation below FMV, and under the Quebec Companies Act if corporate formalities not respected (façade theory).
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations per violation; for directors/officers knowingly implicated, penalties up to CAD 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be named in FINTRAC enforcement if they exercised operational control; criminal liability for laundering proceeds from ticket scalping fraud under Criminal Code s.355.
Unpaid Withholding and Payroll Taxes

Statutory sanction: s.227(8) ITA: unremitted withholdings become a charge on the corporation's property; CRA can register a secured lien and seize bank accounts. Interest accrues at 9%; late-filing penalties up to 10%.

Director personal liability: Directors are absolutely personally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withholdings and QST/GST, notwithstanding dissolution or resignation; they must discharge the debt or face CRA Civil Restitution direction and court-ordered payment assessment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(2), CRA adjusts the price to arm's-length and imposes a 10% penalty (s.247(3)) on the net upward adjustment where documentation is inadequate and the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million. Interest runs from the original return due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate T106 or transfer-pricing documents can be held liable under Quebec civil law for any misleading information; criminal liability if aware of false pricing documentation (s.239).
