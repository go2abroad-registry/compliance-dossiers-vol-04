Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca; Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Business Registry (ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry) for provincial extra-provincial corporate licensing

E-government filing portal

A non-resident wholesaler carrying on business in Ontario must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA before commencing taxable activity. Register online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), supplying the incorporating jurisdiction, registered office, director and resident-agent particulars, and the NAICS 2022 business activity code 423430. For GST/HST purposes, the BN is extended with the 'RT' or 'RC' program accounts; registration is mandatory for any importer of goods into Canada and for any person whose worldwide taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. Voluntary registration is available to foreign entities making taxable supplies in Canada; a non-resident registrant must provide a Canadian contact address or appoint a resident tax representative. The Ontario extra-provincial licence must be obtained through the Ontario Business Registry under the Ontario Business Corporations Act before the corporation can enforce contracts or hold property in Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations; the federal/provincial Business Corporation is a separate taxable person subject to federal Part I tax and Ontario corporate income tax. Available statutory elections: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election allowing non-resident investors to file a net-basis return on Canadian-source income; (ii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election to deem supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (iii) ETA s. 167 election to amalgamate GST/HST registrations of closely related registrants; (iv) Income Tax Act s. 150(2) election for a co-terminus fiscal year; (v) hybrid mismatch and excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) regime under ITA Part VI.1, which requires prescribed filings for cross-border financing with non-resident affiliates. A foreign hybrid entity cannot obtain Canadian corporate tax treatment without incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Ontario Business Corporations Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold measured on aggregate worldwide taxable supplies in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; importers are required to register irrespective of this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (SSD and CPU chips) are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where the supplier delivers the goods to a non-resident purchaser and the goods are exported out of Canada within 60 days of delivery, supported by commercial invoice and export transport documentation. Semiconductor brokerage services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the service is performed wholly outside Canada; services performed partly in Canada are zero-rated if the non-resident recipient is not in Canada at the time the service is rendered and the supplier cannot reasonably determine a taxable use in Canada. Domestic wholesale supplies to GST/HST-registered resellers are subject to 13% HST, but the registered buyer recovers the input tax credit, producing an effective net-zero cash tax burden. Export zero-rating is a relief mechanism, not a refundable rebate, and requires positive documentary proof at the time of the supply.

  • CBSA B13A export declaration or approved electronic equivalent (CADEX / ECA in-transit) showing Ontario origin and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing HS 8471 and HS 8542 semiconductor classifications
  • Bill of lading (ocean, air, or truck manifest) evidencing physical departure from Canada within 60 days of delivery
  • Freight or courier receipts with carrier tracking and destination proof
  • SWIFT TT or escrow inspection-wire settlement confirmation evidencing inward payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent Canadian clearing-bank statement documenting foreign-exchange conversion

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction reporting, or digital stamping at the point of invoicing. The CRA operates a voluntary e-invoicing interoperability pilot based on the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 standard, requiring onboarding of a certified Access Point and issuance of PEPPOL IDs for B2B exchange. A wholesale entity may issue standard electronic invoices without prior tax authority authorization, but under ETA s. 286 it must retain machine-readable books and records sufficient to determine GST/HST liabilities, including commercial invoices, customs declarations, freight evidence, and bank remittance records.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims within four years under ETA s. 298, extended to seven years for misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default. False export zero-rating claims are subject to: an ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the falsely claimed net tax or rebate; an Income Tax Act s. 163(2) penalty of up to 50% of understated income tax; daily compounded prescribed-rate interest; and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 239 / ITA s. 327 carrying fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA operates targeted export-verification audits for semiconductor wholesalers, demanding bills of lading, CBSA transaction history, warehouse exit records, and bank remittance evidence; failure to substantiate an export within the statutory documentation-retention period reverses the zero-rating retroactively, with reassessments for GST/HST, penalties, and interest, and may render the exporter ineligible for future zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no general exchange controls, prior approval requirements, or forced conversion rules. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, and trade settlements may be freely converted and wired outward; the only frictions are standard AML/KYC onboarding, CRS/FATCA self-certification, Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25%, treaty-reduced), and applicable sanctions screening. No profit remittance license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian-dollar or multicurrency accounts, convert through scheduled banks at the prevailing market rate, or use receivables directly as collateral. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline for repatriation; books must reflect accrued export receivables under IFRS, and the CRA applies normal accrual taxation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists. External trade and sanctions matters are administered by Global Affairs Canada, while prudential oversight of financial institutions resides with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary banking/treasury relation; MEDIUM as a disbursement-only auxiliary rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, dLocal) are not Schedule I or II banks; they cannot accept deposits in Canada or provide CAD IBAN-based clearing unless registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. OSFI blocks unauthorized banking/EMI branch activities. These platforms may be used for low-value outbound supplier payments in USD/EUR/CNH but they cannot provide escrow-grade custody, documentary LCs, or reliable evidence for capital injection and revenue traceability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (15.0% federal general corporate rate after the general rate reduction + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable Canadian dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; reduced treaty rates apply (15% portfolio dividend; 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting equity under most DTAs, including the Canada–US treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; balance-due date is 2 months after year-end (3 months for CCPCs eligible under the CAD 500,000 income threshold); quarterly income tax instalments are due when the estimated combined federal/Ontario tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000; GST/HST returns follow the normal calendar or quarterly cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous documentation is required under ITA s. 247(4) when non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 per category for loans, services, and intangibles, and CAD 5,000,000 for tangible goods; a Country-by-Country Report (CBCR) must be filed where the MNE group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000; documentation must be finalized before the T2 filing deadline to secure penalty protection.

Economic substance scope: Canada enforces economic substance through the common-law sham doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) at ITA s. 245, the place-of-effective-management standard for tax residence, and targeted allocation rules for offshore interposed entities; although formal Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Caribbean/Gulf ESR statutes are not codified federally, the CRA will disregard transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose, recharacterize artificial interposition, and deny treaty benefits where the principal purpose is treaty shopping; a Canada-based wholesale broker of semiconductors must maintain real staff, inventory risk, invoicing control, and decision-making in Ontario to sustain its profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements – ITA s. 239 and s. 163(2)

Statutory sanction: On indictment: fine of 100%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction: fine of 50%–200% plus imprisonment up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax with compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally assessable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholdings and jointly liable under s. 160 for transfers to non-arm's-length persons without adequate consideration; criminal conviction may impose travel bans, asset forfeiture, cross-border extradition, and director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance – ITA s. 247

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the transfer-pricing adjustment plus a standalone penalty of 10% of the understated transfer-pricing amount (minimum CAD 500) under ITA s. 247(3); double-tax exposure upon a corresponding upward adjustment by the counterparty jurisdiction; loss of penalty protection where documentation is not contemporaneous.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign inaccurate T2 filings may be personally assessed for the gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2); officers involved in TP-driven evasion schemes face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Economic Substance / Shell Company Abuse – GAAR and Sham Doctrine

Statutory sanction: CRA applies the sham doctrine or GAAR to disregard the shell and reallocate the full trading profit as Canadian Part I income; gross-negligence penalty up to 50% of the avoided tax plus interest; Part XIII withholding is reassessed on deemed repatriated dividends or management fees.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose restitution of tax liabilities on directors who dominate the sham entity; criminal charges for fraud or tax evasion attach travel bans, asset-freezing orders, director disqualification, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML / Sanctions and Unremitted Withholding – PCMLTFA and Part XIII

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation and up to CAD 2,000,000 for repeated violations against a legal person; criminal remedies under the PCMLTFA impose fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; failure to remit Part XIII amounts attracts a 10% failure-to-remit penalty plus daily compounded interest (20% for repeated failures).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted third-party withholdings even after dissolution; senior officers prosecuted under AML enforcement face personal fines, foreign-travel bans, revocation of registrations, and civil forfeiture of the brokerage proceeds.
