Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued for consideration permitted by the Business Corporations Act. The corporation should document subscription, consideration, and issuance in its securities register and board records. Quebec does not generally require incorporation capital to be deposited into a government escrow account; a commercial bank account may be opened after incorporation and funded under the board-approved subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal occupancy and fire-safety approval, maintain compliant exits, alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation procedures, and hazardous-material controls, and complete structural-load and racking inspections for the warehouse. Comply with Quebec occupational health and safety requirements administered by CNESST, including worker training, personal protective equipment, lifting controls, battery and chemical storage, incident reporting, and workplace inspections.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the CRA Business Registration Online portal for obtaining a Business Number and program accounts; Revenu Quebec administers Quebec Sales Tax (QST) registration and compliance through its online services portal.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and register the applicable CRA program accounts, including GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import-export (RM), as relevant. A non-resident corporation may apply through CRA Business Registration Online where eligible, by telephone, or through the prescribed non-resident registration process, providing legal name, incorporation details, registered address, directors, business activities, expected Canadian taxable supplies, and authorized representative information. A corporation making taxable supplies in Quebec must separately register for QST with Revenu Quebec unless a specific exemption applies. The entity must maintain Canadian books and records, file required GST/HST and QST returns, collect tax at the applicable rate, and update registration information promptly. Importers must obtain and use an RM import-export program account through the Canada Border Services Agency and CRA systems.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally separate taxable persons and have no United States-style federal check-the-box election. Available elections are transaction- or status-specific, including corporate reorganizations under the Income Tax Act, amalgamations, wind-ups, section 216 elections for certain Canadian rental income of non-residents, and elections affecting GST/HST treatment such as the closely related election and joint venture elections where statutory conditions are met. Quebec corporate tax treatment generally follows the federal corporate form but requires separate Quebec filings and elections where applicable. Partnership and trust classifications are determined under Canadian law and are not freely elected by a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including supplies of associated persons, measured under the small-supplier rules generally by reference to a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. The threshold applies to GST/HST registration; Quebec QST registration generally follows separate Quebec rules and may be required for persons carrying on business in Quebec or making taxable supplies there, subject to limited small-supplier and non-resident rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Commercial goods supplied for export from Canada may generally be zero-rated for GST/HST where the supplier ships or delivers the goods outside Canada, the recipient exports the goods under qualifying statutory conditions, or another prescribed export rule applies. The supplier must retain sufficient evidence that the goods were exported and must satisfy the applicable timing and delivery conditions. A supplier generally cannot treat goods as zero-rated merely because the purchaser is non-resident or intends to export them later. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the specific export-of-services rules apply; exclusions can cover services connected with real property, tangible personal property, or services performed in Canada. QST zero-rating or export treatment must be tested separately under Quebec legislation.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, buyer, seller, value, currency, and delivery terms
  • Export declaration or applicable Canada Border Services Agency export filing, including Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) data where required
  • Customs release and import entry or equivalent destination-country import evidence where available
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, freight-forwarder record, or other carrier documentation showing shipment outside Canada
  • Packing list and serial-number or part-number records for aircraft parts
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside Canada
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and payment records
  • GST/HST and QST invoices showing the applicable zero-rated treatment and registration numbers
  • Importer-of-record and customs broker records
  • Inward remittance evidence where relevant to commercial substantiation; Canada does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a condition of export zero-rating

Canada and Quebec do not generally impose a universal real-time electronic invoicing clearance mandate for ordinary business-to-business supplies. GST/HST and QST invoices must contain prescribed information, including supplier identity and registration numbers, date, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount where applicable. Electronic invoices are permitted if integrity, authenticity, accessibility, and record-retention requirements are satisfied. CRA and Revenu Quebec generally support electronic filing and payment, but ordinary invoices are not required to receive prior government clearance or a digital fiscal stamp. Customs filings and import/export data may be submitted electronically through CBSA systems, including the CARM system for commercial imports.

CRA or Revenu Quebec may reassess tax not collected, deny input tax credits or input tax refunds, impose interest, and assess penalties where export evidence is incomplete, inaccurate, or fraudulent. A person that knowingly or recklessly participates in false invoicing, fictitious exports, false customs declarations, or fraudulent refund claims may face gross-negligence penalties, civil fraud penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under applicable federal or Quebec law. Amounts improperly refunded or credited may be recovered retrospectively with interest and penalties. Customs violations may additionally result in seizure, monetary penalties, accounting-document demands, AMPS penalties, import-export account consequences, or prosecution. Documentary records should generally be retained for at least six years after the end of the relevant tax year, subject to longer periods where an audit, objection, appeal, reassessment, or statutory exception applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require foreign-exchange licensing, mandatory repatriation, capital-account registration, or approval for foreign ownership, shareholder loans, dividends, or intercompany transfers. Profit remittances are subject to corporate income tax compliance, withholding tax rules under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, applicable Canada tax treaties, transfer-pricing documentation, thin-capitalization limits, shareholder-benefit rules, solvency requirements, sanctions screening, and bank source-of-funds controls. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, controlled goods, or unusual aviation counterparties may be blocked or escalated. Quebec entities must also maintain appropriate corporate, tax, customs, and commercial records and may require French-language documentation or communications under Quebec language rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline, mandatory conversion ratio, or compulsory surrender requirement for export proceeds. The corporation may retain export receipts in Canadian dollars or foreign currency, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering obligations. Export documentation must support customs treatment, origin, valuation, end use, and any zero-rated GST/HST treatment. Foreign-currency receipts should be recorded using an appropriate Canadian accounting and tax methodology, with realized and unrealized foreign-exchange gains or losses documented. Proceeds from controlled aviation articles remain subject to applicable export-control, sanctions, customs, and end-use requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine capital controls; financial institutions and payment providers remain subject to the Bank Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, sanctions legislation, and applicable Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection, foreign-currency accounts, and operational transfers; low as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank or aviation-trade bank. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide services only within its licensed permissions and must satisfy Canadian payment, AML, sanctions, consumer-protection, data, and foreign-exchange requirements applicable to its activities. It cannot be assumed to provide Canadian deposit insurance, Canadian-dollar clearing, documentary trade finance, escrow services, or unrestricted access to Canadian domestic payment rails. Canadian-resident payment activities may trigger registration or licensing under the Retail Payment Activities Act and related requirements, while money services businesses may require FINTRAC registration. Banks may decline fintech-originated funds where beneficial ownership, source of wealth, licensing, or transaction purpose is unclear.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscribed share capital and shareholder loans from the named investor or documented lender directly to the corporation's Canadian bank account through SWIFT or the bank's approved correspondent channel. Use the corporation's exact legal name, Canadian account number, transit and institution numbers, SWIFT/BIC, currency, and registered address. Canada generally has no statutory foreign-capital purpose-code regime; the bank should nevertheless receive a clear payment narrative such as 'share capital subscription' or 'shareholder loan under agreement [reference]'. Before release, provide the subscription or loan agreement, board approval, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, lender identity, ownership percentage, tax treatment, and expected use of funds. Record the transaction in the general ledger and corporate registers, reconcile the incoming wire, and assess withholding tax, transfer pricing, thin capitalization, interest deductibility, and related-party reporting. Do not route funds through unrelated third-party accounts or split transfers to avoid review.Use Canadian bank accounts for supplier payments and maintain a transaction-level audit trail. SWIFT MT103 or its current ISO 20022 equivalent is appropriate for direct commercial wires; include the exact beneficiary, bank identifiers, invoice number, purchase-order number, part numbers, shipment reference, currency, and truthful payment purpose. For high-value or new-counterparty purchases, use a documentary commercial letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, escrow, or controlled disbursement arrangement negotiated with the bank. A commercial LC should specify compliant presentation documents, including commercial invoice, packing list, air waybill or bill of lading, customs information, insurance evidence where applicable, certificates of conformity or traceability, and end-user or export-control documentation when relevant. Screen all parties, aircraft, parts, routes, banks, freight forwarders, and end users against Canadian sanctions and export-control requirements before payment, shipment, and document release. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (Federal 15.0% + Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid to non-residents (domestic rate). Treaty relieving rates: 5% for a US corporation holding at least 10% voting shares; 15% for portfolio investors under Canada-US DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (Form T106 filing threshold). Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is statutorily required for all reportable transactions under s.247(4) and may be requested by the CRA; a CRA audit will expect full Local File and Master File alignment with OECD Guidelines if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the Economic Substance Doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and the doctrine of 'sham' under common law. For treaty benefits, an entity must demonstrate substantial business activities (real presence, active management, Canadian bank accounts, and payroll). The DTA may be denied if the structure lacks commercial substance. No standalone ESR regime, but the CRA may challenge shell arrangements with no business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or misleading income reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec Taxation Act s.36.1 mirrors federal criminal sanctions. Civil penalty under s.163(2) is mandatory: 25% of the amount of tax attempted to evade.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under s.227.1 for unpaid corporate tax withholdings and for civil restitution if they participated in or acquiesced to the evasion. Travel bans (RCMP-facilitated) and asset freezes are possible in criminal proceedings; imprisonment is a direct liability.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR on a tax benefit, and impose significant penalties. DTA relief can be voided resulting in full Canadian withholding tax liability. In Quebec, under the Business Corporations Act, the corporation may be involuntarily dissolved for patterns of non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for CRA assessments, including up to 100% of the tax shortfall if they signed false returns (s.163.2). Directors of sham corporations may be held personally for losses in civil suits.
AML/ATF Failures – Cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act fines up to CAD 2 million per administrative violation; FINTRAC penalties up to CAD 500,000. Criminal fines and confiscation of goods.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for failure to establish adequate AML controls; criminal convictions can lead to imprisonment (up to 5 years) and prohibition from acting as directors. Travel bans and extradition risks arise where payments are tied to international trade offenses.
