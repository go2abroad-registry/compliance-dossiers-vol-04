Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships that carry on business in Ontario or Canada must register for a CRA Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST account, payroll account, and corporate income tax account. Registration is completed online via CRA Business Registration Online. A non-resident partnership must provide partnership information, including each partner's name, address, and residency status. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN plus a two-letter program identifier (e.g., RT for GST/HST). A foreign partnership without permanent establishment in Canada may only require a non-resident account for withholding obligations, depending on the nature of Canadian-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a formal check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is by default a fiscally transparent partnership under the Income Tax Act (ITA); it must file an information return (T5013) and allocate income to partners. The partnership may make an election under subsection 216(1) ITA for non-resident partners to report Canadian-source rental or timber income on a net basis. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership can make a section 156 election (single-supplier election) among closely related entities, or a section 273 election for a joint venture. Hybrid mismatch elections are addressed under the OECD hybrid rules and Canadian legislative responses, but no standalone election form exists for that purpose; eligible taxpayers may rely on treaty and domestic ordering rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies from taxable supply activities (calculated on a rolling basis over any four consecutive calendar quarters); once exceeded, mandatory registration applies from the following quarter, unless already registered on a voluntary basis

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods applies when the supplier exports tangible personal property by shipping or delivering it out of Canada, or by transferring ownership to a non-resident person in Canada for export, provided the exporter retains documentary evidence of exportation within a prescribed timeframe. Zero-rating of services applies to (i) services provided to a non-resident individual who is outside Canada at the time the service is acquired, or (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person (other than an individual) where the service is related to real property or goods situated outside Canada, or to a service performed wholly outside Canada. For helpdesk/SaaS-type services, a supply to a non-resident business of computer-related services (e.g., helpdesk support, cloud deliverables, data processing) is generally zero-rated under section 23 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the service is not in respect of real property in Canada or goods at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or Canadian Export Declaration via the Canada Border Services Agency / Canadian International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical movement from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank document proving receipt of the export payment in foreign currency from a non-resident payer (or such other proof of settlement acceptable to the Canada Revenue Agency)

Canada has no compulsory real-time e-invoicing or continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance requirement. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or via approved electronic filing service providers. Standard electronic invoice formats (e.g., XML/EDI) are accepted for substantiation, but no digital stamp or government API clearance is required. Taxpayers are required to keep electronic records in an accessible and readable format for a minimum of six years.

The CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess GST/HST where supporting export documentation is incomplete, timeliness thresholds are missed, or the remittance is not received. Penalties include a denial of zero-rating causing uncollected tax liability, interest accruing from the original due date, and any of the following: (i) a false-statement or misrepresentation penalty under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act equal to 25% of the amount of any tax sought to be evaded; (ii) gross negligence penalties under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act (50% to 100% of the understated amount); and (iii) criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act or section 239 of the Income Tax Act for tax evasion, with fines up to CAD 200,000 or twice the tax evaded, imprisonment up to five years, or both. Retrospective clawbacks may cover a statutory limitation period of four years from the date of assessment, extendable to seven years in the case of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada imposes no exchange controls: no prior license, no registration, no repatriation quota or profit-remittance barrier. Distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced under CTA/US Treaty) with NR4/NR401 filing obligations. Foreign-currency (USD/EUR) accounts at Schedule I banks are unrestricted and standard.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada has no export proceeds surrender rules, no statutory repatriation deadlines, and no mandatory conversion ratios. SaaS/helpdesk export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, swept via SWIFT, or converted freely through an authorized dealer (Schedule I bank or registered FX dealer).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy; AML surveillance by FINTRAC; prudential supervision by OSFI; Payments Canada operates national clearing and settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) + Ontario general (11.5%) for a taxable corporation; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent, so each partner is taxed on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income at the partner's applicable rate (corporate, individual, or treaty-protected).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. The LP is a pass-through entity, so distributions to partners are not dividends; non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their allocated Canadian-source business income, and any remittance of profits is treated as a distribution of previously taxed capital, not subject to additional statutory dividend withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory for every cross-border non-arm's-length transaction exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in gross value (CRA Information Circular 87-2R); the s. 247(4) penalty applies where the net transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. The LP must retain a full Transfer Pricing policy for inter-company SaaS royalties, support fees, and cost-sharing arrangements even below the threshold if any transacting party is resident in a designated treaty country.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). The CRA instead applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the treaty-shopping rules, and the specified non-resident allocation rules to strip benefits from shell arrangements. The LP must demonstrate real activity in Ontario: employment records, office premises, executed client contracts, billing infrastructure, and decision-making minutes. A limited partner who takes part in control of the business under s. 15(1) of the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act is treated as a general partner and loses the limitation of liability, exposing personal assets to enterprise debts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s. 163(2)); criminal evasion penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded (s. 239(1)); imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years by indictment; interest compounds daily on all assessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under s. 227.1; CRA may assess directors personally even after corporate dissolution, and a court may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure of personal property.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(4)), capped at the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; compensatory interest; the CRA may deny treaty benefits and reassess the LP allocation as artificial.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly authorized the pricing scheme face gross negligence penalties and potential prosecution; controlling limited partners who directed the scheme may lose limited-liability protection and be held jointly liable for the entire assessment.
Shell Entity / No Economic Substance in Ontario

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the LP under GAAR and assess the beneficial owners directly; the Ontario Ministry may dissolve the LP; late-filing penalties apply until dissolution; creditors may attack the LP as a sham and void the liability shield.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil: the general partner's directors and any controlling limited partner are personally liable for all partnership debts; criminal evasion charges may lead to extradition, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
AML / FINTRAC Obligation Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 100,000 for an individual and CAD 500,000 for an entity per contravention; prosecution for failure to report a suspicious transaction or failing to register carries imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the directing mind of the LP's general partner who knew, ought to have known, or were willfully blind to the contravention are personally liable for the penalty if the LP fails to pay within 30 days; personal assets and travel privileges are at risk.
