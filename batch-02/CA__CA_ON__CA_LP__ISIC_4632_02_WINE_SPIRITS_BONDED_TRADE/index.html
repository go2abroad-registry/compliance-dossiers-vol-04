Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Domestic fiscal authority with exclusive jurisdiction over federal income tax, GST/HST, and excise duties; administered through the CRA Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-business/business-registration-online-service.html) and the Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) for Ontario provincial corporate minimum tax and Ontario excise-related licensing interfaces.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the limited partnership requires a separate Business Number (BN) – a Canadian partnership carrying on a commercial activity must be registered with the CRA under its own BN, distinct from the partners' BNs, unless the partnership is a 'specified member' arrangement or the partners already hold BNs and elect administrative consolidation. Step 2: Complete the Business Registration Online (BRO) application or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', providing partnership articles of limited partnership filed under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990) or the extra-provincial registration in Ontario. Step 3: Register for program accounts: (a) RZ – GST/HST; (b) RD – payroll source deductions if a Canadian-resident partner is remunerated; (c) RP – excise duty account under the Excise Act, 2001, mandatory for a licensed bonded warehouse proprietor dealing in spirits and wine; (d) RV – excise tax account if applicable. Step 4: For non-resident partners, submit Form RC447 (request for non-resident account registration) and execute the CRA authorization (Form RC59) appointing a representative in Canada for service of process. Step 5: Obtain an Ontario Registration Number via the Ontario Business Information System when filing the Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership Registration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services, distinct from the federal BN. Step 6: For an Ontario bonded trade business, obtain AGCO (Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario) licensing approvals before the CRA activates the excise duty account; the CRA will cross-reference the AGCO licence to confirm the premises qualify as a 'bonded warehouse' under section 90 of the Excise Act, 2001. The average CRA processing time for a BN and GST/HST account is 5–10 business days; excise licence processing for bonded warehouses averages 60–90 calendar days due to premises inspections by CRA excise officers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A limited partnership (LP) formed or extra-provincially registered under Ontario law is, under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)), a flow-through vehicle that is not itself a taxable entity; partnership income, gains, losses, and input tax credits (in a proportionate share) are allocated annually to each partner and taxed at the partner level. Canadian-resident partners include their share under the applicable personal or corporate rates; non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%, reduced by treaty) on certain allocations and, absent an election, may be taxable under Part I on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership is treated as a separate 'person' under section 123 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA); it independently registers and files GST/HST returns, then its net tax may flow to partners for ITC proportionality in accordance with ETA section 272.2 and the CRA's partnership GST/HST administrative policy. Territorial scope: Canada taxes worldwide income of resident partners on a residence basis, while the partnership itself operates on a territorial commercial basis subject to GST/HST on supplies made in Canada; exports of the bonded wine and spirits are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Available elections relevant to the Ontario LP bonded trading structure are: (1) ITA subsection 216(4) election – a non-resident partner with a Canadian business carried on through the LP may file a Part I tax return on net Canadian-source income, in lieu of the standard Part XIII gross-withholding treatment, thereby accessing deductions and treaty benefits; (2) ITA subsection 216.1(1) election and section 96(3) elections – an LP may, with concurrence of all partners, elect to treat an interest in the partnership as being held on capital account or adjust fiscal period end dates, although administrative prudence favours a December 31 fiscal year-end aligned with the CRA's calendar-year assumption for partnerships; (3) ETA section 156 election – a GST/HST 'close association' election between general partners and the LP to deem supplies between them to be at fair market value or nil, reducing irrecoverable ITC cascades; (4) ETA subsection 272.1(2) election – registered partners may elect for a partnership to make a separate GST/HST registration that computes ITCs based on the 'substantial interest' formula; (5) Excise Act, 2001 bonded warehouse 'licence holder election' – an election to file excise duty returns on a quarterly basis rather than monthly when the monthly average duty liability is below the statutory threshold; (6) Hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance elections are available only in the narrow parameters of ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6 for interest deductibility alignment, but for a typical wine/spirits wholesale LP no such election is beneficial and would expose the structure to ETA clawbacks. Ontario provincial elections: an LP that is a 'large business' under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act may elect consolidated Ontario corporate minimum tax filing, but this is rare for wholesale pass-through structures and is not recommended without an Ontario resident corporate partner confirming an effective Ontario tax rate analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – The small supplier threshold under ETA section 148 governs whether the LP must register for GST/HST; a registrant whose worldwide taxable (including zero-rated) supply revenues from Canadian activities in a single calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive quarters exceed CAD 30,000 must register. A wine or spirits bonded warehouse wholesaler will almost invariably exceed this threshold in the first quarter of active trading, and in practice the LP should voluntarily register immediately upon AGCO licensing even if projected revenue is below threshold, in order to claim input tax credits on the high-value inventory procurement (aged Scotch and Bordeaux lots) and on bonded warehouse overhead. Separate Ontario registration thresholds do not exist; Ontario HST is a piggyback formula within the federal return, so a single CRA GST/HST account covers both the 5% federal and 8% Ontario components. Non-resident suppliers are likewise required to register under the 'non-resident supplier' rules of ETA sections 240(1) and 241, with no small supplier exemption available for supplies made through a permanent establishment in Ontario.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for bonded wine and spirits exports is available under paragraph 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, which zero-rates a supply of tangible personal property (including excisable goods) made by a Canadian supplier that is exported directly by the recipient or by the supplier, provided that (i) the goods are shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, (ii) the supplier retains documentary proof of export that satisfies section 22 of the GST/HST Memoranda Series 7-4 (Exports), and (iii) the goods are not further relieved for domestic consumption through an Ontario retail sale. For the bonded trade, excise duty relief is independently claimed under subsections 35(1) and 36(1) of the Excise Act, 2001: a licensed bonded warehouse proprietor may remove spirits or wine from the warehouse without payment of duty when the goods are destined for export; relief becomes final only when the CRA receives proof of actual export via the Canadian Customs Coding Form B13A (or the electronic CADAR/CANPARS declaration). Zero-rating of 'offshore services' ancillary to the bonded trade – brokerage, freight management, and title transfer facilitation – is likewise available under paragraph 5(b) of Schedule VI Part V for services supplied to a non-resident person where the services relate to tangible personal property situated outside Canada at the time the service is performed. The LP must additionally satisfy the Ontario-specific AGCO export documentation requirement, including an LCBO export authorization (Form 2) for wine exits, and the consignment must be physically removed from Canada within a reasonable period, customarily interpreted by the CRA as within 60–90 days of the supply; failure to effect export within the administrative timeframe causes the supply to revert to the 13% rate in Ontario.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government-authorized digital stamping regime currently applies in Canada or Ontario, as of the 2024–2025 taxable period. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian Electronic Invoicing (PCEI) framework aligned with the PEPPOL network standards, and a taxpayer may transmit invoices through the CRA's e-invoicing pilot (launched in 2022 and progressively expanded) for faster ITC allocation, but there is no statutory obligation to invoice electronically or to obtain CRA pre-clearance per transaction. The Excise Act, 2001, however, imposes a mandatory paper/electronic record-keeping and duty-payment system for bonded warehouse proprietors, requiring each withdrawal from the bonded premises to be supported by an Excise Duty and Goods and Stores Declaration (Form B-11) filed in the CRA's Excise Duty System (EDS) portal, together with a transfer warrant for inter-warehouse movement; liquor inventory movements between bonded warehouses are tracked through the CRA's Internet reporting system. Revenue Quebec, if the entity expands to Quebec, would require multilingual e-invoicing, but this is outside the Ontario scope. The practical mandate for an AGCO-licensed Ontario bonded wine/spirits wholesaler is that all commercial invoices, duty relief certificates, and customs export declarations must be digitally transmitted to CBSA via the Canadian Single Window/ACE equivalent (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management, CARN) within 30 days of the event, but this is a customs declaration mandate, not a real-time tax clearance stamp.

The CRA's International Large Business and Aggressive Tax Planning directorate applies elevated audit intensity to bonded alcohol exporters because of the historical abuse of export zero-rating and excise duty relief. Penalties and clawback mechanisms include: (1) Reassessment and duty recovery – under ETA subsection 296(1) and Excise Act, 2001 subsection 195(2), the CRA may, without limitation beyond the normal four-year reassessment window in cases of misrepresentation (three additional years where negligence or wilful default is shown), retroactively reverse the zero-rating, reinstate the 13% HST on the full wholesale consideration, and assess all excise duties forgone on the spirits and wine if export proof is deficient; interest accrues at the prescribed CRA rate (currently compounded daily) from the original due date of the return. (2) Excise Act further penalty – failure to export within the conditions of the bonded warehouse licence triggers assessment under Excise Act, 2001 section 244, imposing a forfeiture equal to 150% of the duty-excised amount where goods cannot be traced to a foreign destination. (3) ETA false statements penalty – subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act imposes a penalty of CAD 25,000 plus 100% of the net tax sought to be evaded if a taxpayer, knowingly or with gross negligence, makes an underlying false statement in an export claim; criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and Criminal Code section 380 can compound fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for fraud in duty avoidance. (4) Clawback of input tax credits – the CRA may, under ETA subsection 298(2), disallow all ITCs claimed in respect of the exported inventory if the zero-rating is overturned, and the LP becomes jointly and severally liable under ETA subsection 272.3(2) for unpaid net tax of its partners where the partnership is deemed a 'registrant' engaging in a sham arrangement. (5) International compliance mitigation – under the CRA's Extrapyramidal export verification program, a minimum of 25% of bonded alcohol exporters are selected for a line-by-line document match of the B13A electronic export reports to the bank's SWIFT payment records; where the 180-day repatriation deadline is breached without obtaining a CRA-approved extension or where the counterparty is resident in a low-tax jurisdiction, the CRA presumes the property was diverted into the domestic Canadian market, triggering full 13% HST and excise reassessments plus a CAD 10,000 per-occurrence administrative penalty under the Excise Act, 2001 licensing regime.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under IMF Article VIII; exchange-control legislation in Canada was repealed in 1951 and there is no practical or legal distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions. The CAD floats freely; no central-bank permits, FX licensing or transaction-level reporting to the Bank of Canada is required for inbound partner capital contributions, outbound trade payments, loan service or profit distributions. Remittance hurdles are fiscal, not exchange-regulatory: (i) non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their allocated share of Canadian business income and must file Canadian returns, and distributions are not subject to Part XIII withholding; (ii) passive payments (interest, royalties, management or administration fees) attract Part XIII withholding at 25% statutory, reduced by applicable treaties (US, UK, France); (iii) HST at 13% (Ontario) applies to domestic sales with zero-rating for export sales; (iv) excise duty under the Excise Act, 2001 is deferred while goods remain in a CBSA bonded warehouse and becomes payable at release for domestic consumption; (v) a non-Canadian acquisition of control of the Canadian wholesale business may trigger Investment Canada Act notification, which the onboarding bank will include in enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation or domiciling of export proceeds; the LP may lawfully retain EUR/GBP/USD receipts offshore. Binding operational duties: FINTRAC large-value transaction and wire-transfer reporting at CAD 10,000; CBSA export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System; excise-duty abatement or refund documentation for spirits/wine exported from bond; and continuous sanctions/PEP screening of all counterparties under the Special Economic Measures Act and UN/OFAC resolutions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - sole issuing authority for Canadian bank notes and manager of the official international reserves; no exchange-control permissions or approvals are required from it. Cross-border and FX policy is co-administered by the Department of Finance Canada (Currency Act, Financial Administration Act), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs, excise and bonded warehouses, Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions (Special Economic Measures Act, UN Act), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium as an ancillary rail. Canada has no EU-style EMI licence; non-bank payment providers must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act regime phased in during 2024-2025. Foreign digital EMIs cannot join Payments Canada and therefore cannot access Lynx RTGS, ACSS cheque/direct-debit clearing, or provide credible custody of bonded-duty funds; they are also not accepted by the Liquor Control Board of Ontario (LCBO) as evidence of financial standing. Use Wise, Payoneer or Airwallex-class platforms only for low-value supplier disbursements and payroll, never for duty payments, escrow balances or LC-backed trade.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial-ownership opacity: corporate general partner combined with multiple non-resident limited partners, creating nominee or structuring suspicion.

Root cause: Beneficial-ownership opacity: corporate general partner combined with multiple non-resident limited partners, creating nominee or structuring suspicion.

Defense evidence: Current Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration; complete partner register identifying each partner holding 25% or more interest or control; registry extracts and constitutional documents for the GP and each corporate partner; executed source-of-wealth/source-of-funds declarations; Canadian counsel legal opinion on LP validity and authority; CRS self-certifications (or W-8BEN-E for US partners) for every non-resident partner; Investment Canada Act notification or undertaking where required.
Trade-based money-laundering typology: high-value alcohol, lumpy vintage payments, duty deferral and in-bond transfers are classic red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners remit by SWIFT MT103 (Fedwire/CHIPS from the US; SEPA/CHAPS from UK/EU) to the Capital Escrow Account at the Ontario bank. Beneficiary coding uses the bank BIC and the Canadian institution-transit-account number (no IBAN); Field 70 must carry the consistent narrative 'Capital contribution to Ontario LP Bonded Wine and Spirits Wholesale - ISIC 4632'. FINTRAC large-value reporting applies at CAD 10,000; the bank verifies beneficial ownership, source of funds, partnership registration, escrow conditions and CRS/FATCA attestations before releasing funds. Escrow disbursement requires GP instructions plus confirmation that AGCO licensing, CBSA bonded-warehouse status and any Investment Canada Act clearance are obtained. Canada maintains no federal fixed purpose-code list; banks apply internal nature-and-purpose codes, so consistency of the wire narrative across all fields is the principal compliance anchor.Supplier settlements: SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers in EUR (Bordeaux), GBP (Scotland) and USD (bulk spirits); Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700, usance 60-90 days) for multi-shipment vintage parcels; documentary collections (D/P, D/A) for repeat counterparties. Canadian-dollar flows (excise duty to CBSA, HST remittances, logistics, partner distributions) clear via Payments Canada ACSS for low value and Lynx RTGS for large value. The LCBO, as the largest Ontario buyer, pays by electronic funds transfer under agreed trade terms; duty-free and export sales apply zero-rated HST with CBSA export declarations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); an Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96 so no Part I tax is payable at the LP level, and each partner pays tax on its allocable share; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian business income attributable to the LP's Canadian permanent establishment.
