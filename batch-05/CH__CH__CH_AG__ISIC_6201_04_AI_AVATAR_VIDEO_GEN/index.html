Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), VAT Division in Berne; online portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch (VAT e-office 'SuisseTax'/'SuiteTax' module). Cantonal direct-tax administration: Canton Zug – Tax Office (Steuerverwaltung des Kantons Zug).

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft before the Zug Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Upon registration, the Federal Statistical Office automatically allocates the business identifier UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / numéro IDE, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx), which serves as the statutory corporate tax identification number for federal, cantonal and communal direct taxes. The Commercial Register Office transmits the registration to the Zug Tax Office, triggering the corporate tax files. For indirect taxation, the AG must effect a separate VAT registration with the ESTV through the VAT e-portal once taxable turnover generated in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000 per calendar year under Art. 10 of the Swiss VAT Act (MWSTG); the federal VAT number is the UID plus the suffix 'MWST'. A foreign-controlled AG acts through its Swiss directors and is not by Swiss VAT law obliged to appoint a local tax representative. Voluntary VAT registration below the threshold is available and is commonly elected by software exporters to preserve input VAT deduction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is a corporation with juridical personality; no pass-through or transparent classification exists at federal level. The AG is resident in Switzerland if it has its statutory seat or its place of effective management in Swiss territory (Art. 50 DBG), and it is taxed on its worldwide income as a separate taxpayer at federal, cantonal and municipal level (federal profit tax of 8.5% calculated on post-federal-tax profit, economically equivalent to approximately 7.8% of pre-tax profit, plus cantonal/communal corporate profit tax in Canton Zug according to the applicable statutory multipliers and special regimes). Foreign permanent establishment income and foreign real property income are exempt under Swiss territorial allocation practice and applicable double-taxation treaties. Non-resident ownership is expressly permitted. Distributable dividends are subject to Swiss withholding tax at 35%, reduced under treaties or participation-relief rules.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification exists in Switzerland; the AG is mandatorily entity-level taxed. The following express elections are available: (i) VAT fiscal unity under Art. 22 Swiss VAT Act upon written application to the ESTV, renewable for a minimum five-year term, allowing closely controlled group entities to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) VAT accounting method election between the effective input-tax-deduction method (Art. 37 para. 1 MWSTG) and the flat-rate Saldo method (Art. 37 para. 4 MWSTG) for taxpayers below the statutory maximum turnover of approximately CHF 5.1 million, indexed; (iii) voluntary early VAT registration even below the CHF 100,000 threshold; (iv) at cantonal level, Zug innovation-box and patent-box elections together with any applicable R&D-related cantonal deductions. Swiss domestic law contains no separate hybrid-mismatch election; the Swiss general anti-abuse rule (Art. 16 DBG / Art. 21 VStG) and OECD BEPS-aligned measures govern mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year, excluding exempt turnover, generated from supplies in Switzerland (Art. 10 para. 1 letter a and Art. 107 MWSTG). For foreign businesses exclusively supplying electronically supplied services to Swiss recipients, the same CHF 100,000 threshold applies; above it, the foreign SaaS provider must register for Swiss VAT and charge the applicable Swiss rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 49 MWSTG, supplies of physical goods qualify for the effective zero rate if the goods leave Swiss customs territory and the exporter substantiates export by means of a Swiss customs declaration. For AI avatar video-generation SaaS, an electronically supplied service under ISIC 6201, the territorial rule of Art. 7 para. 3 letter a in connection with Art. 8 para. 2 letter k MWSTG removes the supply from Swiss VAT when the recipient has its business seat or residence abroad: no Swiss VAT is charged and the exporter retains full deductibility of attributable input VAT. If a foreign B2C customer is physically in Switzerland at the time of supply, Swiss VAT at 8.1% applies. Where Paddle acts as merchant of record, the Swiss AG invoices the intermediary's foreign legal entity, an out-of-scope export for Swiss VAT; where Stripe is used with the Swiss AG as merchant of record, destination-country VAT/GST obligations for each foreign end customer must be administered outside Switzerland.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung through the Swiss NCTS/Passar system) as the principal proof of physical removal for goods; for intangible SaaS deliveries, the written supply contract, purchase order and place-of-use evidence form the export record
  • Transport evidence showing departure from Swiss customs territory — Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or CIM railway consignment note; for digital services, server access logs and IP/use-location records of the foreign recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or functional equivalent — SWIFT/MT103 credit confirmation, Stripe payout statement or Paddle settlement report evidencing receipt of the foreign sale proceeds, together with the underlying commercial invoice

No real-time transaction clearance, no continuous-control-transaction device and no digital fiscal stamping is currently imposed by Swiss federal law. The ESTV relies on periodic VAT settlement through the 'SuisseTax'/'SuiteTax' e-portal. Invoices must satisfy the statutory content requirements of Art. 26 MWSTG, including issuer name and address, VAT identification number, date of issue, recipient, nature of supply, consideration, tax rate and tax due. Electronic invoicing formats are accepted, notably the Swiss QR-bill, the Swiss eBill standard and PEPPOL, without pre-clearance API connectivity to the ESTV. Fiscal records must be archived for the statutory ten-year retention period and retrievable without delay in reliable electronic form for audit purposes.

Swiss law does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the figure is applied here as a prudential internal compliance benchmark for export documentation archiving and as the customary threshold used by Swiss tax examiners when reviewing export substantiation files. In the event of disallowed zero-rating or fraudulent substantiation, the taxpayer faces: (i) retrospective reassessment at the standard 8.1% rate with statutory default interest of 5% per annum; (ii) extension of the limitation period to ten years after the end of the relevant fiscal year, or fifteen years in cases of tax evasion (Art. 99 MWSTG); (iii) procedural-violation fines of up to CHF 1,000 per infringement (Art. 95 MWSTG); (iv) intentional VAT evasion fines equal to the evaded tax, from CHF 100 up to CHF 400,000, while negligent evasion triggers a fine of one quarter of the evaded VAT, from CHF 100 up to CHF 100,000 (Art. 96 MWSTG); and (v) potential director liability for wilful or negligent conduct. Goods falsely declared for export may additionally trigger customs penalties under the Swiss Customs Act with full recovery of the zero-rated treatment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Switzerland has no exchange control legislation, no capital transfer authorisation regime, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend repatriation, loan repayment, or liquidation proceeds. The Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital (Bundesgesetz über den freien Kapital- und Zahlungsverkehr, SR 940.0) guarantees unrestricted cross-border capital flows; there is no bifurcation between current and capital account approvals. Dividend payments to foreign shareholders are subject to statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax, but full or partial relief is available under applicable double taxation treaties (e.g., 0% under the Switzerland–Hong Kong treaty for qualifying corporate shareholders; 5%–15% under most OECD treaties), provided the relevant Form DA-1 refund application is filed with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV). No drag on liquidity, but practical bank-level anti-money laundering (GwG) source-of-funds documentation is mandatory for cross-border remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds for any currency. The legal entity may freely retain invoice currency balances (USD, EUR, CHF) or convert through sanctioned credit institutions at prevailing market rates. The only compliance layer is the internal accounting standard requirement under the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR Art. 957 f.) and SWISS GAAP FER: foreign currency positions must be translated transparently at closing rates, and any unrealised FX gains/losses recognised in the income statement. Additionally, the SNB conducting monetary statistics surveys may require reporting of aggregate external asset/liability positions, but not transactional surrender.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank, Bundesplatz 1, 3003 Bern, Switzerland; operational oversight delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), Laupenstrasse 27, 3003 Bern, and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF), Bern. SNB has full monetary policy and currency control authority; no separate foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate. Swiss-regulated fintech EMIs holding a FINMA fintech license under Art. 1b Banking Act (e.g., Yapeal, Klarpay, Innopay) are viable for payroll, supplier disbursements, and high-volume SaaS collections; they are not acceptable as sole custodian for capital escrow or statutory audit confirmation where a Tier-1 bank relationship is required. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be used as collection-only rails for Stripe/Paddle payouts, but legal constraints apply: (i) deposit-taking above CHF 100 million requires a full banking license; (ii) an unlicensed foreign EMI cannot hold funds overnight on behalf of the AG without Swiss establishment, risking unlicensed financial intermediation under Art. 1 Banking Act; (iii) dual-UBO verifications, FATCA, AEOI, and GwG Art. 3 beneficial-owner transparency raise operational frictions; (iv) DeepLink's Streisand protocol cannot subordinate to an EMI whose SWIFT BIC lacks proper correspondent agreements. Recommend: core treasury on UBS/ZKB, EMI as supplementary flow engine.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct corporate income tax (after-tax basis on net profit); combined cantonal and municipal headline rate between 11.9% and 21.0% depending on canton of registered office (illustrative Zug AG aggregate rate approximately 11.9%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer, Art. 2 VStG) on the gross amount of any dividend, bonus share, or other distribution of profit; the producing Swiss AG or its paying agent must withhold and remit to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV); the DTA may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, 10% or 15% depending on the treaty partner and shareholder structure

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Swiss Local File is expected where the Swiss AG's annual gross revenue exceeds CHF 80,000,000 and it engages in cross-border related-party transactions; a Master File aligned with OECD Guidelines is expected where the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue above CHF 1,000,000,000; irrespective of the CHF 80 million threshold, ESTV practice requires sufficient arm's length documentation for every material cross-border transaction, including software royalty payments, intercompany financing, management fees, R&D cost-sharing, and cloud infrastructure sublicensing, in order to shift the burden of proof

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations separate from tax law; substance requirements are enforced through: (i) the actual management principle under Art. 50 DBG, which deems the Swiss AG to be resident where its effective administration takes place; (ii) the established judicial doctrine of Steuerumgehung (abuse of law) which allows recharacterisation of artificial legal structures; (iii) the MLI Principal Purpose Test ratified and applied to Swiss double tax conventions; and (iv) administrative practice requiring real local substance (leased office, qualified staff, local board meetings, bank accounts, business decision-making records) for holding companies, IP licence companies, and financing companies to retain access to the participation exemption, DTA reductions, CFE withholding tax relief, and refund mechanisms

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Art. 175 DBG

Statutory sanction: Federal fine normally equal to the amount of evaded federal tax, reducible for minor contraventions and increasable up to double the evaded tax for grave cases; payment of the evaded tax and default interest at 3.5% to 4.5% p.a.; cantonal penalties and interest calculated separately; criminal tax fraud committed by forged or falsified documentation (Art. 186 DBG) additionally carries custodial sentence of up to three years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign, approve, or cause false return filings are personally exposed to criminal fines and, in documented fraud cases, imprisonment; Art. 102 StGB permits corporate criminal liability with fines up to CHF 5,000,000 where the underlying individual cannot be sanctioned; civil restitution to the shareholder company for damages may follow under Art. 754 OR; international administrative assistance can trigger asset freezes, seizure of bank accounts, and travel restrictions in the context of mutual legal assistance requests
Negligent Tax Understatement and Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing procedural default fine up to CHF 10,000; penalty of 30% of the evaded tax for gross negligence; assessment by estimation of the tax authorities with discretionary charging of income and assets; default interest on the additional assessment; withdrawal of the automatic extension for tax advisers only after repeated violations

Director personal liability: The responsible director or fiduciary who caused the neglect is subject to personal fine; where the imposed fine and interest remain uncollectible from the company, the director may be pursued for damages in civil proceedings; repeat violators may lose the privilege of electronic filing and face enhanced audit scrutiny for five years
Withholding Tax Non-Remittance (Verrechnungssteuer Violation)

Statutory sanction: Claim by ESTV for the full 35% withholding tax on the gross distribution, plus 5% default interest and a surcharge; the tax debt is not extinguished by liquidation; refusal of future relief at source rulings; criminal sanction under the VStG for intentional failure with fine up to CHF 30,000 and imprisonment in aggravated cases

Director personal liability: Art. 12 VStG creates direct, unlimited, joint and several personal liability of directors and the de facto manager for the withholding tax debt if they knowingly or negligently fail to deduct, declare, or remit the tax; the State can execute directly against personal Swiss bank accounts and assets; directors resident abroad remain subject to enforcement via treaty mutual assistance and may be blocked from further Swiss directorships
Constructive Dividends and Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Full recharacterisation of the excess amount as a constructive dividend; disallowance of deducted expenses; assessment of 35% Swiss withholding tax on the deemed distribution; federal tax evasion fine of up to 100% of evaded tax where intent is shown; default interest of 3.5% to 4.5% p.a.; transfer pricing documentation failure shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and may result in a 50% surcharge on the additional tax attributed to undocumented cross-border transactions

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or implemented the non-arm's-length arrangements are jointly and severally liable to the Swiss tax authorities for the withholding tax and penalties directly; the parent company may also sue the directors for the resulting corporate damage under Art. 754 OR; in cross-border cases, directors face personal travel bans and attachment of assets through the Swiss Federal Debt Enforcement and Bankruptcy Act when the company failed to remit assessed amounts
Shell Company and Abusive Substance Structures (Steuerumgehung / PPT Denial)

Statutory sanction: Ignoring the Swiss entity for tax purposes under the effective-management doctrine; attribution of income to the beneficial owner abroad; denial of DTA relief under the Principal Purpose Test including rejection of refund claims; denial of the participation exemption and patent box; additional assessment of Swiss federal, cantonal, municipal, and withholding taxes for ten years retroactively; dissolution proceedings by the commercial register authority

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes when they acted as formal directors without substance and executed the abusive structure; the Federal Criminal Court may convict the de facto supervisor for aiding tax evasion; foreign directors face a ban on future Swiss AG board duties after any insolvency or tax execution triggered by the shell's artificial capitalisation; Art. 102 StGB allows fines up to CHF 5,000,000 on the entity
AML and Financial Compliance Failure (Geldwäschereischtens / GwG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to CHF 500,000 under Art. 39 GwG for breach of due diligence and reporting obligations; violations of Swiss sanctions legislation (Embargo Act and SEG) carry fines of up to CHF 1,000,000 and imprisonment for responsible natural persons; the Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) or designated self-regulatory body may impose enforcement measures; freezing and confiscation of assets connected to money laundering under Art. 70 StGB

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML or compliance officer are personally liable for violations of financial crime controls; money laundering under Art. 305bis StGB carries a custodial penalty of up to three years, extendable where the offence is committed in a professional capacity; the responsible board member may be personally sued for restitution and dereliction of duties under Art. 754 OR; travel bans and provisional detention are available where there is a reasonable suspicion of organised financial crime
