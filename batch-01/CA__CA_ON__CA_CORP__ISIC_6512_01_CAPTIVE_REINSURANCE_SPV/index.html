Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a US-style check-the-box election. A captive business corporation is automatically an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Relevant mechanisms: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s.156, under which taxable supplies between related registrants may be treated as made for nil consideration; (ii) election under ITA s.216 allowing a non-resident insurer to file a Canadian net-basis income tax return rather than being subject to Part XIV insurance withholding; (iii) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules under ITA ss.18.4–18.6, which operate as mandatory self-adjustments, not elections, for taxation years beginning after 2021, neutralizing deduction/no-inclusion outcomes for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid payers, reverse hybrids, and imported mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold); threshold is measured on annual worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies but excluding exempt supplies such as reinsurance premiums and other financial services; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory, and voluntary registration is available for non-resident commercial nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, zero-rating applies to goods exported by a registrant to a non-resident (Part V), to specified offshore services (Part VIII), and to financial services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not resident in Canada and is not registered for GST/HST (Part IX). A captive reinsurance SPV may apply 0% GST/HST to reinsurance and retrocession premiums ceded by a foreign parent or foreign affiliate if the ceding insurer is a non-resident and is not registered under the ETA, the insured risk is situated outside Canada, and the supply is not ancillary to a taxable Canadian supply chain. The SPV must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of non-resident status, risk situs, arm's-length premium determination, and absence of ETA registration to substantiate the zero-rated claim and any resulting refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CBSA CAED electronic export report) showing HS classification, export value, and exporter and importer identification where tangible property is exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing physical export from Canada and delivery to a non-resident consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation documenting premium and reserve repatriation from the non-resident ceding insurer into the Canadian captive bank account
  • Executed reinsurance treaty, excess-of-loss certificates, bordereaux, and actuarial reserve schedules evidencing non-resident risk situs, underwriting exposure, and arm's-length premium
  • Non-resident status evidence for the ceding insurer (foreign certificate of incorporation or company registry extract) and written confirmation that the recipient is not registered under the Excise Tax Act

CRA does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or per-invoice API pre-approval. Registrants must issue GST/HST invoices containing supplier BN, tax amount, and recipient details, retain complete transaction-level records, and file GST/HST returns electronically once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million. Electronic invoicing under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework is voluntary at this stage; no federal or Ontario fiscal pre-clearance gateway applies to captive reinsurance transactions.

CRA may reassess within the ordinary four-year limitation window; the limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default, or fraud, is established under ITA s.152(4). Fraudulent export zero-rating or false non-resident designation exposes the captive to: (i) ETA s.280 false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded, plus retroactive full or partial refund clawback with arrears interest; (ii) criminal prosecution under ETA s.282 with fines and imprisonment up to five years; (iii) ITA s.163(2) gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of understated tax; (iv) GAAR reassessment under ITA s.245 and ETA s.274 where the captive zero-rating structure lacks bona fide commercial substance or artificially shifts Canadian risk into offshore zero-rated flows; and (v) consequential FSRA licence review, suspension, or revocation upon material tax misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no capital-account exchange controls, no central-bank pre-approval for cross-border wire transfers, and no restriction on foreign-currency deposit balances. Profit remittance obstacles are fiscal rather than regulatory: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, potentially treaty-reduced to 5% for substantial shareholdings under an applicable tax treaty), thin-capitalization limits under Income Tax Act subsections 18(4) to 18(6), and section 247 transfer-pricing documentation. For a captive reinsurance SPV, OSFI/FSRA minimum capital and solvency tests (e.g., federal Minimum Capital Test or provincial equivalent) constrain upstream distributions because regulatory capital must be maintained in liquid assets within Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export or reinsurance-premium proceeds, no repatriation deadline, and no required conversion ratio into CAD. Foreign-currency accounts may be held indefinitely. Operational constraints: the captive must translate FX positions to CAD under IFRS 17 financial reporting and may elect CAD as functional currency under Income Tax Act section 261. FINTRAC requires Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) for cross-border EFTs exceeding CAN $10,000 and Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Reports (CCMIR) for currency or specified monetary instruments exceeding CAN $10,000 entering or leaving the country.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and issuer of Canadian dollars; exchange-control and cross-border funds reporting administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; prudential supervision of financial institutions by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); Ontario captive insurance regulation by FSRA (Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario); payments and clearing oversight by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary or regulatory-capital banking relationship; Medium as a supplementary payment-collections channel. Offshore digital EMIs (e-money institutions) are not licensed to take deposits or operate as Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, and they cannot participate directly in Payments Canada clearing systems. Such firms, including Wise and Revolut, must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC; the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) will impose transitional registration and safeguarding obligations on payment service providers. FSRA/OSFI prudential expectations for a captive reinsurer require that underwriting reserves and required capital reside at a regulated Canadian deposit-taking institution; an EMI account is not acceptable for those balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

USD wires are routed to the bank's U.S. correspondent and converted to CAD upon instruction.
