Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

An Ontario Limited Partnership is a separate GST/HST person under the Excise Tax Act but a fiscal pass-through for income tax; it requires a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) plus a GST/HST (RT) program account. SOP: (1) Register the Ontario LP under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario); where non-resident partners are admitted, complete the extra-provincial LP registration and designate a resident agent. (2) Obtain the BN via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, opening the RT program account where taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntary registration is sought. (3) Register the LP with CBSA for a highway carrier code and CCS/ACROSS commercial accounts to transmit export and in-transit declarations electronically. (4) File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and, for non-resident partners, remit partner-level withholding under section 153(1)(p) of the Income Tax Act using Form NR406. (5) Maintain CRA My Business Account digital credentials and e-file GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE. The LP itself does not pay entity-level income tax; each partner is registered separately for Canadian tax compliance as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime. An Ontario LP is statutorily classified as a fiscal pass-through under section 96 of the Income Tax Act, with no entity-level federal or Ontario income tax. Available elections include: (i) the Excise Tax Act subsection 273 election treating the partnership as a GST/HST registrant in its own right; (ii) the Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(4) fiscal-period election for a non-calendar partnership taxation year; (iii) subsection 98(3) rollover election to transfer partnership property to a taxable Canadian corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (iv) subsection 97(2) joint incorporation election where partners transfer property to a corporation; (v) treaty-based tie-breaker and hybrid-mismatch filings for non-resident partners under the Canada-US Tax Convention (Article IV) and the OECD Multilateral Instrument as implemented in the ITA; and (vi) the ETA subsection 211 rebate elections for capital property attributable to zero-rated export activities. No election converts the LP into a taxable corporation without a formal rollover and incorporation step.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Capital-account and current-account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Profit remittance for limited partnerships is generally taxed at the partner level; non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII on certain payments (interest, royalties, management fees, dividends) unless reduced by treaty. However, a limited partner’s share of active business income is typically taxed under Part I as Canadian-source business income, not as a withholding-taxable royalty or dividend. No exchange-control approval is required for remittances, but anti-avoidance rules under the Tax Act (e.g., thinly capitalized rules, section 212.3) and GAAR may indirectly restrict aggressive repatriation structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporting trucking entities may retain receivables in any currency in Canadian or foreign bank accounts. All CAD-denominated and foreign-currency balances are freely usable. Exporters must comply with FINTRAC reporting thresholds for cross-border currency transfers (e.g., $10,000 CAD equivalent) and with Canada Border Services Agency requirements for TIR carnets and commercial goods documentation, but no conversion or surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with prudential and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No dedicated foreign exchange decree or control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a secondary treasury workstream for multi-currency receivables and vendor payments, but Low for first-tier statutory or TIR guarantee clearing. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may register as MSBs in Ontario and obtain FINTRAC registration; however, they do not hold Canadian bank charters and cannot issue TIR-related securities or maintain escrow in a manner accepted by the Canadian Customs bond guarantee program. An Ontario LP should maintain a domestic Schedule I bank account for the capital escrow, customs account, and fuel-card settlements, while an EMI can be used for subsidiary foreign-currency disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Ontario general corporate income tax rate of 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%). A limited partnership is a flow-through entity; the headline rate applies to corporate partners on their allocated share of partnership income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by Canadian resident corporations to non-residents. For a limited partnership, profit distributions do not constitute dividends; instead, non-resident partners are taxed directly under Part I on their allocated share of Canadian-source business income. Where the LP acts through a corporate general partner and pays out profits as dividends, the 25% statutory rate applies to such corporate dividends, subject to treaty reduction (e.g., 5% for a U.S. corporate shareholder holding at least 10% voting rights, 15% for portfolio dividends).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the partnership fiscal period-end (e.g., for a December 31 year-end, due on or before May 31 of the following calendar year).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 – aggregate fair market value of controlled cross-border transactions in a taxation year triggers the mandatory filing of Form T106 and contemporaneous Local File documentation. Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when the multinational group has annual consolidated revenue equal to or greater than EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the CRA enforces economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245 of the Income Tax Act) and the limited-partner status tests under s. 96(2.2). A limited partnership used purely as an invoice or holding vehicle, with no Ontario office, no management, no bank account, no asset risk, and no physical operation of TIR vehicles, may be recharacterised as a trust or deemed a sham. If the partnership is classified as a SIFT trust, allocations to non-resident partners are recharacterised as taxable Canadian dividends subject to Part XIII. Further, the Limitation on Benefits and Principal Purpose Test clauses in Canada's modern treaties (including the Canada–US treaty protocol) may deny treaty rate reductions to a partner that lacks transactional substance, is capitalised for tax-avoidance purposes, or operates as a conduit for third-country residents.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements under Income Tax Act

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under s. 380 of the Criminal Code for tax fraud – imprisonment for up to five years; fines from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax; reassessment with gross-negligence penalties under s. 163(2), which can reach 50% of the tax understated; revocation of all CRA compliance clearance and denial of TIR carrier licences.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner corporation may be assessed personally for unremitted source withholdings (Part XIII and payroll) and GST/HST under s. 227.1 and s. 323 of the Excise Tax Act; if convicted of tax fraud, travel bans may be imposed under CRA's Flagrant Tax Evasion Program; personal net worth orders and garnishment of assets are automatic upon civil judgment.
Shell Company / Artificial Partnership Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA will apply GAAR to deny the partnership structure and recompute taxable income entirely in the hands of the Canadian general partner; the reallocation may be treated as a deemed dividend subject to Part XIII withholding; a 15% penalty under s. 163.2 of the Income Tax Act may be imposed on the wheels and advisers for promoting the tax avoidance scheme; the Canada–US treaty's Limitation on Benefits clause will serve to deny reduced rates.

Director personal liability: As the general partner is stripped of its limited-partner shield, directors become personally liable for the recomputed tax and penalties under s. 227.1; courts may pierce the corporate veil and issue a travel ban under the Canada–US Reciprocal Tax Convention; personal civil recovery for tax evasion will attach to directors' Canadian real property and bank accounts.
Anti-Money Laundering / Unregistered Money Services Business (MSB)

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) may trigger administrative monetary penalties of up to C$500,000 for individuals and C$1,000,000 for corporations; criminal violations under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code carry imprisonment of up to 10 years and forfeiture of property proceeds; failure to maintain a FINTRAC compliance regime results in mandatory officer certification and unannounced examinations.

Director personal liability: Directors who know or ought to know of the unlicensed activity may be charged as 'aiding and abetting' under s. 21 of the Criminal Code; civil restitution can be enforced against directors' personal assets under Ontario's Civil Remedies Statute; travel interdictions are issued against principals who fail to appear for AML investigations.
Customs / TIR Carnet Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Customs Act and CBSA Enforcement Guidelines, penalties may reach C$25,000 per contravention; the TIR guarantee chain (IRU through the Canadian Bankers' Association) will claim the duties and taxes evaded from the carrier, then the Ontario limited partnership is debarred from future TIR operations; serious smuggling can lead to vehicle seizure under s. 135 of the Customs Act and criminal liability for customs evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors sign personal indemnity to the TIR surety upon issuance of each carnet; failure to cover a guarantee call sails into director personal assets; repeated TIR violations cause an interdiction (travel ban) from the CBSA for all principals, and the Ontario Ministry of Transportation reviews a commercial vehicle operator's record to revoke the operating licence of the general partner corporation.
