Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a Canadian limited partnership. A Canadian LP is generally treated as a partnership for Canadian income-tax purposes: the partnership computes income, and partners include their allocated shares under applicable law. A partnership information return does not itself make the LP a corporation for income-tax purposes. There is no general hybrid-mismatch election that changes this classification; specific cross-border hybrid-mismatch and withholding rules may apply to particular arrangements. A foreign-formed entity's Canadian classification depends on its legal characteristics and applicable law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding supplies of financial services and goodwill from the sale of a business) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters for the GST/HST small-supplier test. Special rules apply to non-residents, digital-economy supplies, and specified QST registrations; the threshold is not a universal exemption from registration. A person carrying on business in Canada may have a registration obligation based on its activities even if the general small-supplier threshold is not met. For specified non-resident digital suppliers making supplies to Canadian consumers, special GST/HST and QST regimes generally use a CAD 30,000 threshold over a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating may apply to qualifying exported goods and specified services supplied to non-residents, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions. A SaaS subscription is not automatically zero-rated because a customer is foreign: analyze the recipient's residence, the nature and recipient of the supply, place-of-supply rules, and exclusions, including rules for services related to Canadian real property or tangible personal property and certain services supplied to individuals in Canada. Supplies to Canadian consumers may be taxable even where a non-resident platform or payment intermediary is used. Québec QST treatment must be analyzed separately under Québec law. A registrant may generally claim eligible input tax credits or input tax refunds for tax paid on inputs used to make taxable, including zero-rated, supplies, subject to documentation and allocation rules.

  • For exported goods, retain the applicable customs export declaration and export evidence, commercial invoices, shipping records, and bill of lading or equivalent carrier documentation; the required evidence depends on the transaction and export method.
  • For exported services or SaaS, retain contracts, customer and recipient identity and address evidence, service descriptions, invoices, payment records, and evidence supporting non-resident status and the statutory zero-rating conditions.
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general Canadian GST/HST or QST statutory prerequisite for zero-rating. Retain foreign-exchange and payment records where relevant to substantiate the transaction.

Canada and Québec do not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance, fiscal stamping, or a universal e-invoicing API for ordinary SaaS invoices. GST/QST registrants must issue invoices or other supporting documents containing prescribed information when required and retain adequate records. Québec has specific electronic-invoicing requirements in certain public-procurement contexts, including covered construction contracts; these are not a general consumer SaaS clearance mandate.

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Canada. Tax authorities may deny zero-rating, assess uncollected GST/HST or QST, interest, and applicable penalties where eligibility or records are inadequate. Gross-negligence penalties may apply to false statements or omissions; tax evasion can result in additional civil or criminal consequences, including fines and imprisonment. Assessments may be reassessed under the applicable limitation periods, with extended periods potentially available for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are generally unrestricted, with no separate 'capital account' conversion licence or prior-exchange-approval requirement. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely transferable and bank deposits in USD and other major currencies are permitted. Profit remittances from a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident partners are subject to ordinary Canadian income tax rules and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding tax on actual distributions paid to non-residents, but there is no exchange-control approval or mandatory FX moratorium. Non-resident partners must obtain a Canadian tax identification number and may need to file a Section 216 return or NR4 reporting to claim treaty relief. In practice, remittances are cleared through the Canadian Payments Association / Payments Canada rails without FX quota restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. A Quebec exporter or cross-border SaaS provider is entitled to retain export receivables in the invoiced currency, settle them through Canadian chartered banks, and convert only as needed for CAD operating expenses. The only mandatory elements are: (i) reporting transactions of CAD 10,000 or more (or equivalent) to FINTRAC where designated information applies; (ii) maintaining supporting commercial documentation for the 'underlying trade or payment purpose' under AML/CFT rules; and (iii) proper accounting treatment of foreign currency and unrealized exchange gains under IFRS and Revenue Quebec income-tax filings. There is no fixed timeline for repatriation and no mandatory percentage conversion in Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange supervisory and AML/CFT oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and in Quebec the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for financial-market participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for reliance as the sole banking layer. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMI / neobank providers (for example, Wise Platform, Adyen, Payoneer-style business accounts) are not prudentially regulated as Canadian banks under the Bank Act and cannot obtain direct OSFI-regulated deposit interchange without holding an authorized foreign-bank branch licence in Canada. They may be used as limited payment-composition and cash-in/cash-out rails, provided the primary treasury is held with a Canadian Tier-1 bank and funds are sorely settled through the licensed institution. Merchant-of-record payment service providers used for the micro-SaaS, such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy, handle card acquiring and seller-of-record tax collection, but the merchant payout account must be a Canadian bank account or a verified payment intermediary account with clear source-of-funds provenance and FINTRAC-registered money-service-business status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the CA_LP are effected through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages from the partner/adviser/investor bank to the Quebec LP's Tier-1 Canadian account. The wire must express a clear purpose code/underlying-reference such as 'Capital Contribution – Limited Partnership Subscription' or 'Partner Capital Contribution' and be supported by (i) the executed subscription agreement, (ii) a partnership capital schedule, (iii) the REQ registration extract, and (iv) for non-resident partners, an investment-industry reference or source-of-funds letter if required. Canada does not impose exchange-approval or minimum-stay requirements on equity contributions; nevertheless, if the injection is a foreign direct investment controls review may be triggered under the Investment Canada Act if the entity has Canadian assets and is acquired by a non-Canadian, but for the stated consumer micro-SaaS the compliance threshold is factually negligible. Wires should be made in equivalent CAD or economically justified USD, and tax-recording must allocate the contribution to partners' capital accounts for Revenue Quebec and income-tax filings.Settlement rails for outbound disbursements include: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct bank-to-bank wire for vendor payments, contractor payouts, and tax remittances; (ii) Payments Canada electronic funds transfer (EFT) / wire through RBC, TD, BMO, CIBC, NBC or Desjardins for Canadian-dollar settlements; (iii) and, where procurement of physical hardware or bulky software-resale goods is later required, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) opened by the Canadian bank for larger and purchase-order-backed transactions, although a Commercial L/C is inefficient and unnecessary for subscription revenue and micro-disbursements. In practice, all operating outflows flow through either the principal operating account or a segregated USD commercial account, and are protected by the SaaS controller's three-way invoice/order/payout reconciliation. Multi-currency vendor payments may be sent by MT103 from the bank's FX conversion layer; for recurring revenue, the preferred payment-rail are payouts settled by Paddle or Lemon Squeezy into the Canadian bank account following their merchant-of-record withholding of applicable sales taxes, leaving no need for manual cross-border disbursements to end customers.Deliverable forward FX contract (CAD/USD and CAD/GBP/CAD/EUR where liquid) executed with a Canadian Schedule I bank under an ISDA/credit-support annex tailored to the LP's sizeNon-deliverable forward (NDF) where the currency pair is non-convertible or lacks deep intraday hedging liquidity; permissible in Canada only through a bank counterparty and documented as a trade-based derivative, not used as speculationCross-currency swap (semi-structured) between CAD and USD to convert operating cash flows while preserving the USD receivable profile from subscription revenuePlain-vanilla FX option (call/put on USD/CAD) if the bank treasury approves the options-trading capacity and the LP establishes no speculation purposeFor the micro-SaaS context, a laddered multi-tranche forward naturally lowering monthly hedging volumes is permissible; no derivative clearing obligation applies as the LP is not a 'financial entity' for central-clearing purposes if it uses end-user exemption or bank bilateral arrangements

They may be used as limited payment-composition and cash-in/cash-out rails, provided the primary treasury is held with a Canadian Tier-1 bank and funds are sorely settled through the licensed institution. Merchant-of-record payment service providers used for the micro-SaaS, such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy, handle card acquiring and seller-of-record tax collection, but the merchant payout account must be a Canadian bank account or a verified payment intermediary account with clear source-of-funds provenance and FINTRAC-registered money-service-business status.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Quebec limited-partnership level because a partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not itself pay income tax; income is allocated to partners. If income is earned through a taxable Canadian corporation, the general combined federal and Quebec rate is generally 26.5% (15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec), and the qualifying small-business combined rate is generally 12.2% (9% federal plus 3.2% Quebec), subject to eligibility and provincial limits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. A partnership does not pay dividends; distributions generally follow partnership allocation and basis rules. A corporate partner may receive a partnership distribution without dividend characterization, but withholding and Canadian-source-income analysis remain necessary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate threshold is relevant to the T106 information return for reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents. Canadian contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required under section 247 when non-arm's-length cross-border transactions are subject to the Canadian transfer-pricing rules; Canada does not use a universal statutory Local File/Master File threshold in the same manner as some OECD jurisdictions. Large multinational enterprises may have country-by-country reporting obligations, generally using a EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold under the applicable Canadian rules and filing conditions.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE or Cayman ESR regimes. Substance remains relevant under section 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the common-law sham and artificiality doctrines, treaty principal-purpose or limitation-on-benefits provisions, beneficial-ownership analysis, transfer-pricing rules, partnership anti-avoidance rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. The LP should maintain genuine commercial activity, decision-making, records, personnel or outsourced operational control, software-development evidence, customer contracts, bank accounts, and risk-control functions consistent with its stated business model. A payment-platform account or registered address alone does not establish Canadian substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, gross-negligence penalty generally equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, civil fines, and criminal prosecution. Tax evasion convictions may result in fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years under the Income Tax Act; other statutes may impose additional sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal exposure and personal assessment for source deductions, GST/HST, QST, and other trust amounts if they failed to exercise due diligence. Civil restitution may be ordered. Travel bans are not an automatic tax remedy but may arise from bail, criminal process, immigration, or enforcement circumstances. Imprisonment is possible after conviction for intentional tax offences.
Failure to remit withholding, payroll, GST/HST, or QST trust amounts

Statutory sanction: Trust-account assessment, mandatory remittance, interest, penalties, director-liability assessment, collection action, certificate enforcement, and prosecution. GST/HST and QST penalties may be imposed independently of income-tax penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and certain indirect taxes where statutory conditions are met, subject to due-diligence defenses and limitation periods. Corporate insolvency does not automatically extinguish these liabilities.
Shell company, sham partnership, or nominee arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of partnership treatment, recharacterization of income, GAAR application, transfer-pricing adjustments, loss denial, withholding assessments, interest, penalties, beneficial-ownership reporting consequences, and possible Criminal Code or tax prosecution where deception is intentional.

Director personal liability: General limited-liability protection is not a shield against personal participation in fraud, conspiracy, sham transactions, tax evasion, money laundering, or statutory trust liabilities. Courts may grant restitution, fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment; veil piercing is exceptional and fact-dependent.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Section 247 adjustment to arm's-length terms, denial of expenses, secondary adjustments, 10% transfer-pricing penalty on unreported adjustments where statutory conditions are met, contemporaneous-documentation penalty, interest, and possible treaty competent-authority proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where conduct involves knowing misrepresentation, fraudulent tax planning, withholding failures, or breach of trust. Ordinary transfer-pricing reassessment does not by itself pierce the corporate veil.
Permanent-establishment and source misclassification

Statutory sanction: Canadian and Quebec income-tax assessments, payroll and indirect-tax registration, withholding obligations, interest, late-filing penalties, instalment penalties, and possible treaty dispute. Treaty protection depends on the actual permanent-establishment facts and profit attribution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for trust amounts and may face penalties for knowingly false filings. Criminal consequences require the applicable intent and statutory proof.
Money laundering, proceeds of crime, and sanctions evasion

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting, account restriction, asset seizure and forfeiture, administrative monetary penalties, criminal prosecution, substantial fines, and imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, Criminal Code, and applicable sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, beneficial owners, and controlling persons may be prosecuted personally. Conviction may result in forfeiture, restitution, travel restrictions imposed through criminal procedure, and imprisonment. Corporate indemnification does not eliminate criminal liability.
Beneficial-ownership and corporate-records non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Corporate or partnership penalties, filing refusal, administrative enforcement, prosecution, fines, and possible restrictions on the entity's ability to obtain or maintain registration or banking services.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize or knowingly submit false information may face personal fines and prosecution. A registered agent, accountant, or payment processor does not assume the directors' statutory responsibility.
Improper profit repatriation and withholding

Statutory sanction: Withholding-tax assessment against the Canadian payer, interest, penalties, gross-up exposure, denial of deductions, NR4 non-compliance penalties, and potential director-liability assessment where withheld amounts were not remitted.

Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed for unremitted source deductions and may face prosecution for deliberate false reporting or diversion of trust funds. Ordinary classification disputes generally create corporate liability rather than automatic personal liability.
Electronic commerce and indirect-tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Retroactive tax assessment, interest, filing penalties, gross-negligence penalties, denial of input tax credits, platform-account interruption, and possible prosecution for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for collected but unremitted GST/HST or QST and for intentional misrepresentations. Customer refunds do not automatically eliminate the tax obligation without supporting adjustment documentation.
