Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.8, as amended; Partnership Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.5; Ont. Reg. 125/20. Formation is by filing a Declaration of Limited Partnership with the Ontario Business Registry (filing fee C$60, approximately US$44).

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1 under the Limited Partnerships Act, filed through the Ontario Business Registry)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement for Ontario limited partnerships. Partners may contribute cash or property; contributions may be deferred or conditional only as set out in the limited partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Office occupancy requires compliance with the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act. A fire safety plan and adequate means of egress are required. No special structural load inspection or industrial workplace clearance applies to a non-manufacturing data/API business.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement for Ontario limited partnerships. Partners may contribute cash or property; contributions may be deferred or conditional only as set out in the limited partnership agreement.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal self-service portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); provincial registration counterpart: Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, ServiceOntario Business Information System (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry).

E-government filing portal

An Ontario limited partnership first files its Limited Partnership Declaration with ServiceOntario (no minimum capital; non-resident ownership permitted). It then obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account from the CRA by submitting Form RC1 (Application for a Business Number) or by telephone/online; non-resident partners without a Canadian SIN/BN must appoint an authorized representative (Form T183CORP / T183) and may use the CRA My Business Account once representation is registered. The CRA issues a 9-digit BN carrying the 'RT' program code as the GST/HST account identifier; the same BN also serves for payroll ('RP') and import/export ('RM') accounts, although no RM account is required for pure data/API service exports.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96): the LP computes income at partnership level, but each partner (including non-residents) is taxed on their share. For GST/HST, however, the LP is a separate 'person' and registrant under the Excise Tax Act (ETA s. 248 definition, s. 240 registration); Ontario likewise imposes no separate entity-level tax on the LP. — An Ontario limited partnership first files its Limited Partnership Declaration with ServiceOntario (no minimum capital; non-resident ownership permitted). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96): the LP computes income at partnership level, but each partner (including non-residents) is taxed on their share. For GST/HST, however, the LP is a separate 'person' and registrant under the Excise Tax Act (ETA s. 248 definition, s. 240 registration); Ontario likewise imposes no separate entity-level tax on the LP.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification is statutorily fixed. Available elections include: (i) non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period election (ITA s. 249.1(4)/(5)) with interim-period income rules; (ii) ITA s. 216(4) election permitting a non-resident partner to file a full Canadian return and be taxed on a net basis; (iii) hybrid-mismatch recharacterization is mandatory, not elective, under ITA ss. 18.2-18.8 (OECD BEPS Action 2), denying deductions where payments produce double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes in a partner's foreign jurisdiction; (iv) GST/HST elections for closely related corporations (ETA ss. 156-157) are generally unavailable to this data-services LP; (v) ITA s. 126 foreign tax credit elections may be made by individual partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: the LP (and associates) must have total taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated exports, of not more than CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and not more than CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter. For non-resident suppliers, only taxable supplies made in Canada count toward the threshold; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with returns due within 29 days. A non-resident provider of API/digital services crossing CAD 30,000 of Canadian-sourced supplies must register and collect HST on supplies to Canadian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act: (i) goods exported by the supplier under ETA s. 20 (continuous-journey export or documented export within 60 days); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person under Sch. VI Part V s. 23, provided the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant and the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada - web-scraping, data extraction, and API gateway services qualify either as data-processing services supplied to non-residents or as general services to non-residents where the recipient's billing address is outside Canada; (iii) exported digital/incorporeal property qualifies where the supplier retains prescribed evidence. The supplier must obtain and retain non-resident status evidence (e.g., CRA Form GST 174 Certificate of Status, a foreign tax registration certificate, or invoice/billing address records) within 180 days of billing.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) or carrier export report for any tangible merchandise shipped
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing a continuous export journey and delivery outside Canada where goods are physically exported by the LP
  • Commercial contract, API licensing agreement, and zero-rated invoices clearly identifying the non-resident customer (for services zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI s. 23)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or settlement statement evidencing that the export consideration was received from outside Canada
  • CRA GST 493 export records checklist and customer GST/HST registration status evidence (Form GST 174 or foreign equivalent)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval stamping, or mandatory digital stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework operational since 2023. Mandatory elements are: electronic filing of GST/HST returns by most registrants (GST/HST NETFILE or EDI/GST34 transmission); retention of books and records in accessible electronic form (ETA s. 286); and use of CRA APIs (Represent a Client / Application-to-Application gateway) for third-party filers. In audit, the CRA may require an electronic 'Schedule of Canadian and Foreign Export Transactions' (Form GST 120) reconciling all zero-rated export billings.

The CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies within the normal 4-year limitation period (unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or a signed waiver exists), clawing back input tax credits claimed on exported services and assessing uncollected HST plus interest where export documentation is defective. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: s. 285 (false statements) of 2x the tax sought to be evaded; s. 296 (gross negligence) of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net understatement; and s. 280 for failure to collect, remit, or report. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327(1) carries fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment, with general partners jointly and severally liable for the LP's obligations (ETA s. 325). Under the Income Tax Act, ITA s. 163(2) imposes a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax, s. 239 provides parallel criminal liability, and artificial export-restructuring arrangements may be struck down under the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a liberalized foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Act administered by the Bank of Canada. Current account transactions including profit remittances from Canadian corporations are freely convertible at market rates. Capital account transactions require prior Bank of Canada approval for certain investments exceeding prescribed thresholds. Profit remittance for compliant entities faces minimal hurdles but may involve enhanced due diligence and documentation for high-risk sectors such as digital data services, including robust AML filings and tax reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-based exports such as web scraping and proxy API gateway operations do not constitute goods exports and therefore trigger no mandatory surrender requirements under the Export and Import Controls Act. Foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated to Canada within 30 days for most transactions and recorded for Canadian tax purposes. No statutory conversion ratios apply; all transactions occur at prevailing market rates with full compliance to FINTRAC monitoring obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility rating. Stripe is widely utilized by Canadian businesses for cross-border payments. Offshore digital EMIs face regulatory constraints under Canada's Payment Services Act and FINTRAC guidelines; full licensing may be required for certain crypto-related services involving USDC.
