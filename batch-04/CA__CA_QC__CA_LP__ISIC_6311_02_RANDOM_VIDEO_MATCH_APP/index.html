Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); provincial income/QST collection delegated to Revenu Québec via CLIC portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/CLIC/).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership first obtains a CRA business number (BN) and GST/HST account using Business Registration Online. If the LP carries on commercial activity in Québec, it must also register with the Registraire des entreprises for a Quebec Enterprise Identifier (NEQ) and open a QST account with Revenu Québec, either online or by postal filing of the applicable registration forms. Non-resident partners that receive Canadian-source income must obtain or confirm an individual/corporate TIN and, where needed, request tariff/export program account codes (RM) for customs. The LP must maintain a Canadian-recognized agency or access point for service of notices and may appoint a representative in Québec where disclosed partner operations are managed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - a Québec Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax, not a separate taxpayer; income/loss flows to partners. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on their share of Canadian-source partnership income (Part I tax, subject to treaty), and to Part XIII withholding where payments are distributions or deemed interest/royalty income. For GST/QST, however, the partnership is treated as a separate registrant/person for supplies made by the partnership.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; the LP is automatically flow-through. The partners may elect a non-calendar fiscal period under ITA s.249.0.8 if all partners are non-SME and conditions are met. For hybrid mismatch purposes, ITA s.18.4 operates automatically and does not provide a domestic check-the-box election. A limited partnership may make a partnership VAT grouping election under ETA s.273 or the provincial equivalent where conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters from taxable supplies (including specified digital goods or remote services) to customers in Canada, for a non-resident supplier; a similar CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST registration by remote foreign digital service providers selling to consumers in Québec. Domestic GST/HST general small-supplier threshold remains CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies by businesses carrying on taxable activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of a service or intangible personal property (including digital real-time video-match/SIP/telephony functionality) is zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, where the supply is not made in Canada and is not consumed in Canada; if supplied to a person ordinarily resident in Canada or where the location/technical means indicate Canadian use, 14.975% combined tax must be charged. For goods exported from Canada, zero-rating requires actual physical export and documentary proof that the exporter exported, sold and delivered tangible or digital product outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/B13B) for any tangible media/device carrying the application or cryptographic keys, filed with CBSA.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or carrier proof from warehouse to final destination outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103/Paymentwall and Apple IAP merchant settlement statements evidencing receipt of export proceeds from non-Canadian counterparties.
  • Signed zero-rating certificate (GST/HST Status Certificate form) or customer self-declaration confirming non-resident status and foreign consumption.
  • Detailed invoice and transaction-level logs matching user IP/IMEI/device residency to payment rails, supporting zero-rated treatment.

Canada has no mandatory continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Electronic invoices and records are valid without pre-authorization if all prescribed facts are clearly stated. CRA accepts e-invoices (PDF/XML/EDI) and supports GST/HST NETFILE filing; Revenu Québec accepts QST returns via CLIC. No government API clearance interface is required for issuance, but the supplier must retain audit-quality transaction logs with timestamp, IP geo-location, recipient payment method and bank settlement data.

CRA/Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating retroactively and assess GST/QST plus default interest if export documentation is incomplete, if the recipient is shown to be in Canada, or if funds receivable are not repatriated and evidenced within the 180-day commercial benchmark. Reassessment can extend beyond the four-year normal period where false documentation or ignorance of facts is established. Knowing, fraudulent or wilful statements in support of zero-rating attract penalties of up to 25% of net federal tax assessed (Excise Tax Act s.280), civil penalties of the greater of $250 and 25% of the GST amount in issue, denial of refund claims, s.239 criminal fines on conviction of up to 200% of evaded tax and up to five years' imprisonment; a promoter or registrant may also be named in the CRA's Voluntary Disclosures or evasion program.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible. Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no restrictions on current or capital account transactions. For the Quebec Limited Partnership (CA_LP) profile: inward capital contributions are unrestricted and free from approval; remittance of operating profits to non-resident partners is not subject to Canadian withholding tax as a distribution, but non-resident partners are taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their allocable share of Canadian-source partnership income and must file Canadian and Quebec (Revenu Québec) returns. Care must be taken to avoid Part XIII withholding on payments characterized as management fees, interest, royalties, or rents payable to non-resident partners. No measurable profit-remittance hurdle exists beyond standard tax filing, transfer-pricing documentation, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no repatriation or conversion obligation has since been reintroduced. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in offshore or domestic accounts without restriction. FINTRAC requires the reporting of cross-border currency or monetary instrument movements of CAD 10,000 or more and requires regulated financial institutions to record and report international electronic fund transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more. GST/HST zero-rating applies to exported services of the video match app; there is no FX surrender mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX administration and prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction monitoring and reporting administration by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act, the Bank Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no centralized exchange-control licensing authority; the Bank of Canada manages the floating CAD and maintains the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Lynx high-value payment rails.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (conditional). Canada has no EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; offshore digital EMIs operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC and, where performing retail payment functions, register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada (regime in force since November 2024). They are legally barred from accepting deposits in Canada unless authorized under the Bank Act. Use of offshore EMIs is viable for operational sub-accounts, multi-currency liquidity pockets, and settlement of Apple IAP / Paymentwall balances, but they are not acceptable as the primary clearing bank or for capital escrow holding. Supporting the medium rating is the enhanced regulatory scrutiny of video chat P2P flows and the absence of CDIC deposit insurance on EMI balances.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Quebec LP entity level (fiscally transparent); a taxable Canadian corporation is subject to a combined 26.5% federal/provincial headline rate (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends to non-residents; tax treaties may reduce the rate to 5% (corporate 10%+ shareholder) or 15% (portfolio dividends).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions triggers mandatory T106/TP-1151 reporting and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; CRA risk-based Local/Master File requests typically arise for transactions exceeding CAD 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations in Quebec or Canada. However, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s.245) and Quebec's analogous anti-avoidance rule (Taxation Act s.1079.1) deny tax and treaty benefits where an LP or any partner engages in artificial transactions without genuine commercial substance. The LP must be validly constituted, maintain real activities (e.g., actual video-matching infrastructure and staff), and each partner's rights/duties must be exercised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty under Income Tax Act s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction under s.239 faces a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years' imprisonment; Quebec parallel penalties under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and knowingly participating officers are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions; criminal charges can lead to personal restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans during prosecution, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.247(4) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000), plus interest and reassessment; late filing penalties for T106 of up to CAD 5,000; Quebec equivalents under Taxation Act s.1087.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners who knowingly approve false TP studies or misstate income can be assessed gross negligence penalties personally; in cases of fraudulent conduit arrangements, courts may disregard the corporate form and impose personal liability.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment (Income Tax Act s.245 / Quebec Taxation Act s.1079.1) denying the treaty and tax benefit, plus interest and a 25% penalty on the tax avoided; Canada's anti-treaty-shopping rules and LOB/PPT provisions disallow undue treaty relief.

Director personal liability: If the LP is found to be a sham or fraudulent conduit, the court can pierce the corporate veil and hold the general partner/directors personally liable for unpaid tax and joint tort damages; travel bans and director/professional disqualification orders may be imposed.
AML, MSB Registration, and Beneficial Ownership Reporting

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Quebec registry false/omitted declarations subject to fines up to CAD 5,000 and registry cancellation; criminal penalties include imprisonment for obstruction.

Director personal liability: Officers and general partners are personally exposed to AML penalties, prohibition orders, and criminal liability where they knowingly aided or failed to prevent money laundering through the payment rails; civil restitution and travel restrictions can accompany criminal AML proceedings.
