Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an Ontario limited partnership (CA_LP), virtual offices are generally permissible for non-distinguishing administrative functions, but the LP must maintain a registered office address in Ontario at which legal process and official notices may be served; the registered office must be a physical address in Ontario capable of accepting service during normal business hours; a virtual office provider that supplies a physical street address, mail receipt, and forwarding services to Ontario is permissible for the statutory registered office as long as the provider is authorized to accept service and the address is not solely a post office box; however, to establish commercial substance for Canadian Revenue Agency (CRA) and cross-border tax treaty eligibility, the LP should maintain substantive physical premises in Ontario including one or more staffed offices, a client-facing reception, secure data rooms and telecommunications infrastructure; virtual office alone will not satisfy residency-based commercial substance tests for Canada-U.S. treaty benefits, nor will it satisfy Canada Revenue Agency's general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and treaty shopping analysis; for the AI data labeling BPO business targeting ISIC 8220/NAICS 518210, the CRA may examine substance based on payroll, management activities, decision-making, data handling and server infrastructure; a virtual office at a permitted law-firm location may establish minimal legal domicile for LP registration, but 'specialized virtual office service' that offers merely a registered agent will be classified as low substance and carries a material risk for audit exposure; accordingly, virtual office permissibility is 'Conditionally Permissible' — acceptable for registry and service of process, but commercial substance should be fulfilled via a separately leased operational office, local data processing facility, or Ontario-based subcontracted operations center; the Ontario Business Registry will require an Ontario physical mailing address for the LP's head office, which may differ from registered office as long as a statement of alternate address is included; for bank account opening for the target payment rails (MassPay/PayPal/Wise), a virtual office address is often disqualified by KYC departments unless real lease and utility bill evidence is produced; thus the recommended structure is to lease a shared operational office in Toronto (Ontario) for substance and to list that address as the chief executive office in the LP declaration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery via the Ontario Business Registry for provincial LP registration and Employer Health Tax (EHT) accounts.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the Ontario Limited Partnership registration number under the Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990, c. L.16) from the Ontario Business Registry. Step 2: Register with CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 Request for a Business Number (entity type 'Partnership', requesting program account RT for GST/HST) or through BRO. Step 3: File the T5013 Partnership Information Return within five months after the LP's fiscal year-end, including Schedule 50 partner-level detail; this return is mandatory where the LP has non-resident or corporate partners. Step 5: Open a Canadian bank account in the LP's name and retain payment-rail settlement statements (Mass_PayPal_Payout, Wise, SWIFT MT103) as inward remittance evidence. Step 6: Confirm the LP's permanent establishment address and notify CRA of any changes to the designated member resident in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada); no entity-level federal or Ontario income tax is imposed; income/loss flows to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is itself a distinct 'person' registered as a principal. Non-resident partners are subject to Canadian Part I tax on their share of Canadian-source partnership income and may be taxable in Ontario on the Ontario permanent establishment share, subject to any applicable treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; partnership transparency is mandatory. Available elections and designations include: (i) GST/HST section 179 ETA election for a resident recipient to self-assess GST/HST on services acquired from a non-resident subcontractor; (ii) GST/HST section 273 ETA election treating supplies made by a non-resident through a Canadian agent as supplies of the agent; (iii) Income Tax Act section 216 election for a non-resident to file a Canadian return on specified Canadian-source income; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.4 and 94.1, which disallow a deduction for payments characterized inconsistently due to transparency of the LP in a foreign jurisdiction; (v) designation of the LP's fiscal period and income allocation ratio on the T5013 under ITA subsection 96(1)(b); (vi) voluntary small-supplier registration under ETA section 240 to accelerate input tax credit recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is effective from the month in which the threshold is crossed. A partnership below the threshold is a 'small supplier' and may, but need not, register; a non-resident supplier of digital services to Canada is subject to the same C$30,000 threshold under the simplified registration regime effective July 1, 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may apply 0% GST/HST under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act to a supply of AI data-labeling, bounding-box annotation, or RLHF curation services made to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant, provided the supply is not for consumption in Canada, is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not rendered to an individual present in Canada. The LP must maintain documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status: contract and purchase order, the recipient's foreign business address and country of incorporation, and the service delivery model (digital transmission logs or physical export records). If the recipient is a non-resident GST/HST registrant, zero-rating remains available unless the service falls within the Canadian use/consumption exceptions; where the supply is made to a Canadian registrant for its commercial activities, the normal 13% HST rate applies.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed through the Canada Border Services Agency / Canada Export Reporting System) — required for physical media or dataset-carrying hardware exports; not applicable to purely electronic deliveries
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill consigned to the non-resident customer, plus CRA Form GST190 Proof of Export or third-party carrier manifests corroborating export date and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 statements and Mass_PayPal_Payout/Wise settlement records evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the LP's Canadian bank account within the 180-day benchmark

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory B2B e-invoicing, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1,000,000 and electronic remittance for large remitters above C$1,500,000. Registrants must retain invoices, agreements, and supporting export documentation for six years under ETA section 223. XML/PDF e-invoices are accepted where they preserve documentary integrity; Pan-Canadian e-invoice standards apply only to government procurement. Ontario Business Registry filings are mandatory for provincial registration and updates.

If CRA determines the 0% rate was unsubstantiated, the LP is liable to self-assess the uncollected 13% HST plus daily interest under ETA section 280. A false or omitted statement made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence triggers a penalty equal to the greater of C$100 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; wilful evasion on indictment is punishable by fines of up to C$25,000 plus 100% of the evaded tax and up to two years' imprisonment (ETA section 281). CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, fraud, or omission of a required T5013 Schedule 50 partner item is established. Late or deficient T5013 filings attract ITA section 162(2.01) penalties of C$25 per day to a maximum of C$2,500, with an additional penalty of up to C$100 for a partner who knowingly makes a false statement in the return. CRA also applies transfer-pricing analytics under ITA section 247 at the partner level to verify that the LP performs substantive activities in Canada and that export fees are arm's length.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility is maintained for CAD under the Currency Act. Profit remittances, partnership allocations, and loan repayments to non-resident partners are not gated by Bank of Canada or OSFI approval. The sole cross-border constraints are tax compliance obligations: Part I income tax on the Ontario LP's Canadian-source income allocated to non-resident partners (through the T5013 partnership information return) and Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on certain passive payments (interest, royalties, management fees) that are not arm's-length business profits. The Investment Canada Act applies net-benefit review only to material foreign acquisitions of Canadian businesses above monetary thresholds; a data-labeling BPO acquisition is typically below the threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement applies to export receipts. Canadian entities may retain, convert, or repatriate foreign currency earnings freely. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central bank settlement requirement. Applicable compliance duties are limited to FINTRAC's large cross-border currency transaction reports (threshold: CAD 10,000 or equivalent), MSB-related record-keeping, and the bank's own anti-money-laundering (AML) monitoring of incoming SWIFT credits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); no dedicated foreign exchange administration body exists as Canada operates an unrestricted liberalized FX regime.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario) applied at the corporate partner level; the Canadian limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and has no separate corporate income tax rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable to distributions from a fiscally transparent Canadian limited partnership. If a Canadian-resident corporation pays dividends, the Part XIII statutory withholding tax rate is 25%, subject to treaty reduction (typically 5% for substantial corporate ownership and 15% otherwise under Canada-U.S.).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is expected where cross-border non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed C$1,000,000 in aggregate in the fiscal period, or C$250,000 with a single non-resident counterparty; Form T106 must be filed annually for reportable transactions, and CRA may demand documentation even below these thresholds under s.247(6).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations', but the GAAR, the s.247(2) transfer-pricing recharacterization rule, and Ontario at-risk rules require real commercial functions, staffing, funding, and risk assumption. A non-resident limited partner that exercises management control over the Ontario LP may lose limited liability and be deemed to carry on a business in Canada, with income fully taxable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / concealment of Canadian-source revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with interest; gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of understated federal tax (minimum C$100); criminal prosecution under ITA ss.238/239 may impose fines from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: CRA may pursue directors of a corporate general partner under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted income tax, GST/HST and source deductions; courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and civil restitution judgments when corporate interests are used to conceal deliberate non-compliance.
Shell arrangement / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits and loss allocations under the GAAR; transfer-pricing recharacterization under ITA s.247(2); 10% penalty on adjustments under s.247(3); interest to date; Ontario may reassess the partnership as a taxable entity if substance is absent.

Director personal liability: A limited partner who participates in management loses the Ontario Limited Partnership Act liability shield and becomes personally liable for partnership obligations; directors of the corporate general partner who approve artificial arrangements may face director's liability assessments, civil restitution, and personal liability in fraud proceedings.
AML / beneficial-ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of LP registration and loss of filing status; false declarations to the Ontario registry attract administrative fines; FINTRAC/PCMLTFA non-compliance can result in monetary penalties up to C$100,000 for individuals and C$500,000 for entities, with criminal money-laundering penalties up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Registered general partners and their directors are publicly identified; deliberate AML abuse pierces the limited-liability shield and allows criminal prosecution, asset seizure, travel bans, and personal monetary restitution against directors who knowingly facilitate laundering through data-labelling contracts.
Transfer-pricing mispricing / cross-border profit stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices to arm's-length terms under s.247(2); penalty equal to 10% of the adjustment under s.247(3) plus interest; failure to provide documentation within 90 days triggers additional penalties of C$1,000 to C$5,000 per failure and may eliminate certain relief under s.247(6).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T106 returns or participate in offshore invoice padding may be jointly and severally assessed under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted withholding; CRA may seek personal civil restitution, travel bans, and, in fraudulent cases, imprisonment for directors as active participants.
