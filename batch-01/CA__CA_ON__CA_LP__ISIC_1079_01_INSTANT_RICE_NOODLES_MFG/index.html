Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST and Business Number registration through CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance - Retail Sales Tax (RST) and Ontario Business Registry for provincial licensing

        
E-government filing portal
A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), a nine-digit account number with a corresponding GST/HST account prefix 'RT' if the partnership is engaged in taxable supplies. Registration is mandatory once the partnership exceeds the small supplier threshold (CDN $30,000 in a single quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters). Foreign entities may apply online via CRA My Business Account using Form RC1, or submit Form RC391 (GST/HST rebate and registration for non-residents). Supporting documentation must include partnership registration evidence, the partnership's constitutive documents, and a declaration of the non-resident's Canadian representative (Form RC59, Business Information, and particulars). A separate Ontario RST registration may be required for taxable retail sales activities not subject to HST, administered via the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
Canada does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box classification. Limited partnerships are fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax, while the partnership itself is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for common-law partnership supplies between closely related corporations (not typical for LP interests); (ii) partnership capital cost allowance election pursuant to subsection 13(7.1) and section 1100 of the Income Tax Act; (iii) foreign affiliate and hybrid mismatch elections under Part XXXIX / section 163.2 of the Income Tax Regulations; and (iv) joint GST/HST election for partnership property or services under Form RC4590, Elections for GST/HST. No federal or provincial election permits entity-level corporate taxation of an LP without restructuring into a corporation.

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies on the supply of goods exported by a registrant (section 23, Excise Tax Act), provided the goods are shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, or supplied to a non-resident person and exported as part of the supply. For instant rice noodle products, the manufacturer must demonstrate that physical possession and ownership transferred in Canada and goods were exported unconditionally, directly by the supplier or through an export agent. Supplies of services , including manufacturing services, are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the GST/HST and the service is performed in Canada, unless the service relates to real property located in Canada or goods to which the non-resident has title. Offshore service zero-rating may also apply under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for services supplied to a non-resident person, subject to exclusions.

The CRA may deny zero-rating and assess tax, interest, and penalties if the exporter fails to substantiate export with contemporaneous evidence. For fraudulent export zero-rating or false claims, the Excise Tax Act provides for civil penalties of up to 25% of the amount claimed (section 280) and gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the understated net tax (section 285). Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations; there is no exchange-control law, no central-bank approval requirement, and no distinction between current and capital account transactions for foreign exchange purposes. Inward capital contributions to an Ontario limited partnership, shareholder loans, trade settlements, repatriation of partnership profits, dividends, and royalties are all freely convertible into CAD or any foreign currency. The Investment Canada Act applies only to foreign acquisitions/establishments through a net-benefit or national-security review mechanism and does not restrict currency flows. Partnership allocations to non-resident limited partners are freely remittable but remain subject to Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement) and to CRA reporting through T5013, NR4, and applicable FATCA/CRS disclosures.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Canada. Canadian exporters, including Ontario limited partnerships deriving revenue from retort river snail noodle (Luosifen) products, are not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender export receipts within any timeline and may retain and settle in any freely chosen currency under commercial agreements. Foreign-currency export proceeds are reportable only for statistical purposes (Statistics Canada Export/Import Reporting Program and balance-of-payments surveys), for corporate and GST/HST compliance under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, and for bank FINTRAC large-transaction reports where single EFT receipts exceed CAD 10,000.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); trade and capital-account policy administered by the Department of Finance Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium. Global fintech EMIs operating in Canada, including Wise Canada, Payoneer, Airwallex, Convera (formerly Western Union Business Solutions), and OFX, are registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and may offer CAD payout/inward collection and multi-currency wallets. Their viability for high-volume food-manufacturing treasury flows is only Medium because: (1) they impose per-transaction velocity and daily volume caps unsuitable for seasonal raw-material procurement; (2) they generally cannot service documentary Letters of Credit, banker's acceptances, or SWIFT MT700/MT707 trade instruments; (3) Canadian Schedule I banks increasingly reject EMI-originated or EMI-beneficiary wires as high-risk correspondent flows; and (4) PIPEDA and FINTRAC rules prohibit EMI accounts from being used as unlicensed payment aggregators for unrelated third-party supplier chains. The Ontario LP should therefore restrict EMI usage to ancillary disbursements (minor office expenses, market-testing payments, travel) and run operational trade settlement and treasury clearing exclusively through chartered Schedule I banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners and intercompany shareholder loans must be routed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers to the Ontario LP's designated CAD or USD operational account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using the beneficiary bank's institution number, transit number, and account number. Canada does not require a central-bank FX purpose code; however, the receiving bank must file FINTRAC large-value EFT reports for any transaction over CAD 10,000 and will require the remitter to complete a source-of-funds declaration. The SWIFT remittance field should state 'Capital Contribution / Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date] / Partner Reference [ID]' without misleading trade descriptions. For aggregate cross-border funding exceeding CAD 100,000, the LP and corporate partners must monitor CRA reporting under T5013 (Partnership Information Return), Schedule 50 (partnership ownership) and any applicable T1134 (associated foreign affiliate) obligations; no Bank of Canada approval or registration is required. Verification procedures: SWIFT gpi end-to-end tracking of the MT103, daily bank statement reconciliation against the capital-call ledger, and booking of any USD-to-CAD conversion at the Bank of Canada daily average exchange rate or the agreed treasury rate.Outbound trade settlements for imported raw materials, retort packaging, seasonings, and machinery should be cleared via: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers with full invoice and contract references for transactions below CAD 250,000; (2) SWIFT MT700 irrevocable at-sight Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit, optionally confirmed by a Canadian bank, for high-value seasonal procurement from Chinese, Vietnamese, and Thai suppliers, with MT707 amendments, MT734 refusal advice, and MT754 acceptance advice as applicable; (3) SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) priority rails to obtain same-day traceability and status tracking; and (4) Payments Canada ACSS and LVTS rails for domestic Ontario disbursements (trade creditors, logistics providers, and utility payments). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. Global fintech EMIs operating in Canada, including Wise Canada, Payoneer, Airwallex, Convera (formerly Western Union Business Solutions), and OFX, are registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and may offer CAD payout/inward collection and multi-currency wallets. Their viability for high-volume food-manufacturing treasury flows is only Medium because: (1) they impose per-transaction velocity and daily volume caps unsuitable for seasonal raw-material procurement; (2) they generally cannot service documentary Letters of Credit, banker's acceptances, or SWIFT MT700/MT707 trade instruments; (3) Canadian Schedule I banks increasingly reject EMI-originated or EMI-beneficiary wires as high-risk correspondent flows; and (4) PIPEDA and FINTRAC rules prohibit EMI accounts from being used as unlicensed payment aggregators for unrelated third-party supplier chains. The Ontario LP should therefore restrict EMI usage to ancillary disbursements (minor office expenses, market-testing payments, travel) and run operational trade settlement and treasury clearing exclusively through chartered Schedule I banks.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
26.5% combined federal (15%) + Ontario general (11.5%) applied to allocated taxable income of corporate limited partners; the Ontario LP itself is fiscally transparent and has no entity-level corporate income tax.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under ITA s. 212(2). For an Ontario LP, distributions to partners are not dividends; distributions of previously taxed income or return of capital carry no withholding, but allocations of Canadian-source interest, rents, royalties, and management fees paid to non-resident partners trigger 25% Part XIII withholding unless treaty-reduced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate threshold for filing Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) under ITA s. 233.2; no statutory de-minimis for contemporaneous s. 247 documentation, but CRA's operational guidelines expect master-file and local-file documentation for controlled transactions with a gross value exceeding CAD 1,000,000 per year, including benchmarking analysis, contract summary, and functional-risk profile.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute (unlike UAE), but the CRA applies the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and a common-law substance-over-form doctrine. An Ontario LP is respected for tax purposes only if it is validly formed, has a resident general partner exercising management authority, maintains a registered office, carries on genuine commercial production activity, and has the economic capacity to realize the attributed profits; non-resident partners seeking treaty benefits on LP income must satisfy beneficial-ownership and any-limitation-on-benefits clauses; a shell LP used solely to route royalties or shift income to a low-tax jurisdiction will be disregarded and reassessed under GAAR, with a 25% penalty on the tax benefit and potential a 50% gross-negligence penalty.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s. 163(2) of 50% of the federal tax understated; criminal conviction under ITA s. 239(1) - fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, or if that amount is not ascertainable, a fine of CAD 25,000 to CAD 100,000, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; for summary conviction, a fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 2 years imprisonment; forfeiture orders and court-ordered restitution are mandatory; the tax is collectible with 8-year limitation for prosecution by indictment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unpaid Part I and Part XIII taxes under ITA s. 227.1 with a 2-year due-diligence defence; directors who sign false returns face criminal prosecution as 'persons' under s. 239, personal restitution orders, seizure of personal assets, travel bans under CRA's collection program, and extradition risk under the Canada-US Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty.

      

      
      

        Aggressive Tax Avoidance / Shell Company / GAAR
        
Statutory sanction: GAAR (ITA s. 245) denies the tax benefit and the CRA reassesses the full additional tax; penalty under s. 245(4) of 25% of the tax benefit denied; additional s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% can be stacked; the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act allows the provincial regulator to dissolve the LP retroactively; Ontario Securities Commission penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for misleading filings; suspension of all tax treaty relief for 60 months.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or acquiesce in the sham are personally assessed under s. 227.1 for the repaid amount, and the 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine exposes the general partner's directors to civil restitution from creditors and suppliers who relied on the LP's apparent substance; personal orders can impose travel bans and prohibit the director from acting as a director of any Ontario company under the OBCA s. 118(2).

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing / T106 Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: CRA re-characterizes the transaction using the best arm's-length method; penalty under ITA s. 247(3) equal to the lesser of 10% of the gross adjustment and CAD 24,000 when the adjustment exceeds the higher of CAD 1,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; late-filing penalty for T106 of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500), plus a CAD 500 or CAD 2,500 penalty for a first or repeated failure; additional 50% gross-negligence penalty where documents are fabricated; denial of loss carryforwards allocated from the LP.

        
Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who direct pricing decisions are jointly liable for the s. 247(3) penalty because the penalty is deemed to be a tax debt of the partnership; the 2-year due-diligence defence is unavailable if the director was directly involved in the controlled transaction; the CRA may also issue a requirement to pay (s. 224.1.1) against personal bank accounts of directors who knowingly participated, and travel bans apply while the debt remains outstanding.

      

      
      

        Part XIII Withholding Failure on Non-Resident Distributions
        
Statutory sanction: The LP is jointly and severally liable for the full 25% of the gross amount paid or credited, plus interest at the prescribed rate (currently 5% per annum escalated quarterly); penalty under ITA s. 227(9) of 10% of the amount required to be withheld (20% for a repeat failure); failure to remit for 10+ days triggers a 10% remittance penalty; the non-resident partner's treaty relief is suspended until the withholding deficit is paid.

        
Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable under s. 227.1 for the entire unremitted Part XIII amount, without the standard due-diligence defence if they failed to obtain the TRC or NR6; the CRA can seize personal assets and impose a travel ban under the Income Tax Act's 'Canada Border Services Agency holdback' protocol for unpaid withholding liabilities exceeding CAD 25,000.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA / FINTRAC)
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation for a corporation (and the LP is treated as a reporting entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act); on indictment, individuals face a fine up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; the FINTRAC can also impose a Notice of Violation with penalties of up to CAD 100,000 for misleading records; regulatory suspension of the business licence for the food manufacturing operation under the Safe Food for Canadians Act.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly or negligently approve cash-handling procedures that breach FINTRAC requirements are personally liable for the administrative penalty; criminal liability extends to directors under s. 49 of the PCMLTFA; additional sanctions include travel bans, asset freezes under the Criminal Code s. 11, and a public ban from holding directorships in any federally regulated financial institution for up to 10 years.
