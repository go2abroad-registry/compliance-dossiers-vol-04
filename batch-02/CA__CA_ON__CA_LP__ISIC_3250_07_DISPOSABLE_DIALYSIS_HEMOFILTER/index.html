Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario provincial tax administration is the Ontario Ministry of Finance, with entity registration through the Ontario Business Registry; the CRA remains the national tax authority for federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-based limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The application must include legal name of the partnership, jurisdiction of formation, Canadian business address, formation date, names and addresses of all general and limited partners, principal business activity (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112), and any non-resident partner details. After CRA approval, the 9-digit BN is issued, and program accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and, if applicable, excise tax (RE) are assigned. The Ontario Business Registry registration may be required for provincial purposes, but the BN serves as the common tax identification number for federal, provincial, and municipal tax interactions, including customs and export declarations. Non-resident partners may need to file additional returns or provide treaty-based information before registration is fully processed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' election. A Canadian limited partnership is statutorily treated as a pass-through entity for income tax purposes and as a separate person for GST/HST purposes. Specific elections exist under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, including the GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s.170), shared liability election for partnership registrations, income tax partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s.249.1(10)), and the non-resident partner election to file on net rental income (ITA s.216). Cross-border hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4–18.6 are mandatory and provide no elective relief to reclassify a partnership as a taxable entity for Canadian federal tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold; ETA s.148). Once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, supplies of tangible personal property made by a registrant are zero-rated when the goods are delivered to a recipient outside Canada or to a common carrier for export. The export must be substantiated by documentary evidence retained by the exporter. For the manufacture of hollow fiber dialyzers under ISIC 3250, exports of finished devices to non-resident buyers qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate, provided the goods are physically shipped from Canada, exported in an unmodified condition, and the supplier keeps conclusive proof of export, including shipping documents, commercial invoice, and payment evidence. Certain services supplied in connection with exported goods or performed wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13 filed or transmitted through CERS)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air waybill naming the exporter and consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Transport contract and proof of delivery to the overseas destination

No statutory mandate currently exists for real-time digital fiscal clearance, API-based government invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Tax invoices must include the prescribed information under ETA s.169(1) and may be issued electronically. For exports, the Canada Border Services Agency requires electronic export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), with API-based transmission available for high-volume filers; digital stamping is not a Canadian GST/HST requirement.

CRA may reassess zero-rated exports within the standard four-year limitation period, or indefinitely where fraud, wilful default, or misrepresentation is present, and may disallow input tax credits, claw back previously refunded amounts, and impose interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: a gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the tax misstated or sought to be evaded (s.280); a penalty of $100 plus interest for failure to provide required documentation (s.281); penalties for false statements under s.285; and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Export zero-rating will also be scrutinized when foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day benchmark; unreasonable delays or discrepancies may trigger a CRA audit and retrospective reassessment, with additional penalties for documentation failures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely and fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for inward or outward transfers, no surrender requirements, and no restriction on profit, dividend, capital, or distribution repatriation by non-residents. For the Ontario Limited Partnership (CA_LP), Canadian-source active business income allocated to a non-resident limited partner is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) as income from carrying on business in Canada; distributions of such income are not subject to Part XIII withholding tax, but the non-resident partner must self-assess Canadian tax and file the required return. Return-of-capital distributions are non-taxable and freely remittable. Passive-type payments (interest, rents, royalties, management fees) to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding, subject to treaty reduction. Inbound equity injection establishing a new Canadian business by a non-Canadian investor triggers an Investment Canada Act (ICA) notification to ISED (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada); acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business is subject to the WTO-investor enterprise-value net-benefit review threshold (for 2024 approximately CAD 1.334 billion; the annually published threshold should be confirmed) and a reduced economic-benefit review (approximately CAD 5 million direct asset value) for foreign state-owned enterprises. Large international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement that export invoices be settled through Canadian accounts. The CA_LP may hold USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency proceeds in resident or non-resident accounts, and may convert to CAD at will through any licensed bank branch or its home banking platform. FINTRAC reporting applies only to large cross-border fund transfers (CAD 10,000 or more), not to the act of holding or converting export receipts. No central-bank approval is required to open foreign-currency accounts domestically.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and lender of last resort; exchange-control authority rests with the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada) under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no specialised foreign-exchange administration agency. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) supervises AML/ATF compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) regulates and supervises federally chartered banks. Payments infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada (Lynx for large-value CAD, ACSS for retail clearing), with the Bank of Canada providing final settlement accounts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - supplementary rail only. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic money institution (EMI) passporting regime. Non-bank cross-border payment providers are regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA and must register with FINTRAC; core retail payment functions are also subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force 6 November 2024). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may legally serve the CA_LP for small-value cross-border collections and disbursements if they hold a valid Canadian MSB registration or operate through FINTRAC-licensed agents, but they do not have Canadian bank status, do not confer CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and offer limited protection for large capital escrows. Legal constraint: the LP must maintain its primary capital escrow and operating accounts with a domestic Schedule I bank; EMI relationships require a General Partner resolution, must remain subordinate to the primary banking relationship, and all EMI flows must be fully traceable to the LP's CRA books. Offshore EMIs settled from tax-haven jurisdictions may trigger enhanced due diligence at the core Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the remitting foreign investor (limited partner) to the CA_LP's Canadian account at a Schedule I bank. Beneficiary must be the exact legal name of the Limited Partnership; bank details must include beneficiary SWIFT/BIC, Canadian clearing code (institution number + branch/transit + account number), and payment reference 'CAP-CALL-2025-01 - LP Capital Contribution' linking to the capital call notice and capital-account ledger. Recommend ISO 20022 purpose code CCAP (Capital Building) for pure equity-type capital contributions, or CORT (Trade Settlement) where the injection simultaneously funds initial supplier payments; some banks accept INVS (Investment). For USD corridors, route through the bank's US clearing branch or correspondent with CHIPS/Fedwire credentials; for CAD, settlement occurs through Lynx (Bank of Canada). FINTRAC large-cross-border transaction reporting applies to transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - supplementary rail only. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic money institution (EMI) passporting regime. Non-bank cross-border payment providers are regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA and must register with FINTRAC; core retail payment functions are also subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force 6 November 2024). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may legally serve the CA_LP for small-value cross-border collections and disbursements if they hold a valid Canadian MSB registration or operate through FINTRAC-licensed agents, but they do not have Canadian bank status, do not confer CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and offer limited protection for large capital escrows. Legal constraint: the LP must maintain its primary capital escrow and operating accounts with a domestic Schedule I bank; EMI relationships require a General Partner resolution, must remain subordinate to the primary banking relationship, and all EMI flows must be fully traceable to the LP's CRA books. Offshore EMIs settled from tax-haven jurisdictions may trigger enhanced due diligence at the core Canadian bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (15% federal general corporate rate + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate), applied at the corporate-partner level because the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends or deemed dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; distributions from the Ontario LP are not dividends and instead follow partnership pass-through rules, with the non-resident partner bearing Part I income tax on the PE-attributable share rather than Part XIII withholding on the distribution itself.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 aggregate amount of controlled transactions per non-arm's length non-resident in a taxation year, above which the ITA s. 247(3) transfer pricing penalty applies; the T106 Information Return threshold is C$1,000,000 aggregate across all controlled cross-border transactions requiring annual disclosure.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime of the Caribbean type; instead, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule under ITA s. 245, the OECD Pillar Two Global Minimum Tax Act effective 2024 (15% minimum effective rate for MNE groups with revenue above EUR 750 million), the principal purpose test incorporated via the BEPS Multilateral Instrument into Canada's covered treaties, and the substantive business-purpose requirements inherent in the partnership and permanent establishment rules; an Ontario LP with no employees, premises, production activity or genuine risk assumption can be recharacterized as a sham for Canadian tax purposes.
