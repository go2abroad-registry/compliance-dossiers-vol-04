Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity must incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act, then obtain a Business Number (BN) and register for applicable GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax accounts via CRA's Business Registration Online. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST unless classified as a small supplier. The BN serves as the primary statutory tax identifier. For income tax, a separate corporate account is established by filing Form RC1 or through online onboarding.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign or domestic entity must incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act, then obtain a Business Number (BN) and register for applicable GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax accounts via CRA's Business Registration Online. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST unless classified as a small supplier. The BN serves as the primary statutory tax identifier. For income tax, a separate corporate account is established by filing Form RC1 or through online onboarding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal tax law. Certain tax elections under the Income Tax Act are available (e.g., s. 216 for non-resident rental income, s. 217 for non-resident employment income). For hybrid mismatch arrangements, specific integrity rules under sections 94B–94D of the Income Tax Act apply and override contractual or tax classification mismatches; no elective hybrid avoidance mechanism is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (including associated persons); non-resident digital services providers and vendors supplying goods to Canada may be subject to mandatory registration rules if not small suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to eligible taxable supplies of goods sold by a registrant and shipped directly to a recipient outside Canada, provided the registrant retains documentary evidence that the goods were exported (including via automated dropshipping where the supplier ships the goods to a foreign buyer). Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated where the service does not relate to Canadian real estate or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the non-resident is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed. Digital goods and software supplied by download to non-residents generally qualify for zero-rating. A GST/HST registrant must substantiate zero-rating claims within the normal record-keeping period (six years) and be prepared to produce evidence upon CRA audit.

  • Customs export declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A) or commercial export declaration via CBSA portal
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or courier waybill showing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, quantities, and sale value
  • Proof of international payment or foreign currency remittance (e.g., bank transfer records, Stripe/PayPal settlement reports)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing is currently imposed. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service (Peppol BIS) is available for B2B exchange. Taxpayers must keep records in a legible, accessible format that permits verification by CRA; sales invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST information. GST/HST returns may be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE, including API-based integration.

CRA may deny zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete or incongruent with sales records, resulting in reassessment of uncollected GST/HST plus interest. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the understated tax, section 285.1 penalties, fines, prosecution for tax evasion, and potential director liability. Retroactive clawback periods generally extend to the full statutory reassessment period (three years for ordinary taxpayers; extended for misrepresentation or negligence).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally restrict the conversion or repatriation of lawful trade receipts, dividends, interest, loan repayments, sale proceeds, or contributed capital. A Canadian corporation may remit profits to non-resident shareholders subject to available corporate law authority, solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, treaty relief, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, shareholder-benefit rules, GST/HST considerations, and the bank's AML and sanctions review. Payments to related non-residents must be supported by arm's-length agreements, invoices, board approvals, tax documentation, and evidence of services or financing. Banks may delay or reject transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions, sanctioned parties, opaque ownership, unusual payment chains, or insufficient source-of-funds evidence. Cross-border cash and monetary-instrument movements of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency when physically imported or exported, and financial institutions and reporting entities have separate AML reporting obligations for qualifying electronic transfers and suspicious transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory repatriation period, or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. The corporation should maintain complete commercial records, reconcile receipts to sales and shipping documentation, report taxable income in Canadian dollars under applicable tax rules, and retain evidence supporting transfer-pricing, GST/HST, customs, sanctions, and AML compliance. Foreign-currency balances and monetary items must be accounted for under the applicable Canadian accounting framework, with exchange gains and losses recognized as required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign exchange administration imposing exchange controls or mandatory currency-conversion requirements. Federal AML, sanctions, payment-system, tax, customs, and securities regulators may nevertheless affect cross-border flows, including the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and applicable provincial securities regulators.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and payout; Low as a substitute for a primary Canadian corporate bank account. Canada does not use a single EU-style electronic-money-institution licensing category. An offshore EMI, payment institution, or wallet provider must be legally permitted to serve Canadian users and must satisfy its home-jurisdiction licensing, sanctions, AML, payment-services, safeguarding, and data requirements. A Canadian payment service provider may be required to register with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act framework when its activities fall within the applicable scope and effective requirements. Offshore accounts do not eliminate Canadian corporate tax, books-and-records, beneficial-ownership, AML, sanctions, GST/HST, or foreign-reporting obligations. Stripe and PayPal access is subject to entity verification, Canadian business registration, settlement-account ownership, website and product review, chargeback controls, prohibited-business rules, reserves, and transaction-history underwriting. Customer funds should not be commingled with operating funds, and unsupported third-party collection or payout structures create material account-freeze and AML risk.

No revenue or asset threshold exists for Ontario private corporations; an audit becomes compulsory only if shareholders holding at least 5% of voting shares request it. Extra-provincial or federally regulated distributions require CBCA audit rules.
