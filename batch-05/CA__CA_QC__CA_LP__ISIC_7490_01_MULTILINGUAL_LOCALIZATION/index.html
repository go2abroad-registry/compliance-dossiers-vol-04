Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and income tax administration (Business Registration Online portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html); Revenu Québec – Quebec provincial QST and income tax administration (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/); Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Business Registry) for the enterprise number (NEQ) prerequisite.

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership (LP) controlled by non-resident partners: (1) Register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec through the Clé d'accès digitally; this issues the NEQ. (2) Apply to Revenu Québec for a QST account (source deductions and QST registrations vector) using Revenu Québec's online service; the LP is a separate 'person' for QST purposes. (3) Apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account and, if payroll or import/export are involved, applicable RP/RC/RM accounts; use Business Registration Online or the CRA's services for non-residents. (4) The LP must indicate whether all partners are unlimited-residency, for Canadian income tax the partnership is transparent and each partner reports Canadian-source income, so non-resident partners may additionally need a Canadian tax account or treaty relief context. (5) If the LP carries on business in Quebec, it must file the Québec registration application within 30 days of commencement and acquire any required Quebec enterprise numbers before invoicing. No minimum capital is required, and foreign general partners may serve as managers, but a legal no-employee registration status does not supersede QST/GST registration obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent; residents/non-residents compute their pro-rata share of income (or loss) directly. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership itself is treated as a separate registrant/person when carrying on a commercial activity in Canada/Quebec. The LP’s localization business will therefore be a 'pass-through' for income tax but a taxpayer/registrant for indirect taxes.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. Applicable statutory elections and designations include: (1) election under subsection 152(4) of the Income Tax Act for a partnership to deem a fiscal year-end for the LP where needed; (2) designation of a partner as the 'designated member' responsible for GST/HST filings (Excise Tax Act, section 267.2); (3) filing of Form GST20 (election for closely related corporations/partnerships to treat taxable supplies as non-taxable) only in narrow related-party corporate contexts; (4) use of the Quick Method election for GST/HST under section 229 of the Excise Tax Act if turnover is within thresholds, but unlikely beneficial for export B2B zero-rated services; and (5) for cross-border treaty/hybrid purposes, Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act, section 5902 and OECD AI measures) disallow double-deduction outcomes for partnerships. The LP should not assume that a foreign classification provision overrides Canadian transparent treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies in a calendar quarter and in immediately preceding four quarters, or C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter (temporary threshold if taxable supply has reasonably been expected to exceed C$30,000 within one calendar month) for both GST and QST; once exceeded, the LP must register, effective from the earlier supply date. Non-resident LPs crossing this threshold while carrying on economic activity in Quebec/Canada are subject to mandatory registration, unless they are 'small suppliers' with global worldwide turnover below the threshold and no carrying-on-in-Canada commercial activity. For export zero-rated services, registration can be voluntary and typically advantageous for input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, supplies of services are generally zero-rated under section 23 if supplied to a non-resident person, unless the service is 'performed in Canada' and a listed exception applies (e.g., a service performed directly on tangible Canadian-situated property). For a professional multilingual translation and media dubbing LP, services delivered electronically or with final deliverables rendered to offshore clients are outside the scope/zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident and not registered for GST/HST for that supply, provided the supply is not made in the course of the recipient's Canadian business or tied to real property situated in Canada. Quebec QST zero-rating is aligned under Schedule IV of the QST regulation; a QST-registered LP may zero-rate the same export/offshore services. Additional zero-rating can apply to digital products within the 'intangible personal property' category when supplied to non-residents. The LP must obtain documentary evidence establishing the non-resident status of the recipient (e.g., business number, incorporated jurisdiction, banking address, shipping IP/domain platform) before applying the 0% rate.

No government-mandated real-time fiscal clearance API, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control system applies for GST/HST/QST. The CRA and Revenu Québec do not require pre-clearance of invoices. A registered LP must issue, by law, invoices containing prescribed information: name/address of supplier and recipient, BN/QST registration number, date of supply, description, and tax amount/rate. Electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) is permissible; invoices must be in an EDI or PDF/XML format capable of being printed or converted to human-readable format. Revenu Québec requires that QST registrants show the QST registration number on invoices provided to persons with a QST account. Since 2021, Revenu Québec supports electronic data interchange for prescribed returns but does not impose a clearance stamp. For CRA audit purposes, ERP/accounting system API logs and invoice export files must be retained for six years; no advance digital authorization is needed.

Under section 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act, anyone who makes a false or omits a material fact in a return may pay a penalty of C$25 or, if knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, 50% of the resulting net tax understatement (or C$100 minimum), in addition to interest. Revenu Québec imposes comparable penalties under section 1050 of the QST Act (QLR c. T-0.1) and section 56 of the Regulation respecting penalties; penalties may reach 50% of the QST at issue. CRA/Revenu Québec audit programs specifically focus on zero-rating claims by service exporters: the CRA may reassess GST/HST/QST as taxable if the non-resident recipient is not substantiated, if the service was performed in Canada for the recipient's Canadian business, or if the consideration is not attributable to an export activity. Retrospective clawbacks extend the normal assessment period from 4 to 5 additional years (i.e., up to 7 years for the CRA; Revenu Québec up to 7 years) where negligence is shown, and unlimited periods apply for fraudulent conduct. A false claim can result in seizure of records, criminal prosecution under section 328 of the Excise Tax Act (notably for illegal rebate/refund fraud) with imprisonment up to 5 years for indictable offences, revocation of voluntary registration privileges, denial of input tax credits, and inclusion of any refund received in income or net tax. For international transaction tracing, CRA and Revenu Québec may issue third-party demand letters to Wise, Payoneer, banks, and payment processors if complete foreign money movement evidence is not maintained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada imposes no monetary authority approval, no exchange-control permit, and no capital-repatriation licensing on the proposed Quebec limited partnership. Non-resident limited partners may subscribe capital in CAD or any freely deliverable foreign currency, and profits may be remitted offshore without central-bank consent. The only compliance encumbrances are (i) payer-side Canadian withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on partnership distributions to non-resident partners, (ii) FATCA/CRS financial-account reporting, (iii) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer (EFT) reporting for international wires of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iv) standard Quebec QST/GST and payroll obligations for the LP's local operating activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canadian law. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds within any period. The LP may lawfully retain 100% of foreign-currency export earnings in USD, EUR, GBP, or CNH accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks without enforced conversion to CAD. The only binding obligations are ordinary business record-keeping, participation in Statistics Canada international-transactions surveys when formally selected, and compliance with FINTRAC EFT-reporting obligations for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), together with the Department of Finance Canada as the monetary and exchange-rate policy authority; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) is the operative supervisory agency for cross-border funds-flow monitoring because Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High, conditional. Wise B2B (Wise Payments Limited, Canada branch) and Payoneer are lawful Canadian payment service providers registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and as FINTRAC Money Services Businesses; they are well suited for concentrated inbound collection of localized invoice receipts from US, EU, and East-Asian streaming-platform clients and for low-value disbursements to freelance translators. They cannot, however, substitute for a tier-1 chartered bank because they lack Lynx large-value settlement access, are not CDIC-insured deposit institutions, and cannot support Quebec payroll, QST remittance, or T5013 tax-clearing workflows. The proper treasury design is a dual-rail structure: a Schedule I bank account as the sole ledger of record and tax-clearing hub, with Wise B2B/Payoneer used strictly as receive-rail concentrators and small-ticket payout intermediaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: the non-resident limited partner instructs its global bank to transmit an SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Schedule I clearing bank in Montreal (for example, National Bank of Canada, SWIFT BNDCCAMMINT), with beneficiary field naming CA_LP as the Quebec limited partnership, Field 57D carrying the LP's specific CAD or USD account, Field 70 encoded 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / LP ISIC 7490', and the ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC where the hybrid message format permits. Settlement occurs in central-bank money through Lynx (Canada's RTGS) in near-real time. No ministerial approval or exchange-control permit is required; however, the receiving bank will apply enhanced KYC on funds until the REQ certificate and signing mandates are verified, and the LP must include the contribution in FINTRAC EFT reporting if the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High, conditional. Wise B2B (Wise Payments Limited, Canada branch) and Payoneer are lawful Canadian payment service providers registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and as FINTRAC Money Services Businesses; they are well suited for concentrated inbound collection of localized invoice receipts from US, EU, and East-Asian streaming-platform clients and for low-value disbursements to freelance translators. They cannot, however, substitute for a tier-1 chartered bank because they lack Lynx large-value settlement access, are not CDIC-insured deposit institutions, and cannot support Quebec payroll, QST remittance, or T5013 tax-clearing workflows. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (fiscally transparent limited partnership; corporate partners are subject to combined federal–Quebec general corporate rate of 26.5% for active business income, plus applicable surtaxes)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (federal Part XIII rate applicable to dividend distributions from Canadian-resident corporations; not directly triggered by the LP's pass-through profit allocations. However, the LP must apply Part XIII withholding on any non-arm's-length payments to non-residents that are recharacterized as interest, rents, royalties, or management fees. Treaty rates generally reduce this to 5%–15% depending on the recipient and ownership percentage; for Canada–US this may be 0% on certain dividends in portfolio/100% ownership contexts).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires a complete contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation package when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident exceeds CAD 1 million in the taxation year. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for multinational enterprises with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 1 billion (approx. US$750 million) irrespective of the Canadian threshold. For the LP, if it provides localization services to an overseas parent or affiliated platform, the intercompany charge-out rate must be supported by a functional analysis and benchmark study.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' akin to Gulf jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the CRA anti-avoidance doctrine require every transaction to have genuine commercial and economic substance. A limited partnership that is a hollow conduit, lacks management and operational presence in Quebec, or engages in circular invoicing to shift profits to low- or zero-tax jurisdictions will be recharacterized by CRA or Revenu Québec. Moreover, Quebec's Limited Partnership Act and Civil Code require the partnership to be formed for real activities; a façade may result in the imposition of GAAR penalties, denial of treaty benefits, and retroactive corporate residency classification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under s.239(1) of the Income Tax Act, imprisonment for up to five years plus a fine equal to up to 200% of the amount of tax evaded. In Quebec, s.602 of the Tax Administration Act imposes civil penalties of 25% to 100% of the evaded amount for gross negligence or willful default. Criminal conviction also triggers automatic exclusion from federal government contracting for a minimum of ten years.

Director personal liability: General partners of the LP are personally liable for the partnership's tax debts if they participated in the evasion. Limited partners who actively manage or control the LP, contrary to the Quebec Limited Partnership Act (Civil Code Article 2246), lose their limited-liability shield and become jointly and severally liable with the general partners for the full amount.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII for Non-Resident Partners

Statutory sanction: The LP is liable for the full 25% Part XIII tax withheld at source, plus compound interest and a penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount for failure to remit. If the failure is deliberate, a further 50% civil penalty may be imposed under s.227(1). Repeated non-remittance can trigger criminal prosecution under s.239(2) with fines up to CAD 1,000 per day and imprisonment up to two years.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally responsible for the withholding liability under s.224 of the Income Tax Act. Limited partners who direct the payment or participate in the decision to omit withholding will be considered 'responsible officers' and can be personally assessed for penalties.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Section 247 of the Income Tax Act mandates a transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's-length price; a penalty of 10% of the total adjustment (less any amount paid by a foreign affiliate) applies unless the taxpayer made 'reasonable efforts' to determine the correct price. GAAR under s.245 can additionally re-characterize the entire series of transactions and deny the benefit, resulting in reassessment with interest and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: General partners who are party to the TP manipulation are jointly liable for the corporate penalty under s.247. Additionally, if the recharacterization leaves the LP with no economic profit, the CRA may pierce the partnership veil and hold the partners personally for the uncovered tax plus interest.
Money Laundering / Organized False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties for non-compliance reach CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment for the officers involved. False invoicing may be prosecuted as tax fraud under the Criminal Code, leading to up to seven years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors/managing partners who personally approve or benefit from the fake invoicing scheme are criminally liable. The corporate veil is pierced; a court may order civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment, while limited partners who participate in management are treated as general partners for liability purposes.
