Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal administration of income tax and the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance — provincial tax policy, with Ontario HST collected by CRA; ServiceOntario — statutory registration of limited partnerships under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — export declaration and customs clearance for vessel deliveries.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the Ontario limited partnership: file Form 1 (Declaration under the Limited Partnerships Act) with ServiceOntario, identifying the general partner(s), the registered office, and the business address; the statutory declaration must be filed before the LP carries on business. Step 2 — Obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), opening the GST/HST account (RT program), payroll account (RP, if employees are engaged at the shipyard), and import/export account (RM program). Step 3 — Non-resident general partner or non-resident corporate limited partners: file CRA Form RC391 (Application to Register a Non-Resident Corporation for a Business Number and GST/HST Account) where the non-resident itself carries on business through the Ontario LP; each non-resident partner with a Canadian permanent establishment is separately registrable. Step 4 — The same CRA BN constitutes the Ontario HST registration; no separate provincial HST registration exists. Step 5 — After registration, the LP must file annual T5013 (Partnership Information Return) identifying partners and allocating income/loss for each fiscal period. Step 6 — Obtain a CBSA client identifier (CCI) or importer/exporter number through the CBSA 'Import-Export Reporting' portal for export declarations of completed vessels.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act (Canada) s.96, an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: partnership income, losses, and tax credits are computed at the LP level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement, with each partner (individual, corporation, trust, or other partnership) taxed on its allocable share. The LP is not an income-tax taxpayer. For GST/HST, the LP is a distinct 'person' from its partners under Excise Tax Act s.272.1(1), and the partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations under ETA s.272.1(3). Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners taxed on worldwide income allocated from the LP; non-resident partners taxed only on income attributed to a Canadian business carried on through the Ontario permanent establishment, subject to treaty relief. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act (Canada) s.96, an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: partnership income, losses, and tax credits are computed at the LP level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement, with each partner (individual, corporation, trust, or other partnership) taxed on its allocable share. The LP is not an income-tax taxpayer. For GST/HST, the LP is a distinct 'person' from its partners under Excise Tax Act s.272.1(1), and the partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations under ETA s.272.1(3). Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners taxed on worldwide income allocated from the LP; non-resident partners taxed only on income attributed to a Canadian business carried on through the Ontario permanent establishment, subject to treaty relief. Ontario does not impose a capital tax on LPs; Ontario corporate minimum tax may apply at the corporate partner level.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Available statutory mechanisms relevant to an Ontario shipbuilding LP include: (a) Deemed 'Canadian partnership' status under ITA s.96, confirmed by the partnership agreement and CRA administrative practice, with no formal election required; (b) allocation in the partnership agreement of profits/losses among the general and limited partners, effective for tax provided allocations are not actuated primarily by a tax-avoidance purpose (ITA s.103 anti-avoidance rule imposes a reasonable-allocation standard); (c) ETA 'closely related persons' election (s.156) is generally NOT available between an LP and its partners because a partnership is not 'related' to any person; (d) ETA s.273 election (financial institution grouping) is inapplicable to shipbuilding; (e) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.4, 25.8) operate mechanically without an election — cross-border partners using the LP must document whether payments are subject to hybrid deduction/non-inclusion treatment under OECD BEPS Action 2; (f) informal CRA 'large taxpayer' account consolidation and single-window electronic filing mandates apply to designated large LPs (annual gross revenue threshold of CAD 250 million); no other entity-level characterization election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s.148(1) applies to the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (aggregated with associated persons): if the LP's total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding calendar quarters, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory. A non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP causes the LP to be treated as a Canadian resident for the threshold; voluntary registration is available and is economically necessary for an exporting shipyard to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on steel, components, labor, and overhead inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% under ETA s.23 applies to the sale of a newly built ocean container vessel (tangible personal property) by the Ontario shipyard to a non-resident purchaser where the vessel is exported from Canada as soon after delivery as is reasonable in the circumstances and the shipyard retains contemporaneous export evidence. If the vessel is delivered at a Canadian port and sails directly to a foreign destination, the export condition is satisfied. Offshore naval architecture, engineering, and ship-management services supplied to a non-resident shipowner are zero-rated under ETA s.9 unless the services relate to Canadian real property or to goods situated in Canada at the time of performance; vessel repair, conversion, or commissioning services performed in Ontario on a non-resident vessel are zero-rated under ETA s.10 where the vessel is exported after the work. Zero-rating allows full recovery of the 13% HST embedded in shipbuilding inputs through ITCs; any excess ITC over output tax is refunded by CRA on a net basis (ETA s.169). Substantiation required per CRA publications: original ocean Bill of Lading; CBSA B3-3 or electronic export report filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) for shipments valued at or above CAD 2,000; commercial invoice and packing list; inward remittance certificate or bank/escrow disbursement advice evidencing receipt of sale proceeds in foreign currency or CAD; and port authority or CBSA clearance/departure certificate. Canada does not statutorily impose a 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is the commercial and project-finance covenant tied to the maritime installment letter-of-credit and escrow payment rails, and a longer unsubtantiated delay in foreign-currency remittance will heighten CRA and CBSA audit risk.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA B3-3 form or electronic export report filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) / Export Reporting System Portal, prior to or upon vessel departure, including the Canadian exporter identifier, value in CAD (exceeds CAD 2,000), and destination country of the ship
  • Ocean Bill of Lading — original negotiable or sea waybill evidencing the movement of the container vessel from a Canadian port (e.g., Port of Hamilton, Thunder Bay, or Halifax) to a named foreign delivery port and consigned to the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank foreign-exchange transaction document (SWIFT MT103, documentary letter-of-credit settlement, or escrow disbursement advice) proving that full export sale proceeds were received and repatriated into the LP's designated Canadian bank account
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — descriptive shipbuilder invoice identifying the vessel's hull/IMO number, describing the completed deep-sea container vessel as tangible personal property, and stating the zero-rated export supply under ETA s.23
  • CBSA Port Clearance Certificate / Department of Transport vessel documentation — evidence of the ship's physical exit from Canadian waters and, where applicable, Form A48 (ships' stores) or certificate of registry cancellation/foreign flag change

Canada does NOT operate a mandatory real-time e-invoicing, centralized fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. CRA accepts electronic invoices and maintains them in auditable format (ETA s.286 record-keeping); invoices must be produced to CRA on demand in English or French. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol-based, adopted for federal procurement) is voluntary for B2B transactions; shipyards that supply the Canadian Coast Guard or federal marine programs may be contractually required to submit electronic invoices through the Government of Canada e-invoicing portal. No API-based tax clearance gateway is required prior to ship export or vessel delivery.

CRA audit of the shipyard's export zero-rating requires strict contemporaneous documentation. Where the export documents are incomplete, or the vessel is diverted to Canadian use or chartered back to a Canadian person, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable at 13% HST, disallow corresponding ITCs, and assess arrears interest and penalties retroactively. The normal GST/HST reassessment window is four years (ETA s.298); it extends to ten years where the LP has made a false statement or misrepresentation attributable to negligence (ETA s.298(4)). Gross negligence penalties: under ETA s.285, the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax that was sought to be avoided or evaded; under ITA s.163(2) for income-tax filings, 50% of the understated tax. Criminal tax evasion (ETA s.327.1, ITA s.239) is indictable or summary — on conviction for indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to five years; on summary conviction, a fine of up to 50% and/or imprisonment up to two years. The general partner and limited partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST (ETA s.272.1(3)). CRA's Criminal Investigations Program (CIP) and the Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP) treat deliberate misuse of export zero-rating, phantom export documentation, and unrepatriated export proceeds as high-priority marine-industry enforcement targets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no exchange-control statute restricting current or capital account transactions, foreign-currency account retention, or inward foreign direct investment; no regulatory approval is required for capital contributions, distribution remittances, or loan repayments. Profit remittances by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident partners are unrestricted but carry Canadian non-resident withholding tax obligations (base 25% under the Income Tax Act, typically reduced under an applicable tax treaty such as the Canada-U.S. or Canada-China treaty) and may require Part I filings where the non-resident partner is deemed to carry on business in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation window applies to export receipts in Canada. A shipyard may retain USD export proceeds indefinitely in Canadian or foreign bank accounts. Applicable non-FX compliance obligations include export permits under the Export and Import Permits Act for controlled or dual-use goods, sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, CBSA customs declarations, and FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reporting for every transaction of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and note-issuing authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF supervision of reporting entities by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); sanctions administration and export controls by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for ancillary vendor disbursements and payroll; Low for capital escrow, maritime installment letters of credit, and high-value settlement rails. Canada has no EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; offshore digital EMIs must register as money services businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian AML compliance officer, and obtain a domestic sponsor bank to access Payments Canada clearing rails (Lynx/ACSS). They cannot hold escrow funds on their own balance sheet and cannot advise or confirm documentary credits; therefore non-bank fintech platforms are unsuitable as primary treasury counterparties for this shipyard profile.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner capital contributions should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the segregated capital escrow account at the selected Tier-1 Canadian bank, with remittance narratives referencing the executed subscription agreement and contribution schedule. Canada imposes no FX registration or capital-control filing, but FINTRAC international EFT reporting is triggered at CAD 10,000 and the bank will require a board-approved contribution list, UBO declarations, and source-of-funds dossiers. Where funding arrives in USD, the receiving bank may execute a competitive USDCAD conversion at the prevailing clearing rate or credit a USD operating sub-account; no Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required for the capital account entry.Operating disbursements use SWIFT MT103 and MT202 with invoice-structured reference fields; CAD high-value payments clear through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system and smaller items through ACSS; USD interbank settlement is executed via CLS Bank. Shipyard milestone payments to suppliers and subcontractors are structured as commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) advised and confirmed by the Canadian bank, with standby letters of credit and performance guarantees under URDG 758; escrow drawdowns require engineer-verified milestone certificates; Export Development Canada (EDC) facilities can provide export credit guarantees and buyer-financing support.Deliverable CAD/USD and CAD/EUR forward contracts with tenors aligned to shipbuilding milestone datesFX swaps and rollover structures for managing escrow and working-capital floatCross-currency swaps under ISDA/CSA matching long-term USD receivables against CAD-denominated payroll and steel costsVanilla FX options and collars to protect gross margins on fixed-price installment contractsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed offshore for third-market currencies subject to capital controls (e.g., CNY and INR), with sanctioned currencies prohibitedInterest rate swaps and caps on CAD/USD floating-rate lending tranches supporting the capital escrowCommodity forwards or swaps for hot-rolled coil steel input price protection, documented as an economic hedge of the shipyard's purchase commitments

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium for ancillary vendor disbursements and payroll; Low for capital escrow, maritime installment letters of credit, and high-value settlement rails. Canada has no EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; offshore digital EMIs must register as money services businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian AML compliance officer, and obtain a domestic sponsor bank to access Payments Canada clearing rails (Lynx/ACSS). They cannot hold escrow funds on their own balance sheet and cannot advise or confirm documentary credits; therefore non-bank fintech platforms are unsuitable as primary treasury counterparties for this shipyard profile.
