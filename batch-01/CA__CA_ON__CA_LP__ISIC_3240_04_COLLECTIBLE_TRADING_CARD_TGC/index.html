Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST Registry, Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for provincial tax notices, though HST is administered federally under a single CRA account)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the Business Registration Online portal. The partnership must provide its partnership agreement, identify its general and limited partners, and appoint an authorized representative with Canadian presence contact information. After the BN is issued, the partnership must register for the GST/HST account, unless it qualifies as a small supplier. For Ontario HST purposes, no separate provincial registration is required, but the partnership must indicate the Ontario registration point through the CRA account and comply with Ontario-specific returns, if applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; for GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and must account for GST/HST independently from its partners; for income tax, each partner includes their distributive share of Canadian-source income. — A foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the Business Registration Online portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; for GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and must account for GST/HST independently from its partners; for income tax, each partner includes their distributive share of Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally a flow-through entity for income tax; no election is required to be pass-through. GST/HST elections may be available to members of a closely related group, including the ability to elect for a reporting period or to use the special 'quick method' for the partnership's taxable supplies, subject to eligibility conditions. Canada has adopted hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (sections 163.2 and 18.2) and related interpretive provisions; partnerships should ensure no hybrid mismatch causes a denial of deduction or unintended inclusion for a partner resident in another jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 Canadian dollars (CAD) annual world-wide taxable supplies from Canada; a non-resident partnership making taxable supplies in Canada must register if its Canadian gross revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or 12 consecutive months, unless supplies are all zero-rated exports. The partnership may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods by unconditionally transferring ownership for consideration and, where applicable, the goods are exported by the recipient or the supplier in accordance with the exporter's export requirements. For services performed on or in respect of goods destined for export, supplied to non-residents, or for exported intangible personal property, zero-rating may apply if they meet the conditions set out in sections 7 and 9 of Part V of Schedule VI. The trading card manufacturer must establish that the goods are actually exported, shipped to a non-resident, and not used in Canada after export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA-approved proof of export for goods shipped from Canada
  • Commercial invoice or contract evidencing the sale terms, description, and export destination
  • Bill of Lading (air waybill, ocean bill of lading, or courier destination proof) showing the consignee outside Canada and the foreign destination
  • Proof of payment from the non-resident purchaser, including a foreign exchange record or SWIFT/transfer confirmation if the contract is denominated in foreign currency
  • Packing slip or weight certificate where required to confirm the physical movement of the goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement showing the receipt of funds in Canada from the foreign buyer within the prescribed period

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically if the registrant's annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, or if the registrant is a GST/HST registrant with a reporting period of one month. Invoicing must contain the vendor's BN, the date, a description (for zero-rated exports, the documentary evidence must be retained), but there is no government pre-clearance API. The CRA may require electronic funds transfer for refunds; mandatory electronic records retention applies.

The CRA may deny zero-rating if the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export and actual receipt of funds from a non-resident purchaser within a reasonable period. Penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act (false statements or omissions) provide for a penalty of 25% of the net federal tax amount sought to be evaded; knowingly making false statements may lead to criminal prosecution under section 298. Where a zero-rated refund is claimed in error or without proper documentation, the CRA will reassess the tax owing, recover the refund, and may apply interest retroactively from the date of the original refund payment. For serious cases involving fraudulent export documentation, the penalty may rise to twice the amount of the tax evaded, and partner-level liability may arise if the partnership structure is used to conceal the fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The federal Foreign Exchange Control Act has been repealed; the Bank Act and the Currency Act do not impose exchange controls, pre-approval regimes or repatriation obligations. Ontario limited partnerships ('LPs') may receive capital injections from non-resident limited partners in CAD, USD or any freely convertible currency, and may remit profits, distributions and capital withdrawals without central bank authorization. The principal impediments are taxation, not convertibility: profit allocations to non-resident partners are generally subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25%, reduced under the Canada-US Tax Treaty (typically 15% or 5% for portfolio interests), unless the LP files a Section 216 net-basis election. Investment Canada Act approval is not engaged for passive trading-card publishing activities below the reviewable threshold; no federal or Ontario exchange-control permit is required. The only mandatory notification is FINTRAC reporting of cross-border currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, and standard CBSA export declarations for physical goods.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts and are not required to surrender proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any authorized dealer. For the trading-card LP, obligations are limited to: (i) CBSA export declarations under the Customs Act for each physical shipment of holographic cold-foil trading cards, including commercial invoices, HS classification and OECD/transfer-pricing supporting documentation; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border currency reports where monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or lebih cross the border; and (iii) bank record-keeping and LCTR filing for single or linked transactions of CAD 10,000 or more. No de-risking or conversion gate applies, and the LP may invoice in CAD, USD or EUR at commercial discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary policy, currency issuance and foreign exchange market oversight); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential supervision of federally regulated banks and authorized foreign banks); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/CFT regulation, money service business registration and cross-border currency transaction reporting); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) (customs and export declaration enforcement).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for FINTRAC-registered Canadian financial-technology payment providers; Stripe Canada Inc. and Authorize.Net (through its Canadian payment-channel and Visa/Cybersource infrastructure) are lawful acquirers that settle card transactions in CAD directly to the LP's domestic bank account. Rating: MEDIUM-LOW for offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions. A foreign money service business may process cross-border payments for the LP only if it does not carry on payment services in Canada, or if it is registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC; Canadian Schedule I banks commonly refuse settlement accounts for unregistered offshore EMIs due to correspondent-banking and AML de-risking policies. Legal constraints: any person or foreign entity performing funds transfer, payment processing, virtual-currency dealing or money transmission touching Ontario clients must register as an MSB under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); soliciting deposits or holding client funds in Ontario may also engage provincial licensing and escrow rules. The optimal structure is a domestic bank account for dual-currency clearing, with Stripe and Authorize.Net acting as licensed payment gateways, and an offshore EMI used only for non-Canadian e-wallet disbursements under an MSB-compliant agency agreement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: (i) each non-resident limited partner initiates a SWIFT MT103 credit to the LP's Canadian settlement account at an Ontario Schedule I bank, using the legal name 'Name of LP Limited Partnership' and the account number; (ii) the remittance field must state 'Capital contribution to Ontario Limited Partnership — no goods/services' to avoid false GST/HST or customs characterisation; (iii) CAD or USD funds are cleared through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and the retail ACSS; (iv) where the aggregate cash contribution equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, a FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Report or cross-border currency report must be filed by the bank or the LP; (v) the LP records the contribution on the capital accounts and issues a credited-capital statement to the partner. Purpose codes: Canada does not operate an ITRS-style mandatory numeric purpose-code system; the bank's wire instruction uses a plain-English purpose field (e.g., 'CAPC', 'corporate capital contribution'). No Bank of Canada, CBSA or Investment Canada Act approval is required, provided no reviewable 'cultural business' threshold and no controlling Canadian-owned business rules are implicated. If the injection is in-kind (e.g., IP licences for card artwork), a Customs Form B3 or deemed-import valuation, plus an independent valuation letter, should accompany the corporate records.Rails for trade settlement: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to overseas printers, foil suppliers, and IP rights holders; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued by the Ontario branch under ICC UCP 600 (and, where applicable, URC 522 collections) accompanied by bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin, and inspection certificates; (iii) same-day domestic CAD payments through Payments Canada's LVTS and the retail Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); (iv) USD-denominated disbursements via Canadian banks' nostro correspondent accounts in New York (e.g., RBC, TD and Scotiabank USD-clearing networks) or through the USD side of LVTS; (v) for royalty payments, MT103 narrative must state 'Royalties under IP licence' and the payer must obtain Form NR301 or treaty attestations to support reduced Part XIII withholding. Trade finance: the LP may draw a standby letter of credit or performance guarantee for print-run vendors; documentary requirements include the commercial invoice, packing list, port-to-port transport document and the GP's issuing-authorisation resolution. Turnover: No mandatory independent audit is imposed on an Ontario limited partnership solely by the Limited Partnerships Act. A statutory or compulsory audit may be triggered by the partnership agreement, securities legislation (e.g., reporting issuer status), financial institution covenants, or a valid request by limited partners representing not less than the proportion specified in the partnership agreement. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate; applicable to corporate partners, as the LP is a pass-through entity)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (statutory rate for dividends from Canadian corporations; for partnership distributions, business income is generally not subject to withholding, but passive income may be subject to Part XIII tax)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods and services; CAD 250,000 for intangible property and interest transactions with non-residents.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific ESR regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules require economic substance and arm's length dealings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties; travel bans and asset seizure may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization; penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal fines for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they failed to comply with AML obligations.
