Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal; for Quebec: Revenu Québec – Clé numérique and Registraire des entreprises du Québec

E-government filing portal

Foreign or resident corporation must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act, then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or postal application. Simultaneously, the corporation registers with Revenu Québec through the Registraire des entreprises to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and a QST registration number if making taxable supplies in Quebec. The BN serves as federal TIN for GST/HST, corporate income tax, and payroll remittances; the NEQ serves as the provincial identifier for QST and Quebec corporate income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – Canadian resident corporation taxed at the corporate level on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through or check-the-box; Quebec corporation also subject to Quebec corporate income tax administered by Revenu Québec on income allocable to Quebec. — Foreign or resident corporation must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act, then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or postal application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – Canadian resident corporation taxed at the corporate level on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through or check-the-box; Quebec corporation also subject to Quebec corporate income tax administered by Revenu Québec on income allocable to Quebec.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections include: fiscal period election under section 249.1; subsection 85(1) rollover election for transfer of property to a taxable Canadian corporation; eligible capital property/goodwill elections under the pre-2017 rules; weak-section 256 association elections for the small business deduction; no hybrid-mismatch election exists, though section 18.6 of the Income Tax Act is the anti-hybrid rule (2021).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable (non-zero-rated) supplies made by the corporation in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST (Excise Tax Act, section 240) and QST (Taxation Act, SQ s. 425.1 et seq.); QST registration is also compulsory if the corporation has an establishment (établissement) in Quebec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported under customary documentary proof are zero-rated under Division III of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For the supply of a licence to use or distribute copyrighted cinematic works, the supply is zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and substantially all (at least 90%) of the consideration is attributable to a right to exploit the intellectual property outside Canada; the supplier must obtain and retain evidence establishing non-residency of the recipient and the foreign exploitation of the content. Copyright distribution royalties attributed to foreign territories may therefore qualify for the 0% rate, while supplies for use in Canada or consumed by a domestic recipient are taxable at 14.975%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13) or pre-arrival/release documentation for any tangible film prints or master materials physically exported
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, foreign-exchange conversion record, or wire-transfer receipt showing payment received from the non-resident buyer
  • Executed international copyright licence agreement and tax invoice (GST/QST invoice) identifying the foreign licensee, territory, and title

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept standard electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, or Peppol-compliant XML) without a central government clearance step. Businesses must retain fully readable invoices containing prescribed registration numbers; voluntary Peppol exchange may be used but is not statutory.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST and QST at full rates where export evidence is insufficient, within four years of a normal assessment or seven years in the case of misrepresentation, with no limitation period for fraud. Civil penalties include section 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act (50% of the tax attributable to false or misleading statements), gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of any refund or input tax credit claimed, and daily interest on unpaid reassessments. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion from zero-rated export claims carries fines of up to $50,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. Failure to repatriate foreign currency within 180 days does not itself create a tax clawback, but prolonged overseas retention without documentary support heightens audit suspicion of non-bona-fide export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency with no exchange-control licensing on current-account or capital-account transactions. Film licensing royalties are freely repatriable once Part XIII withholding tax is satisfied (federal base rate 25%, reduced to 10% under the Canada-US tax treaty for copyright royalties and to comparable levels under most UK/EU treaties). Dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder attract Part XIII WHT at 25% with treaty reductions (0%-15% depending on ownership structures). Profit remittance is not subject to Central Bank approval, no branch office requirement applies to a Canadian-resident licensor, and no post-remittance fiscal equalization is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion period, and no minimum currency-repatriation ratio for export receipts or royalty income. A Quebec film-licensing corporation may retain 100% of international minimum guarantees and royalties in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. The only statutory constraints are FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, CRS/FATCA information filing, and standard Income Tax Act foreign-accrual rules, none of which impose surrender or conversion duties on an active Canadian operating licensor.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) act jointly as central monetary and prudential authorities; no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority exists in Canada, with FINTRAC responsible for cross-border transaction monitoring and compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as the sole corporate treasury provider; High as a collection and small-payment utility. Legal constraints: under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), only banks and credit unions may accept deposit liabilities in Canada. Non-bank fintech EMIs must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and, where they provide payment services to users in Canada, must also be registered as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (registration mandatory since September 8, 2025). Offshore digital EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot hold CAD demand deposits as Canadian depository accounts, and lack direct SWIFT-nostro clearing depth; they are therefore best used for inbound collection of low-value minimum guarantees, not for capital escrow, FX hedging, or operating liquidity.

Defense evidence: Notarised certified passport copies for each director and UBO; government-issued proof of residential address (utility bill, bank statement, or agency-issued document); completed source-of-wealth questionnaire; bank reference letter from the UBO's principal relationship bank; corporate beneficial-ownership register; and a signed undertaking to notify the bank of any ownership change within 15 days.
Intangible film-licensing revenue streams with no physical goods movement (royalties, minimum guarantees, and distribution advances).

Recommended MT103 narrative fields: 'Capital subscription - share capital increase' or 'Shareholder loan funding'. Supporting documentation required at the bank: executed shareholder subscription agreement, board resolution, and an updated register of shareholders. No Canadian exchange-control filing or Central Bank approval applies to foreign direct investment. Statutory Canadian rate 25% under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act on gross dividends paid to non-residents of Canada; reduced to 5% under most comprehensive income tax treaties where the beneficial owner holds at least 10% of the voting shares, or 15% for portfolio shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required where the multinational group reports consolidated revenue above CAD 500,000,000 and the Canadian entity's gross revenue exceeds CAD 5,000,000; Local File documentation is expected where Canadian gross revenue exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; mandatory T2 Schedule 97 disclosure applies at the CAD 1,000,000 transaction threshold even where full documentation is not otherwise required.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations registry; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act permits CRA to recharacterize arrangements lacking genuine economic substance, and OECD Global Minimum Tax rules under Pillar Two effective from 2024/2025 impose a 15% effective tax floor for large multinational groups. The Quebec tax authorities expect physical office presence, resident management, actual negotiation and governance of film licence agreements within Quebec, and maintenance of board records demonstrating business purpose; a passive letterbox licensing company is exposed to sham recharacterization, GAAR reassessments and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Omission

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act, subsection 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to five years; civil penalties for gross negligence under subsection 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax plus compounded daily arrears interest; CRA jeopardy orders and immediate asset freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; personal restitution orders under the Criminal Code; travel bans issued as bail or recognizance conditions; corporate veil pierced in Quebec civil proceedings where the company is shown to be a mere facade for individual fraud.
Transfer Pricing Penalties

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act, subsection 247(3): penalty equal to 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment, minimum CAD 1,000 per year, plus interest; denial of deduction under subsection 247(1); additional gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax; reassessment periods extended under subsection 152(4).

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized false or incomplete intercompany documentation are exposed to civil liability as culpable officers; CRA may assert transferee liability under section 160; directors may be personally assessed for penalized amounts when they knowingly participated in the mispricing arrangement; travel restrictions can be imposed pending criminal transfer-pricing investigations.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act section 245: deduction denied, transaction recharacterized and tax reassessed with interest; third-party civil penalties under section 163.2 of up to CAD 100,000 plus gross compensation penalties; treaty benefits denied under anti-abuse provisions in tax treaties; provincial reassessment at Quebec's full statutory rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters jointly liable for penalties when the scheme was arranged through shell entities; false corporate filings attract criminal sanctions under the Canada Business Corporations Act; personal seizure of assets obtained by fraud; travel bans and surrender of passports may accompany money-laundering or tax evasion proceedings.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative fines up to CAD 500,000 for corporations, plus Criminal Code money-laundering offences under section 462.31 carrying imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC compliance audits may suspend or revoke operational authorizations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined up to CAD 100,000 and disqualified from serving as officers; proceeds attributable to unrecorded licensing transactions are subject to criminal forfeiture; personal travel restrictions and mortgage-like liens on personal assets are enforced in serious money-laundering cases; imprisonment of directors follows convictions for knowingly directing laundering activity.
