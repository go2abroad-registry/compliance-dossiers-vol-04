Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an Ontario limited partnership; capital contributions are private contractual amounts stated in the limited partnership agreement. No escrow account requirement applies; contributions may be satisfied in cash, property, or by a promissory note, provided the limited partner does not take part in management and thereby retain limited liability.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, and My Payment; Ontario Ministry of Finance / ON-biz for provincial taxpayer registration coordination.

E-government filing portal

A foreign owner of an Ontario limited partnership must apply for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and obtain a separate GST/HST account for the LP, using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online portal. The application must include: certified copy of the LP declaration under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act; the full partnership agreement; identification of all general and limited partners and their residency status; the LP's Canadian address and representative for service; and an attestation that the LP is engaged in a taxable commercial activity. No minimum capital requirement applies, and non-resident ownership is permitted.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada); income, gains, losses, and credits computed at the partnership level flow through to partners in accordance with their share. The LP has no separate federal income tax liability. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada); income, gains, losses, and credits computed at the partnership level flow through to partners in accordance with their share. The LP has no separate federal income tax liability. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a distinct 'person' and must register separately once it exceeds the taxable supply threshold.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for a Canadian limited partnership; flow-through treatment is statutory and mandatory. Available elections are limited to process matters, including: a partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(5) ITA (Form T1139/T1151) to use a non-calendar-year taxation year; partner allocation adjustments under section 96 ITA; and various administrative GST/HST elections concerning nominal consideration or annual filing. Entity-level corporate taxation is not an available election for an Ontario LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in gross revenue from taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the small-supplier threshold). Registration becomes mandatory once the LP ceases to be a small supplier. Non-resident vendors carrying on business in Canada with an Ontario inventory or fulfillment presence have an economic nexus and must register even below the threshold if they are not otherwise a small supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply by the Ontario LP is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the goods (handmade epoxy-resin dice) are exported by the supplier as part of the supply and the goods are delivered outside Canada as soon as is feasible. The recipient must be a non-resident, or the goods must be intended for use or consumption outside Canada. For offshore services, zero-rating applies when the supply is made to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is directly related to exported goods. The LP must retain documentary proof of shipment and payment, and may claim input tax credits to generate a refund where input GST/HST exceeds output tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A via CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transportation document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-payment evidence
  • Commercial Invoice showing sale to a non-resident customer and export destination

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or pre-sale invoice approval mandate applies in Canada at the federal or Ontario level. CRA requires registrants to maintain commercial invoices and all underlying documentation sufficient to substantiate tax status. HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and remittances are made through CRA My Payment. For exports, the CBSA Export Reporting requirement is mandatory, using Form B13A through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or an approved service provider; CRA and CBSA can cross-match export declarations with GST/HST returns to validate zero-rating claims.

CRA audit scrutiny is focused on proof of export, custody chain, and payment. If zero-rating is disallowed, the supply becomes taxable at 13% HST and the LP is subject to reassessment on the full consideration, plus daily prescribed interest and gross-negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including civil penalties up to 25% of the net tax understated for false statements or omissions, and potential criminal prosecution for deliberate fraud or misrepresentation. Retroactive clawback of input tax credits associated with the export is also possible, with a four-year reassessment window for registrants and no statute barrier where misrepresentation was fraudulent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under an open capital account. Canada imposes no exchange controls on capital or current account transactions. Business earnings and capital may generally be remitted abroad without official exchange authorization; however, distributions of income from an Ontario limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax, and the LP must file prescribed CRA forms and remit withholdings. There are no restrictions on foreign ownership of a Canadian LP, but foreign partners must undergo FINTRAC/KYC verification. No statutory approval is required for profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender foreign currency proceeds or to convert export receipts into CAD within a prescribed timeline. Exporters may maintain foreign currency bank accounts and settle in any freely usable currency. Cross-border receipts must nevertheless comply with FATF Travel Rule information requirements and FINTRAC reporting thresholds under the PCMLTFA, including large cash transaction reports and electronic funds transfer reports where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CFT and cross-border reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary treasury venue. Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) are not chartered banks in Canada, cannot accept deposits from Canadians as part of their unlicensed deposit-taking business without regulatory authorization, and do not enjoy CDIC deposit insurance. They may be used as ancillary pay-out/settlement wallets for small marketplace receipts (e.g., Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal payouts), but the LP must maintain a domestic CAD operating account with a Canadian bank or credit union. OFSI/AML constraints require the EMI to be registered as a money services business in Canada and to perform Canadian KYC; legal exposure exists if the EMI routes funds through an unregistered payment intermediary.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the registered limited partnership itself (flow-through entity); compute at partner level: combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate 26.5% (15% + 11.5%), M&P 25% (15% + 10%), CCPC small business 12.2% (9% + 3.2%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

N/A for a limited partnership (no dividends). For comparison, a Canadian taxable corporation would pay 25% Part XIII withholding on non-resident dividends; Canada-US treaty: 5% for a 10%+ voting corporate shareholder, 15% for other shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 required when aggregate reportable non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in the partnership's fiscal year. CRA contemporaneous documentation (Local File) is expected at a CAD 500,000 threshold for significant intangible or transaction values; full Master File/Local File for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) not applicable in Canada. General anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA) applies; CRA will disregard a transaction or characterisation lacking bona fide economic substance, with a penalty of up to 50% of the avoided tax if the arrangement is artificial or contrived.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Allocations

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax (s.163(2)); criminal charges under s.239(1) expose the partnership and its agents to fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; interest is compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who actively participate in control, or who knowingly sign a false T5013, lose limited liability; general partners and corporate directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for any resulting unremitted tax plus penalties.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under s.247(2) adjusting arm's length price; penalty under s.247(3) equal to the greater of 10% of the net adjustment and CAD 5,000; interests and gross-up if the LP does not provide documentation within 6 months.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be assessed personally under s.227.1 if the partnership fails to fulfil its Part XIII/TP withholding obligations, up to the gross amount due, if they were informed and failed to act.
GAAR Abuse (Shell/Interposed LP)

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) disallows the tax benefit; misclassification as a Canadian partnership can trigger CRA issuance of a waiver or assessment under s.246; penalty up to 25% of the amount of the tax sought to be avoided if the arrangement is abusive.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil of the general partner and hold the promoter as a ‘director’; travel bans and export block under the Income Tax Act apply for unpaid assessed taxes.
AML / KYC Deficiency (Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Failure under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: civil penalties up to CAD 1 million for administrative violations; criminal penalties of CAD 500,000 for knowing non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be indicted for money laundering; personal assets forfeiture available under s.462.38 CC; in Ontario, directors can be restrained from re-entering Canada if proceedings pending.
