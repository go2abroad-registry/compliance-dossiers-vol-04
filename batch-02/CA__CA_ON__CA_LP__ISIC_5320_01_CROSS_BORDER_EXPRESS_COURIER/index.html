Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html and CRA My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/my-business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST through the CRA Business Registration Online system, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number Registration) with the CRA. The application must identify the partnership, all general partners, non-resident partners, the Canadian agent or representative, and the principal place of business in Ontario. Authorized representatives may register online with immediate issuance of the BN and a distinct GST/HST account number (RT suffix). Non-resident partners must obtain their own individual or corporate BN where Canadian withholding obligations apply. Post-registration, all returns and remittances are filed electronically through CRA My Business Account or certified tax preparation software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act; taxable income or loss is allocated to partners and reported in each partner's hands, subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding on distributions to non-resident partners. For GST/HST purposes, however, the limited partnership is treated as a separate 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and must register, collect, and remit GST/HST at the partnership level.)

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or elective entity classification regime. Relevant statutory elections for a Canadian limited partnership include: (i) subsection 97(2) deemed disposition election on transfers of property to the partnership; (ii) subsection 96(3) designation of a non-calendar partnership fiscal year-end; (iii) section 216 election for a non-resident partner to file a Canadian income tax return to claim treaty relief on Canadian-source income; (iv) optional GST/HST registration under subsection 240(2) of the Excise Tax Act for small suppliers; (v) election under subsection 150(2) of the Income Tax Act for a non-resident corporation to report no tax payable where applicable; and (vi) hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act, which apply where a foreign jurisdiction's entity classification produces deductible/income-timing mismatches inconsistent with Canadian characterization of the limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); a non-resident that carries on business in Canada and exceeds this threshold must register, and a non-resident that does not carry on business in Canada may voluntarily register. Once registered, supplies made in Ontario are subject to 13% HST, while zero-rated or exported supplies are charged at 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act to: (i) exports of tangible personal property (sections 16 and 17) where the courier physically delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada and the goods remain outside Canada for at least 12 months or are consumed in commercial or industrial use abroad; (ii) supplies of transportation services for exported goods under section 20, including outbound international express courier and freight-forwarding services, to the extent the transportation is provided by the courier operator and the goods are shipped from Canada; and (iii) services performed on goods for export where the goods are ultimately exported. In a cross-border express courier business, the international leg may be zero-rated, but the domestic pickup or deadhead leg within Canada remains subject to 13% HST; each shipment must be substantiated with evidence of physical removal from Canada.

  • B13A Customs Export Declaration or approved electronic equivalent transmitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list describing the goods, value, and destination
  • Master or House Air Waybill (Bill of Lading) evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Proof of foreign delivery, including destination customs entry confirmation or signed delivery record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or wire-transfer/ACH proof of payment from the foreign consignee or principal
  • Carrier's freight manifest authorized by the CBSA

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance of invoices. GST/HST invoices must contain the vendor's business name and BN, date of supply, a description of the supply, the tax rate, and the tax amount, and supporting records must be retained for six years. Electronic invoicing in EDI, XML, or PEPPOL-compliant formats is permitted and encouraged under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service; non-resident registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, CRA My Business Account, or a recognized service provider, with remittances made through the CRA's financial channels.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax within the standard four-year limitation period, which is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Improper zero-rating exposes the registrant to reassessment of unpaid tax, interest running from the original filing date, and administrative penalties: under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act, a 6% penalty applies for false statements made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence, and a 10% penalty applies to repeat violations under subsection 285(2); failure to register, collect, or remit can attract fines and prosecution. For income tax, gross negligence penalties of 50% of the additional tax payable may be asserted under subsection 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, and fabricated export documentation may support findings of civil fraud. The CRA maintains specialized audit programs for exporters and courier operations; where bills of lading are inconsistent with customs declarations, destination delivery evidence is missing, or freight manifests are incomplete, the entire zero-rating claim may be clawed back with statutory interest and penalties, and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) may be invoked for artificial export schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible regime under the Bank of Canada Act with unrestricted capital account and current account convertibility. Profit remittances face no approval requirements, surrender obligations, or regulatory delays; funds may be repatriated freely via authorized dealers at prevailing market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required. Exporters retain full discretion to hold proceeds in foreign currency accounts or convert at market rates without statutory timelines, minimum conversion ratios, or central bank approvals.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Federal (15%) and Ontario (11.5%) general corporate rate for active business income; the limited partnership itself is tax-transparent and the rate applies at the corporate partner level, while individual partners are taxed at progressive marginal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax under Income Tax Act s. 212(2) when a Canadian resident corporation pays dividends to a non-resident. For a limited partnership flow-through, distributions of taxed Canadian-source income are generally not subject to a separate dividend withholding tax; a subsequent dividend by a Canadian corporate partner to non-resident shareholders attracts 25%, reduced by tax treaty (Canada-US DTA: 5% for 10%+ corporate shareholders, 15% for portfolio; Canada-UK DTA: 5% for substantial holders, 15% otherwise).

Substance / Business Rationale Disclosure Voluntary but prudent; describes the commercial purpose of using an LP structure in Ontario and confirms the LP is not a shell or conduit. CRA can request this in audit; the non-resident partner should be able to demonstrate the LP's assets, employees, and management decisions are situated in Canada. Ontario Annual Return late filing penalty is CAD 5 per day, minimum CAD 150, maximum CAD 1,500; additional administrative dissolution risk; corporate late-filing penalties under the Ontario Corporations Tax are mirrored by CRA penalties of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month and up to 50% gross negligence penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for cross-border controlled transactions when the Canadian taxpayer's consolidated group revenue is at or above CAD 50,000,000 and the aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions (excluding financing) is at or above CAD 5,000,000. A Master File is mandatory at the CAD 50,000,000 group-revenue level; a Local File is prepared above the transaction threshold. Below these thresholds, CRA can still require documentation where risk is high, so an Ontario LP engaged in cross-border courier services should prepare a Local File if cross-border intercompany transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 annually as an internal risk control.

Economic substance scope: No Ontario or federal analogue to UAE-style ESR exists; however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s. 245, the sham doctrine and substance-over-form to disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. An Ontario LP must maintain a physical courier hub or contractual logistics operations within Ontario, employ staff, exercise management decision-making in Canada, and demonstrate that the LP is not a shell created to divert Canadian-source revenue to a non-resident or treaty jurisdiction. If the LP has no real business, CRA may recharacterize it as a conduit, deny treaty benefits, and apply Part XIII withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or Omitted Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years; civil penalties include s. 163(2) gross negligence at 50% of understated tax; T5013 late-filing penalty up to CAD 2,500; interest under s. 161.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a general partner may be personally liable for unremitted amounts under s. 227.1; in criminal tax evasion cases, courts can impose civil restitution orders, travel bans arising from the Criminal Code, and imprisonment up to five years; Ontario courts may pierce the LP veil if the non-resident partner threatened control.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR (s. 245), disregard the LP as sham, assess Part XIII withholding on all distributions, recharacterize offshore intra-group services as Canadian income, and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(8); the Ontario Registrar may strike the LP from the register for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be held jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding under s. 227.1; personal restitution orders attach to assets; where treaty abuse and fraud are alleged, travel bans are common pending cross-border enforcement, and imprisonment is possible under s. 239.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Section 247(8) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment with no reasonable-effort limitation; additional interest under s. 247; s. 163(2) gross negligence 50% penalty may apply; Schedule 27 penalties for failure to provide information up to CAD 24,000 per completed return.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in approving or knowing TP mispricing can be assessed personally under s. 227.1 for corpus of unreported tax; civil restitution orders can be made against their personal assets; repeated wrongdoing may trigger criminal tax evasion prosecution and associated travel bans.
Non-Resident Withholding Failure on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for full Part XIII tax plus 28% compounded daily interest; failure-to-withhold penalty under s. 227(8) is 10% of the unwithheld amount; s. 215 trust liability; CRA may assess penalty for gross negligence and, if remittance is knowingly withheld, criminal prosecution under s. 227(10).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner are personally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding, including interest and penalties; travel bans and extradition can apply where the tax debt is large and the director leaves Canada; imprisonment is possible for up to five years under s. 239.
