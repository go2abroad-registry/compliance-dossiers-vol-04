Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; online portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes.html) and Revenu Québec Clic Revenu (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act. After incorporation, the corporation obtains a federal Business Number (BN) automatically via the corporate registry transfer to CRA, or by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Because captive reinsurers are financial institutions, registration for GST/HST (BN + RT account) and Québec QST (BN + SQ account) is mandatory irrespective of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Non-resident corporations must appoint a Canadian representative for service of process and may register through a TaxServicesRepresentative. The BN serves as the TIN for federal tax purposes; the QST number is the provincial VAT identifier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act. No pass-through or check-the-box election exists. Non-resident ownership is permitted, and the entity is not eligible for tax-exempt-zone treatment.

Available election forms

No hybrid mismatch or check-the-box elections are available under Canadian tax law. Relevant elections for a captive reinsurer include: (i) the GST/HST section 150 election between closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for consideration; (ii) the corresponding election under the Quebec QST legislation; (iii) section 216 Income Tax Act election for non-residents receiving rental/real-property income (if applicable); and (iv) treaty-based Part XIII waiver elections where surplus funds are distributed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST; however, captive reinsurance companies are 'financial institutions' under ETA s.149 and QST legislation, and are therefore subject to mandatory registration with no threshold exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of a financial service made to a non-resident person is zero-rated unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply, or a risk located in Canada. Captive reinsurance covering foreign risks qualifies for 0% GST and 0% QST. The same rule applies mutatis mutandis under the Quebec QST legislation. The reinsurer must demonstrate that the underlying ceded risks are situated outside Canada.

  • Reinsurance policy/binder or contract demonstrating non-resident counterparty and foreign risk location
  • Premium invoice and settlement statement
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank remittance advice proving inward premium payment
  • Non-resident status evidence (CRA NR301 certificate, treaty residence form, or foreign corporate registry extract)
  • Statement-of-location declaration confirming underlying risks are situated outside Canada
  • Customs export declaration and bill of lading are retained on file where the captive reinsurance programme covers physical goods in cross-border transit

No real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping requirement is imposed by the CRA or Revenu Québec. Electronic invoicing is voluntary; registered entities must maintain digital records and file returns electronically once annual revenues exceed CAD 1 million. Electronic payments and remittance statements are mandatory. Periodic GST/HST (GST34) and QST (VD-514) filings are required, with quarterly or monthly frequency depending on revenue.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rating within four years for ordinary audits and up to ten years for misrepresentation. Penalties under ETA s.285 include fines ranging from CAD 250 to 50% of net tax attributable to false statements, plus interest; wilful evasion may trigger criminal prosecution under s.327. The QST regime imposes equivalent penalties. Failure to repatriate premium proceeds within the 180-day benchmark may trigger scrutiny on the commercial substance of the transaction, leading to disallowance of input tax credits, reversal of zero-rating, or reassessment of Part XIII withholding taxes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Canadian dollar. There are no general exchange control restrictions applicable to corporate treasury operations, captive reinsurance premium collection, claims payments, capital injections, or dividend/interest remittances by a Quebec captive reinsurance SPV. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders remain subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (usually at the treaty-reduced rate) and must satisfy Quebec corporate law solvency and capital maintenance requirements. No prior central bank or foreign exchange administration approval is required for conversion, repatriation, or cross-border wire transfers, but financial institutions are required to screen transactions for sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and terrorist-financing obligations under FINTRAC and OSFI guidance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for receivables earned by domestic entities. A captive reinsurance SPV domiciled in Quebec may retain foreign-denominated premium reserves outside Canada if consistent with its AMF-licensed business plan and Canadian taxation rules. All receipts must, nevertheless, be traced through the licensed reinsurance books and explained in annual statutory filings for source-of-funds and substance verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with prudential oversight of banks and authorized foreign banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); registration and conduct oversight of money services businesses and retail payment service providers under the Bank of Canada and FINTRAC regimes. For reinsurance entities licensed in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) exercises statutory licensing and ongoing financial surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for licensed and Canadian-registered fintech payment processors; Low for offshore digital EMI suppliers. Canada does not recognise the European-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence. A fintech firm or offshore EMI seeking to provide cross-border payment or virtual-account services to a Quebec captive reinsurance SPV must either be a permitted bank or a payment service provider registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), and must have FINTRAC registration as a money services business where applicable. Direct usage of an unlicensed offshore EMI as a principal operating treasury account is not legally viable because the regulator would not recognise it as a safe deposit institution and the captive would face material AML, sanctions screening, and auditability failures.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Complex offshore ownership chain obscuring ultimate beneficial owners

Defense evidence: Shareholder register from corporate registry, full beneficial ownership schedule through each layer, notarised trust deeds and declaration of control, corporate bylaws, written source-of-wealth narratives, and an independent legal opinion confirming ultimate beneficial owner identification.
Absence of local economic substance or physical Quebec footprint

Root cause: Absence of local economic substance or physical Quebec footprint

Defense evidence: Quebec incorporating certificate, AMF captive or reinsurance licence with active status, management agreement with Quebec-licensed manager, board meeting minutes from Quebec, audited financial statements evidencing real capitalisation, payroll and tax filings for any Quebec personnel, and a substantive business plan showing risk-taking and claims administration in Canada.
Unclear source of injected shareholder capital or premium funding

Root cause: Unclear source of injected shareholder capital or premium funding

Defense evidence: Bank statements covering 12 months, audited parent group consolidation, notarised board resolutions approving capital injection, signed subscription and shareholder loan documents, proof of underlying operational commercial purpose for the captive, and tax characterisation analysis confirming the capital is not disguised dividend repatriation.
Sanctions, PEP, or adverse media indicators on a beneficiary or counterparty

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or adverse media indicators on a beneficiary or counterparty

Defense evidence: Comprehensive sanctions screening reports from a reputable third-party database, PEP and adverse-media clearance certificates for all directors, shareholders and beneficial owners, engagement letters, legal opinions confirming the absence of designated persons, and contractual covenants restricting dealings with sanctioned entities.
Reinsurance charter exceeds permissible business scope or risks misclassification as a financing company

Root cause: Reinsurance charter exceeds permissible business scope or risks misclassification as a financing company

Defense evidence: Exact ISIC 6512 and NAICS 524130 business description, AMF licence conditions, actuarial opinion confirming the company acts as a captive reinsurer and not a consumer lender, form and substance documentation demonstrating underwriting and risk assumption, and annual statutory filings supporting the captive purpose.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound CAD or USD capital contributions, paid-up capital deposits, and initial premium funding into the Quebec captive reinsurance SPV should be routed through tier-1 Canadian clearing banks using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages for correspondent banking and Payments Canada for domestic CAD clearing. The remitter's reference field must clearly identify the economic purpose, e.g., 'capital injection', 'paid-up capital contribution', 'reinsurance premium', or 'reserve funding'. Where US dollar funding is received, the transaction must be processed through the Canadian bank's US-dollar correspondent network, typically via New York clearing or CHIPS, with a bank identifier code and the captive's legal entity identifier. Verification requires the captive's AMF licence, board resolution authorising the specific inbound transaction, line-of-business documentation, and correspondence from the funding entity confirming the source of wealth. No central bank permit is required; however, due diligence under the Income Tax Act and Quebec subsidy disclosure rules must be satisfied, and Canadian withholding tax implications for deemed interest or taxable capital injections should be assessed in advance.Outbound disbursements for reinsurance claims, retrocession premium payments, loss adjustments, and service provider fees should use SWIFT MT103 for single payments with structured remittance information and SWIFT gpi tracking, or MT202 for nested correspondent treasury transfers. For settlement of trade obligations between the captive and cedants or brokers where documentary credit criteria exist, commercial letters of credit may be opened through a Canadian chartered bank under UCP 600, with bills of exchange drawn on the captive's operational account. Domestic Canadian-dollar payments may also be cleared through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) in real-time. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (Federal 15% + Quebec Provincial 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (federal withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; Quebec does not impose a separate dividend WHT).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for Local File (transactions with non-residents); CAD 50,000,000 for Master File (group revenue threshold).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. Captive reinsurance entities must demonstrate real economic activity, including adequate staff, premises, and risk management functions, to avoid GAAR challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 50% of understated tax, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (via director's liability certificates) and may face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may recharacterize transactions, disallow deductions, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. CRA may also assess transfer pricing penalties up to 10% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the arrangement, and may face personal assessments and travel restrictions.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations, and criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment for officers and directors.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful non-compliance.
