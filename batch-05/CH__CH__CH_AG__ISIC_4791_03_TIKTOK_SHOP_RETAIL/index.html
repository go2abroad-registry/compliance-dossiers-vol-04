Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital CHF 100,000 under Art. 632 OR; at least 20% of each share's nominal amount, and in no case less than CHF 50,000, must be fully paid up at incorporation. The paid-in cash funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) held at a Swiss regulated bank and may be released only after the Commercial Register entry has been effected.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV; Administration fédérale des contributions AFC; Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC) — official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT and UID matters handled through ESTV's Suite-Online e-portal. Cantonal and communal tax authorities administer corporate income tax for the Aktiengesellschaft.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in Switzerland must apply for a Swiss business identifier (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) and VAT number. Procedure: (1) verify place-of-supply and Swiss VAT liability; (2) complete UID/TIN application through Suite-Online or paper form to the ESTV; (3) appoint a Swiss VAT representative if the entity has no Swiss seat, domicile, or permanent establishment; (4) obtain the UID in the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX, which serves as the VAT identification number; (5) register with the relevant cantonal tax administration for income tax purposes if the AG or Swiss PE has Swiss tax obligations; (6) maintain commercial books and ensure customs filing authority through e-dec or a licensed customs agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification is fixed by the Commercial Register; an Aktiengesellschaft is automatically a taxable corporation subject to Swiss federal, cantonal, and communal income tax at entity level. Available statutory elections include the VAT group election under Article 67 of the Swiss VAT Act for closely-linked entities under common control, input VAT allocation elections (actual use, pro-rata, mixed methods), and certain elections for foreign companies regarding Swiss real estate or permanent establishment treatment. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognised under domestic Swiss law, and treaty and anti-abuse provisions govern mismatched arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in net taxable turnover (excluding Swiss VAT) generated annually from worldwide taxable supplies for Swiss-established businesses or from Swiss-source supplies for foreign sellers. Voluntary registration is available. Foreign suppliers of B2C digital services and goods to Swiss customers may also be liable once Swiss turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per year, subject to the statutory place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from Switzerland to a foreign country qualifies for 0% VAT when the supplier establishes that the goods physically left Swiss territory, typically via an electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) or equivalent internationally accepted transport documentation. Services supplied to foreign recipients qualify for 0% VAT when the place of supply is outside Switzerland under Article 18 of the Swiss VAT Act, including e-commerce digital services and goods physically shipped from Switzerland to foreign consumers. For a TikTok Shop retailer, cross-border sales to consumers abroad may be zero-rated where export and payment evidence are complete and the goods are exported in compliance with customs procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration / E-Ausfuhr)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transit document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/PSP statement proving receipt of foreign proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based invoice clearance regime exists in Switzerland. Invoices may be issued on paper or electronically. For domestic and cross-border B2B transactions, electronic invoicing using the Swiss QR-bill and XML/EDIFACT/eBill formats is standard but not mandatory. The ESTV operates Suite-Online for electronic VAT filing. Taxpayers must retain invoices and export documentation for 10 years and satisfy statutory invoice content requirements, including supplier UID, description of the supply, tax rate, and consideration.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration applies strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If the exporter cannot provide customs, transport, and payment evidence, VAT at 8.1% is assessed and any input VAT previously deducted on related purchases is clawed back. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under the Swiss VAT Act, giving rise to retroactive assessments, default interest, civil penalties of up to three times the tax evaded, criminal fines, and imprisonment of up to two years in serious cases. Additional turnover-based penalties of up to CHF 1,000,000 may apply for deliberate violations, and the mandatory 10-year retention period allows substantial retrospective audit review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland applies a full convertibility regime for both current account and capital account transactions. The Swiss franc is freely convertible and there are no exchange controls, mandatory approvals, surrender requirements, or quantitative restrictions on inbound or outbound fund flows. Dividends, interest, royalties, branch profit remittances, and proceeds from liquidation may be transferred abroad freely, subject only to Swiss withholding tax at standard rates where applicable (domestically 35%, potentially reduced under an applicable double tax treaty) and to the debtor's compliance with general civil law obligations. This assessment confirms that the CH_AG TikTok Shop retail entity may receive foreign investment and repatriate profits without prior exchange authorization. However, SNB statistical reporting obligations under the National Bank Act and the Reporting Ordinance may require periodic submission of balance-of-payments data for cross-border transactions above defined thresholds, and the beneficial owner and source of funds must be documented to the satisfaction of the resident bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule in Switzerland. No fixed timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio into Swiss francs apply to export receipts or proceeds from social commerce sales. Exporters and cross-border e-commerce operators are free to retain revenues in foreign currency or to convert them at their discretion through licensed banks, which may place the funds in multicurrency accounts. The only practical constraint is the SNB statistical duty for the reporting entity or resident bank to record cross-border payment transactions when cumulative thresholds are exceeded (transactions above the annual threshold of CHF 10 million in either receipts or payments, or assets held abroad above CHF 10 million, must be reported to the SNB in accordance with the Swiss Reporting Ordinance). No documentation other than normal accounting records and the relevant e-commerce payout statements is required for such statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border settlement and disbursement where the fintech EMI has an established Swiss license, or a European license that permits regulated activity in Switzerland only through a FINMA-authorised branch or an agent relationship. A purely offshore or non-regulated digital EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a Swiss resident company at a banking-grade level and may expose the entity to regulatory and counterparty risks. Practical viability is therefore rated as 'Medium-High' if the platform operates via a Swiss-licensed payment institution or arranges a custody/banking partner in Switzerland; otherwise the legal constraint of the Swiss Banking Act, the Financial Institutions Act, and the Anti-Money Laundering Act makes direct reliance on a non-Swiss EMI unacceptable for treasury reconciliation. For TikTok Shop payouts and Wise B2B transactions, the preferred compliance model is to maintain a Swiss bank account as the master account and use the EMI only as a receiving/clearing mechanism for small-value cross-border transfers where full UBO and sanction-screening data can be exchanged.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection is routed as a clearly labelled SWIFT MT103/SCT wire from the beneficial owner or shareholder to the CH_AG's dedicated Swiss bank account. No prior exchange control or central bank approval is required; nevertheless the originating bank will apply standard sanctions screening and the recipient bank will require a payment reference such as 'Capital Contribution CH_AG' or 'Share Capital' together with a copy of the shareholder resolution and the capital call notice for statutory share capital deposits. For the registered share capital, inward remittances should be directed to a blocked capital escrow account with the Tier-1 Swiss bank, where they remain unavailable until the bank confirms the legal pledge to the commercial register. For additional equity or shareholder loans exceeding CHF 10 million per annum, the entity must ensure SNB balance-of-payments reporting is completed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 1,000,000 (intercompany transactions exceeding this threshold require a Local File; Master File required if group turnover exceeds CHF 1 billion or intercompany transactions exceed CHF 10 million).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but substance requirements are embedded in tax law and treaty practice. To benefit from DTA relief and avoid anti-abuse rules, a company must demonstrate real economic activity, including adequate premises, employees, and decision-making functions in Switzerland. The Federal Tax Administration applies the OECD's Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and substance-over-form principles. For holding companies, a minimum substance requirement (e.g., at least one director resident in Switzerland, board meetings held in Switzerland) is necessary to access treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Article 175 of the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax (DBG): imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to CHF 30,000. In severe cases, the fine may be up to three times the tax evaded. Civil penalties include interest on unpaid tax at 4% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax evasion if they knowingly participated or failed to act. They may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, and civil restitution. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed. In cases of gross negligence, directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and potential reclassification of income as taxable in Switzerland. The company may be subject to a 35% withholding tax on deemed distributions. Administrative fines up to CHF 10,000 and mandatory liquidation may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is deemed a sham. They may face criminal charges for aiding tax evasion, personal fines, and disqualification from serving as directors. Travel bans may be imposed during investigations.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), fines up to CHF 500,000 for non-compliance. Criminal penalties for money laundering under Article 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine. The company may be blacklisted and subject to asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. They face imprisonment, personal fines, and civil liability for damages. Travel bans and extradition may be enforced.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by the tax authority, resulting in additional tax, interest, and penalties up to 20% of the underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax if they signed inaccurate returns. They may face fines and, in cases of intentional mispricing, criminal prosecution.
