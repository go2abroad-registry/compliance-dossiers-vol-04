Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration. Ontario provincial corporate income tax and the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) are administered by the CRA under the Ontario–Canada Tax Collection Agreement. Primary portal: CRA 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/my-business-account.html. Provincial registry: Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery (Companies and Partnerships Act) for extra-provincial licensing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or the Canadian Business Corporation must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (i) apply via CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) for Ontario, or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA; (ii) request a federal corporate income tax program account ('RC') and, where taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a GST/HST program account ('RT'); (iii) attach certified copies of the certificate of incorporation/continuance, articles, foreign registration certificate, proof of registered office in Ontario, and the signing officer's legal authority; (iv) await written BN confirmation; (v) use the BN/RT account code on all GST/HST returns, customs B13A export declarations (where goods are exported), and T2 corporate income tax returns. Non-resident corporations not yet 'carrying on business in Canada' that make taxable supplies in Canada are required to register for GST/HST even below the general threshold, unless all supplies are zero-rated. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the CAD 30,000 threshold. No payroll (RP) account is needed unless the localization agency hires employees in Canada; cross-border contractor payments via Wise_B2B or Payoneer do not create a Canadian payroll obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canada does not permit pass-through treatment for corporations. A federal/provincial Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act, taxable on worldwide income (with foreign tax credit relief), and is a 'taxable Canadian corporation'. Ontario corporate income tax is levied under the Taxation Act, 2007 and collected by the CRA. There is no territorial tax election for a Canadian-incorporated company.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available statutory elections relevant to a localization agency include: (i) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) to treat taxable supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (ii) election under section 150 ETA to treat intercompany supplies as nil; (iii) subsection 216(4) Income Tax Act election for a non-resident to file a net-basis return on Canadian-source rental income; (iv) deemed-resident election under paragraph 114.1 ITA; (v) foreign affiliate distribution elections under section 93 ITA. On hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada applies targeted anti-avoidance rules (e.g., section 91(7) 'excessive foreign tax credit generator' and section 95(1) rules) but offers no elective hybrid tax classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the annual worldwide value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding capital receipts and goodwill) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration under section 240 ETA. For a non-resident corporation not carrying on business in Canada, the threshold is effectively CAD 0: registration is required for any taxable supply made in Canada unless the only supplies made in Canada are zero-rated. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada benefits from the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI ETA: (A) goods physically exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export from Canada (section 1, Part V), supported by prescribed export documentation; (B) services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST (section 7, Part V), unless the service is in respect of Canadian real property, in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or is a personal service rendered to an individual present in Canada — CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.3 confirms that translation, dubbing, subtitling, and digital post-production services delivered electronically to a foreign client satisfy section 7; (C) intangible personal property such as copyrights and licensing rights in the localized media supplied to a non-resident (section 10, Part V). The localization agency must retain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and non-registration under GST/HST, the location of performance, electronic delivery path, and foreign payment routing (Wise_B2B/Payoneer). If a voice actor performs in person in Canada, the service remains zero-rated only if the contract is with the non-resident corporate recipient and the use/consumption occurs outside Canada; physical performance in Canada alone does not defeat section 7 unless the service is rendered to an individual in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) — B13A equivalent for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice containing all statutory ETA information and indicating 'Zero-Rated Export'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice or Wise_B2B/Payoneer transaction confirmation evidencing receipt of payment from outside Canada
  • Non-resident status evidence (foreign tax registration or residency certificate) and signed declaration that the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and does not consume the service in Canada
  • Service delivery records, including digital file transfer logs, contractual descriptions of the localized works, and IP licensing agreements

No national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, continuous transaction control, or QR-code clearance mandate currently applies in Canada or Ontario. The CRA mandates electronic filing: (i) GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE for registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million; (ii) T2 corporate returns via CRA-approved electronic filer services for corporations with gross revenue over CAD 1 million; (iii) customs export declarations via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework. Invoices must contain the prescribed information under section 3 of the Information on Invoices Regulations: supplier name/Business Number, date, description of supply, terms of payment, and tax treatment (e.g., 'zero-rated export').

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within 4 years (extended to 8 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established). If export zero-rating is denied, the supplier is liable for uncollected 13% HST on the full consideration, plus compound interest at prescribed CRA rates. Penalties include: 5% of any unremitted amount plus 5% per month for up to 6 months under ETA section 280; gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the tax shortfall for knowingly making false or misleading statements under ETA section 285.1 and ITA section 163(2); criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA section 239. Deficiencies in export documentation (missing B/L, remittance proof, or non-resident evidence) create a rebuttable presumption that the supply was made in Canada and attract full clawback of input tax credits previously claimed. The CRA applies heightened audit scrutiny where zero-rated export claims involve related non-resident entities or digital services; the taxpayer must maintain per-project export evidence files for at least 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control regime, no quantitative restriction on the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or capital, and no restriction on the use of foreign currency accounts. Profit remittances are freely transferable subject to standard Canadian withholding tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the application of any relevant tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose any statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any government authority. Export revenues may be retained in foreign currency, converted at any licensed financial institution, or held in a non-resident-ven Cash Management Account as the corporate treasury determines. FINTRAC reporting of international electronic fund transfers equal to or above CAD 10,000 and normal tax reporting remain the only applicable thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy and regulatory administration are coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). No standalone exchange-control administration is established.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility as supplementary cross-border payment rails, but with clear legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise Business and Payoneer are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada). They must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and may also require registration as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada if providing payment services to Canadian clients. These EMIs cannot offer Canadian deposit insurance (CDIC), credit, or full banking services. For an Ontario localization agency, they are viable for low-value contractor disbursements and receipt aggregation, but a tier-1 Canadian operational bank account must remain the core treasury and settlement backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection shall be routed through SWIFT MT103 with Global Payments Innovation (GPI) tracking, from the remitting shareholder's bank to the Ontario operating account. The wire narrative must identify the purpose in Field 70 or 77T: 'Share capital contribution' for equity injection, or 'Shareholder loan' if structured as an intercompany debt facility. Canadian banks do not mandate statutory purpose codes; however, for ISO 20022-oriented platforms, the external purpose code 'EQUI' may be used for equity transactions or 'LOAN' for shareholder advances. Verification requires the Canadian receiving bank to obtain a certified board resolution authorizing the capital raise, a signed share subscription agreement, the updated corporate register, and, where the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, automatic FINTRAC reporting of the international electronic fund transfer. The receiving bank will also perform source-of-funds due diligence on the remitting shareholder.Outbound trade disbursements to international localization partners and subcontractors are settled primarily through SWIFT MT103 (single beneficiary) or SWIFT MT202/MT103 for interbank treasury transfers. CAD-denominated payments clear through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); USD payments clear through CHIPS or Fedwire. For low-value, high-frequency contractor payments (e.g., per-word or per-minute dubbing services), Wise Business or Payoneer may be utilized as auxiliary facilitation rails, but the final settlement of these rails is typically executed back through the originating Canadian bank to ensure CDIC-insured account integrity. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) under ICC UCP 600 are permissible but not commercially standard for service-based localization contracts; documentary credits are recommended only when a counterparty in a high-risk jurisdiction requires bank-backed settlement.
