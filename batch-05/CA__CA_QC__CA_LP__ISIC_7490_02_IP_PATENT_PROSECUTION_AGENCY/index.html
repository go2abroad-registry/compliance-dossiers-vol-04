Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) — federal tax authority for GST/HST, federal income tax, and customs matters; portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE. Revenu Québec — Quebec tax authority for QST, Quebec income tax, payroll contributions, and related filings; portal: Clic Revenu (Entreprises).

E-government filing portal

A Quebec limited partnership must first obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. It must then register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and, where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada or otherwise elects, a GST/HST program account. Registration with Revenu Québec for QST is also required if the LP is not a small supplier or if it elects to register. Non-resident partners must provide foreign tax identification data and the LP must maintain a Canadian address and agent for service. Applications are filed through CRA Form RC1 and Revenu Québec electronic registration; certified partnership documents and proof of general partner authority are generally required for foreign-controlled LPs.

It must then register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and, where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada or otherwise elects, a GST/HST program account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not apply a U.S.-style check-the-box regime; the CRA classifies a limited partnership as a flow-through entity. Available elections include voluntary GST/HST and QST registration before the mandatory threshold, non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period elections under ITA s.249.1, functional-currency reporting elections under ITA s.261, and elections to have non-resident partners compute certain Canadian-source income on an elective basis. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by mandatory adjustments under ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3 rather than by elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a calendar quarter and preceding four calendar quarters for GST/HST small-supplier status; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST small-supplier status in Quebec. A Quebec LP carrying on commercial activity must nonetheless obtain the NEQ and register for GST/HST/QST if any threshold is exceeded or if it elects to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Granted under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and corresponding QST rules where a supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST or QST, the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply, and otherwise qualifies as a zero-rated service to a non-resident. Services rendered by a patent prosecution agency for foreign patent or trademark prosecution before foreign intellectual-property offices may be zero-rated where the recipient is non-resident, the work product is delivered abroad, and use of the service occurs outside Canada. A tax invoice must clearly indicate the zero-rated nature of the supply and the applicable statutory provision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) where physical priority documents, drawings, or other tangible items are exported; for pure services, the export contract and non-resident certification serve as the export record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, required only when physical goods or documents move across borders
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Non-Resident Client Declaration with supporting proof of foreign residence, permanent establishment address, and non-registration status
  • Detailed zero-rated invoice, statement of work, and service completion report referencing the patent application or trademark registration matter

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically above prescribed revenue thresholds, and QST returns must be filed in accordance with Revenu Québec electronic filing requirements. Pan-Canadian electronic invoicing is voluntary and no API clearance or transaction-control stamping is mandatory for a patent prosecution agency.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess claimed export zero-rating, claw back any input tax credits or refunds, and impose unremitted GST/HST and QST with interest if the documentation does not establish a bona fide export to a non-resident. Penalties include late-remittance penalties under ETA s.280 equal to 5% plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months, gross-negligence and false-statement penalties under ETA s.285 of up to 25% of the amount at issue, and the same or substantially similar penalties under Quebec QST legislation. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may additionally attract criminal tax-evasion fines and imprisonment, and the limitation period for reassessment is extended where misrepresentation or wilful default is established.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no exchange-control statute: the Currency Act (RSC, 1985, c. C-52) establishes the Canadian dollar as legal tender but imposes no restrictions on holding, converting or transferring foreign currency; the CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account, consistent with OECD Article VIII and CUSMA obligations. Profit and capital remittances are unrestricted in quantum and timing. The operative hurdles for a Quebec limited partnership ('société en commandite') are tax-driven: a non-resident partner's allocable share of the partnership's Canadian-source business income is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act, and distributions to a non-resident partner attract Part XIII withholding under section 212.13 of the Income Tax Act at the 25% statutory rate, generally reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable tax treaty, with analogous provincial withholding exposure under the Taxation Act (Quebec) where income is sourced to a Quebec establishment. Bank-level practicalities are FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for every international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, FATCA/CRS account disclosure, and SEMA/UNA trade-control screening before any cross-border payment is released.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary and settlement authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border capital-movement, sanctions and export-control authority under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA), the Freezing Assets of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act and the United Nations Act, administered by Global Affairs Canada; anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Quebec corporate-registry and beneficial-ownership oversight by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ).
