Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG obtains its statutory business identification number (UID, equivalent to the Swiss TIN) automatically upon inscription in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the cantonal register office; the UID is displayed in Zefix. For Swiss VAT, the AG must file a VAT registration application with the ESTV's Register of Value Added Tax via the FTA e-portal within 30 days of commencing or exceeding the CHF 100,000 taxable-turnover threshold; the ESTV assigns a VAT number in the format CHE-123.456.789 MWST. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to federal, cantonal and communal profit and capital taxes; no domestic pass-through or check-the-box classification is available; Swiss tax law applies a modified territoriality principle, exempting foreign permanent-establishment profits and foreign real property from Swiss corporate income tax while retaining progression).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. Elective mechanisms available: (i) VAT grouping for closely integrated companies (economic, organisational and financial integration with direct/indirect majority control) to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) Swiss group taxation election for direct-tax purposes (Steuergruppe) allowing a controlling Swiss parent and its majority-held Swiss subsidiaries to file as a consolidated taxpayer; (iii) STAF incentive elections (patent box, R&D super-deduction, notional interest deduction) applied for with the competent cantonal tax authority; (iv) voluntary VAT registration option for below-threshold taxpayers, enabling input-tax recovery. Hybrid mismatch treatment is non-elective: Swiss law applies mandatory anti-hybrid rules recharacterizing payments under blended arrangements; foreign entity classifications do not bind the Swiss tax administration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland provides full, unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no foreign-exchange control law, no requirement to obtain permission for opening or operating foreign-currency accounts, and no restriction on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or capital wind-up proceeds. The only fiscal friction on shareholder profit remittance is the standard 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends; this may be reduced or fully refunded under an applicable double-taxation treaty or where the recipient is a Swiss beneficial owner, and it constitutes a fiscal cost rather than an exchange-convertibility barrier. Foreign-ownership of a Swiss AG is unrestricted, and the Swiss franc is freely tradable in spot, forward, and swap markets. Therefore, profit remittance hurdles are negligible; treasury planning should focus on treaty eligibility, substance, and documentary support for reduced withholding rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland does not impose any statutory timeline for export-receipt repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio of export proceeds into CHF, and no surrender or escrow regime for export earnings. A Swiss AG may retain invoiced proceeds in original foreign currency in any Swiss or foreign account, convert at its own discretion, and settle commercial obligations in any currency. As internal treasury best practice, a notional conversion of export receipts into CHF within 30 days of receipt is recommended to minimise accounting FX volatility, but this is not a legal obligation.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banca Nazionale Svizzera) as the monetary and currency-issuing authority; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists. Prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), and tax/double-taxation treaty policy by the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF).

Feasibility rating: HIGH for Wise (Wise Payments AG, Zug, holding a FINMA fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, with Swiss IBAN issuance) and for Stripe (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd acting under the Swiss correspondent/fintech umbrella via local merchant accounts). MEDIUM for offshore challenger EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Payoneer, which may on-board the Swiss AG but typically issue only foreign IBANs (GB/LT/IE) and do not constitute a replacement for a regulated Swiss bank account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: foreign shareholder transfers at least CHF 50,000 (the statutory paid-in portion for CHF 100,000 nominal capital) via SWIFT MT103 to the bank's notarial escrow sub-account opened in the company's name before incorporation; the bank requires a copy of the notarised deed of incorporation and a board resolution designating the operational account; the purpose narrative must explicitly state 'share capital contribution according to notarial deed' and the bank maps it to an appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g., 'COMC' or 'INVS') for statistical/AML classification. If the transfer originates from an EMI or crypto wallet, a complete source-of-funds verification package must accompany the instruction; remittance address and order must match the contributor's documented KYC profile. Post-incorporation operating inflows from overseas sales are tagged with the invoice reference and service description, avoiding any confusion with capital increases; any subsequent capital increase above CHF 100,000 nominal requires a notarial deed and Commercial Register registration before the transfer can be received.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers and MT202 cover payments for direct CHF/EUR/USD/GBP supplier and licensor outpayments; (ii) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for CHF domestic and cross-border CHF settlement; (iii) EURO-SIC and SEPA Credit Transfer via Swiss bridge interfaces for EUR-denominated trade within SEPA; (iv) commercial letters of credit (documentary credits under UCP 600) advised/confirmed by the Swiss relationship bank for high-value co-production milestone payments and IP acquisitions; (v) card and payment-facilitator rails (Stripe/Wise) for micro-transactions, subject to daily sweeps to the Swiss treasury account. Each outbound instruction must attach the supporting invoice/contract and be classified with SWIFT purpose/transaction codes such as 'ROYL' for software royalties, 'SERV' for media production services, and 'LIC' for licensing fees. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for Wise (Wise Payments AG, Zug, holding a FINMA fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, with Swiss IBAN issuance) and for Stripe (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd acting under the Swiss correspondent/fintech umbrella via local merchant accounts). MEDIUM for offshore challenger EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Payoneer, which may on-board the Swiss AG but typically issue only foreign IBANs (GB/LT/IE) and do not constitute a replacement for a regulated Swiss bank account. Legal constraint: Swiss law (Banking Act Art. 1) requires a banking licence for any entity professionally accepting deposit-like funds from the public in Switzerland; an offshore EMI rendering deposit or payment services into Switzerland without FINMA authorisation violates Swiss regulatory law and exposes the treasury to compliance cascading risk. Therefore the AG must maintain its primary liquidity and statutory capital in a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank or Swiss branch; EMIs are permitted as collection/aggregation endpoints (Stripe/Wise) subject to mandatory daily sweeps into the Swiss bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal corporate income tax on net profit after tax (effective federal rate approximately 7.83% pre-tax) plus cantonal and communal taxes resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 11.5%–21.0% depending on domicile (e.g., Zug ~14.6%, Zurich ~21.0%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax on gross dividends paid by an AG; reduced by applicable double taxation agreement (DTA) to 0%–15% for direct and portfolio investors.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax return and financial statements of the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end (31 December for calendar-year companies); extension of up to an additional 6 months available upon reasoned request; Swiss Federal Tax Administration may impose final deadlines under the federal tax harmonization rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss entities of a multinational group with group annual gross revenues of CHF 50 million or more must prepare a Master File and Local File under the Swiss transfer pricing documentation practice; a Country-by-Country (CbC) Report is mandatory where consolidated group revenue exceeds CHF 900 million. Below the threshold, arm's length documentation must still be available on request to justify intercompany pricing (e.g., AI license fees charged to foreign affiliates).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not impose a general Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime comparable to UAE; however, treaty eligibility, participation relief, and qualifying IP incentives require real economic substance: substantive premises, qualified staff, management decisions in Switzerland, and active business activity. The Federal Supreme Court and Swiss tax practice deny treaty benefits or privileges to letterbox AGs lacking minimum commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Swiss Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment up to 10 years; interest on additional tax; fine up to 200% of evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG (Federal Tax Act); publication of criminal judgments; imprisonment in severe cases under Art. 176 DBG.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns may be held jointly and severally liable for the AG's unpaid tax, liable for restitution, subject to travel bans and asset seizure, and imprisonment up to 3 years or 5 years if acting for self-enrichment.
Anticipatory Tax Fraud (WHT non-payment)

Statutory sanction: Anticipatory tax remains due by law; penalty up to 200% of unpaid tax; interest at 3.25% p.a.; criminal sanctions for attempted tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers are personally liable for the withheld amount; court-ordered personal bankruptcy in fraud cases; restriction on travel and binding administrative compulsory measures.
Transfer Pricing and Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fiscal income adjustment to arm's length value; additional cantonal and federal tax plus late interest; denial of DTA treaty relief for outbound subsidiary payments; publication of assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax adjustment on hidden profit distributions; travel bans and asset freezing in significant evasion projects; criminal liability where TP manipulation leads to wrongful loss of tax.
Money Laundering and AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code (CC): imprisonment up to 3 years (or 5 years in serious cases) and fine up to CHF 500,000; confiscation of assets; administrative sanctions under FINMA Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance (AMLO) up to CHF 10 million.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal conviction of directors involved; civil liability for investor loss; mandatory travel restrictions and extradition risk; disqualification from serving as director in Switzerland.
Shell Company Abuse and Tax Benefit Denial

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax authorities may set aside invoices and re-attribute profit to the beneficial owner; legal liability creates a constructive dividend subject to 35% WHT and additional taxes; treaty benefits denied under Article 29 anti-abuse provisions; company may be struck off in case of abandoned registered office.

Director personal liability: Personal joint liability of board directors; seizure of personal assets; ordering of restitution to tax authorities; travel bans; in aggravated cases imprisonment for tax fraud or falsity in commercial documents.
