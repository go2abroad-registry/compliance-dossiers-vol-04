Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV / AFC), Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer, Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; portal: https://www.esty.admin.ch and https://www.business.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration for a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr/IVA-Nr/TVA-Nr) using the official registration form via the BusinessTax online portal or paper submission. If the foreign entity has no Swiss registered office or permanent establishment, it must appoint a Swiss tax representative/fiscal proxy (Art. 67 of the Swiss VAT Act). Required documentation typically includes the foreign commercial registry extract, articles of association, director identification, confirmation of Swiss representative, and estimated turnover details. The VAT number is linked to the Swiss Business Identifier (UID).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is subject to Swiss corporate income income tax at federal, cantonal, and communal levels; no fiscal transparency under domestic law. Profits are taxed in the hands of the company, and distributions are separately subject to Swiss withholding tax

Available election forms

Not applicable under Swiss domestic law: Switzerland does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and an AG cannot elect pass-through treatment. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed through OECD MLI provisions, domestic substance requirements, anti-abuse case law, and potential foreign tax classification elections by the investor rather than by the Swiss entity

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act); non-established foreign suppliers supplying digital, goods, or services in Switzerland are required to register once their Swiss taxable turnover reaches this threshold unless the supply falls under full reverse charge. Voluntary registration is available below CHF 100,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Switzerland are zero-rated under Art. 23 Swiss VAT Act where the goods are physically transported to a foreign destination, the power of disposal is transferred to the foreign purchaser, and the exporter retains documentary proof of export (customs export declaration and transport documents). Related services are zero-rated if the services are directly attributable to exported goods and performed on the goods prior to export, or if the recipient is a foreign consumer for cross-border services. Services that construct, alter, or process goods for foreign principals are generally exempt with input tax credit when the place of supply is deemed foreign

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrschein / déclaration douanière d'exportation)
  • Bill of Lading or international waybill (Airway Bill / Sea Waybill / CMR consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign counterparty
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract/order confirmation identifying the foreign buyer

Switzerland imposes no centralized real-time fiscal clearance or stamping obligation. VAT-compliant invoicing is required under Art. 26 Swiss VAT Act, including supplier and recipient identification, date, type and quantity, remuneration, applicable tax rate, and VAT amount. The Swiss QR Invoice (QR-Rechnung) is the standard digital payment invoice format for QR-bill processing; machine-readable B2B e-invoicing (EDI/XRechnung-like standards) is optional and not tied to a continuous control/clearance mechanism

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may retroactively deny zero-rating if the export evidence is incomplete or false. Reassessment at 8.1% VAT applies, plus default interest at 2.5% per annum. Under Swiss VAT Act Art. 105, intentional tax evasion carries a penalty up to 3 times the evaded tax; negligent violation carries a penalty of up to 25% of the tax if VAT was placed in jeopardy through negligence (with graded increases). False documentation and fraudulent zero-rating claims may additionally trigger criminal fines and director liability, prohibition of input tax deduction for the invoicing entity, and exposure under Swiss anti-money-laundering reporting obligations

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised current and capital account. The CHF is freely convertible and no SNB approval or licence is required for cross-border payments, dividends, loan servicing, trade settlements, or capital movements. Profit remittance faces no foreign-exchange barrier, but a statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) applies to dividends unless reduced under an applicable double-taxation treaty; no prior conversion or repatriation licence is required. Inward equity and shareholder-loan injections, portfolio flows, and direct investments are unrestricted under the free-movement-of-capital principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes neither mandatory repatriation nor any conversion requirement on export receipts. The CH_AG may hold foreign-currency proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts, and there is no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio applicable to export receivables under SNB or SECO rules.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA, SR 941.10); the State Secretariat for International Financial Affairs (SIF) coordinates international monetary policy; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists because Switzerland maintains full currency convertibility with no exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for FINMA-supervised Swiss fintech entities and licensed payment institutions operating under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (fintech licence, public-deposit cap of CHF 100 million) or FINMA payment-service registration. Neobanking platforms such as Yuh, Neon, and Zak, and SEPA-integrated EMI gateways, are viable complementary rails for Amazon Pay and Stripe payout concentration and supplier disbursement; however, they lack direct SIC/SWIFT settlement membership and are therefore classified as Medium-to-High for primary treasury use. Offshore non-Swiss EMIs without FINMA authorisation are rated Low-Medium because they require pass-through KYC and cannot guarantee Swiss IBAN or SIC settlement finality; a tier-1 Swiss commercial bank remains the mandatory anchor account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfers using ISO 20022 purpose codes (EQUI for equity, LOAN for shareholder debt, CAPT for capital reinforcement). No SNB exchange-control approval is required, but the CHF 100,000 minimum legal capital must be paid into a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) or dedicated CHF operational account at a Swiss authorised bank, evidenced by the notarial incorporation deed and the bank's release certificate to the board. All inbound funding above issued capital must be supported by a board resolution on capital increase, and inbound transfers from non-EEA jurisdictions should include the UID number (CHE-…), the legal entity identifier of the remitter, and an explicit reference to the purpose code to pre-empt AML round-tripping interpretations.Supplier payments for raw wood panels, CO2 laser tubes, optics, packaging, and third-party logistics are cleared via SEPA Instant or SEPA B2B Credit Transfers for EEA vendors, and via SWIFT MT103 with field 70 remittance information coded TRAD for Asian and US counterparties. Documentary risk control includes irrevocable letters of credit (UCP 600) for first-transaction suppliers, TT payment against scanned BL and commercial invoice for recurring vendors, and open-account terms only after a six-month payment-performance track record.
