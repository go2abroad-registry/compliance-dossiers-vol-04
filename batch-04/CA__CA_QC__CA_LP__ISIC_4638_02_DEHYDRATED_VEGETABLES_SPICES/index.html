Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and Income Tax administration (portal: CRA My Business Account, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), provincial income tax and provincial registration (portal: CLIC, https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (société en commandite) must: (1) register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), a prerequisite for QST; (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or the CRA online registration portal, selecting the GST/HST (RT) program account; (3) file a parallel QST registration with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V or the CLIC portal, producing a TVQ registration number in the format 1234567890 TQ 0001; (4) where the partnership carries on business in Canada, appoint a Canadian-resident representative for service of documents and, if requested, post security under ETA s. 241; and (5) display the BN and TVQ numbers on all commercial invoices. The general partner acts on behalf of the partnership throughout. Online BN issuance is immediate; QST/NEQ processing generally requires 5–10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — under s. 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), the LP is fiscally transparent: income, losses and taxable capital gains flow through to general and limited partners per the partnership agreement, and the partnership pays no federal or provincial income tax, although it must file an information return (T5013 and Quebec TP-600). For GST/HST/QST, however, the partnership is itself a separate registrant. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income allocated to them, subject to treaty relief, and may be exposed to Part XIII withholding on allocations of management fees, interest or royalties. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under s. 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), the LP is fiscally transparent: income, losses and taxable capital gains flow through to general and limited partners per the partnership agreement, and the partnership pays no federal or provincial income tax, although it must file an information return (T5013 and Quebec TP-600). For GST/HST/QST, however, the partnership is itself a separate registrant. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income allocated to them, subject to treaty relief, and may be exposed to Part XIII withholding on allocations of management fees, interest or royalties. No tax-exempt zone applies to ordinary spice-trading activity in Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; corporate versus partnership status is determined exclusively by corporate and partnership law. Available elections relevant to the LP include: (a) partnership non-calendar fiscal-period election under s. 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act; (b) GST/HST qualifying-partnership election under ETA s. 273, mirrored by QST Act s. 459.30, to treat supplies between registrant partners holding 90%+ interests as nil-consideration; (c) annual or quarterly GST/HST/QST filing elections available to small suppliers; and (d) the Quick Method accounting election under ETA s. 227.1 (GST) with a QST parallel, for eligible registrants. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4 and 94B) apply to deductible payments channelled through partnerships; no elective hybrid-entity regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST: ETA s. 240(1) and s. 148 small-supplier rule; QST: QST Act s. 413). A non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Because export zero-rated supplies count toward the threshold while generating no net tax, the LP will nonetheless register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on Canadian procurement, warehousing and freight costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper are zero-rated supplies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1(a) (goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier) and under the parallel QST rule (QST Act, application of Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1). To claim the 0% rate, the LP must demonstrate that: (i) the goods were physically removed from Canada in commercial quantities; (ii) the sale was made to an arm's-length non-resident purchaser; and (iii) contemporaneous export evidence was retained (B13A export declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, carrier and freight records). Supply to a non-resident registrant that subsequently exports the goods may be zero-rated under s. 1(d) upon notice and proof of the recipient's export. Warehousing, packing and tolling services performed for a non-resident with no Canadian establishment may be zero-rated under Part V, s. 7. CRA interpretive policy is set out in GST/HST Memorandum 4.4.4 (Export of Goods).

  • Customs Export Declaration — B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) record filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List bearing HS 0712/0904 classification and the LP's BN/TVQ registration numbers
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing payment by the non-resident buyer

Canada and Quebec impose no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal-clearance API, or digital-stamping regime. Electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) are legally valid where integrity and legibility are preserved (ETA s. 286(1.2); Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 34.8). Mandatory electronic filing and remittance: GST/HST registrants with taxable revenues above CAD 1.5M must file and remit electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; Revenu Québec requires electronic QST filing and, for large businesses, XML/EDIB data exchange. CRA audit extraction follows the standard Audit Data Extraction (ADE) tool; no QR-code or government stamping is required on invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a claimed zero-rated export within the normal four-year reassessment window, or indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is proven, restoring GST at 5% and QST at 9.975% on the full invoice value, denying input tax credits, and charging arrears interest. Gross-negligence penalties of up to 25% of the relevant tax apply under ETA s. 280(1), with a QST Act equivalent; repeated false statements may trigger additional civil penalties of up to CAD 25,000 under ETA s. 327.1. False export declarations also attract CBSA penalties under the Customs Act of up to CAD 25,000 per declaration and potential criminal liability for fraudulent documentation under the Criminal Code. Export evidence must be retained for six years; deficiency in documentation results in retrospective reversal of the zero-rating together with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under both current and capital account transactions. There are no Canadian exchange control laws restricting foreign investment, capital repatriation, dividend/profit remittance, or conversion of CAD to foreign currency. Non-resident investors may freely remit profits, subject to Canadian withholding tax rules and the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters may retain and hold foreign currency revenues offshore, and repatriation is not mandated by the Bank of Canada or any regulator.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign exchange regulation is decentralized, with prudential oversight by OSFI, anti-money laundering supervision by FINTRAC, and derivatives/fintech licensing by provincial regulators such as the Autorité des marchés financiers in Quebec.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital banks and electronic money institutions are not treated as full-service banking counterparts by Canadian correspondent banks. An EMI serving Canadian clients or clearing CAD-denominated flows must register as a money services business with FINTRAC and may require Quebec-specific licensing if operating in the province. Canadian tier-1 banks will generally reject letters, counterparty references, or source-of-funds certifications from unregulated or offshore EMIs for treasury purposes.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax rate (federal 15.0% + Quebec 11.5%). The Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (pass-through); a corporate partner pays Part I tax on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income. A non-resident corporate partner with a permanent establishment in Canada/Quebec is taxed at the same combined rate on income attributed to the PE; absent a PE, treaty protection may exempt active business income. An individual partner is taxed at graduated personal rates up to 53.31% combined (Quebec 25.75% + federal 33%) plus applicable surtaxes.
