Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible for a low-risk consulting limited partnership only if it provides a physical street address in Quebec at which legal documents and notices can be received and a natural person or registered agent can accept service. A post-office box or purely digital address does not satisfy the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises. For tax and substance purposes, a virtual office alone will not establish commercial substance in Quebec; the partnership should maintain real management oversight, client-facing records, and decision-making evidence in Quebec where possible.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal income tax (My Business Account portal: www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html; Business Registration Online: broc-riar.cra-arc.gc.ca); Revenu Québec for Quebec QST and Quebec income tax (clicSÉUR enterprise portal: www.revenuquebec.ca; Registraire des entreprises du Québec for entity registration).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Quebec limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). 2) The general partner applies through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 for a CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account, declaring the legal type as partnership. 3) Register the QST account with Revenu Québec via clicSÉUR using the BN/NEQ (forms LM-1/LM-2) and open the partnership's Québec tax file. 4) Each non-resident limited partner, being deemed to carry on business in Quebec through the LP, obtains its own Canadian identification number for filing T5013/T1044 and Part XIII withholding obligations. 5) File the annual T5013 partnership information return with CRA and the equivalent Quebec partnership return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA ss. 96-103 and the mirroring provisions of the Quebec Taxation Act: the partnership computes income at the entity level but is not a taxpayer; each partner's allocable share is taxed in the partner's hands. Non-resident limited partners are deemed to carry on business in Quebec and Canada and are taxable on their Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty protection and Part XIII withholding on allocations and guaranteed payments.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; partnership classification is statutory, not elective. Available elections: (i) alternative fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) allowing a non-calendar fiscal year-end for the partnership when all partners are non-calendar-year filers; (ii) s. 103(1) election permitting partners to elect a reasonable allocation of income differing from stated profit-sharing ratios; (iii) designated-member election for T5013 filing purposes; (iv) for GST/HST, the partnership may elect to have the general partner file and remit on behalf of all partners. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4) apply mechanically where the Quebec LP is treated as opaque by a foreign partner jurisdiction; no election is available, but ex-ante characterization analysis is recommended.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 - mandatory GST/HST and QST registration is triggered when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). Voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits (ITCs) and input tax refunds (IVRs).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Advisory and consulting services (ISIC 7020) supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V, paragraph 7 of the Excise Tax Act and the mirroring rule in the Quebec Sales Tax Act (Schedule D, services to non-residents), provided: (a) the recipient is not resident in Canada; (b) the service is not performed in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (c) the recipient is not a GST/HST/QST registrant acquiring the service for use in its Canadian commercial activity. The overseas market-entry consultancy scope, which targets foreign markets only, satisfies these criteria if the contract clearly excludes Canadian on-shore deliverables and the benefit is enjoyed outside Canada.

  • Canadian export declaration (B13A/CAED or CBSA EDI export reporting) for any tangible incidental deliverables
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export where applicable
  • Commercial invoice quoting the zero-rating code (Schedule VI Part V, para. 7) and referencing the non-resident contract
  • Foreign-currency inward remittance evidence (bank SWIFT or Wise B2B receipt) matched to the invoice
  • Signed service agreement confirming foreign-market scope and absence of Canadian real/tangible property nexus

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or CRA-approved EDI/API software for registrants above prescribed thresholds. Revenu Québec mandates electronic filing for QST returns and has confirmed a phased introduction of mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and event-based e-reporting beginning in the 2026-2027 period, initially for large enterprises, with XML e-invoices transmitted through certified digital service providers.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit zero-rated consultancy claims against the stated scope-of-work, the non-resident status of the recipient, and actual bank remittance evidence; a finding that services were rendered in or for Canada re-characterizes the supply as taxable at 14.975% (5% GST + 9.975% QST) and permits disallowance of input tax credits. Penalties: GST/HST evasion penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax (ETA s. 285.1); false statements penalty of the greater of C$250 and 25% of the tax sought (ETA s. 280); gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated income tax (ITA s. 163.2); Quebec QST evasion penalty of up to C$25,000 plus 125% of evaded QST (Quebec Taxation Act s. 1059); interest runs from the balance-due date. The normal reassessment period is four years but is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established. No statutory Canadian FX repatriation deadline exists; the 180-day benchmark is an administrative intercompany collection and transfer-pricing documentation norm.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account convertibility and a substantially liberalized capital account. Profits, dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees are freely remittable but are generally subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 25%, reduced by treaty) and to FATCA/CRS informational reporting. Capital inflows such as equity or intercompany loans require no routine central bank authorization, but transactions by non-Canadians establishing control of a Canadian business may require notification or review under the Investment Canada Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender mandates or forced conversion timelines for export proceeds. Exporters may maintain receipts in any freely convertible currency in Canadian or foreign bank accounts. Practical compliance requires accurate invoicing, bank deposit records, and tax records under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act (GST/QST with respect to zero-rated services).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) acts as monetary and regulatory authority over retail payment systems; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates federally chartered banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/KYC supervision; there is no standalone foreign-exchange control authority in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-High viability for payment processing only. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise can provide cross-border payment and multi-currency facilitation without being chartered banks in Canada, but they may not take deposits or offer lending products. They must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, and if operating in Quebec, likely coordinate with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) with respect to money-services business licensing. Payments under the ISIC 7020 advisory scope can use EMI rails for low-value disbursements, but capital escrow and high-value treaty-sensitive flows should remain in tier-1 Canadian banking channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital for the Quebec limited partnership should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with an instructive statement referencing the partnership's Quebec enterprise registry number (NEQ), Canadian Business Number (BN), and transaction purpose. Equity contributions require no prior foreign-exchange approval; however, intercompany loan advances must be documented with a formal loan agreement, interest rate benchmark, and repayment schedule to satisfy Canadian tax thin capitalization and transfer-pricing rules. Investment Canada Act notification is mandatory for any acquisition of control that crosses statutory thresholds. Wires should designate a purpose code consistent with the OECD ISO 20022 categorization, such as direct investment or intercompany lending, and the receiving bank should receive an executed source-of-funds declaration.Outbound advisory fee settlements should use SWIFT MT103 (International Wire) or Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) when bank intermediation is required. For payments to overseas consultants, payment instructions should include complete invoice numbers, service descriptions, and tax treaty documentation to support reduced withholding rates where applicable. Same-day funding within Canada occurs via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the new Real-Time Rail (RTR); cross-border clearing in USD can be effected through CHIPS/Fedwire correspondent accounts, while EUR or Asian settlements require attention to time-zone cut-offs and intermediary bank charges.
