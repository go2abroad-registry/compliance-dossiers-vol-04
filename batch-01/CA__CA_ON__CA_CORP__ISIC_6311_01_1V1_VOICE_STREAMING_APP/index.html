Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals; Ontario Ministry of Finance administered through CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement (Ontario Corporation Tax).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (or federal CBCA with Ontario extra-provincial registration); obtain Ontario Business Registry initial filing. 2) Register with CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory TIN for corporate income tax (RC program), GST/HST (RT program), and payroll (RP program). 3) Non-resident shareholders are permitted; if the corporation is a non-resident-owned corporation, additional reporting applies (T2 Schedule 17). 4) Ontario corporation tax returns (T2-CO) are filed with CRA which administers Ontario corporate income tax under a single-admin agreement; Ontario due dates mirror federal returns (six months after fiscal year-end).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Canadian-incorporated business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada); no pass-through or S-corp analogue. Combined federal + Ontario general corporate income tax rate: 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); first CAD 500,000 of active business income (subject to associated-corporation aggregation and CAD 10M–15M taxable capital phase-outs) qualifies for the federal small business deduction (9%) and Ontario small business rate (3.2%), combined effective ~12.2%. Profits distributed to Canadian-resident shareholders are subject to the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credit integration regime; payments to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding (typically 25%, treaty-reduced). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Canadian-incorporated business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada); no pass-through or S-corp analogue. Combined federal + Ontario general corporate income tax rate: 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); first CAD 500,000 of active business income (subject to associated-corporation aggregation and CAD 10M–15M taxable capital phase-outs) qualifies for the federal small business deduction (9%) and Ontario small business rate (3.2%), combined effective ~12.2%. Profits distributed to Canadian-resident shareholders are subject to the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credit integration regime; payments to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding (typically 25%, treaty-reduced). No territorial exemption — Canada taxes worldwide active and passive income of resident corporations with foreign tax credits and the foreign affiliate surplus/FAPI system.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' classification election; classification follows the legal form under the CBCA/OBCA. Relevant statutory elections: (a) ETA s.156 and s.157 elections for closely-related corporations and qualifying subsidiaries to eliminate GST/HST on intercorporate supplies/recoveries; (b) ITA s.216 election allowing non-residents with Canadian rental income to file on net basis instead of 25% Part XIII gross withholding; (c) ITA ss.93(1.1)/93.1 elections for foreign affiliate dividend designations; (d) hybrid mismatch rules (in force for payments made after June 30, 2023) contain no elective check-the-box but impose legislative ordering rules (ITA ss.18.5–18.6) for structured arrangements; (e) under Canada's Global Minimum Tax Act (2024), a Canadian-headquartered group meeting the EUR 750M threshold may elect the qualified domestic top-up tax regime (Pillar Two, applicable to fiscal years commencing after December 31, 2023).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold): a person is required to register once CAD 30,000 or more of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) is made in the current calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold is shared with associated persons and includes supplies made from outside Canada; Canadian resident suppliers exceeding the threshold must register with an effective date at the beginning of the calendar month in which the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, s.23 provided the recipient is not registered under Part IX, the supply is not made to a non-resident individual in circumvention of the rules, and the service does not relate to real/tangible property situated in Canada. For digital/voice-streaming app subscriptions and virtual goods (intangible personal property), supplies to non-resident businesses or consumers are generally made outside Canada under the digital place-of-supply rules (recipient located outside Canada); such supplies are either zero-rated B2B exports or outside-the-scope B2C supplies. Where zero-rated or outside-scope, the supplier retains full input tax credits attributable to export revenue if documented as exports. The DST (Digital Services Tax) Act, effective January 1, 2024, applies a 3% tax on in-scope online advertising, social media, marketplace, and data revenue only for multinational groups with EUR 750M global revenue and CAD 20M Canadian DST revenue — not applicable to early-stage app, but monitored.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current account and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar floats freely with no surrender, conversion, approval, or registration requirements for trade-related or capital-related flows. Profit remittance hurdles are confined to taxation, not FX control: non-resident shareholders receiving dividends or deemed distributions from a Canadian Business Corporation face Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, generally reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable income tax treaty; inter-company payments must also satisfy transfer-pricing rules under section 247 of the Income Tax Act and the earnings-stripping limitations in section 18.4 (excessive interest and financing expenses) for large corporations. Non-residents may freely hold USD and other foreign currencies in Canadian bank accounts, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, dividends, and management fees is unrestricted. CRS/FATCA self-certification and FINTRAC reporting are the only procedural formalities attached to cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation or exchange into domestic currency. An ISIC 6311 exporter of digital voice-streaming services may lawfully retain 100% of foreign currency receipts indefinitely in a USD or multicurrency account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The only binding obligations are record-keeping and statistical: goods exporters file through the Canadian Export Reporting System (G13), while service exporters must respond to Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services surveys and ensure that each cross-border financial transaction of C$10,000 or more is reported by the financial intermediary to FINTRAC as a Large Value Electronic Funds Transfer. Exchange of export receipts into CAD is a purely commercial operation executed at the bank's prevailing spot rate, with no central bank intervention or commission levy.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I/II banks under the Bank Act, and FINTRAC for cross-border electronic funds transfer and currency transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Minister of Finance retains residual emergency powers under the Currency Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the Emergency Economic Measures Act, but Canada maintains no standing foreign exchange control administration or licensing regime for routine commercial FX transactions.
