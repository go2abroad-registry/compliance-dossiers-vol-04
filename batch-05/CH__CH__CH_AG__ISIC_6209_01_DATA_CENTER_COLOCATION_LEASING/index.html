Minimum share capital of CHF 100,000, fully subscribed at incorporation; at least 20% of each subscribed share's par value, with aggregate paid-in cash not below CHF 50,000, must be deposited into a bank blocked account in the name of the future company, and the bank's blocking confirmation (Bestätigung nach Art. 632 Abs. 3 OR) must be annexed to the deed; funds remain escrowed until the Commercial Register entry, unless replaced by a qualified contribution-in-kind report (Sacheinlagebericht).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); portal: www.estv.admin.ch (VAT e-services), www.uid.admin.ch (central UID register); cantonal tax administrations handle cantonal and communal direct taxes; Federal Customs Administration (Oberzolldirektion OZD / Federal Customs Administration FCA) operates the customs export/import procedure (e-dec / Passar).

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft obtains its statutory business identifier UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, format CHE-###.###.###) automatically upon entry in the cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister) after notarial incorporation; no separate TIN application is required. The Commercial Register notification triggers the cantonal direct tax assessment file. For VAT, the AG must file a VAT registration declaration with the ESTV/AFC through the VAT e-portal within 60 days after the end of the calendar year in which worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 (Art. 10, 11, 66 MWSTG); the ESTV then issues the VAT number CHE-###.###.### MWST. Foreign non-resident entities with Swiss-source taxable supplies exceeding CHF 100,000 must use the simplified non-established-person registration procedure via the ESTV and may be required to appoint a Swiss tax representative; alternatively, a foreign entity that is not required to register may claim input VAT refunds through the reciprocal Form 15 EUR procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Swiss AG is resident in Switzerland, has unlimited tax liability on worldwide profits (Art. 50 DBG/LIFD), and is additionally subject to cantonal and communal corporate income tax and capital tax; there is no check-the-box or flow-through/transparent regime under Swiss law. The combined federal, cantonal and communal profit tax rate ranges from approximately 11.6% to 21% depending on canton; the federal portion is a flat 8.5% on net profit (effective federal rate approx. 7.8% after deduction of cantonal tax, itself tax-deductible). Distributions and interest to shareholders attract 35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) subject to treaty relief, whereas operating rentals for colocation services fall outside the withholding tax net. For VAT, the AG is a taxable person (Art. 10 MWSTG) and supplies of data centre colocation services are standard-rated at 8.1% unless a zero-rating or out-of-scope place-of-supply rule applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration (opt-in) for a taxable person below the CHF 100,000 threshold, and voluntary VAT group registration (Unternehmensgruppe, Art. 21 MWSTG) among Swiss entities forming an integrated enterprise; (ii) election between the effective input-tax deduction method and the simplified apportioned-rate method (Saldosteuersatz, Art. 37 MWSTG) for taxpayers below the applicable turnover ceilings; (iii) the place-of-supply declaration under Art. 18(2)(b) MWSTG, under which a supplier of cross-border services may subject to Swiss VAT services that are effectively used or exploited in Switzerland in order to preserve input-VAT recovery, while services supplied to a foreign business recipient qualify for zero-rating under Art. 23(2) MWSTG if the foreign-status evidence is complete; (iv) for federal direct tax, elections relating to the participation exemption (Beteiligungsabzug, Art. 69 DBG), the R&D super-deduction, patent box, and notional interest deduction introduced by the 2020 Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (STAF/WEF 2020). Swiss law does not provide corporate income tax consolidation or fiscal unity for direct tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies of goods and services (Art. 10 and 11 MWSTG); non-established foreign entities count Swiss-source turnover only; mandatory registration applies from the moment the threshold is exceeded, with a notification deadline of 60 days after the end of the relevant calendar year; voluntary registration is available to all taxable persons, and a VAT group can be registered where a parent and its Swiss group companies form a financially, economically, and organisationally integrated enterprise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (tax-free with full input-VAT deduction, Art. 23 MWSTG) applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Switzerland, evidenced by a customs export declaration; and (b) services supplied to a recipient with its seat or residence abroad, provided the service is qualified as a business-to-business supply under Art. 18(2) MWSTG and is not effectively used or exploited in Switzerland. For data centre colocation, the Federal Tax Administration treats server housing as a service connected to movable tangible property located in Switzerland; therefore the supply occurs where the server is situated. A Swiss AG may invoice a foreign colocation customer at the zero rate only if it can prove: the customer is an entrepreneur or legal person with a foreign seat and no Swiss permanent establishment; the service is supplied for the customer's foreign business activity and is not used or exploited in Switzerland; and the external documentation (contract, foreign VAT/UID certificate, payment evidence) is conclusive at the time of invoicing. Where the foreign customer uses the service in Switzerland, the supply remains subject to Swiss VAT and the supplier may elect Swiss taxation to secure input-tax recovery; otherwise, services outside scope under the recipient-location rule need no Swiss VAT charge but still require equivalent proof. Import VAT and reverse-charge obligations are administered by the Federal Customs Administration; the EU OSS/MOSS regimes do not apply in Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued via the e-dec / Passar procedure, including the customs tariff number, gross weight, statistical value, and the export-license or leaving certificate where applicable
  • Commercial invoice containing the mandatory Art. 26 MWSTG data elements, the applicable legal basis for zero-rating, and the currency and incoterms of the export supply
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from Switzerland: CMR waybill, airway bill (AWB/IATA), or bill of lading, issued within the statutory export period
  • Customs or carrier evidence of arrival of the goods in the foreign destination country (customs entry or arrival stamp) for tangible goods exports
  • Written colocation or bare-metal hosting contract with the foreign customer specifying the place of consumption, the technical service scope (rack space, power, cooling, cross-connects, remote hands), and the Swiss infrastructure location
  • Certificate of the foreign customer's statutory business registration, such as a foreign VAT ID, USt-IdNr., BEVAT number, OSS VAT ID, or chamber-of-commerce extract, together with a declaration that the customer has no Swiss permanent establishment
  • Bank payment evidence or Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103, credit advice) showing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign customer within the documented payment term

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transmission, or digital stamping mandate comparable to Italy or Latin American regimes. E-invoicing is voluntary and market-driven, with Swiss eBill (SIX), Peppol BIS, and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X formats accepted. Mandatory invoice elements are set by Art. 26 MWSTG and include supplier UID/VAT number, recipient UID/VAT number, supply date, description, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount; simplified invoices with fewer elements are lawful below CHF 400. Since 1 January 2022, domestic invoices must carry the QR-invoice (SwissQR) payment slip, but this is a payment standard, not a fiscal-clearance mechanism. For zero-rated exports, the exporter must retain the invoice and a complete evidence set; foreign business recipients may be identified by their foreign VAT/UID number rather than a Swiss UID.

The export evidence bundle must be retained for at least 10 years (Art. 32 and 58 MWSTG; commercial records), and the export evidence must be complete, consistent, and conclusive already at the time of invoicing. Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating triggers retroactive VAT assessment at the standard rate of 8.1% on the gross consideration, plus default interest at 5% per annum (Art. 27, 30 and 32 MWSTG). Administrative fines for intentional tax evasion reach up to four times the evaded tax, capped at CHF 800,000 (Art. 70 MWSTG); disciplinary fines of up to CHF 10,000 apply for procedural breaches (Art. 72 MWSTG); wilful tax fraud is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to five years (Art. 78 MWSTG). The taxpayer additionally loses the corresponding input-VAT deduction, and the foreign customer may face local reverse-charge assessments. Switzerland imposes no exchange-control or statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day term in compliance practice serves as the evidentiary ceiling for receiving and substantiating export consideration and aligning arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation. If the consideration is not repatriated within that period without adequate justification, the payment may be recharacterised as shareholder support or a constructive dividend subject to the 35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Art. 4 and 13 VStG), together with interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised and convertible currency regime. The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible for both current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties, leases) and capital-account transactions (equity, shareholder debt, dividends, liquidation proceeds) under the Federal Act on Currency and Means of Payment (WZG). There are no capital controls, no foreign-exchange licensing requirements, and no prior SNB approval is required for cross-border capital movements or profit remittance by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The only statutory levy on outbound dividends is the 35% Swiss federal withholding tax, which is recoverable or reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty or the notification procedure for qualifying corporate shareholders; automatic exchange of information (AEOI) reporting applies. For a data-centre colocation business, contracts may be freely denominated in CHF, EUR, or USD, and no restriction is imposed on the collection of cross-border colocation fees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline under Swiss monetary law. Colocation and service export receipts may be retained in any fully convertible currency in either domestic or foreign bank accounts. For balance-of-payments statistics only, the SNB aggregates bank reporting; no individual company filing, conversion mandate, or surrender deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, including operational oversight of Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) and EuroSIC clearing railways.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury use. Offshore EU/UK-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot issue a Swiss CH-IBAN, lack access to SIC/EuroSIC settlement, and cannot satisfy the statutory blocked deposit verification required for the AG minimum share capital. FINMA-authorised fintech banks holding a FinTech licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (public-deposit cap of CHF 100 million), such as Neon or Yapeal, may serve only as secondary accounts for low-value recurring tenant-fee collections; they generally do not offer documentary letters of credit, FX hedging, or same-day CHF clearing. The legal constraint under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) and banking practice is that the capital escrow and primary liquidity rails must be held with a FINMA-supervised bank participating in Swiss clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial equity contribution (minimum nominal CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up and freely at the disposal of the board) must be credited to the bank's segregated blocked capital account (Sperrkonto). Routing: SWIFT MT103, or an internal Swiss payment via SIC for domestic shareholders, with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity-like capital) or 'COST' (capital contribution); the remitter must be the subscribing shareholder, never a third party. The commercial register requires the bank's capital deposit certificate (Einzahlungsbestätigung / Attestation de libération) before registration is finalised. Subsequent shareholder loans must be routed via MT103 with purpose code 'LEND' or 'INTC', supported by a written shareholder-loan agreement at arm's-length interest, and are governed by the capital-liability provisions of the Swiss Code of Obligations (Articles 632 et seq.).Clearing-rail matrix: (i) domestic CHF disbursements via SIC real-time final settlement; (ii) EUR payments within the SEPA zone, including Switzerland as a SEPA participating country, via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Direct Debit (Core or B2B scheme) for recurring monthly tenant collections, cleared through EuroSIC; (iii) non-SEPA cross-border payments via SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose codes referencing the underlying colocation or service invoice; (iv) high-value data-centre hardware imports (servers, cooling, and power infrastructure exceeding CHF 100,000 equivalent) through an irrevocable confirmed documentary Letter of Credit (UCP 600) or standby letter of credit payable at sight, settled via SWIFT; and (v) tax and social-security disbursements to Swiss authorities through the Swiss QR-bill (QR-IBAN) or SIC direct debit.Outright FX forwards (CHF/EUR, CHF/USD) up to 12-month tenor, executed under an ISDA or Swiss Master Agreement for OTC derivatives with the account-holding bankFX swaps (tom-next, spot-forward, and forward-forward swaps) limited to balance-sheet liquidity managementPlain-vanilla European-style FX options (call and put) with a documented non-speculative hedging purpose and a bank credit-line limitCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) matched to the currency and interest profile of the data-centre facility loan or capital leaseNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only as a hedge for the exotic counterparty-currency leg of a commercial contract; not market-standard for CHF because the Swiss franc is fully convertible

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for core treasury use. Offshore EU/UK-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot issue a Swiss CH-IBAN, lack access to SIC/EuroSIC settlement, and cannot satisfy the statutory blocked deposit verification required for the AG minimum share capital. FINMA-authorised fintech banks holding a FinTech licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (public-deposit cap of CHF 100 million), such as Neon or Yapeal, may serve only as secondary accounts for low-value recurring tenant-fee collections; they generally do not offer documentary letters of credit, FX hedging, or same-day CHF clearing. The legal constraint under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) and banking practice is that the capital escrow and primary liquidity rails must be held with a FINMA-supervised bank participating in Swiss clearing.
