Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — central federal tax authority administering GST/HST, income tax, partnership return filing, and Business Number registration; Ontario Ministry of Finance — responsible for the Ontario provincial component of HST, administered through CRA under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement. Online portals: CRA Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Ontario Business Registry (ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Ontario must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before or upon commencing taxable activity. The non-resident LP or its general partner completes Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or registers online through CRA My Business Account. The BN serves as the tax identification number and the LP program account is used for GST/HST and payroll. No separate TIN is issued; Ontario LP registration under the Limited Partnerships Act is obtained through the Ontario Business Registry. The LP must disclose the general partner's identity and foreign jurisdiction details; a CRA trust/partnership account may be assigned for filing T5013 partnership information returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists in Canada. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the federal Income Tax Act (ITA s.96); partners report allocable income separately. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada may elect under ITA s.216 to compute Canadian-source business income on a net basis and be taxed on that amount in Canada. Hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance rules under ITA ss.18.4–18.6 are mandatory and cannot be displaced by elective check-the-box treatment. GST/HST status does not create entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. Factoring and accounts receivable assignments are generally exempt financial supplies under Part VII of Schedule V, which affects threshold calculation; a non-resident factor carrying on cross-border factoring may nevertheless be required to register if it makes other taxable supplies, and voluntary registration is permitted. Exempt financial services and zero-rated exported financial services are treated separately for threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports them, the export is supported by documentary evidence, and the goods are not substantially consumed in Canada before export. Financial services, including trade finance, accounts receivable factoring and reverse factoring, are generally exempt; however, certain financial services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part IX, including eligible exported financial services, where the service is supplied to a non-resident recipient, the substance is a financial service rather than a Canadian advisory or administrative service, and the supply is not related to a Canadian establishment or prescribed financial instrument. The LP claiming zero-rating must demonstrate that the factoring service is performed for and used by the non-resident counterparty and that invoicing and payment flows evidence the cross-border nature. Exempt domestic factoring of Canadian receivables does not attract zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA export reporting record, including Canada Customs Coding Form B3 or AES/CERS equivalent, evidencing the goods were exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading / ocean, air, rail or highway waybill showing the consignee and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or wire transfer record evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment and proceeds repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and underlying factoring or export contract naming the non-resident buyer or factor, including foreign tax ID or incorporation identifier

Canada currently has no mandatory national real-time fiscal clearance, no CRA API-based pre-clearance stamping, and no digital stamp requirement for GST/HST invoices. CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices; electronic invoices and records must be readable, accurate, and retained in a format supporting CRA audit. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE system where required. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing aligned with Peppol BIS is being adopted progressively but has not yet become a statutory clearance mandate in Ontario.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within the normal four-year period, extended to twelve years where the return contains a misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, omission or fraudulent intent. If zero-rating is denied, the LP is liable for the unremitted 13% HST, compound daily interest, and penalties. Gross negligence penalties can apply under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, generally the greater of C$100 and 25% of the net tax sought to be recovered. Evasion can lead to summary conviction or indictment, fines, and imprisonment. The general partner of the Ontario LP may also face joint and several liability for the LP's GST/HST obligations where it acts as a directing mind or fails to maintain adequate books and records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account, and Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange-control approval, licence, or central-bank conversion ticket is required for trade settlements, profit remittances, dividends, interest, capital injections, or loan repayments. The only practical profit-remittance hurdle is tax, not FX: distributions to non-resident limited partners attract Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., 15% under the Canada-US Tax Convention), and the Ontario LP must file T5013/NR4 information returns. There is no repatriation quota, no conversion queue, no surrender obligation, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks. The CAD operates under a floating exchange-rate regime and the Bank of Canada does not ration or license private-sector FX transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds; exporters and factoring platforms may retain foreign-currency balances in resident or non-resident accounts at Schedule I banks. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent. Remaining compliance duties are limited to FINTRAC large electronic-funds-transfer reporting (C$10,000 or more), balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada, and proper accounting of foreign-exchange gains and losses in the LP's books.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority; no separate foreign exchange administration exists in Canada. Prudential regulation of Schedule I commercial banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing oversight is vested in FINTRAC; the Department of Finance sets policy under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and the Income Tax Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. EU and UK electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not have passporting rights into Canada; a payment service provider must be registered or authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada and must have a Canadian place of business to offer payment services, with the mandatory registration regime fully in force since 7 November 2024. Such EMIs may therefore serve only auxiliary low-value supplier payouts and cannot act as the capital escrow custodian or the primary clearing rail, because they do not settle CAD via Lynx/ACSS/SWIFT as an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank. The factoring model itself is an enhanced-compliance risk: unless the LP operates true-sale factoring of its own purchased receivables, it risks FINTRAC classification as a money services business under the PCMLTFA, which would impose MSB registration, a full compliance program, and travel-rule obligations. The defence is to confine all funds flows to the LP's own principal accounts and to document each receivable purchase.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate for corporate partners; limited partnerships are flow-through and do not pay entity-level income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII (ITA s.212(1)(a)) for taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. For a Limited Partnership, distributions are not dividends; non-resident corporate partners with a Canadian permanent establishment are potentially subject to a 25% Part XIV branch profit tax on after-tax Canadian business profits, reduced to 5% for US corporate partners owning 10% or more of the partnership under the Canada-US DTA. Non-corporate non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on allocated Canadian-source income, with no additional withholding at the LP level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate face value of a controlled transaction with a non-resident related party; for cross-border factoring transactions, both the purchase face value of receivables and the implicit financing fee are countable. Where the aggregate value of services, intangibles, or financial instruments exceeds this threshold, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File, Master File, and a CRA country-by-country report if applicable) must be prepared before the earlier of the return filing date or the deadline in a CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a separate statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres. However, the Income Tax Act s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule ('GAAR') and the common law 'substance over form' and 'sham' doctrines will recharacterize a limited partnership that lacks economic substance, a business purpose, or a real Canadian presence. To be respected, the LP must maintain a principal office in Ontario, employ or engage Canadian management with real decision-making authority, execute factoring contracts in Canada, carry appropriate books and records in Ontario, and have sufficient capital relative to the risk assumed. A pure pass-through shell outside Canada with no Ontario substance will be denied treaty benefits and subject to GAAR reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: upon conviction, fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to two years. Civil liability includes the greater of the 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) or a 10% late-filing penalty under s.162(2), plus daily interest and compound interest. The CRA may also freeze bank accounts and issue garnishment orders.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted withholding or source deductions; directors who authorized or acquiesced in the evasion may be personally assessed, prohibited from leaving Canada by court-ordered travel bans, and given custodial sentences of up to two years.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment may disallow the avoidance transaction, adjust income to arm's-length amounts, and impose a 'GAAR penalty' only if a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation; indirect consequences include a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2), disgorgement of tax benefits, and denial of treaty rights under the Limitation on Benefits clause.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they signed an inaccurate T5013 or directed a sham structure; courts may issue a declaration of quantum of tax, order civil restitution of diverted amounts, prohibit the director from leaving Canada, and, if fraud is established, impose imprisonment. CRA administrative practice seeks personal liability against directors of a general corporate partner where the partnership assets are insufficient to satisfy collectible arrears.
Anti-Money Laundering / Unregistered Money Services Business

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, a failure to register as an MSB triggers liability to a FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD $250,000 for each violation for a non-bank entity; on indictment, monetary penalties are unlimited and imprisonment may reach two years. Ontario may also suspend or revoke a trade finance licence under the Provincial Money-Service Business Licensing Act, and the Canada Business Corporations Act allows the Attorney General to apply for a restraining order against the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, or condoned unregistered MSB activity can be charged personally; criminal sanctions include fines and imprisonment up to five years under the Criminal Code, travel bans, asset freezes, and personal confiscation of proceeds of crime. Directors of the corporate general partner are routinely named in FINTRAC penalty notices and may be barred from future directorship.
Transfer Pricing Documentation / Misalignment

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess the partnership under ITA s.247(2) and apply the s.247(3) transfer pricing penalty: 10% of the excess (or shortfall) adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CAD $5,000,000 or 10% of the partnership's gross revenue for the period, whichever is greater. Penalties are non-deductible; interest accrues from the initial tax return filing date; a default penalty for missing TP records can be CAD $1,000 per month up to CAD $24,000.

Director personal liability: If a director knowingly participated in a transfer pricing manipulation or signed a false allocation, the CRA can assert liability against the director for the 10% TP penalty under the 'director's liability' provisions of s.227.1 (where penalties equal unremitted amounts) and, in egregious circumstances, prosecute the director for tax evasion, which carries imprisonment and cancellation of passport or travel privileges.
