Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration and Tax Portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/businesses.html)

        
E-government filing portal
Domestic Canadian entities including Limited Partnerships register with the Canada Revenue Agency using Form T2125 to obtain a Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identification number. For Ontario-based LPs, registration with the Ontario Ministry of Finance is also required for provincial tax purposes. The process involves submitting the form online via the CRA My Business account or by mail with supporting documents such as the partnership agreement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no surrender requirements, exchange controls, or prior approval hurdles for capital injections, loan repayments, or profit and distribution remittances by a Canadian Limited Partnership (LP). Non-resident limited partners may receive distributions without FX approval; the only practical constraints are the LP agreement, Ontario partnership law, Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act where applicable, FATCA/CRS reporting, and FINTRAC reporting for large cash or wire transfers above CAD 10,000.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Canadian law does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, including SaaS subscription revenues and licensing fees. Export proceeds may be retained in original foreign currency, held in foreign-denominated Canadian bank accounts, or converted on the open market. Firms must only observe general AML reporting rules, specifically FINTRAC large-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting for cross-border movements above CAD 10,000.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) serves as the central monetary authority; Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control agency or exchange-control law. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates prudential banking conduct, while FINTRAC administers cross-border currency and wire reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium, with material constraints as a supplementary collection and payment rail, not as primary treasury infrastructure. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Platform, Stripe Treasury, and Payoneer can offer expedited USD/EUR settlement and multi-currency collection. However, an offshore EMI is not a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II bank; if the EMI holds itself out as providing payment services to Canadians it must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain Canadian AML compliance. The LP should retain a Canadian chartered bank as the master treasury account and use EMIs only for isolated B2B collection or contractor payouts, subject to verified sanctions screening and reconciliation controls.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Opaque beneficial ownership of the LP, including non-resident limited partners or layered corporate general-partner entities
        
Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership of the LP, including non-resident limited partners or layered corporate general-partner entities

        
Defense evidence: Signed Limited Partnership Agreement, complete register of limited partners, one-page ownership and control chain, full UBO declaration by the general partner, notarized passport copies of all material signatories and beneficial owners, certificate of LP registration from Ontario, good-standing certificates for corporate partners, and a FINTRAC-compliant written beneficial-ownership record kept current at all times.

      

      
      

        High-volume SaaS subscription receipts from foreign logistics fleets with fragmented small-ticket cross-border transactions
        
Root cause: High-volume SaaS subscription receipts from foreign logistics fleets with fragmented small-ticket cross-border transactions

        
Defense evidence: Technology service agreements and MSA with customers, current pricing schedules, sample B2B invoices, Stripe settlement reports and gateway transaction logs, website terms of service and privacy policy, a written AML/ATF risk assessment for the logistics SaaS vertical, monthly revenue reconciliation from payment processors to bank statements, and transaction monitoring logs for unusually large single-batch settlements.

      

      
      

        Transfers to or from jurisdictions with heightened sanctions or AML exposure, or payments to newly formed contractor entities
        
Root cause: Transfers to or from jurisdictions with heightened sanctions or AML exposure, or payments to newly formed contractor entities

        
Defense evidence: OFAC/UN/EU/UK sanctions screening results for the LP, all counterparties, and remitting banks; geolocation evidence for end customers; contractual clauses confirming no sanctioned end-user deployment; contractor invoices with banking details matching corporate registries; IP and device metadata from the SaaS platform; and a documented escalation matrix for denied-party screening hits.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant routing for LP capital injections is effected by SWIFT MT103 wires from each limited partner to the LP's Canadian operating account, using the LP legal name and account number, the bank's institution/transit/account identifiers, and a detailed payment narrative such as 'capital contribution per LP agreement dated [date]' or 'capital call reference [number]'. Canada does not impose mandatory SWIFT purpose codes; however, remitters and receiving banks must provide accurate narrative descriptions, complete OFAC and FINTRAC screening, and a completed FATCA/CRS self-certification for non-resident contributors. For ongoing SaaS platform operations, inbound recurring license fees may use Stripe Billing settlement into the same Canadian operating account, with settlement reports retained for audit. All inbound or outbound EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more require FINTRAC reporting; inflows connected to discounted promissory notes or shareholder loans must be documented by signed loan agreements to prevent deemed capitalization issues.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments, contractor disbursements, and supplier settlements; Canadian domestic ACH (via Payments Canada) for CAD-denominated payroll and operational expenses; commercial documentary letters of credit issued by the Canadian relationship bank for large fleet hardware purchases or logistics equipment procurement where the supplier requires bank payment undertakings. USD payments may be cleared through SWIFT/CHIPS or the bank's internal USD account network; low-value partner and contractor payouts may route through Interac e-Transfer or the CPA's Real-Time Rail where available. All outbound disbursements must be supported by invoices or contracts, logged with final-beneficiary details, and screened against sanctions and politically exposed person lists.Plain-vanilla deliverable foreign exchange forwards (FX forwards) for USD/CAD and key operating currencies, executed through the Canadian relationship bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank FX termsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-established currencies where physical settlement is impractical due to convertibility obstaclesCross-currency swaps for institutional-scale matched principal conversions on capital calls or debt facilitiesFX options and zero-cost collar structures where permitted as non-speculative hedging for commercial revenues
