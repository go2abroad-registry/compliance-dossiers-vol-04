Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni), Main VAT Division (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; official portal www.estv.admin.ch; corporate identifier portal www.uid.ch; commercial register search www.zefix.ch. Cantonal/communal direct taxes: competent cantonal tax authority of the registered seat (e.g., Steuerverwaltung Zug) and communal tax administrations for federal, cantonal and communal profit taxes.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporation of the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister) automatically triggers issuance of the Swiss Business Identifier (UID-CH) ex officio by the Federal Statistical Office; no separate UID application is needed. (2) For Swiss VAT, the AG must file registration with the FTA Main VAT Division within 30 days after exceeding the statutory turnover threshold (Art. 10 para 2 lit. a, Art. 66 MWSTG). The VAT number is identical to the UID-CH with the suffix '-MWST' (e.g., CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx-MWST). Registration is delivered via the FTA online e-service (Swiss VAT registration form / MWST 31) and becomes effective on the date notified. (3) For direct federal, cantonal and communal income tax, the AG files an annual tax return with the cantonal tax authority of its statutory seat; the tax domicile is the registered office (Art. 50 DBG, Art. 20 StHG). No separate federal TIN is issued; the UID-CH serves as the uniform tax identifier for federal, cantonal and VAT purposes. (4) Foreign entities with a Swiss VAT or direct tax nexus register via the FTA foreign-entity registration form (Formular 501) and obtain a UID-CH; a foreign company operating through a Swiss permanent establishment must register its Swiss branch in the Commercial Register and file branch tax returns. (5) Re-registration obligations arise upon group VAT election (Art. 13 MWSTG), transfer of seat, or reorganization.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Swiss AG is an independent legal entity resident in Switzerland and is fully subject to Swiss corporate income tax. Statutory federal profit tax rate: 8.5% (effective approx. 4.66% after deduction of federal tax as a business expense); combined cantonal/communal effective rates (e.g., in Zug) approx. 8.5%–10.5%, yielding an overall federal-plus-cantonal effective burden typically 11.5%–15% depending on canton and finance mix. Territorial scope: unlimited tax liability on worldwide income, with relief for foreign permanent establishments and real estate via applicable double tax treaties and overall income allocation. The AG is a per se corporation under US check-the-box principles (no pass-through election available). The entity has a statutory minimum capital of CHF 100,000 (50% paid up) and unrestricted non-resident ownership.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; the AG is per se taxable. Available statutory elections and mitigation mechanisms: (1) Participation exemption for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income (10% minimum holding / CHF 1 million market value threshold, Art. 20 para 1bis DBG and corresponding StHG provisions); (2) VAT group taxation election under Art. 13 MWSTG, allowing commercial consolidation for VAT purposes; (3) Option to waive VAT exemption on real estate supplies under Art. 22 MWSTG; (4) Voluntary VAT registration below the threshold to claim input VAT recovery (Art. 11 MWSTG); (5) Thin-capitalization safe-harbor structuring with prescribed debt-equity ratios applicable to shareholder loans; (6) Hybrid mismatch rules under Art. 58a–58f DBG (effective since 1 January 2020) impose automatic, non-elective matching adjustments, with disclosure-based reliefs for certain hybrid instruments; no elective anti-hybrid regime exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 (annual turnover derived from supplies located in Switzerland for entrepreneurs domiciled or resident in Switzerland; Art. 10 para 2 lit. a MWSTG). The threshold is worldwide in respect of taxable Swiss-located turnover and includes supplies taxable at 0% as well as tax-exempt supplies attributable to the Swiss activity; it excludes supplies located abroad under the place-of-supply rules (Art. 7–8 MWSTG). A resident AG that primarily bills foreign marine underwriting recipients abroad may therefore remain unregistered if only foreign-located supplies are made; non-resident entrepreneurs are liable to Swiss VAT from the first taxable supply in Switzerland with no threshold. Registration must be filed within 30 days after the threshold is exceeded (Art. 66 MWSTG). Voluntary registration is permitted for input VAT deduction (Art. 11 MWSTG).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Marine hull and cargo damage survey services of a Swiss AG qualify for Swiss export treatment at 0% where the services are located in Switzerland under the statutory place-of-supply rules (Art. 7–8 MWSTG) but are supplied for a foreign recipient, notably a foreign insurer, reinsurer, P&I club or shipowner. Under Art. 23 para 2 MWSTG, export zero-rating applies to services that are performed from Switzerland for the benefit of an entrepreneur situated abroad, provided the benefit is realized abroad and the recipient is proven foreign. In the conventional B2B case where the foreign underwriter is the recipient and the service is deemed supplied at the recipient's foreign seat, the supply falls entirely outside Swiss VAT (no Swiss VAT is charged and no zero-rating has to be claimed). If the surveying activity is performed on Swiss territory (e.g., Rhine port cargo inspection) and the service is nonetheless located in Switzerland for the foreign recipient, the 0% export rate can be claimed upon substantiation. Exportation of physical goods linked to survey work (test samples, equipment) follows Art. 23 para 1 MWSTG and requires a sworn Swiss customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Déclaration d'exportation) with movement reference number from Swiss Customs, where any goods are exported in connection with the survey assignment
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill/CMR road consignment note covering international movement of survey-related goods
  • Commercial invoice with express 0% export/foreign-supply reference, currency of account and service description of vessel hull/cargo claims inspection
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank account statement documenting receipt of the survey fee from the foreign underwriter abroad
  • Signed survey engagement order or service contract evidencing the foreign recipient's legal address, foreign VAT/identification number and the marine insurance claims context
  • Survey report and navigation/damage documentation proving the executed marine hull/cargo damage inspection (ISIC 5222 scope) at the specified location

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous API transmission or digital stamping regime applies to Swiss VAT; Switzerland operates on self-assessment and documentary audit. Mandatory invoice content under Art. 26 MWSTG: seller name/address/registration number or foreign ID, buyer name/address, date, nature of the supply, consideration, and separate statement of the VAT rate and amount. Domestic QR-billing (Swiss QR Code) and eBill are established norms; structured e-invoicing (B2G) becomes mandatory for invoices to the federal administration effective 1 January 2026. Marine surveyors should maintain Peppol BIS 3.0/electronic invoicing capability for EU recipients that impose e-invoicing. Statutory record-retention obligation: 10 years (Art. 958f CO; Art. 83 MWSTG).

Swiss VAT law contains no express statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; however, the 180-day window is applied here as the documentary settlement benchmark for zero-rating evidence (inward remittance certificate) to withstand FTA audit. Retrospective VAT assessments and input VAT clawbacks follow a 5-year limitation period in standard cases (Art. 72 para 3 MWSTG) and a 10-year period for tax evasion (Art. 71 para 3 MWSTG); default interest runs at the statutory overdue rate (currently 4.75% p.a.). Intentional VAT evasion attracts an administrative fine of up to four times the evaded tax (Art. 97 para 2 MWSTG); negligence is punishable under Art. 97 para 3 MWSTG, and jeopardizing tax revenue under Art. 96 MWSTG carries fixed regulatory fines. Fraus legis or abusive use of certificates further triggers restitution of input VAT, disqualification of the 0% rate, and re-assessment at 8.1% (or applicable reduced rates) on the full survey fee. In aggravated cases involving forged customs declarations or fictitious foreign recipients, criminal prosecution under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 146 fraud), the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law, and the direct tax evasion provisions (Art. 186–188 DBG by analogy) may result in custodial penalties and publication of the sanction. The marine surveyor must therefore preserve a complete, unbroken evidence chain: service contract, foreign-recipient identification, intercontinental transport/delivery proof, SWIFT/remittance advice, and underlying survey field records.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for resident or non-resident shareholders, subject to corporate law solvency tests and withholding tax compliance. The SNB focuses on monetary policy and financial stability, not transactional approval. Marine insurance surveying fee receipts from foreign underwriters are classified as services on the current account and are freely creditable to Swiss bank accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert foreign currency receipts into CHF. Marine survey service revenues earned from non-resident underwriters may be retained in foreign currency (e.g. USD, EUR) or converted freely. No mandatory conversion ratio or timeline applies. The only obligation is accurate reporting of cross-border service income in the corporate tax return and, where applicable, VAT treatment under Swiss value added tax law.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for ancillary payment processing, but Low as a primary corporate bank account for a Swiss AG. Offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex cannot serve as a substitute for a Swiss licensed bank account for statutory corporate purposes (e.g. AG capital maintenance, Swiss withholding tax declarations, creditor protections). They may be used as secondary collection/payment rails for low-value underwriting wires if they hold an FCA/EMI license or EU EMI license, provided the company maintains its principal transactional relationship with a Swiss-regulated bank. Legal constraint: Swiss anti-money laundering (AMLA) requires the financial intermediary to be licensed in Switzerland or operate under a recognized foreign equivalent; many EMIs restrict business models involving insurance claims and surveying due to FinTech sector risk policies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital for a Swiss AG must be deposited into a blocked Swiss bank account (Sperrkonto) verified by a Swiss notary and the Commercial Register. Subsequent capital increases use a dividend/contribution route: wire transfer from the shareholder's bank account to the AG's account at UBS/ZKB/BCV with purpose code 'Erhöhung des Aktienkapitals' or 'capital contribution reserve (Agio)'; provide beneficiary legal entity identifier (LEI), UID number, and notarized board resolution. For non-CHF injections, use MT103 with SWIFT field 72 clarifying 'Reserve from capital increase, no remuneration, no commercial transaction'; the bank will issue an exact confirmation for the commercial register. FX conversion of foreign-currency capital is permitted at market rate; the SNB has no approval role.For service fee disbursements to subcontractor surveyors, paying agents, and industry associations: use SEPA/CH-Interbank Clearing SIC for EUR/CHF within Switzerland/EEA; for non-EEA and USD remittances, use SWIFT MT103 with advantageous clearing through correspondent banks (UBS correspondent network or Citibank US for USD). For insured-client refunds or claim-related settlements where permitted, use Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) only in exceptional pre-advance payment scenarios; standard practice for service disbursements is corporate credit transfer, not LC. ACH/EBPP rails are not used for high-value cross-border disbursements. VAT-flagged payments require distinctly annotated purpose codes such as 'Insurance surveying services, no claims handling' to avoid misinterpretation under AML rules.Outright FX forwards (USD/CHF, EUR/CHF) OTC with UBS, ZKB, or BCV under an ISDA/CSA master agreement, permitted for trade-related exposure up to 12 months tenorFX swaps (swap/roll of spot and forward) for working capital rebalancingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible but unnecessary for CHF as CHF is fully convertible; only warranted for exotic currencies like Nigerian Naira or Egyptian Pound if payments come from distressed marine jurisdictionsCurrency swaps with a maturity above 12 months for matched purpose (e.g. foreign currency loan hedging), requiring treasury policy approval and Swiss bank credit approval

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High for ancillary payment processing, but Low as a primary corporate bank account for a Swiss AG. Offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex cannot serve as a substitute for a Swiss licensed bank account for statutory corporate purposes (e.g. AG capital maintenance, Swiss withholding tax declarations, creditor protections). They may be used as secondary collection/payment rails for low-value underwriting wires if they hold an FCA/EMI license or EU EMI license, provided the company maintains its principal transactional relationship with a Swiss-regulated bank. Legal constraint: Swiss anti-money laundering (AMLA) requires the financial intermediary to be licensed in Switzerland or operate under a recognized foreign equivalent; many EMIs restrict business models involving insurance claims and surveying due to FinTech sector risk policies.
