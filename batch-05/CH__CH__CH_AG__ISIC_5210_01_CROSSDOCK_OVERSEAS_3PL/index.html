Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully convertible market economy for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no surrender or conversion mandates, no central-bank authorisation for profit remittance, and no domestic FX licensing requirement for corporate treasury activities. The CHF is freely traded onshore and offshore via SWIFT, SIC/euroSIC, the Continuous Linked Settlement (CLS) system, and currency brokers. The practical hurdles for a Swiss AG in a cross-border 3PL/overseas-warehouse business are not FX-quantitative controls but (a) Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act documentation standards on account opening, (b) the 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends and participating debentures when cash is extracted, where treaty relief may reduce this rate, and (c) the obligation of the Swiss AG to be able to prove source of funds and economic substance for its inbound capital and outbound trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no Swiss statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory timeline for repatriation, no minimum conversion ratio into CHF, and no central-bank approval for holding export receivables offshore. Export receipts may be credited to any bank account in any currency. Only statutory reporting duties through banks for balance-of-payments statistics duties remain, but do not require currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque Nationale Suisse, Banca Nazionale Svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium for treasury-operations transactional use and Low for holding the Swiss AGs primary capital or reserve funds. Legal constraints: Swiss Banking Act Article 1 and Article 1b require a banking license or FINMA fintech license to commercially accept or hold public deposits in Switzerland; an EEA EMI license does not passport into Switzerland. Therefore Airwallex, Wise, Revolut Business and similar digital treasuries may be used as technical payment rails for low-value disbursements, receivables collection and conversion, but they cannot be the sole liquidity settlement venue. The key dealer/relationship bank must remain FINMA-supervised and hold the bulk liquidity in SIC-participating accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound capital by SWIFT MT103 or MT202COV to a SIC-participating Swiss bank account held by the Swiss AG. The beneficiary account number should be in IBAN CH format. In the payment message, include structured purpose code CAPT (capital transfer) or free text such as GELEISTETE_EINLAGE_KAPITAL_RESERVEN with a reference to the board capitalisation resolution. The receiving bank will conduct a source-of-funds and sanctions screening, and may request the underlying board minutes, contribution agreement, and evidence of the remitting bank with a valid banking licence. Because Switzerland does not levy FX controls, the capital may be contributed in CHF, EUR or USD; if the AG issues new shares, the commercial register filing will be required and capital formation proved. No SNB foreign-exchange approval is required. For amounts exceeding CHF 10,000, FINMA-compliant identification of the remitting beneficial owner is mandatory.Use the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system for same-day CHF domestic disbursements to warehouses and staff; use euroSIC for EUR-denominated eurozone payments, and SWIFT MT103 for all non-EEA/USD trade settlements. For documentary trade, issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700 under UCP600, payable at sight or by deferred payment at the bank's discount; payment instructions use the 59 field for the overseas supplier and 72 field for necessary MT103 delivery. The AG may also use an EMI such as Airwallex to stream low-value cross-dock payouts to Chinese or UK merchant warehouses, but the underlying treasury must convert currency only through licensed FX counterparties and settle residual balances via bank wire. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory rate plus cantonal/communal profit taxes; aggregate effective rate 11.9%–21% depending on canton (approx. 11.9% in Zug, 14.0% in Zurich, 21.0% in Geneva)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends distributed by an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) to shareholders, regardless of the shareholder's residence

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; extensions of up to 3 additional months granted on written request; provisional payments are due quarterly during the tax year

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax law does not codify a formal local-file/master-file threshold; however, the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and cantonal authorities require documentation on request where intragroup transactions exceed CHF 1 million in aggregate, or where the transaction volume is material; CbC reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CHF 900 million or more in the prior fiscal year (consistent with OECD minimum standards). A simplified Single-Entity documentation file is sufficient for related-party transactions below CHF 1 million aggregate and CHF 200,000 for interest/royalties

Economic substance scope: Swiss courts and administrative practice apply a substance-over-form doctrine; an enterprise must demonstrate real economic activity (office premises, operational employees, actual logistics functions, and risk management) to retain tax residence and treaty benefits. Entities acting as 'letterbox' or sham companies with no substance are recharacterized, denied treaty access, and treated as transparent for Swiss tax purposes, triggering reassessment, penalties, and revocation of the AG's business license

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income tax plus interest at 3.5%; administrative penalty up to 3 times the evaded tax (Art. 175 Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act); criminal fine for attempted evasion; repeat offenders risk publicity of penalty decisions

Director personal liability: Directors or management officers who knowingly sign false tax returns are jointly and severally liable for the imposed penalties; criminal convictions may lead to imprisonment of up to 3 years (or 10 years for qualified aggravated tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG), travel ban, and confiscation of personal assets
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the AG as a transparent pass-through entity, denial of Swiss treaty benefits, full reassessment of profits in the hands of the direct shareholder, and revocation of the business license; administrative fine up to CHF 20,000 under anti-abuse and register provisions

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil allows creditors and tax authorities to hold directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes; directors also face Swiss criminal prosecution for organized tax evasion (Art. 327 of the Swiss Penal Code) with imprisonment up to 5 years and deprivation of travel documents
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing Swiss taxable income; recategorization of the excess as a constructive dividend subject to 35% withholding tax and federal/ cantonal penalty interest; documentation penalty of 10% of the adjustment, up to CHF 100,000; for serious cases additional assessment up to 3 times the additional tax

Director personal liability: Managing directors who approve or execute non-arm's-length intragroup contracts are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax on constructive dividends and related penalties; criminal proceedings for falsification of accounts may result in imprisonment up to 3 years and permanent exclusion from serving as director in Switzerland
Withholding Tax Fraud / Failure to Collect

Statutory sanction: Full collection of the unpaid withholding tax plus interest of 3.5% p.a.; administrative penalty up to 3 times the amount evaded; elimination of shareholder rights (voting and dividend claims) for tax-evading beneficiaries; limited statute of limitations of 10 years but extended to 15 years for intentional fraud

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and personally liable for the full withholding tax debt if they fail to collect and remit, irrespective of corporate insolvency; criminal prosecution for unlawful issuance of dividends leads to imprisonment up to 3 years; travel bans and asset freezes are routinely imposed during criminal investigations
Money Laundering / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), failure to identify beneficial owners and report suspicious transactions results in fines up to CHF 200,000; confiscation of all proceeds; criminal offense for serious cases leads to imprisonment up to 3 years; the firm's license to operate a financial intermediary, if any, is revoked

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately turn a blind eye to laundered funds or sign false declaration forms commit or are accomplices to money laundering (Art. 305bis Swiss Penal Code) and face imprisonment of up to 3 years (extended to 10 years for qualified money laundering); civil liability requires personal restitution of unpaid taxes plus damages; travel bans and bank account freezes apply
