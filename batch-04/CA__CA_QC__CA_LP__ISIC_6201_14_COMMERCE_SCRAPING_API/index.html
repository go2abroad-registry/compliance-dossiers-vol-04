Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Agence du revenu du Canada; principal portal for Business Registration Online / My Business Account (GST/HST, payroll and corporate income tax), and Revenu Québec - bureau des services aux entreprises / Mon dossier d'entreprise (Quebec QST and provincial partnership registration).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or resident general partner must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online. The BN and related program accounts, including the GST/HST account, are issued electronically upon submission of prescribed partnership particulars (legal name, jurisdiction of formation, place of business, partners' identity and percentages). In parallel, the limited partnership must register with Revenu Québec to obtain a QST account and a Quebec establishment identifier using the 'Entreprise' online registration service or the prescribed ART-1/RBQ form. Foreign entities with no Canadian or Quebec establishment may use a representative for service of process and tax administration where required. The CRA BN and Revenu Québec QST number must be quoted on all invoices, customs documents and tax returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident or resident general partner must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online. The BN and related program accounts, including the GST/HST account, are issued electronically upon submission of prescribed partnership particulars (legal name, jurisdiction of formation, place of business, partners' identity and percentages). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification system; a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for federal income tax and Quebec taxation. The partnership is not a taxpayer for income tax; each partner computes and reports its allocable share of Canadian-source income. Elections available include partnership fiscal period selection, allocation formula in the partnership agreement, capital cost allowance elections, material interest in a partnership elections, and the annual/quarterly GST/HST filing election. In Quebec, the association may elect to be a 'specified investment flow-through' or similar under Quebec rules where applicable, and jointly elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for an 'elected combined fiscal period'. No hybrid mismatch election exists because Canada applies anti-hybrid rules (section 18.2/18.3 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec mirror provisions) that prevent deduction/inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the 'small supplier' threshold under the Excise Tax Act. A taxable person providing worldwide taxable supplies of more than CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or over the four preceding calendar quarters must register for GST/HST and QST; Quebec applies a materially parallel threshold for QST purposes. Once exceeded, registration is mandatory effective from the date the threshold is first exceeded. Non-resident digital suppliers are also subject to simplified GST/HST registration rules irrespective of the CAD 30,000 general threshold where the supply is made to Canadian consumers in the ordinary course of e-commerce.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods that are exported in accordance with the Excise Tax Act may be zero-rated when the supplier sells goods to a recipient outside Canada and the goods are delivered by the supplier or common carrier to a destination outside Canada within 60 days, or are otherwise exported as part of a commercial shipment. Supplies of services (including data pipeline API aggregation, scraping services and proxy gateway services) are eligible for zero-rating when performed for a non-resident recipient, supplied to an individual outside Canada, or supplied in respect of real or tangible personal property situated outside Canada, provided the service is not in connection with property intended for use in Canada and is not supplied while the non-resident is present in Canada. Data/commerce scraping API supplied by a Canadian limited partnership to foreign customers and not consumed in the Canadian marketplace is an offshore service and qualifies for 0% GST/HST. The supplier must retain contemporaneous business records demonstrating the non-resident identity of the recipient, the place of supply, the nature of the performance, and the remittance of foreign currency consideration.

  • A valid Customs Export Declaration through CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) electronic system or the equivalent Canadian Customs Invoice and cargo control documentation
  • Original or verified copy of the Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail or truck) showing the exporter as the shipper and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement/foreign exchange transaction record evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract or service order demonstrating the recipient is a non-resident and that the place of supply is outside Canada
  • Carrier proof-of-export package, including outbound manifest or waybill, delivery confirmation at the destination, and any certificate of foreign customs clearance where applicable

No universal electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists at either the federal or Quebec level. Registrants must issue invoices containing mandatory information (e.g., 'GST/HST' registration number and, for QST, the QST identification number), and must retain source documents and electronic records in a prescribed manner. CRA requires electronic filing for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed prescribed thresholds using GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI protocols; Quebec mandates e-filing of QST returns through Revenu Québec's online portal. API-based submission is available for high-volume filers and tax professionals. There is no government-issued digital stamp, digital signature or pre-clearance of each invoice before issuance.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated export claim beyond the normal four-year limitation period in cases of misrepresentation, neglect or fraud (8 years or longer if wilful default). A successful reversal is subject to recaptured GST/HST and QST, arrears interest, and penalties. The Excise Tax Act imposes monetary penalties for false statements or omissions in respect of net tax: generally not less than CAD 250 and up to 5% of the understated net tax for negligence or intentional error, with a 10% penalty for repeat violations or deliberate tax evasion. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating can lead to fines and imprisonment. Quebec law imposes parallel administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Act and may deny the input tax refund claim, de-register the exporter and impose civil liability on the general partner for the limited partnership's unpaid taxes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally imposes no exchange-control registration, capital repatriation approval, mandatory local-currency conversion, or profit-remittance withholding solely because funds cross the border. Remittances remain subject to bank sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax reporting, transfer-pricing requirements, partnership allocation rules, and applicable withholding tax. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian income-tax withholding, commonly under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable treaty and supported by prescribed documentation. Related-party funding should be supported by a partnership agreement, contribution or loan instrument, transfer-pricing analysis, beneficial-ownership evidence, and a clear business purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may be retained in Canadian dollars or foreign currency and may be received into Canadian or offshore accounts, subject to contractual banking requirements, sanctions controls, tax books and records, GST/HST treatment, and applicable customs and export-control obligations. Foreign-currency receipts should be recorded using an appropriate accounting exchange rate and reconciled to bank statements. If the business exports controlled goods, technology, personal information, or services subject to sanctions or sectoral controls, separate federal authorization or reporting may apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign-exchange policy is administered within the federal legal framework, including the Department of Finance Canada, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the United Nations Act. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration requiring prior approval for ordinary commercial remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and treasury support; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider may support CAD, USD, card, ACH, wire, or stablecoin-related flows only within the permissions of its home-country license and its correspondent or sponsor-bank arrangements. It does not automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, Canadian payment-service-provider registration, Canadian trust protection, or authority to conduct regulated activities in Canada. A foreign PSP providing payment functions to end users in Canada may fall within the Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act framework, while money-services-business obligations may apply under Canadian anti-money-laundering law depending on the services and operating model. Stripe onboarding is subject to Stripe's Canadian availability, merchant underwriting, beneficial-owner verification, prohibited-business rules, chargeback controls, and settlement-account requirements. USDC receipts should be handled through a regulated or appropriately registered provider, documented as customer receipts or treasury transactions, and reconciled to CAD accounting records. Stablecoin acceptance does not remove sanctions, AML, consumer-protection, tax, GST/HST, or source-of-funds obligations.

An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider may support CAD, USD, card, ACH, wire, or stablecoin-related flows only within the permissions of its home-country license and its correspondent or sponsor-bank arrangements. It does not automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, Canadian payment-service-provider registration, Canadian trust protection, or authority to conduct regulated activities in Canada. A foreign PSP providing payment functions to end users in Canada may fall within the Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act framework, while money-services-business obligations may apply under Canadian anti-money-laundering law depending on the services and operating model.
