Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) / My Business Account portal; and Revenu Québec (RQ) - CLIC portal for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial employer/payroll accounts. Quebec administers its own provincial VAT (QST) outside the federal Harmonized Sales Tax system.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ); 2) Open a CRA Business Number (BN) with the required program accounts (RC for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RT for income tax reference) via BRO or Form RC1; foreign partnerships without a Canadian establishment register through an authorized representative using Form RC59; 3) Register with Revenu Québec for QST (QST registration is conditional on prior GST/HST registration), the QPIP source-deduction account, and CNESST work-assessment coverage if the agency maintains a Quebec establishment or pays Quebec-based crew; 4) Because the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax, no separate corporate income-tax TIN is issued to the partnership; the partnership files annual information returns (T5013 and TP-600) and each partner uses its own domestic tax identification number, supplemented by a Canadian BN when the partner is required to file a Canadian return. Non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII or section 116 withholding on Canadian-source amounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Income Tax Act (section 96) and the Quebec Taxation Act (section 599), the limited partnership is fiscally transparent: income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in proportion to their partnership interests. The partnership is, however, a separate person for GST/HST and QST purposes and must register, collect, and remit indirect taxes independently.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate an IRS-style check-the-box election; partnership transparency is mandatory. Relevant elections available to a Quebec LP: (i) partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1 and QTA counterpart) to align or select a non-calendar year-end; (ii) agent/principal GST/HST election under ETA section 211 and the mirror Quebec election; (iii) closely related persons election under ETA section 174 and QSTA section 173 to zero-rate supplies of movable property between affiliates; (iv) EIFEL group/co-designation elections under the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation rules applying to cross-border partnerships from 2024; (v) optional annual, quarterly, or installment GST/HST and QST filing elections; (vi) for non-resident partners, elections under ITA section 216 and Part XIII withholding variations to reduce or eliminate Canadian withholding on maritime or leasing income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 per four consecutive calendar quarters (the general 'small supplier' threshold) for both GST/HST (ETA s. 148) and QST (QSTA s. 16). Registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000. A nil threshold applies to certain non-resident suppliers of telecommunication or courier services, and a special worldwide-revenue test applies to non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada. Voluntary registration is available once the partnership has begun carrying on a business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Crew recruitment and vessel crew-management services may be zero-rated as supplies made to non-residents under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and under the mirror provisions of the QSTA (Schedule II, Part I) if: (i) the recipient is a non-resident shipowner, ship manager, or charterer; (ii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property located in Canada - crew placement for international voyages normally meets this condition because the vessel operates in global waterborne trade; (iii) the non-resident recipient does not carry on a business in Canada with respect to that service; and (iv) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply. In addition, the agency commission may be zero-rated under the international commercial transportation service provisions (Schedule VI sections 13-15) where the crewing service is integral to an international outbound freight or passenger voyage. The agency must obtain and retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the counterparty's non-resident status before the consideration becomes due.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED) for any physical export of goods or ship stores, retained where tangible property leaves Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill naming the chartered vessel and the foreign owner or operator
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing the foreign-currency conversion and receipt of export proceeds from a non-resident bank account
  • Commercial service invoice and executed Crewing Agreement identifying the non-resident vessel principal and the international voyage
  • Crew manifest or seafarer rosters confirming the service was rendered on an international voyage beyond Canadian territorial waters
  • Signed statutory declaration of the non-resident status and non-registration of the recipient, supporting the zero-rating claim

Canada operates a periodic self-assessed VAT return model (GST34 for GST/HST and VDZ-1 for QST) with no national real-time fiscal clearance, no Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) mandate, and no mandatory digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary; PEPPOL and EDI formats are permitted. Invoices must nevertheless contain the prescribed disclosure elements under ETA Schedule V and QSTA section 226, and if books are kept electronically, the underlying records must be retained in an electronically readable format. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns and remittance schedules for large registrants via CLIC, and the CRA mandates electronic funds transfer for remittances exceeding C$50,000.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to heightened audit scrutiny by the CRA and Revenu Québec through bank-record cross-checks, counterparty corporate searches, and treaty information exchanges. Although Canada has no statutory exchange-control regime, the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is the standard documentary expectation; failure to evidence repatriation of export consideration within 180 days will trigger audit red flags and possible denial of zero-rating. The normal reassessment window is four years, but misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud extends the period indefinitely. Upon disallowance, the agency is assessed for uncollected GST (5%) and QST (9.975%) with statutory interest, plus: (i) failure-to-remit penalties under ETA section 280 at 6%-8% of the shortfall (minimum C$250 or C$1,000 for corporations and trusts); (ii) false-statements penalties under ETA section 285 and the parallel Quebec provision of 25% of the unreported tax; (iii) a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ETA section 280.1 for knowingly making false statements; and (iv) criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and QSTA section 93 for fraudulent evasion, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years. Clawbacks may also extend to the GST/QST input tax credits previously claimed on related expenses, and responsible directors, partners, or promoters may face joint and several liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII and imposes no prior approval requirement for capital subscription, foreign loans, dividend payments, or partnership distributions. A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) may receive inbound capital and repatriate surplus to non-resident partners without Bank of Canada approval. The principal compliance hurdles are tax-related: remittances to non-residents may attract Canadian Part XIII withholding when characterized as interest, dividends, rent, royalties, or management fees; Canadian partnership income allocated to non-resident partners must be reported through T5013 partnership returns and applicable Quebec non-resident forms; treaty relief must be documented. These are tax clearance hurdles, not foreign-exchange restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules in Canada. Export and service receipts, including ship-crew recruitment and management fees, may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency for an unlimited period; no percentage of proceeds must be converted into CAD. Reporting obligations are limited to financial-transaction reports: financial institutions must report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and Statistics Canada/Bank of Canada may collect balance-of-payments survey data on cross-border service receipts. No surrender certificate or exchange-control approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Bank acts as fiscal agent for the Government of Canada's Exchange Fund Account and oversees key payment and settlement systems. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; the Department of Finance exercises overall foreign-exchange policy authority, OSFI prudentially supervises federal banks, and FINTRAC administers AML and electronic-funds-transfer reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary settlement bank, MEDIUM as a non-core ancillary payment rail. Offshore digital EMIs may not take deposits or open Canadian-listed bank accounts without authorization under the Bank Act; they may only provide money-transfer services if registered as a FINTRAC money services business and, where applicable, licensed under Quebec's Act respecting money-services businesses. Such accounts are not CDIC-insured and are generally unacceptable for capital escrow, crew payroll trust, or SWIFT-backed commercial settlement. Use EMIs only for pre-funded vendor disbursements or small operational allowances.
