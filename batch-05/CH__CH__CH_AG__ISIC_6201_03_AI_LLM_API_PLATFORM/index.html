Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA; official portal www.estv.admin.ch) with cantonal tax authorities for corporate income tax assessment at the registered-seat canton.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG automatically receives the Swiss business identifier (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer or UID) when entered in the Commercial Register. For VAT purposes, the AG must file an online registration to the ESTV under Art. 10 of the VAT Act (Loi sur la TVA/MWSTG) in order to obtain the VAT number (MWST/TVA-Nr) if taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. Foreign entities seeking a Swiss presence first register a branch or subsidiary in the Commercial Register, triggering UID issuance, then apply for VAT and cantonal tax IDs through the same ESTV and cantonal channels.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – The Swiss AG is a separate corporate tax subject, liable to federal corporate income tax, cantonal/communal profit and capital taxes, and Swiss withholding tax on distributions. No pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Swiss law. Statutory elections are limited to VAT group taxation (Art. 14 VAT Act) and federal tax group taxation ('Steuerliche Tragerschaft' / 'statut de groupe'), whereby qualifying Swiss group companies can elect to be taxed as a single federal profit tax unit. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Swiss domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover from Swiss supplies, excluding VAT; foreign suppliers of digital services to Swiss private consumers may have registration obligations irrespective of turnover based on place-of-customer rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported from Switzerland and proof of export is furnished. For the AI LLM API platform, offshore supplies of digital/database services to foreign business customers are outside Swiss VAT scope under place-of-supply rules, or qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is a non-Swiss business and documentary evidence of foreign establishment is retained. Services to Swiss private consumers do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrschein / declaration via Swiss customs e-dec process)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (international transport evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank credit statement (payment evidence)

Switzerland does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, stamping, or mandatory government-API approval of invoices. Swiss QR-bill requirements govern payment documents. VAT compliance is carried through periodic returns filed via the ESTV secure eService portal, with electronic records accepted, but there is no continuous transaction-control clearance requirement.

ESTV audits zero-rated claims by matching export declarations, international transport documents, contracts and payment evidence. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating is reversed with reassessment, default interest, and administrative fines. Intentional VAT evasion carries criminal fines and possible imprisonment, with a 10-year limitation period for retrospective reassessment in tax evasion cases; the ordinary assessment period is generally 5 years. There is no statutory Swiss FX repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark is applied administratively for payment reconciliation in export substantiation reviews.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalized and convertible currency regime without distinction between capital and current account transactions; non-residents may freely hold Swiss francs and repatriate dividends, profits, royalties, and capital in any convertible currency. No prior authorization is required for profit remittance or capital returns, subject only to standard anti-money laundering documentary obligations imposed on Swiss banks. The Swiss franc is not subject to exchange controls, and there are no restrictions on foreign currency accounts, but the SNB retains statutory powers to impose temporary protective measures in extraordinary circumstances, including negative interest rates and mandatory reserve requirements, although such measures have historically targeted domestic liquidity rather than cross-border convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender or repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into Swiss francs; exporters and digital service providers, including AI API platforms, are free to retain, reinvest, or convert foreign currency receivables in the open interbank market. Banking clients must nevertheless comply with the Swiss Code of Obligations and FINMA anti-money laundering directives regarding the identification of the economic background and the source of funds when routing export receipts through Swiss bank accounts, and any inward payment exceeding CHF 15,000 may trigger enhanced documentary scrutiny if the transaction appears unusual.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) in conjunction with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market policy, and FINMA for banking supervision and anti-money laundering oversight

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for capital contribution into the Swiss AG require the funds to be routed from a bank account held in the name of the non-resident shareholder or its clearly disclosed holding vehicle. The wire must reference the purpose code 'KEH' or 'INVE' by internal bank practice, although Swiss law does not mandate a specific SWIFT purpose code; the mandatory narrative in the payment field should state 'Einlage Kapitalerhöhung' or 'Capital Contribution / CH-AG equity injection', accompanied by a board resolution approving the contribution and a notarised deed of increase in capital if applicable. The beneficiary account must be the Swiss AG's capital escrow account. Prior to execution, the bank must receive a signed source-of-funds letter from the shareholder, a copy of the passport or certificate of incorporation, the bank statement showing the shareholder's funds, and, where the contribution exceeds CHF 1 million, an audited confirmation of the economic background and the valuation of any monetary or non-monetary consideration. Payment can be made in CHF, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency; the receiving Swiss bank will convert to CHF only pursuant to a standing treasury instruction. Verification of the transaction is completed via the bank's sanction screening, adverse-media check, and a positive match of the shareholder name against the corporate register excerpt.Outbound trade settlements for the AI LLM API platform's supplier contracts, cloud infrastructure payments, and cross-border licensing royalties are executed through the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system for same-day domestic CHF payments, and through SWIFT MT103 for inbound/outbound cross-border fiat wires, typically via the corridor banking network of UBS, ZKB, or another Swiss clearing bank. For medium-value commercial transactions exceeding CHF 50,000, the Swiss bank may require a full narrative in the SWIFT field 70 to describe the contract invoice number, the underlying service, and the commercial purpose; invoices must be issued under Swiss VAT law, and Swiss withholding tax on royalties and licence fees (up to 4.4% or treaty-reduced) must be settled with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration before repatriation to foreign service providers.
