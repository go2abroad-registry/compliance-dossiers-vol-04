Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online Portal (my Business Account)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a CRA business number (BN) through the Business Registration Online portal or by completing Form RC1. For GST/HST, the entity may be required to register for a GST/HST account under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act if it is not a small supplier. Non-resident entities may also apply for a non-resident business account and must provide proof of a Canadian agent or place of business if required. A separate Ontario Master Business License or partnership registration is not mandatory for the federal tax account but may be required for provincial remittances; Ontario registration is effectuated through the Ontario Business Registry for extra-provincial LP registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; no check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Partners report income on a pass-through basis. For GST/HST purposes, the LP may elect to have members (general partner) act as agent; where eligible, a closely related corporation election under sections 150-156 of the Excise Tax Act may apply to simplify supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 163.2-163.5 of the Income Tax Act may require conforming election notices where cross-border partnership interests create deductible/mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies in Canada (excluding zero-rated exports) in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents may voluntarily register at lower thresholds

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (including ECG patient monitors) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the seller exports the goods directly by commercial carrier, mail, or own conveyance to a destination outside Canada. Qualifying supplies must be made to an export customer and the supplier must maintain documentary proof of export within prescribed time limits. Offshore services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated under Schedule VII. The Ontario HST portion (8%) is not applied to zero-rated exports, and input tax credits may be claimed to recover any GST/HST paid on inputs, subject to ITC substantiation rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via CBSA or authorized service provider; Form B13A or equivalent ACE data submission), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing or clearing platform at the federal or Ontario provincial level. The CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible and retainable, and businesses must retain digital records if issued electronically. GST/HST returns are filed through CRA’s GST/HST NETFILE or the My Business Account API. Ontario does not operate a separate digital stamping regime. Businesses issuing e-invoices must ensure compliance with Canada’s electronic document record-retention requirements under ETA section 286.

The CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess to recapture uncollected GST/HST plus interest and penalties where export documentation is deficient or the supply is not in fact exported to a non-resident. False statements or misleading facts in an application, return, or document can give rise to civil penalties under subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act (up to 25% of the net tax evaded) and criminal liability under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 380 of the Criminal Code for tax evasion, which can include fines and imprisonment. The Ontario portion is similarly clawed back if the goods are not properly exported and substantiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; there are no exchange controls, no licensing of FX transactions, and no ceiling on profit remittance. An Ontario limited partnership may freely distribute cash to non-resident limited partners in CAD or USD once income tax is provisioned; non-resident partners are subject to Part I tax on their allocable share of the LP's Canadian business income under sections 96 and 115 of the Income Tax Act, with treaty relief available for PE and LOB limitations. Remittance hurdles are limited to (i) bank-level enhanced due diligence on opaque UBO chains, (ii) OFAC/SEMA blocking of USD outflows to comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions through US clearing rails, and (iii) documentation thresholds such as FINTRAC reporting of cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more. Part XIII withholding applies only where a distribution is legally characterized as dividend, interest, rent, royalty, or management fee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists; export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency account at a Canadian bank. The mandatory obligations are: (i) CBSA export declaration (G13/B13A) for goods with customs value above CAD 2,000; (ii) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting of every cross-border international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, including the invoice and source-of-funds narrative; and (iii) accurate FX gain/loss recognition in the partnership's income under ITA section 39(1). There is no timeline for repatriation and no ratio of export receipts that must be converted into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority; FX administration is decentralized: OSFI supervises Schedule I banks, FINTRAC administers EFT and AML reporting under the PCMLTFA, and CBSA administers export declarations. Canada has no separate foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: HIGH as an ancillary settlement rail; LOW as a primary or sole operating bank. Foreign digital EMIs (EU/UK e-money institutions such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer) cannot take deposits from the public in Canada unless authorized under section 524 of the Bank Act or registered with FINTRAC as a money services business; an offshore EMI without Canadian registration lacks status to hold the LP's treasury as a primary depository. Practical use is permitted for inbound client collections under CAD 50,000 per transaction and small supplier disbursements, provided a tier-1 Canadian bank remains the primary account holder and the EMI is either FINTRAC-registered in Canada or is a recognized regulated institution in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction. Wire transfers from unregistered EMIs to the Canadian account trigger enhanced due diligence and may produce false-positive suspicious transaction reports unless the LP maintains invoice-level MT103 trails and source-of-funds declarations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent executes a capital call referencing the Ontario LP declaration before transfer, then remits via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) to the LP's operating account at the tier-1 Canadian bank, with narrative 'Capital contribution - LP name, capital call #[date]'. The receiving bank maps the transaction to ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (capital payment) or 'INTC' (intercompany financing) to prevent misclassification as trade settlement. Settlement rails are: CAD through Payments Canada Lynx; USD through Fedwire or CHIPS via the Canadian bank's US affiliate. No Bank of Canada approval is required. The bank verifies: (i) originator is not sanctions blacklisted; (ii) beneficial ownership reconciles with the LP's KYC file; (iii) aggregate injection over CAD 150,000 within 30 days triggers enhanced due diligence requiring the capital-call audit trail and source-of-funds memorandum; and (iv) every cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is reported to FINTRAC.Primary rail for supplier settlements is SWIFT MT103 direct remittance with invoice, bill-of-lading, and packing-list reference numbers embedded in the remittance information; CAD outflows clear through Lynx while USD outflows proceed via Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's US affiliate. For secured trade settlements, the Canadian bank issues an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700; standard terms are sight or up to 60 days usance against clean-on-board ocean B/L or airway bill, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and, when applicable, the supplier's ISO 13485 quality certificate. If the LP acts as intermediate buyer between a US end-customer and an Asian contract manufacturer, a back-to-back LC can be structured to align payment risk. Each outflow must be matched to a specific purchase order and master supply contract to withstand trade-based AML scrutiny. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined headline (federal 15% general + Ontario 11.5% general); the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent — no entity-level levy; each partner is taxed under Part I on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income (non-resident corporate partners at 26.5% combined if taxable; non-resident individuals at graduated provincial/federal marginal rates).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File no later than 6 months after the corporate partner's fiscal year-end; balance-due day: 2 months after year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months for CCPCs with Canadian-controlled status; non-resident corporations with no Canadian employees must remit estimated Part I tax within the same balance-due window to avoid prescribed-rate interest.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due no later than the 5th month after the LP's fiscal period-end; information slips (T5013 Supplementary) and NR4 slips for non-resident partners must be distributed by the same deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 60 days after the anniversary of registration; revocation of LP registration occurs if the annual return is delinquent, converting the LP to a general partnership exposure for limited partners.

Page 1 - Identification BN/account, jurisdiction of incorporation/creation, fiscal period, province of permanent establishment, industry code (NAICS 334510). Non-resident corporate partner: enter its foreign address, Canadian PE declaration, and the province of operation (Ontario); no Ontario permanent establishment allocation is made unless a fixed base exists. Late filing: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (s. 162(1)); repeat filing failure within 3 years: 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months; gross negligence: 50% of understated tax (s. 163(2)); non-payment interest at prescribed rates (compounded daily).
Page 3 - Income statement/Schedule 1 Net income (loss) for tax purposes, including the LP's allocated income or loss. Allocated share from the T5013 is reported as Canadian business income, not dividend income; treaty recharacterization cannot convert business income into a fully exempt gain absent a PE. Late filing: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (s. 162(1)); repeat filing failure within 3 years: 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months; gross negligence: 50% of understated tax (s. 163(2)); non-payment interest at prescribed rates (compounded daily).
Schedule 1 (S1) - Reconciliations Additions/deductions under ss. 9-143, including CCA claims (Class 50/53 for medical device manufacturing equipment). Deductions are apportioned to the Canadian PE; SR&ED ITCs are prorated to the Canadian-source portion. Late filing: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (s. 162(1)); repeat filing failure within 3 years: 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months; gross negligence: 50% of understated tax (s. 163(2)); non-payment interest at prescribed rates (compounded daily).
Schedule 9 - Unused tax losses Non-capital loss carryforwards applied to taxable income. Only losses attributable to the Canadian business/PE may be claimed by the non-resident partner. Part I tax calculation - Schedules 14/15/16 Federal tax, provincial allocation, M&P surtax credits, SR&ED ITC recapture. Provincial allocation is based on Ontario permanent establishment gross revenue and wage ratios; the non-resident corporate partner must file an Ontario corporate allocation schedule even if all sales are abroad. Part 1 - Partnership identification LP name, registration number (Ontario LP number), business address, fiscal period, general partner identification, NAICS/ISIC code. Attach a declaration of the non-resident limited partners' jurisdictions and residency status; the LP must obtain SIN, BN, or non-resident account numbers for all partners. Failure to file a T5013 when required: penalty of $25 per day for each day late, up to C$2,500 (s. 162(7) as administered); false statements or omissions: 50% penalty of the amount involved (s. 163(2)); the CRA may also be prohibited? no, penalties accrue; interest on unreported allocations; criminal exposure under s. 239 if wilful.
Part 3 - Income statement (Boxes 110-190) Gross revenue, cost of goods sold, gross profit, expenses, net business income/loss (Box 121), net taxable capital gains (Box 130), other amounts (Box 131). Report revenues and expenses attributable to the Ontario manufacturing activity; intercompany service fees to non-residents must be shown separately and supported by transfer-pricing documentation. Failure to file a T5013 when required: penalty of $25 per day for each day late, up to C$2,500 (s. 162(7) as administered); false statements or omissions: 50% penalty of the amount involved (s. 163(2)); the CRA may also be prohibited? no, penalties accrue; interest on unreported allocations; criminal exposure under s. 239 if wilful.
Schedule 1 - Allocation of income/loss Pro rata allocation of each item of income/loss/credit to partners per the LP agreement. Allocations must have a bona fide, on-going commercial basis; special allocations of losses to non-resident limited partners raise Limited Partnership Act passive-loss restrictions and CRA anti-abuse scrutiny. Failure to file a T5013 when required: penalty of $25 per day for each day late, up to C$2,500 (s. 162(7) as administered); false statements or omissions: 50% penalty of the amount involved (s. 163(2)); the CRA may also be prohibited? no, penalties accrue; interest on unreported allocations; criminal exposure under s. 239 if wilful.
Schedule 6 - Partners' information Each partner's name, tax identification, address, residency, percentage of allocation, capital and drawings. Mark 'non-resident' and identify the relevant treaty; the non-resident partner's allocated business income remains taxable under Part I regardless of the non-resident indicator. Failure to file a T5013 when required: penalty of $25 per day for each day late, up to C$2,500 (s. 162(7) as administered); false statements or omissions: 50% penalty of the amount involved (s. 163(2)); the CRA may also be prohibited? no, penalties accrue; interest on unreported allocations; criminal exposure under s. 239 if wilful.
NR4 slip - non-resident payments Distributions or guaranteed payments to non-resident partners. If the partnership distributes Canadian-source income that is not business income allocable to the partner's Canadian PE, the LP must withhold 25% Part XIII tax and remit by the 15th day of the following month; treaty relief is available only upon receipt of NR301. Failure to file a T5013 when required: penalty of $25 per day for each day late, up to C$2,500 (s. 162(7) as administered); false statements or omissions: 50% penalty of the amount involved (s. 163(2)); the CRA may also be prohibited? no, penalties accrue; interest on unreported allocations; criminal exposure under s. 239 if wilful.
Annual Return Confirm LP name, jurisdiction, general partner, registered office, and status of the limited partnership. Non-resident limited partners must not be identified as general partners; maintain a registered office and general partner located in Ontario. Late filing fees under the Business Corporations Act/ServiceOntario schedule; failure to file for 2 consecutive years triggers administrative dissolution, exposing the non-resident limited partner to unlimited liability for LP obligations.
