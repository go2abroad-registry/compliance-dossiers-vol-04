Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; and for Quebec, Revenu Québec, through clicSÉRIE-affaires at revenuquebec.ca, with enterprise registration administered by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec Limited Partnership must first obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. It must then obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account by submitting Form RC1 through the CRA's Business Registration Online portal, by mail, or by fax. For QST, the partnership must register with Revenu Québec using the LM-102 form or clicSÉRIE-affaires; registration can be completed by the general partner or an authorized Canadian agent. If annual worldwide taxable supplies are CAD 30,000 or less, registration is not mandatory for GST/HST/QST, but voluntary registration is commercially necessary to recover input tax credits and to zero-rate exports. Quebec does not impose any minimum capital requirement for limited partnerships.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity election. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent by statute under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act: income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. Partners may elect, through the partnership agreement, the allocation of taxable income and losses, subject to anti-avoidance and reasonableness rules. A non-resident partner may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to compute Canadian income tax on an actual net basis for certain Canadian property income; for active business income, the partnership files Form T5013 and each partner reports its allocated share. For GST/HST and QST, eligible small registrants may elect the Quick Method and simplified accounting rules, but no hybrid-mismatch election is available to Canadian limited partnerships.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). The threshold may be bypassed for imported taxable goods or taxable real property.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supplier may zero-rate the sale of tangible personal property exported from Canada under subsection 23(1) of the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are removed from Canada and the supplier maintains proof of export. Delivery to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada is sufficient if the carrier's bill of lading names a destination outside Canada. Offshore services rendered to non-residents and services relating to exported goods are generally zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V. QST zero-rating follows the same factual export test. The goods must not be consumed, used, or retained in Canada prior to export.

  • Canada Customs B13A Export Declaration or CBSA Authorized Exporter (AER) report where required by threshold or control type
  • Commercial invoice stating HS code under ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930, product description, quantity, and export sale price
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing the ultimate destination outside Canada
  • Packing list and customs broker records evidencing export from Quebec
  • Carrier-issued proof of delivery or destination-country customs entry
  • Foreign exchange transaction advice or bank inward remittance certificate confirming payment in foreign currency

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing. GST/HST and QST are enforced through audit-based compliance. Invoices must contain prescribed content under ETA section 223 and QSRA section 450, including the supplier's name, BN/NEQ, invoice date, description of goods, and tax amounts. No PEPPOL or equivalent standard is mandatory. Revenu Québec may require specified electronic records on audit, but pre-clearance of invoices is not part of the Canadian indirect tax regime.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/HST and QST within four years, extended to ten years for misrepresentation, wilful default, or fraud. Zero-ratings are clawed back where export documentation is incomplete, commercial terms are not supported, or goods are diverted for domestic use. Civil penalties for false statements amount to 10% of the misstated tax for carelessness and 50% for gross negligence; ETA section 280 and section 285.1 permit additional penalties of up to $20,000 plus 100% of net tax and prescribed interest. Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239 and ETA section 403 may result in fines up to $500,000 and imprisonment up to five years; Quebec imposes equivalent QST clawback, penalty, and fraud provisions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the limited partnership level (flow-through); combined federal/Quebec general corporate headline for a taxable corporate partner is 26.5% (federal 15% after abatement plus Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII on Canadian-source payments to non-resident persons; for a limited partnership, no dividend withholding arises at the LP level, but a non-resident partner's allocated share of Canadian-source business income is subject to 25% Part XIII withholding unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation are required when the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year. Canada does not maintain separate Local File/Master File filing thresholds; the CRA may request Local File/Master File-style information for material controlled transactions at any time and expects production within three months.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); it enforces substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), treaty-shopping limitation under the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and the 'carrying on business in Canada' test. A Quebec LP must maintain a physical office, employees, bank accounts, books and records, and board-level decision-making in Quebec; otherwise the CRA and Revenu Québec may disregard the partnership, reallocate income, or deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Suppression of Income

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence under ITA s.239(1): fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years; civil reassessment with gross-negligence penalty of 50% and interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts and penalties; directors of a corporate general partner may be assessed under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted amounts; limited partners who participate in management lose the limited-liability shield and may face travel restrictions pending prosecution.
Late / Non-Filing of Partnership Returns and Withholding

Statutory sanction: Federal and Quebec late-filing penalties (CAD 25/day up to CAD 2,500), T106 penalty of CAD 100 per month up to CAD 2,500, plus interest; failure-to-withhold penalties of 10% of the unwithheld amount, increasing to 20% for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes, even after the LP is dissolved; officers who directed the withholding failure face personal monetary penalties and no claim to corporate-indemnification protection.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Abusive Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's-length price; transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment; denial of deduction under GAAR; retroactive interest; and penalties under Quebec law for knowingly false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners who caused or acquiesced in the mispricing may be jointly liable for the penalty; active limited partners are treated as general partners and cannot hide behind the LP's corporate shield.
