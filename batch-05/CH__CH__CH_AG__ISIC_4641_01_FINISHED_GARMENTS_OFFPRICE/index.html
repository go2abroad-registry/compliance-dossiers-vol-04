Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug); final supervisory authority is the Federal Commercial Register Office within the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, SR 220), Articles 620-663b on the Aktiengesellschaft (stock corporation); incorporation requirements Articles 626-633, minimum capital Article 632, capital deposit confirmation Article 634, commercial register entry Article 640, registration application Article 643; supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, SR 221.411) and the Swiss Civil Code (SR 210) on legal personality.

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister (Application for Entry into the Commercial Register), consisting of the notarised public deed of incorporation and the company's Articles of Incorporation (Statuten).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (Article 632 OR). At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before filing; the funds are held in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank and released only upon Commercial Register entry. A bank confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) must accompany the application; any contribution in kind requires a review report by a licensed audit firm.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification of the warehouse and office premises under the Swiss fire protection standard (VKF/AEAI) issued by the Building Insurance of Zug; structural load inspection certificate for pallet racking issued by the municipal building inspectorate; occupational safety clearance from SUVA and the Federal Coordination Commission for Occupational Safety (EKAS) covering manual handling of garment bales, forklift traffic, emergency escape plans, first-aid equipment and statutory accident insurance cover.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (Article 632 OR). At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before filing; the funds are held in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank and released only upon Commercial Register entry. A bank confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) must accompany the application; any contribution in kind requires a review report by a licensed audit firm.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), VAT and Special Taxes Division; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and filing portal: S-VAT (system-tva.swiss / vat-form.estv.admin.ch); UID registry: www.uid.admin.ch. Cantonal and communal tax administrations administer direct taxes at their respective levels.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT via the S-VAT portal using the official VAT registration form, either immediately upon commencing activities or at the moment it exceeds the statutory CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover threshold (Art. 63 VAT Act). The FTA then assigns the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / identification d'entreprise IDE) and the MWST/TVA VAT registration number. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft must additionally obtain a UID through the cantonal commercial register (Handelsregisteramt) before or upon incorporation; the UID serves as the sole statutory business tax and VAT identification number for all tax authorities. Non-resident entities may nominate a Swiss tax representative for VAT compliance when they have no domestic seat or establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss resident companies (AG) are subject to federal corporate income tax at the statutory rate of 8.5% (effective approximately 15–21% including cantonal and communal profit taxes depending on canton). Territorial scope: worldwide income for tax residents, with unilateral relief exempting foreign permanent establishment profits and real estate (bracket system) and a participation exemption for dividends and capital gains from qualifying substantial shareholdings; the STAF 2020 framework abolished privileged tax statuses (holding, mixed, principal company) and replaced them with broadly applicable instruments (patent box, R&D super-deduction, notional interest deduction). No general tax-exempt zones exist for the wholesale apparel trading sector; ordinary domestic tax rates apply throughout Switzerland. — A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT via the S-VAT portal using the official VAT registration form, either immediately upon commencing activities or at the moment it exceeds the statutory CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover threshold (Art. 63 VAT Act). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss resident companies (AG) are subject to federal corporate income tax at the statutory rate of 8.5% (effective approximately 15–21% including cantonal and communal profit taxes depending on canton). Territorial scope: worldwide income for tax residents, with unilateral relief exempting foreign permanent establishment profits and real estate (bracket system) and a participation exemption for dividends and capital gains from qualifying substantial shareholdings; the STAF 2020 framework abolished privileged tax statuses (holding, mixed, principal company) and replaced them with broadly applicable instruments (patent box, R&D super-deduction, notional interest deduction). No general tax-exempt zones exist for the wholesale apparel trading sector; ordinary domestic tax rates apply throughout Switzerland.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or analogous entity classification election exists under Swiss law. Available elections: (1) VAT group taxation election (Art. 20 VAT Act) combining economically integrated enterprises into a single taxable person; (2) election of the partial taxation method or the effective input VAT apportionment method for mixed-use activities; (3) under the STAF 2020 rules, election of the patent box regime or the R&D additional deduction for qualifying income; (4) for distributions from qualifying participations, election of the 'partial taxation method' at cantonal level; (5) Swiss anti-hybrid mismatch rules (effective 1 January 2020) apply IPIC/ATAD2-style deduction/no-inclusion disallowances; no elective opt-out is available for hybrid entities, but structured transactions are assessed for tax treaty and substance override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide taxable turnover (Art. 10 VAT Act) for entrepreneurs with their seat or place of business in Switzerland; foreign entities supplying services from abroad or digital B2C services register when the CHF 100,000 threshold from Swiss-source supplies is reached (Art. 11 para. 1 VAT Act). Low-value import consignments below CHF 65 import value are exempt from import VAT; no simplified small-business scheme is available once turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, except for charities, sport event organisers and non-profit entities with separate thresholds (e.g., CHF 150,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (tax-exempt with entitlement to input VAT deduction) under Art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act when the exporter proves that the goods were actually transported or dispatched abroad and the recipient is outside Switzerland (or the goods are placed into a customs warehouse). Goods must leave Switzerland in an unaltered state or after minor processing supported by a valid outward customs declaration. Services qualify as zero-rated export services (Art. 23 para. 2 VAT Act) where the recipient is located abroad or the service relates to movable tangible property exported from Switzerland, provided the service is demonstrably rendered for foreign use. Full documentary evidence of export and of the recipient's foreign residence/seat is mandatory for the 0% rate to apply; the burden of proof lies on the exporter in all cases.

  • Outward customs export declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration / déclaration d'exportation) processed via the Swiss customs clearance system (e-dec or ESA)
  • Commercial invoice (sales invoice for off-price garments) issued to the foreign buyer and conforming to Art. 26 VAT Act invoice content requirements
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or CMR road consignment note evidencing actual cross-border transport
  • Proof of receipt/payment of export consideration (SWIFT bank statement or inward remittance confirmation) demonstrating the foreign-linked commercial flow
  • Period-end export register and, where applicable, warehouse movement records (for customs warehouses or third-country transit)

Switzerland does not apply a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, government API stamping, or digital stamping regime for B2B or B2C invoices. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the standardised electronic invoice format, and eBill is offered by retail banks. A Federal Council reform announced in 2024 envisages mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing with a phased introduction from 2028; implementing legislation is pending. Until then, VAT deduction is substantiated by conventional invoices that meet Art. 26 VAT Act content requirements; no government pre-clearance or electronic signature mandate applies.

The FTA audits zero-rated export claims primarily via desk reviews of customs and payment evidence; any non-documented or fraudulent zero-rating is retroactively clawed back by means of VAT re-assessment (Nachbelastung) plus default interest at 4.5% per annum. Willful or grossly negligent export VAT evasion constitutes tax evasion under Art. 56 VAT Act, for which the FTA may impose a penalty of up to three times the VAT evaded (or one time where negligence is committed), in addition to criminal fines of up to CHF 100,000; simple procedural infractions carry fines up to CHF 10,000. The limitation period for VAT enforcement is generally five years from the end of the relevant fiscal period, extended to ten years in cases of deliberate evasion, and the FTA retains the right to require security or arrange company-level VAT rulings for repeated offenders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account; the Swiss franc is freely convertible for residents and non-residents, and there are no exchange-control restrictions under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) or the National Bank Act. Profit remittance, debt service, liquidation proceeds and trade payments may flow abroad without prior SNB authorisation. The only structural hurdle to outbound distributions is the 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividend payments, which is refundable or treaty-reduced under applicable double-taxation agreements, and the absence of capital controls does not relieve the collecting Swiss bank from its statutory AML/CFT documentation duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters of off-price garments may retain proceeds in CHF or any freely convertible foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts, and the SNB requires no repatriation declaration other than statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the Swiss bank for individual cross-border payments exceeding CHF 10 million within a calendar year.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banca Nazionale Svizzera / Banca Naziunala Svizra), with settlement infrastructure operated by SIX Interbank Clearing Ltd via SIC and EuroSIC

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal corporate income tax levied on post-tax profit (equivalent to approx. 7.83% on a pre-tax basis); combined federal, cantonal and communal effective rate for a Zug AG approximates 11.9% for the 2025 assessment period.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed by a Swiss AG; fully refundable or reduced under applicable double taxation agreements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must generally be filed by 31 March of the subsequent year; canton Zug normally grants an automatic extension to 30 September, with additional extension available on reasoned request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation is expected where a Swiss taxpayer's aggregate annual intragroup transactions exceed CHF 10 million across any category (goods, services, financing, intangibles); below that threshold, a simplified short-form ('Kurzbericht') documentation is accepted. Switzerland imposes no separate statutory TP return, but the FTA and cantons may formally order documentation under the federal assessment procedure and apply penalties for non-production.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations model; however, the risk-based substance doctrine operates through the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test (PPT), the Swiss general anti-abuse clause and administrative practice requiring physical presence, qualified personnel, active decision-making, real inventory movement and commercial premises in Switzerland. A garment liquidation trading AG lacking genuine substance in Zug would be denied tax treaty benefits, exposed to look-through taxation of the foreign shareholders, and reclassified as a shell company, with retroactive assessment and criminal implications.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of one to three times the evaded federal tax (Art. 175 DBG); cantonal recoupment interest at statutory rate; loss of appeal rights; publication of the final penalty decision; reinstatement of assessments for ten years in cases of intentional omission.

Director personal liability: The responsible director, CFO or head of finance is subject to personal criminal fines and, in aggravated or repeated cases, criminal conviction with imprisonment up to three years; corporate res judicata does not extinguish individual culpability.
Qualified Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to five years and a monetary fine under the Swiss Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrG); directly assessed fraudulent profit tax; unlimited limitation period for false accounting instruments.

Director personal liability: Directors and others signatory to fraudulent records face personal criminal prosecution, travel restrictions and bank account freezes; courts may impose unlimited personal liability for the resulting tax damage upon proof of willful participation.
Failure to Deduct and Remit Anticipatory Tax (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: Statutory payment claim for the full 35% of the gross distribution plus statutory default interest; administrative penalty of up to the amount of tax evaded; blockage of any treaty refund claim for the period of the violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable without a prior benefit requirement to the FTA for unremitted anticipatory tax; deliberate omission constitutes criminal fiscal misconduct with personal fines and potential custodial sentence.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the PPT anti-abuse rule; reclassification of the AG as economically transparent; addition of profit at the Swiss level as hidden profit distributions; withdrawal of cooperative tax ruling; retroactive tax assessments and negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who operate the letterbox entity expose themselves to civil restructuring and bankruptcy liability with potential personal restitution of misallocated profits, entry into the commercial register exclusion procedure, and travel bans in the context of tax execution proceedings.
Money Laundering (AML) Deficiency in Cash Trading

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty against the company of up to CHF 500,000 for violation of due diligence, identification and reporting duties; forfeiture of laundered assets; suspension of the entity from public procurement and payment settlement infrastructure; additional supervisory enforcement orders.

Director personal liability: Board members and compliance officers are personally liable for breaches of AML due diligence duties; where intent or criminal negligence is proven, personal criminal conviction with imprisonment up to five years, personal fines, disqualification from serving as directors, and civil liability for all consequential tax losses sustained by the estate.
