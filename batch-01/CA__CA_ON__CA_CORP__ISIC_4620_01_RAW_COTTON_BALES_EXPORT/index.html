Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax authority; online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Ontario corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the tax-collection agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; GST/HST in Ontario is administered exclusively by CRA as a harmonized province.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or the Ontario Business Corporations Act (ServiceOntario) and obtain the certificate or articles plus, for Ontario, the Ontario Information Record. Step 2: Apply in one submission for the nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, opening the corporate income tax (RC), GST/HST (RT) and customs or exporter (RM/RD) program accounts. Step 3: Open the GST/HST (RT) account before worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (zero-rated cotton exports count toward the threshold), or voluntarily to recover input tax credits on export-related purchases. Step 4: File the combined federal–Ontario T2 corporate income tax return and periodic GST/HST returns through My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE; no separate Ontario corporate tax registration is required because Ontario participates in the federal tax-collection agreement.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box pass-through election exists for a CBCA/OBCA business corporation; the corporation is a separate taxpayer on worldwide income. Relevant elections for the ISIC 4620 export broker: (1) ITA s. 249.1 election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (2) ETA s. 156 closely-related-corporation election to treat intercompany taxable supplies as nil consideration; (3) ETA s. 177 agent–principal election, deeming the disclosed broker's commission supplies to be made by the non-resident principal and the commission to be nil for GST/HST; (4) ETA s. 273 joint-venture election; and (5) associated-CCPC election to allocate the small-business deduction business limit (ITA s. 125). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; no exchange-control legislation restricts capital movements, profit remittances, dividend repatriation, or the conversion of Canadian dollars (CAD). The Canadian dollar floats freely in global foreign-exchange markets, and there is no requirement to obtain central-bank approvals for cross-border capital flows. Dividends, interest, royalties, and branch profits may be repatriated to non-resident shareholders or head offices subject exclusively to Canada's Part XIII withholding tax rules, tax-treaty relief, and normal corporate solvency/estoppel requirements. Corporate treasury operations may maintain CAD and foreign-currency accounts at authorized Canadian financial institutions without exchange-control permits; the only administrative obligations are statistical reporting through the Canadian Balance of Payments survey and PCMLTFA transaction reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, or the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a prescribed timeline in Canada. Export receipts invoiced in US dollars or other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at authorized financial institutions, converted to CAD at commercial rates, or applied directly to offshore purchase and logistics obligations; the sole compliance imperatives are FINTRAC large-cash and cross-border electronic-funds-transfer reporting (thresholds of CAD 10,000 and above) and the completion of accurate originator/beneficiary information required under FINTRAC regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with material constraints. Canadian-regulated payment service providers, money service businesses registered under the PCMLTFA, and restricted-purpose banks established under the Bank Act may serve as auxiliary payment gateways for cotton sale proceeds and can hold segregated CAD trust accounts; however, they cannot independently provide core treasury functions such as commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, capital-escrow custody, trade-facility guarantees, or full ISDA-documented FX hedging. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed in Canada are viable only as supplementary collection points in producer jurisdictions, and they must never operate as the sole clearing rail for the Canadian-resident corporate treasury structure. A Schedule I or Schedule II chartered bank, or an authorized foreign bank branch, must remain the primary operating, escrow, and clearing counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: inbound equity subscriptions or shareholder loans should be settled by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the designated CAD or USD clearing account at the tier-1 Canadian bank, with the corporate account name, legal-entity identifier (LEI) or account number quoted in the beneficiary line, and a full narrative purpose-code descriptor stating either 'Equity subscription for working capital pursuant to CBCA articles of incorporation' or 'Shareholder loan for trade-finance collateral and cotton-bale inventory procurement'. FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting thresholds apply to all wires of CAD 10,000 or above, and the remitting bank must complete originator information before the financial institution accepts the credit. Non-resident equity injections must also be assessed against the Investment Canada Act notification thresholds when acquisition of control of a Canadian business is involved; interest-bearing shareholder loans must be documented with a promissory note and arm's-length interest rate to preserve Part XIII withholding-tax deductibility under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 generic wire transfers for advances, brokerage commissions, freight payments, demurrage, and producer disbursements to cotton ginners and yarn suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT700 commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 with Incoterms 2020 conditions for settlement of large raw-cotton bale and synthetic-yarn trades, including draft and document schedules; (iii) documentary collections (SWIFT MT400/MT910) for sight drafts against bills of lading and consignment-based cotton deliveries in producer jurisdictions; and (iv) Payments Canada Lynx, the high-value real-time gross settlement system, for domestic CAD interbank clearing.
