Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are determined in the partnership contract; limited partners must contribute cash or property (not industry or services), and no escrow or blocked-account rule is imposed by provincial law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by municipal fire department (Service de sécurité incendie) annually; structural load inspection by a licensed professional engineer for any mezzanine/racking; workplace clearance under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and provincial income tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec Online Services / CléNumérique.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity obtains a federal Business Number (BN) for the limited partnership by filing CRA Form RC1 or by registering online through My Business Account; the partnership is the registrant, not the partners. After the BN is issued, add the GST/HST program account (RT). Then register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, and use the resulting attestation plus the BN to register for QST with Revenu Québec, obtaining the TQ program account. Registrant must supply partnership agreement, foreign registry certificate (if any), Quebec attestation, and names/nationalities/addresses of general and limited partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entity obtains a federal Business Number (BN) for the limited partnership by filing CRA Form RC1 or by registering online through My Business Account; the partnership is the registrant, not the partners. After the BN is issued, add the GST/HST program account (RT). Then register the extra-provincial limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, and use the resulting attestation plus the BN to register for QST with Revenu Québec, obtaining the TQ program account. Registrant must supply partnership agreement, foreign registry certificate (if any), Quebec attestation, and names/nationalities/addresses of general and limited partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box, entity classification election, or hybrid-mismatch election is available at the federal or Quebec level. A Quebec limited partnership is automatically a flow-through partnership for income tax under the Income Tax Act and the Taxation Act (Quebec), and is separately a 'person' for GST/HST and QST under the Excise Tax Act and the Act respecting the Québec sales tax. The only relevant elections concern accounting year-end (ITA s. 249.1) and the allocation of net income among partners; these do not alter the default classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made by the partnership in Canada over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for QST, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies in Quebec. Voluntary registration below threshold is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A rail freight intermodal expedited freight-forwarding supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V (exported goods) and Part VII (transportation services), where the goods are physically exported from Canada to a destination outside Canada under a contract with a non-resident recipient, title passes outside Canada, and CRA-acceptable evidence of export is obtained by the partnership. The supply must be part of a continuous journey from a Canadian point to a foreign point; a domestic-only leg or freight-management service unconnected to export remains taxable at 5% GST and 9.975% QST. Services supplied to non-residents that are not exports still require registration and are taxable if performed in Canada.

  • Canada Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 / B13A; rail e-manifest via CBSA eManifest or Canadian International Trade Portal)
  • Intermodal through bill of lading / rail waybill naming the foreign destination and consignee, and containing carrier signature and date of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer within 180 days

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-stamping, or mandatory API clearance regime has been enacted by CRA or Revenu Québec as of 2026. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing is being promoted through the voluntary Peppol framework, but there is no legal requirement to use e-invoices for GST/HST/QST. Registrants above prescribed filing thresholds must file GST/HST and QST returns electronically via CRA NETFILE and Revenu Québec Online Services; source documents must be maintained, including electronic records, to substantiate zero-rated exports.

CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinize export zero-rating claims; the registrant must retain export evidence for six years. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is re-characterized as taxable, and the registrant is assessed for unremitted GST/HST/QST plus interest, and the input tax credits (ITCs) are denied. Knowingly false statements attract a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of net tax understated (ETA s. 285; Quebec equivalent under the Tax Administration Act), in addition to fines on conviction and, in fraud cases, unlimited reassessment periods. Reassessment generally occurs within four years, or eight years for carelessness/misrepresentation; retroactive clawback of refunds/ITCs may be made for the period covered by the reinvestigation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital account; there is no differential treatment of non-resident capital inflows, no exchange permit regime, and no repatriation licensing requirement. A Quebec limited partnership may receive capital contributions in CAD or any freely convertible foreign currency without Bank of Canada authorization. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are executed primarily as partnership distributions, subject to Canadian income tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, at treaty-reduced rates where applicable (e.g., US/Canada, China/Canada treaties), but not to any FX convertibility hurdle. Practical friction points are limited to bank AML/transaction monitoring, documentary proof of economic substance, and alignment of GST/HST/import reporting for cross-border logistics services; none constitute legal currency convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriating export or freight services receipts. A Canadian exporter or freight forwarding limited partnership may freely retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign bank accounts, denominate invoices in USD, EUR or CNY, and convert via the banking system at negotiated market rates. The only compliance obligation is FINTRAC reporting for physical cross-border transportation of currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000; electronic funds transfers are reported through financial institution transaction records and FINTRAC suspicious/structured reporting thresholds apply without surrender or conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian bank notes; prudential regulation and supervision of Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has adopted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no foreign exchange control law or central bank approval mechanism for current or capital account transactions. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) is the designated financial intelligence unit overseeing cross-border currency and monetary instrument reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury operations. Legal constraints: Canadian banking law requires deposit-taking institutions to be federally chartered under the Bank Act; an offshore electronic money institution (EMI) or digital bank is not recognized as a bank in Canada and can neither accept deposits nor hold partnership operational funds in a Canadian-regulated manner unless it secures an OSFI-authorized deposit-taking license, which is impracticable for a fintech EMI. Foreign EMIs may, however, operate as unregulated payment service providers only if they register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and implement a Canadian AML/ATF program; such entities may be used solely for ancillary low-value disbursement settlements, not for the capital escrow account or core cross-border liquidity concentration. Neobanks and EMI debit-card rails (e.g., Wise, Revolut) are not acceptable substitutes for LVTS/SWIFT correspondent treasury infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be routed through the SWIFT network from the partner's home jurisdiction to the Quebec LP's bank account using a correspondent-cleared MT103, with the SWIFT message including the partnership's legal name, the account of the National Bank or other Schedule I institution, and a structured payment reference such as 'CA-QC LP Capital Contribution / [Capital Call Notice No.]'. The CAD leg is settled over the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) operated by Payments Canada, with same-day finality for amounts exceeding standard ACSS limits. For USD contributions, clearing occurs via a New York ClearHouse Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or Federal Reserve Fedwire correspondent leg, then converted to CAD internally by the receiving bank if the escrow account is CAD-denominated. Acceptable narrative purpose codes mirroring ISO 20022 conventions include 'CAPT' (capital transfer) or 'CNCE' (capital contribution) plus supporting capital call evidence; FINTRAC does not require pre-approval but the bank will evidence the underlying partnership capital agreement under its EDD programme. Cross-border verification must match the remitter's identity against the partner register filed in Quebec, and any contribution exceeding institutional thresholds triggers source-of-funds documentation retention.Outbound settlements for railway haulage, terminal gate fees, detention/demurrage penalties, and intermodal equipment leases are disbursed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the Quebec LP's operational account, with settlement over LVTS for CAD, Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, and TARGET2/EBA SEPA for EUR legs. Commercial letters of credit (documentary credits) are permissible clearing rails for large drayage or block-train agreements payable against presentation of railway waybills (CIM/SMGS consignment notes), terminal receipts, and multimodal transport bills of lading; the partnership may act both as applicant for import LCs and as beneficiary under master service agreements. Where counterparties are in China, standby LCs or demand guarantees are routed through China's SWIFT-connected banks with URDG 758 terms. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec headline general corporate income tax rate of 26.5% (federal 15% plus Quebec 11.5%). A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent; the rate applies at the partner level on allocated partnership income rather than at the LP entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada's Part XIII statutory withholding tax rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident is 25%. Distributions from a limited partnership are not treated as Part XIII dividends; they are partnership profit allocations subject to ordinary Part I tax at the partner level, unless the distribution constitutes an amount paid or credited of a nature covered by Part XIII, such as interest, royalty, rent, or management fee.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under section 247(4) of the Income Tax Act, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required where the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents equals or exceeds C$1,000,000 in the taxation year. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue above C$750,000,000 must also file a Country-by-Country Report with the CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations of the type used in Caribbean offshore centres, but the CRA and Revenu Quebec apply the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the common law sham doctrine. A Quebec limited partnership must demonstrate real commercial substance through employees, premises, equipment, management decisions, and risk-bearing capacity; a shell LP created merely to divert rail freight profits offshore may be disregarded or reconstituted for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the federal Income Tax Act, gross negligence penalties of 50% of the tax understated plus late filing penalties apply; Quebec's Taxation Act imposes similar penalties up to 50%. Criminal tax evasion fines can reach 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner of an LP may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and provincial tax, and civil recovery may pierce the LP veil. Travel bans, asset seizure warrants, and imprisonment are available for serious evasion offences.
Shell Company and Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to recharacterize the entire arrangement and deny treaty benefits; Revenu Quebec may likewise disregard the LP for provincial tax purposes. Penalties can reach 50% of the tax avoided, and interest is charged from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for the obligations of a sham LP; individuals who directed the sham may be jointly assessed for the corporate deficiency. Quebec courts can issue injunctions, asset freezes, and personal restitution orders.
Systematic Non-Filing and Incomplete Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: The CRA imposes a penalty of C$500 per month for each incomplete or unavailable transfer pricing document, with a minimum penalty of C$1,000. Late filing of corporate or partnership returns attracts 5% of the balance due plus 1% per month surcharge up to 12 months, and Revenu Quebec applies a parallel regime with daily or monthly surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally liable for source deductions and GST/QST that the LP fails to remit. The general partner of a Quebec limited partnership carries unlimited personal liability for the partnership's statutory debts where the non-filing is attributable to the general partner.
AML, Sanctions and Money Laundering Exposure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC may impose administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for most reporting entities and C$1,000,000 for money services businesses. Criminal money laundering under the Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years, asset forfeiture, and fines at the court's discretion.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner who knowingly participate in or consent to an AML violation are personally liable under compliance orders and may face criminal charges. Sanctions-related offences can trigger travel bans, asset freezes, and personal confiscation orders.
