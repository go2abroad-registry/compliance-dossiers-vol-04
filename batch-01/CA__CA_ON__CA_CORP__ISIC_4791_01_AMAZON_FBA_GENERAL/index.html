Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca and My Business Account (MBD). Import/export customs control is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via eManifest/ACROSS; Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of Ontario under the Federal–Provincial Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate as an Ontario Business Corporation under the OBCA or a federal corporation under the CBCA (extra-provincial registration in Ontario). 2. Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) simultaneously electing GST/HST program account (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) accounts. 3. The GST/HST registration number is the BN suffixed with 'RT0001'; a separate Ontario tax registration is not required because Ontario participates in the federal HST system and Ontario corporate tax collection. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian business corporation is an independent taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no default tax-transparent treatment applies to a corporation. Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) may access the small business deduction (Ontario combined rate of 12.2% on active business income up to CAD 500,000) versus 26.5% general corporate rate; the federal dividend tax credit partially integrates distributions but does not alter entity-level taxation. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; domestic sales and Ontario HST apply within the provincial HST regime.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections and designations: (a) ETA s. 156 election for nil consideration between related registrants; (b) ETA Quick Method of Accounting for registrants with taxable supplies under CAD 400,000 (CAD 800,000 for public service bodies); (c) ETA simplified annual GST/HST filing election; (d) Income Tax Act s. 256(1) agreement allocating the small business deduction limit among associated CCPCs; (e) s. 85(1) rollover elections on qualifying transfers (relevant to migration of inventory/intangibles); (f) ETA s. 179 election designating an agent or consignee status for cross-border marketplace supply chain; (g) ETA Schedule VI certifications for zero-rated export supplies; (h) formal deemed-supplier arrangement under the 2021 cross-border e-commerce framework where a marketplace platform (Amazon.ca) collects and remits GST/HST on behalf of an unregistered non-resident fulfilment vendor.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST registration is mandatory once the supplier's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240; small supplier threshold). Amazon FBA operators typically exceed this threshold upon operating commencement and are advised to register immediately; non-resident fulfilment vendors selling inventory stored in Canadian fulfilment centres are deemed to carry on business in Canada and cannot rely on small-supplier status where the marketplace platform acts as deemed supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 5–6 where the supplier exports, or causes the export of, the tangible personal property by delivery to the purchaser or a common carrier for shipment outside Canada within a reasonable time (normally 60–90 days), and documentation proving export is retained. Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 when supplied to a non-resident provided the service is not performed wholly in Canada and does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply; digital marketplace and software-as-a-service fees to non-residents qualify with proper contracts and billing records. Amazon FBA-specific: sales shipped to Canadian customers through Amazon.ca fulfilment centres are taxable at 13% HST in Ontario; export sales made from Canadian inventory through Amazon cross-border channels (US destinations) qualify for 0% GST/HST where title passes abroad, goods are physically exported by the FBA export carrier, and the corresponding export documentation is retained. Imported inventory entering Ontario via FBA is subject to 13% HST at importation, recoverable as an input tax credit (ITC) by the GST/HST registrant.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or qualified in-transit exemption for low-value shipments) with CBSA approval and unique transaction number
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods, quantities, and stated 0% GST/HST classification
  • Transport document evidencing departure from Canada: Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier trailer manifest issued by the common carrier
  • CBSA/ACROSS declaration report or licensed customs broker certificate confirming the date and port of export
  • Bank receipt, SWIFT confirmation, or Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds in the Canadian entity's account

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping regime is operational in Canada. A registrant must issue a sales invoice or simplified receipt containing prescribed information: supplier name, BN and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, invoice number, a description of the goods/services, tax payable, and the status of zero-rated/exempt supplies (ETA s. 223). Electronic invoicing is expressly permitted and the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing program (Peppol network) is being phased in voluntarily from 2024–2025. Cross-border movements through CBSA require eManifest/ACI data (arrival certification) and, for exports, the B13A declaration or low-value in-transit exemption; Amazon Seller Central acts as the primary transaction reconciliation portal and no API-based fiscal clearance is currently mandated for B2C marketplace invoices.

CRA's Export Verification Program and CBSA joint audits scrutinize e-commerce exporters for documented physical export, revenue recognition, and FX inflow. Where zero-rating documentation is deficient, CRA will claw back the 0% treatment and reassess the full supply at 13% HST, with arrears interest at the prescribed rate (currently 7%–9% per annum compound) from the date of the original return plus a penalty under ETA s. 280(1) equal to 6% of any unremitted net tax amounts. Knowingly making false statements, omitting material information, or claiming fraudulent export refunds attracts a s. 285(1) penalty of CAD 250 or 25% of the net tax, and under s. 285(1.2) a 50% misrepresentation penalty on misstated credits; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327(1)(a) and Criminal Code s. 380 can result in imprisonment of 5–14 years. Fraudulent refund attempts extend the normal four-year reassessment period to 10 years or indefinitely; CRA may also issue a requirements notice to Amazon to withhold and remit marketplace disbursements for the settling of assessed GST/HST liabilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a free-floating exchange rate; Canada has observed IMF Article VIII obligations since 1952. No exchange licensing, approval, quotas, or surrender requirements apply to residents or non-residents. Dividends, branch remittances, interest, and royalties are freely transferable abroad, subject only to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced rates typically 5% to 15% for direct dividends, and 0% to 15% for interest and royalties under the Canada-US, Canada-China, and Canada-Hong Kong tax treaties) and to money-laundering reporting thresholds. Capital injections and loan repayments are unrestricted. FINTRAC requires financial institutions to report inbound and outbound electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction or per 24-hour series; this is a compliance reporting obligation, not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion obligation exists in Canada. An exporter may retain, transfer, or convert foreign currency freely in domestic or offshore accounts. Foreign-currency accounting and CAD-equivalent translation (including the functional-currency election on Form T1133 under section 261 of the Income Tax Act) apply only for tax reporting purposes. Practical reporting duties: the settlement bank files FINTRAC EFT reports for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the exporter must retain underlying trade documentation to evidence economic substance. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority, operating the Canadian-dollar free-float and general monetary policy pursuant to the Bank of Canada Act. Foreign exchange policy and the official Exchange Fund Account are administered by the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada) under the Currency Act. Prudential supervision of Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no specialized foreign-exchange control bureau; the Foreign Exchange Control Board was dissolved in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational collection and disbursement flows; LOW for statutory capital escrow and regulated treasury purposes. Stripe Canada, Ltd. (FINTRAC-registered) acts as a merchant acquirer and payout processor suitable for Amazon marketplace receivables, but does not provide interbank FX dealing, derivative credit lines, or SWIFT trade disbursement services. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound equity from the non-resident shareholder via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian operating account, with remittance narrative stating: SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION - [legal entity name] - Reference [board resolution number]. Canada does not maintain a statutory central-bank purpose-code list; the receiving bank maps the transaction to an internal capital-flow category for FINTRAC EFT reporting, which is mandatory for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. The receiving bank will request: (1) a board resolution authorizing share issuance and account signatories; (2) the share subscription agreement; (3) evidence of the shareholder's source of funds (originator bank statement and parent tax records); (4) an updated share register; and (5) for subscriptions exceeding CAD 100,000 in cost, a determination of Form T1135 foreign-property reporting exposure for the Canadian company. Equity subscriptions are not subject to Part XIII withholding tax.
