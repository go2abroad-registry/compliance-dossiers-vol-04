Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce) of the Canton of the registered office (canton to be selected by the incorporators, e.g. Zurich or Zug), supervised by the Federal Commercial Register Department (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations, SR 220 (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft); in particular Arts. 621 (formation), 629 (registered share capital), 632 (minimum capital and paid-up minimum), 634 (foundation deed), 640-646 (registered shares), 716 (Board of Directors) and 934 (application for registration); Federal Act on the Commercial Register (SR 221.411.1); Companies Names Act (SR 221.411.4); Federal Act on Games of Chance and Money Games (SR 935.0) Art. 3 scope exclusion for non-remunerated social games.

Formation / constitutional filings

Certified public Foundation Deed of Incorporation (Errichtungsakt) containing the Articles of Association (Statuten), executed before a Swiss notary, together with the electronic submission to the Commercial Register pursuant to Art. 934 OR; the registry issues a Certificate of Registration (Handelsregisterauszug / Extrait du registre du commerce).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Nominal minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 (Art. 632 para. 1 OR) of which at least 20% under each subscribed share class, and not less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, must be paid up in cash prior to registration (Art. 632 para. 3 OR). The paid-in capital must be deposited into a blocked capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a FINMA-supervised bank, is reserved for use by the company in formation, and is released upon the company's registration; an undertaking to refund the deposit (Rückerstattungserklärung) is given if registration is refused or abandoned.

Funding & unlock path: Cantonal fire-certification certificate (VKF/AEAI Gebrauchs- und Funktionsabnahme) for the leasehold office, structural load-inspection certificate per SIA 261 for the selected premises, occupational workplace clearance under the Federal Labour Act (SR 822.11) covering VDU ergonomics, emergency exits and first-aid equipment, and employer enrolment in the SUVA accident clearance and safety-co-operation programme; data centres used for the digital game platform must be operated by a certified Swiss TIER-III provider with verifiable business-continuity clearance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenoessische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV / Administration federale des contributions, AFC) - VAT Main Division, Berne; official portals: www.estv.admin.ch for guidance and filings, 'Fiscus' as the secure e-communication and VAT return filing platform, www.zefix.ch for the central commercial register, and www.uid.admin.ch for the Unified Business Identifier (UID) register.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) in the cantonal commercial register (Zefix); the company automatically receives the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which is the base Swiss tax identification number for all tax relations. Step 2 - Apply for Swiss VAT registration with the FTA/ESTV if annual taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 (resident taxpayer, Art. 10 para. 1 and 2 MWSTG) or if the company is a non-resident supplier of electronic services to Swiss private consumers (mandatory from the first franc, Art. 10 para. 3 MWSTG); the FTA issues the MWST-Nr (VAT number, UID-based) normally within 2-4 weeks on Form 201/202 registration. Step 3 - Non-resident digital publisher without Swiss seat: use the simplified registration procedure (vereinfachte Steueranmeldung) under Art. 67-69 MWSTG, appoint a Swiss tax representative (domiciliary agent) and obtain a Swiss VAT ID even in the absence of a permanent establishment. Step 4 - Cantonal direct tax registration: only if a Swiss permanent establishment, office or employees exist, register the AG for corporate income tax and net wealth tax with the competent cantonal tax administration (e.g., Zug); a pure offshore/foreign-routed app-publishing activity with no Swiss substance does not trigger cantonal registration. Step 5 - Gaming-specific clearance: obtain a legal memorandum confirming that pure simulated social casino (no real-money payout, no withdrawal) is not a game of chance under the Swiss Federal Gambling Act (Geldspielgesetz) and therefore requires no gambling concession; licensed real-money online casinos are VAT-exempt under Art. 21 para. 2 no. 16 MWSTG, while unlicensed social casino IAP remains standard-rated at 8.1%.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Swiss AG is a fully taxable separate legal person, subject to Swiss corporate income tax at federal, cantonal and communal level (federal statutory rate 8.5% after tax, approximately 18% effective before cantonal/communal relief; Zug combined effective rate around 11.9%) and to cantonal net wealth tax on equity. Unlimited tax liability applies for worldwide income with territorial carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate (exemption with progression). Switzerland does not recognize check-the-box or any pass-through election for an AG. For Swiss VAT, the AG is a taxable person under Art. 10 MWSTG with full input VAT deduction rights on costs attributable to taxable and zero-rated export supplies; mixed-use input VAT is apportioned under Art. 30-31 MWSTG.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows Swiss corporate law. Available elections and statutory mechanisms: (i) VAT group taxation (Gruppenbesteuerung, Art. 21 para. 3 MWSTG) consolidating Swiss-resident group companies into one VAT person; (ii) participation relief election on qualified dividends and capital gains (Beteiligungsabzug, Art. 69 DBG) subject to minimum 10% shareholding or CHF 1,000,000 participation value; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) on qualifying excess Swiss equity (Art. 25a-25b DBG, in force since 2020), particularly used by Zug financing/treasury entities; (iv) election of the small-business VAT exemption (Art. 11 MWSTG) for residents under CHF 100,000 turnover, not available to non-resident digital suppliers; (v) no hybrid-mismatch election - Swiss law statutorily denies deductions for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities and reverse hybrids under Art. 8a DBG and Art. 61a VStG (BEPS Action 2 alignment), so financing and royalty structures must be documented as non-abusive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover for Swiss-resident businesses and for non-resident B2B service suppliers where the Swiss recipient is liable for tax (Art. 10 para. 1 and 2 MWSTG); ZERO threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronic services (including downloadable social casino apps and in-app purchases) to private consumers resident in Switzerland - registration is mandatory from the first transaction (Art. 10 para. 3 MWSTG). Reduced rates: 2.6% (basic necessities) and 3.8% (accommodation); digital gaming/IAP supplies always attract the 8.1% standard rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 23 para. 1 no. 1 and Art. 37 MWSTG, exported goods and exported services are exempt with input VAT deduction (effective 0% rate). For a Swiss AG publishing simulated social casino via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing: (i) B2B supply of game licences, sublicences or advertising services to foreign app-store operators or foreign advertisers: place of receipt abroad, 0% with full input VAT recovery; (ii) B2C in-app purchases by foreign consumers: place of supply is the consumer's residence (Art. 18 para. 1 letter d MWSTG), therefore outside Switzerland, 0%; (iii) B2C supplies to Swiss consumers: Swiss place of supply, standard 8.1% (no reduced rate). To sustain 0%, the AG must demonstrate the residence of each foreign customer through app-store billing-address data, Google Play/Apple App Store country codes, payment-card issuer country, geolocation/IP evidence and platform settlement reports. Where Apple or Google acts as merchant of record, the platform usually discharges Swiss VAT on Swiss consumer sales; the AG must retain the platform invoices and settlement statements to avoid double assessment of the same IAP consideration. Simulated social casino without real-money payout is not licensed Swiss gambling, so the Art. 21 para. 2 no. 16 MWSTG exemption does not apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitschein/AEB) - mandatory only for physical dispatches incident to gaming hardware or promotional merchandise; for pure digital downloads this item is substituted by platform sales and subscriber reports
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Airwaybill - transport evidence required when physical goods are exported; not applicable to downloadable app content, replaced by ISP and platform traffic evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank and payment service provider (PSP) credit records evidencing net proceeds credited to the Swiss AG from foreign app-store operators and advertising counterparties
  • App-Store Sales and Subscriber Reports - Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console monthly country-level billing files (ISO 3166 customer codes, bill-to addresses, payment-country data, purchase timestamps)
  • Merchant-of-Record Platform Invoices - Apple/Google consolidated VAT invoices and settlement statements showing the foreign platform as reseller or disclosed agent for Swiss consumer sales

Switzerland has NO real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no API tax-stamping and no mandatory state e-invoicing comparable to Latin American or EU models. VAT reporting is period-based: taxpayers file periodic returns (quarterly, half-yearly or annually) electronically to the ESTV via the secure Fiscus portal. Invoices must contain the mandatory elements of Art. 26 MWSTG (name and address of supplier, VAT ID, recipient, date, type and quantity of supply, consideration, and VAT amount/rate). Since 1 July 2020, all payments referencing a Swiss QR-IBAN must use the standardized Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) with embedded QR code; this is a payment-slip standard, not a tax clearance stamp. All e-invoices and underlying accounting vouchers must be retained in an electronically verifiable format for 10 years (Art. 958f of the Swiss Code of Obligations) and must be made available to ESTV auditors in a structured data export upon request.

The ESTV's VAT division specifically targets zero-rated digital exporters (MWST-Info 02, electronic services) and applies higher risk scoring to social/gaming apps because of in-app purchase complexity and platform commission flows. If the 0% export rate is claimed without contemporaneous residence evidence, the FTA retrospectively recharacterizes the supply as Swiss-taxable: output VAT is assessed on the full consideration, input VAT is clawed back, and default interest accrues under Art. 87 MWSTG from the statutory due date. Retrospective reassessment is statute-barred after 5 years for ordinary errors but extends to 10 years where tax evasion is shown (Art. 39 para. 2 MWSTG). Penalties: breach of procedural duties - warning up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 70 MWSTG); negligent understatement - fine up to one-third of the evaded tax (Art. 71 para. 2 MWSTG); intentional evasion, including fabricated export documentation, sham merchant-of-record arrangements or shell-company routing of app proceeds - fine up to 4 times the evaded tax (Art. 71 para. 1 MWSTG), plus criminal prosecution under Articles 186-187 of the Swiss Penal Code for fraudulent tax evasion (imprisonment up to 3 years, up to 10 years for organized constellations); directors and compliance officers are individually liable. Switzerland imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier is applied as an internal treasury benchmark for verified FX conversion and documentary completeness of export consideration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Swiss law does not distinguish between residents and non-residents for foreign-exchange purposes and imposes no exchange-control approval on dividends, profit remittances, loan repayments, royalties or licence fees. The only material hurdles are corporate and tax formalities, including the Swiss 35% anticipatory withholding tax on certain profit distributions and treaty-based relief or refund procedures. Cross-border revenues from social-casino app sales are treated as ordinary service or goods receipts and may be converted or remitted freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland has no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio and no fixed timeline for export receipts. An exporter may retain, invest or convert proceeds in any currency; only anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds, tax return disclosure and, where applicable, statistical surveys of the Swiss National Bank apply.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera; responsible for monetary policy, currency issuance and financial system stability; no comprehensive foreign-exchange-control mandate; FINMA supervises banking and money-laundering conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMIs as a primary treasury rail; Medium for FINMA-licensed or fintech-supervised payment institutions as ancillary transaction sub-accounts. An offshore EMI cannot passport an EU or UK licence into Switzerland and cannot access SIC or offer Swiss-located CHF account numbers without a Swiss banking partner or FINMA licence. Use such EMIs only for ancillary multicurrency collection; keep the main escrow and operating liquidity in a Swiss-licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if turnover > CHF 50 million; Master File required if group turnover > CHF 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but requires real economic substance (office, staff, decision-making) to establish tax residency and to benefit from DTAs. Anti-abuse rules (e.g., GAAR) apply to shell companies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to CHF 250,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years; additional civil penalties of up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for restitution of evaded taxes, subject to travel bans and potential imprisonment if found complicit.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, retroactive tax assessments, and penalties up to CHF 100,000; potential reclassification as a transparent entity.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the sham structure; travel bans may be imposed.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CHF 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; corporate fines up to CHF 5 million; revocation of business licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, and may be subject to asset freezes and travel bans.
