Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), official portal www.estv.admin.ch; UID registry and commercial register portal www.zefix.ch; cantonal tax administrations impose and administer cantonal/communal corporate income tax and capital tax.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss or foreign entity engaged in taxable activities in Switzerland must obtain a Swiss VAT registration number (MWST-Nr / TVA-Nr / IVA-Nr) by filing the VAT registration form with ESTV before the start of taxable activity. The business identification number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer UID / numéro IDE / numero IDI) is issued automatically upon entry in the Swiss Commercial Register (Zefix) and serves as the common tax number for federal, cantonal, and municipal tax purposes. A non-resident company without a Swiss registered office but with Swiss-source turnover above CHF 100,000 must use the simplified foreign-supplier registration scheme, submitting ESTV Form 034 and, where requested, appointing or arranging a fiscal representative. The cantonal tax number is allocated by the cantonal tax office of the registered seat or permanent establishment once the first tax filing is accepted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available under Swiss law; legal form determines tax classification (Aktiengesellschaft is a taxable entity). Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Swiss VAT group election (Art. 11 of the Swiss VAT Act), allowing a fiscal unity to file as one taxpayer; (ii) participation deduction election for qualifying dividends and capital gains (Art. 69 and Art. 70 of the Federal Direct Tax Act); (iii) patent box election for qualifying IP income (reduced taxation of up to 90% of such income, Art. 24b para. 2 of the Federal Direct Tax Act); (iv) notional interest deduction on excess equity as an optional cantonal measure after the Swiss Tax Reform 17; (v) election to apply simplified export zero-rating documentation for small consignments; and (vi) election for the disclosure of hidden reserves in a tax-neutral restructuring under Art. 61 of the Federal Direct Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective: Swiss law imposes mandatory anti-abuse limitation on deduction denial for cross-border hybrid arrangements consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual world-wide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 VATA), after deducting exempt supplies; foreign suppliers performing supplies in Switzerland exceeding CHF 100,000 Swiss-source turnover must register irrespective of any permanent establishment; foreign suppliers of electronic services to Swiss private customers are liable to register regardless of the threshold using the simplified OSS-like procedure, unless turnover is below CHF 100,000 and they apply the small-business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% Swiss VAT where the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of Switzerland to a foreign destination and are intended for permanent export or to be processed abroad (Art. 23 VATA). Services qualify for zero-rating when the place of supply is outside Switzerland, including remote software services, screen-sharing and IoT telemetry supplied to a recipient with an establishment abroad, provided the effective benefit and usage occur outside Switzerland (Art. 18 para. 2 lit. b VATA). For ISIC 6201 remote desktop software, zero-rating is available where the foreign recipient is a business or non-Swiss private person resident abroad and the supply consists of a digital service the place of which is determined by the customer's residence or establishment, not by the server location.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec) confirming the physical exit of exported goods or the technical export declaration number for service-related documentation
  • Bill of Lading / CMB / CMR or Air Waybill evidencing transport from the Swiss customs territory to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate showing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency or through a Swiss bank account in the name of the exporter
  • Commercial invoice, contract, and (where applicable) Swiss export zero-rating supporting statement identifying the foreign recipient, ISIC 6201 scope description, and the applicable zero-rate basis

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system and does not require digital stamping or government pre-clearance of invoices. Swiss VAT law recognizes paper and electronic invoices equally. For domestic B2B invoicing, the financial industry standard is the Swiss QR-bill and the SIX eBill platform (VeDA/SIX); PEPPOL BIS UBL-based e-invoicing is increasingly accepted for public procurement and large commercial counterparties. The ESTV provides an online e-services portal for VAT returns, XML-based periodic files, and structured electronic registration. There is no mandatory API-based continuous transaction control; eBill or QR-bill interchange uses proprietary bank APIs and contractual PEPPOL sample by mutual agreement.

ESTV may retrospectively assess VAT on goods or services claimed as zero-rated exports if the exporter fails to deliver a complete export evidence bundle within the statutory inspection period. The regular limitation period for VAT assessments is ten years. In case of fraudulent substantiation such as fictitious exports, false e-dec data, or forged inbound remittance certificates, the VAT is clawed back together with default interest (currently 4.5% per annum) and may attract an administrative fine of up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 39 VATA). Deliberate tax evasion can additionally incur criminal fines, and customs offences for false export declarations are sanctioned separately with confiscation and penalties. For remote software and digital services, ESTV scrutinizes the place-of-establishment of the recipient and any evidence of use/benefit in Switzerland; a mismatch between contract counterparty, payment origin, and IP addresses may trigger full reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible with no foreign-exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at 35% on dividends, which may be reduced or refunded under an applicable tax treaty or through the Swiss participation relief regime. No exchange-control approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation, but corporate substance, transfer-pricing documentation, and the absence of abusive tax structuring must be demonstrated to preserve treaty access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency in Swiss or offshore bank accounts, convert freely at market rates, and use such funds for operational or investment purposes without SNB authorization.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Eidgenössische Finanzmarktaufsicht FINMA (regulatory authority for banks and financial intermediaries)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-moderate for core treasury clearing. Switzerland is not in the EEA, so foreign electronic money institutions cannot passport into Switzerland; a third-country EMI requires either a FINMA banking licence or a licensed Swiss bank partnership to issue local IBANs and process SIC/SEPA payments. EMIs are viable only as complementary merchant collection gateways (e.g., Stripe, Paddle) and may not serve as the primary regulated clearing rail for inbound capital or large trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for direct CHF or EUR transfers from the beneficial owner's account to the Swiss AG's capital escrow account. The payment must reference 'Share Capital Contribution' and include a bank reference identifying the purpose code for direct investment or capital contribution. No SNB exchange-control approval is required, but the receiving Swiss bank must obtain source-of-funds evidence and may require beneficiary attestation. For amounts above CHF 50,000, Swiss AML and international reporting standards trigger standard enhanced screening; cross-border payments above applicable thresholds may also need inclusion in the Swiss National Bank balance-of-payments reporting survey if the bank reports it on behalf of the enterprise. Ensure the beneficiary legal name, address, and IBAN exactly match the corporate register entry.Prime clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments, SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank settlement transfers, SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated payments within Europe, and SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for domestic CHF payments. Trade settlements may be effected via irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) using SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT710 amendment, with SWIFT MT754 reimbursement for commercial LC settlement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Switzerland imposes direct federal tax (DFT) at 8.5% of net profit after federal tax, equivalent to an effective 7.83% of pre-tax profit; combined federal, cantonal and communal effective corporate income tax rate in the canton of Zug (2024) is approximately 11.9% on pre-tax profit; the statutory combined range across Swiss cantons is approximately 11.9% to 21.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is 35% of the gross dividend, withheld by the Swiss resident paying company (the AG) and remitted to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/SIF) within 30 days of distribution (Art. 13, 17 VStG).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is triggered in Switzerland when the consolidated group annual turnover exceeds CHF 80 million for the sale of goods or CHF 20 million for services (Art. 58a DBG, FTA Circular on TP documentation); for a remote screen share and IoT telemetry software entity, the CHF 20 million services threshold applies. Below these thresholds the AG is not exempt from establishing arm's-length pricing; the tax authorities may request documentation and impose penalties up to CHF 10,000 plus interest where the taxpayer fails to substantiate the transaction price. A Swiss safe harbour for low-value-adding intra-group routine services allows a cost-plus mark-up of or an equivalent net margin of approximately 5%, aligning with the OECD simplified approach.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no general Economic Substance Regulation in the style of the UAE or other offshore regimes; however, the AG must demonstrate genuine substance in Switzerland to sustain treaty benefits and domestic participation relief. Substance indicia include a Swiss registered office with physical premises, a Swiss VAT registration where required, a Swiss bank account, payroll and social security contributions for Swiss staff, board meetings and actual management decisions taken in Switzerland, and local development or monitoring of the core IoT software IP. A letterbox or sham company whose management and critical functions are located abroad may be recharacterised under the abuse-of-law doctrine (Art. 21 VStG and Swiss tax jurisprudence), leading to denial of the treaty refund and participation exemption, reallocation of profits to the foreign parent, and back-taxes with penalty. Additionally, groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more are subject to the Swiss OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax rules (QDMTT and IIR) at 15% effective rate from 2025; the present low-revenue AG is outside that scope.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Federal penalty of up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 175 DBG); cantonal Zug penalty of up to three times the evaded tax; reduction to two-thirds for negligence; default interest of approximately 4.5% per annum; criminal tax fraud with fraudulent books or documents (Art. 186 DBG) is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years, and the tax debt remains collectible with compulsory enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers who cause or permit the evasion are jointly and severally liable under Art. 52 DBG for the company's unpaid taxes and under Art. 12 VStG for unremitted withholding tax; the corporate veil may be pierced where funds are diverted; individual criminal convictions can trigger travel bans, asset freezes, extradition requests, and disqualification from holding Swiss board positions.
Failure to Withhold and Remit 35% Anticipatory Tax

Statutory sanction: Collection of the full unremitted 35% withholding tax from the AG plus default interest; administrative fine of up to three times the evaded withholding tax under the VStG; criminal prosecution for wilful non-remittance under Art. 63-65 VStG with imprisonment up to three years or monetary penalty.

Director personal liability: Board members are personally, jointly and severally liable under Art. 12 VStG for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties, regardless of whether the company remains solvent; civil recovery actions may reach personal assets, and significant unpaid withholding tax can lead to bankruptcy proceedings, director travel restrictions, and personal criminal records.
Shell Company, Sham Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose provisions of the Switzerland-China DTA, reallocate profits to the foreign shareholder under Swiss substance-over-form and anti-abuse doctrine, refuse the participation relief, and impose evasion penalties of up to three times the associated tax; elimination of the AG's status as a tax-resident company may result in its income being taxed in the hands of the foreign parent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations or tax-residence attestations face civil liability for the refused refunds and penalties, criminal proceedings for aiding tax evasion, and potential personal seizure of assets; repeated abuse may trigger a cantonal prohibition on acting as director and a Swiss entry or return ban.
Money Laundering and Payment-Rails Compliance (AMLA / Embargo)

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), a company acting as a professional payment provider without the required SRO membership or FINMA authorisation may face fines of up to CHF 500,000 and revocation of authorisations; under Art. 305bis SCC, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years; violations of Swiss embargoes and sanctions under the Embargo Act and SECO directives carry fines up to CHF 500,000 plus confiscation and debarment from public procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or through gross negligence allow the platform to process illicit payments are criminally liable under Art. 29 VStrR and Art. 305ter SCC for inadequate financial due diligence; personal fines, travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed, and the corporate veil may be disregarded for civil restitution to victims of the illegal access.
Payroll and Social Security Liability Exposure

Statutory sanction: Swiss social security institutions will assess unpaid contributions with interest and surcharges of up to 5% of the missed amount; VAT shortfalls are subject to penalties of up to three times the evaded tax under Art. 70 MWSTG, plus default interest; deliberate VAT fraud triggers criminal proceedings with imprisonment up to three years; the employer can be barred from benefiting from payment facilitation providers if compliance flags arise.

Director personal liability: Under Art. 52 DBG and the analogous provisions of the Swiss tax and social security statutes, directors are personally liable where culpable failure caused the non-payment; unpaid pension and social security debts are privileged claims in bankruptcy and directors face travel bans if they flee the country while debts are outstanding, as well as civil action by the compensation fund for the shortfall.
