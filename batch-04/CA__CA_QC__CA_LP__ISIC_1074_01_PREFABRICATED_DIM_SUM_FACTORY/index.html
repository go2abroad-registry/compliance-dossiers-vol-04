Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the United States. A limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes. Partners allocate income, gains, losses, and credits under the partnership agreement in accordance with the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Québec), and the LP files an information return (T5013 and applicable Quebec schedules). For GST/HST and QST, a partnership may elect to become a registrant under simplified procedures where eligible, and a non-resident supplier may apply for direct or simplified registration. Anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Act apply to deny deductions where payments give rise to hybrid mismatch outcomes in the residence jurisdiction of a partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small supplier threshold: C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or C$30,000 in total taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; the same C$30,000 threshold applies for QST registration. A non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada must evaluate deemed registration requirements, as the small supplier exception may not apply where a permanent establishment exists or where supplies are made in the course of a Canadian business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to tangible personal property (e.g., industrially quick-frozen dim sum) supplied to a non-resident purchaser and exported by the supplier, by the purchaser, or by the supplier's agent. Under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, goods exported as part of a sale are zero-rated if the supplier retains proof of export within the required timeframe. Under Quebec's QST system, a corresponding zero-rating applies to property exported from Quebec. Services supplied for an export transaction may also be zero-rated where reasonable and directly connected to the exported goods. The exporter must show continuous possession and transport of the goods to a destination outside Canada, and the goods must not be further processed or consumed in Canada before export. Frozen dim sum is eligible for input tax credit recovery for GST/HST and QST even when zero-rated, subject to substantiation rules.

  • B13A Export Declaration or Electronic Export Report submitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where required
  • Commercial invoice with full description of frozen dim sum, quantity, unit price, terms of sale (Incoterms), and shipping details
  • Bill of Lading / straight or order bill of lading / air waybill issued by the carrier
  • Packing list matching the commercial invoice and export declaration
  • Proof of export from the carrier, including terminal receipts, cargo control documents, or vessel/aircraft departure confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or wire transfer / bank receipt evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Non-resident purchaser evidence, such as a purchase order and corporate registration documentation
  • Certificate of origin and any phytosanitary / food safety certificates required by the destination jurisdiction, where applicable

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance/stamping regime is currently implemented in Canada or Quebec. Suppliers must issue valid tax invoices containing prescribed GST/HST and QST information. Electronic invoices and records are acceptable if kept in a readable and auditable format; CRA and Revenu Québec do not require prior approval for electronic invoicing, but all invoicing data must be retained for six years and be accessible on audit. Businesses may voluntarily adopt PEPPOL or EDI formats, but no statutory API-integrated fiscal stamping obligation exists for frozen food exporters.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal limitation period, deny zero-rating, and recover the uncollected GST/HST and QST together with interest from the date the tax would otherwise have been payable. Penalties include a gross negligence penalty of 50% of any tax amount understated or refund overstated, civil penalties for late or false export documentation, and escalation to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, with substantial fines and potential imprisonment for wilful evasion. Export records, including customs declarations and proof of physical export, must be retained for six years. If the period for documentary substantiation is not observed, the zero-rating treatment may be unwound after the fact and the exporter may also be denied input tax credits in respect of the export supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII obligations; no exchange controls differentiate current-account versus capital-account transactions. Capital inflows (non-resident limited partner contributions to the Quebec LP) and outflows (profit distributions, trade settlements, interest and fee remittances) are unrestricted. The operative hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., under the Canada-US Treaty), Quebec provincial withholding obligations, and the pass-through reporting regime requiring the LP to file Partnership Information Returns (T5013) and, in Quebec, the Relevé 22 / CO-17 schedules. Profit remittance to a non-resident limited partner is legally free, but the LP must secure partner-level tax residence certificates and maintain source documents sufficient to prevent withholding shortfalls or treaty-relief rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained in foreign-denominated accounts or converted to CAD at an arm's-length spot rate without central bank approval. Indirect compliance obligations apply: (i) for GST/HST zero-rating of exported frozen dim sum under section 142 of the Excise Tax Act, the exporter must retain proof of export within prescribed timeframes; (ii) customs export declarations must state the value in CAD using the Bank of Canada daily or monthly average exchange rate as applicable; and (iii) FINTRAC large-currency-transaction and international-electronic-funds-transfer reporting thresholds must be monitored on all export-related account activity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec-specific supervisory oversight for money services businesses is exercised by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with material legal guardrails. Canada does not grant full banking licences to foreign digital EMI platforms; such entities may operate only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or payment service providers registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, licensed by the AMF under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Quebec). Non-resident EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) can provide multi-currency wallets, payment initiation, and conversion services, but they cannot accept deposits in the Canadian banking sense or hold client funds as a Schedule I bank. Operational balances must ultimately be safeguarded with an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank. The LP may use an EMI for small trade-receivable collections, but the primary treasury ledger, letter-of-credit issuance, and borrowing relationships must remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank to preserve CRA audit comfort and tax-entity clarity. Legal constraints include compulsory FINTRAC MSB registration before any Canadian-facing operation, AMF licensing in Quebec, a Canadian physical presence requirement for the MSB applicant, and strict segregation of third-party funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Quebec LP must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for correspondent-level transfers) to the LP's CAD-denominated operational account at a Canadian Schedule I bank; CAD settlement is cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx system (real-time gross settlement) and USD-denominated contributions clear via the Canadian bank's US correspondent through CHIPS or Fedwire. Field 70 (Remittance Information) should identify 'Capital Contribution to CA_LP per Partnership Agreement dated [date]'; field 77B may reference the contributing partner's name and REQ registration number. Canada has no statutory SWIFT purpose-code regime, but banks apply internal transaction-coding templates (e.g., business capital contribution vs operating funds), and FINTRAC international-electronic-funds-transfer reporting applies to inbound transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Supporting verification for each contribution: the partnership agreement, a capital-call notice, the REQ registration extract, and a general-partner statement confirming that the funds are not third-party loans or unreported sales proceeds; the contributing partner's remittance instructions must match the beneficial-ownership file to avert sanctions false-positives.Trade disbursements for raw materials (wheat flour, frozen seafood, vegetable oils, packaging) and production equipment should be settled through four clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers from the bank's trade portal for routine supplier payments using pre-registered beneficiary templates and reference to purchase-order numbers; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or up-to-90-day usance) issued by the Canadian bank in favour of overseas suppliers and advised/confirmed through the beneficiary's bank, covering documentary terms (commercial invoice, full-set bills of lading, phytosanitary or certificate of origin); (iii) domestic CAD payments through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or Lynx for high-value payments; and (iv) USD payments to US suppliers through CHIPS/Fedwire via the bank's US clearing correspondent. All disbursements must be supported by purchase orders and ERP inventory records, and must reference the LP's GST/HST registration number to preserve input tax credits. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate feasibility with material legal guardrails. Canada does not grant full banking licences to foreign digital EMI platforms; such entities may operate only as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or payment service providers registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, licensed by the AMF under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Quebec). Non-resident EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) can provide multi-currency wallets, payment initiation, and conversion services, but they cannot accept deposits in the Canadian banking sense or hold client funds as a Schedule I bank. Operational balances must ultimately be safeguarded with an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank. The LP may use an EMI for small trade-receivable collections, but the primary treasury ledger, letter-of-credit issuance, and borrowing relationships must remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank to preserve CRA audit comfort and tax-entity clarity. Legal constraints include compulsory FINTRAC MSB registration before any Canadian-facing operation, AMF licensing in Quebec, a Canadian physical presence requirement for the MSB applicant, and strict segregation of third-party funds.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions; Local File and Master File required by CRA administrative practice for groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 100 million; CbC report for groups with consolidated revenue over CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act permits CRA to disregard transactions whose primary purpose is tax avoidance and that lack economic substance. For limited partnerships, the CRA scrutinizes whether the non-resident partner bears real risk and receives substantive profits only where the limited partner has actual capital investment and participation in business management

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Filing False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2): 50% of the misstated tax; criminal tax evasion under sections 238 and 239: fine up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus payment of all taxes and interest

Director personal liability: Directors collectively liable under section 227.1 for unremitted employee payroll deductions and Part XIII withholding (assessments are protected by a 48-hour director liability hold); criminal prosecution extends to controlling partners; CRA can seek travel bans and cross-border warrants
Transfer Pricing and Failure to Maintain Documentation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under subsection 247(3): 10% of the net adjustment on the non-arm's-length amount, minimum CAD 500; additional tax reassessment at full corporate rate; with GAAR, 25% avoidance penalty under subsection 245(1)

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, assent, or acquiesce in such arrangements are jointly and severally subject to a restricted debt in bankruptcy and to director liability for GST/HST and payroll-related violations; are not protected by the corporate veil in serious misrepresentations
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR: denial of treaty benefits and full reassessment; subsection 245(3) penalty equal to 25% of the tax benefit (increased to 50% for false statements); cancellation of certain partnership allocations under the stated capital and deemed-residence rules

Director personal liability: CRA can apply the 'corporate veil-piercing' doctrine in civil fraud actions; directors may be personally assessed for amounts deducted but not remitted, and are liable for damages to creditors where they intentionally keep a sham entity
Anti-Money Laundering / AML / Unregistered FINTRAC Entity

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation; criminal fine/ministerial penalty of CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for deliberate diversion of payments

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for civil penalties if they knowingly fail to establish a compliance program, fail to report a transaction, or participate in a conspiracy; FINTRAC may issue a prohibition against the LP and its directors
