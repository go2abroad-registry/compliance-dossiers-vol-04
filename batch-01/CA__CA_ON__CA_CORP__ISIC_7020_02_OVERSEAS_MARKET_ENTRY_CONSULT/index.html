Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST program via My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign entities obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by applying to the CRA (Form RC1 or via the Business Registration Online service). For GST/HST, the foreign entity must apply for a separate GST/HST account (program code RT) under the same BN. Non-resident registrants may be required to file GST/HST returns electronically and may use a Canadian representative. Ontario does not issue a distinct provincial tax ID because HST is administered federally; Ontario corporate income tax is also collected by the CRA under the Ontario corporate tax (CT) account. A legal entity incorporated federally or in Ontario will receive a BN immediately upon articles of incorporation through the Companies Office and CRA integration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer; no pass-through treatment. Canadian-resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Ontario is taxable on Canadian-source business income and certain capital gains. Ontario branch profits of a foreign corporation are subject to federal and Ontario income tax at the corporate level plus an additional branch tax. No tax-exempt zone or special territorial regime applies to ordinary consultancies.

        
Available election forms
No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada. Targeted statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election for non-residents with Canadian rental income; (ii) s. 217 election regarding non-resident employment income; (iii) GST/HST election (Form RC4592) for closely related corporations to stream supplies and zero-rating; (iv) amalgamation and winding-up elections; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4 to 18.6) apply to deny deductions or include income in mismatch arrangements, but no generic classification election is available.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 — taxable supplies made worldwide by a non-resident over any four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this 'small supplier' threshold mandates GST/HST registration. Registration is also mandatory for a non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Canada unless the small supplier exception applies; voluntary registration is permitted for most non-residents and enables input tax credit recovery.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under Excise Tax Act s. 22 (goods) and s. 23 (services), a supply is zero-rated at 0% where: (i) for services — the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the service is acquired for use in the non-resident's commercial activity outside Canada; overseas market entry consultancy supplied to a non-resident client qualifies if the benefit is realized outside Canada and the invoice bears the supplier's BN; (ii) for goods — the goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export in accordance with s. 22 and the exporter retains proof of export. Adequate contemporaneous documentation must demonstrate the supply was made to a non-resident and was not consumed in Canada.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government e-invoicing clearance regime exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic records and invoices, provided that prescribed invoice information is shown (vendor BN, recipient, transaction date, tax amount, and rate). GST/HST registrants generally must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE if their revenues exceed the prescribed threshold (CAD 1 million or where the registrant is a prescribed class). Peppol e-invoicing adoption is voluntary; there is no CRA-mandated API clearance or pre-validation of invoices before issuance.

      
CRA may reassess within the normal 3-year limitation period, extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Where zero-rating is denied, CRA recharacterizes the supply as taxable in Canada and assesses the unpaid 13% HST together with refundable interest and arrears interest. Penalties include: (i) failure to file or remit penalty at 5% plus 1% per full month of default; (ii) false statements or omissions penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated net tax; (iii) gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the amount; (iv) criminal fraud charges for knowingly claiming export zero-rating without actual export or delivery of services outside Canada. CRA conducts rigorous export-documentation audits; a missing B13A, inconsistent payment remittance, or absence of non-resident proof will trigger full clawback and loss of input tax credits.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar is freely convertible and Canada maintains no general exchange-control legislation. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed, and neither the Currency Act nor the Bank of Canada Act imposes restrictions on current-account or capital-account convertibility. Profit remittance is therefore legally unrestricted subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and any applicable Canada-U.S. or Canada-foreign tax treaty relief; thin-capitalization limitations in subsection 18(4) of the Income Tax Act are relevant to shareholder advances, and Part XXVI.1 cross-border tax-reporting rules may require information filings for inbound capital inflows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; exporters may lawfully retain and use foreign currency proceeds in CAD-dollar or multi-currency accounts. Reporting obligations arise solely under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): a FINTRAC Exchange of Currency Report (ECER) is required for non-account cash foreign-exchange transactions above CAD 10,000, and a Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report is required for physical cross-border movements above CAD 10,000. For wire transfers, the standard Threshold/Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) and Large Virtual Currency Transaction Report (LVCTR) triggers apply at the CAD 10,000 level.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary and exchange-rate authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and currency transaction reporting oversight is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational visa-wire-style disbursements and supplier payments; Low-Medium for capital escrow, settlement of regulated transactions, or any mandate requiring access to Payments Canada clearing. Canada does not issue an EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licence; Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.), Payoneer, and comparable platforms operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Such entities are not clearing members of Lynx (the high-value CAD settlement system) and generally cannot provide true escrow, CDIC-insured deposit, overdraft, or trade-finance credit. The recommended architecture is a Big Six bank prime account supplemented by a Wise Business / FINTRAC-registered MSB rail for low-value cross-border payout efficiency in USD/EUR/GBP.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is non-resident, holds voting control through a foreign holding company, or appears on an adverse-media or PEP-screen match
        
Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is non-resident, holds voting control through a foreign holding company, or appears on an adverse-media or PEP-screen match

        
Defense evidence: Notarized corporate registry extracts for every entity in the ownership chain; certified shareholder register and share-transfer ledger; Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) or foreign tax-authority tax identification numbers; sworn source-of-wealth declaration supported by audited financial statements or brokerage statements; confirmation that the Ontario-incorporated business corporation is managed and controlled from arm's-length premises in Canada; and an organizational chart reconciled to the CDS/FinCEN-style beneficial ownership declaration.

      

      
      

        Expected transaction profile is inconsistent with the declared ISIC 7020 market-entry consulting activity (e.g., high-velocity wires, third-party payouts, or invoicing in high-risk jurisdictions)
        
Root cause: Expected transaction profile is inconsistent with the declared ISIC 7020 market-entry consulting activity (e.g., high-velocity wires, third-party payouts, or invoicing in high-risk jurisdictions)

        
Defense evidence: Signed client engagement agreements for cross-border market-entry feasibility studies and regulatory licensing agency services; fee schedule and invoice sample showing clear service milestones; list of counter-party jurisdictions with corresponding EIU or OECD risk ratings; projected annual turnover and treasury flow forecast; and a written compliance narrative confirming that no cash-intensive, crypto-adjacent, or gambling-related counterparties are contemplated.

      

      
      

        Capital injection wires arrive from unrelated or high-risk offshore accounts, or the stated source of funds is an unsupported shareholder loan
        
Root cause: Capital injection wires arrive from unrelated or high-risk offshore accounts, or the stated source of funds is an unsupported shareholder loan

        
Defense evidence: Executed subscription agreement and board resolution authorizing share issuance; certified letters from the remitting bank identifying the beneficial owner of the sending account; source-of-funds evidence for the injected capital (tax returns, liquidation statements of a divested business, real-estate sale certificates); signed confirmation of the intra-group loan terms (interest rate, repayment schedule) if the funds are characterized as shareholder debt; and a schedule matching the wire amount to the issued share consideration for stamping under the Ontario Business Corporations Act.

      

      
      

        Fintech or MSB payout rail triggers repeated supervision exceptions due to irregular beneficiary-name mismatches or sub-account aggregation
        
Root cause: Fintech or MSB payout rail triggers repeated supervision exceptions due to irregular beneficiary-name mismatches or sub-account aggregation

        
Defense evidence: Executed Wise/EMI Terms of Service and FINTRAC MSB registration certificate; operational procedure requiring full legal-name matching of beneficiaries on every MT103 and payout instruction; six-month transaction reconciliation showing consistent purpose codes under ISIC 7020; a documented escalation path with the MSB relationship manager for name-address verification failures; and a fallback wire instruction protocol designating RBC or TD as the settled-authorization bank.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity injections should be routed as an SWIFT MT103 bank wire from the investor's account directly to the Ontario corporation's dedicated Capital Subscription / Shareholder Contribution account held at a Schedule I Canadian bank; the narrative and purpose code must be explicit (e.g., 'B035 Capital / share subscription' or 'INVCAP-EQUITY') to prevent recharacterization as a trading receipt or a loan requiring Part XIII withholding. Prior to settlement, the corporate treasury must deliver the executed subscription agreement, board resolution approving the issuance, and a share-certificate undertaking to the receiving bank for its client-identification file; the remitting bank must provide an institution-to-institution verification that the sending account belongs to the disclosed shareholder. For amounts above CAD 100,000, the bank will typically require a source-of-funds letter and may apply enhanced due diligence given the non-resident ownership structure; inbound transfers are then reported by the bank under the PCMLTFA threshold system, not by the corporate client.Operating and trade disbursements should use the SWIFT MT103 customer credit-transfer rail for cross-border supplier, contractor, and licensing-fee payments, with Receiving Banks verified through their BIC codes and the beneficiary's IBAN or account number accurately matched. Documentary trade settlements (import of goods, project deliverables) should be structured through commercial letters of credit (documentary L/C and standby L/C) issued by the corporate client's Schedule I bank, with invoice, packing-list, and certificate-of-origin conforming documents presented to the paying bank. CAD-denominated domestic outflows settle over Lynx (the successor to LVTS) for immediate finality, while USD/EUR payables are cleared through correspondent banking relationships or the bank's internal cross-currency book-transfer facility. All outbound wires must be screened against the Canadian Economic Sanctions Regulations (Office of the Superintendent methodology), and the bank's FINTRAC reporting obligations apply for every wire of CAD 10,000 or more.Outright deliverable FX forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD) executed with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I or Schedule II foreign bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a 1995 CSA credit-support annex.Vanilla FX options (European-style calls and puts) on USD/CAD used as non-speculative cover for forecast disbursements denominated in foreign currency.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCS) and coupon swaps to align foreign-currency revenue with CAD operating expenses where the notional principal is grounded in a documented commercial contract.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for minor exotic pairs not freely tradable on the Canadian market, notwithstanding that CAD itself is fully convertible and CAD NDFs are not a standard hedging product.FX margin facilities and rolling spot contracts with a mark-to-market collateral arrangement that does not materially transfer financial risk away from the Canadian corporate borrower.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational visa-wire-style disbursements and supplier payments; Low-Medium for capital escrow, settlement of regulated transactions, or any mandate requiring access to Payments Canada clearing. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
25.0% combined effective general corporate rate (Federal 15.0% + Ontario 10.0%); net federal rate after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction equals 15%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% statutory dividend withholding tax under Part XIII (ITA s.212(2)) applied to all dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-residents. Canada-US treaty rate: 5% where the beneficial owner is a US company owning 10% or more of the voting shares (with 365-day holding requirement for lower LOB rates); 15% for portfolio holdings. Substantially similar 5%/15% structures appear in most comprehensive Canadian DTAs (e.g., Canada-UK).

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
T2 is due no later than six (6) months after the end of the taxation year (e.g., a December 31 year-end must be filed by June 30). Balance-due day is within two (2) months after the taxation year-end for most corporations, extended to three (3) months for a CCPC that is entitled to the small business deduction on its CANADIAN tax balance.

      

      
    

    

      
Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

      

        

          

            BoxField
            Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
          

          
            
            

              T2 Page 1 - Identification area
              Legal name, CRA Business Number (BN), registered office address, fiscal period commencement and end dates, and the 'type of corporation' (e.g., public, CCPC, non-resident owned).
              Non-resident corporations must quote the BN assigned for the Canadian business, identify the Canadian permanent establishment (PE) or agent, and complete the non-resident identification fields; a resident corporation filing with non-resident shareholders must still use its BT/BC BN and list the country of residence of each non-resident director/officer in the prescribed schedule.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
            

              Line 100 - Taxable Income (per Schedule 1, T2S1)
              Central taxable income figure: financial statement net income adjusted in Schedule 1 for permanent/temporary differences (e.g., non-deductible fines, meals/entertainment 50% limitation, capital cost allowance, reserves, and other discretionary amounts), then reduced by loss carryovers and deductions (e.g., net capital loss, non-capital loss carryforwards).
              A non-resident corporation computes taxable income only for the Canadian portion of its business carried on through a PE using the PE's separate-entity accounts (thin capitalization and Part XIII expense allocation rules applied); gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property are separately reported even if no PE exists.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
            

              Line 130 / Line 140 / Line 157 - Federal basic tax, provincial abatement and general rate reduction
              Federal basic tax is 38% of taxable income (line 130), reduced by a 10% provincial abatement (line 140) for income earned in a Canadian province and by the 13% general rate reduction (line 157) where income is not eligible for the small business deduction; net federal rate for general income equals 15%.
              Non-resident corporations claim the 10% abatement only to the extent the income is allocated to a Canadian province; if income is earned in a territory or is treaty-exempt, no abatement is available. The general rate reduction applies only to income not benefiting from the SBD.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
            

              Schedule 5, Line 500 - Ontario corporate tax computation
              Ontario income tax assessed at 10.0% on Ontario taxable income allocated to an Ontario PE, with a 3.2% rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income of a qualifying CCPC; SBD limitation and Ontario tax credits (e.g., Ontario Innovation Tax Credit) are computed in the provincial schedules.
              Non-resident corporations with an Ontario PE allocate income under Ontario Regulation 181/98 (or treaty PE attribution) and pay Ontario tax at the 10% general rate; they do not qualify for the Ontario small business deduction unless resident and controlled under the CCPC test.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
            

              Schedule 7 - Foreign tax credits / T2209 & T2036
              Foreign-source business and non-business income tax credits claimed federally and provincially to mitigate double taxation of the entity's cross-border consulting income; foreign taxes creditable are those paid to foreign governments on income included in taxable income.
              Non-resident corporations cannot claim foreign tax credits in the T2 for taxes paid to their country of residence; the T2 picks up only Canadian-source income, while foreign-source income of a non-resident is generally outside the Canadian return, subject to the deemed-residence and FAPI rules.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
            

              T106 Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents
              Mandatory information return filed by the corporation reporting reportable cross-border transactions with non-arm's length non-residents (e.g., management fees, consulting fees, cost-contribution arrangements, loans, and royalty payments) above the prescribed threshold.
              Both Canadian-resident and non-resident corporations carrying on a business in Canada must attach T106 when the aggregate annual value of transactions with a non-arm's length non-resident exceeds CAD 100,000; the T106 must identify the methodology applied and the terms of the intercompany agreements.
              Late T2 filing penalty: 5% of the balance of tax due plus 1% per complete month of delay, capped at 12 months (total 17%); for repeat late filers or deliberate negligence: 10% plus 2% per month, capped at 20 months (total 50%). Additionally, late or deficient instalments attract prescribed-rate compound daily interest (currently 8%-9% CAD); failure to file T106 draws penalties of CAD 100 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500) and the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) can attach when an adjustment exceeds the threshold. Criminal prosecution for willful failure to file (ITA s.238(1)) can add fines of CAD 1,000-CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction.
            

            
          
        

      

    

    

      
Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No fixed CAD-dollar documentation threshold; contemporaneous 'complete and accurate' transfer-pricing documentation is required by the time the T2 is filed (CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R). The 10% transfer-pricing penalty (ITA s.247(3)) is triggered when the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue for the year, and the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts with contemporaneous documentation (the 'due diligence defence'). T106 filing threshold: CAD 100,000 aggregate annual value of reportable transactions with each non-arm's length non-resident. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue not less than CAD 1.1 billion (EUR 750 million); CRA Master File/Local File guidance aligns with OECD BEPS Action 13.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in Gulf/offshore regimes; substance is nonetheless enforced through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) where transactions with negligible economic substance collapse for tax purposes; (ii) treaty-limitation-on-benefits (LOB) clauses (e.g., Canada-US Article XXIX A) requiring substantive business presence; (iii) the CFC/FAPI regime (s.91) re-attributing passive foreign income of controlled foreign affiliates; and (iv) CRA transfer-pricing secondary adjustments and 'sham' doctrines for shell arrangements. A market-entry consultancy entity must maintain actual office premises, operating personnel, board minutes, books and records, and intercompany documents consistent with the functions, assets and risks (FAR) attributed.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return (Income Tax Act s.239)
        
Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine of 50%-100% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment: fine of 100%-200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (ITA s.239); full reassessment of all open years, gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax (s.163(2)), and revocation of charitable/tax preferences if applicable.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who 'participated in, assented to or acquiesced in' the fraud are personally prosecutable and can be sentenced to imprisonment; CRA can issue director's liability assessments under s.227.1 for unremitted withholdings, and courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a sham or alter ego. Conviction triggers criminal records, restriction of travel (subject to bail/parole conditions), and civil restitution of the full tax shortfall plus interest.

      

      
      

        Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII / Source Deductions (Director's Liability)
        
Statutory sanction: The payer corporation is liable for the entire amount not withheld (ITA s.215), plus interest and penalties; late remittance penalties run from 3%-10% of the unremitted amount plus daily prescribed-rate interest; failure to comply with withholding obligations can support criminal charges under s.238/239.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 (and ETA s.323 for GST/HST) for all unremitted amounts, interest and penalties where the corporation fails to remit and the director failed to exercise reasonable care; CRA enforces through director's liability assessments, garnishment, asset seizure, and Federal Court judgments. Travel disruption is indirect: outstanding director assessments block CRA clearance certificates on departure and restrict cross-border asset transfers.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / T106 Failures (ITA s.247, s.162)
        
Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess on the arm's length basis, deny interest deductibility, apply the 10% transfer-pricing penalty on net adjustments exceeding the lesser of CAD 5 million and 10% of gross revenue, levy T106 late penalties (CAD 100/day up to CAD 2,500), and apply GAAR to annihilate tax benefits; interest compounds daily and can double the tax exposure within years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T106 declarations or approve a scheme with no economic substance can be assessed under the general director liability provisions for unremitted taxes and prosecuted for tax evasion; foreign-based directors remain personally exposed where the corporation is a 'naked shell' and the veil is pierced; CRA also notifies exchange of information (EOIR) to the residence country, exposing cross-border personal assets.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA) / Shell-Company Complicity
        
Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation (new 2022+ regime) plus criminal penalties for MSB without registration (imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines); from 2024, the enhanced beneficial ownership registry under the Canada Business Corporations Act subjects non-filing corporations to criminal fines up to CAD 200,000 for directors/officers.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly facilitate money laundering or operate an unlicensed MSB face personal criminal liability, extradition risk, seizure of personal assets, and are subject to travel bans and global asset freezes; corporate veil will be pierced where the entity functions as a mere instrumentality, exposing shareholders to restitution and the directors to insolvency-law disqualification and director's liability orders.
