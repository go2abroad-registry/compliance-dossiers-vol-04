Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or mail-forwarding-only address is not statutorily accepted as the sole registered seat of a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The registered office must be the actual administrative seat or a physical office identifiable in the canton of registration. A licensed domiciliation or corporate services provider can be used in most cantons, provided it maintains a real physical office at the registered address and the company can demonstrate adequate administrative substance. Highly vulnerable arrangements such as a letterbox office increase tax and commercial registry scrutiny and may be rejected by the registrar.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

An Aktiengesellschaft must maintain a minimum share capital of CHF 100,000. Before registration, at least 20% of the nominal capital, subject to a floor of CHF 50,000, must be fully paid up in cash and held in a blocked (escrow) account with a Swiss bank; contributions in kind require a licensed statutory auditor's report and specific valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety clearance certificate from the Zug Fire Safety Police (Feuerpolizei Zug) based on office occupancy load and evacuation routes; provide a structural load assessment of the rented premises; and complete the workplace inspection required under the Swiss Labor Act (ArG) and the SUVA occupational health and risk evaluation under Article 82 UVG, including first-aid provisions and evacuation instructions for employees.

Paid-in capital rule: An Aktiengesellschaft must maintain a minimum share capital of CHF 100,000. Before registration, at least 20% of the nominal capital, subject to a floor of CHF 50,000, must be fully paid up in cash and held in a blocked (escrow) account with a Swiss bank; contributions in kind require a licensed statutory auditor's report and specific valuation.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
WHEREAS the share capital of CHF 100,000 has been fully subscribed, unanimously RESOLVED THAT: (1) the subscription of 1,000 registered shares of CHF 100 nominal value is hereby approved and allocated to the incorporator; (2) the registered office of the company is adopted at Grabenstrasse 4, 6300 Zug, Switzerland; (3) [Name] is appointed Chairman of the Board of Directors and Chief Executive Officer, with sole signature authority; (4) the paid-up portion of CHF 50,000, exceeding the statutory minimum, is confirmed as available in the blocked escrow account; (5) the Board authorizes the submission of the incorporation declaration to the Zug Commercial Register and instructs the officers to register the company for VAT and social insurance.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial manufacturing operation is established; the Cantonal Office for Economy and Labour of Zug (Amt für Wirtschaft und Arbeit, AWA) retains general competence for permanent commercial occupation authorizations without requiring a special sectoral license for software services.

Category: No special operating business license (Sonderbewilligung) is mandated under Swiss federal or cantonal law for a pure payroll software and SaaS company; the commercial register entry, together with the municipal business notification, constitutes the lawful operation authorization.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/AFC/FTA) — www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax authorities via SwissTax (www.suisse-tax.ch) and the Federal Tax Administration's portal for VAT (www.imea.admin.ch)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Swiss AG must first obtain a Swiss Company Identification Number (UID) from the Federal Statistical Office's UID register (www.uid.ch) via the commercial register entry; the UID serves as the VAT number and employer identification. For VAT, the company must complete the electronic VAT registration form (Formulaire 764) on the ESTV's e-service portal (www.imea.admin.ch) or via the SwissTax portal, providing the UID, business purpose, and estimated turnover. The cantonal tax administration automatically issues the corporate income tax master data after commercial register registration. A foreign entity without a Swiss permanent establishment but engaging in taxable activities in Switzerland must register for VAT through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration using a foreign-entity UID application, if turnover exceeds CHF 100,000.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Swiss AG must first obtain a Swiss Company Identification Number (UID) from the Federal Statistical Office's UID register (www.uid.ch) via the commercial register entry; the UID serves as the VAT number and employer identification. For VAT, the company must complete the electronic VAT registration form (Formulaire 764) on the ESTV's e-service portal (www.imea.admin.ch) or via the SwissTax portal, providing the UID, business purpose, and estimated turnover. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists under Swiss tax law; Swiss AGs are per se corporations subject to corporate income tax at the federal, cantonal, and communal levels. However, VAT grouping (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) election is available for closely linked Swiss entities under Article 21 of the Swiss VAT Act. For export zero-rating, an entity may elect to apply for the simplified VAT lump-sum tax deduction method. No hybrid mismatch elections are available; Switzerland applies mandatory hybrid mismatch rules consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide taxable turnover from taxable supplies in Switzerland, excluding exempt supplies; for non-resident entities, a lower threshold of CHF 100,000 applies to Swiss-source supplies; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: zero-rated when goods are exported to a foreign country or to a bonded warehouse/tax-free zone, provided the exporter or the recipient transfers the goods out of Switzerland; proof of export must be substantiated. Services: zero-rated for services directly related to export goods (including transport, logistics, insurance, commissioning) and for services to recipients abroad if the place of supply is outside Switzerland; Swiss payroll SaaS services to non-resident clients are generally zero-rated as intangible services if the recipient's place of business is abroad (Article 8(2) VAT Act), subject to the recipient being an entrepreneur and the service not being used in Switzerland. A written confirmation from the foreign recipient is required for the application of the reverse-charge/zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / déclaration en douane d'exportation) — e-dec export or NCTS
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR (international transport document)
  • Commercial invoice with export reference and Swiss seller VAT number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of consideration from abroad (recommended for audit substantiation)

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing system exists for domestic VAT. Switzerland permits electronic invoices (e.g., Swiss QR-bill, EDIFACT, XML) subject to the general invoicing requirements under Article 26 of the Swiss VAT Act (must include: seller name, UID/VAT number, recipient, date, supply description, consideration, applicable tax rate, and tax amount). Since 2024, the Swiss QR-Invoice is mandatory for QR-bill payments, but not for all invoices. There is no government-run clearance/API stamping; taxpayers retain invoices for 10 years and must submit periodic VAT returns electronically through the ESTV's e-service portal or approved third-party software using the Swiss e-dec (SecureWin) transport mechanism.

If the taxpayer fails to prove the export or the recipient's foreign status within 60 days of a request during an audit, the claimed zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back with interest. Fraudulent VAT evasion (including deliberate misapplication of zero-rating) is a criminal offense under Article 61 of the Swiss VAT Act, punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and/or a fine up to CHF 30,000 (tax penalty up to 3 times the tax evaded). Negligent tax evasion carries penalties up to 3 times the tax evaded; in addition, late-payment interest accrues at 3-4% annually. The Federal Tax Administration can also impose a procedural penalty of up to CHF 10,000 for failure to maintain adequate export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Swiss Franc (CHF) is freely tradable with no exchange controls, no balance-of-payment restrictions, and no requirement for central bank approval on cross-border remittances. Profit repatriation, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable, subject only to withholding tax (35% dividends) which may be reduced under applicable tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no legal duty to surrender or repatriate export proceeds. There is no statutory conversion timeline or minimum conversion ratio. Export receipts may be held in any convertible currency and employed freely for liquidity, hedging, or reinvestment purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore non-Swiss EMIs cannot passport into Switzerland under an EU/EEA licence. A FINMA-licensed payment institution or a Swiss banking partner is mandatory. Digital EMIs may provide a PI-layer but full treasury autonomy requires active partnership with a Swiss-regulated institution; foreign EMIs face significant restrictions in opening sponsor accounts and cannot offer payroll clearing without local regulatory cover.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax statutory rate 8.5%, levied on profit after federal tax (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal, and communal taxation in Zug is approximately 11.5% to 16% depending on profit level and local multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions; no further cantonal dividend withholding tax; reduced rates apply only by treaty or statutory restitution procedures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year tax period ending 31 December, the return is due no later than 30 September of the following year in most cantons; for a non-calendar fiscal year, filing is generally due within six months after the balance-sheet date; extension may be granted upon written application before the applicable deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss federal law does not prescribe a statutory monetary threshold for Local File or Master File documentation. In practice, the Swiss tax administration expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation when intercompany transactions are material, including intragroup royalties, management fees, software licensing, and financing. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated turnover of at least CHF 900 million. A Swiss AG with smaller revenue must still demonstrate arm's-length behavior through a local file, benchmarking study, or functional analysis whenever transactions with foreign related parties exceed ordinary commercial scale.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no separate Economic Substance Regulation in the form used by some low-tax jurisdictions, but Swiss tax law, jurisprudence, and treaty-abuse doctrines require real economic substance for corporate tax residence, treaty eligibility, and preferential tax regimes. A Swiss AG must have a valid registered office, actual decision-making management in Switzerland, substantive board meetings, employees performing payroll and software development functions, books and records kept in Switzerland, and business purpose. The patent box, notional interest deduction, and participation exemption can be denied if the company lacks local substance or is used as a letterbox.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false corporate income tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of suppressed profit, late-payment interest, and administrative tax penalty in an amount generally equal to or exceeding the tax evaded; under the Swiss Federal Direct Tax Act, wilful tax evasion may be prosecuted as tax fraud and is punishable by a monetary criminal penalty and imprisonment in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns or caused the company to evade taxes face direct civil liability under Article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, personal orders for restitution of evaded taxes, travel bans, disqualification from board service, and custodial sanctions where tax fraud is proven.
Transfer pricing manipulation and sham intercompany arrangements

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment by the tax authority, disallowance of excessive deductions, additional tax assessment, penalty interest, procedural fine up to CHF 10,000 for missing documentation, and potential criminal tax penalties in cases involving fabricated intercompany agreements.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute backdated or fictitious agreements face personal liability for resulting tax losses, exclusion from business travel and board mandates during criminal proceedings, confiscation of bonuses connected to the arrangement, and imprisonment for involvement in tax fraud.
Violation of Swiss withholding tax obligations on dividend and interest repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may issue a formal demand against the company for 35% of the gross distribution plus interest, impose penalty for non-withholding or late remittance, and refuse deduction of penalties; deliberate non-declaration is treated as criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Board members and executives are jointly and severally liable with the company for unpaid withholding tax if they knowingly instructed or tolerated non-compliance, and may be subject to personal restitution orders, asset freezing, bank travel restrictions, and criminal imprisonment in serious cases.
Shell company abuse and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Commercial Register and tax authorities may disregard the shell acquisition, retroactively reclassify the transaction as a liquidation and re-establishment, deny treaty benefits, impose capital-formation obligations, levy fines for false declarations, and in severe cases order ex officio dissolution of the company.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the de facto beneficial owner who used the shell as their personal instrument face veil piercing, personal civil restitution of hidden assets, travel bans, revocation of commercial signatory rights, and imprisonment for criminal falsification of tax and commercial register documents.
AML and payment-rail compliance violations in payroll automation

Statutory sanction: Enforcement action by FINMA, referral to the Swiss Money Laundering Reporting Office, confiscation of illegal assets, administrative fines, criminal fines up to CHF 500,000 under Articles 305bis and 305ter of the Swiss Criminal Code, and prohibition from engaging in financial-market activity.

Director personal liability: Members of the board and management who consciously enable illicit payment flows are personally liable for breaches of Swiss criminal law, may be ordered to pay civil restitution to the company or clients, face travel bans and custodial sentences up to three years or more for qualified money laundering, and may be banned from any future management role in financial or payment businesses.
