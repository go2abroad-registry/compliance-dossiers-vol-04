Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), operating under the cantonal Department of Economic Affairs; final entries are published in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SHAB/SOGC).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR / Code des obligations, CO; SR/R.S. 220), Articles 620–763 concerning the Aktiengesellschaft (stock corporation); supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV; SR 221.411), Article 936 OR on registration, and the Cantonal Notarization Act for the public deed.

Formation / constitutional filings

Public Deed of Incorporation (Gründungsurkunde) executed before a certified Swiss notary, accompanied by the Registration Application to the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung), the escrow capital payment confirmation issued by a Swiss bank, and costs/registration fee statement.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 39 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000. At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up, being a minimum aggregate of CHF 50,000. The paid-in amount must be deposited and irrevocably blocked in an escrow account at a Swiss bank, evidenced by a bank certificate, unless alternative security is provided; capital in kind is permitted provided such contribution is fully disclosed, valued, and audited.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required from Feuerwehr Zug under the building permit, including smoke detection, evacuation route, and extinguishing equipment; structural load inspection of the registered office premises by the Municipal Building Inspectorate of Zug; occupational safety clearance obtained through the SUVA workplace inspection and annual risk assessment in accordance with Art. 82 of the Swiss Accident Insurance Act (UVG) and the Labour Act (ArG).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), official portal www.estv.admin.ch; UID business identifier registry www.uid.ch; Swiss commercial register (Zefix) for company identification; for corporate income tax, the cantonal tax administration of the registered office (e.g., Zug, Zurich) remains the competent collection body, while ESTV administers federal income tax, VAT, withholding tax and stamp duties.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Assess VAT registration duty under Art. 10-14 MWSTG; a foreign entity without a Swiss seat is taxable from the first supply of goods or services performed in or into Switzerland, with no CHF 100,000 threshold exemption; voluntary registration is possible for a foreign business that purchases goods in Switzerland or has demonstrable taxable activity. STEP 2: Where the foreign entity has no Swiss permanent establishment, appointment of a Swiss tax representative or fiscal agent domiciled in Switzerland is mandatory (Art. 67(2) MWSTG); execute a written power of attorney and file it with the ESTV. STEP 3: File the electronic VAT registration application through the ESTV e-service portal (form 23 / registration for VAT), attaching: home-country commercial register extract or certificate of incorporation, Articles of Association / memorandum, passport or ID of legal representatives, board resolution authorizing the Swiss representative, and a brief description of the B2B marketplace business model and target counter-parties. STEP 4: ESTV verifies the file and issues the Swiss business identifier UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, e.g., CHE-123.456.789) with the suffix MWST, which simultaneously constitutes the VAT registration number; processing typically takes 5 to 20 working days. STEP 5: If the entity later creates a branch or permanent establishment in Switzerland, register the Swiss AG (Aktiengesellschaft) in the commercial register of the canton of domicile through a notary, obtain the UID via Zefix, and notify the cantonal tax administration for direct taxes; the VAT registration remains administered by the ESTV.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a check-the-box entity classification; a Swiss AG is mandatorily taxed as an independent corporate entity. Available statutory elections relevant to the B2B marketplace: (1) VAT group election (Art. 21 MWSTG) allowing closely linked entities to be one VAT taxpayer with a single UID; (2) cash-basis VAT accounting method (Istmethode, taxation on received consideration) electable for turnover up to CHF 5 million; otherwise the accrual method (Sollmethode) applies mandatorily; (3) voluntary VAT registration for businesses below the CHF 100,000 threshold; (4) small-business VAT exemption election (Art. 11 MWSTG) with forfeiture of input VAT recovery; (5) customs simplifications (local clearance, AEO, e-dec export authorization) which are procedural elections; (6) tax incentive elections at cantonal level: patent box without separate expiry, NID, cantonal holding privilege status (for qualifying investment holdings). Hybrid mismatch: Switzerland ratified the OECD MLI on 26 August 2019 (in force 1 December 2019; effect 1 January 2020); no separate domestic hybrid or dual-consolidation election exists, but the MLI Principal Purpose Test and treaty override provisions neutralize hybrid arrangements; Swiss domestic law does not permit a partnership-style pass-through election for an AG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of worldwide annual taxable turnover calculated on all supplies (excluding exempt supplies per Art. 10(2)(a) and Art. 21 MWSTG). A foreign supplier without seat in Switzerland is not entitled to the threshold and must register from the first taxable supply located in Switzerland, including distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to non-business recipients; a foreign marketplace operator facilitating such supplies is deemed to be the supplier from 1 January 2025 under Art. 20a MWSTG and must register with the ESTV regardless of the freedom-of-trade threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods by a Swiss-registered business is zero-rated under Art. 23(1) MWSTG where the goods are physically transported abroad as part of a taxable supply and the export is evidenced by customs documentation; the 0% rate also covers direct costs ancillary to the export (transport, loading, commission for the export transaction). Exported B2B marketplace platform services (digital intermediation, listing, commission charges) provided to recipients whose economic seat is abroad fall outside the Swiss place-of-supply under Art. 18(1)(a) MWSTG and are outside the scope of Swiss VAT rather than technically zero-rated; no VAT is chargeable on such invoices and no Swiss input VAT deduction is associated with them. For physical goods sold by the Swiss AG through its B2B platform to foreign buyers, the zero-rating condition requires that title to the goods transfers abroad and that the Swiss exporter keeps verifiable proof of departure, membership of the customs-export documentation, and bank evidence of payment received from the non-resident counter-party; Swiss VAT law prescribes no specific statutory deadline for the receipt of foreign currency, but after the 180-day benchmark without inward remittance the ESTV will treat the export proof as incomplete unless documented exceptional terms exist.

  • Customs export declaration filed in the Swiss customs IT systems (e-dec, Passar / CRC / NCTS) with the printed export acknowledgment and, where applicable, the customs seal of the border office (Ausfuhrabfertigung)
  • Transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Swiss territory: ocean Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR international road consignment note, or CIM rail consignment note
  • Commercial export invoice including Incoterms, customs value, quantity, full goods description, HS codes, and exporter UID-MWST number
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit confirmation (SWIFT MT103 or account statement) naming the Swiss export entity as beneficiary for the invoiced value
  • Customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbescheinigung) issued by Swiss customs and, if applicable, EUR.1 / ATR movement certificates for preferential trade arrangements
  • Packing list and freight booking evidence (recommended supplementary documents to reconcile quantities and support the customs declaration)
  • Marketplace order records and platform-generated contract documentation establishing the B2B relationship, buyer identity, and payment settlement chain

Switzerland does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; there is no government-authorization API for invoice validation. Invoices must contain the statutory minimum content of Art. 26 MWSTG: full supplier name and address, UID plus MWST suffix, recipient name/address for supplies above CHF 400 or on request, date or period of supply, type, quantity and designation of the goods/services, consideration excluding tax, and applicable VAT rate; QR-invoices (Swiss QR-Code with QR-IBAN) are mandatory from 30 June 2020 for payment slips and serve as the standardized e-invoice payment trigger. For B2B electronic invoicing, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 is the recommended national exchange standard; federal administration procurement (B2G) is migrating to mandatory Peppol-based e-invoicing; ESTV may request electronic delivery of VAT returns via its e-service (SwissTax/Inward e-services). No real-time clearance requirement applies to the marketplace platform, but from 1 January 2025 platforms deemed suppliers (Art. 20a MWSTG) must submit periodic electronic reporting and maintain transactional records usable by the ESTV upon demand.

The ESTV scrutinizes export zero-rating claims by cross-matching the customs export declaration, transport document, invoice sequence, platform contract data and banking remittances; if any mandatory document is missing, invalid, or inconsistent, the supply is re-characterized as a domestic taxable supply and Swiss VAT of 8.1% (or the applicable reduced rate) is retroactively assessed on the full invoice amount, together with default interest at the statutory rate of 4.5% per annum (2024). Tax evasion under Art. 58 MWSTG attracts a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; qualified VAT fraud under Art. 59 MWSTG is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and/or a fine, and the prison penalty can be combined. The statutory limitation periods for retrospective assessment are 5 years for ordinary cases and 10 years for fraud or willful evasion; clawback applies to all supplies for which the exporter cannot produce the required export proof within the assessment period. In addition, the taxpayer must retain all books, vouchers, customs declarations, B/L, AWB/CMR and payment certificates for 10 years (Art. 42 MWSTG); a refund of input VAT claimed in connection with an invalid export is treated as a false credit and may be recovered with interest, plus penal sanctions. Estonian-style or non-substantive export window dressing, round-tripping of goods via bonded warehouses, and delayed or missing FX repatriation beyond the 180-day benchmark without documented commercial justification are red flags that trigger extended audit depth and possible criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Switzerland imposes no exchange controls, no limitations on profit repatriation, and no prior central-bank approval for cross-border wire transfers, foreign-currency accounts, or investments by non-residents. Dividends and liquidation proceeds of an AG are freely remittable to foreign shareholders, subject to a 35% federal withholding tax, reducible under the relevant double-taxation treaty (DTT), including the Swiss–China DTT (2013) for Chinese shareholders. No restrictions apply to the holding of CNY, USD, or EUR accounts by Swiss-resident treasury entities. No SNB reporting is required except statistical balance-of-payments filings for external assets/liabilities above CHF 10M.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule exists under Swiss law. Exporters and cross-border platform operators may retain the full amount of export proceeds in any currency indefinitely, with no mandatory conversion timeline or ratio. Receipts from B2B platform sales may be kept separately in foreign currency for hedging or reinvestment purposes without any requirement to repatriate or convert into CHF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank, together with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking conduct and money-laundering oversight. Exchange control policy is anchored in the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) and the Federal Act on Economic Measures (EMG), which codifies Switzerland's long-standing liberal foreign-exchange regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low feasibility. A FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech company under Art. 1b Banking Act or a licensed payment institution may offer corporate e-money accounts and access to the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system; however, offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK, Lithuania, Singapore EMI licensees) without a FINMA branch or Swiss partnership cannot hold CHF IBANs, cannot obtain direct SIC membership, and are prohibited from performing payment services for Swiss-resident entities under Art. 4 of the Banking Act. For Alibaba Pay settlement receipts, a Swiss-licensed EMI with a bank partner may receive CNY/USD flows, but legal certainty and prudential safeguarding are materially stronger with a Tier-1 bank relationship. A direct FINMA banking license is not required for the B2B marketplace itself because ordinary wholesale platform operation is not a regulated financial activity, but any treasury function holding client funds would trigger either a banking license or fintech-license perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection should be routed via SWIFT MT103 (single-customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV for interbank cover payments where the beneficiary is a Swiss bank. For CHF funding the wire should settle over SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) in real time; for foreign currency (USD/EUR/CNY), the credit must be processed via the correspondent bank of the Swiss clearing institution (e.g., UBS New York, ZKB Frankfurt, or Bank of China Zurich for CNY). The transaction narrative must reference the Commercial Register filing number and notarial deed; the SNB payment-statistics purpose code 'S62' (capital transfers) is required for equity injections, while 'S30' (foreign trade in goods) is required for operational trade remittances. Verification procedures: funds must be booked to a dedicated capital reserve account, the CHF 100M threshold triggering stamp duty must be checked, and the SNB external-assets reporting must be filed monthly when the receivable from non-residents exceeds CHF 10M.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier wires, using the beneficiary's IBAN and BIC with purpose code 'S30' for goods and 'S31' for services; (ii) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700/MT707 for large consignments from Chinese manufacturers; (iii) SIC real-time gross settlement for domestic CHF disbursements to Swiss warehouses or fulfilment providers; (iv) SEPA Instant/SEPA Credit Transfer for suppliers within the EU/EEA; (v) Alibaba Pay Trade Assurance settlement with funds released through Alipay Global's escrow chain to the Chinese supplier's CNY or USD account. For USD disbursements, CHIPS or Fedwire via the bank's U.S. correspondent is the standard. Liquidity should be structured as: a master operational account (CHF), a foreign-currency sub-ledger (EUR/USD/CNY), and a segregated escrow ledger for marketplace pass-through funds to avoid commingling risk in the event of platform insolvency.Outright FX forwards (deliverable) on CHF/USD, CHF/EUR, and CHF/CNH with tenors up to 12 months, executed under a standard ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or Swiss Master Agreement for OTC derivatives with the clearing bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the CNH/USD and onshore CNY/USD rates, offered by FINMA-licensed counterparties in Zurich and Geneva, requiring provisional margin collateral under an ISDA CSACross-currency swaps (CCS) between CHF and USD or CNY for both principal and coupon exchange, settled via continuous linked settlement (CLS) to mitigate settlement risk, counterparty limited to Tier-1 banksEuropean-style FX options (EUR/CHF, CNY/CHF) with upfront premium in CHF, permissible for hedged trade flows but not for speculative positions under corporate mandate rulesFX swaps (spot vs. forward) for short-term liquidity re-profiling where the marketplace receives USD and must fund EUR supplier obligations without touching the CHF treasury base, cleared via SIX x-clear or bilaterally with CLS settlement

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-Low feasibility. A FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech company under Art. 1b Banking Act or a licensed payment institution may offer corporate e-money accounts and access to the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system; however, offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK, Lithuania, Singapore EMI licensees) without a FINMA branch or Swiss partnership cannot hold CHF IBANs, cannot obtain direct SIC membership, and are prohibited from performing payment services for Swiss-resident entities under Art. 4 of the Banking Act. For Alibaba Pay settlement receipts, a Swiss-licensed EMI with a bank partner may receive CNY/USD flows, but legal certainty and prudential safeguarding are materially stronger with a Tier-1 bank relationship. A direct FINMA banking license is not required for the B2B marketplace itself because ordinary wholesale platform operation is not a regulated financial activity, but any treasury function holding client funds would trigger either a banking license or fintech-license perimeter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed CHF 1 million (goods) or CHF 500,000 (services) per year; Master File required if group turnover exceeds CHF 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have specific ESR, but requires economic substance for tax residency and to avoid treaty abuse; companies must have actual substance (office, staff, decision-making) to benefit from tax treaties and participation exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 3 times the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participate in evasion; travel bans and imprisonment up to 3 years.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of taxable income, penalties up to 100% of tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties and criminal prosecution.
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CHF 500,000, revocation of license, criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML breaches, including imprisonment up to 5 years.
