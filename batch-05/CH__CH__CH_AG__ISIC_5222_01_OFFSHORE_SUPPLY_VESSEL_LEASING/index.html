Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an Aktiengesellschaft (AG), the statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 must be paid up and deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank; the bank's escrow attestation must be presented to the notary and the Commercial Register Office. Release of escrowed funds is only permitted after proof of registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for shore offices, fuel storage and quay facilities by the Cantonal Fire Inspectorate; structural load inspection of quays and cargo handling equipment by the Cantonal Building Authority; occupational health and safety clearance for marine crews and operations under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and the Maritime Labour Convention.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (Art. 10 Swiss Value Added Tax Act), including taxable supplies of goods and services; foreign entities only liable when that turnover is attributable to Switzerland, and must obtain a Swiss VAT number and appoint a fiscal representative if non-resident.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the physical export of goods evidenced by Swiss customs export attestation (Art. 18 VAT Act) and to services directly ancillary to such export. An offshore supply-vessel charter may be zero-rated where the vessel is delivered outside Switzerland, used exclusively outside Swiss customs territory, or the lessee is established abroad and the operational use occurs beyond the Swiss continental shelf; place-of-supply rules and supporting documentation must conclusively demonstrate foreign use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / e-dec certified Customs Decision)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill / Charter Party
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of foreign payment settlement)
  • Commercial invoice and lease agreement
  • Vessel AIS/geolocation or voyage statements proving exclusive non-Swiss use

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API validation is in force in Switzerland. Commercial invoices may be issued in paper or PDF; electronic invoicing is mandatory only for supplies to the federal administration (XML/eBill standards). Export proof and complete books and records must be retained.

ESTV audits exports with documentary substantiation; failure to justify zero-rating within the retention/export evidence period results in retrospective VAT assessment at 8.1% plus default interest. Knowingly false attestation of export or foreign use constitutes VAT evasion, triggering administrative fines of up to three times the tax evaded and criminal imprisonment under Art. 96/97 Swiss VAT Act; records must be retained ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the OECD Code of Liberalisation. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely floating and freely convertible; no prior regulatory approval is required for foreign investment, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or profit repatriation. Dividend distributions by a Swiss AG are subject to a 35% Swiss withholding tax, which may be reduced to zero or a lower treaty rate if the beneficial owner provides proof of entitlement and the proper relief request is filed. Balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the SNB applies to banks and, at the Group level, to direct investment positions and cross-border financial assets/liabilities exceeding regulatory thresholds, but these are reporting obligations rather than convertibility controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters are free to maintain, invest, and repatriate proceeds in any convertible currency. There is no statutory time limit for repatriation; however, all cross-border receipts and payments must be routed through a licensed financial intermediary and are subject to Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act verification. For the offshore vessel leasing entity, transfer pricing documentation and contractual invoice terms must satisfy OECD arm's-length principles to avoid tax adjustments.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) as central bank; foreign exchange regulation and financial market policy administered jointly by the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). Switzerland has no separate exchange-control authority and imposes no general restrictions on cross-border capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: Low-Medium. Swiss-licensed EMIs and digital banks (fintech institutions under FINMA's FinTech Ordinance) and licensed payment institutions under FinIA can provide multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT access, and basic payment accounts. Legal constraints, however, are significant: (a) only a FINMA-licensed securities firm or bank may issue the escrow confirmation required by the Commercial Register for share capital deposits; (b) many EMIs explicitly exclude offshore marine clients as enhanced-risk and will not provide the capital-formation confirmation; (c) EEA/UK EMIs without a Swiss fintech license may not operate a Swiss-resident corporate payment account on a cross-border basis without FINMA authorization or a valid passporting arrangement; they may only offer ancillary FX services. Consequently, the principal operating treasury account should be established with a tier-1 Swiss bank; EMIs may be used as secondary FX conversion rails or for low-value disbursements only where they hold a FINMA license and accept the client risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the initial share-capital injection: (1) the AG's shareholders execute a notarized share subscription agreement in Switzerland; (2) each subscribing shareholder transfers funds from his/her own bank account to the 'Capital Escrow Account' (Sperrkonto) at the Swiss bank using SWIFT MT103 with the payment reference matching the subscription agreement and with a clear remittance purpose such as 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'CAPITAL INJECTION'; (3) the Swiss bank automatically tests the transaction under the Commercial Register framework and issues a legally valid escrow confirmation to the notary, attesting that the entire minimum capital (CHF 100,000 for an AG) is available and freely disposable by the signatories; (4) after the notarial deed is filed and the Commercial Register registers the AG, the account is unblocked and becomes the operational treasury account; (5) where capital is injected in foreign currency, conversion into CHF is recorded at the bank's spot rate and the origin of funds must be evidenced; (6) no payments from third parties except the registered subscriber are permitted, and the bank will reject misreference wires as 'unconfirmed capital'. For larger injections above the minimum capitalisation, the bank may request audited financial forecasts and evidence of the overall transaction structure.Outbound treasury settlements are executed through multiple rails: (a) Domestic CHF payments use the SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) system; (b) Euro-denominated trade and charter payments are cleared via SEPA and TARGET2-SECB (or EuroSIC) using MT103/MT202; (c) USD, GBP, NOK, and other freely convertible cross-border flows use SWIFT MT103/MT202 with standard clearing through the receiving bank's correspondent network; (d) commercial and standby letter-of-credit operations are issued as SWIFT MT700/MT760 under ICC UCP 600 and URDG 758 rules to secure shipyard instalments and charterer performance; (e) maritime-specific disbursements such as port dues, bunkering, crew wages, and vessel stores may be sent to designated agents through dedicated 'maritime clearing templates' or through an escrow-service agent using a dual-control payment instruction format; (f) the underlying outbound payment must be supported by the relevant charter contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration if physical goods move, and the SWIFT payment reference must align with BOP statistical categories (e.g., 'TRADE PAYMENT', 'SHIPPING FREIGHT', 'VESSEL OPERATIONS', 'CHARTER HIRE'). For all disbursements, the bank performs transaction monitoring and may block or suspend payments where the commercial documentation is incomplete or where the beneficial recipient does not match the trade party.Deliverable FX outright forwards between CHF, EUR, USD, GBP, and other freely convertible currencies, tenors up to 12 months, documented under an ISDA/AMF master agreement or executed on a covered spot/forward basis with margin calls.FX swaps and rolling forward contracts used for intra-company treasury repositioning, intercompany loan servicing, and precise management of charter-hire receipts against operating disbursements.European-style currency options (call/put) with premium paid upfront, suitable for hedging forecasted charter income and lease payments in volatile but convertible currencies such as NOK, SGD, or ZAR; bank credit limits apply for maturities beyond 24 months.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) converting floating-rate USD lease receivables into fixed CHF or EUR rate exposures, matched to the underlying charter or leasing obligations.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) exclusively for currencies without a deliverable offshore market (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) to hedge contracted lease cash flows that cannot be settled onshore, subject to Swiss margin rules and FINMA conduct-of-business requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined effective corporate income tax rate of approximately 11.6% to 11.9% in Canton Zug for an Aktiengesellschaft (federal direct tax at 8.5% of net after-tax profit, equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit, plus Zug cantonal and communal income tax of roughly 3.8% to 4.1%). The national statutory headline rate at the federal level only is 8.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on any dividend, constructive dividend, or value-shifting distribution made by the AG to its shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by 31 March (paper) or 30 April (electronic filing) of the following year; an extension to 30 September is routinely granted upon request, and the statutory maximum deadline ends six months after the business year close pursuant to Art. 124-125 DBG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Swiss Local File is mandatory when the Swiss AG has annual Swiss turnover above CHF 80 million or controlled intra-group transactions exceeding CHF 5 million per transaction category; a consolidated Master File is expected when the multinational group exceeds CHF 1 billion consolidated gross revenue. Even below these thresholds, ESTV practice requires a contemporaneous arm's-length benchmark for all major intercompany vessel charters, financing, and service arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no statutory Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE ESR regime, but economic substance is enforced through: (1) the general anti-avoidance doctrine (Steuerumgehung) under Art. 210 DBG and settled Federal Supreme Court case law; (2) the sub-stance-over-form principle governing hidden equity and financing recharges; (3) the ESTV's transfer pricing and thin-capitalization safe-haven rules (debt-to-equity ratios for asset leasing, generally 6:1 to 8:1 for financed vessels with a requirement of arm's-length interest); and (4) treaty beneficial-ownership and Principal Purpose Test analysis. The offshore supply-vessel leasing AG must demonstrate genuine decision-making in Switzerland: board meetings actually held, charter contracts negotiated and signed in Switzerland, bank accounts and invoicing operated from Zug, the vessel registered in or managed through a substantive Swiss operation, and crew/insurance functions documented. A letterbox AG with all commercial decisions made overseas will lose treaty benefits, participation deduction, and may face deemed distributions of revenue to the true offshore manager.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) and Criminal Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment (Nachsteuer), default interest at 3.5% to 4% per annum, and an evasion penalty of 50% to 250% of the evaded federal tax under Art. 175-177 DBG, mirrored by cantonal penalties; criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG carries custodial sentencing for the individuals involved and limitation periods of 10 years (15 years for falsified books).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers responsible for the false filings are jointly liable for the corporate tax debt under Art. 12 DBG; the tax authority will issue a personal liability order (Haftungsverfügung), may assert civil restitution, attach personal bank accounts and real estate, impose travel bans during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment of up to three years per individual defendant.
Dividend Withholding Tax Evasion and Abuse of DTA Refund

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full 35% withholding tax from the AG, default interest, and administrative fines under the Swiss Withholding Tax Act (VStG); denial of all treaty relief for abusive arrangements and reclassification of the payment as a taxable constructive dividend at the full 35%; the transaction is 'versteuert' as if no treaty applied, plus procedural penalties and criminal monetary sanctions up to CHF 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the dividend resolution or authorize the non-withheld distribution are personally liable for the withheld amount and may be sued by the tax authority; criminal conviction for wilful non-collection carries imprisonment up to three years, and directors face confiscation of personal assets located in Switzerland and a prohibition from serving on Swiss boards.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length profit, secondary adjustment classifying the excess as constructive dividend subject to 35% WHT, additional interest, and evasion penalties of up to 250% of the reassessed tax for intentional mispricing; incomplete documentation also triggers an official estimated assessment shifting the burden of proof to the taxpayer, alongside procedural fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length pricing are jointly liable under Art. 12 DBG for the tax underpayment; the same facts may qualify as criminal tax fraud, leading to personal restitution claims, asset attachment, travel bans, and disqualification as bankruptcy trustees when the AG is rendered insolvent by the TP adjustment.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance (Letterbox AG)

Statutory sanction: Application of the Steuerumgehung (abuse) doctrine and refusal to respect the Swiss AG's separate legal status; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, denial of the participation deduction, re-attribution of the full chartered income to the effective foreign beneficiary, and deemed distribution subject to 35% WHT; worse, the commercial register may order dissolution of the letterbox entity and retrospective tax assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors consenting to the shell arrangement face joint and several liability for all reclassified corporate taxes, personal fines for aiding tax violations, criminal prosecution for participation in a sham structure, and both civil and criminal seizure of personal property; the corporate veil is pierced, so the sole foreign shareholder is also directly liable.
Money Laundering and Financing of Prohibited Operations (AML)

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Art. 305bis Swiss Penal Code: imprisonment up to three years and fines up to CHF 250,000; confiscation of the vessel and all traced proceeds (Art. 70 CP); administrative enforcement by the Money Laundering Reporting Office (MROS) and FINMA where applicable, including freezing orders on Swiss bank and escrow accounts; and an automatic 35% WHT re-characterization of suspicious distributions if round-tripping comes to light.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecutable for money laundering where they knew or should have known of the illicit origins; penalties include custodial sentences, fines computed on the laundered value, asset confiscation, travel bans issued by the prosecutorial authorities, and civil compensation obligations; under the Swiss Code of Obligations, directors are also liable for the AG's consequent losses through breach of AML duties.
