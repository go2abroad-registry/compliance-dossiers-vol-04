Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal income tax, GST/HST policy, and customs/export administration; Revenu Québec (Revenue Quebec) as the sole administrator of GST/HST and Quebec Sales Tax (QST / TVQ, Taxe de vente du Québec) for registrants with an establishment in Quebec, pursuant to the 2022 CRA-Revenu Québec administrative delegation. Registration infrastructure portals: CRA 'My Business Account' portal and Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' (CLIC) portal, interconnected through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (CIDRE/ONES) for business registry data.

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) constituted or extra-provincially registered in Quebec: (1) file the declaration de société en commandite or extra-provincial registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and obtain the Numéro d'entreprise du Québec (NEQ); (2) obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA's 'Register a Business' service, indicating the partnership's legal nature and program accounts (GST/HST account 'RT', payroll 'RP', income tax 'RC', and import/export 'RM'); (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec using the QST account linked to the NEQ; for a Quebec-based business the GST/HST registration is completed simultaneously through Revenu Québec's integrated business registration process; (4) where the LP is managed from outside Canada or has non-resident partners, file CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' under the non-resident business stream and appoint a Canadian representative for service; (5) provide certified English-French translations of constitutional documents where required by Quebec law. Completion of steps 1–3 activates the LP's separate indirect tax identity distinct from the partners' personal tax identities.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — a Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the federal Income Tax Act (s. 96) and Quebec Taxation Act, meaning the partnership does not pay income tax and income/loss flow through proportionately to general and limited partners. For indirect tax purposes, however, the LP is a separate statutory 'person' under the Excise Tax Act (s. 123) and the Quebec Sales Tax Act, required to register, collect, and remit GST/HST and QST in its own name. The LP files an information return (T5013 / Relevé 15 in Quebec) but no T2 corporate return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the federal Income Tax Act (s. 96) and Quebec Taxation Act, meaning the partnership does not pay income tax and income/loss flow through proportionately to general and limited partners. For indirect tax purposes, however, the LP is a separate statutory 'person' under the Excise Tax Act (s. 123) and the Quebec Sales Tax Act, required to register, collect, and remit GST/HST and QST in its own name. The LP files an information return (T5013 / Relevé 15 in Quebec) but no T2 corporate return. Territorial scope: a non-resident limited partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP bears Canadian income tax on net Canada-source business income computed under Part I of the ITA; no exemption at the partnership level applies unless a tax treaty allocates the business income outside Canada.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law; an LP's pass-through classification is automatic and non-elective. Hybrid mismatch rules are statutory and self-executing under ITA sections 18.4–18.6 (OECD BEPS Action 2 alignment), with no elective opt-out; however, where a foreign entity uses a US-style check-the-box classification, CRA's administrative policy respects that foreign classification for Canadian tax purposes, and the deduction deferral / deduction non-inclusion rules may then be triggered. Available elections relevant to a Canadian LP: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election permitting closely related registrants, including a qualifying partnership and its members, to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST and QST (mirrored in the QS Act); (ii) ITA s. 96(3) filial election? No; rather, disputes? — the principal filing election is the mandatory designation of a 'representative partner' for T5013 purposes; and (iii) for non-resident partners, a s. 216 election is not applicable to business income but a partner may elect under ITA s. 115.1 in limited dispositions. No other statutory election alters the LP's default flow-through characterization or the 14.5% combined indirect tax application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold applies where the registrant's total taxable supplies (other than financial services and zero-rated supplies) made in Canada, and separately in Quebec for QST, are CAD 30,000 or less in the current calendar quarter and the four immediately preceding calendar quarters. A supplier that exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter, or that carries on business through a permanent establishment in Quebec, becomes a mandatory GST/HST and QST registrant; voluntary registration is available where taxable commercial activity has begun. Non-resident online/digital suppliers may be subject to separate deemed-registration rules, but these are peripheral to a physical-goods manufacturer. Input tax credit (ITC) recovery requires registrant status; export-oriented manufacturers commonly register immediately despite the CAD 30,000 threshold to recover embedded GST/QST on pre-production inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A taxable supply of tangible personal property (including self-heating exothermic-reaction instant meal kits manufactured in Quebec) is zero-rated at 0% GST and 0% QST when the supplier exports the goods as part of the supply, under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 and QSTA parallel provisions. Conditions: (a) the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada under a contract of carriage, or transfers possession to a common carrier / customs broker for export; (b) the goods are actually exported without further manufacturing, processing, or use in Canada; (c) title and risk of loss pass to a non-resident purchaser (or a registrant export agent in some structures); and (d) the goods are exported in the ordinary course of the supplier's commercial activity within a commercially reasonable time. Related services (freight, logistics, contract packing, and technical support) supplied to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant are likewise zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7, unless the service is rendered in Canada to an individual or is directly connected with real or tangible property situated in Canada. Domestic prepared-meal supply in Quebec would ordinarily be taxable at 14.5%, but export supplies supersede that treatment.

  • Commercial invoice stating exporter identity, BN/NEQ, harmonized system code (ISIC 1079 / NAICS 311999), unit value, currency, and consignee details

None — Canada and Quebec currently impose no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), mandatory API-based tax stamping, or central digital stamping of invoices. Electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML, Peppol-BIS 3.0) are legally admissible and encouraged under the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework, and Revenu Québec recognises e-invoices under its 'Revenu Québec Electronic Invoicing' guidelines, but no government clearance platform must approve each invoice before issuance. Registrants must retain complete books, records, and source exporting documents for six years from the end of the fiscal year to which they relate; failure to retain records is independently penalised.

On audit, CRA and Revenu Québec jointly deny zero-rating where the exporter cannot produce the above documentary bundle or where goods were not shipped within a commercially reasonable time; the full 14.5% GST/QST is reassessed on the supplier, any refunds or input tax credits previously claimed are clawed back, and arrears interest runs from the original remittance due date. False statements or gross negligence in export claims attract ETA s. 285.1 penalties equal to 25% of the denied tax amount, with a parallel QSTA penalty (up to 25%); knowingly making false statements or destroying records can lead to criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327, fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment, in addition to CRA's civil penalties (ITA s. 163 / ETA s. 280). Documentary records must be preserved for six years and the burden of proving export rests with the exporting partnership. Note: Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is maintained here as the internal documentary-evidence window consistent with prudent export-compliance practice, after which proof of export becomes administratively difficult to establish.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has no exchange-control law and does not require central bank approval for inbound investment, outbound trade settlement, dividends, partnership distributions, or liquidation remittances. Non-resident limited partners may receive CAD or foreign currency freely, subject only to standard withholding tax obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and Quebec withholding where applicable, transfer-pricing documentation requirements for related-party cross-border transactions, and sanctions compliance. All payments routed through Canadian Schedule I banks must still satisfy bank KYC and FINTRAC record-keeping requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules in Canada. Exporters are not required to repatriate, convert, or deposit export proceeds within a specified statutory period. Export receipts may be retained in a foreign-currency operating account or converted at the bank's prevailing FX rate. The only requirements are general Canada Revenue Agency record-keeping, transfer-pricing contemporaneous documentation for cross-border related-party sales, Canada Border Services Agency export reporting where applicable, and FINTRAC large transaction or electronic funds transfer reporting triggers for physical or electronic fund movements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank and monetary authority); Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange administration. Foreign exchange transaction reporting and AML/CFT supervision are handled by FINTRAC; prudential regulation of federally incorporated banks is handled by OSFI; Quebec-authorised deposit institutions and money services businesses are also supervised by the Autorité des marchés financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for use as primary banking rail; acceptable only as an ancillary FX execution venue. Canada does not recognise EU-style EMI passports. A foreign digital EMI or money-services business that receives or transmits funds for a Quebec LP must either be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC or use a registered Canadian partner. EMIs cannot offer Canadian deposit guarantees and are not CDIC-insured; therefore the LP must maintain its principal treasury and clearing accounts at a Schedule I bank. An EMI may be added as an agent for low-value trade payments or currency conversion, but all capital contributions and high-value trade settlement should clear through the domestic bank network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Quebec LP must receive all non-resident partner capital contributions via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank wire into its designated Schedule I bank operating account. The wire instruction must identify the LP by exact legal name, the provincial registration number, the beneficiary account number, and a remittance reference such as 'LP capital contribution — [partner name] — [date]'. The ordering bank must use the appropriate economic purpose code accepted by the Canadian receiving bank, such as capital equity participation, foreign direct investment, or intercompany loan. The bank will require the underlying capital call notice, the LP agreement, a source-of-funds certificate, and confirmation that no cash or non-banking transfer has occurred. Proceeds are cleared on value date with no conversion restriction, but any CAD equivalent over the bank's reporting threshold is subject to FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting obligations.Trade settlements may be executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account import disbursements; irrevocable documentary commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 for high-value or first-time supplier transactions; and domestic Canadian clearing methods such as Interac or the Canadian High-Value Payment System when the supplier is domestic. A US-dollar or other foreign-currency disbursement should be routed through the bank's cross-border wire network and accompanied by invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, certificates of origin, and inspection reports. For food-contact packaging or self-heating components, the bank may additionally request the supplier's food-safety or export quality-management certificates as commercial support.Outright deliverable forward contracts on CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/trade-settlement currenciesFX swaps to roll over expiring forward contracts without disruptionCross-currency swaps to convert long-term CAD capital into USD or EUR trade liabilitiesEuropean-style currency options for event-driven procurement coverNDFs are generally not a permissible or required instrument for CAD because CAD is fully convertible and has a deep deliverable forward market; an NDF may only be considered under an approved ISDA master agreement where the non-deliverable currency leg is subject to offshore statutory convertibility restrictions and is not directly Canadian.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for use as primary banking rail; acceptable only as an ancillary FX execution venue. Canada does not recognise EU-style EMI passports. A foreign digital EMI or money-services business that receives or transmits funds for a Quebec LP must either be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC or use a registered Canadian partner. EMIs cannot offer Canadian deposit guarantees and are not CDIC-insured; therefore the LP must maintain its principal treasury and clearing accounts at a Schedule I bank. An EMI may be added as an agent for low-value trade payments or currency conversion, but all capital contributions and high-value trade settlement should clear through the domestic bank network.
