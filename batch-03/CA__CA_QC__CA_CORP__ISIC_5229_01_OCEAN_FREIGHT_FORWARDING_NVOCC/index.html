Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec corporate income tax and Quebec Sales Tax (QST). Portals: CRA Business Registration Online (cra.gc.ca/businesses), Revenu Québec Online Services (revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign NVOCC carrying on business in Canada or Quebec must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identifier via Business Registration Online; the registration requires incorporation proof, foreign tax identification/passport, and director/resolution particulars. The same non-resident entity must separately register for QST with Revenu Québec and obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec if it has a permanent establishment or carries on an enterprise in Quebec. A GST/HST account is mandatory if taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters or in any single calendar quarter; otherwise voluntary registration may be used to recover input tax credits. The foreign NVOCC should also open the appropriate CRA program accounts for corporate income installments, assigned agent/file-non-resident filing, and any requested Canadian payroll or import/export program accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal or Quebec Business Corporation is a separate taxable person for both Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes; Canadian-resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source business and capital gains income, generally limited by treaty to permanent establishment profits.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; an incorporated NVOCC is per se an entity taxed at corporate level. Hybrid mismatch election: no in-force hybrid-mismatch legislation or domestic check-the-box election exists. Functional elections relevant to this foreign-owned NVOCC include voluntary GST/HST registration under Excise Tax Act s. 240(2.1), appointment of a Canadian agent for service/filing, Quebec allocation election among permanent establishments, and treaty-based branch return confirmation to avoid Part XIV branch tax on unremitted Canadian business income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada, measured over any single quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters, for both GST/HST and QST; a non-resident NVOCC making no Canadian taxable supply may remain unregistered but is not entitled to claim input tax credits. Once the threshold is exceeded, registration and collection begin immediately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) a supply of tangible goods exported from Canada in accordance with the vendor’s export documentation, and (ii) qualifying freight transportation services rendered by the NVOCC in respect of goods exported from Canada to a foreign destination, including through a non-resident Canadian-supplied service. The NVOCC must retain direct and continuous transport evidence showing the goods left Canada to a foreign port, a service contract naming the non-resident or exporting recipient, and bank evidence of foreign payment received in the course of normal trade; otherwise the supply is taxable at 14.975%.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or approved CAED electronic export declaration with border-crossover proof
  • Original ocean bill of lading or non-negotiable sea waybill issued to/through the NVOCC and dock receipt evidencing loading at a Canadian port for a foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/forex statement proving receipt of export payment from a foreign buyer within the statutory substantiation period

No national real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing mandate, or digital stamping requirement is in force federally or in Quebec. Large GST/HST registrants with gross revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file electronically; Quebec registrants with turnover exceeding CAD 10,000,000 must file QST returns electronically. NVOCC export invoices must nonetheless meet ordinary GST/QST substantiation records, including carrier contracts, bills of lading, and export declarations.

CRA and Revenu Québec closely audit freight-forwarder zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate export goods or services results in reassessment of unrecovered GST/QST plus compound interest; wilful or negligent false statements attract gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax, and Quebec may impose analogous administration penalties. Deliberately fraudulent export zero-rating triggers criminal prosecution, penalties of up to 100% of tax evaded, loss of input tax credits, and retrospective clawback of refunds paid for misdescribed or repatriation-failed shipments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, freely convertible currency with open current and capital accounts. No exchange-control permits, central-bank approvals, or non-resident convertibility limits apply to trade settlements, capital contributions, or profit remittance. Outbound dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees are unrestricted but remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced to 5%–15%; interest to arm's-length non-residents is typically exempt under the Canada–US Treaty and similar income-tax treaties, and Québec imposes a separate 15% provincial withholding administered by Revenu Québec). Inward direct investment is screened solely under the Investment Canada Act via s.12 notification and, where applicable, net-benefit review thresholds, not by FX regulation. Canadian financial institutions report cross-border payment instructions to the Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), and FINTRAC requires electronic-funds-transfer reports for transactions of C$10,000 or more in aggregate within consecutive 24-hour periods.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls and mandatory surrender of export proceeds in 1951 with the wind-down of the Exchange Control Act. An NVOCC/ocean freight forwarder may retain all foreign-currency freight-collect receivables and billings without conversion, may maintain multi-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, and faces no prescribed timeline or conversion ratio. Residual obligations are: (i) translation of foreign-currency results into Canadian dollars for income-tax reporting under Income Tax Act (ITA) s.261 using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; (ii) FINTRAC/Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) reporting of cross-border currency movements and electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more; and (iii) filing trade-in-services and balance-of-payments survey data with Statistics Canada when freight-forwarding revenues exceed reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF reporting oversight by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), and exchange-control policy responsibility residing with the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Canada does not issue offshore 'electronic money institution' (EMI) licences under the Bank Act, and a pure UK/EMEA EMI cannot act as the primary clearing bank or custody network for a Québec operating corporation. Canadian digital-payment providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and, as a practical matter, must settle through an OSFI-regulated chartered bank or a Canadian financial-services cooperative. Offshore EMI platforms may be used only as an overlay for cross-border client collections or multi-currency virtual IBANs, provided they are white-labelled with a Schedule I bank, are covered by a FINTRAC-approved agency/principal reporting arrangement, and do not hold the entity's operational liquidity outside the Canadian clearing system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident subscription proceeds and shareholder advances are remitted by SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the corporation's segregated Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I Québec bank. The remittance descriptor must cite 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan' together with the corporate identifiers (CBCA incorporation number and RÉQ enterprise number). CAD funds clear irrevocably and in real time through Lynx, the Bank of Canada's high-value payment system; USD funds post via FedWire/CHIPS through the bank's correspondent network. The receiving institution files a FINTRAC EFT report where transfers aggregate C$10,000 within 24 hours, and the Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada ITRS captures the cross-border payment instruction. A non-Canadian investor establishing the business is separately obliged to file an Investment Canada Act Form C-88 notification not later than 30 days after commencement of the Québec operation; net-benefit review applies only if the prescribed enterprise-value/asset thresholds are met. Shareholder debt must be evidenced by a written loan agreement with arm's-length interest, excepted-party, or prescribed-rate parity documentation, and interest paid to a non-resident shareholder remains subject to Part XIII withholding (treaty-reduced where applicable), with thin-capitalization analysis performed under ITA ss.15 and 18 and recharacterization risk addressed before injection.Freight payments to ocean carriers, foreign sub-agents, terminal operators, container depots, and overseas vendors settle through SWIFT MT103 (direct beneficiary credit) or MT202 COV (bank-to-bank cover) from the operational CAD/USD accounts. High-value CAD payments clear same-day via Lynx; USD cross-border payments use correspondent clearing over FedWire/CHIPS; EUR disbursements, where carriers invoice in euro, clear via SEPA/TARGET2. Commercial letters of credit (UCP600 documentary credits) support cargo-document-dependent payables and provide financing security over bills of lading; the issuing bank negotiates at the applicable discount rate and takes an assignment of the transport documents. ACH Direct is appropriate only for domestic Canadian vendor payments, staff reimbursements, and tax remittances; cross-border ACH IAT is not recommended for scalable trade settlement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Under the Canada-US tax treaty: 5% for dividends paid to a US corporation owning at least 10% of voting shares; 15% in all other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is no later than six months after the fiscal year-end, regardless of the corporation’s location of nativity of the taxation year. Balance of tax payable for a CCPC claiming SBD is due two months after year-end; all other corporations must remit within three months after year-end. Quebec instalments must follow Revenu Québec’s instalment calendar.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 must be filed within six months after the taxation year-end. Payment balances follow the federal schedule, generally due two months after year-end for eligible CCPCs and three months for large or non-CCPC corporations; specific instalment deadlines are prescribed by Revenu Québec.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing local file documentation is required where reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with any single non-resident exceed CAD 1 million in aggregate annual value. Master File and Country-by-Country Notification (Form RC4649) are required for MNE groups whose consolidated annual revenue was at least CAD 750 million in the preceding fiscal year. The documentation must be prepared by the corporate filing due date to avoid the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the sham doctrine, and the CRA’s transfer-pricing authority require genuine business purpose and real economic presence. A Quebec freight forwarding or NVOCC company must maintain physical offices, operating employees, cargo contracts, billing staff, and decision-making in Quebec; a paper entity used to route invoice settlements to an offshore affiliate will be recharacterized, reassessed, and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s. 239(1), conviction carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years; civil penalty under s. 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax plus daily compound interest; CRA may also impose third-party or gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns can be held jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withheld tax; upon criminal prosecution, pre-trial bail conditions may include travel bans; conviction can result in imprisonment, monetary restitution orders, and disqualification from serving as a director.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke s. 247(2) to reallocate arm's-length prices and s. 245 GAAR to deny the tax benefit; a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies, plus gross-negligence penalties and reassessment interest; treaty benefit can be denied and withholding taxes recomputed at 25%.

Director personal liability: A director of a sham entity can be treated as the true taxpayer through veil-lifting, liable for the company’s unpaid Quebec and federal taxes; s. 160 permits recovery of amounts transferred out of the corporation; travel bans may be imposed during criminal proceedings for false declarations; imprisonment of up to five years applies on conviction.
Transfer Pricing / Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late or missing T106 filing attracts CAD 100 per month per non-resident, capped at CAD 2,500; the transfer-pricing penalty is 10% of the net adjustment; failure to remit Part XIII WHT triggers a 10% remittance penalty plus daily interest; additional fines may be imposed per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding even if the corporation is insolvent; CRA may issue certificates of lien and seize personal assets; criminal charges can result in travel restrictions as bail conditions and imprisonment; civil restitution of all unpaid tax, interest, and penalties is recoverable in the Federal Court.
AML / Money Laundering Facilitation

Statutory sanction: If the entity conducts money-transfer activity as part of its freight-forwarding services and fails to register as a money services business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative monetary penalties may be imposed up to CAD 1 million per violation; criminal liability for laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carries up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be charged individually for facilitating money laundering; they face criminal fines, forfeiture orders, travel bans pending trial, and imprisonment up to 10 years; directors who authorize or turn a blind eye to suspicious transactions are treated as parties to the offence.
