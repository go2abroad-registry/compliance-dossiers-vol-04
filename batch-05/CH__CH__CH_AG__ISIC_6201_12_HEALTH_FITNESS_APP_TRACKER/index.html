Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum issued share capital of CHF 100,000. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A CH_AG is fully subject to direct federal (8.5% flat pre-tax rate), cantonal and communal income tax; no pass-through treatment. Residence: statutory seat or place of effective management in Switzerland (worldwide unlimited tax liability). For VAT, the AG is an independent taxable person with potential group-VAT grouping under Art. 11 MWSTG.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. Available elections (indirect tax): (i) VAT group registration election for closely affiliated entities (Art. 11 and Art. 56 MWSTV; irrelevance of invoicing between group members); (ii) voluntary VAT registration below the CHF 100,000 threshold (Art. 11 para. 1 MGWSTG); (iii) option to tax real estate under Art. 22 MWSTG; (iv) simplified/deemed input-tax methods (saldo method / Art. 37 MWSTV). For direct tax (Stage 4 context): STAF measures – patent box, R&D super-deduction, notional interest deduction (NID), and 'deemed profit' adjustments for principal/agent structures – operate as elective reliefs, not as flow-through classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover. For foreign suppliers (including digital app publishers) only Switzerland-source turnover counts; mandatory registration of foreign digital services providers for Swiss B2C supplies once CHF 100,000 turnover from Swiss consumers is exceeded (Art. 10/11 MWSGT; Art. 51-53 MWSTV). Small businesses below threshold may opt in; foreign service suppliers providing only B2B supplies to Swiss businesses normally have no registration obligation (reverse charge applies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and cross-border services qualify for 0% VAT (exemption with credit of input tax) under Art. 23 para. 1 and 2 MWSTG. For the digital fitness-app activity: (i) B2B supplies of software/licensing/subscription services performed for a foreign recipient are zero-rated if the recipient's business seat/establishment is located abroad and the benefit is received abroad; (ii) B2C tele-app services are placed at the consumer's habitual residence; sales to non-Ch consumers are outside Swiss VAT scope; (iii) sales to Swiss consumers are subject to Swiss VAT at 8.1% unless the platform (Apple/Google) is the liable intermediate. Main conditions: written contract or invoice identifying the foreign customer, no Swiss enumeration (UID) of the recipient, complete export proof retained by supplier for 10 years, and – where services are effectively used within Switzerland – no zero-rating even if invoiced abroad.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Switzerland. Invoicing must contain VAT case data (Art. 26 MWSTG; invoice data: supplier's VAT ID, recipient, date, description, rate, amount of VAT). Federal/technical standards: Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) has replaced legacy payment slips since 1 January 2021; voluntary Peppol BIS e-invoicing and EBICS-based transmission are accepted. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised, freely convertible currency system covering both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no foreign-currency surrender obligations, no cumulative account balance ceilings, and no profit repatriation limits for direct equity investors. Dividend remittances from an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) are generally subject to a 35% Swiss withholding tax, which may be reduced under double-taxation treaties, but this is a tax matter, not an FX control. The CHF is a globally reserve and freely convertible currency; the SNB does not require licences or pre-approvals for ordinary corporate treasury transactions. Capital movements are broadly unrestricted under the Swiss Foreign Trade and Payments Act (FTPA) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, with very limited emergency power vesting in the Federal Council.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion threshold, or surrender requirement applies to export proceeds from the digital fitness-tracker application. The Swiss legal framework does not impose any obligation to convert foreign currency receipts into CHF. The Swiss AG may retain export revenue in the original currency (USD, EUR, GBP, etc.) in multi-currency bank accounts, use it directly for settlement, or convert to CHF through the free interbank market without central-bank notification.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and clearing rails. Switzerland does not belong to the EEA, so a UK, Lithuanian or Irish electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot passport payment services into Switzerland. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) may offer foreign-currency collection accounts as secondary disbursement channels but are not accepted by the Swiss Commercial Registry as proof of paid-up share capital, and they cannot provide a law-governed Swiss IBAN for payroll, VAT/Federal Tax, or social security (AHV) settlement. For a health-fitness app sold through Apple/Google Billing, using an EMI as an upfront corporate treasury account is functionally viable at a transactional level, but fails the substance test required by Tier-1 Swiss banks; therefore, an EMI should only be used as a pre-funded sub-account for app-store merchant proceeds, with regular sweep settling to the Swiss bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Swiss AG's minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 (with at least CHF 50,000 paid up before registration) must be remitted via a compliant SWIFT MT103 from the parent company or ultimate shareholder. The payment instruction must contain Field 70 (Remittance Information) with the following domestic commerce marker: 'Cash capital contribution in accordance with articles of association [Article X] for the purpose of CH_AG registered undertaking.' The Swiss beneficiary bank will require the account to be flagged as an escrow account (Sperrkonto) so that fund release is blocked until the Commercial Registry (Handelsregisteramt) issues the certificate of incorporation. If the shareholder is located in the EU/EEA, a SEPA direct credit (Sepa-Institutszahlung) may be used in EUR, and the SNB reference exchange rate will convert to CHF on value date; however, Tier-1 banks often mandate MT103 for audit, because the SEPA format does not unequivocally identify the originator residual. In strict compliance, the bank will screen the originator against OFAC, EU Consolidated Restrictions, and the Swiss SECO blocker list before clearing.Clearing rails for settlement of operating trade disbursements include the following: (1) SWIFT MT103 (standard credit transfer, single payment) for invoices to suppliers, tax authorities, and individual contractors; (2) SWIFT MT700/MT707 to issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (UCP 600) for significant hardware/procurement or clinical trial contracts requiring title documents; (3) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for CHF-denominated disbursements to domestic beneficiaries with same-day laminal value; (4) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR payables to bank accounts in the SEPA region, processed through the Swiss SIC gateway but without charge; (5) Apple iOS App Store and Google Play Billing are not clearing rails themselves; merchant activity is remitted via J.P. Morgan or Bank of America money movement networks, settling in EMEA through SEPA/A Wire and then automatically swept to the Swiss bank via SWIFT MT101 multi-cash. For live transaction processing, the treasury team should set up a multi-currency current account with a concentration account in CHF, using a zero-balance sweeping structure to minimise FX exposure.Outright FX forwards (physical delivery) - the primary instrument for hedging CHF/USD, CHF/EUR, and CHF/GBP cash-flow mismatches, subject to standard ISDA 2002 Swiss law documentation with a bilateral credit support annex.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (XCCY) - allowed for long-term exchange of fixed/floating payment obligations, commonly used when the digital health-app company raises capital in USD and lends to the Swiss operating subsidiary in CHF.OTC FX options (European exercise) - permitted within the Swiss Banking Act and FINMA circular 2013/7 rules, but require a clean ISDA Score and CSA credit enhancement if used by a company without a financial-market dealer licence.FX swaps (or forex roll-overs) - used for simultaneous spot and forward legs to manage short-term liquidity between collection currencies (USD from App Store) and disbursement currencies (CHF for Swiss payroll).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - not permissible/not practically available for CHF in onshore Switzerland because the CHF is fully convertible and is not treated as an inconvertible or emerging-market currency; an NDF involving CHF would be considered an artificial restriction and may raise FINMA AML concern over speculative position-taking.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and clearing rails. Switzerland does not belong to the EEA, so a UK, Lithuanian or Irish electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot passport payment services into Switzerland. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) may offer foreign-currency collection accounts as secondary disbursement channels but are not accepted by the Swiss Commercial Registry as proof of paid-up share capital, and they cannot provide a law-governed Swiss IBAN for payroll, VAT/Federal Tax, or social security (AHV) settlement. For a health-fitness app sold through Apple/Google Billing, using an EMI as an upfront corporate treasury account is functionally viable at a transactional level, but fails the substance test required by Tier-1 Swiss banks; therefore, an EMI should only be used as a pre-funded sub-account for app-store merchant proceeds, with regular sweep settling to the Swiss bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% (Swiss federal direct corporate income tax; combined federal/cantonal/communal effective statutory rate varies by canton between approximately 11.9% and 21.1%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends and other profit distributions paid by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft, withheld from gross distributions to shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no explicit statutory threshold for mandatory Local File/Master File documentation. The Swiss Federal Tax Administration’s administrative guidance requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where a Swiss taxpayer engages in material cross-border related-party transactions. In practice, formal Local File documentation is expected when controlled transactions exceed CHF 1,000,000 per category on an annual basis; Master File documentation is expected for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above CHF 50,000,000. Below these thresholds, the taxpayer must still maintain sufficient evidence of arm’s length pricing in its audit file.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland is not a jurisdiction subject to the Gulf Cooperation Council Economic Substance Regulations. However, Swiss tax law contains a substantive general anti-abuse rule and substance requirements for treaty entitlement and preferential tax treatment. A Swiss AG must maintain genuine economic activity in Switzerland: real office premises, independent board functions, employees, assets, and actual decision-making support for the digital health and fitness app business. A letterbox or shell company may be denied treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test and may lose Swiss participation exemption or patent box relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 175 of the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Taxes, a fine of up to 2.5 times the tax evaded is imposed, plus arrears interest and the reassessment of tax. In aggravated cases, the same fine applies for cantonal taxes, and the criminal record reflects a wilful tax offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the false return are subject to individual criminal liability, personal fines, and civil restitution claims. Where the corporate form is used as a sham, piercing the corporate veil permits seizure of personal assets. Travel bans may be requested by investigating authorities in cross-border cases, and serious tax fraud can lead to imprisonment under Swiss criminal procedure.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding rate relief, loss of Swiss participation exemption and patent box benefits, cancellation of favourable tax rulings, reclassification of transactions, and imposition of ordinary tax with interest penalties. The FTA may also levy fines for providing false information in tax filings.

Director personal liability: If directors operate the entity purely as a facade, the corporate veil is pierced for unpaid corporate taxes and fraud claims. Directors face civil restitution to the tax authority, personal liability for unremitted withholding taxes, travel bans pending criminal investigation, and imprisonment where intentional documentary fraud is proven.
Money Laundering (Geldwäscherei)

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis of the Swiss Penal Code provides imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of up to CHF 500,000 for money laundering. In aggravated cases involving criminal groups, a prison term of up to five years applies. Derivative confiscation of laundered assets is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently fail to prevent the use of corporate bank accounts for money laundering are personally exposed to criminal prosecution. They may also face civil liability for damage caused to investors, asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors of Swiss companies.
Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 40 of the Swiss Federal Withholding Tax Act, the taxpayer is liable for a fine of up to 2.5 times the withheld amount evaded, in addition to the payment of the unpaid withholding tax and default interest. Administrative procedures permit seizure of corporate assets for the overdue withholding tax debt.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax if they intentionally or negligently fail to comply with the tax deduction and remittance obligations. This statutory in personam liability pierces the corporate veil, allowing tax authorities to collect directly from directors’ personal assets; travel bans and personal criminal prosecution follow in serious cases.
