Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), 555 Mackenzie Avenue, Sudbury ON P3C 4X5. Primary digital portals: My Business Account (myBA) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html; Business Registration Online (BRO); GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario's Ministry of Finance (employer health tax and provincial assessments) is secondary; all federal income tax, GST/HST and T5013 filings for the Ontario LP flow exclusively through CRA.

E-government filing portal

The Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) is a distinct registrant and must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as its single statutory TIN. Steps: (1) Test nexus: confirm the LP 'carries on business in Canada' under ITA s. 253 and Excise Tax Act s. 123(1) — Canadian general partner, Ontario business address and injection-molding plant satisfy the test. (2) File a single application via Business Registration Online (BRO); non-resident partners require an authorized representative (RepID/Web access code) or a paper filing of Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with a Form NR3010 profile, directed to the Sudbury Tax Centre international desk. (3) In the same application open the necessary CRA program accounts: GST/HST under the 'RT' program identifier; payroll under 'RM' if the LP has employees; the LP has no corporate income tax account because it is fiscally transparent. (4) Register the partnership's natural persons/entities: the LP must hold an Ontario Limited Partnership declaration on file with the Ontario Ministry (Companies and Personal Property Security Branch) before CRA finalizes the BN. (5) Obtain the T5013 program identifier 'PR' account on the same BN for the annual Partnership Information Return. Online registration issues the BN instantly; paper or non-resident applications take 4–6 weeks; retroactive effective dating is available where registration is requested within 30 days of the registration obligation arising.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The CA_LP is fiscally transparent under ITA ss. 96–98 and Ontario law; the partnership file no income tax return and pays no Part I tax; each partner, whether Canadian resident or non-resident, separately computes income/loss from the injection-molding business as allocated under the partnership agreement and reports it on the partner's own T1/T2 return. Canadian-resident partners pay federal and Ontario provincial tax on the allocated income. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on their share of Canadian-source partnership income and must file an NR6 undertaking and an NR4/T1 return for that income; the LP must nonetheless file a T5013 Partnership Information Return with CRA reporting each partner's share. GST/HST registration is treated separately from income tax transparency: the LP itself is the 'person' making taxable supplies and is the GST/HST registrant.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available statutory elections relevant to an LP toy manufacturer: (1) Fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(7) to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period where all partners consent; (2) Election under ITA s. 216 for a non-resident partner to file on Canadian rental income rather than under Part I; (3) GST/HST Quick Method election under Excise Tax Act s. 221.2 for eligible small suppliers with annual worldwide taxable supplies not exceeding C$400,000 (simplified remittance, but limited ITCs); (4) Streamlined Accounting administrative initiative of CRA permitting simplified input tax credit substantiation for imported production goods (pilot extended to 2025); (5) Hybrid mismatch arrangements: the ITA anti-hybrid rules (ss. 18.4–18.6, 111.5–111.8, 212.3) apply automatically to deny deductions or impose withholding where the LP has offshore hybrid instruments, and there is no elective opt-out; compliance is achieved through statutory factual determination and treaty-structure planning.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 per calendar quarter and per trailing four-quarter basis in annual worldwide taxable supplies under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1) (small supplier threshold). All zero-rated export sales count as taxable supplies for the threshold calculation. A manufacturer making only 0% export supplies will nevertheless exceed C$30,000 and must register; voluntary registration is mandatory in practice to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on steel injection-molding machinery, plastic resin, tooling and logistics services. Registration effective date: earliest of the first day of the month following the C$30,000 excess, any earlier chosen effective date, or the date a non-resident carries on business in Canada with an Ontario permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of the action figure / mecha model kit supplies is available under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1–7 of the Excise Tax Act: (a) goods delivered to a common carrier or consignee for export and actually exported in the same state as at the time of supply; goods sold FOB and shipped to a non-resident distributor or end customer in the United States, Asia or Europe qualify. (b) commercial services performed for a non-resident person with no Canadian residence and supplied in respect of goods exported by the non-resident (e.g., design, finishing and packaging of toys for an overseas licensee) qualify for zero-rating. No FOB destination or Incoterm limitation applies provided the supplier retains proof of export; the manufacturer must charge 0% HST on all qualifying export invoices, claim full ITCs on domestic production inputs, and, when the ITC balance exceeds output tax, file for a quarterly or monthly GST/HST refund.

No real-time fiscal clearance, no tax-authority digital stamping, no clearance-code or QR-mandated invoice regime exists in Canada as of 2025. B2B electronic invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework based on the PEPPOL network (launched 2023). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/MyBA) where annual revenue exceeds C$1.5 million or where the registrant is a new registration; otherwise paper filing remains permitted. All invoices (paper or electronic) must contain the supplier's BN, the registrant's name, the date, the description of goods and the HST charged (or zero-rated indication). Documentation retention is six years from the end of the taxpayer's fiscal year.

The CRA may reassess within four years (seven years where misrepresentation, neglect, or omission is attributable to negligence) and will claw back ITCs and zero-rated treatment retrospectively where export documentation is incomplete or inconsistent. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act: s. 280 — a penalty of 50% of any refund/rebate claimed erroneously where the claim is made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence; compound arrears interest under s. 280.1 accrues daily. Criminal liability under s. 327 ETA for willful evasion carries fines up to C$200,000 (or 200% of the tax sought) plus imprisonment up to five years; partners of the LP are jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST obligations. The LP must retain the original export documentary bundle for six years. Although Canada does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds, the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects (i) the typical CRA audit expectation that export receivables be collected in the ordinary course, (ii) bank / export-credit normal settlement cycles, and (iii) the practical limitation that open export receivables beyond 180 days will trigger heightened audit scrutiny and potential denial of the zero-rating claim on the ground that the transaction lacked the commercial substance of a genuine export sale. The LP should therefore incorporate a 180-day collection covenant into its export contracts and mark long-outstanding receivables for audit monitoring.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar floats freely and there are no restrictions on profit remittance, partnership distributions, dividend payments, or repatriation of capital by non-resident limited partners. Currency conversion is effected at market rates without any Bank of Canada, Ministry of Finance, or OSFI approval requirement, and no certification is needed to move funds onshore or offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds into CAD. The Ontario LP may retain export receipts in USD, CNY, JPY, or other currencies in permitted foreign-currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks. Export earnings remain subject to Canadian income tax regardless of currency or location of holding, but no FX surrender certificate or central-bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and administrator of the Exchange Fund Account under Part II of the Bank of Canada Act; no dedicated foreign exchange control administration exists because Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951. The Minister of Finance retains policy authority over the Exchange Fund Account; OSFI supervises deposit-taking institutions prudentially; FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for AML/KYC compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary clearing rail only; an offshore digital EMI must never serve as the primary depository for LP capital or escrow. EMIs operating in Canada must register as money service businesses with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; they cannot accept deposits within the meaning of the Bank Act (Canada), their balances are not CDIC-insured, and they are not authorized to perform the custodial functions of a Canadian chartered bank. The Ontario LP may permissibly use EMIs and PayPal for small-value e-commerce retail collections and CNH/JPY vendor settlements, provided that the capital escrow account and the main operational account remain at a Schedule I bank and that a documented downstream-account governance and reconciliation policy is maintained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident LP capital contributions must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the Schedule I bank operational or capital escrow account, with the remittance described under the BPM6 direct investment equity capital category. The documentary package must include the executed capital call notice, LP subscription agreement, certified general partner resolution or letter of direction, CRA business number for the partnership, and sworn source-of-funds declarations per contributor. Inbound funds clear through Payments Canada Lynx (real-time gross settlement) after conversion to CAD, or remain in a permitted foreign-currency account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (federal general rate 15% + Ontario general corporate rate 11.5%); Canadian-controlled private corporation manufacturing rates and the Ontario small business deduction may reduce taxable income below thresholds, but the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on taxable dividends from Canadian resident corporations. For a transparent LP, cash distributions to non-resident limited partners attributable to active Canadian business income are generally not subject to dividend withholding tax if such income is taxable under Part I in Canada through a permanent establishment; distributions derived from Canadian-source royalties, interest, or other Part XIII income may be withheld at 25% absent treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 per non-resident party per fiscal year for aggregate non-arm's length transactions; this threshold triggers T106 filing and the obligation to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. The documentation must be available within six months of a written CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, the Canada Revenue Agency applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership tests, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to disregard artificial transactions. An Ontario limited partnership carrying on high-precision plastic injection molding must maintain real economic activity in Ontario: actual manufacturing plant, qualified staff, inventory control, commercial risk, and meaningful decision-making. Non-resident partners must have substance in their treaty residence country to access treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under ITA 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under ITA 239(1) is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years, with minimum imprisonment of 12 months in aggravated cases. CRA may reassess beyond normal limitation periods where misrepresentation is planned.

Director personal liability: Managing general partner directors are personally assessable for knowingly participating in misrepresentations; the corporate veil is pierced for sham entities. Directors may face civil restitution under Ontario partnership law, travel restrictions where Crown tax debt exceeds CAD 50,000, and imprisonment or extradition for criminal tax evasion.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247(2); the transfer pricing penalty under section 247(3) equals 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue. Interest accrues from the original due date. T106 penalties apply at CAD 1,000 per month for up to 24 months.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fabricated TP reports or incomplete T106 disclosures can be jointly assessed as inter vivos trustees under ITA 160 and ordered to pay equitable compensation. Travel bans may be sought where the taxpayer attempts to flee Canada while a significant reassessment is contested.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Conduit LP

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 denies the tax benefit and re-characterizes transactions; Part XIII withholding of 25% is assessed on stripped amounts; treaty limitation-of-benefits clauses reject the claim. Administrative penalties of 50% may apply for false statements. The CRA may assess third-party penalties against professional advisors who devise the conduit.

Director personal liability: Directors of a foreign corporate general partner are treated as controlling minds; the court may lift the corporate veil and impose personal civil restitution, prohibit departure from Canada pending criminal investigation, and, in money-laundering cases, impose extradition and imprisonment.
AML / Unlicensed Remittance / Criminal Proceeds

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 10,000 per violation against individuals and higher amounts for entities; Criminal Code section 462.31 makes laundering of proceeds of crime an indictable offense punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; corporate fines are at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the managing general partner are individually liable where they are knowingly reckless to the laundering of property; personal consent or procurement of financial evasion triggers criminal liability, potential restraining orders freezing personal assets, travel bans, and extradition for related organized crime offenses.
