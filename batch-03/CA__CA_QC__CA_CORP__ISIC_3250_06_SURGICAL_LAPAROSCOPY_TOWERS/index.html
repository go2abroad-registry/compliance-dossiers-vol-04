Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal corporate tax and GST/HST; Revenu Québec – provincial corporate tax and Quebec Sales Tax (QST). Portals: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html (cra.gc.ca) and https://www.revenuquebec.ca/.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain/invoke a CRA Business Number (BN) by registering on the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or mailing Form RC1. The GST/HST account is opened using the same BN. For Quebec, the entity must then register with Revenu Québec, obtaining a Quebec Business Number and QST number, generally through 'Registre des entreprises' or Revenu Québec's online service. Non-resident filers may designate a Canadian agent for service of process and must provide director/officer addresses, entity constitutional documents, and a Canadian financial institution for tax remittance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists. Tax classification is statutory: corporations are always separate taxpayers under subsection 104(2) analogies, subject to formal dividend and salary tax mechanics. Limited elections are available: treaty-protected attribution under Income Tax Act subsection 216.1, non-resident withholding corporate partner elections under subsection 253.1, and hybrid mismatch expense denial elections under Part XII.6 (BEPS Action 2) where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable sales (revenue) for both GST/HST and QST small-supplier exception. Non-resident businesses may nevertheless be required to register if carrying on a business in Canada, supplying real property, or vying for Quebec government contracts.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of qualifying tangible goods is zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and mirrors in Quebec QST law, provided goods leave Canada within a prescribed time (usually 60 days) and are supported by documentary evidence. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under section 2-3 of Schedule VI if the services are supplied to a non-resident and the services are not attributable to Canadian-situated risks/income. For medical devices, including ultra-HD 4K rigid endoscopes, zero-rating requires an export exemption certificate (form GST119 or equivalent) and commercial justification of sale to a non-resident purchaser.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form or AES/ACROSS electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice containing Incoterms, product description, HS/NAICS classification, and sale value
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic) or Air Waybill showing consignee outside Canada
  • Packing List and export cargo manifest referencing container/serial numbers of endoscopy towers
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / swift MT103 bank wire evidence proving payment from foreign buyer
  • Export Exemption Certificate or equivalent statutory declaration retained by the exporter

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is permissible, and both CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic records provided authenticity, integrity, and readability are preserved. Quebec law imposes standardized billing content requirements, but there is no pre-approval/API clearance requirement before issuance.

CRA and Revenu Québec will reassess any claimed export zero-rating where evidence is inadequate, retroactively applies GST/HST/QST plus interest, and may impose civil penalties: a minimum assessable penalty of CAD 100 under ETA s.285, gross negligence penalty of 25% of misreported tax under ETA s.285.1 and QSA s.331, and in severe cases criminal prosecution under ETA s.312 and Income Tax Act s.239, exposing directors to personal liability and seizure/out-of-country collection under the Mutual Assistance Tax Act. Clawback period is generally 4 years, extended to 10 years for willful misrepresentation, with no time limit where false documentation or deliberate evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account; Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no restriction on payments or transfers for international transactions. Capital inflows (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans) and outflows (dividends, interest, royalties, capital repatriation, loan repayments) are unrestricted subject to ordinary tax compliance. Profit remittance friction consists solely of non-resident withholding tax under Income Tax Act Part XIII: 25% statutory rate on dividends and certain payments, generally reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Tax Convention where the beneficial owner is a US corporation holding at least 10% of the voting stock of CA_CORP, with comparable treaty reductions for other jurisdictions. Additional reporting includes T106 information returns and Revenu Québec TP-30 compliance; no central-bank or exchange-control clearance is required for FDI in the ISIC 3250 medical-device manufacturing sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no time-bound repatriation deadline. Export receipts from the sale of 4K laparoscopic towers may be retained indefinitely in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in a Canadian foreign-currency account. The only mandatory reporting obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Statistics Canada Balance of Payments and International Transaction reporting where exporter response thresholds are met; and (iii) customs export declarations through the Canadian International Trade Single Window. Exported medical devices qualify for GST/HST zero-rating as physical exports, but no FX conversion, surrender, or central-bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential supervision; AML surveillance of cross-border wire transfers is executed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Quebec financial-markets oversight is provided by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for money-services businesses and market integrity. Canada operates a freely floating exchange-rate regime; no exchange-control authority imposes approval requirements on cross-border trade or investment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, conditional. Canada has no EU-style Electronic Money Institution licence; a foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may lawfully serve the Quebec subsidiary only after (i) FINTRAC registration as a Money Services Business (MSB), (ii) Quebec AMF money-services business licensing under the Act respecting money-services businesses, and (iii) settlement sponsorship through a Canadian Schedule I bank or credit union. These platforms are compliant and effective for low-value supplier disbursements, employee travel cards, and mid-market currency conversion, but are not acceptable as the sole treasury rail because they do not offer domestic CAD clearing (Lynx/ACSS) participation, commercial import L/C issuance, or bank-guaranteed CAD/USD forwards. Recommendation: maintain the Schedule I bank relationship as the primary clearing and trade-finance rail, and use the MSB platform only as a supplementary cross-border payout channel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; Quebec does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Income Tax Act does not state a single fixed monetary threshold, but CRA administrative practice expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for any controlled cross-border transaction once the aggregate fair-market value of such transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; transactions below this amount and where gross revenue is below CAD 5,000,000 are at reduced audit priority. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 880,000,000 or more must file Country-by-Country Reporting in Canada, and separate Master File and Local File records are expected by CRA when audits of large taxpayers are undertaken.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres. Instead, CRA invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s. 245, the common-law sham doctrine, and substance-over-form principles to disregard entities that lack real board oversight, employees, assets, or business risks. For a Quebec medical-device manufacturer, a taxable permanent establishment is recognized only where there is actual physical presence, personnel, and managerial autonomy in the province; nominee directors or circular intercompany invoicing will cause the corporate form to be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Indictable tax evasion under ITA s. 239(1) carries a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. Summary conviction carries a fine up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; Quebec's Tax Administration Act imposes additional penal fines for willful evasion of provincial tax.

Director personal liability: Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil where the directors use the corporation as a sham or agent. Directors are personally assessed for the unpaid tax, source deductions, interest, and penalties; they may also face criminal imprisonment, travel bans under the enforcement process, and seizure of personal and corporate assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: CRA will deny the tax benefit under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in ITA s. 245(2). A 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163.2 can be applied; the foreign parent may be deemed to have a permanent establishment in Canada, subjecting it to federal and Quebec corporate tax on all Canadian sales. Quebec may also revoke the registration of a shell corporation.

Director personal liability: Directory personal liability attaches where the sham corporation is used to shield true principals. Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties, and they face asset freeze orders, non-dischargeable judgments, and restrictions on leaving Canada during civil or criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s. 247(2); transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Daily compounding interest applies to tax and penalties; failure to maintain records may also incur a separate offence under ITA s. 238 for up to CAD 25,000 per occurrence.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163.2. Where profits have been stripped through dividends to non-residents, directors can be pursued for unpaid corporate tax under the corporate veil doctrine, with judicial orders for restitution and travel bans in cross-border enforcement.
AML/CTF Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC can impose administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for an individual per compliance breach. Money laundering is an indictable offence under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carrying imprisonment for up to 14 years and an unlimited fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in laundering proceeds of crime are personally criminally liable. The corporate veil does not protect a director from personal fine, asset forfeiture, travel restrictions during prosecution, or director disqualification; CRA and Revenu Québec may also jointly assess directors for tax that arises from concealed transaction flows.
