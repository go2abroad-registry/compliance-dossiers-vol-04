Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Ontario's HST and corporate income tax are administered by CRA under the harmonized tax arrangement.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian Business Corporation, determine whether it is carrying on business in Canada and whether GST/HST registration is mandatory. Register for the federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the CRA Business Registration Online process where available; non-resident applicants should submit certified articles of incorporation, proof of foreign address and any authorized representative appointment. CRA issues a 9-digit BN and, upon registration, a GST/HST program account shown as BN + RT0001. No separate Ontario tax account is required for income tax or HST. Non-resident digital suppliers may use CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process and file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and is taxed at the entity level, not as a pass-through entity. As a resident Canadian corporation it is subject to worldwide income tax, and for Ontario HST purposes it is a registrant where taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian domestic tax law. A small supplier may elect to register voluntarily for GST/HST under ETA s. 240 to recover input tax credits; the election generally binds the person for at least one year. A Canadian corporation cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or trust for Canadian tax purposes. A US parent's check-the-box election for US federal purposes has no effect on CRA classification; the Canadian corporation remains fully taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act and remains a GST/HST registrant where required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident digital suppliers above the same threshold with supplies to Canadian consumers are required to register for GST/HST, subject to the small-supplier rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada can be zero-rated if the registrant exports them before use and retains documentary proof of export. Services and intangible personal property, including a fitness/health app licence or subscription, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and does not use or exploit the service primarily in Canada. Digital app-store sales to Canadian residents are subject to 13% HST in Ontario; sales to non-residents are generally zero-rated if the merchant-of-record and platform arrangements are properly documented. Where Apple IAP or Google Play Billing acts as merchant of record, CRA's digital platform rules may place the collection obligation on the platform; the developer must verify that platform remittance covers the Canadian HST exposure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) with the CBSA export transaction number, required for physical goods; not applicable to pure digital app downloads.
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the foreign customer and showing the exported or offshore-service character of the supply.
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or carrier-provided proof of export for goods; for digital services, server logs, app-store generated receipts, or download records evidencing delivery to a non-resident.
  • Inward remittance record or equivalent bank statement proving receipt of foreign currency proceeds in the expected repatriation window.
  • Residency due-diligence records, including customer address, IP-based jurisdictional data, store region, and non-Canadian payment instrument, supporting the non-resident status of the recipient.

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, SAF-T-type transaction reporting, or mandatory API-based invoice approval. A GST/HST registrant must keep complete books and records for at least six years and issue a GST/HST invoice containing the supplier's name, Business Number, invoice date, description of the supply, tax paid or payable, and total amount. Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain the required particulars and remain accessible to CRA. CRA e-services provide optional online filing and account management but do not require per-transaction pre-clearance.

CRA may reassess within four years after the return due date in ordinary cases, extend the reassessment period to seven years for misrepresentation or omission, and reassess without time limit in cases of fraud. If zero-rating is disallowed for insufficient export documentation or failure to prove non-resident status, 13% HST is assessed on the full sales value, input tax credits may be denied, interest runs from the due date, and civil penalties may apply, including 50% of the additional tax for gross negligence. Directors may be held liable for unremitted HST, and fraudulent export zero-rating claims may give rise to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No Exchange Control Act is in force, and no official approval, license, or registration is required for dividend remittances, loan repayments, royalties, or equity injections into or out of Canada. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to: (a) corporate solvency and solvency-test requirements under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) section 38 before dividends are paid; (b) non-resident withholding tax of 25% on dividends, reduced under applicable income tax treaty where the recipient is eligible; and (c) FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for physical movement or wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation, conversion mandate, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Canada. Inbound subscription and app-store revenues from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing may be retained in USD or converted to CAD at the holder's discretion, subject only to the record-keeping, large-transaction reporting, and cross-border movement reporting obligations imposed under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange policy and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision of money services businesses.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%; combined general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident shareholder from Canadian-source income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the taxation year-end. The final balance of tax is due within 2 months after the year-end, or within 3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions in a taxation year, triggering Form T106 disclosure and mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. CRA also expects an OECD-aligned Local File and Master File for corporate groups with consolidated revenue exceeding C$100 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s.245 denies tax benefits obtained from artificial arrangements lacking real economic substance. Ontario corporate law additionally requires a registered office, resident directors, corporate records, and evidence of Canadian mind-and-management for treaty eligibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.163(2) civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under s.380 of the Criminal Code attracting fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorize false filings can be held jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted amounts; corporate veil piercing permitted for fraud, with travel bans, seizure orders, and criminal imprisonment available.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income under ITA s.247(2) and impose a penalty under s.247(12) equal to 10% of the net adjustment if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to document the transactions contemporaneously.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP disclosures may face personal assessment for the 10% penalty; if the corporation is insolvent, CRA can pursue directors for restitution and obtain charging orders over personal assets.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment for the full unremitted withholding tax, gross-up interest, and penalties; s.238 makes failure to remit a criminal offence attracting fines and imprisonment where willful.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax under s.227.1 unless a due-diligence defence is established; personal civil liability extends to interest, penalties, and enforcement via director liability notices.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR under s.245 to eliminate the tax benefit, reallocate income to the substantive activity, deny treaty benefits, and impose a 50% gross-negligence penalty in addition to reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham entity can be named in common-law fraudulent conveyance actions; personal liability arises for taxes deemed evaded where the corporate veil is pierced, and court-ordered travel restrictions may be imposed during investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Sanctions Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative penalties up to C$300,000 for a first violation; repeat violations attract fines up to C$500,000, and FINTRAC reporting failures can result in criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for willful blindness or involvement in money-laundering transactions; individual liability includes fines up to C$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years for each wilful offense.
