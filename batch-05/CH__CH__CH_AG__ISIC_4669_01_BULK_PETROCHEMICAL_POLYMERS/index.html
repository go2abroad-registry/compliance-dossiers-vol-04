Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), VAT Main Division, Bern; online portal: e-tax (www.estv.admin.ch, www.e-tax.admin.ch); UID Business Identifier Register administered by the Federal Statistical Office (www.uid.admin.ch).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Swiss Business Identifier (UID) through the UID Register (www.uid.admin.ch) or when registered in the Cantonal Commercial Register of the seat of business. A UID is mandatory for VAT registration, for employing staff in Switzerland, for property ownership, or when acting as a customs declarant. VAT registration is filed electronically with the ESTV using Form 102 (VAT registration / Formulaire d'enregistrement TVA) via the e-tax portal; the statutory VAT number is the UID with the suffix 'MWST/TVA/IVA', format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST. A Swiss tax representative (domiciliary) may be appointed for foreign registrants. For direct federal and cantonal taxes, the AG files under the UID plus the cantonal tax number assigned by the competent cantonal tax administration of the registered office.

Residents are taxed on worldwide income, with statutory exemption for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate (unilateral territorial carve-out). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is an opaque limited-liability stock corporation subject to federal corporate income tax at a flat 8.5% rate (after federal prepayment) plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; effective aggregate rates typically range from 11% to 21% depending on canton (e.g., Zug approximately 12%). Residents are taxed on worldwide income, with statutory exemption for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate (unilateral territorial carve-out). The AG is not a pass-through vehicle; no income is attributed to shareholders for Swiss tax purposes. Capital tax is levied at cantonal level (typically 0.03%-0.1% of taxable equity).

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and rulings include: (1) VAT group taxation election under Art. 12 MWSTG, treating a controlling entity and its Swiss subsidiaries as one taxpayer; (2) simplified flat-rate VAT accounting election for businesses with turnover below CHF 5 million; (3) VAT option to tax real estate under Art. 22 MWSTG; (4) principal company / warehouse tax ruling election for multinational trading and logistics houses; (5) notional interest deduction election for excess financing costs; (6) offset of deemed capital contribution (agio) elections on share premium accounts. Hybrid mismatch rules (OECD BEPS Action 2) are domestically applied and disallow deduction in case of hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General threshold: CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies for businesses with Swiss seat or permanent establishment (Art. 10 para 2 lit. a MWSTG); foreign suppliers without Swiss establishment must register when taxable supplies performed in Switzerland exceed CHF 100,000 per year (Art. 10 para 2 lit. b); special thresholds: CHF 150,000 for agricultural and forestry businesses and CHF 250,000 for religious, charitable, and similar institutions; voluntary registration is available under Art. 11 MWSTG to recover input VAT; since 1 January 2025, electronic marketplace/platform operators are deemed suppliers of third-party goods and may trigger mandatory registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical export of goods under Art. 23 para 1 MWSTG, including exports by the supplier or a third party for the supplier's account to a foreign destination, provided the export is conclusively proven by a customs document. Services directly related to exported or transiting goods (Art. 23 para 2), freight and logistics services in direct connection with export transport, and B2B services with place of supply abroad (Art. 18) are also zero-rated. Consignment stock transfers to foreign warehouses qualify for zero-rating if the customs export declaration and subsequent foreign import are evidenced. The supplier is not entitled to input VAT deduction reduction for zero-rated exports; input VAT refund is granted via the ordinary return mechanism. Proof of export and payment must be retained for at least ten years.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / NAC Ausfuhranmeldung / déclaration d'exportation) or equivalent foreign customs export clearance record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical movement to foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT invoice containing the statutory invoice particulars under Art. 26 MWSTG
  • Sales contract with Incoterms 2020 and risk-transfer clause
  • SWIFT MT103 / payment confirmation or inward remittance certificate from the Swiss bank evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • UID VAT certificate of the Swiss exporting entity and, where applicable, warehouse or consignment stock documentation

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital tax stamping. Statutory invoice rules under Art. 26 MWSTG require the QR-bill format (IBAN-based QR invoice) for B2C invoices and standardized invoice content for B2B. The Swiss eBill standard (pain.009) is the accepted B2B/retail electronic invoicing format under SIX/AEBA governance. For customs, electronic filing is mandatory via the e-dec and e-dec web system and the New Customs Clearance System (NAC), which exchange goods movement data with VAT filings. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing for tax authority data harvesting is under Federal Council legislative discussion but not yet in force.

The ESTV and cantonal audit bodies conduct cyclical and risk-based verification of zero-rating evidence. Missing or inadequate export proof triggers retrospective reassessment at the standard 8.1% VAT rate plus default interest (currently 4.5% per annum). A fraudulent or systematic zero-rating claim constitutes tax evasion under Art. 58-59 MWSTG and the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR); penalties range from a summary fine of up to CHF 25,000 for simple infringement to a criminal fine of up to CHF 250,000 or imprisonment of up to three years for wilful evasion. In aggravated cases involving falsified documents, the VAT amount is recoverable in full with the tax claim secured by statutory retention rights, bespoke seizure measures, and director/officer joint and several liability under Swiss company law. There is no statutory fixed FX repatriation deadline under Swiss VAT law, but the 180-day benchmark is applied as banking and prudence practice; failure to repatriate proceeds within a commercially reasonable period is treated as an indicator of export sham and may trigger the penalty and clawback procedure described above.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) in both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisations, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no restrictions on holding or using foreign currencies within Switzerland. Profit remittance and dividend payments are fully convertible, but dividends are subject to the 35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) unless reduced or eliminated under the applicable double taxation treaty or the Swiss participation exemption/relief regime. Practical hurdles to profit remittance are not regulatory but bank-driven: mandatory AMLA/KYC due diligence, source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening, substance review, and Swiss National Bank balance-of-payments statistical reporting when monthly cross-border payment volumes exceed CHF 1 million. Capital and current account transactions receive identical treatment; both are unrestricted in law, subject only to corporate, tax, AML, and statistical reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency and held in Swiss or foreign bank accounts without central bank approval. The only relevant obligations are: (i) under the Federal Law on the Reporting of Statistical Data on International Trade in Services and Payments, a Swiss-resident exporter must report to the Swiss National Bank any monthly aggregate of cross-border payments received from abroad for exports if that aggregate equals or exceeds CHF 1 million, within the prescribed reporting deadline; (ii) all export/import trade documents must be retained for AML, customs, VAT, and audit purposes; and (iii) no SNB conversion or surrender authorization is required.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera; the Federal Council through the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF) is responsible for international financial policy, but the SNB is the sole central bank and monetary authority with no distinct foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. A Swiss-licensed fintech holding a banking licence or a fintech licence under the Swiss Banking Act/FINMASA may offer virtual current accounts and payment services. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK, Malta, or Lithuania) cannot hold CHF client funds or provide escrow-like services without a Swiss banking licence. Offshore EMIs may only be used as supplementary payment rails if they maintain pass-through accounts at a licensed Swiss bank; they are not viable for capital escrow, statutory capital deposits, or strong trade-finance transactions, and many Swiss counterparties and international producers reject them for commodity settlements. Legal constraints under the Swiss Banking Act and AMLA require the underlying payment transaction to be ultimately cleared by a Swiss-regulated or recognised foreign bank.

Root cause: Transaction size disproportionately exceeds company substance, expected turnover, or available working capital

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injection: the foreign shareholder sends a SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer (or MT202 COV for larger interbank transfers) to the Swiss AG's CHF liquidity account with the reference 'Equity contribution to share capital in accordance with notarial deed dated DD-MM-YYYY'. The remitter must be the actual shareholder of record; the beneficiary must be the registered Swiss corporate account. The Swiss bank will request the notarised capital increase deed or founding deed, a commercial register extract, and a source-of-funds confirmation before making funds available. If the shareholder uses a shareholder loan, the reference must state 'Direct loan agreement under Swiss Code of Obligations' and a signed loan agreement must be provided. The transaction must be recorded for Swiss National Bank balance-of-payments statistics if aggregate cross-border inflows in the reporting month exceed CHF 1 million. The bank's internal purpose/transaction code for direct investment or equity capital injection should be used; no Swiss foreign exchange control approval is required, but AMLA verification and sanctions screening are mandatory.For settlement of polymer purchase obligations, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments in USD/EUR/CHF, using full remittance information and supported by purchase orders and invoices; (ii) documentary collection under URC 522 (D/P or D/A) for trade settlements without letters of credit; (iii) irrevocable documentary credits issued or negotiated via SWIFT MT700/MT707, optionally confirmed by a major Swiss or international bank when suppliers require a confirmation; and (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer/TARGET2 for EUR payments within the European Economic Area. All outbound transfers must be covered by a clear underlying trade contract, be sanctioned-screened, and comply with FINMA's AML guidelines on unusual transactions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. A Swiss-licensed fintech holding a banking licence or a fintech licence under the Swiss Banking Act/FINMASA may offer virtual current accounts and payment services. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK, Malta, or Lithuania) cannot hold CHF client funds or provide escrow-like services without a Swiss banking licence. Offshore EMIs may only be used as supplementary payment rails if they maintain pass-through accounts at a licensed Swiss bank; they are not viable for capital escrow, statutory capital deposits, or strong trade-finance transactions, and many Swiss counterparties and international producers reject them for commodity settlements. Legal constraints under the Swiss Banking Act and AMLA require the underlying payment transaction to be ultimately cleared by a Swiss-regulated or recognised foreign bank.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate tax return must be filed within six months after the close of the financial year. For a standard calendar-year Swiss AG, the statutory base deadline is 31 March of the following year. The tax administration routinely grants an extension to 30 June and, upon justified request, to 30 September or later where the audited financial statements are not yet approved. Extension of the filing deadline does not defer the obligation to pay assessed taxes, and interest accrues from the legal due date.
