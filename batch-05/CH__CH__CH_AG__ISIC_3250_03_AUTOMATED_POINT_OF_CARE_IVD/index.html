Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Swiss federal law; an AG is always taxed as a separate legal entity at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Available statutory elections include the VAT group election (Organschaft under Art. 22 VAT Act), cantonal tax status elections such as holding company and mixed/domicile company regimes (subject to phase-out restrictions effective 2020), and the election to apply the notional interest deduction on equity. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Swiss tax classification is based on statutory legal form; a foreign-law check-the-box designation has no Swiss domestic tax effect, and Swiss anti-hybrid measures follow OECD/BEPS alignment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 para. 2 lit. a Swiss VAT Act); for foreign suppliers without Swiss domicile, the threshold is measured on Swiss turnover and a Swiss fiscal representative is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated treatment for Swiss VAT applies to the export of goods when the goods are physically transported or dispatched to a foreign country and the recipient is abroad, as proven by a customs export declaration. Services performed directly on, or in connection with, exported goods, and certain services supplied to recipients with place of supply outside Switzerland, are also zero-rated. The supplier must hold verifiable documentary evidence showing that the goods left the Swiss customs territory or that the service is attributable to an external place of supply; otherwise the standard 8.1% rate must be charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss customs export declaration via Passar/ETariff, including the export notification)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign currency consideration

Switzerland does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC). Electronic invoices are legally recognised provided they guarantee authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility under Art. 26a VAT Act. The QR-bill payment slip standard is mandatory for payment slips on open invoices; electronic invoices in the Swiss eBill/XML format are customary. No digital stamping or pre-clearance approval is required; however, exporters must maintain a Swiss SAF-T (Audit File) compatible accounting archive for audit readiness.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may reassess zero-rated exports within a 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of confirmed tax evasion or serious documentary deficiency (Art. 72 VAT Act). If the export evidence or payment documentation is found deficient, the 0% rate is retrospectively reversed and the outstanding VAT of 8.1% is collected with default interest. For intentional underpayment of VAT, a penalty of up to three times the evaded tax may be imposed under Art. 100 VAT Act; for negligent conduct, penalties of up to one time the evaded tax apply. The Swiss customs authorities may also impose separate customs sanctions for false declarations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There are no exchange controls, no central bank authorisation requirements for foreign currency transactions, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or capital withdrawal. CHF, EUR, USD and all major trade currencies are freely convertible in both directions without licence. The only regulatory frictions are (i) the 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax on Swiss-source dividends, which is refundable or reducible under an applicable double taxation treaty; (ii) SNB direct reporting obligations for balance-of-payments statistics whenever external assets or liabilities exceed CHF 2 million or annual external payments exceed CHF 1 million; and (iii) the statutory duty of Swiss banks to verify the economic background of every transaction under Article 7 of the Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA), including documentary proof of the underlying commercial purpose of treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist under Swiss law. There is no mandatory surrender rule, no statutory conversion timeline, and no forced repatriation quota for export receipts. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft may retain export proceeds in any fully convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The only binding obligations are commercial bookkeeping and account documentation under Articles 957 et seq. of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO), plus the SNB direct-reporting thresholds to the Swiss National Bank for external assets, external liabilities, and annual foreign payment flows. No conversion ratio or liquidation of foreign currency balances is ever mandated.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and AML/CFT supervision; the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) for trade policy and export controls; and the Anti-Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (MROS) for suspicious transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the required CHF treasury, escrow, and LC-clearing functions. Non-Swiss offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not supervised by FINMA, cannot access the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system as direct participants, and cannot issue genuine CH IBANs for payroll, tax, or utility payments routed through SIC. A Swiss FinTech licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act allows only limited public deposit-taking and does not authorise credit granting, commercial letters of credit, or trade finance. Licensed Swiss digital banks may be used only as ancillary payroll and petty-cash accounts; the principal escrow, operational, and cross-border settlement relationship must reside with a full commercial bank under Swiss banking supervision.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital must be wired by SWIFT MT103 to the Capital Escrow Account (Sperrkonto) held in the name of the Swiss AG at a FINMA-licensed bank, quoting the Swiss IBAN of the escrow account and the payment reference 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital / CO 634 / Sperrkonto' together with the date and number of the notarial deed of incorporation. The bank will verify the credit, match the total to the share-capital amount, and issue the non-transferable capital-verification certificate (Gründungsbestätigung) required for the Commercial Register. Non-Swiss offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not supervised by FINMA, cannot access the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system as direct participants, and cannot issue genuine CH IBANs for payroll, tax, or utility payments routed through SIC. A Swiss FinTech licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act allows only limited public deposit-taking and does not authorise credit granting, commercial letters of credit, or trade finance. Licensed Swiss digital banks may be used only as ancillary payroll and petty-cash accounts; the principal escrow, operational, and cross-border settlement relationship must reside with a full commercial bank under Swiss banking supervision.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax (CIT) of 8.5% assessed on after-tax profit (equivalent to approximately 9.3% on pre-tax profit); plus cantonal and communal profit taxes, producing aggregate effective tax rates of approximately 11.9%-21.0% depending on the canton of residence (per Zug and Zurich benchmarks).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions by an Aktiengesellschaft, regardless of the residence of the shareholder; refundable to Swiss residents and subject to treaty reduction for non-residents at rates generally ranging from 0% to 15% depending on DTA and shareholding percentage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Swiss transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required for taxpayers classified as 'large' for Swiss tax purposes (annual turnover exceeding CHF 50 million, balance sheet total exceeding CHF 20 million, or more than 200 full-time employees) or when annual cross-border intra-group goods/services transactions exceed CHF 10 million. A Master File is required for Swiss entities that are members of a multinational group with consolidated group turnover exceeding CHF 50 million, in line with OECD BEPS Action 13 standards.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply offshore-type Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, substantive economic substance is mandatory through the Swiss General Anti-Abuse Rule (Statutenwechsel doctrine), treaty abuse prohibitions under Art. 22 of the OECD Model, and the Steuerdomizilwechsel regime for migration to low-tax jurisdictions. A Swiss AG manufacturing IVD microfluidic cartridges and analyzers must demonstrate real entrepreneurial functions (production, warehousing, R&D, strategic management) and adequate substance in Switzerland to retain treaty benefits, patent box claims, and QDMTT compliance under Pillar Two from 2024.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung / Soustraction fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 175 DBG, a fine of up to three times the amount of the evaded federal tax plus statutory interest; cantonal penalties of comparable magnitude; criminal prosecution for fraud in the event of forged documentation. Repeat or aggravated violations may result in publication of the final assessment and disqualification from reliefs.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable as principals or accomplices; penalties include imprisonment for up to 36 months and fines. Where the board ordered, participated in, or negligently failed to prevent the false declaration, personal joint and several liability for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties may be asserted.
Withholding Tax Violation (Anticipatory Tax fraud)

Statutory sanction: Assessment against the company of the uncollected 35% anticipatory tax plus default interest; administrative fines up to CHF 100,000; criminal prosecution under the Swiss Withholding Tax Act (VStG Art. 64); publication of the judgement in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Board members are personally liable for remittance of uncollected anticipatory tax; in serious criminal investigations, travel restrictions, provisional confiscation of passports, and asset freezes may be imposed; in egregious cases directors face imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable profit on deemed arm's-length terms; recapture of group relief benefits; penalty up to three times the evaded tax for wilful conduct; late payment interest at the official rate; possible denial of loss carryforwards and order for amended filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly submit misleading Local File/Master File reports face criminal sanctions for tax evasion; civil liability for restitution of the adjusted tax and penalties; imprisonment up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and reclassification of the company as a conduit; retroactive obligation to withhold 35% Swiss anticipatory tax on dividends; application of the Steuerdomizilwechsel special tax on hidden reserves upon migration; penalties of up to 150% of added tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be ordered to cooperate in the tax reinstatement; personal civil liability under Art. 754 CO for issuing profit distributions without substance; repeated abuse may trigger travel restrictions and criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion.
AML and Financial Crimes (Money Laundering / Mis-invoicing)

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 71 StGB), confiscation of the proceeds of crime; penalty for legal entities of up to CHF 5 million; mandatory filing of a suspicious activity report with the Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (MROS); potential administrative withdrawal of licences.

Director personal liability: Directors may face imprisonment of up to 3 years, personal restitution of laundered amounts, travel bans and passport seizure during proceedings, provisional freezing of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as directors of Swiss companies.
