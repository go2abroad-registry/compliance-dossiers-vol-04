Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), Direction de l'immatriculation, 2085, boulevard Charest Ouest, Québec, QC G1N 2L8.

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec, Book 4, Limited Partnership (société en commandite), arts. 2235-2247; Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-44.1, ss. 20-22 and related provisions. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not apply to a Quebec limited partnership; the entity is a contractual partnership registered with the Quebec enterprise register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation d'une société en commandite (Declaration of Registration of a Limited Partnership) filed through the Registraire des entreprises' online portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement. Capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement; limited partners are liable only to the extent of their agreed contribution (Civil Code of Québec, art. 2243 applicable to commanditaires).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the commercial landlord must provide a certificate of compliance with municipal fire prevention standards, and the office lease must include the building's occupancy load and means of evacuation. CNESST occupational health and safety requirements for a software office are limited to a first-aid kit, emergency evacuation plan, and ergonomic workstations; no structural load inspection is mandatory because no industrial machinery or heavy equipment is operated. If a future data centre is physically occupied, a civil defence/security plan and structural certification would be separately assessed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement. Capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement; limited partners are liable only to the extent of their agreed contribution (Civil Code of Québec, art. 2243 applicable to commanditaires).

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, by the General Partner of the Limited Partnership, acting as the governing party, that: (1) The capital subscription commitments of the limited partners set forth in the subscription schedule are accepted and shall be credited to their partnership capital accounts. (2) The registered office of the Limited Partnership in Quebec is adopted at the address appearing in the declaration of registration, and the Registraire des entreprises is authorized to file such declaration and any subsequent amendments. (3) The General Partner appoints the undersigned as chief manager, secretary-treasurer, and compliance officer, each with authority to execute filings, open banking accounts, and execute documents required for the formation of the Limited Partnership. (4) The Partnership Agreement is ratified and the officers shall complete all ancillary filings.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210. No Quebec industrial licensing board requires a permit for software development, DevOps tools, database, or API monitoring cloud infrastructure; local municipal commercial occupancy rules may apply to the office lease.

Category: Not applicable — the activity is not a licensed trade or industrial undertaking. A general municipal commercial permit may be required for the physical office, but no provincial operating licence is mandatory.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST and federal income tax withholding; Revenu Québec (Agence du revenu du Québec) for QST and provincial levies. Primary portals: cra.gc.ca (Business Registration Online) and revenuquebec.ca (Mon dossier d'entreprise).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec Limited Partnership (CA_LP) must: (1) file a Declaration of Registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (2) register with the CRA via Business Registration Online (Form RC348) to obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN), and concurrently open GST/HST and Payroll accounts; (3) register with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V (Demande d'inscription au fichier de la TVQ) referencing the BN and NEQ to obtain the QST registration number; (4) file the Federal Partnership Information Return (Form T5013 / T5013SUM) annually with schedule T5013-SS for each non-resident partner; and (5) where the foreign general partner is a U.S. entity, file Form RC463 (Conduit Entities) election to clarify the flow-through status. Non-resident partners with no Canadian employment income are not required to file T1/T2, but must file a T5013 partnership code and may be required to pay Quebec tax by instalments under QTA s. 1030.0.1.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Quebec Limited Partnership (CA_LP) must: (1) file a Declaration of Registration under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (2) register with the CRA via Business Registration Online (Form RC348) to obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN), and concurrently open GST/HST and Payroll accounts; (3) register with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V (Demande d'inscription au fichier de la TVQ) referencing the BN and NEQ to obtain the QST registration number; (4) file the Federal Partnership Information Return (Form T5013 / T5013SUM) annually with schedule T5013-SS for each non-resident partner; and (5) where the foreign general partner is a U.S. entity, file Form RC463 (Conduit Entities) election to clarify the flow-through status. Non-resident partners with no Canadian employment income are not required to file T1/T2, but must file a T5013 partnership code and may be required to pay Quebec tax by instalments under QTA s. 1030.0.1.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax under Subdivision 132 of the Income Tax Act; the LP itself is not a taxpayer. Partners, including non-resident limited partners, are taxable on their allocated share of income. Available elections include: (i) check-the-box / conduit election under Form RC463 for U.S. LLC or LLP partners to align classification with the U.S. check-the-box regime; (ii) accelerated capital cost allowance election under s. 13(19) / s. 1103 of the Income Tax Regulations for eligible software and cloud infrastructure; (iii) optional GST/HST registration election for foreign persons providing downloads or software services under ETA s. 240(0.1) and s. 240(1.1) — a non-resident carrying on business in Canada can voluntarily register even below threshold; (iv) QST joint filing election under QTA s. 449.1 for closely-related registrants; and (v) hybrid mismatch arrangement election (where applicable, avoiding a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch under s. 18.4 and s. 18.6 ITA, aligned with SOR/2022-49, and the Canada–U.S. Tax Convention Article IV(6)(d) conduit rule).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST) and CAD 30,000 (QST) over any consecutive four-calendar-quarter period for the small supplier threshold. For non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers (B2C), mandatory registration applies without a minimum threshold effective July 1, 2021 under ETA s. 240(0.1) and amendments from Bill C-261; however, for true business-to-business exports to non-residents, no registration is required and zero-rating applies; the LP may nonetheless voluntarily register to claim input tax credits (ETA s. 169, s. 240(1.1)). QST threshold parallels the federal rule under QTA s. 415.1.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of developer infrastructure, database monitoring, and API monitoring cloud services constitutes a supply of a service under ETA s. 123(1) and is zero-rated under ETA s. 23(1) where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person (not a Canadian resident or a person carrying on business in Canada that is registered for GST/HST); (ii) the service is performed for the recipient outside Canada or in relation to foreign real property / foreign goods; and (iii) the service is not part of a supply made to a Canadian establishment of the recipient. CRA Technical Information Bulletin B-090 confirms zero-rating for supplies of services to non-residents where the recipient's place of business is outside Canada and the service is not rendered simultaneously to a Canadian-based audience. For QST, zero-rating is granted under QTA s. 200.1 on the same fact pattern. Substantiation requires a signed zero-rating declaration (general or specific) obtained before the invoice date; the declaration must be renewed on a periodic basis (commonly annually) and kept with the tax file. Since the LP exports SaaS, the LP may also rely on the 'intangible personal property' zero-rating provision in ETA s. 23(2) for supplies of software licences, patents, and know-how to non-residents.

  • Executed zero-rating declaration (ETA s. 23(1) and QTA s. 200.1) from the non-resident recipient, including foreign business address, GST/HST status and statement of intended use outside Canada
  • Signed SaaS master subscription agreement or service order evidencing offshore consumption, cloud infrastructure location, and international delivery terms (INCOTERM or cloud SLA)
  • Commercial invoice (electronic PDF/XML) showing 0% GST/QST, LP's BN and QST registration number, and reference to 'Export — Zero-rated supply under ETA s. 23 / QTA s. 200.1'
  • Bank settlement record (Stripe Billing export remittance, Mercury ACH SWIFT wire, or foreign-currency receivables report) demonstrating payment from the non-resident partner or third-party customer in USD or other foreign currency without Canadian clearing
  • Outbound network traffic and CDN access logs evidencing termination of services at foreign IP geolocations and absence of Canadian IP consumption for the invoiced period
  • Quebec enterprise registry certificate (NEQ attestation) and copy of the LP's certificate of limited partnership evidencing the foreign jurisdiction of the principal place of administration

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoicing API as of 2025. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices in standard formats (PDF/A, XML, EDIFACT, PEPPOL). Mandatory invoice content for a Quebec supply: supplier's identity, BN and QST registration number (TVQ number), date, description, amount, and separate display of GST and QST or an explicit zero-rating reference (e.g., 'ZERORATED — ETA s. 23(1) / QTA s. 200.1'). For exported cloud services, Revenu Québec requires that the QST account of the supplier appear on the invoice and that a zero-rating declaration from the non-resident recipient be retained. CRA's eAudit programme accepts full electronic books and records; no API-based clearance or fiscal stamping is mandated. The federal GST/HST return is due electronically via GST34-RE or NETFILE; the QST return is filed through Revenu Québec's EDl Web service. Businesses exceeding CAD 24,000 in annual taxable supplies of GST/QST remittings must use electronic payment and mandated XML schema for QST return transmission.

Where CRA or Revenu Québec determines that the zero-rating was claimed without proper substantiation or that the service was actually consumed in Canada, the LP is reassessed for 5% GST plus 9.975% QST on the full consideration, plus interest under ETA s. 280 (prescribed daily rate, currently 9% annual on overdue amounts) and QTA s. 1080 (prescribed interest rate). Gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285(2) and QTA s. 1025.1 apply at the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the net tax, with additional s. 285.1 false-statement penalties of CAD 5,000 per occurrence. Wilful evasion, including falsely documenting an export to a non-resident, constitutes an indictable offence under ETA s. 239(1) and QTA s. 1031, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and an unlimited fine. The CRA audit programme 'GST/HST Audit of Exported Services and Intangible Supplies' (RC4056) applies a chain-of-evidence test: the declaration, invoice, payment in foreign currency, and offshore consumption records must be contemporaneous; retrospective export documentation prepared after audit notification is disregarded. The general four-year limitation period under ETA s. 298(2) and QTA s. 1060 is extended to six years for non-arm's-length or third-party misrepresentation audits, and there is no limitation period where wilful misrepresentation or fraud is established. Refund claims for input tax credits associated with export operations are scrutinized under ETA s. 169(1)(a); recovery is denied ab initio if the underlying supply was not properly zero-rated, triggering full clawback plus compound interest from the original filing date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both capital and current account transactions. No exchange control approval is required for profit remittance; however, distributions and remittances from Canadian-source income are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents, generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Banks may require Form NR301 confirmation, a Certificate of Compliance or tax clearance for large one-off remittances, and FINTRAC reports for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted, subject to anti-money-laundering and sanctions review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio of export receipts into Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds abroad or in Canadian bank accounts at their commercial discretion; all transactions above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC cross-border report format, and documentation must evidence the commercial invoice underlying any repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with the Department of Finance Canada overseeing currency policy and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforcing cross-border transaction reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canadian-regulated fintech platforms and payment intermediaries (such as Stripe, Moneris, and Payfare) may support collection and disbursement flows, but a foreign digital EMI cannot directly open an Canadian-dollar settlement account without either establishing a Canadian permanent establishment or partnering with a federally regulated bank. The Quebec Act respecting money-services businesses requires registration with the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for entities remitting funds for third parties; using an EMI solely for treasury processing of own flows may be permissible without a licence, but banks will treat intermediary-funded accounts as higher-risk and may refuse onboarding if the EMI is not identifiable as the originator or beneficiary on all transactions.
