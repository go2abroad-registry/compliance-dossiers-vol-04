Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership. The Declaration of Registration must state the agreed capital contributions; contributions may be in cash, in kind or services, may be payable in instalments, and no escrow account is required by law. A nominal contribution may be recorded when no cash is initially subscribed. Limited partners' liability is limited to their agreed contribution, while general partners may be fully liable for partnership obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire prevention inspection confirming egress, extinguishers, emergency lighting and fire alarm system; certificate of occupancy confirming permitted occupant load; CNESST clearance and prevention program where employees are present; liability insurance and building owner authorization for the registered office address.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) national portal for the federal Business Number (BN); for Quebec-based taxpayers, Revenu Québec administers both federal GST/HST and provincial QST under the Canada-Quebec Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement, with registration effected via Revenu Québec's 'Clients' portal (Saisie-Zéro / mon dossier d'entreprise) and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Business Registry) for the partnership declaration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-led Quebec Limited Partnership must first file a Declaration of Partnership (Déclaration de société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, obtaining the Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ). Concurrently, the partnership must register for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online service or through Revenu Québec's integrated single-window registration for GST/HST and QST once the total global taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any 12-month rolling period. The partnership must appoint an agent for service of process in Quebec; non-resident partners must provide residency certificates and the general partner's identification details. The QST registration number and GST/HST registration number will be cross-referenced to the BN/NEQ, enabling a single electronic filing profile for federal and provincial indirect tax returns (GST/HST and QST combined remittance).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity classification election; limited partnerships are statutorily fiscally transparent. A Quebec LP is not a legal person and is treated as a flow-through under the Income Tax Act (ITA) s.96 and Quebec Taxation Act, with income allocated to partners. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under ITA s.99/Quebec equivalents; (ii) optional capital cost allowance elections for partnership property (ITA s.97); (iii) non-resident partner withholding elections; and (iv) for hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada applies BEPS Action 2 rules (ITA ss.18.2 to 18.8) disallowing deduction/non-inclusion outcomes — no elective hybrid relief is available; a Quebec LP with a corporate general partner may nonetheless elect under ITA s.150/152 to file separate partnership information returns excluding certain non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (gross revenue) over any consecutive 12-month period; QST: parallel CAD 30,000 threshold. Non-resident suppliers supplying services to Quebec consumers (including digital services effective 2019) may be deemed to carry on business in Quebec and become liable to register without a physical presence; once registered, registration is compulsory regardless of threshold for non-residents engaged in a commercial activity in a visible manner.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied by a Quebec LP to a non-resident client (offshore corporate secretarial and registered agent services) are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Sch. VI, s.7 and QST Act Sch. IV, s.6 where: (i) the service is performed for a non-resident recipient; (ii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or a service to a person who is a Canadian resident for the purpose of the supply; and (iii) the supply is supported by documentary evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient. Where the LP acts as registered agent for Canadian companies but invoices through a non-resident intermediary, the zero-rating may be preserved only under the 'agent' provisions of ETA Sch. IX / QSTA Sch. IX. Input tax credits (ITCs/QST refunds) are available where the zero-rated supply is made in the course of a commercial activity; where the LP is a 'financial institution' or makes exempt supplies, partial restrictions apply.

  • Written services agreement evidencing the non-resident recipient and stated place of supply
  • Commercial invoice with explicit zero-rating indicator and identification of the offshore recipient
  • Certificate of residency of the non-resident client (tax residency certificate or equivalent KYC evidence)
  • Inward remittance evidence (bank wire swift confirmation, SWIFT MT103, or corporate payment trail in foreign currency)
  • FINTRAC KYC/AML file for the registered agent engagement, including UBO declarations for the offshore entity
  • Quebec Registraire des entreprises extract confirming the LP's active registration and general partner details
  • Internal work order or service completion evidence proving no supply of Canadian real property or tangible goods occurred
  • Where incidental goods are transmitted, a valid Canadian Customs Export Declaration (under Customs Act) and G7/G13 export reporting

Neither Canada nor Quebec mandates real-time fiscal clearance, Digital Fiscal Stamp validation, or API-based clearance at issuance. Electronic invoices (EDI, XML, PDF) are legally valid if they are recorded with reliable audit trails under CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.6 and Revenu Québec Verification Guide TP-246.4. Revenu Québec mandates that any electronic invoicing system preserve an unalterable, sequential control ledger and produce accessible records within a reasonable period; there is currently no QR-code stamping requirement. Businesses must file GST/HST and QST returns electronically via Revenu Québec's portal using structured XML/EIF (Fichier d'échange informatisé) under the Standard d'échange informatique (SEI) protocol; payments are settled through Interac, wire transfer, or pre-authorized debit.

Revenu Québec and CRA jointly audit zero-rated exports through desk reviews and field examinations. Under ETA s.285 and s.296 and QST Act equivalent provisions, knowingly claiming a false zero-rating, facilitating a fraudulent ITC refund, or providing false documentation triggers penalties of 25% to 250% of the tax amount sought, plus daily interest under s.280. Reassessment windows extend from the normal 4-year period to 7 years where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. 'Gross negligence' penalties under ITA s.163.2 can reach 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under ETA s.326 (evasion) and s.327 (false statements) carries fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals, and large corporate fines. A clawback operates retroactively to the first day of the zero-rated supply, invalidating all ITCs/refund claims associated with the supply unless the LP can reconstruct contemporaneous export evidence within 30 days of the assessment notice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations: the Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital transactions. Profit remittance by a Québec limited partnership is unrestricted; non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), subject to treaty relief. Capital repatriation is likewise unrestricted, subject to general anti-avoidance and, where applicable, section 116 compliance for dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. Exporters may retain, invest, or repatriate export receipts in any currency without mandatory conversion timelines or ratios. Income recognition and foreign-currency reporting obligations apply under the Income Tax Act and, for payments received or made in the ordinary course, under reporting requirements for cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; exchange-control authority is not vested in a single agency because Canada maintains no general foreign-exchange-control system. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates banks and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises Québec financial-services firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions are not authorized to access Canadian deposit-taking infrastructure or direct membership in Payments Canada. Such entities may obtain correspondent banking through a Canadian Schedule I bank only if they are licensed or registered as money-services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where operating in Québec, are registered with the AMF. Their use is viable for ancillary FX and payment processing, but not as a substitute for a Canadian clearing bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners are routed through SWIFT MT103 or equivalent priority-wire messages to the partnership's Canadian operational or escrow account. The remitter must include a clear payment reference identifying the contributing limited partner, the partnership name, and the purpose, for example 'capital contribution under partnership agreement dated [date]'. Canadian banks require documentary backup: executed partnership agreement, resolutions approving the capital call, a notarized certificate of incumbency, and confirmation of the business purpose 'registered agent and company secretarial services'. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting applies. For cross-border electronic funds transfers, the bank must obtain and record beneficial ownership information. Where funds originate from a partner's bank account in a foreign jurisdiction, the receiving Canadian bank will apply correspondent-banking due diligence; the instruction must include a valid purpose code or standardized remittance advice used by the sending jurisdiction, together with the full legal name and address of the sender and beneficiary.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and operational payments are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for high-value cross-border wires, SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank transfers, and commercial letters of credit (MT700 and related amendments) where goods or services are being settled under documentary credit. Domestic Canadian-dollar clearing may use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail for urgent low-value payments. USD disbursements from Canadian accounts are cleared through the bank's US clearing network or via correspondent banks in the United States. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Quebec limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not itself subject to ordinary Canadian corporate income tax. Income is allocated to partners. For a taxable Canadian corporation carrying on business in Quebec, the general combined federal and Quebec corporate income tax rate is generally 26.5%: 15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec. The applicable rate for a partner depends on its status, residency, legal form, income type, and available credits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard Canadian Part XIII withholding tax rate on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident is 25%. A treaty commonly reduces the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders meeting ownership and other treaty conditions. A limited partnership distribution is not automatically a dividend; its treatment follows the underlying income, payment, and anti-avoidance rules. A corporate partner distributing after-tax profits may create a separate dividend withholding obligation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 return and related slips are generally due within 5 months after the partnership fiscal period-end, with a 6-month deadline applying in specified circumstances, including certain partnerships with a non-resident partner. The return should be filed within 5 months unless the applicable CRA rule confirms the 6-month deadline. Form TP-600 is generally due within 6 months after the partnership fiscal period-end. A partnership with a December 31 year-end therefore generally files by May 31 for the CRA return and June 30 for the Quebec return, subject to the applicable statutory rule and electronic-filing requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 is the principal threshold relevant to Form T106 reporting for aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents. It is not a general safe-harbour threshold below which transfer-pricing documentation is unnecessary. Contemporaneous documentation is required under Income Tax Act section 247 for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a qualifying multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year, or the prescribed Canadian-dollar equivalent.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate, comprehensive Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes in certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under section 245 GAAR, the principal-purpose test in treaties, beneficial-ownership analysis, section 247 transfer-pricing rules, corporate-residence principles, and specific anti-avoidance provisions. A registered-agent or corporate-secretarial business should maintain genuine Canadian commercial support for its claimed functions, including personnel or contractors, decision-making, contracts, customer records, books, banking controls, risk management, and evidence that fees reflect functions performed and risks assumed. Quebec tax authorities may challenge artificial allocations, sham arrangements, nominee structures, or unsupported offshore management charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate false reporting

Statutory sanction: Federal tax evasion under Income Tax Act section 239 may result in a fine generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction, and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment, in addition to tax, interest, and civil penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, general partners, and controlling persons may face criminal prosecution and personal liability where they authorized, assented to, or participated in the conduct. Imprisonment is possible after conviction. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from bail, probation, immigration, or court orders. Civil restitution and recovery of unpaid tax may be ordered or pursued.
Failure to remit payroll, Part XIII withholding, GST/HST, or QST

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unremitted amounts, interest, late-remittance penalties, and reporting penalties. Failure to deduct or remit can result in penalties commonly ranging from 10% to 20% of the amount not deducted or remitted, with higher penalties for repeated or deliberate failures. GST/HST and QST assessments may include tax, interest, gross-negligence penalties, and director-liability assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable under Income Tax Act section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and under Excise Tax Act section 323 for unremitted GST/HST, subject to statutory due-diligence and limitation rules. Quebec director-liability provisions may similarly apply to QST and source deductions. Personal recovery can include asset seizure and garnishment; imprisonment requires separate criminal conduct.
Shell company, sham partnership, or abusive nominee structure

Statutory sanction: CRA or Revenu Quebec may recharacterize the arrangement, deny deductions, reallocate income, apply GAAR, impose transfer-pricing adjustments, assess withholding tax, interest, gross-negligence penalties, and prosecute fraud or tax evasion. Corporate registry, beneficial-ownership, and partnership records may be reviewed or compelled.

Director personal liability: Limited liability does not protect persons who commit fraud, act as general partners, direct unlawful conduct, or use the entity as an instrument of evasion. Courts may impose personal civil liability and restitution. Travel bans are not automatic and require a legal basis such as bail or court order; imprisonment depends on conviction for an applicable offence.
Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties may reach up to CAD 100,000 for an individual and CAD 500,000 for an entity per violation, depending on the violation and classification. Criminal money-laundering and terrorist-financing offences can result in substantial fines and imprisonment, including imprisonment of up to 10 years for indictable money-laundering offences in applicable circumstances.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, directors, partners, and beneficial owners may face personal administrative or criminal exposure where they directed, consented to, or participated in non-compliance. Proceeds may be restrained, seized, or forfeited. Bail, probation, or court orders may restrict travel; a travel ban is not an automatic penalty.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and offshore profit shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under Income Tax Act section 247, deny or recharacterize deductions, assess withholding tax, and impose a transfer-pricing adjustment penalty generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where the statutory conditions are met. Interest and gross-negligence penalties may also apply. Mutual-agreement procedures may be available under an applicable treaty but do not suspend all filing and payment obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil claims and criminal exposure if the pricing was knowingly fraudulent or formed part of evasion. Mere commercial mispricing generally creates entity-level tax exposure rather than automatic personal liability, but veil protection can fail for fraud, sham conduct, or knowing participation.
Failure to maintain corporate, partnership, and beneficial-ownership records

Statutory sanction: CRA, Revenu Quebec, FINTRAC, and corporate registries may impose information demands, penalties, reassessments, denial of deductions, registration consequences, and compliance orders. Inadequate records can shift evidentiary burdens and support gross-negligence findings.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and designated compliance personnel may be personally liable for statutory penalties or prosecution where records were deliberately destroyed, falsified, or withheld. Civil restitution and asset-preservation orders may follow; imprisonment requires an applicable conviction.
