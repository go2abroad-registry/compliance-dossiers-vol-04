Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration portal: Canada.ca (Business Registration Online, My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE). Ontario corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under a single-administration agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

An Ontario Business Corporation must procure a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. For GST/HST, a separate RT program account is required. Registration is executed online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) for a corporation. The procedure for non-resident-owned entities: (1) incorporate under the Ontario Business Corporations Act or Canada Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain a BN by submitting the corporation's legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office address in Ontario, board of directors and officer details, and the corporation's estimated taxable supply revenue; (3) determine GST/HST registration requirement: registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; below that, voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate exports; (4) apply for the GST/HST account (RT), the corporate income tax account (RC), and, if any, payroll (RP) and import/export (RM) accounts; (5) for non-resident directors or shareholders, provide a completed Form RC458? (Residency declaration) is not statutory, but a certificate of non-resident status and a Canadian address for service is required for the CRA. Non-resident corporations are also required to designate a person resident in Canada for service of process and notices under the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; every Business Corporation is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Part I, 15% federal basic rate plus Ontario 10% provincial rate on net taxable income; higher rates for non-CCPCs). A resident Canadian corporation whose shares are not controlled by non-residents may hold 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) status, giving access to the small business deduction, but no election is required if the facts of control qualify. Available statutory elections for this export services corporation include: (i) the corporation and its Canadian subsidiaries may elect under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to file a consolidated GST/HST return; (ii) a non-resident corporate shareholder may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act to compute Part I tax on Canadian-source business income on a net basis rather than being subject to Part XIII withholding on gross distributions; (iii) a closely related non-resident corporation may elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to avoid the 50% self-assessment rule on non-arm's length taxable supplies in Canada; (iv) a voluntary GST/HST registration election under subsection 240(2.1) may be filed to bring the non-resident into the net tax system; and (v) hybrid mismatch arrangements are not elective — sections 18.2, 18.3, 17.2 and 17.3 of the Income Tax Act apply automatically to deny deductions or include income in respect of offshore hybrid mismatch payments, subject to a three-year transition exception for existing arrangements that must be documented at inception.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current account and capital account. There is no prior exchange-control approval requirement for capital contributions, dividend distributions, trade payments, or shareholder loans sourced from abroad. Profit remittance is constrained only by Canadian income tax mechanics: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% on dividends, reduced to 5% on dividends paid to a corporate shareholder resident in a treaty jurisdiction holding at least 10% of voting power where the Canada-U.S. or other applicable tax treaty so provides, and by corporate law tests of solvency and board authorization. Return of paid-up capital is generally non-taxable and can be repatriated without convertibility restriction, but excessive corporate distributions above paid-up capital can be re-characterized as dividends under subsection 84(1) of the Income Tax Act. The Investment Canada Act may require notification or net-benefit review only if a non-Canadian acquires control of an existing Canadian business; a newly formed Ontario corporation receiving ordinary working capital injections is not itself a regulated acquisition of control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. A Canadian exporter of 3D game art services may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in a Canadian bank foreign-currency account or may convert them to CAD at commercial rates. All export earnings must be accurately reported for income tax purposes under the accrual method, and underlying contracts and invoices must support the taxable revenue position. Currency transported physically in or out of Canada exceeding C$10,000 must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) upon crossing. FINTRAC reporting obligations arise for large cash transactions, electronic funds transfers over C$10,000 in a single transaction, and suspicious transactions, but there is no surrender or exchange control timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; exchange control administration is not centralized in Canada. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the national financial intelligence unit and AML/CFT supervisor. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally incorporated banks. The Foreign Exchange Control regime itself has been substantially liberalized: Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement and does not maintain exchange controls on current or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium for ancillary receivables and vendor/supplier payments; Low for a primary corporate treasury account. Offshore digital EMI institutions and cross-border payment service providers are not authorized banks under the Bank Act and cannot offer deposit-taking accounts in Canada unless they are appropriately accredited under Canadian financial services law. A licensed Canadian Schedule I bank is mandatory as the main settlement and liquidity nexus for a cross-border 3D art BPO. Credentialed fintech PSPs, including digital EMIs and money services businesses, may be used for low-value inbound client payments from platforms such as PayPal, but they must be registered or exempt under the Retail Payment Activities Act and cannot replace the Canadian bank clearing relationship. The entity should integrate such PSPs through a controlled receivable sweep into the primary bank account rather than as an independent treasury buffer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident shareholders or parent companies are cleared through SWIFT MT103/MT202 remittances to the Ontario operating account at a Schedule I bank. Canada does not operate a purpose-code approval regime, but the remittance information should specify 'Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan Disbursement' and the precise Canadian beneficiary bank details, including institution number, transit number, account number, and SWIFT/BIC. The bank will screen the transaction for sanctions and suspicious activity; the corporate client must be prepared to produce the shareholder resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and beneficial ownership register upon request. For payments above C$10,000, FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting may be required, and any large, unusual, or non-commercial flows must be documented to avoid suspicion of money laundering. Funds may be held in CAD or swapped into USD through the bank's spot desk; no exchange allocation permit is needed.Outbound vendor, subcontractor, and licensing disbursements should be settled primarily through SWIFT MT103 transfer instructions from the Canadian bank treasury platform. For larger or recurring procurements with foreign 3D art tool vendors, software licensors, or selective offshore studios, a Commercial Letter of Credit opened by the Canadian bank's trade finance desk provides payment security, particularly when the counterparty requires documentary evidence of performance. Documentary collections and bank guarantees may also be used for milestone-based outsourcing agreements. Domestic Canadian payroll and operating expenses are settled through EFT/ACH-style pre-authorized debits and wire credits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate value of controlled transactions between the Canadian entity and a non-resident related party in a taxation year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is CAD 750 million consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a statutory Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and CRA administrative doctrine require transactions to have a genuine business purpose; artificial structures or booking of profits in jurisdictions without economic substance are subject to reassessment, penalties, and the application of anti-avoidance provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act; corporation subject to professional restitution and repayment of all taxes, interest, and penalties

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized or acquiesced in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid taxes; they may be subject to personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, and imprisonment
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess the corporation, deny the small business deduction, impose GAAR penalties of 25% of any tax benefit obtained, and apply transfer pricing reassessments

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the unpaid taxes and penalties to the extent of the benefit obtained, plus civil restitution and prohibition from acting as directors
Part XIII Withholding Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100% of the amount required to be withheld, as well as fines and interest; CRA can audit and assess the corporation directly

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding amounts; personal restitution up to CAD 100,000 or more depending on the amount; travel bans for repeated non-compliance
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Payments Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to CAD 1 million per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; eventual cessation order against the corporation

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to establish significant compliance procedures face personal fines and imprisonment of up to 2 years; civil restitution and travel bans under the AML legal frameworks
