Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal for federal Business Numbers and GST/HST accounts; Revenu Quebec, including the clicSÉQUR and online tax-services portals, for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) registration and administration.

E-government filing portal

A corporation must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or through the applicable federal or provincial incorporation process. It must register for a GST/HST program account (RT) when required or voluntarily eligible. A corporation carrying on business in Quebec must generally register for QST with Revenu Quebec when it is not a small supplier and makes taxable supplies in Quebec; the resulting QST identification number is separate from the federal BN. Non-resident entities must provide legal-entity, incorporation, Canadian business-activity, responsible-person, banking, authorized-representative, and supporting commercial documents. GST/HST and QST registration does not itself establish corporate residence, permanent establishment, or income-tax residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian corporation is generally taxed as a separate legal entity. Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for corporations. A corporation may elect for specific statutory treatments, including capital-cost allowance elections, fiscal-period elections, functional-currency reporting where permitted, and certain GST/HST or QST elections. Canadian unlimited liability corporations may in limited circumstances be eligible for entity-classification treatment under foreign law, but this is not a general Canadian pass-through election. Partnership, trust, amalgamation, wind-up, and rollover provisions are separate statutory regimes and do not convert an ordinary corporation into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding certain supplies, measured under the applicable small-supplier rules. For GST/HST, registration is generally required when the threshold is exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. For QST, the principal small-supplier threshold is generally CAD 30,000 for taxable supplies in the relevant circumstances. Voluntary registration may be available below the threshold, subject to statutory conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated for GST/HST when the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or shipment to a destination outside Canada and retention of evidence establishing export. Goods sold in Canada to a non-resident are not automatically zero-rated; the applicable export, delivery, possession, or prescribed non-resident rules must be met. In Quebec, qualifying exports are generally zero-rated for QST under the Quebec legislation. Offshore services may qualify for zero-rating when supplied to a non-resident and the statutory service, use, performance, and exclusion conditions are satisfied. Bitumen and asphalt products exported in containerized shipments should be supported by consistent commercial terms, customs data, transport documents, destination evidence, and payment records. Input-tax refunds or input tax credits require valid supplier documentation and direct attribution to commercial activities, subject to the applicable GST/HST and QST rules.

  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or applicable Canadian export-reporting record, including the applicable CERS or other approved reporting data where required
  • Commercial invoice and purchase or sales contract identifying the goods, parties, value, currency, Incoterms, destination, and tax treatment
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, rail waybill, truck manifest, or equivalent carrier document
  • Packing list, container number, seal number, weight, product description, and quantity records

  • Proof of payment, bank remittance records, and accounts-receivable reconciliation

  • Foreign-customer identification and evidence that the goods were delivered or made available outside Canada where required
  • GST/HST and QST sales invoices or credit notes showing the applicable zero-rated treatment and transaction references

Canada and Quebec do not generally impose a universal real-time electronic-invoice clearance or digital-tax-stamping mandate for ordinary domestic business-to-business transactions. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information, maintain books and records, calculate and collect applicable GST/HST and QST, file returns electronically where required or selected, and retain supporting records. Electronic data interchange and digital records are permitted if they remain accessible, reliable, and auditable. Export transactions should retain customs, shipping, contractual, payment, and delivery evidence sufficient to establish zero-rating.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days or within another fixed period. Zero-rating may be denied or clawed back through reassessment where export conditions or evidentiary requirements are not established. The supplier may become liable for uncollected GST/HST and QST, applicable interest, and gross-negligence or other administrative penalties. False statements, deliberate evasion, fabricated documentation, or improper refund claims may trigger civil penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act, the Quebec tax legislation, and applicable customs law. Customs violations may also result in seizure, forfeiture, administrative monetary penalties, reassessment of duties and taxes, and importer or exporter compliance action. Records should generally be retained for at least six years after the end of the relevant tax year, subject to longer periods required by reassessment, objection, litigation, or specific statutory rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial, investment, financing, and repatriation transactions. Canada has no general capital-account approval requirement, mandatory foreign-exchange surrender regime, or restriction on non-resident ownership of a Canadian corporation. Current-account payments, including imports, exports, services, interest, dividends, and other ordinary business remittances, may generally be made through Canadian financial institutions subject to bank compliance, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax withholding, corporate authorization, and documentary support. Profit remittances to non-residents are generally permitted after determination of legally distributable amounts and satisfaction of applicable Canadian withholding-tax obligations. Dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and related-party payments may require withholding under the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief and prescribed documentation, including a valid beneficial-owner and treaty-residence analysis. Dividends must not be paid if prohibited by the Canada Business Corporations Act or applicable Quebec corporate statute, including solvency and capital-maintenance limitations. Intercompany pricing must comply with Canadian transfer-pricing rules and arm's-length requirements. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, restricted goods, suspicious counterparties, or undisclosed beneficial owners may be blocked, delayed, reported, or rejected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency export receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts, receive them in Canadian dollars, or convert them at commercially agreed rates. Banks may request invoices, bills of lading, customs records, contracts, beneficial-owner information, and the commercial purpose of transfers. International electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, including multiple linked transfers meeting the applicable threshold within the prescribed period, are subject to financial-institution reporting to FINTRAC under Canadian anti-money-laundering rules. Export-control, sanctions, customs, tax, and trade-reporting obligations remain separate from foreign-exchange conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls; applicable federal oversight also includes the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for trade-related controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collection, payment, and foreign-currency functions; Low as a substitute for a primary Canadian operating bank or documentary trade-finance provider. An offshore electronic-money institution generally cannot accept Canadian deposits, conduct deposit-taking, or provide regulated payment services in Canada merely by holding an offshore authorization. Viability depends on the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian registration or exemption status, correspondent-bank access, sanctions controls, Canadian payment-system access, data and outsourcing arrangements, and customer-risk policy. Canadian-dollar settlement may require a regulated Canadian financial institution or approved payment service provider. Offshore EMI balances may receive less Canadian deposit protection, and banks may apply enhanced due diligence to funds routed through payment intermediaries. Use only for documented treasury purposes after legal confirmation of the provider's Canadian permissions and contractual segregation of client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through the Canadian corporation's account at a regulated Canadian bank by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent correspondent-bank wire, with the legal entity as beneficiary and the remitter identified as the shareholder or approved lender. Use a clear payment reference such as 'equity subscription', 'shareholder loan', or 'capital contribution'; Canada does not impose a universal statutory purpose-code format, but the bank may require an internal purpose classification. Before receipt, provide the subscription or loan agreement, board and shareholder approvals, capitalization table, sender and beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and currency, and any intercompany pricing or withholding analysis. Reconcile the received amount to the corporate ledger and issue shares or record the loan only under executed corporate documentation. Do not route capital through employee, director, customer, supplier, personal, or unrelated third-party accounts. Confirm correspondent-bank requirements, sanctions screening, payment transparency, intermediary-bank fees, and return procedures. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained or converted; any required accounting, tax, and foreign-exchange gain or loss treatment should be recorded under Canadian standards and applicable tax rules.Use regulated Canadian-bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire transfers for ordinary supplier, freight, insurance, customs, and service payments, supported by purchase contracts, commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, customs documentation, beneficiary verification, and sanctions screening. Use Commercial Letters of Credit, including irrevocable documentary LCs confirmed where commercially required, for higher-value or higher-risk bitumen shipments; define compliant presentation requirements, shipment terms, inspection certificates, origin documents, insurance evidence, and discrepancy procedures under the agreed ICC rules, typically UCP 600 for documentary credits and ISBP practices. For bank-to-bank settlement, use authenticated SWIFT messaging and segregate payment approval, beneficiary-master-file maintenance, and reconciliation duties.
