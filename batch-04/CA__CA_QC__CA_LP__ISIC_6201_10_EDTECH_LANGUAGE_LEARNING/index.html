Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement and no escrow account requirement exists under the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance under the Fire Safety Act (CQLR c S-3.1) and applicable Montréal by-laws, with a municipal fire safety inspection for the office premises; occupational health registration with CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail) and implementation of a workplace health and safety program under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1); structural load inspection not required for standard office occupancy; the building lessor must provide a valid occupancy certificate and proof of compliance with the Québec Construction Code.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement and no escrow account requirement exists under the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian and Quebec income tax purposes under Income Tax Act subsection 96(2) and the Civil Code of Quebec partnership rules. Income, gains, losses, and investment tax credits flow through to partners, while the partnership files an information return (federal T5013 and Quebec RL-16). For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is itself a taxable 'person' and a registrant to the extent it makes taxable supplies in Canada/Quebec, and it obtains its own BN/NEQ-based tax account. Territorial scope: the LP is subject to Canadian taxation only to the extent it carries on a business or realizes taxable Canadian-source income; a non-resident partner is taxable on the partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification election equivalent to US Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3. The limited partnership's pass-through status is statutory and mandatory. Available elections relevant to the LP: (i) subsection 249.1(6) election to adopt a partnership fiscal period matching majority interest partners; (ii) subsection 96(3) election for transfers of partnership property at agreed tax values; (iii) Excise Tax Act section 156 election to treat supplies between closely related registrants as nil consideration for GST/HST/QST purposes; and (iv) various GST/HST and QST election forms for sales, self-supply, and qualifying real property acquisitions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: Registration is mandatory once the worldwide annual taxable supplies of the registrant (excluding certain zero-rated and financial supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 for GST/HST, and the equivalent small-supplier threshold applies for QST under Revenu Québec rules. Exceptions and mandatory registration rules apply to non-residents who solicit business in Canada or supply prescribed digital services to persons in Quebec even below the threshold in specific circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated if supplied and delivered outside Canada, or subject to continuous exportation in a carrier that exports them prior to use in Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated when the recipient is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed and the service is not required to be performed in Canada. Digital supply of EdTech language-learning software and automated tutoring services qualifies for zero-rating as a supply of intangible personal property or a service made to a non-resident recipient, provided the recipient receives it outside Canada and does not use it in Canada; records must evidence geographic delivery and non-resident status (e.g., IP address, billing address, app store download jurisdiction).

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A/B13B) where tangible media or goods are exported
  • Customs export clearance record and proof of departure (carrier airway bill, bill of lading, port/customs stamp)
  • Commercial invoice with incoterms and HS/schedule classification
  • Signed contract or terms of service demonstrating non-resident buyer/tutor recipient
  • Country-specific digital delivery logs showing IP locations and download/access records
  • Payment evidence and inward remittance certificate or app store payout statements
  • Packing list and proof of outside-Canada destination for any tangible export component
  • Any certificate of origin or export permit if applicable

Canada and Quebec do not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing validation, or digital stamping for general EdTech B2C/B2B transactions. Registrants must issue tax invoices containing required GST/HST and QST information, preserve electronic records, and file returns electronically through CRA and Revenu Québec online filing/API channels when turnover exceeds prescribed thresholds. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework for e-invoicing is voluntary. For App Store and Play Store sales, the taxpayer must retain Apple/Google payment reports and invoice data to document the GST/HST and QST treatment of each downloadable digital supply.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated exports within the normal reassessment period (normally four years, or longer in the case of negligence/misrepresentation) and claw back the zero-rating, thereby assessing uncollected GST/HST and QST, disallowing input tax credits, and applying statutory interest. Civil penalties include false statement penalties up to 25% of the tax amount (or 50% for gross negligence), failure to file penalties of 5% plus 1% per month, and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines, imprisonment, and punitive damages. CRA may also disregard transactions under anti-avoidance rules when non-resident paper flows are merely formal and no substantive export occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange control law, no prior-approval regime for profit remittances, and no restriction on capital withdrawals or dividends by non-residents. The only material frictions are tax-driven: a non-resident limited partner receiving partnership income characterized as non-active business income is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (base rate 25%, reduced by treaty to commonly 5-15% depending on the partner's residence). Management fees or royalties remitted to non-residents attract 25% withholding unless treaty relief is filed with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). For Quebec, Revenu Quebec applies the same remittance framework, and the LP must hold a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and register for GST/HST/QST where taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Canadian residents may freely retain foreign currency offshore or in Foreign-Currency Bank Accounts (FCBA) at authorized Canadian banks. The only binding obligations are reporting-based: (i) FINTRAC requires Canadian financial intermediaries to report every cross-border electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more; (ii) commercial goods exports from Canada require CBSA export reporting under the Export Reporting and Accounting Regulations when the shipment value exceeds C$2,000 or falls within controlled categories; (iii) EdTech service exports are not subject to CBSA export reporting, but subscription revenues must be included in the partnership's Canadian tax filings and, if selected in Statistics Canada's survey sample, in Balance of Payments reporting forms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and financial-stability authority. There is no centralized foreign-exchange administration; cross-border financial transaction reporting is supervised by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers cross-border currency declarations and export reporting under the Customs Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM. Canada does not use the EU-style EMI licence; offshore digital EMIs are lawful only as auxiliary payment processors, not as full banking rails for a Quebec LP. Any payment service provider settling CAD for the LP must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if operating in Quebec, must also hold the Quebec money-services business permit administered by Revenu Quebec under the Act respecting money-services businesses. Practical blueprint: Apple IAP and Google Play Billing serve as the primary collection layer (Apple/Google treasury entities remit net proceeds in USD/EUR via SWIFT to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian bank account), while a registered Canadian PSP (e.g., Stripe Canada, PayPal Canada, or the bank's own payment gateway) handles direct web-subscription collections. Offshore EMIs cannot provide Canadian settlement accounts or CAD clearing without a Canadian MSB registration and a local presence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all non-resident capital contributions as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) into a segregated capital-injection/operating account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state: CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_LP LIMITED PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE], using ISO 20022 purpose code QUASICAPITAL or CAPT so the beneficiary bank classifies the flow as equity rather than trade or debt. All transfers equal to or exceeding C$10,000 are automatically reported to FINTRAC by the receiving bank. Large inward direct investments may additionally be captured by Statistics Canada Balance of Payments surveys. Supporting documents for the receiving bank: executed capital-call resolution, GP authorization, source-of-funds evidence, a capital-account statement certified by the partnership accountant before drawdown, and, where US partners are present, a US tax characterization memo (PFIC/partnership analysis) included in the KYC file.Core clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all external vendor payables, with USD/EUR clearing executed by the Canadian bank's corresponding banking network (US and European correspondents); (ii) CAD payables to Canadian vendors cleared through Payments Canada Lynx RTGS (high value) and ACSS (retail ACH); (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707/MT720 where deferred-payment trade finance is required for content or licensing procurement; (iv) corporate purchasing cards for low-value SaaS and subscription tooling; and (v) FX conversion executed on the spot market by the bank's treasury desk on the same value date as the MT103 to eliminate the float and dual-currency mismatch from Apple/Google settlement cycles (Apple payouts approximately T+45 days; Google approximately T+30 days). Apple IAP and Google Play Billing remit net proceeds in USD/EUR through Apple Distribution International (Ireland) and Google Payment entities; these must be documented to the bank as on-behalf-of third-party processor flows. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: MEDIUM. Canada does not use the EU-style EMI licence; offshore digital EMIs are lawful only as auxiliary payment processors, not as full banking rails for a Quebec LP. Any payment service provider settling CAD for the LP must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if operating in Quebec, must also hold the Quebec money-services business permit administered by Revenu Quebec under the Act respecting money-services businesses. Practical blueprint: Apple IAP and Google Play Billing serve as the primary collection layer (Apple/Google treasury entities remit net proceeds in USD/EUR via SWIFT to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian bank account), while a registered Canadian PSP (e.g., Stripe Canada, PayPal Canada, or the bank's own payment gateway) handles direct web-subscription collections. Offshore EMIs cannot provide Canadian settlement accounts or CAD clearing without a Canadian MSB registration and a local presence.
