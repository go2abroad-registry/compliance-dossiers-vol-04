Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Hauptabteilung für Mehrwertsteuer; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; online e-services portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch/e-service

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG/SA) obtains a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the cantonal Commercial Register Office upon incorporation; the UID is then activated by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration for VAT purposes and serves as the MWST/TVA number. Foreign enterprises not established in Switzerland but conducting taxable supplies in Switzerland must appoint a Swiss VAT tax representative jointly and severally liable under Art. 97(5) MWSTG, submit the official VAT registration form (application to ESTV), receive a UID/MWST-Nr, and file quarterly or semi-annual VAT returns. Registration is mandatory once taxable worldwide turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per year excluding VAT; voluntary registration is available to non-established foreign entities for cost-recovery purposes only where the volume of Swiss-source supplies does not exceed the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (an Aktiengesellschaft is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Swiss federal, cantonal and communal income taxes on its net profits; shareholders are separately taxed on dividends and capital gains; no pass-through or check-the-box treatment is recognised in Swiss tax law)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no general hybrid-mismatch election is available. Available Swiss statutory tax elections include: VAT small-business exception waiver election (Art. 13(2) MWSTG); VAT group taxation election for closely controlled Swiss companies (Art. 11 MWSTG); simplified saldo taxation of VAT input/output balances (Art. 37 MWSTG); election between effective rendering of accounts and total-saldo flat rates for transport and certain trades; and, for direct tax purposes, specific deductions such as participation relief and notional-interest-deduction provisions under cantonal tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual net worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding VAT), unless explicitly exempt under MWSTG; suppliers below threshold may waive exemption and register voluntarily

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported used construction excavators is available under Art. 18(1)(a)-(c) MWSTG where the AG supplies movable physical goods and transports or arranges transport so that the goods are physically removed from Switzerland and delivered to a foreign recipient abroad, and the goods are not used or consumed in Switzerland. Services directly connected with the exported goods (e.g., warranty or repair services performed abroad by a foreign subcontractor) may qualify under Art. 19(1) MWSTG where the service recipient is domiciled abroad and the service is not effectively used in Switzerland. The exporter must substantiate the export with documentary evidence, obtainable by the customs declaration release and by confirming delivery abroad; export invoices must state that the supply is zero-rated under the Swiss VAT Act and must show the recipient's foreign address.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (NCTS customs declaration and/or ATS-ch-Overlay proof of exit from the Swiss customs territory)
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, CMR consignment note or air waybill evidencing physical delivery to a foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT credit evidence confirming that the purchase price was remitted from the foreign buyer's account; where irrevocable letter of credit (LC) is used, the LC and settlement statement are also required

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital-stamping or continuous-transaction-control regime. B2B and B2C invoicing by Swiss VAT-registered businesses must contain the statutory VAT invoice particulars (Art. 26 MWSTG); QR-billing (QR-Rechnung) is the standard structured payment instrument for CHF invoices; electronic data interchange formats such as Swiss e-invoicing standards (Swico/SEDEX, FACITO) are permitted but not compulsory. No API-based government clearance is currently required.

Zero-rated export treatment is subject to retrospective audit by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration, which can reassess the supply at 8.1% Swiss VAT plus default interest if the exporter cannot produce complete export-proof documentation within the statutory ten-year retention period. Intentional or deliberate understatement of VAT or false export documentation constitutes tax evasion (Art. 186 MWSTG) and is punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded, plus any imposed ancillary tax penalties; negligent violation attracts fines and interest under Arts. 173 and 175 MWSTG. Customs export misdeclarations may trigger separate Swiss customs penalties and confiscation measures. In practice, for commodity trades such as used construction excavators, export documentation and price repatriation should be completed within 180 days from export declaration to mitigate heightened AML compliance scrutiny and to preserve the zero-rating evidentiary record.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility. The Swiss franc (CHF) is a freely floating, freely convertible currency under both current and capital account OECD liberalisation principles and Swiss constitutional guarantees on free movement of capital. There is no exchange control statute, no prior notification or approval requirement for purchasing or selling foreign currency, no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency accounts in Switzerland, and no limitation on dividend, profit, interest, or liquidation proceeds remittance from a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft to non-resident shareholders. The only friction is the ordinary Swiss anticipatory withholding tax of 35% on dividends, which is reduced or refunded under applicable double tax treaties, and there is no requirement to repatriate trade receivables, no mandatory hedging rule, and no license gate for FDI inflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no fixed repatriation timeline, and no duty to deposit export earnings with the SNB for used hydraulic excavators, rollers, or any other exported goods. Export proceeds in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in Swiss or foreign banks. The only passive reporting duties are: (i) SNB statistical balance-of-payments surveys for enterprises with quarterly cross-border transaction volume above the CHF 1.5 million reporting threshold; (ii) customs export value declarations in the TARES/NCTS process administered by the Federal Customs Administration; (iii) AMLA cash transaction reporting for cash movements exceeding CHF 10,000; and (iv) SECO embargo compliance, notably the prohibition on trade and receivable collection involving sanctioned Russian or Belarusian counterparties in the construction machinery sector.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank; French: Banque nationale suisse) as the central bank and monetary authority of the Swiss Confederation, acting alongside the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking conduct and AML supervision, and the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) for international sanctions and export controls. Switzerland maintains no separate foreign exchange administration; the SNB does not issue foreign exchange licences, approvals, or registration certificates for corporate current-account or capital-account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium as a supplementary payment conduit. A FINMA-authorised FinTech licence holder under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, or an EU/EEA PSD2 electronic money institution (e.g. Airwallex, Revolut Business), may be used for low-value incidental payment traffic, but such providers cannot issue SWIFT documentary letters of credit, do not provide a bankable capital-deposit confirmation acceptable to Swiss notaries, and generally lack the correspondent banking depth required for six-to-seven-figure used construction equipment settlements. Offshore EMIs also create AML friction: Swiss banks apply enhanced due diligence where corporate funds cycle through non-regulated payment intermediaries, and several first-tier Swiss banks impose contractual prohibitions on routing concentration of trade inflows through EMI wrappers. The compliant architecture is a fully licensed Swiss bank as principal treasury and clearing anchor, with any EMI layer restricted to lightweight operational micropayments and expressly disclosed in the KYC file.

If residual risk persists, the treasury may pre-fund the account through a Swiss notary or fiduciary escrow pending full clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant capital injection protocol for the Swiss AG is as follows: (1) the board of directors resolves the capital formation and designates the receiving bank; (2) the AG opens a dedicated capital escrow sub-account, commonly known as the Sperrkonto, at the chosen Tier-1 Swiss bank; (3) the shareholder wires the statutory liberating fraction of the CHF 100,000 minimum share capital from a personal account at a licensed bank, never from a non-regulated PSP or cash-accepting intermediary; (4) the incoming SWIFT MT103 must carry an explicit purpose designation in field 70, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CASH' or clear wording such as 'Liberierung Aktienkapital / contribution to share capital of CH_AG'; (5) the bank performs the AMLA Article 6 source-of-funds review, sanctions screens the remitter, and thereafter issues the formal paid-up capital confirmation (Bankbestätigung) attached to the notarised incorporation deed; (6) the notary files the capital evidence with the Swiss Commercial Register; (7) only after registration may the escrow account be reclassified as a free operational account. For subsequent shareholder loans, the AG must execute a written loan agreement, apply an arm's length interest rate consistent with Swiss tax practice, and report the liability to the cantonal tax authority to prevent recharacterisation as hidden equity; no interest deduction is available on disguised equity.Outbound clearing rails for used excavator trading: (i) open-account settlements and pre-payments via SWIFT MT103 with UETR tracking, beneficiary sanctions pre-screening, and invoice matching; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfers in EUR for low and medium-value payments to European counterparties holding SEPA IBANs; (iii) documentary collections (CAD/DP) via SWIFT MT400/MT410 for control of title against payment in high-value export contracts with EM counterparties; (iv) commercial letters of credit issued by the Swiss bank under SWIFT MT700/MT710 pursuant to UCP 600, with transport and commercial documents checked against L/C terms, suitable for sales to Central Asian and Middle Eastern construction companies; (v) Bank Payment Obligations (BPO) under the ISO 20022 trade services utility for open-account corridors where both counterparties are onboarded; and (vi) supplier disbursements to auction houses and OEM spare-part vendors via MT103 with tax-invoice matching and correct INCO clause references. Settlement in restricted emerging currencies should be avoided; USD and EUR are the preferred settlement currencies for equipment sourced from Japanese, EU, or North American auctions.Vanilla OTC FX forwards in CHF/EUR and CHF/USD under an ISDA 2002 or Swiss Master Agreement, with tenors up to 12 months for trade-payment certaintyOTC FX swaps (swap-in and swap-out) allowing short-term rolling of export proceeds between CHF and hard currencies without exposing the AG to long-dated riskCross-currency swaps (CIRS) converting trade-finance interest and principal liabilities between CHF, EUR, and USD to match the underlying equipment purchase and sale cash flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in liquid emerging-market currencies (offshore CNY, KZT, UZS, NGN, EGP, TRY) executed through the Swiss bank's global trading desk, subject to bankability and market depthOTC vanilla FX options (calls and puts) and zero-cost collar structures hedging the CHF profit margin on committed US-dollar or euro-denominated excavator sale contracts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium as a supplementary payment conduit. A FINMA-authorised FinTech licence holder under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, or an EU/EEA PSD2 electronic money institution (e.g. Airwallex, Revolut Business), may be used for low-value incidental payment traffic, but such providers cannot issue SWIFT documentary letters of credit, do not provide a bankable capital-deposit confirmation acceptable to Swiss notaries, and generally lack the correspondent banking depth required for six-to-seven-figure used construction equipment settlements. Offshore EMIs also create AML friction: Swiss banks apply enhanced due diligence where corporate funds cycle through non-regulated payment intermediaries, and several first-tier Swiss banks impose contractual prohibitions on routing concentration of trade inflows through EMI wrappers. The compliant architecture is a fully licensed Swiss bank as principal treasury and clearing anchor, with any EMI layer restricted to lightweight operational micropayments and expressly disclosed in the KYC file.
