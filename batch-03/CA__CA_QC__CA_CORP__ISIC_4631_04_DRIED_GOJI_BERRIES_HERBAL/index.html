Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration via the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax schedules, portal https://www.revenuquebec.ca; Registraire des entreprises du Québec — issuance of the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), portal https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Quebec Business Corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (or both), and obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec; (2) if federally incorporated, complete extra-provincial registration with the Registraire des entreprises when the registered office or a place of business is located in Quebec; (3) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or via the CRA Business Registration Online service — non-residents with Canadian nexus may use Form RC4020 and must appoint a representative for service; (4) activate the GST/HST account (RT), the corporate tax program account (RC), and any import/export account (RM); (5) register the QST account (RD program) with Revenu Québec once taxable supplies are made in Quebec; (6) retain the certificate of incorporation, articles, NEQ confirmation, BN, and QST registration in the statutory corporate file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a per se taxable person under the Income Tax Act and may not elect pass-through treatment; resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income, while the territorial scope for non-resident corporations limits Canadian tax to Canadian-source income (subject to treaty relief). Quebec levies a separate provincial corporate income tax on income allocable to Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification election; corporate status is statutory. Relevant elections include: subsection 85(1) Income Tax Act rollover election on incorporation or asset transfers; section 156 Excise Tax Act election for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nominal consideration for GST/HST; early GST/HST registration election for small suppliers under section 233 of the Excise Tax Act; the associated-enterprise QST election under section 21 of the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; and the hybrid-mismatch deduction restrictions under sections 18.4–18.6 Income Tax Act, under which Canada applies structured-arrangement rules rather than elective relief for hybrid mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the small supplier threshold for GST/HST is based on the value of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated exports) in the current calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; the QST threshold is C$30,000 of taxable supplies made in Quebec over the same look-back periods. This bulk exporter's export revenues count toward the threshold, making GST/HST and QST registration mandatory once C$30,000 is exceeded; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (i) goods exported by the registrant supplier to a non-resident consignee outside Canada (section 1); and (ii) services supplied to non-residents, including logistics and preparation services directly related to the exported goods (section 7). The dried goji berry and herbal botanical exports qualify when legal ownership passes to the non-resident buyer, the goods are shipped directly from Quebec to a foreign destination, and the exporter maintains the prescribed documentary evidence. The registrant may also claim input tax credits (refunds) for GST/HST and QST paid on related inputs, subject to the same substantiation standard.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) electronic transmission (G13 form) completed by the exporter or licensed customs broker
  • Commercial invoice with full botanical description, HS classification (HS 0813 for dried goji berries / HS 1211 for herbal botanicals), quantity, unit value, Incoterms, and foreign destination
  • Bill of lading (ocean, air, or truck) or other contract of carriage proving departure from Canada and arrival at a foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 wire-transfer record, or bank credit advice evidencing settlement by the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and certificate of origin for the herbal consignment
  • GST/HST and QST export ledger reconciliation, CRA export sales listing, and retained input tax credit notes

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST or QST. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper and electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) that comply with section 223 of the Excise Tax Act and the equivalent QST invoicing provisions; the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoice network is voluntary at this stage. Export sellers must retain the original invoice, export declaration, and carrier documentation in readable electronic form to substantiate zero-rating.

CRA and Revenu Québec auditors reverse zero-rating where the export documentation bundle is incomplete or untimely, assessing GST/HST and QST on the full invoice value with arrears interest under the Excise Tax Act and section 285 gross-negligence penalties (up to 25% of the understated tax or a C$250 minimum, whichever is greater). Deliberate false claims attract criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 380 of the Criminal Code, plus revocation of GST/HST registration under section 241. CRA may also impose transfer-pricing penalties (10% of gross mispricing) under subsection 247(3) on non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, and absence of FX repatriation within 180 days is treated by auditors as an indicator of unreported foreign revenue or unremitted export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible currency. Canada has dismantled all exchange controls for both current-account (trade in goods and services, remittances, travel) and capital-account (direct and portfolio investment, borrowing, repatriation) transactions. No administrative approval or central bank consent is required for convertibility. Profit remittances, dividends, management fees, and royalty payments to non-residents are freely transferable, subject solely to standard anti-money laundering, sanctions screening, and beneficial ownership verification by the remitting financial institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists. Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds to Canada within a specific period. Exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts in Canada or abroad; however, any proceeds received in a Canadian account are subject to standard FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., CAD 10,000 equivalent) and must be supported by underlying commercial documents for audit and transfer purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) – sole monetary authority; Foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada, with anti-money laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and currency reporting at borders by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). No single foreign exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada does not legally recognize 'Electronic Money Institutions' as a distinct bank or deposit-taking category. Non-bank fintech payment providers must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) with FINTRAC. They may facilitate cross-border payment transmission, FX conversion, and virtual IBAN issuance, but they cannot hold client funds as deposits, cannot issue regulated letters of credit, and are subject to additional provincial licensing under Quebec's Money-Services Businesses Act. Legal constraints: (i) no deposit insurance (CDIC) coverage; (ii) funds must be held in trust accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank; (iii) no direct access to the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); (iv) limited to payment-only instruments. For an export operating entity, use of an MSB is practical for small-value ancillary transfers but not for bulk trade settlements or capital escrow purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an inbound injection (equity or debt) from a non-resident parent to the Quebec entity must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian bank's correspondent account (CAD or USD) under SWIFT field 50K (ordering customer) and field 72 (bank-to-bank information). Recommended purpose codes (ISO 20022 external purpose codes): 'FDIN' for foreign direct investment / equity capital, 'LIQU' for trade-related liquidity support, or 'TRAD' for advance payment on goods. For shareholder loans, use 'LOAN' or 'INTC' (intercompany loan). The remitter should state in field 70 a concise narrative such as 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN / FC INVESTMENT / CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - FOR OPERATING EXPENSES OF DRIED GOJI BERRY SOLUTIONS.' Verification: the receiving bank will perform sanctions screening on all parties, confirm the corporate name of the beneficiary, and may request a copy of the executed shareholder loan or board resolution, applicable Investment Canada Act notification name of ultimate investor, and supporting invoice if the injection is trade-related. Amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under a Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR), though the report is filed by the bank, and not by the client.For commercial settlements from the Quebec entity to foreign suppliers and logistics providers, the primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 (direct wire transfer) for open-account, partial prepayments, or final settlement, using purpose code 'GOODS' or 'TRAD', and (2) Documentary Letter of Credit (L/C) via SWIFT MT700, commonly a sight irrevocable L/C confirmed by the seller's bank (for large bulk herbal consignments). For periodic payments like freight and insurance, MT103 with the relevant corporate general purpose code is sufficient. Canada's internal clearing infrastructure (LVTS / Automated Clearing Settlement System) is only for CAD-denominated domestic wires; cross-border outbound flows must use SWIFT. Practical guidance: keep a dedicated foreign-currency denomination (USD or CNY) for trade payments to avoid double conversion; maintain a reserve in the operating account to cover hedging margins; and ensure all outward remittances references include the original invoice number and a cross-reference to the applicable import permit if the destination country requires one.Canadian dollar / US dollar foreign exchange forwards (plain vanilla FX swaps) – permitted for all non-financial trading corporates without derivatives licensingCross-currency swaps (CAD/USD, CAD/CNY) for long-term purchase price fixed-currency obligations – available from Schedule I banks under an ISDA Master Agreement or a master FX confirmOver-the-counter options (put and call) on USD/CAD for trade receivable and payable protection – unregulated for commercial hedgersNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted when hedging USD/CNH or exotic currencies due to CNH onshore non-transferability, using the recognized offshore CNH fixing

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 is due on or before the day that is six months after the end of the taxation year. For a Canadian-controlled private corporation, the federal balance-due date is generally three months after year-end; for other corporations it is generally two months after year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 is due six months after the fiscal year-end. Quebec payment deadlines align with the corporation type: generally the second or third month after year-end for most corporations; installment obligations arise when tax payable exceeds the quarterly threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 aggregate value of same-nature non-arm's-length cross-border transactions in a taxation year is the practical threshold triggering Canada's contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation expectation and the section 247 penalty; a C$750,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold triggers Country-by-Country reporting. A formal Local File/Master File is not separately mandated by the Income Tax Act, but CRA and Revenu Québec follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and require complete, contemporaneous and substantiated documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule, the legal sham doctrine, foreign accrual property income rules, and the treaty beneficial-ownership requirement. A Quebec entity that merely invoices dried goji berry exports while holding no inventory, officers, employees, board activity or commercial risk in Quebec is vulnerable to being disregarded for tax purposes, with distributions or invoiced margins recharacterized as taxable Canadian income or Part XIII dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years; civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) of 50% of the net understatement; Revenu Québec imposes parallel sanctions under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraud are jointly and severally liable with the corporation; the corporate veil is pierced and personal assets may be seized. Serious fraud prosecutions may trigger RCMP/Canada Border Services Agency asset freezes, international extradition requests, and travel alerts against non-cooperating director-shareholders.
Transfer Pricing / Sham Intercompany Sourcing

Statutory sanction: Under section 247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment applies where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million and 10% of gross revenue; interest runs on all reassessed amounts. Recharacterization can convert excessive payments into taxable dividends subject to Part XIII withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct the preparation of sham transfer-pricing policies or knowingly sign false documentation may be assessed personally for gross negligence and, where fraud is established, may face criminal prosecution and personal confiscation orders.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA and Revenu Québec may apply the GAAR to deny treaty benefits, disregard the entity, and impose tax, interest and penalties on all diverted profits. Beneficial-ownership analysis under tax treaties will disallow reduced withholding rates.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and non-resident beneficial owners cannot shelter behind the limited liability shell; courts may pierce the veil, hold directors personally liable for withheld or evaded amounts, impose asset freezes, and issue travel bans where shell funds are moved out of Canada.
Part XIII Withholding Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Section 215 of the Income Tax Act makes the Canadian payer liable for the full amount that should have been withheld, plus interest and penalties. Revenu Québec may also assess the corporation as agent for the non-resident recipient.

Director personal liability: Under section 227.1, directors are personally liable for amounts the corporation failed to withhold and remit if the corporation cannot pay. This liability survives corporate wind-up and bankruptcy discharge restrictions apply; CRA cannot be constrained by asset migration.
AML and Export Proceeds Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties reach high administrative monetary amounts per violation; Criminal Code money-laundering carries imprisonment up to 14 years; customs false declaration penalties and export permit revocation apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly execute or direct the unreported wire transfers may be prosecuted personally; conviction may result in personal fines, imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and regulatory bans on acting as directors. Cross-border enforcement can include Interpol travel alerts and extradition.
