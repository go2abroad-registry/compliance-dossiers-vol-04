Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an Aktiengesellschaft, minimum share capital is CHF 100,000. At formation, each subscriber must pay in at least 20% of the nominal value per share, but in no case less than CHF 50,000 total; these funds must be deposited in a blocked Swiss bank account in the company's name and may be released only upon presentation of the final Commercial Register extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load inspections are not required for the virtual software operation unless a physical office is rented in Canton Zug. If an office is occupied, the company must obtain a cantonal fire safety clearance, ensure workplace compliance under Swiss Labour Law (ArG), and complete the SUVA occupational accident insurance notification.

Paid-in capital rule: For an Aktiengesellschaft, minimum share capital is CHF 100,000. At formation, each subscriber must pay in at least 20% of the nominal value per share, but in no case less than CHF 50,000 total; these funds must be deposited in a blocked Swiss bank account in the company's name and may be released only upon presentation of the final Commercial Register extract.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Eigerstrasse 65, 3003 Bern. Official portals: www.estv.admin.ch (incl. VAT eServices, MWST/MWTR), www.uid.admin.ch (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / UID registry), and www.ezv.admin.ch for customs/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities with a Swiss VAT nexus (supply of goods stored in Switzerland, or B2C/B2B supply of services to Swiss recipients with turnover > CHF 100,000 p.a.) must first obtain a Swiss UID (business identification number) from the Federal Statistical Office via the UID portal, submitting the UID application form or the FTA's 'Registration of a foreign enterprise for VAT' form. The UID is assigned within 1-2 weeks and simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number (MWST-Nr) when the VAT registration declaration (Formular 754/Fiche 754) is filed. Non-established foreign companies without a Swiss branch may register online via FTA eService 'Registration for VAT purposes' and must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (business agent with Swiss residency) if turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. A customs account at the Federal Office for Customs (e-dec web) is required for export declarations. Once registered, the VAT return must be filed electronically each quarter (or semi-annually/annual under simplified prior-authorization) and the UID must be printed on invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope_Reference: an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a resident corporation fully taxable on worldwide income; Swiss federal taxation applies a single flat rate (18.04% effective CIT incl. federal direct tax of ~8.5% after deduction), while cantonal/communal taxes apply income and capital taxes on a territorial/effective-nexus basis (profits attributable to a Swiss establishment or real property are taxable in Switzerland). No pass-through treatment is available for AG; shareholders are taxed only on distributed dividends (participation relief applies at 50-100% for qualifying holdings); CFC/hybrid rules follow OECD BEPS standards without mandatory check-the-box elections.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to an AG: (i) VAT group taxation election under Art. 21 MWSTG/VAT Act for closely-linked enterprises (one VAT group, single tax number) — requires at least 70% voting/equity control and uniform management; (ii) voluntary VAT registration for small businesses below the CHF 100,000 threshold (Art. 34 VAT Act) allowing input VAT recovery; (iii) election for net-tax-rate (saldo method) for companies with turnover up to CHF 5,030,000 p.a. instead of exact input deduction; (iv) partial taxation/dividend relief: election to apply Swiss participation exemption (Art. 69 DBG) — not a check-the-box but an annual, binding declaration in the tax return; (v) no hybrid-mismatch election under Swiss ordinary law; Swiss tax treaties follow OECD BEPS Action 2 and the domestic rule disfavours hybrid structures via substance requirements and abuse clauses. Any election is made in writing to the cantonal tax administration, irrevocable for the fiscal year concerned.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 worldwide annual taxable turnover (consolidated, excluding exempt supplies) for Swiss-domiciled businesses (Art. 10 VAT Act). Since 1 Jan 2018, non-Swiss businesses providing electronic, telecom and digital services to Swiss private consumers (B2C) must register for VAT if their worldwide turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, and they must charge 8.1% VAT on Swiss B2C sales. Small businesses with turnover below CHF 100,000 may apply for exemption (Art. 11 VAT Act) but forfeit input VAT recovery. There is no separate state-level sales tax; the canton of Valais and others apply local tourist taxes outside VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods: 0% VAT under Art. 23(1)(a) VAT Act applies to movable goods physically transferred abroad (direct transport out of Switzerland by the supplier, the customer, or a commissioned carrier) provided that export is proven and the goods leave Switzerland within 30 days of the last invoice (or 60 days with extension). Export of services: 0% applies to services supplied to customers with a business seat or a residence abroad, including mobile application license downloads and subscriptions, if the recipient is a foreign VAT-liable enterprise or a private individual resident abroad and the service is not attributable to a Swiss permanent establishment; digital distribution via Apple IAP/Google Play requires proof that the end-customer is non-Swiss (store jurisdiction, billing address, platform seller reports). Certain services linked to Swiss real property or tangible goods remain taxable at 8.1% and do not qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec/Swiss-Impex exit confirmation or the A.I. G/E-form with customs stamp or NCTS exit movement reference number)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR consignment note or equivalent proof of physical dispatch/transport contract
  • Export invoice with supplier UID/MWST number, recipient address (foreign), goods/service description, and explicit 0% rate indication
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment proof from a Swiss or foreign bank confirming the foreign customer remitted consideration in convertible currency (best-practice FATF-aligned evidence; not a standalone Swiss VAT statutory requirement)
  • Apple IAP/Google Play seller sales reports, customer geo-data and app store annual statements substantiating the digital service place-of-supply as foreign

Switzerland does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal-clearance or digital-stamping system. VAT returns are filed via FTA eServices (e-Steuererklärung MWST) with structured JSON/XML via the SWICO interface; no government pre-approval of invoices is required. Since 2023, the QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory for any Swiss payment slip in CHF, embedding an electronic invoice payload; Peppol (UBL 2.1) e-invoicing is voluntarily supported by Swiss ERP providers and banks. B2B invoice retention obligations (10 years) apply, and the VAT invoice must contain: supplier UID/MWST number, recipient UID (for B2B), date, description, tax rate, and amount. Audit authorities may require digital export of underlying ledger data, but no transaction-level clearance API or e-verification token is imposed.

Swiss VAT audits (ESTV and cantonal tax authorities) may invalidate zero-rating and retrospectively claw back 8.1%/2.6%/3.7% VAT (depending on rate applicable at invoicing time) plus default interest (Art. 87 VAT Act, 4.5% p.a. minimum) if export documentation is incomplete, goods are demonstrably re-imported without justification, or the consideration is not paid within the standard commercial period (in practice, 180 days is the internal FTA reference for audit scrutiny — no formal statutory deadline exists; after 180 days, FTA may demand a written justification and, if unpaid, must re-verify the export condition). Penalties: negligent tax shortfall triggers a fine under Art. 102(1) VAT Act of up to 3% of the additional tax; deliberate tax evasion (artful measures to mislead, false export certificates) triggers a fine up to 3x the tax evaded (Art. 98(1) VAT Act); fraud, forgery or falsification of export proof is criminalized under Art. 117 VAT Act and Art. 251 Swiss Criminal Code (forgery of a document — 8.1% VAT clawback plus criminal imprisonment up to 5 years). The VAT reassessment period is 10 years for undeclared/recovered amounts in case of incomplete records (Art. 75 VAT Act — five years under regular returns, extended to ten years where no return was filed or tax evasion is determined).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised capital and current account regime under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) and the Foreign Exchange Ordinance (FEO). The Swiss franc is fully convertible; there are no exchange controls, approval requirements, or surrender obligations for current or capital account transactions. Dividend and profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to Swiss corporate withholding tax of 35% (reduced or refundable under applicable double taxation treaties) and FATCA/CRS reporting obligations. A Swiss AG may open foreign-currency accounts abroad, receive inbound equity capital, and repatriate liquidation proceeds without central bank consent. The only practical hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds verification and, where relevant, sanctions screening under Swiss and international sanctions law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Swiss law imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. App-store revenues earned from foreign jurisdictions may be retained in any currency and converted freely through any Swiss bank. The SNB requires banks to report cross-border flows for balance-of-payments statistics, but this does not create any surrender, retention, or conversion obligation for the exporting company.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - Schweizerische Nationalbank (German), Banque Nationale Suisse (French), Banca Nazionale Svizzera (Italian).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as primary treasury; Medium as auxiliary collection and payout channel. Legal constraints: Offshore electronic-money institutions (UK/EU EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Modulr) are not supervised as banks by FINMA and cannot provide a Swiss IBAN or hold Swiss settlement balances. A Swiss AG may use such EMIs for small-value multi-currency disbursements to foreign contractors, but FINMA Circular 2008/07 on outsourcing requires the Swiss relationship bank to know the complete payment chain, and treasury-critical liquidity must remain in a FINMA-licensed bank. Since the business model is an App Store freemium mobile utility, EMIs can be used only for non-core payouts; all capital injections, dividend flows, and reserve liquidity must be channelled through a Swiss regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a foreign shareholder must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008) credit transfer to the Swiss AG's capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) at UBS AG or Zürcher Kantonalbank. The payment must identify the beneficiary by Swiss commercial register number (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) and include a clear purpose narrative in field 70 such as 'Kapitalerhöhung / paid-in share capital'; where the receiving bank requires a structured external purpose code, the relationship manager will map the transaction to the bank's equity-capital purpose category. Verification provisions: the bank requires (i) a certified copy of the notarial deed for the capital increase; (ii) the shareholders' or board resolution approving the contribution; (iii) proof of identity and source of wealth of the contributing shareholder; and (iv) confirmation that the funds are not derived from crime. After the capital increase is registered in the Swiss Commercial Register, the Sperrkonto is released for operational use upon presentation of the registration confirmation. Legal constraints: Offshore electronic-money institutions (UK/EU EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Modulr) are not supervised as banks by FINMA and cannot provide a Swiss IBAN or hold Swiss settlement balances.
