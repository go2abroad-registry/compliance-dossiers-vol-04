Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a Quebec limited partnership. The parties must agree on the capital contributions in the partnership agreement, and those contributions are recorded in capital accounts; no escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required by Quebec law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the local fire-prevention service; structural-load inspection for warehouse racking and high-density storage; building-code compliance inspection; emergency-exit and fire-separation approval; CNESST occupational-health clearance for warehouse workers, including training and equipment-specific safety checks.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a Quebec limited partnership. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dual-authority regime. Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – 'My Business Account' portal and GST/HST NETFILE (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial: Revenu Québec – clicSÉQUR portal (www.revenuquebec.ca); entity registration via the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ).

E-government filing portal

Register the LP with REQ for the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) using declaration form AR-1A/A. Obtain the federal 15-character Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, requesting the 'RT' GST/HST trust account. Open the QST account at Revenu Québec via clicSÉQUR using the BN/NEQ linkage and forms LM-1/VDR; non-resident partners must file Form RC151 (CRA) and AP-150 (Quebec) and designate the general partner as representative (ETA s. 273; QST Act s. 422). Parallel processing completes within 2-5 business days; registration retroactively applies to the date of the first taxable supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the LP is a transparent partnership for federal and Quebec income tax (ITA s. 96; Taxation Act s. 665) with no entity-level tax; each partner is taxed on their allocable share. For GST/HST and QST, the LP is nonetheless a distinct 'person' and mandatory registrant in its own right (ETA s. 123(1); QST Act s. 1) for supplies of reverse-logistics services.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows statutory default. Elections available to a Quebec LP: (i) ITA s. 96 partnership allocation designation; (ii) ITA s. 216(1) non-resident partner net-basis election for rental/timber income; (iii) ETA s. 273(2) partnership representative election; (iv) ETA ss. 156-157 closely-related entity elections (zero-rated or deemed non-supply inter-company transactions); (v) ETA s. 179 election for services supplied to non-residents; (vi) QST mirror elections under QST Act ss. 422-423; (vii) anti-hybrid designations under ITA ss. 18.2(5) and 18.3(2) to neutralize deductible/non-inclusion hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies – the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a) and QST Act s. 411(1), tested over any single calendar quarter and the preceding four quarters. A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada is registerable without regard to the threshold (ETA s. 240(1); QST Act s. 405); voluntary registration is available once a taxable supply is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST/QST) applies to: (i) supplies of goods sold to a non-resident recipient and exported from Canada within 60 days (Schedule VI Part V s. 1; QST Act Schedule IV); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident under Schedule VI Part V ss. 7 or 9, including grading, refurbishment, repair, and freight arrangement on goods brought into Canada solely for processing and thereafter exported; (iii) services supplied to a non-resident that do not relate to Canadian real property or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at performance. Reverse-logistics processing of returned merchandise destined for re-export to the US principal qualifies under s. 9 provided the goods are actually exported within the required period and the recipient is not a GST/QST registrant; where the s. 7(c) domestic-goods exclusion applies, 14.975% GST/QST must be charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS electronic B13A or paper Canada Customs Coding Form with CBSA stamp)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the returned-goods purchase and grading report
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX receipt documenting settlement of the export sale proceeds
  • Cargo control documents (A6/C8 manifest) and CBSA proof of exit for the re-exported goods
  • Third-party warehouse or freight-forwarder certificate for the reverse-logistics custody chain

No real-time government clearance, digital stamping, or prior fiscal approval exists in Canada or Quebec; the system is audit-based, requiring prescribed invoice fields (ETA s. 223; QST Act s. 432). Mandatory electronic filing and remittance apply where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million or monthly reporting cycles are used; Revenu Québec imposes e-filing for QST returns and payments for large registrants. A voluntary pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing standard is being deployed (2023-2027 roadmap) and CRA has consulted on future mandatory B2B e-invoicing, but no statutory real-time API clearance mandate is currently in force.

CRA and Revenu Québec conduct joint audits of zero-rated export claims and may reassess within 4 years (ETA s. 298; QST Act s. 1073), extended to 7-10 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud; no limitation period exists for fraudulent export schemes. Penalties: 25% of the overstated refund/rebate for false claims (ETA s. 285(1); QST Act s. 1033.4); additional 50% penalty where gross negligence is established, plus prescribed interest compounded daily; criminal prosecution for evasion is possible under ETA s. 327(1) (up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines). Retroactive clawback of the zero rating is automatic: the full 14.975% GST/HST/QST becomes payable on misclassified supplies, with the foreign partner jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted QST and GST/HST.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility; Canada complies with IMF Article VIII and imposes no exchange-control approvals on inward capital contributions or outward profit remittance. Non-resident limited partners are nonetheless exposed to Canadian tax at remittance: Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced) applies to dividends, rents, royalties and management fees, and Part I tax applies if the LP carries on business in Canada. The Quebec Tax Administration Act similarly imposes withholding on certain payments to non-residents. A pure capital contribution to a Quebec LP is non-taxable and freely convertible. Thin-capitalization rules under subsection 18(4) and section 212.3 back-to-back loan rules constrain excessive debt-funded contributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or forced conversion ratio exists in Canada. Reverse-logistics fees invoiced in USD, CNY or other currencies may be retained in foreign-currency accounts in Canada or offshore without conversion. Only systemic transparency obligations apply: FINTRAC reporting on large cash and electronic fund transfers (CAD $10,000-plus), CBSA commercial declarations under the Customs Act, and conversion of foreign exchange into Canadian dollars for tax returns under Income Tax Act subsection 261(2).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and FX reserve authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control ministry; the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act define the CAD legal-tender and settlement framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: HIGH for operating liquidity, LOW for capital escrow. Airwallex (Airwallex (Canada) Inc.) and comparable platforms operate as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses under the PCMLTFA, not as deposit-taking banks; they may issue CAD/USD/multi-currency operating sub-accounts, execute payments over SWIFT/local ACH, and quote interbank-like FX spreads. Legal constraints: MSBs cannot take deposits or provide credit; pooled funds are safeguarded but not CDIC-insured; the platform is disqualified as a third-party escrow or trust for statutory capital contributions; and the LP must still maintain a primary clearing account at a Schedule I bank to satisfy OSFI B-8 settlement-risk requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period; for a December 31 fiscal year-end, the due date is May 31 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Identification area (BN/name/fiscal period) Enter the partnership's business number, legal name, registered address, fiscal period start/end dates, and the general partner's contact information. Disclose the general partner and every non-resident limited partner; state the applicable tax treaty country and attach the partner's tax residence certificate where treaty relief is claimed. Late filing attracts a CRA penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day late, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 2,500; continued non-filing can lead to prosecution under ITA s.238, additional gross-negligence penalties, and loss of certain deduction claims.
T5013 Summary – Schedule 5 allocation Allocate partnership income, loss, deductions, and other relevant tax attributes among partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and ITA s.96. Allocate only Canadian-source business income to non-resident partners and compute Part XIII withholding on amounts paid or credited to them. Late filing attracts a CRA penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day late, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 2,500; continued non-filing can lead to prosecution under ITA s.238, additional gross-negligence penalties, and loss of certain deduction claims.
T5013 Summary – at-risk amount Report each limited partner's at-risk amount for purposes of limiting deductible partnership losses. Restrict non-resident limited partner loss deductions to their at-risk amount; carry forward any disallowed amount under the partner's own loss rules. Late filing attracts a CRA penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day late, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 2,500; continued non-filing can lead to prosecution under ITA s.238, additional gross-negligence penalties, and loss of certain deduction claims.
Part XIII withholding section Report Canadian-source payments or credits to non-resident partners and the amount of non-resident tax withheld. Apply the 25% statutory Part XIII rate unless a treaty-reduced rate is substantiated by a completed NR-series withholding declaration and a valid tax residence certificate. Late filing attracts a CRA penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day late, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 2,500; continued non-filing can lead to prosecution under ITA s.238, additional gross-negligence penalties, and loss of certain deduction claims.
