Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – electronic portal: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); in Quebec, Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/) administers provincial income tax, QST, and employer deductions.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA. Step 1: Determine if registration is mandatory (non-resident with an establishment in Canada/Quebec, import/export activity, or taxable revenues exceeding CAD 30,000 in a four-quarter period). Step 2: Apply via CRA's Business Registration Online portal or Form RC1, providing partnership legal name, address, key business activities (e.g., NAICS 325412), and partner information. Step 3: The BN is issued, to which the GST/HST account (RT) and, if applicable, payroll (RP) and customs (RM) accounts are linked. Step 4: In Quebec, separately register with Revenu Québec for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) (enterprise number) and provincial employer accounts, using the same BN where possible. Step 5: For export shipments, obtain an exporter identifier or use the RM account for customs declarations with CBSA. Registration is completed before invoicing any taxable supplies in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box system. For the limited partnership, the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA) provides the following relevant elections: (i) partnership income allocation election under ITA s. 103, allowing allocation of income or loss other than by partnership agreement; (ii) election under ITA s. 216 for non-resident partners to compute Canadian tax on a net basis on certain Canadian-source income such as rental or timber royalties; (iii) for hybrid mismatch, Canada has enacted mandatory rules addressing BEPS Action 2 (ITA ss. 18.5, 18.6, 18.7, 94, 94.1) with limited elections to relieve double taxation, e.g., under the treaty or domestic provisions; (iv) the partnership may file an election under ITA s. 249(5) to establish a non-calendar fiscal period in certain agricultural or fishing cases. Under Quebec, fiscal elections are integrated with federal classification; a partnership may elect under the Taxation Act (Quebec) ss. 103 equivalent for provincial purposes. No direct elections exist to alter the pass-through character of an LP for income tax; limited exceptions such as 'public limited partnerships' may be treated as corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – For GST/HST, the small supplier threshold is worldwide taxable revenues (excluding zero-rated exports of financial services) of CAD 30,000 or less in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, measured per person/partnership. For QST, the threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable sales in Quebec (worldwide revenues excluding financial services and certain supplies) for the preceding four quarters. A non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada may be required to register for GST/HST if taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000; otherwise voluntary registration may be appropriate to recover input tax credits. Export-only businesses still count zero-rated exports toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods that are specifically exported from Canada are zero-rated for GST/HST under s. 1 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier obtains and retains evidence of export in the form specified (e.g., customs documentation, shipping records, consignee information). Services performed on exported goods (e.g., chemical fractionation, toll processing, quality control) may be zero-rated if the service is performed wholly or partly in Canada and the tangible property is subsequently exported, or if the service is supplied to a non-resident and not for use in Canada, subject to the usual rules. For QST, equivalent zero-rating applies under Quebec's Taxation Act when the goods are delivered outside Quebec. The supply must be made by the partnership to a foreign purchaser; goods must be actually shipped out of Canada within a reasonable period. Inward processing, temporary import/export relief, and specific pharma export incentives may require additional documentation. The claim to zero-rating falls on the supplier; the CRA strictly interprets export evidence rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A Form or electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) identifying Canadian exporter and foreign destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment)

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API mandate, or digital stamping is currently in force. The CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns and retains transaction records; Quebec mandates the use of a Revenu Québec-certified billing system (Registre de la vente/SAE) for enterprises that make retail or non-retail sales through a point of sale, but this does not apply to wholesale B2B exports of heparin crude/fractionation services. B2B invoicing, electronic interchange, and export documentation must follow standard commercial practice; there is no mandatory centralized e-invoicing clearance. The CRA accepts electronic records upon audit subject to strict data retention and integrity requirements. Quebec has announced a phased e-invoicing roadmap in line with OECD recommendations, but no legal mandate exists to date.

The CRA may, upon audit, reassess the partnership’s net tax to disallow zero-rating where export evidence is inadequate, erroneous, or fraudulent. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: (i) failure to comply with registration/invoicing requirements – penalties up to CAD 2,500; (ii) false statements or omissions – penalties equal to the greater of CAD 100 or 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (or 10% of the net tax if careless or willful); (iii) fraud – penalties up to 200% of tax evaded plus interest. For QST, penalties mirror the federal regime under the Quebec Taxation Act. Retrospective reassessment periods: ordinary limitation four years; six years if a taxpayer has made an error of omission or failing to report; indefinite in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. The exporter must retain all export documents for at least six years from the end of the fiscal period. Clawback mechanisms permit full reversal of claimed input tax credits and zero-rating resulting in additional taxes plus interest, and in severe cases criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code (Canada).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible; Canada adhered to IMF Article VIII obligations on 25 March 1994 and maintains no exchange-control legislation, no capital/current account distinction, and no preclearance for remittances. Inward equity for a Quebec Limited Partnership and outward profit distributions are unrestricted at the FX level; the practical friction sits in Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (25% base, reduced by treaty) on interest, dividends, rents, royalties, management fees and certain partnership distributions to non-residents, plus Quebec provincial withholding equivalents, CRS/FATCA classification, and FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds. Remittance planning for CA_LP must evidence the Canadian-source/permanent-establishment allocation to avoid over-withholding and to support treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation requirement for export receipts in Canada. CA_LP may keep heparin sodium export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency, provided export transactions are fully traceable under transfer-pricing documentation, Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence (DEL) export/import declarations, and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) customs records; any single wire of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC, and monetary instruments transported across the border must be declared on Form CBSA E677 if exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and lender of last resort; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential regulator of federally regulated Canadian banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — AML/ATF regulator under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Department of Finance Canada — policy authority over foreign exchange and international financial affairs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a stand-alone channel; acceptable only as a complementary FX/payment utility over a Tier-1 bank core. Canada has no EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment firms are money services businesses (MSBs) or foreign MSBs under the PCMLTFA and must be registered with FINTRAC. An offshore EMI cannot lawfully collect deposits in Canada without a Bank Act licence, enjoys no CDIC deposit insurance, and is not supervised by OSFI; moreover, Tier-1 Canadian banks treat unregistered foreign EMIs as high-risk payment service providers and routinely reject wires ordering such entities. Feasible architecture: a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB subsidiary or a regulated FX/fintech treasury platform (API/SWIFT access) executing currency conversion and disbursements under confirmations, while all custody and settlement remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route foreign capital calls as SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR) or domestic instant/large-value wires (CAD via Payments Canada Lynx) into a named capital escrow account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The instruction must include the LP full legal name and Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC plus transit and account number, and — for USD — the US correspondent ABA and final beneficiary wire details; payment narrative must cite purpose code 'direct investment / equity contribution to a Canadian limited partnership', the capital call reference, and subscription agreement identifier. Canadian banks report all single electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC and classify the beneficial owner for CRS/FATCA; inbound funds will be held pending reconciliation against a pre-submitted capital-call notice and source-of-funds pack. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a stand-alone channel; acceptable only as a complementary FX/payment utility over a Tier-1 bank core. Canada has no EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment firms are money services businesses (MSBs) or foreign MSBs under the PCMLTFA and must be registered with FINTRAC. An offshore EMI cannot lawfully collect deposits in Canada without a Bank Act licence, enjoys no CDIC deposit insurance, and is not supervised by OSFI; moreover, Tier-1 Canadian banks treat unregistered foreign EMIs as high-risk payment service providers and routinely reject wires ordering such entities. Feasible architecture: a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB subsidiary or a regulated FX/fintech treasury platform (API/SWIFT access) executing currency conversion and disbursements under confirmations, while all custody and settlement remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank.
