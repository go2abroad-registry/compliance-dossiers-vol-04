Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange rate system with unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital accounts. There are no statutory exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements for external remittances, and no distinction between capital and current account transactions. Profit repatriation by a limited partnership is subject only to domestic income tax and Part XIII withholding tax under applicable tax treaties, not to foreign exchange authorization. Non-resident investors may freely subscribe to and withdraw capital, provided that FINTRAC reporting thresholds for wire transfers and international electronic funds transfers are met. The Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act impose no restriction on holding, transferring, or converting foreign currency balances within or outside Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters are not required to surrender export proceeds to the government or any authorized dealer, and there is no statutory timeline requiring conversion into CAD. Export revenue may be retained in foreign currency accounts with Canadian chartered banks. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reports for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000, and to the Canada Revenue Agency for annual taxpayer reporting. No deposit or reserve margin is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary settlement; Low for primary operating accounts. Under Quebecs Anti-Money Laundering Act and federal Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, any entity providing money-transfer or foreign-exchange services to residents must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain a permanent establishment or agent in Canada. Major offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and PayPal Canada are FINTRAC-registered; however, non-registered foreign EMIs cannot lawfully receive and settle trade payments for a Canadian-domiciled partnership. Additionally, commercial banking agreements restrict the use of PayPal for bulk B2B settlement due to reserve holds and chargeback provisions. The only viable offshore-EMI use is for payment to remote subcontractors, capped at CAD $5,000 per transaction, with each beneficiary KYC-verified. Cross-border clearing for die-cast scale automotive model imports and exports must route through the SWIFT network via a Canadian bank to preserve auditability and mitigate Bank Secrecy Act risks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions to the Quebec Limited Partnership should be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the parent or limited partners' bank directly to the LP's primary operating account at a Canadian chartered bank. The remitter name must exactly match the initial contribution schedule registered in the partnership agreement; any mismatch triggers FINTRAC Enhanced Due Diligence. The transaction must include clear purpose codes: for capital account funding use 'P0120' under SWIFT category 'Investment Income and Financing' (or FINTRAC code 'or-Investment'), accompanied by a detailed invoice or funding notice, and for customer deposits use 'P0135'. All transfers exceeding CAD $10,000 require the bank to file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) with FINTRAC within 30 days. Canada has no special licensing requirement for inbound capital, but the LP must ensure the CRA Form T2/ANZ or relevant provincial filings schedule the contribution appropriately, ensuring no Part XIII tax leakage.Outbound trade settlements for raw zinc alloy, blister package resins, tampo printing inks, and import/export of finished die-cast replicas must use SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for payments under USD 1,000,000. For larger wholesale transactions, Commercial Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) and irrevocable Letters of Credit (LC) issued through a participating Montreal-based bank are preferred. Each MT103 must identify source of funds with a unique beneficiary account number and instruct the beneficiary bank to provide an IMAD reference that ties to the commercial invoice; PA-03 advise and storage endorsements must be avoided. All outbound disbursements over CAD $10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC and must be filed with the CRA under the Income Tax Act reporting requirements (T-batch form).
