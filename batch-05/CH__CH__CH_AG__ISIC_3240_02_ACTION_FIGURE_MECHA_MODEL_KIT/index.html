Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: The Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is a per-se corporation subject to federal, cantonal and municipal income tax on worldwide profits, with carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments, foreign real property and participation exemptions; passthrough treatment is not available for Swiss resident shareholders; export manufacturing income qualifies for the 0% VAT rate with full input VAT deduction.

        
Available election forms
Swiss VAT group election (Art. 15 VAT Act); option to tax exempt real estate supplies (Art. 21(3) VAT Act); cantonal holding, mixed company and principal-company tax status rulings; notional interest deduction election on excess Swiss equity of the AG; participation deduction election (Art. 70 DBG); hybrid mismatch elections reported under Art. 61a DBG; no US check-the-box election domestically, but an associated US parent may file check-the-box elections for solo entities.

Standard VAT / GST rate
8.1%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover for Swiss-established entities (Art. 10(1) VAT Act); non-established foreign suppliers register once Swiss taxable supplies exceed CHF 100,000 per year (Art. 10(3) VAT Act); voluntary taxation election is available below the threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods physically exported from Switzerland are zero-rated under Art. 23(1) VAT Act when the exporter proves delivery to a foreign destination; services directly related to exported goods and services under Art. 23(2) also qualify; the CH_AG must demonstrate per consignment the foreign recipient, value chain and physical dispatch of the action-figure mecha model kits; dispatch into a bonded warehouse or export-only delivery is excluded from domestic Swiss VAT.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital invoice clearance is mandated in Switzerland. Domestic invoices must include a QR-bill payment slip (Swiss Payment Standards, ISO 20022) since 1 July 2020; PEPPOL and other B2B/B2G e-invoice formats are accepted voluntarily; FTA returns are filed via the SaaS e-portal; all invoice and export evidence must be retained electronically for 10 years.

      
On audit, missing or inconsistent export proof triggers reclassification of supplies at the 8.1% standard rate with statutory default interest, denial of input VAT deduction and retroactive deficiency assessments for up to 10 years; negligent evasion triggers a fine of up to 100% of the VAT evaded, intentional evasion a fine of up to 300% (Art. 99 VAT Act) and criminal penalties (Art. 101 VAT Act); false customs declarations are separately sanctionable under Art. 118 Customs Act. Swiss law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, so the 180-day term is enforced by banks and auditors as a documentary benchmark for export substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Switzerland maintains a fully liberalized cross-border capital and payments regime. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible, and there are no general restrictions on current account payments, capital injections, dividend distributions, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is not subject to exchange-control approval; the only practical hurdles are corporate-law solvency tests, statutory reserves, and the 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividend distributions unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the Swiss participation exemption conditions are met. No prior central-bank authorization is required for inbound or outbound transfers.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any convertible currency, used directly for offshore working capital, or converted into CHF at the exporter's discretion. There is no statutory conversion ratio, no prescribed settlement window, and no SNB requirement to convert foreign currency balances. Swiss resident companies may need to comply only with statistical balance-of-payments reporting thresholds of the SNB for large direct investment or cross-border financial claims, not with any exchange-control liquidation obligation.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), acting together with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) in matters of monetary policy, international financial sanctions, and banking supervision.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-conditional. A Swiss AG may use a licensed foreign or Swiss-licensed fintech electronic-money institution (EMI) as a supplementary collections and treasury-optimization rail, but such an account cannot replace a Swiss banking relationship for statutory share-capital deposit, SIC/SNB CHF clearing, or documentary trade instruments. Offshore EMIs that are not licensed or passported into Switzerland create legal uncertainty under the Swiss Financial Institutions Act and may expose the company to reputational and sanctions-screening risks. Acceptable use is limited to low-value inbound customer collections, and the controlling compliance function must ensure every fintech account is covered by the company's tax and financial reporting perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital for the Swiss AG must be wired to the dedicated capital escrow account (Einzahlungskonto) in the name of the company at a Swiss bank before commercial-register registration. The preferred protocol is SWIFT MT103; the payment must cite the company name, subscriber name, and a clear reference such as 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital' (payment of share capital), together with the notarial deed number. Subscribers should transfer from their own bank accounts via their home-country correspondent to the Swiss beneficiary bank, and the corresponding transaction purpose must be a direct investment / equity participation, not a trade settlement. The Swiss bank will issue a blocking confirmation after the funds are credited and held for the statutory period subject to bank-internal lock protocols. Large direct-investment inflows in excess of SNB reporting thresholds must be reported on the relevant SNB balance-of-payments direct-investment form; all remitters and intermediaries must pass sanctions screening before credit.Outbound trade settlements for materials, tooling, and finished goods should be executed using SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for open-account transactions, documentary collections (DP/DA) where moderate trust exists, and commercial letters of credit (irrevocable, confirmed) via SWIFT MT700/MT707 for larger orders. For domestic CHF disbursements, the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system through the SNB provides final settlement. EUR payments to European suppliers may be routed via SEPA (TARGET2), and USD payments via CHIPS/Fedwire correspondents. All outbound messages must identify the commercial purpose using standard categories, the underlying invoice or contract number, and the ultimate beneficial counterparty where requested. PayPal is acceptable for low-value consumer refunds or smaller distributor pre-orders, but not for capital flows or material treasury operations. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
Federal direct tax at 8.5% on post-tax net profit (approximately 7.83% on pre-tax profit), plus cantonal/municipal income tax; Zug effective combined rate approx. 14.6%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
35% statutory Swiss anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends paid by a Swiss AG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if the Swiss entity's gross related-party transactions (revenues plus expenses) exceed CHF 5 million in a tax year; Master File required if the group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds CHF 50 million; Country-by-Country report required file annually with the FTA when consolidated group revenue exceeds CHF 900 million. Below CHF 5 million, documentation does not need to be filed but should remain available upon request.

      
Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the offshore sense; however, for treaty entitlement and for all tax privileges (participation relief, notional interest deduction, patent box), the Swiss AG must maintain real substance: employees, office/manufacturing premises, independent decision-making, and risk-taking functions in Switzerland. A 'letter-box' or conduit AG almost certainly triggers a denial of DTA benefits, retroactive withholding tax at 35% plus default interest, and potential penalties for abuse of tax law (Rechtsmissbrauch).

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)
        
Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of tax, default interest at 4.5%, and criminal penalty up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG). If aggravated by forged documents, the offense is tax fraud under Art. 176 DBG with a fine of up to CHF 250,000 and up to 3 years imprisonment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false tax returns are personally and jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; they may be criminally punished with imprisonment up to 3 years, travel bans, and asset confiscation under Swiss criminal law.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Treaty Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: The FTA will deny the DTA claim, impose a 35% withholding tax on the entire pass-through amount (plus 4.5% default interest), and may re-characterize the distributions as hidden profit distributions subject to additional cantonal tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create a sham Swiss entity can be personally assessed for the 35% unwithheld tax if the company is insolvent; they face criminal charges for participation in tax evasion, fines up to CHF 250,000, and a travel ban pending the criminal tax proceeding.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration
        
Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-determines the taxpayer's income under Art. 58 LIFD and Art. 24 LHID; imposes additional tax and default interest, and may levy a negligence penalty of up to 30% of the supplemental tax assessment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and transfer pricing coordinators are personally liable for the additional tax and penalty if they sign the statement certifying the intercompany transactions; in severe cases they are banned from serving as board members and may be imprisoned for 1 to 3 years for tax evasion.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax Default (Verrechnungssteuer)
        
Statutory sanction: The Swiss AG is liable for the full 35% anticipatory tax (Art. 13 VStG), plus a late-payment penalty of up to 10% and 4.5% interest; unpaid withholding tax may be collected via enforced seizure of assets.

        
Director personal liability: The managing directors who instructed the unauthorized distribution become personally liable for the unpaid 35% and penalties; criminal prosecution for violation of the Swiss Withholding Tax Act can lead to a custodial sentence of up to 2 years.

      

      
      

        AML Red Flag (Shell Manufacturing with Anonymous Payments)
        
Statutory sanction: If the Swiss AG is used to conceal criminal assets, the offense is money laundering under Art. 305bis and 305ter of the Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to 500 day-units, asset freezing, and forfeiture of all proceeds.

        
Director personal liability: Directors involved in laundering funds through the AG face imprisonment from 3 to 5 years, a travel ban pending extradition, civil restitution to the Swiss state and injured shareholders, and disqualification from managing a company in Switzerland.
