Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portal: My Business Account (MyBA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance ONT-TAXS online portal administers the Employer Health Tax (EHT) and other Ontario-specific levies.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes but is itself a registrant for GST/HST. SOP: (1) file the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act declaration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services to obtain the LP Ontario file number; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) with GST/HST program account (prefix 'RT') and Payroll program account ('RP') via BRO, by CRA telephone registration, or by mailing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (3) file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually under the partnership's BN and allocate taxable income/loss to partners, including non-resident partners, who are assessable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada); (4) where Ontario payroll exceeds CAD 490,000, register separately for EHT with the Ontario Ministry of Finance via ONT-TAXS; (5) no minimum capital contribution is required, and non-resident ownership is permissible provided a Canadian-resident general partner maintains the Ontario LP registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification election. Available elections: (1) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) permitting simplified output tax accounting for qualifying registrants; (2) election under ETA ss. 156 and 167 to treat supplies between closely related registrants, or a transfer of a business as a going concern, as made for nil consideration; (3) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 where a partnership has specified members (corporate partners), enabling a non-calendar-year fiscal period; (4) elections under ITA s. 96 and related provisions for partnership income determination and partner allocation; (5) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 are self-executing and deny deductions for duplicated losses or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes without an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital receipts) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person who exceeds this small-supplier threshold becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant; an LP may voluntarily elect registration under ETA s. 240 before crossing the threshold, which is advisable to claim input tax credits on production machinery and export inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a GST/HST registrant's supply of tangible personal property exported from Canada, including canned boba tapioca pearls, where the supplier exports the goods or delivers them to a common carrier for export, or the non-resident purchaser takes delivery in Canada and exports them as soon as is reasonably possible (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1-4; ETA ss. 21-23). Canned tapioca pearls are additionally zero-rated domestically under Schedule VI, Part III as food for human consumption (HS 1903.00) unless the product is classified as an excluded snack; export zero-rating is independent of the domestic food classification. The exporter must retain documentary proof of export within six years: an electronic CBSA export declaration (B13A via GCA/CESR) is mandatory for commercial goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more. Excess input tax credits arising from zero-rated export sales are refundable by CRA, subject to export-proof verification and tax-invoice substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed electronically through CBSA GCA/CESR for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more)
  • Commercial Invoice (detailed product description, unit price, INCOTERMS, HS Code 1903.00, and foreign consignee details)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing delivery to a non-resident consignee at the foreign port of discharge
  • Packing List and Container Movement Record (container number, seal number, booking and voyage data)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt (SWIFT MT103 or TT credit advice evidencing payment received from abroad)
  • Purchase Order, sales contract, and INCOTERMS certificate confirming transfer of risk and title to the offshore buyer

No continuous transaction control (CTC) regime, government pre-clearance API stamping, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping is imposed by Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are accurate, complete, legible, and retained in a readable format for six years. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PEPPOL) is voluntary for B2B transactions; certain federal B2G transactions may require PEPPOL-based e-invoicing where contractually specified. GST/HST returns, including Ontario's 8% provincial component of HST, are remitted to CRA electronically through MyBA or GST/HST NETFILE; monthly, quarterly, and annual reporting periods are available based on revenue ceilings.

Canada does not impose a statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is the CRA audit benchmark where open-account trade terms are used. Failure to secure timely inflow of export proceeds without documented commercial justification may cause CRA to recharacterize the supply as domestic and revert the zero-rating, triggering reassessment, interest, and penalties. Specific exposures: (1) gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the overstated refund or understated net tax under ETA s. 280(1); (2) false-statement penalty under ETA s. 281.1 of up to CAD 25,000 for knowingly false export documentation; (3) extended reassessment window of up to 10 years under ETA s. 298(6) where misrepresentation or fraud is established; (4) CBSA penalties for inaccurate export declarations under Customs Act ss. 109-109.2; (5) criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for willful evasion, with fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment; (6) ordinary reassessment period of four years for refund claims lacking adequate export substantiation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member (obligations accepted 25 March 1952) and maintains no exchange-control legislation. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account: no licences, prior approvals, or foreign-exchange rationing apply to capital injection, trade settlement, profit distribution, or capital repatriation by an Ontario limited partnership. The only practical frictions are: (i) Part XIII withholding tax on partnership distributions to non-resident partners - statutory 25%, generally reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention and other treaties; (ii) Investment Canada Act (ICA) net-benefit review where a non-Canadian partner acquires control of an existing Canadian business above the 2024 WTO threshold of CAD 1.18 billion enterprise value - a greenfield capital contribution into a manufacturing LP is outside ICA review; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more. Profit remittance is thus tax-gated but not FX-gated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts under Canadian federal or Ontario law. An Ontario LP manufacturing and exporting canned boba tapioca pearls may retain, convert, net, or disburse export proceeds in USD or other freely convertible currencies without central-bank approval. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) reporting revenue in CAD for income tax purposes at the Bank of Canada transaction-date exchange rate; (ii) accurate Customs Act B13A export declarations and CUSMA or ECCL commercial documentation; and (iii) maintaining PCMLTFA records for five years, including SWIFT confirmations for cross-border transfers equaling or exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); Department of Finance Canada as the foreign-exchange policy and sanctions authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal bank prudential regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF and cross-border currency transaction reporting agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility as a supplementary rail only; not viable as the primary treasury layer. Canada does not recognise the EU/EEA Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passport, and an offshore EMI cannot accept deposits or provide payment services to a Canadian LP without FINTRAC registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) and applicable provincial licensing. Cross-border fintech platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be ring-fenced for small-ticket vendor disbursements and multi-currency collections, but they cannot issue SWIFT commercial letters of credit, clear through LVTS, or provide regulated FX limit lines. A Canadian Schedule I chartered bank must therefore remain the primary counterparty for LC issuance, MT103 trade settlements, and auditable capital-flow trails; fintech EMI usage should be capped per transaction to avoid fragmenting the PCMLTFA audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital contributions must route as SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers to the LP's account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using the bank's SWIFT/BIC and a field-20C reference matching the capital call schedule. Representative BICs: RBC - ROYCCAT2; TD - TDOMCATT; BMO - BOFMCAM2; Scotiabank - NOSCCATT; CIBC - CIBCCATT. CAD leg clears same-day via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) when received before cut-off, or next-day via the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); USD leg clears through the bank's US correspondent over CHIPS or Fedwire into the USD sub-account. In MT103 field 70, insert the purpose message: 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - ONTARIO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP - ISIC 1030 TAPIOCA PEARL MANUFACTURING'; in field 72 include the Ontario Business Registry number. The receiving bank's KYC desk will hold funds if the beneficiary legal name and address do not match the account mandate, so treasury must pre-register the expected sender list to avoid enhanced-screening delays.Two primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 TT Wire for pre-payments, spot raw-material purchases, and settlements below USD 100,000, cleared via LVTS for CAD or the bank's US correspondent for USD; and (ii) Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (LC at sight or usance) for bulk tapioca-starch imports from Thailand, Vietnam, or Taiwan and for the aseptic tubing installation, with the Canadian issuing bank sending the MT700 and settling under the LC via SWIFT MT103/MT202 reimbursement. Export collections from US buyers of canned boba should be structured under LC or documentary collection on DP/DA terms with proceeds settling into the USD sub-account. Treasury must maintain a single-bank audit trail for all flows above CAD 10,000; fintech EMI rail is authorised only for sub-CAD-200 disbursements.
