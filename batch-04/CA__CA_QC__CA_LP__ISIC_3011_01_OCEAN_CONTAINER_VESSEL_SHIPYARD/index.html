Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) and income tax — digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html); Revenu Quebec for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax and employer accounts — digital portal: Revenu Quebec CLIC (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/services-en-ligne/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the Quebec limited partnership (societe en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ) by filing the declaration of registration (Releve d'inscription) and appointing at least one general partner; obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Step 2 — Apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or online through CRA 'Register a business'; the partnership is the GST/HST registrant and receives a 15-digit BN (account type RT for GST/HST). Step 3 — Apply to Revenu Quebec for a QST account through CLIC using the BN/NEQ; complete Form LM-1 or the CLIC equivalent for QST registration and, if applicable, Quebec employer payroll deductions. Step 4 — Where partners are non-residents, provide the required declarations (e.g., Form RC151 for non-resident individuals or equivalent corporate attestations) and appoint a Canadian resident agent for service if requested by CRA or Revenu Quebec. Step 5 — File the annual partnership information return (T5013 and Quebec TP-600 equivalent) allocating income/loss to partners; a foreign partner without Canadian registration cannot claim input tax credits on purchases made through the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes: income, losses, and tax credits flow through to the partners, and the partnership itself pays no income tax unless it is a 'specified investment flow-through' for certain investment partnership rules. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is a deemed 'person' and must register separately for the shipyard's commercial activities. No territorial tax-exempt zone applies to the Quebec shipyard; the vessel manufacturing activity is subject to normal Canadian and Quebec tax sovereignty.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification system; entity classification is determined by the Income Tax Act and provincial partnership law, and a Quebec limited partnership cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 to 18.7 and 20.2 are mandatory and eliminate deduction/non-inclusion mismatches with no elective exception. Available partnership- and indirect-tax elections include: (i) Income Tax Act section 97(4) election to transfer property into the partnership on a tax-deferred rollover basis rather than at fair market value; (ii) Income Tax Act section 249.1 election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period for the partnership; (iii) Excise Tax Act section 273 'qualifying partnership' election to treat certain supplies between the partnership and its partners as being for fair market value consideration for GST/HST purposes; (iv) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for non-arm's length taxable supplies to be deemed made at fair market value; and (v) for non-resident partners, Income Tax Act section 216 election to pay tax on net Canadian rental income rather than gross withholding. None of these elections alters the pass-through characterization of the limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a supplier becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the identical CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold applies for QST under the Quebec Tax Administration Act. A Quebec limited partnership shipyard that makes exclusively zero-rated exports may nonetheless voluntarily register for GST/HST and QST to recover input tax credits on shipbuilding inputs; the small-supplier threshold does not apply to non-residents who are not carrying on business in Canada, but the LP shipyard is deemed to be carrying on business in Quebec through its shipyard establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec shipyard may zero-rate the sale of a newly constructed ocean-going container vessel when the vessel is sold to a non-resident purchaser and is exported from Canada in the course of the sale. The supply qualifies as a zero-rated export of goods under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier is registered, the vessel is delivered outside Canada, title passes to the non-resident buyer, and the supplier retains satisfactory proof of export. Services performed in respect of the exported vessel (e.g., sea trials, commissioning, and transportation directly related to the export) may also be zero-rated where they are supplied directly in respect of the exported goods. Long-term chartering or leasing of the vessel to a non-resident is not automatically zero-rated unless the vessel is supplied outside Canada and is not made available to the charterer in Canada; the specific charter arrangement must be analyzed separately. The QST zero-rating rules mirror the GST/HST export rules, and the identical documentary evidence must be maintained to support a QST zero-rated supply.

  • CBSA B13 Export Declaration or CAED/NDBMS electronic export filing confirming the vessel's export from Canada
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (or equivalent contract of carriage) stating delivery at a foreign destination
  • Signed shipbuilding sale contract and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident purchaser and the Quebec shipyard as seller
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing the receipt of foreign-currency proceeds in the shipyard's Canadian bank account
  • Certificate of Vessel Transfer / deletion from the Canadian Registry or flag transfer documentation proving permanent export
  • Delivery certificate and sea-trial acceptance certificate executed by the purchaser's superintendent at the point of export
  • Packing list and customs broker's attestation, together with a written GST/HST/QST zero-rating narrative reconciling vessel, components, and services to Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act

Canada does not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. Invoices must contain the prescribed GST/HST/QST information under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec regulations and must be retained by the supplier; e-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the Pan-Canadian Peppol network. For Quebec public-sector shipbuilding contracts, the supplier must submit electronic invoices through the SEAO (Systeme electronique d'appel d'offres) and meet Quebec public procurement e-invoicing requirements. Export transactions must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the National Database Management System (NDBMS), and the export records must be reconciled to the zero-rated supply claimed on the GST/HST QST return.

CRA and Revenu Quebec audit zero-rated export claims by cross-referencing CBSA export declarations, bank remittance records, contract terms, and corporate registries. If the vessel is not actually exported or the supporting documentation is false or incomplete, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at the Quebec combined rate of 14.975% and the tax is reassessed with compound daily interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; the normal four-year reassessment limitation is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Specific penalties include: false-statement penalties of CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act; gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the federal tax understatement under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act; parallel QST penalties and administrative fines up to CAD 25,000 imposed by Revenu Quebec; criminal tax-evasion prosecution under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment; and clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on shipbuilding inputs, together with interest from the date of the original claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations; there is no exchange-control statute (the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951) and no administrative approval is required for foreign investment into a Quebec société en commandite, profit remittances, loan repayments, capital distributions, or liquidation proceeds. The Canadian dollar is fully deliverable, and neither the Bank of Canada nor the Minister of Finance imposes convertibility limits or prioritization of foreign-exchange applications. A Quebec limited partnership engaged in commercial shipbuilding may freely receive capital contributions from non-resident limited partners and remit the pass-through Canadian-source share of profits to those partners, subject only to ordinary Part I income-tax return and installment obligations, treaty-based withholding analysis on payments such as interest or royalties, and mandatory transaction reporting (Statistics Canada balance-of-payments/intra-euro-area reporting, FINTRAC large-value EFT reports, and customs export documentation).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. A Quebec shipyard that exports deep-sea container vessels, hull blocks, marine components, or engineering services may retain export receipts in any currency in any eligible account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, an authorized foreign bank branch, or a Quebec cooperative credit union (caisse Desjardins). Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) goods-export reporting to Statistics Canada (B13 export declaration for shipments above CAD 2,000); (ii) international services and investment transaction reporting above the applicable Statistics Canada ITRS threshold; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting by the financial intermediary for electronic funds transfers above CAD 10,000. Proceeds may be converted to CAD at will or held in USD/EUR for future procurement of steel plate, marine engines, and outfitting equipment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act, in conjunction with the Department of Finance Canada (Minister of Finance) for statutory currency and exchange-reserve matters, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of federally chartered banks, FINTRAC for AML/ATF supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) as the Quebec provincial regulator for financial product distribution and financial-cooperative oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Conditional. Canada does not recognize the European 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category, and a foreign EMI may not take deposits, extend credit, or issue commercial letters of credit in Canada. Digital cross-border payment firms operating in Canada — such as Wise Payments Canada Inc. and Payoneer — must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under PCMLTFA and may be used only for ancillary, low-value operational outflows (crew payroll, subcontracted engineering fees, small-vendor payments, multi-currency market-rate conversion). They are not viable as primary treasury rails for an ISIC 3011 ocean-container-vessel shipyard because they lack: (i) documented-letter-of-credit issuance and confirmation (UCP 600), (ii) capital-escrow custody and milestone-drawdown certification services, (iii) Lynx/ACSS high-value CAD clearing access, (iv) OSFI-regulated deposit insurance and credit facilities, and (v) sanctions/export-control compliance depth suited to Medium_Risk_Enhanced maritime trade. Feasibility rating: Low for capital injection and LC escrow; Conditional-Medium strictly for non-trade disbursements below CAD 100,000 per transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident limited-partner capital contributions: (1) All injections must be wired from the partner's own bank account to the LP's designated CAD/USD operational account at the primary Canadian bank; third-party funding is declined unless pre-approved through a formal capital-call resolution and source-of-funds pack. (2) Each MT103 must carry a precise remittance narrative, e.g., 'LP capital contribution per Sec. X of the 2024 Amended and Restated Limited Partnership Agreement — tranche 2 of CAD [amount]'; the bank maps the transaction to the Canada balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment in Canada (equity) or, for passive investors, portfolio-investment equity, rather than to a goods/services code. (3) Settlement occurs through SWIFT, with CAD leg cleared over Lynx and USD leg via the bank's New York correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire); there is no surrender or conversion mandate, and market-rate conversion is applied if the injection is received in USD. (4) EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000 are automatically reported to FINTRAC; injections exceeding CAD 100,000 are pre-notified to the bank's financial-crime unit with supporting partnership resolutions and beneficial-ownership certificates at least three banking days before value date. (5) The treasury function reconciles each injection against the subscribed capital schedule and records all partner contributions in the LP's capital account ledger to evidence aggregate economic substance.Clearing rails for a commercial container-vessel shipyard: (i) SWIFT MT103 credit transfers for direct cross-border payments to foreign steel mills, marine-engine manufacturers, electrical-systems suppliers, coating vendors, and subcontractors, each supported by purchase orders, invoices, and shipping documents held on file. (ii) Commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 (and URDG 758 for on-demand guarantees/advance-payment bonds) through National Bank / RBC / BMO trade-finance desks, with issuance by MT700, amendments by MT707, negotiation/transfer by MT710/MT720, cover payments by MT202/MT202COV, and confirmation arrangements with Asian and European banks for procurement of engine-room packages and outfitting equipment. (iii) Capital Escrow Account drawdowns: the shipowner or LC-issuing bank deposits each instalment into the segregated escrow; the shipyard draws only upon presentation of a classification-society milestone certificate, a bank-approved drawdown certificate, and corresponding supplier invoices. (iv) Domestic Canadian disbursements (payroll, Quebec subcontractors, Revenu Québec remittances) clear via Lynx for high-value CAD transfers and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batched retail items, with overnight sweep into interest-bearing USD/CAD accounts. (v) USD disbursements to U.S. suppliers clear through the Canadian bank's U.S. dollar clearing channel or a U.S. correspondent; every outbound transfer above CAD 10,000 is screened against FINTRAC EFT reporting, OFAC/UN/EU/Canadian sanctioned-party lists, and export-control watchlists.Outright deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD) executed under a Master Forward Agreement or ISDA 2002 with the primary Canadian bank, requiring collateral under a Credit Support Annex or a working-capital credit facility.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts on non-convertible procurement currencies (offshore CNY/CNH, KRW, INR) for hedging equipment purchases from Asia, available only from credit-approved bank counterparties and booked on an ISDA 1992/2002 master with netting and close-out provisions.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) to convert USD-denominated export-finance or EDC-backed borrowings into CAD floating-rate exposure, and to align interest-payment currency with the LP's operational revenue mix.FX options and zero-cost collar structures (purchased options, put spreads, call spreads) to protect the USD price of exported vessels and to cap the CAD cost of EUR-denominated European equipment packages.Rolling spot (tom-next) hedging and FX swaps for short-dated timing gaps between LC cover payments, milestone drawdowns, and supplier settlements, including swap rollovers to prolong hedge tenors without disturbing underlying trade flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Conditional. Canada does not recognize the European 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category, and a foreign EMI may not take deposits, extend credit, or issue commercial letters of credit in Canada. Digital cross-border payment firms operating in Canada — such as Wise Payments Canada Inc. and Payoneer — must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under PCMLTFA and may be used only for ancillary, low-value operational outflows (crew payroll, subcontracted engineering fees, small-vendor payments, multi-currency market-rate conversion). They are not viable as primary treasury rails for an ISIC 3011 ocean-container-vessel shipyard because they lack: (i) documented-letter-of-credit issuance and confirmation (UCP 600), (ii) capital-escrow custody and milestone-drawdown certification services, (iii) Lynx/ACSS high-value CAD clearing access, (iv) OSFI-regulated deposit insurance and credit facilities, and (v) sanctions/export-control compliance depth suited to Medium_Risk_Enhanced maritime trade. Feasibility rating: Low for capital injection and LC escrow; Conditional-Medium strictly for non-trade disbursements below CAD 100,000 per transaction.
