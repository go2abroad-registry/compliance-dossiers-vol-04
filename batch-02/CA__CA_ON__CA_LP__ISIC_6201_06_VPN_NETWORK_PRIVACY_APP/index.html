Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident forming an Ontario limited partnership must register for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1/RC1-WEB. The registration covers applicable program accounts, including GST/HST, payroll, and, where applicable, corporate income tax. For Ontario provincial purposes, the partnership must also register for the Ontario HST and, if employing staff, for Employer Health Tax (EHT) through the Ontario Ministry of Finance or the BRO. A non-resident limited partner not otherwise carrying on business in Canada is generally not required to file Canadian income tax returns solely due to the LP's registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign non-resident forming an Ontario limited partnership must register for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1/RC1-WEB. The registration covers applicable program accounts, including GST/HST, payroll, and, where applicable, corporate income tax. For Ontario provincial purposes, the partnership must also register for the Ontario HST and, if employing staff, for Employer Health Tax (EHT) through the Ontario Ministry of Finance or the BRO. A non-resident limited partner not otherwise carrying on business in Canada is generally not required to file Canadian income tax returns solely due to the LP's registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box elective classification for partnerships; an Ontario LP is automatically a flow-through entity for Canadian income tax. The partnership may elect to have an earlier GST/HST registration effective date. Non-resident limited partners may jointly elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to file a Part I tax return in lieu of Part XIII non-resident withholding tax. A partnership may also use the GST/HST Quick Method for qualifying small suppliers to simplify remittances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including supplies by associated entities) in any trailing twelve consecutive months, excluding supplies of financial services and certain zero-rated exports; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada and to charges for digital services and intangible personal property supplied to non-residents who are outside Canada at the time of purchase. For a VPN privacy software service exported to a non-resident customer, the supply may be zero-rated under paragraph 7(g) of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the delivery is made by electronic means and the recipient is a non-resident individual or business outside Canada. The supplier must maintain documentary evidence demonstrating the non-resident status and physical location of the customer, the electronic delivery method, and the absence of Canadian use or enjoyment.

  • Customs export declaration (e.g., Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing shipment from Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Commercial invoice describing the exported digital services and non-resident recipient
  • Screenshots or system logs showing digital delivery by email or download
  • IP geolocation evidence confirming the customer is located outside Canada

No national real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance platform, or digital stamping exists for GST/HST. Registrants must issue and retain invoices containing prescribed information. Electronic filing is mandatory for GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenues exceeding CAD 1 million, using CRA's GST/HST NETFILE, GST/HST E-file via a service provider, or My Business Account. CRA may impose API-based record access requirements during audits but does not operate a centralized clearance API.

CRA auditors scrutinize zero-rated export claims and may deny zero-rating where documentation is inadequate, resulting in reassessment of GST/HST at 13%, arrears interest, and penalties. Penalties include 5% of the net tax balance owing plus 1% per full month up to 12 months (for late or deficient filings), and gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided. Making false statements, fraudulent claims, or knowingly participating in sham export arrangements can trigger criminal prosecution, fines of up to CAD 200,000, imprisonment for up to 5 years, and unlimited retrospective clawbacks for amounts previously refunded or credited.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a highly convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Act. Current account transactions, including software service exports and digital SaaS receipts, are freely convertible with minimal restrictions. Capital account transactions are liberalized but require reporting to the Bank of Canada for amounts exceeding thresholds. Profit remittances to non-resident limited partners are permitted after Canadian tax withholding and exchange control reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under Canadian law. All foreign exchange transactions must be reported to the Bank of Canada within 30 days. Proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the entity's discretion without statutory conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (Ontario general corporate rate: 11.5% provincial + 15% federal; limited partnership is fiscally transparent and partners are taxed at their own corporate/personal rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate: 25% on dividends paid to non-residents under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (for corporate shareholders); for a limited partnership, distributions of Canadian-source business income to non-resident limited partners are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (unless the income is attributable to a fixed place of business and therefore Part I taxable, or unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (aggregate fair market value of all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents) in a taxation year triggers the requirement to prepare and retain transfer-pricing documentation (s.247 of the Income Tax Act).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE, but the CRA can challenge transactions under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) if the transactions lack a bona fide commercial purpose. Further, the CRA considers whether a partnership is a valid partnership for tax purposes; a limited partner receiving guaranteed payments may be recharacterized as a loan; and the 'legal substance' of the limited partnership must reflect prudent commercial arrangements. Absence of economic substance can lead to reallocation of income under the transfer pricing rules, and the application of the 'sham' doctrine or GAAR can result in reassessments, penalties, and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded under the Income Tax Act s.238 and s.239; criminal prosecution under s.239 can result in a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment of up to 2 years, and restitution payments.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally liable for corporate tax deducted or withheld (including Part XIII) if not remitted; for the limited partnership, general partners face unlimited liability, and limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management or take part in the evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the transfer pricing rules with penalties under s.247(3) (10% of the net adjustment, minimum CAD 500); potential GAAR recharacterization (s.245) with a 25% penalty plus interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for negligence penalties under s.163.2 (misrepresentation) and cannot rely on the limited partnership's limited liability shield when they knowingly allow such schemes.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 500,000 for violations of Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); referral to FINTRAC; regulatory enforcement by FINTRAC or the Ontario Securities Commission; possible criminal charges and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are subject to administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) and can be personally liable for failing to implement a compliance program; even without a conviction, a director can be banned from managing a company for up to 5 years.
