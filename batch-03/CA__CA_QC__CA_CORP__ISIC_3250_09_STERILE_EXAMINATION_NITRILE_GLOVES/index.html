Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the QBCA; directors may accept share subscription consideration of any amount. A nominal authorized capital of CAD 1,000 divided into common shares is customary. No escrow account or deposit of funds is statutorily mandated for incorporation; however, the subscribed capital should be deposited into the corporate bank account opened after the certificate of incorporation to evidence the shareholders' contribution. Non-resident incorporators are permitted without escrow conditions, subject to notification obligations under the Investment Canada Act when a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business.

S-2.1), including a first-aid plan and chemical-hygiene protocol; mechanical and gas-safety inspection by the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ) where natural-gas curing ovens are installed; and, where applicable, a municipal combustion and air-emissions permit for the thermal oxidizer.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; CRA online portal 'My Business Account', Revenu Québec online portal 'Mon Dossier Entreprises'.

E-government filing portal

Register via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts (e.g., GST/HST, import/export, corporation income tax). Where substantive nexus exists in Quebec, also register with Revenu Québec for the QST account and provincial program accounts; the BN remains the common statutory tax identifier. Non-resident corporations apply using CRA forms RC464/RC4603 and may be registered without Canadian residency; the BN functions as the TIN for tax filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian business corporations are taxed as separate resident taxpayers; no pass-through check-the-box regime is available. — Register via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts (e.g., GST/HST, import/export, corporation income tax). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian business corporations are taxed as separate resident taxpayers; no pass-through check-the-box regime is available.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists; entity classification is non-elective. Available elections are limited and relief-specific, including Income Tax Act s. 216 (non-resident net rental income election). Hybrid mismatch rules are mechanical, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for both GST/HST and QST, measured over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); non-resident vendors may still need registration if they make taxable supplies in Canada and are not small suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada, provided the vendor obtains and retains prescribed export evidence (e.g., customs export declaration, bill of lading and payment proof). Surgical/medical nitrile gloves qualify as zero-rated exports if title passes to a non-resident, goods are physically removed from Canada, and the supply is not for consumption in Canada. Services to non-residents are zero-rated only when supplied to a non-resident and not performed in connection with Canadian real property or goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through CBSA (CARM/ACI or CERS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire transfer evidence
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Cargo control record or other proof of physical export

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Canada. Invoicing/record content is prescribed by CRA and Revenu Québec; mandatory electronic reporting applies to customs import/export through CBSA CARM/ACI e-manifest processes. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing standards (e.g., PEPPOL/EDI) may be adopted contractually.

CRA/Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated exports where documentary proof is missing or false; the entire GST/HST/QST becomes payable with retroactive effect, plus interest. Penalties include late-filing penalties (an initial 5% plus 1% per month), gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax, director liability, and potential criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims. Records must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with fully convertible current-account and capital-account transactions; the Canadian dollar floats freely without rate bands, approvals, or repatriation licensing. Dividend and branch-profit remittances face no FX restriction and are constrained only by Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate, reduced under applicable tax treaties) and Quebec-specific reporting formalities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain receipts indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian authorised banks, subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 cash/EFT reports) and prudential treasury risk policy.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is functionally shared with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), as Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary clearing rail: Canada has no standalone electronic-money-institution licensing regime and the Bank Act reserves the term 'bank' for federally chartered institutions; offshore digital EMIs may act only as FINTRAC-registered money-service-business adjuncts and cannot clear CAD on Lynx, issue banker's acceptances, or confirm commercial letters of credit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 50,000 for services; CAD 100,000 for other transactions (thresholds per non-resident counterparty).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits. Transactions lacking commercial rationale may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to remit source deductions or GST/HST; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations.
