Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca and GST/HST NETFILE; Ontario's 8% provincial HST component is administered and collected by CRA under the Ontario-Canada Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Ontario business corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act; (2) apply via CRA Business Registration Online for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the 'RT' GST/HST program account, or file Form RC1 in paper; (3) a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must register once its world-wide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive quarters (ETA s. 240); (4) voluntary registration is permitted below threshold; (5) Ontario payroll and corporate income tax accounts are attached to the same BN; no Ontario-resident director or registered agent is required for tax registration; CRA e-approval is generally instantaneous or within 5-10 business days for paper.

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, s. 7 for exported data-labeling and RLHF curation services supplied to a non-resident: (i) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the service is not supplied to an individual physically present in Canada; (iii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada; (iv) the service is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; and (v) contemporaneous documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status is maintained. Digital cross-border BPO services performed wholly in Ontario for an offshore AI principal qualify for the 0% rate, with corresponding input tax credit recovery on Canadian costs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (G13/CERS filing with Canada Border Services Agency) - required only for embedded physical goods; for pure services, the zero-rating evidence file substitutes a detailed commercial invoice referencing NAICS 518210 / ISIC 8220
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - applicable only to incidental goods shipments; for electronic delivery, a server transfer log or electronic service delivery receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank or payment-processor FX confirmation of inbound payment in USD/CAD; where settlement is routed via Mass PayPal Payout or Wise, the payout settlement report and FX conversion confirmation from the payment processor
  • Zero-rated export commercial invoice showing the supplier's GST/HST registration number and the annotation 'Supply to non-resident - zero-rated under Schedule VI, s. 7 ETA'
  • Non-resident status evidence - foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign corporate tax identification number, or equivalent OECD tax residency certificate of the overseas counterparty

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing regime exists federally or in Ontario; CRA mandates electronic GST/HST return filing (NETFILE) for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000; XML/API-based submissions are available through CRA My Business Account; voluntary Peppol network interoperability and EDI are accepted for customs declarations (CERS); Quebec will introduce mandatory B2B structured e-invoicing from 2026 but this has no extraterritorial effect on Ontario exporters.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within 4 years (ordinary) or 7 years (misrepresentation) per ETA s. 298; upon disallowance, 13% HST becomes payable with prescribed interest and clawback of input tax credits. Penalties: ETA s. 280 gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the tax or refund misstated; ETA s. 281.1 false statements penalty of 25% of the tax that would have been payable; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327(1) for knowingly making false statements attracts fines up to 250% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. No statutory 180-day remittance deadline exists for exported services; the 180-day figure is the banking/trade-compliance benchmark used for goods-export FX repatriation, with the CRA audit tolerance generally extending to 12 months for services. Exporters must retain the zero-rating documentation bundle for at least 6 years (ETA s. 286).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity injections, cross-border trade settlements, intercompany loans, or dividend and interest repatriation. Profit remittance from Ontario is restricted only by Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; treaty-reduced to 5-15% for dividends depending on holding structure) and by the solvency and financial-assistance tests of the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA). The CAD is a free-floating currency; there is no parity band, margin, or approval regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export or service-proceeds. AI data-labelling receipts may be held in USD, EUR, or any convertible currency indefinitely in domestic or foreign accounts. Obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting: (i) large cash or currency movement at the border exceeding C$10,000; (ii) electronic funds transfer reports for wires of C$10,000 or more; and (iii) CRA Form T1135 for specified foreign property exceeding C$100,000 CAD aggregate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary and systemic overseer; the Minister of Finance administers the Exchange Fund Account. Prudential regulation of banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering supervision is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High for mass disbursements; Medium as a substitute for a primary corporate operating account. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and PayPal (Mass Pay) are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses and are permissible rails for settling low-value RLHF contractor payments worldwide. Legal constraints: (i) an MSB/EMI cannot hold a Capital Escrow Account or issue share-subscription deposit certificates; (ii) CRA payroll source deductions and GST/HST remittance files typically require a Canadian Schedule I bank account; (iii) EMI rails lack correspondent-banking depth for large USD payments and cannot open bank-backed commercial letters of credit. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as treasury anchor; Wise/PayPal as disbursement overlay.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File filing with CRA, but contemporaneous documentation must exist for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions; Form T106 is mandatory where aggregate transaction value exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, economic substance is critical to corporate residence (ITA s.250), general anti-avoidance rule (s.245), treaty Limitation of Benefits or Principal Purpose Test provisions, and anti-hybrid rules; a bare Ontario mail-drop company is vulnerable to GAAR recharacterisation and treaty-benefit denial

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of understated tax; full reassessment plus compound interest and interim CRA collection measures

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in evasion face personal criminal fines, imprisonment, and forfeiture orders; s.227.1 imposes personal liability for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST where company defaults within 6 months and due diligence is not established; travel bans may be imposed as bail conditions
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) recharacterisation of the arrangement; thin-capitalisation rule under s.18(4) disallows interest on debt above 1.5:1 debt-to-equity; anti-hybrid rules (s.18.2); denial of treaty benefits under LOB/PPT; penalty under s.163(2) for false statements in T2

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on the corporate veil for unremitted withholdings; knowing signature of false returns triggers personal penalties; CRA may pursue Ontario court-ordered winding-up and director disqualification for abandoned shell structures
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) transfer price adjustment to arm's length; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of net adjustment where taxpayer does not make reasonable efforts; daily penalties for late T106; CRA audit including information requests with 90-day response; interest on additional tax

Director personal liability: Directors generally not personally liable unless they sign fraudulent transfer pricing documents; deliberate falsification exposes directors to Criminal Code fraud charges, civil fraud tort liability, and personal fines; travel restrictions may be attached to bail
AML and Payment Rails Misconduct

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA registration failure: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering offence: imprisonment up to 10 years; CRA disclosure of foreign receipts and unexplained deposits; asset freezing orders under proceeds-of-crime legislation

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in unregistered money transmission are personally prosecutable; fines up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and imprisonment; court-ordered passport surrender and travel bans during proceedings; director liability for associated tax evasion penalties if funds are undeclared
