Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary business registration and filing portal is My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) accessible through canada.ca; the Ontario Ministry of Finance administers the provincial component of the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) jointly with the CRA under the federal collection agreement and separately operates provincial programs such as Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) registration where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains its statutory Canadian tax identity, the Business Number (BN), from the CRA as follows: (1) determine GST/HST registration nexus; registration is mandatory where the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity and is not a small supplier (i.e., exceeds the CAD $30,000 threshold), or where the entity carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; (2) file Form RC1 Request for a Business Number or register online through Business Registration Online (BRO), supplying the foreign entity's full legal name, registered office address, date and jurisdiction of incorporation, and principal business activity; (3) select the required CRA program accounts, notably the GST/HST account (RT prefix), corporate income tax account (RC prefix) if resident in Canada, import/export account (RM prefix) for Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) purposes, and payroll account (RP prefix) if Canadian employees are engaged; (4) if the entity carries on business in Ontario, obtain an extra-provincial licence under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (Form 15) from the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services via Ontario Business Central; (5) upon approval, the CRA issues a 15-digit BN composed of a 9-digit base number, a 2-letter program identifier, and a 4-digit reference number, which is the statutory TIN and GST/HST VAT registration identifier under the Excise Tax Act (ETA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act; a federally or provincially incorporated corporation resident in Canada is taxable at the corporate level on worldwide income, with no federal pass-through; non-resident-owned business corporations are classified separately and taxed only on Canadian-source income; for GST/HST purposes the entity is a registrant person liable to collect and remit tax on taxable supplies made in Canada; Ontario applies no general tax-exempt zone and the subscription-box commercial activity is fully within the ordinary territorial scope of Canadian taxation).

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box entity classification election; however, the following statutory elections are available: (1) Income Tax Act section 85 rollover election permitting a tax-deferred transfer of eligible property to the corporation on incorporation; (2) Income Tax Act section 216 election allowing a non-resident with Canadian rental or timber income to file a net-basis Canadian income tax return; (3) Excise Tax Act section 156 election among closely related corporations (at least 90% common ownership) to treat inter-corporate supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating HST on intercompany charges; (4) Excise Tax Act section 179 election to treat supplies of financial services as having been made for taxable consideration; (5) voluntary small-supplier registration election under ETA section 240, where a supplier below the CAD $30,000 threshold may elect to register and charge HST; (6) ETA section 221.1 rebate framework for unregistered non-residents supplying outside Canada; hybrid mismatch treatment is determined by the OECD Multilateral Instrument and the Canada-US tax treaty rather than by a domestic check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding sales of capital property and financial services) measured over a single calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, being the small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148; a non-resident supplier of physical subscription boxes fulfilled from a Canadian warehouse or sold to Canadian consumers through Canadian distribution channels is required to register once the threshold is exceeded, or immediately upon commencing commercial activity in Canada where a permanent establishment exists; non-resident suppliers of specified digital supplies must register once specified-supply consideration to Canadian recipients exceeds CAD $30,000 over the preceding 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exports under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where (a) goods are supplied to a non-resident and possession is transferred in Canada, (b) the goods are exported as soon as is reasonably possible, generally within 12 months of delivery, (c) the goods are not further processed in Canada, and (d) the supplier maintains documentary evidence of export including shipping records and customs declarations; services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated where they are performed wholly outside Canada or relate to exported tangible property. For the ISIC 4791 subscription-box activity, shipments delivered to a foreign end-customer from a Canadian fulfillment point qualify as zero-rated exports when carrier proof of border exit and foreign delivery is retained, while shipments delivered within Ontario attract 13% HST. A registrant claims full input tax credits (ITCs) on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports and reflects the zero-rated supplies on line 103 of the GST/HST return; unregistered non-residents may claim a rebate under ETA section 221.1.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CBSA eManifest/ACI export transmission for shipments exceeding CAD $2,000 in value, or the carrier postal/courier export manifest for low-value parcel shipments
  • Commercial Invoice in English or French stating HS classification, Incoterms, declared export value, and destination country outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading or carrier waybill (ocean, air, or road) with proof of delivery at the foreign address (carrier POD, customs stamp, or delivery exception evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency settlement evidence (bank SWIFT/MT103, Stripe or Authorize.Net settlement statement, or foreign-exchange confirmation) demonstrating receipt and repatriation of the export proceeds within 180 days

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or government API clearance for GST/HST invoices; the CRA has, however, adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL standard for B2B transactions. Mandatory obligations include: (a) electronic filing and electronic remittance of GST/HST returns on behalf of every registrant whose annual taxable sales exceed CAD $1.5 million, filed through GST/HST NETFILE or an approved EFILE service provider API; (b) electronic submission of customs export data through the CBSA eManifest and ACE portal; (c) issuance of compliant GST/HST tax invoices showing the supplier's BN/RT registration number, date, tax point, description, consideration, and rate; (d) use of the CRA My Business Account web services API for registrants operating entirely without a Canadian physical presence; (e) retention of electronic books and records in readable format that must be made available to the CRA upon demand.

The CRA operates targeted export-verification audits and cross-references CBSA export transaction data with GST/HST returns; a registrant claiming zero-rating without adequate export evidence is reassessed at the applicable 13% HST on the full value, with retroactive clawback of all input tax credits and interest compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate under ETA section 280. Civil penalties include the gross-negligence penalty of up to 25% of the net tax understated or rebate overstated (ETA section 285.1(1)), the false-statement penalty, and a failure-to-register or failure-to-remit penalty equal to the greater of CAD $100 and 1% of the tax, plus a late-filing penalty of 6% of the tax due and a further 1% per full month of default up to 12 months. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating can result in fines of up to CAD $100,000 or up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years. GST/HST assessments generally have a 4-year statutory window under ETA section 298, but the limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established; supporting books and records must be maintained for 6 years. Though Canada does not impose a formal statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day settlement benchmark is applied by the CRA and commercial lenders as a substantiation norm; failure to demonstrate timely receipt of export proceeds triggers immediate reassessment, penalty, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar under both current and capital accounts, adhering to IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no statutory profit repatriation hurdles, and no requirement for central bank approval on cross-border transfers of capital, dividends, interest, or trade payments. Foreign investors may remit profits freely subject to Ontario corporate solvency tests, standard bank due diligence, withholding taxes on dividends (generally reduced under tax treaties), and reporting via NR4 forms, but no exchange control license or prior approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists under Canadian federal or Ontario provincial law. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds offshore, maintain foreign currency bank accounts, or convert freely to CAD at negotiated market rates. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; no statutory timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, PayPal) are viable as payment facilitators for subscription collection and supplier disbursements, but face legal constraints when functioning within Canada. Any entity carrying on money service business (MSB) activity in Canada must register with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; EMIs may not accept deposits or offer Canadian settlement accounts unless licensed under the Bank Act or exempted. For treasury and clearing, the optimal structure is a licensed Canadian bank operating account used as the primary settlement vehicle, supplemented by an EMI for foreign currency conversion or cross-border niche payments, never as the sole treasury rail.
