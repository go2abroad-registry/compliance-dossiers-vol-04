Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — official portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account service for federal registration; Ontario Ministry of Finance for applicable provincial registrations under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Ontario Limited Partnership must obtain a Business Number (BN) in the partnership's name through the CRA Business Registration Online service or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The application must identify the LP's registered Ontario office, all general partners, and a designated representative/agent in Canada. The CRA will simultaneously open a GST/HST program account where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the small supplier threshold; for exports a voluntary GST/HST registration is available. CRA normally issues the BN and program accounts electronically upon filing; paper filings take 7–10 business days. Ontario does not issue a separate provincial corporate/tax ID for LP purposes beyond the CRA BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax; partners include income/loss shares in their own filings. For GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act, the LP itself is treated as a separate 'person' and must register, collect and remit HST on taxable supplies. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada are subject to Canadian income tax on their Canadian-source share computed under Part I of the Income Tax Act; certain payments to non-resident partners may attract Part XIII withholding.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 96 operate mandatorily. Relevant elections for an Ontario LP include: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 273 election to deem partnership property supplies as supplies made by the partnership; (ii) ITA s. 98(3) rollover election on property transferred to the partnership; (iii) fiscal period election under ITA s. 99 for partnerships whose members use different year-ends; and (iv) treaty-related elections to attribute Canadian carrying-on-business to a permanent establishment for non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1), measured by worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or across the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding zero-rated supplies and supplies made outside Canada).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) goods exported by the GST/HST registrant as commercial exports under ETA Sch. VI Part I, with evidence of physical delivery outside Canada in the same state; and (ii) certain services and intangible supplies made to non-residents under ETA Sch. VI Part V, notably franchise/licence, royalty, recipe and training services supplied to offshore franchisees for use wholly outside Canada. Royalty/licence income from non-resident franchisees must be documented as a supply to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, and the contract must show foreign territorial use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A or approved EDI export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal/air waybill) evidencing export movement from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank foreign-exchange ledger confirmation showing the FX proceeds returned to Canada)

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Ontario/Canada. Invoices must display the HST registration number (BN), date, vendor name, character of the supply and tax charged; electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) are acceptable. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million are required to file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA NETFILE / My Business Account. Record retention is six years under ETA s. 286.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim if the required export documentation bundle is incomplete, converting the supply to taxable HST at 13%. Normal reassessment period is four years, extended to ten years where misrepresentation, negligence or fraud is established. Penalties include late remittance interest, gross negligence penalties of up to 20% of net tax (higher for repeat failures) under Excise Tax Act s. 280, and criminal liability under ETA s. 327 for false statements. Failure to evidence FX repatriation within the 180-day administrative benchmark will be treated as an audit red flag and supports retrospective clawback of the associated HST input tax credits and commissions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar under the IMF Article VIII framework. There are no general exchange controls, no central bank approval for routine commercial remittances, and no government-imposed black-market rates. Profit remittance is not capped by FX law; however, a Canadian limited partnership is a pass-through vehicle, so non-resident Chinese limited partners are taxable on their Canadian-source share of partnership income. Part XIII withholding tax of 25% may be applied to specified payments such as management fees, royalties, interest, rent or other passive Canadian-source amounts, subject to reduction under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement. Capital returns and loan repayments are structurally permissible but require robust documentation to demonstrate non-taxable principal. Every cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and proceeds must pass sanctions screening and anti-money-laundering controls maintained by OSFI-regulated financial institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rule and no statutory conversion ratio. A Canadian LP exporting goods, franchise licences, or intellectual property is free to retain foreign currency or convert it to CAD through a chartered bank, credit union or licensed foreign-exchange dealer. The only systematic obligations are FINTRAC reporting of large cross-border transactions and international electronic funds transfers, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting where applicable, and compliance with export/import permit regimes for food products and commercial equipment. There is no requirement to bring 100% of export proceeds onshore within any specific timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary foreign-currency receipts and low-value supplier payments; Low for core treasury settlement, franchise royalty collection, or capital escrow. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not authorized to take deposits in Canada and cannot open a true Canadian-deposit-account. They may offer payment services, prepaid cards, foreign-exchange conversion and SWIFT rails, but must comply with Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act registration through the Bank of Canada and with FINTRAC money-services-business registration if acting as an MSB. For the Ontario LP, an offshore EMI can be used as an inbound payment collector for small royalty balances or operational FX pocket, but in no way replaces a chartered bank account for statutory compliance, tax identification, or creditor protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing begins at the Chinese remitter. If the Chinese master franchisor injects common equity into the Ontario LP, the Chinese bank requires the remitter to be registered in SAFE's outward direct-investment regime, with the approved cross-border purpose code for overseas investment in a foreign enterprise. The wire is transmitted by SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian LP's designated chartered-bank capital-escrow account. On the Canadian side, the bank verifies the remitter's legal name, the Canadian LP's partnership registration, the UBO declarations, and the CRS/FATCA classification before crediting. All revealed FINTRAC fields must include the full partnership name, business number, remitter's address and a plain-language description reading 'capital contribution under Ontario LP agreement' to avoid unclassified government-entity or trade-settlement flags. Direct foreign individual equity injections from Chinese shareholders should be routed through normal family-remittance or investment channels only where permitted by Chinese law; corporate offshore-investment approval is mandatory for mainland Chinese companies.For Canadian-sourced franchisee royalty and licence fees that flow to the Chinese master franchisor, the primary clearing rail is an SWIFT MT103 international wire executed by a schedule-I Canadian bank after a CAD-to-CNH or CAD-to-CNY FX trade. Domestic Canada-wide operating expenses settle via the Lynx real-time large-value system or the Automated Clearing Settlement System for CAD electronic transfers. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Flow-through entity: no Ontario LP entity-level income tax. Federal general corporate rate 15%; Ontario general corporate rate 11.5% (combined 26.5%) for corporate partners; Ontario top marginal personal rate 53.53% (2024) for individual partners; non-resident partners taxed under Part I on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding under ITA s.212(2) applies to dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation; an Ontario LP's distributions to non-resident partners are not dividends — they are allocations of business income taxable under Part I, protected by the PE threshold in the Canada-China Tax Agreement (1986).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for controlled transactions with Chinese related parties exceeding C$1,000,000 annually for services, intangibles, royalties and interest, and C$5,000,000 for goods; Master File/Local File expectations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of C$750,000,000 or more (country-by-country reporting threshold); a small self-serve franchise paying royalty to a Chinese parent still needs a documented arm's length royalty methodology and a functional/cost analysis to survive a CRA audit.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is instead policed through the GAAR (ITA s.245), treaty beneficial-ownership and PE-attribution rules (Article 7 Canada-China DTA), Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act (a limited partner who participates in management loses limited liability), and CRA audit of whether the LP has real business premises, employees, POS systems, food-inventory, and local decision-making — absence of which converts the Chinese partner's income into Part XIII or Part I taxable amounts and defeats treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Cash Suppression

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under ITA s.163(2) and ETA s.285.1; criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239 — upon indictment a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (summary conviction: up to 2 years and 200% fine); CRA may also impose late-filing penalties and compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Ontario corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unreported GST/HST and payroll source deductions under ITA s.227.1 and ETA s.323; willful evasion can trigger director prosecutions, travel bans, and up to 5 years' imprisonment, with a civil restitution order against personal assets.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under ITA s.245 cancelling the tax benefit, restoring 25% Part XIII withholding and Part I taxation at full rates; denial of Canada-China treaty benefits on beneficial-ownership grounds; 50% gross negligence penalty; interest accruing from the date the tax was originally due; CRA may also void the partnership registration under the sham doctrine for a façade transaction.

Director personal liability: The Canadian general partner's directors face joint and several assessment for unremitted Part XIII amounts; a Chinese limited partner who exercises management functions loses limited liability and becomes jointly and severally liable for all partnership debts, including CRA tax liabilities, and cannot invoke treaty protection for income earned through a Canadian enterprise.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA s.247(2) adjusting the royalty/markup to the arm's length consideration; a 10% transfer pricing penalty on the adjustment amount (s.247(4)) if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to apply the arm's length standard; unpaid Part XIII and Part I assessments plus interest; in egregious cases a 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) and partial treaty benefit denial.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the corporate general partner's tax returns and approve the inflated royalty are jointly assessed for the TP penalty and Part XIII arrears; failure to withhold on the excessive required royalty is a statutory offence attracting 10–20% penalties, civil restitution, and travel-ban / criminal exposure where the mispricing is a deliberate tax evasion scheme.
Withholding and Instalment Default

Statutory sanction: Bond fide non-payment assessments: the partnership is made liable for the full unwithheld amount plus interest under ITA s.215(6) and s.227(8) penalties of 10% of the amount (20% if the failure was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence); late-filed T1159 penalties of C$25 per day (C$100–C$2,500); the CRA may assess the non-resident partner directly and demand security.

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors are personally and jointly liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts, payroll source deductions, GST/HST and Ontario EHT; CRA enforces against personal bank accounts, real estate, and other assets; directors are barred from discharging in bankruptcy for these statutory liabilities and may face criminal prosecution and cross-border asset freeze orders.
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for failure to report a large cash transaction, and higher cumulative penalties for systemic retail non-compliance; the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act also authorizes audits and seizure orders; underlying money laundering is a criminal offence carrying fines up to C$2,000,000 and up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who direct or acquiesce in cash suppression or deposit structuring are personally liable for FINTRAC penalties; ownership/control information failures in the Ontario registry can trigger 'failure to report' charges against individual directors; travel bans, statutory freezing of personal assets, and criminal imprisonment (up to 10 years) for laundering the proceeds of crime through the restaurant chain.
