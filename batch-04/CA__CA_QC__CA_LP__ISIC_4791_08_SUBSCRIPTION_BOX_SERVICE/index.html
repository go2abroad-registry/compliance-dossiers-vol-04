Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions may consist of money, property or rights (CCQ arts. 2253–2254) but a limited partner (commanditaire) may not contribute labour or services; all contributions must be recorded in the partnership register. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; subscription prepayments are deferred revenue held in an operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or through PCI-DSS-compliant merchant settlement accounts (Stripe Billing / Authorize.Net), subject to the Quebec Consumer Protection Act prohibitions on false or unconscionable representations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (attestation de conformité en sécurité incendie) issued by the municipal fire prevention service covering egress, storage of combustible cardboard in compliance with NFPA 13 sprinkler requirements, emergency lighting and signage; structural load clearance for racking and aisle configurations certified by an engineer under the Building Act; mandatory employer registration with the CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail) and adoption of a workplace health and safety program for loading docks and mechanical handling equipment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal GST/HST and Income Tax Act administration; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial income tax and the Quebec enterprise registry. Portals: CRA Business Registration Online, CRA My Business Account, Canada Business Network, and Revenu Québec Cl@rassant.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and the relevant program accounts through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Quebec registration is integrated through Revenu Québec's Form LM-1 and the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec), which also creates the QST account. The partnership, not the general partner, is the registrant for GST/QST and files the applicable information returns. A non-resident LP must designate a Canadian representative for service of process, provide the partnership agreement and the Quebec limited partnership registration extract (société en commandite), and open any required payroll or import/export accounts depending on operational footprint.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income is computed at the entity level for allocation to partners. Relevant elections include: (i) the Income Tax Act s. 96(2.1) election, filed with Form T5013/T5013SUM, allowing a non-resident partner's allocable share of Canadian partnership income to be taxed under Part I as Canadian business income instead of triggering gross-basis Part XIII withholding on distributions; (ii) the Excise Tax Act s. 273 partnership election to treat certain supplies as made by partners for GST/HST; and (iii) the corresponding Revenu Québec QST elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148 and the aligned QST small-supplier rule. Registration is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, tested on total taxable supplies made in Canada and elsewhere; voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate subscription-box goods sold to non-residents provided the tangible personal property is exported from Canada and delivered outside Canada, satisfying Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA (notably s. 1(e) and s. 1(f)) and the corresponding QST provisions. Recurring subscriber services supplied to non-resident customers may also be zero-rated where the services are not substantially performed in Canada and the recipient is outside Canada. Zero-rating is conditional on obtaining and preserving contemporaneous evidence of export, destination, and payment. Canada imposes no statutory outward-remittance deadline; the 180-day figure stated below is an internal repatriation and evidence-matching benchmark for subscription-export collections.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form A15414 or carrier-filed Electronic Export Report) filed within 15 days after export
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification for the subscription box contents, Incoterms, and declaration of Canada as country of origin
  • Transport document evidencing delivery outside Canada: Bill of Lading, sea or air waybill, or international parcel carrier record (Canada Post/USPS/FedEx/DHL)
  • Payment settlement evidence from Stripe Billing or Authorize.Net, including card-settlement reports, SWIFT confirmations, or bank/FX conversion receipts, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, no CRA or Revenu Québec API-based invoice validation, and no digital stamping requirement. Invoices must contain prescribed content, including the supplier's GST/HST and QST registration numbers, date, description of the subscription box goods, and tax charged. Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing is mandatory only for transactions with federal procurers; private-sector B2B e-invoicing in Quebec is governed by commercial agreement rather than tax clearance.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated claim within four years of the original assessment, extended to seven years for misrepresentation arising from neglect, carelessness, or omission, and without limitation in cases of willful fraud. False or misleading statements supporting an export zero-rating claim attract the Excise Tax Act s. 285 penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the overstated refund or net tax, plus interest under s. 280, and the corresponding QST penalty framework. Deliberate or grossly negligent conduct can also trigger the 50% gross-negligence penalty, criminal prosecution under tax evasion provisions, full clawback of the refund, and denial of future export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. No foreign exchange controls restrict the repatriation of dividends, partnership distributions, or loan repayments. The CAD floats freely; cross-border funds movements are subject to anti-money laundering reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), not exchange-control permits. Non-resident partners may remit profits without prior government approval, but banks impose standard source-of-funds and tax compliance documentation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may hold foreign currency balances in Canadian or offshore bank accounts without conversion obligations. However, under the PCMLTFA and FINTRAC regulations, a financial institution must prepare a large cash transaction report for cash payments of CAD 10,000 or more; international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more require reporting by the bank to FINTRAC. No statutory timeline requires conversion of export receipts into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Transferroom) may provide cross-border payment services to a Canadian-domiciled LP if the EMI holds a public money services business (MSB) registration under FINTRAC or operates under a permissible exemption. These EMIs are not licensed as Canadian deposit-taking institutions and therefore cannot offer CDIC-insured CAD accounts, issue Canadian bank clearing/routing numbers, or provide settlement through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS)/Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) directly. Legal constraints include the need to deposit client funds in trust at a Canadian bank, compliance with final payment and sanctions screening, and inability to hold or disburse funds as a substitute for a term operating bank account. For recurring subscription box cash flows, EMIs should be used only as supplementary disbursement conduits, not as the primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be executed via SWIFT MT103 (international credit transfer) from the non-resident partner's bank to the LP's Canadian operating account. Remittance instructions must include a descriptive narrative such as 'Capital contribution to Quebec limited partnership for subscription box business.' The bank will require the exact LP legal name, bank BIC/SWIFT code, and domestic account/routing number (institution + transits). If the transfer amount exceeds CAD 10,000, the receiving bank reports the details to FINTRAC. There is no need for a surrender certificate or government exchange-control permit; however, the LP's KYC file must already include the source of funds and the partnership structure before any inbound credit is booked.Outbound trade payments for international inventory and logistics vendors should be routed primarily over the SWIFT network using MT103 messages, typically in the vendor's invoiced currency or CAD under a spot conversion. For high-value or recurring production purchases, banks may issue commercial letters of credit (clause of UCP 600) with applicable safeguards. Domestic disbursements (e.g., Quebec warehouse leases, carrier invoices) clear via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or through ACSS electronic debit. Bulk and recurring payments can use DTCC or pre-authorized debits; all outbound wire instructions must be pre-validated against bank sanctions lists and the LP's KYC internal procedures. For USD payments, the LP may utilise a USD clearing USD account maintained at the same Canadian bank or a correspondent U.S. bank; otherwise, banks convert internally at a pre-agreed desk rate.Vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and other liquid trade currencies up to typical 12-month tenors.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging market currencies (e.g., RMB, MXN) where offshore deliverable forwards are unavailable or illiquid.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term USD funding or structural trade payables driven by recurring subscription box imports.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at LP entity level (fiscally transparent); corporate partners are subject to combined federal and Quebec corporate rate of 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec) on allocated taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and deemed dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; not typically applied to LP distributions as an LP is fiscally transparent – non-resident partners are instead subject to Part I tax on their allocated Canadian-source business income, with no separate dividend withholding at the partnership level)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of transactions with non-resident related parties triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (and filing of T106 information return) for taxpayers with gross revenue under CAD 50,000,000; for larger taxpayers, any related-party cross-border transaction above a threshold of CAD 5,000,000 requires full Master and Local File documentation. Even below the threshold, all non-arm's length transactions must be reported on Form T106 (or TP-1068 for Quebec) and reflect arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not enforce a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and Part X.1 of the Quebec Taxation Act requires that transactions have a real economic purpose and are not arranged primarily to obtain a tax benefit. For the Quebec LP, the partnership must have substantive commercial activities (warehousing, inventory management, order fulfilment, customer service) and the subscribed capital of limited partners must be actually paid in and at risk; otherwise, deductions and losses may be denied under at-risk rules (s. 96(2.1) and s. 143.2), and a corporate general partner may be treated as a shell if it lacks real control and assets.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 50% (increased to 100% for false statements made on returns filed after April 1, 2024) of the tax sought to be evaded; criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act and section 49 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act carries fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax debts; a limited partner who participates in management loses limited liability; directors or officers of a corporate general partner may be liable for unremitted source deductions (CPP, EI, GST/HST, QST) under section 227.1 of the ITA and may face travel bans, asset freezes, or civil restitution orders under section 160
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR and Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR denying the intended tax benefit; transfer pricing penalties of 10% on the net transfer pricing adjustment if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000 and no reasonable efforts are documented; loss of limited liability protection under Quebec civil law for partners who exercise control; Quebec Registraire des entreprises can also strike the LP off the registry for non-compliance with the shareholder transparency register requirements

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its directors may be held liable under asset-transference rules (section 160 ITA) for tax owed by the partnership if assets were transferred to a non-resident for less than fair value; a director with significant outstanding tax debt may be barred from travel by the CRA under the Tax Collection Rules, and the Quebec Minister of Revenue can issue a restriction certificate preventing the director from selling, leasing, or purchasing certain assets
Transfer Pricing Mispricing with Foreign Affiliates

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment plus a penalty of 10% of the total net adjustment under section 247(3) of the Income Tax Act when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; interest is charged on deficiencies; Revenu Québec may apply a parallel penalty under section 48.0.1 of the Tax Administration Act and disallow the deferral or deduction entirely if the arrangement is artificial

Director personal liability: The general partner (or director of the corporate general partner) is jointly and severally liable for the transfer pricing penalty and interest; if the general partner is a non-resident shell, its directors can be served under Canadian cross-border business standards and may face seizure of Canadian assets, garnishment of subscription box receivables, and personal liability to the extent of distributions received from the LP during insolvency
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Registraire des Entreprises Non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Quebec's Act to Improve Corporate Transparency (2019), an LP can be administratively dissolved or struck off for failing to provide beneficial ownership information; administrative fines up to CAD 100,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for corporations; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties for failing to implement an AML compliance program can reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporate entity and CAD 500,000 for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP can be personally prohibited from serving on any Quebec board for up to five years, and the registraire may impose personal fines; CRA and Revenu Québec can pursue the directors personally for unpaid GST/QST and income tax amounts if the corporate general partner is deemed to be a nominee, effectively piercing the corporate veil; serious AML violations may lead to a personal criminal record, imprisonment, and, where assets in Canada are held through the LP, provisional freezing orders restricting the director's bank accounts
