Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for limited partnerships under the Civil Code of Québec. The capital contributions are fixed in the limited partnership agreement; limited partners must contribute the agreed amount or property in exchange for partnership interests. No provincial escrow account is mandatory, but certain aerodrome, trust company, or non-resident ownership rules may require escrow or account holds if a general partner is a corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire service (e.g., Service de sécurité incendie de Montréal) under the Québec Building Code and the Fire Safety Act, confirming that the injection molding equipment and plastic resin storage areas are compliant with fire separations, sprinklers, and emergency routes. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal registration portal (Business Registration Online via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec — provincial registration portal for QST and NEQ (IN-1082 via https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/)

E-government filing portal

The non-resident limited partnership, having a permanent establishment in Quebec, must first register through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain a federal Business Number (BN). Concurrently, the partnership appoints a Canadian-resident representative and files the Revenu Québec 'Registration Form for a Business' to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and QST registration number. Required supporting documentation includes the certified declaration of constitution, the partnership agreement identifying the general partner, details of the place of business in Quebec, and proof of authority for the agent (GST/HST Form RC59 for non-resident registrants). Registration must be completed before issuing taxable invoices; the GST/HST and QST accounts become active as of the required registration date or the voluntary election date.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Quebec); the partnership files an information return (T5013) but is not liable for income tax on its revenues. Each partner (including non-resident partners in respect of Canadian-source income) is taxable on their pro-rata share of income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Quebec); the partnership files an information return (T5013) but is not liable for income tax on its revenues. Each partner (including non-resident partners in respect of Canadian-source income) is taxable on their pro-rata share of income. However, for GST/HST/QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and registrant, as provided for under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Sales Tax Act (implied partial-taxation hybrid).

Available election forms

Canada and Quebec do not have 'check-the-box' entity classification elections; classification follows statutory rules. Available elections include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under ITA subsection 249.1(4) to adopt an off-calendar year-end and align with non-resident partners' fiscal years; (ii) election under ETA subsection 273(1) to treat a non-resident partner's supplies through the partnership as being made by the partnership (avoiding bifurcated registration); (iii) election to claim the rebate of tax paid on partnership inputs by the general partner, with the partnership receiving the rebate; (iv) hybrid mismatch remedial elections under ITA sections 18.6-18.7 and the OECD BEPS Action 2 domestic rules, which adjust deductions by reference to dual-status foreign check-the-box entities; and (v) QST input tax refund elections allowing the partnership to compute ITRs on a GST-inclusive or streamlined formula.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier exemption applies where worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services and certain capital property sales) are less than CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory under ETA subsection 240(1), and the identical CAD 30,000 threshold triggers compulsory QST registration under the Quebec Sales Tax Act (CO 322 r. 2). No sector-specific threshold applies to NAICS 334118; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits on start-up costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the sale of custom mechanical keyboard caps under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20 of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are physically exported by the supplier or caused to be exported by the non-resident purchaser, and are exported as soon as is reasonably possible after the delivery/sale. Comparable QST zero-rating applies under the Taxation Act and its regulations for goods exported to a destination outside Canada. The partnership must be a registrant, hold evidence of export within the statutory period, and the buyer must be a non-resident for GST/HST purposes. Offshore services (such as design or digital renderings supplied to non-resident clients) may also be zero-rated under Schedule VII, s. 7 of the ETA. Incoming payments through Shopify Payments or Stripe may be received in CAD or foreign currency; Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for zero-rating, so the 180-day window is adopted as the internal documentary-control benchmark and general banking/customs compliance convention.

Canada and Quebec do not operate mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance for e-invoices. Mandated standards include: (i) mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns via CRA My Business Account and QST returns via Revenu Québec CLIC; (ii) issuance of invoices in both English and French where the invoice is used in Quebec (Quebec Charter of the French Language requirements) with equivalent data on each linguistic version; (iii) record retention in XML or machine-readable formats for at least 6 years; and (iv) phased adoption of the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (Peppol-compatible standards, 2024-2026). No digital stamping or mandatory third-party clearance of individual invoices is required; however, the partnership must ensure its Shopify Payments/Stripe billing outputs satisfy CRA and Revenu Québec informational content requirements.

CRA and Revenu Québec exporter verification programs scrutinize zero-rated export claims for documentation sufficiency and diversion risk. If goods are diverted to the domestic Canadian market within two years, or documentation is incomplete, the comprehensive audits may re-assess the supply at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST, disallow related input tax credits, and assess interest from the original due date. Penalties include the ETA s. 280 false-statement penalty of 25% of the net tax amount involved, an additional gross-negligence penalty of 25%, and penalties of up to 50% for misrepresentation under Quebec civil rules. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion or fraudulently claiming zero-rating can result in fines of up to 200% of tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; the limitation period retroactively extends from 4 years to 7 years where misrepresentation is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and investment transactions. Current-account payments, including imports, exports, service fees, royalties, interest, dividends, and ordinary profit distributions, generally do not require central-bank approval or conversion into Canadian dollars. Capital-account flows, including partner contributions, partnership interests, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and repatriations, are generally permitted without exchange-control approval, subject to contractual restrictions, bank compliance review, Canadian tax rules, withholding-tax obligations, sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, beneficial-ownership verification, and applicable reporting. A Quebec limited partnership should maintain executed partnership agreements, partner registers, contribution records, loan agreements, tax forms, invoices, and board or partner resolutions supporting the legal and commercial purpose of each transfer. Non-resident partners may generally receive distributions, but the partnership must evaluate Canadian withholding, information-return, permanent-establishment, transfer-pricing, and treaty implications before payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate or convert export receipts. Export proceeds may normally remain in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts, subject to the account agreement and bank controls. Large cash transactions, international electronic funds transfers, suspicious transactions, and certain cross-border transfers may trigger reporting by financial institutions or other reporting entities under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related regulations. Customs, tax, sales-tax, sanctions, and export-control records must nevertheless be retained and reconciled with accounting records.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing currency controls, mandatory conversion, capital controls, or routine approval requirements for cross-border payments. Anti-money-laundering supervision is principally administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, and prudential supervision of federally regulated banks is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to high for payment acceptance and settlement, but not a substitute for a Canadian bank account in all use cases. Canada does not operate a single European-style electronic-money-institution licensing regime. An offshore digital EMI or payment institution may provide collection, foreign-exchange, card, or payout services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and the Canadian activities permitted by applicable law. Canadian payment-service-provider obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act may apply to prescribed payment functions performed in Canada. Shopify Payments and Stripe availability, settlement currencies, reserve requirements, chargeback exposure, prohibited-business rules, identity verification, and Canadian tax information must be confirmed directly with each provider. Offshore wallets may be rejected for partner capital, payroll, tax remittances, regulated trust funds, or high-volume commercial flows. Maintain a Canadian operating account for tax, payroll, refunds, supplier payments, and liquidity back-up.

Canadian payment-service-provider obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act may apply to prescribed payment functions performed in Canada. Shopify Payments and Stripe availability, settlement currencies, reserve requirements, chargeback exposure, prohibited-business rules, identity verification, and Canadian tax information must be confirmed directly with each provider. Offshore wallets may be rejected for partner capital, payroll, tax remittances, regulated trust funds, or high-volume commercial flows. Maintain a Canadian operating account for tax, payroll, refunds, supplier payments, and liquidity back-up.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

NIL at the Limited Partnership level (fiscally transparent flow-through). Applicable combined federal and Quebec general corporate rate for a corporate partner is 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec); individual partners are taxed at progressive personal rates, plus the Quebec Health Contribution Fund where applicable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

N/A — a Limited Partnership does not declare dividends. For distributions or allocations paid or credited to a non-resident limited partner, the statutory Part XIII withholding rate is 25% on the gross amount unless reduced by a tax treaty; active business income sourced to a Quebec permanent establishment may instead be taxed under Part I on a net basis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents must be filed where the aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal period. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) should be prepared whenever such threshold is met; CBCR/Country-by-Country Reporting documentation is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245, Income Tax Act), Quebec's equivalent GAAR (section 1079.1, Taxation Act), and CRA administrative practice require that a Quebec LP creates, manages, and executes genuine commercial activities in Quebec. For a custom mechanical keyboard keycap operation, economic substance is demonstrated by physical injection-moulding equipment, real inventory in Quebec, employees or subcontractors, management decisions, and commercial risk exposure. An LP with no office, no employees, and no local decision-making risks re-characterization as a sham or conduit without tax effect.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239 and Quebec Taxation Act corresponding offences: fines from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment, and seizure of assets; CRA may also impose gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax and vacate the normal reassessment limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unremitted withholding taxes and interest if not exercised reasonable care; criminal conviction for evasion allows civil recovery against personal assets, court-ordered travel restrictions, bail conditions, and extradition exposure in cases involving cross-border concealment.
Shell Company / Sham Limited Partnership

Statutory sanction: GAAR permits CRA and Revenu Quebec to deny the tax benefit, reallocate income to the actual economic operator, and assess gross-negligence penalties plus interest; Revenu Quebec can cancel QST registrations retroactively and deem a permanent establishment in Quebec on the foreign parent.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil will be pierced where the LP is a mere instrumentality; general partners and directing minds are personally liable for unremitted GST/QST and unpaid corporate taxes, and risk personal disqualification orders and asset freezes in civil enforcement proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, CRA may adjust the transaction to arm's-length terms and impose a penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts; interest accrues from the original due date, and the T106 filing omission itself attracts daily information-return penalties up to CAD 2,500; double taxation may be mitigated only through mutual agreement procedure under the applicable DTA.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's-length arrangements can be named in a penalty assessment under section 247 as 'liable persons'; in fraud cases, personal liability for the company's tax debt may be asserted through the unpaid-source-deduction provisions and civil conspiracy claims.
Anti-Money Laundering and Structuring

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for a first violation; knowing money laundering is a criminal offence punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment; Revenu Quebec and FINTRAC may freeze accounts and confiscate property connected to the unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly controlled or facilitated the structuring are criminally liable despite the LP's limited liability; the limited liability shield is forfeited for personal participation in an offence, and individuals face arrest warrants, bail travel restrictions, and orders of restitution or forfeiture.
