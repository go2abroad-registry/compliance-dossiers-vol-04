Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in all cases not less than CHF 50,000 in total, must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount is deposited in a blocked escrow account in the name of the company under incorporation with a Swiss bank, and is released only upon entry of the company in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (Brandschutznachweis) from the cantonal fire prevention authority; structural load inspection and fit-out acceptance by the building inspectorate; occupational health and safety clearance under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and the Accident Insurance Act (UVG), including a Suva risk assessment for kitchen fire suppression systems and ventilation duct hygiene.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in all cases not less than CHF 50,000 in total, must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount is deposited in a blocked escrow account in the name of the company under incorporation with a Swiss bank, and is released only upon entry of the company in the Commercial Register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV / AFC / AFF) — official portal: estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax administrations (e.g., Zuger Kantonale Steuerverwaltung) for income tax assessment of the Aktiengesellschaft

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG obtains its statutory Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID / IDE / IDI) upon mandatory entry in the Commercial Register (Zefix / Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt). For Swiss VAT purposes the same UID is activated at the Swiss Federal Tax Administration by filing the registration form for VAT liability (Mehrwertsteuer-Formular) via the ESTV ePortal 'EasyGov' or 'etoile' once the CHF 250,000 taxable turnover threshold is met; foreign entities with a Swiss establishment or permanent establishment must register through the same procedure by providing certified articles of association, power of attorney, and a tax domicile declaration under Art. 11, Art. 66 and Art. 67 del Swiss Federal Act on Value Added Tax (MWSTG / LTVA). The UID serves as both business identification and VAT ID, following the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None — Switzerland has no check-the-box regime. An Aktiengesellschaft is per se a tax-transparent entity for Swiss income tax purposes (subject to Swiss corporate income tax at the federal, cantonal and communal levels). No hybrid mismatch or entity classification elections are available under Swiss law; optional instruments are limited to VAT group registration (Mehrwertsteuer-Gruppierung) under Art. 15 MWSTG and group taxation for federal direct tax only under the domestic group taxation regime of Art. 75 DBG, which does not alter the legal entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 in worldwide taxable turnover per fiscal year (Art. 10 para. 1 MWSTG); no separate reduced threshold for foreign-owned entities; a lower CHF 150,000 threshold applies only to non-profit institutions and charitable organisations and CHF 100,000 for public-benefit entities — not applicable to a commercial AG operating an overseas hotpot restaurant. Voluntary registration permitted from CHF 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically transported from Switzerland to a foreign destination qualifies for the 0% VAT rate under Art. 23 MWSTG provided the exporter of record holds valid customs declarations and proof of transport. Services (including catering and condiment supply royalties) supplied to foreign recipients are zero-rated when the recipient's place of business is abroad and the service is effectively used abroad (Art. 18 and Art. 23 para. 2 MWSTG); restaurant supplies delivered inside Switzerland to foreign tourists remain taxable at the standard 8.1% or reduced 2.6% rate as applicable. The zero-rating entitlement requires that the supplier is properly registered, issues compliant invoices, and files quarterly VAT returns; input VAT on purchases attributable to zero-rated exports is fully creditable either by way of the invoice method or the net-tax-rate method where legally available.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / déclaration d'exportation) filed electronically through the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (EZV / OGD) incl. NCTS export accompanying documents and seals
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and underlying export contract evidencing the sale and destination
  • Transport documentation: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR consignment note or Swiss postal export receipt proving physical cross-border dispatch
  • Proof of payment: Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation or bank extract evidencing receipt of export proceeds from abroad
  • VAT export evidence ledger and Swiss VAT return (reporting zero-rated export turnover), maintained for the statutory 10-year retention period

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions in an increasing number of cantons and at the federal level (based on eBill / Swiss QR Invoice / Factur-X and ZUGFeRD formats), while B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary. Transmission and exchange of XML and JSON-based e-invoice files is permitted and must be archived in original format for a 10-year retention period. VAT returns are filed electronically (VAT portal of ESTV 'etax'), with staggered filing dates; no instantaneous tax authority clearance or per-transaction stamping applies. Export transactions must be evidenced electronically via the customs system in force at exportation and kept in an auditable electronic export dossier.

Switzerland imposes no separate exchange-control repatriation deadline; however the Swiss Federal Tax Administration applies a strict substantive-evidence approach in export VAT audits and may treat missing proof of physical export or missing payment flow as an unlawful zero-rating application. Penalties under Art. 138 MWSTG include a surcharge of one to four times the tax evaded, CHF 60,000 maximum for unintentional negligence and up to CHF 180,000 plus recovery of tax for intentional violations; criminal sanctions may be imposed for wilful falsification of export records under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 186-193 StGB / Art. 118-119 MWSTG), including imprisonment of up to three years, asset forfeiture, disqualification of directors, and retrospective clawback of input VAT deductions with 4.5% compensatory interest per annum, computed from the date of the original refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full, unrestricted currency convertibility across both capital and current account transactions. There is no legislative difference in treatment between capital account contributions and current account profit remittances; dividends, branch profits, loan repayments, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely in CHF or any foreign currency without prior administrative approval. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to (i) Swiss anticipatory tax at 35% on dividends paid by a Swiss AG, recoverable partially under double-tax treaties; (ii) bank-internal KYC documentation proving beneficial ownership, economic substance, and source of funds; and (iii) transfer pricing documentation for cross-border intragroup payments to overseas branches or condiment suppliers. No exchange control permission from the SNB or SECO is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion quota, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export or trading proceeds. The hotpot restaurant AG may lawfully retain foreign currency receivables in Swiss or overseas bank accounts indefinitely. The only binding obligations are commercial bookkeeping under the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR Art. 957 et seq.), VAT accounting under the Swiss VAT Act, and arm's-length transfer pricing documentation for cross-border trade and intercompany settlements with the overseas branch network.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale Suisse under the National Bank Act (NBA) and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA/WZG), with prudential supervision and enforcement delegated operationally to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low for Stage 4 cross-border treasury. Swiss FINMA-licensed fintech providers (Yuh, Neon, and exempt payment service platforms under the Fintech Ordinance) are viable only for domestic merchant acquiring, customer wallet refunds, and small-value retail FX; they lack full banking licences and therefore cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, act as correspondent anchor for inbound ODI capital, or provide SIC-eligible clearing settlement for large CHF transactions. PSD2 European E-money Institutions (EMIs) holding multi-currency IBANs can legally service Swiss corporates only for payment initiation and account information services, and are structurally unsuitable for LC issuance, import financing, and UCP 600 trade documents. The recommended architecture is to use fintech rails exclusively for front-of-house collections and to route all inbound capital injections and outbound trade disbursements through Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer routing for inbound equity and shareholder loans: SWIFT MT103 (gpi) or MT202 for large-value injections addressed to the Tier-1 Swiss account, carrying explicit purpose codes — 'EQUI' for share capital contributions supported by notarised board resolutions and commercial register extract; 'LOAN' for shareholder loans evidenced by a signed loan agreement; 'CACA' or 'INTC' for current-account funding of overseas branch operations. For funds originating from mainland China, the remitting bank must evidence completed ODI/SAFE registration and the Swiss receiving bank must maintain full originator information and a structured payment reference matching the AG's internal ledger. Frauds and sanctions screening applies on the full payment chain; any discrepancy between the stated purpose and the AG's commercial register capital schedule triggers automatic compliance hold requiring notarised documentation. All injections are journalised against the capital contribution or intercompany loan ledger before deployment.Trade settlement to overseas suppliers and branch vendors is executed through the Tier-1 bank's trade finance desk over four permissible rails: (i) Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Swiss bank via SWIFT MT700/MT710 and governed by UCP 600, with presentation of transport documents (bills of lading, CMR notes, air waybills) and commercial invoice compliance; (ii) direct SWIFT MT103 corporate payments for invoices below the LC threshold, with required purpose code narrative and standard anti-money laundering screening; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) for EUR-denominated purchases from European condiment and equipment suppliers; (iv) internal branch funding in CHF via SWIFT MT101/MT103 toward the overseas branch operating accounts. All outbound channels are protected by a dual-approval four-eyes payment factory with pre-set transaction limits and a bank-facilitated FX conversion at the corporate mid-rate plus 0.25%-0.50% spread; supplier auto-pay runs execute only against matching three-way statements of purchase order, goods receipt, and invoice.Outright FX Forward (Vanilla) negotiated against CHF with a licensed Tier-1 Swiss bank, provided the underlying trade invoice or branch funding schedule is evidencedFX Swap (spot/forward combination) permitted for short-term working capital and same-settlement clearing of EUR, USD, and CHF pairsCross-Currency Swap (CCS) for converting a foreign-currency shareholder loan into CHF financing, with ISDA/CSA master agreement requiredNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) with net settlement for restricted-convertibility currencies (e.g., CNY, KRW) where the remitting jurisdiction limits offshore deliverability, subject to strict underlying-trade documentationVanilla options and zero-cost participating collars for protecting the overseas hotpot branch revenue stream from adverse FX moves, available only after credit-line approval and suitability clearance by the bank's treasury advisory desk
