Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account restriction are imposed by the Civil Code of Québec or the REQ. Capital commitments are fixed by the partnership agreement; limited partners must contribute money or property (not services). In practice, an initial cash contribution should be funded to the LP bank account to cover first-year registry, tax and professional fees before trading begins.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory: (i) CNESST employer registration and occupational health and safety prevention program under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1) once any employee is hired; (ii) fire-safety inspection and occupancy certificate from the municipal fire prevention service under the Fire Safety Act (CQLR c S-3.4), which will include review of evacuation plans and emergency lighting; (iii) structural load compliance of the warehouse floor for copper cathode stacks, certified by a Quebec engineer per the Quebec Construction Code (CQLR c B-1.1, r. 2); (iv) no Certificate of Authorization under the Dam Safety Act applies unless material is stored near a watercourse.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal administrator for GST/HST and federal income tax, accessible through the CRA My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE; Revenu Québec (RQ) — provincial administrator for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial income tax, accessible through the Mon dossier d'entreprise and Cl@SSI e-services portals; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) — enterprise registrar and issuer of the Québec enterprise number (NEQ) for limited partnerships.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Quebec limited partnership (CA_LP): (1) file the declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the NEQ, which serves as the provincial tax identifier; (2) apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, including a GST/HST account where taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or where the LP carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; (3) register with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V for the QST account and any required provincial tax accounts; (4) file the partnership information return, comprising CRA Form T5013 Summary and T5013 slips together with Revenu Québec RL-15 summaries, to allocate income and losses to partners; (5) non-resident general partners carrying on activity in Quebec must appoint a Quebec attorney or representative and, where required, provide Revenu Québec with an undertaking for tax remittance, while passive limited partners are not separately registered unless their own activities create a Quebec permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; the CA_LP is fiscally transparent by default under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 96 and the corresponding Quebec Taxation Act provisions, so no election is required to secure pass-through treatment. Statutory elections available to the LP include: (i) ITA section 97(2) election to defer partnership income on property contributions made to the LP; (ii) ITA sections 98(3) and 98(5) elections on partnership merger, dissolution, and distribution; (iii) ITA section 85(1) rollover elections when partnership property is transferred to a taxable Canadian corporation; (iv) GST/HST and QST elections among affiliated registrants for supplies between them; (v) the functional currency election under ITA section 261 permitting tax accounts in US dollars; and (vi) hybrid mismatch coordination rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.21, which apply mechanically in alignment with the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework and require no elective filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies for both the GST/HST and QST small-supplier exemptions; GST and QST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the threshold or when the LP carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; GST applies at importation with no threshold for copper cathodes brought into Canada; special no-threshold registration regimes exist for non-residents supplying specified digital and intangible services to Canadian consumers but do not apply to the physical goods trading scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, section 20 of the Excise Tax Act, mirrored for QST purposes, a supply of copper cathodes is zero-rated where the CA_LP sells the goods in the ordinary course of business and the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside Canada, with possession or ownership transferring outside Canada. Direct exports must be supported by transport documents evidencing the foreign destination. For LME warrant trades, zero-rating applies where the warrant covers metal stored in non-Canadian LME warehouses because the supply occurs outside Canada; where the warrant requires delivery from a Quebec warehouse, the LP must physically export the goods and retain proof of border departure. Qualifying services supplied to non-residents in connection with offshore metal, such as storage, handling, freight arranging, and brokerage, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act. The 0% rate preserves full GST/HST and QST input tax credit entitlement for the LP, and net refunds may be claimed on each periodic return.

  • Bill of Lading — original negotiable copy or authenticated electronic equivalent showing the foreign discharge port

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoice validation mandate currently exists at the federal or Quebec level. Suppliers must issue invoices containing the prescribed data, including supplier name, federal Business Number and GST/HST registration number, QST registration number, invoice date, description of goods, and tax amounts, and may issue invoices electronically by mutual consent. Filing is performed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec e-services using EDI, XML, or API channels. Quebec has announced a roadmap toward mandatory B2B electronic invoicing built on the PEPPOL and CT XML standards, with phased implementation expected from 2026 for large enterprises and subsequent phases for smaller suppliers.

Export zero-rating claims on copper cathodes are subject to heightened audit scrutiny by the CRA and Revenu Québec, with particular focus on related-party cross-border sales, circular LME warrant trades, reshipment of goods back into Canada, and missing transfer-pricing documentation. Where export substantiation is insufficient, the authorities will reverse the 0% rate, reassess GST/HST and QST at the combined 14.975% rate with compound interest from the original filing due date, and may disallow input tax credits. Civil penalties include fixed compliance penalties for failure to register, file, or provide prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, and false-statement or gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated net tax under Excise Tax Act section 285 and the corresponding Quebec Tax Administration Act provisions. Criminal tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act can result in fines of up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years for directors, partners, or agents involved. Reassessment beyond the standard limitation windows is available in cases of misrepresentation, and there is no limitation period where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under Canada's Article VIII IMF obligations. No approval, registration quota, or licensing applies to inbound capital subscriptions, outbound trade payments, or profit repatriation from a Quebec LP. Non-resident partners of a Quebec LP remain taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to the Quebec permanent establishment; Part XIII withholding tax applies (base 25%, treaty-reduced) only to passive remittances (dividends, interest, royalties), not to commercial trade proceeds or genuine capital repayments. The Investment Canada Act applies only for non-Canadian control of a Canadian wholesale business above review thresholds; otherwise a notification suffices, and no FX deposit requirement is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls with the expiry of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act impose no surrender obligation, no conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. A Quebec exporter of copper cathodes may retain 100% of USD/EUR proceeds onshore in a US-dollar concentration account or offshore without any Bank of Canada reporting, provided each electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more is captured in FINTRAC's EFTR regime.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and manager of the Exchange Fund Account, but Canada does not operate a centralized foreign-exchange control administration. Oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada (policy), OSFI (banking solvency and reserve requirements), FINTRAC (cross-border electronic fund transfer reporting under the PCMLTFA), and Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for money-service business registration. No exchange-control license is required for copper cathode wholesale trading.
