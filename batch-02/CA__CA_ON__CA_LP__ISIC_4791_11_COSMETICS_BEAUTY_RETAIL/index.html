Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Program; business registration and returns through CRA's 'My Business Account' portal. For Ontario-specific tax filings, the Ontario Ministry of Finance administers the Employer Health Tax (not applicable to a pure partnership) and provincial land transfer tax; Ontario's 8% provincial component of the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) is collected and administered by the CRA, with no separate Ontario HST registration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA before registering for GST/HST. The procedure is: (1) prepare the partnership's identity details including the legal name, address, and the BN or Social Insurance Number of each general partner and, where applicable, of limited partners (non-resident individuals may need an Individual Tax Number); (2) complete and submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', or register electronically through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; (3) select the GST/HST program account and provide estimated annual taxable supplies in Canada and worldwide, along with the intended date of the first taxable supply; (4) if the partnership has no Canadian residence, appoint an authorized representative or agent in Canada for CRA communications; (5) after issuance of the BN and the GST/HST program account number, the partnership may issue invoices showing the BN; (6) register for Ontario provincial program accounts only if the partnership has employees (Ontario Employer Health Tax) or engages in other provincially-regulated activities; no separate Ontario tax registration is required for HST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election for entity classification under the Income Tax Act. A limited partnership is statutorily treated as a flow-through entity: the partnership files an information return (Form T5013) but does not pay income tax; each partner is taxed directly on their partnership income. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership is deemed a separate 'person' and may register in the partnership's name. Available elections relevant to cross-border structures include the partnership's ability to elect to have certain members be considered the representative under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act, and the joint election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related entities (not typically available for a limited partnership). Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 93.3, 94.2 and 94.3 of the Income Tax Act may recharacterize a foreign limited partnership or its members for Canadian tax purposes, but these rules are self-executing and do not require an affirmative election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – the 'small supplier' threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies (including GST/HST-exclusive value) in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters and in the current calendar quarter. A non-resident limited partnership making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST once it exceeds this threshold, unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt. For non-resident online retailers using a Canadian fulfillment centre or seller of digital products, special rules may lower or alter the threshold; marketplace facilitators may be liable to collect on behalf of non-resident sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods exported from Canada is available under Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when a registrant sells cosmetics or personal care products to a non-resident customer and the goods are exported by the supplier or by a common carrier to a destination outside Canada. The supply must be delivered or shipped to an address outside Canada and the exporter must retain proof of export within the meaning of the Export and Import of Goods Regulations. Services, including online beauty-related services, supplied to non-residents are generally zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI if the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not performed on real property situated in Canada or on personal property physically located in Canada at the time of performance. For online DTC retail in cosmetics, the dominant physical delivery of goods to international customers will typically qualify as an export zero-rating; the seller must be registered for GST/HST and must not have claimed input tax credits on the exported goods in a manner inconsistent with the export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES / CAED) or the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where the shipment is an actual export from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by a common carrier indicating origin in Canada and destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice detailing the goods, quantities, value, buyer, and seller
  • Certificate of export or delivery confirmation from the freight forwarder or carrier
  • Foreign customer order records and online transaction evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer in Canadian or foreign currency

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance regime. The CRA does not require digital stamping, API-based invoice clearance, or pre-approval of individual invoices. A registered GST/HST filer must issue and retain valid invoices that include the vendor's BN, the date of supply, the nature of the supply, the consideration payable, and either the GST/HST included or the rate and amount of tax charged. Electronic invoices and records are acceptable if they are legible and support an audit trail. For exports, the CRA may require commercial documents, export declarations, waybills, and carrier tracking data to substantiate zero-rating, but no electronic clearance system applies.

Canada does not impose a fixed statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST/HST export zero-rating; the 180-day period included in this dossier is a customary banking/record-keeping benchmark and not a CRA requirement. If a claimed export is not substantiated, the CRA may reassess the supply at the applicable 13% HST rate and disallow any input tax credits previously claimed. Penalties for false statements or omissions under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act can be up to 25% of the understated net tax for gross negligence or knowingly making false statements; additionally, interest under section 280/281 will accrue from the date the tax should have been remitted. In criminal or egregious cases, civil fraud penalties and prosecution are possible. The CRA actively audits cross-border e-commerce exporters for export documentation, and overturning a zero-rating assessment requires the registrant to provide contemporaneous proof of export, freight documentation, and payment records. Retroactive clawback of zero-rated benefits is available up to the normal reassessment period of four years, or longer where a taxpayer has been grossly negligent or committed fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no prior-authorization filing for inward or outward wires, and no restriction on foreign ownership of an Ontario limited partnership except review thresholds under the Investment Canada Act applicable to non-cultural control acquisitions. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than FX-based. Because the Ontario LP is a pass-through entity, a non-resident partner is deemed to carry on the partnership's business in Canada and is taxable under Part I on its allocable share. Distributions of partnership operating profits to non-resident partners trigger a 15% withholding-security obligation under Income Tax Regulations section 805, unless the partner posts an acceptable security, indemnity, or waiver approved by the Canada Revenue Agency. Passive remittances, including management fees, interest, and royalties, are separately subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% for a U.S. corporate partner owning at least 10% of the LP and 15% otherwise under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention. For DTC cosmetics retail, collections received through Shopify Payments and PayPal are converted into CAD at commercial settlement spreads at the merchant account level; Canadian law imposes no cap on FX margins and no barrier to re-invoicing in USD, EUR, or CNY.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for goods exported from Ontario. Cosmetic and personal-care proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, swept to a non-resident foreign-currency account, or converted at the LP's commercial discretion. Under Income Tax Act section 261, an eligible qualifying LP may elect a functional-currency reporting regime in USD for Canadian tax computation. The only binding obligations are record-keeping on source documents (Canadian export entry summaries, commercial invoices, customs declarations, FX trade tickets) and FINTRAC reporting of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, including same-day aggregated transfers, together with mandatory large-virtual-currency transaction reporting where settlement involves crypto-assets.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act and responsible for currency issuance, monetary policy, the national payments system, and supervision of retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; prudential supervision of chartered banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT supervision of financial intermediaries is vested in FINTRAC. The Minister of Finance retains residual authority over exchange-rate intervention and official reserve management, exercised through the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for Canadian regulated payment-service providers and FINTRAC-registered money-service businesses with an OSFI-supervised banking partner (e.g., PayPal Canada ULC, Wise Canada Ltd., and Shopify Payments as credit-card acquiring through chartered acquirers); LOW for offshore e-money institutions that hold no Canadian licence. Under the Bank Act, only chartered banks may take deposit-style liabilities, and under the Retail Payment Activities Act a payment service provider performing payment functions in Canada must be registered with the Bank of Canada. Offshore EMIs, including E.U.-authorised e-money institutions, cannot maintain a domestic settlement account, cannot access Payments Canada clearing rails (ACSS or Lynx), and are therefore usable only as collection corridors followed by daily or weekly sweeps into the LP's Tier 1 bank account. Any such arrangement requires contractual indemnities, FINTRAC record-keeping, and written confirmation that aggregate balances never constitute banking deposits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5% = 26.5% combined reference corporate rate; the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level corporate income tax. Each partner is taxed on its allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. Not applicable to LP cash distributions, which are returns of capital or income allocations and are not subject to dividend withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 5 months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period. T5013 slips for each partner are due by the last day of February following the calendar year in which the fiscal period ends.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 60 days after the anniversary date of the LP's registration in Ontario (Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, s. 6.2).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual transactions with non-arm's length non-residents triggers mandatory Form T106 filing, along with contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to a Canadian group head with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately CAD 1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations on an Ontario LP. However, the CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245 and the transfer-pricing reallocation rules under ITA section 247 to disregard or recharacterize transactions lacking real commercial substance. An LP with no genuine business activity in Ontario, or one used solely as a treaty-shopping conduit, risks being treated as a shell for DTA purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded under ITA section 163. Criminal conviction on indictment: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years (ITA section 239).

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be personally assessed under ITA section 160 for non-arm's length transfers; courts may impose restitution orders, travel bans, CRA asset freezes, and CBSA inadmissibility for foreign principals.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under ITA section 245 with denial of the tax benefit; transfer-pricing reallocation under ITA section 247 plus a 10% penalty on the net adjustment exceeding CAD 100,000; denial of treaty relief under the limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose test in the applicable DTA.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the LP is a mere agent; general partner directors are jointly and severally liable for all reassessed amounts. Criminal tax proceedings may trigger judicial travel restrictions and prohibition against departing Canada.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA, with maximum entity penalties reaching CAD 2,000,000 per violation and applicable daily amounts; Ontario ISC violations attract fines up to CAD 25,000 for entities and CAD 5,000 per individual, with continued default penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner face criminal prosecution for money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; civil forfeiture of the LP's accounts may be ordered; directors may be subject to travel bans and removal from management of regulated activities.
Withholding and Remittance Defaults

Statutory sanction: ITA sections 227(8) and 227(9): penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (recent quarterly rates approximate 8% to 9%); where the default is knowing or grossly negligent, the penalty rises to 20% and the GP is assessed for the full liability.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly liable under ITA section 227.1 for the full unremitted withholding; the CRA may issue a requirement to pay to banks and garnish personal accounts; non-resident directors of the LP may be denied re-entry to Canada until the liability is satisfied.
