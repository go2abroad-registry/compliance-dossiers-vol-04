Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate tax administration; Registraire des entreprises du Québec – business registry (NEQ) filing.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident manufacturer must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Québec Business Corporations Act, or register as an extra-provincial corporation, and obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises. For federal tax accounts, the foreign corporation applies through CRA’s Business Registration Online or via Form RC11 to obtain a 9-digit Federal Business Number, adding program accounts for GST/HST (type RT) and payroll (type RP) if applicable. For QST, registration is effected separately through Revenu Québec by filing the applicable registration form or through the integrated business registration portal. Where no Canadian residence or fixed establishment exists, non-resident corporations must appoint a representative for service and satisfy Canadian shipping/commercial substance requirements to maintain accounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident and non-resident business corporations are separate legal taxpayers under the Income Tax Act; corporate income is not pass-through to shareholders, and inter-corporate distributions are dividends, not deductible payments). — A non-resident manufacturer must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Québec Business Corporations Act, or register as an extra-provincial corporation, and obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident and non-resident business corporations are separate legal taxpayers under the Income Tax Act; corporate income is not pass-through to shareholders, and inter-corporate distributions are dividends, not deductible payments).

Available election forms

No United-States-style check-the-box election exists. Statutory elections include: Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation; s. 216 election enabling a non-resident to compute Canadian rental or timber royalty income on a net basis instead of gross Part XIII withholding; s. 217 election respecting non-resident trust distributions; s. 261 functional currency election for tax reporting; and QST/GST/HST deemed-supplier rules. Hybrid-mismatch limitations are imposed by Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 to 18.4, with no unilateral pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD (GST/HST registration threshold: global taxable supplies other than zero-rated supplies must be or exceed CAD $30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; QST registration threshold: same CAD $30,000 threshold applied to taxable supplies made in Quebec). Non-resident corporations making taxable supplies in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded, unless an exemption for non-resident small suppliers applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to tangible personal property supplied by a registrant where the goods are exported from Canada as part of the supply. Freeze-dried pet raw meat and pet-food products are not zero-rated basic groceries for Canadian domestic supply, but export sales are zero-rated if the goods are shipped or delivered to a destination outside Canada, and the supplier maintains proof of export. Zero-rating also applies to services and intangibles supplied to a non-resident if they are not consumed or used in Canada. For QST, a supply of goods is zero-rated where delivery occurs outside Quebec. Evidence of export must generally be obtained and retained within the prescribed time contemplated by the Excise Tax Act and QST regulations.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or equivalent ECI electronic cargo declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and complete packing list identifying goods, weights, and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing final destination outside Canada and shipper/receiver details
  • Proof of export issued by a customs broker or carrier confirming exit from Canada or delivery outside Quebec
  • Payment and foreign exchange records evidencing remittance from the foreign buyer, including an Inward Remittance Certificate where used for banking and audit substantiation

Canada and Quebec do not mandate continuous transaction controls, real-time tax clearance, digital sealing, or mandatory API integration to the CRA or Revenu Québec. Electronic invoices and records are acceptable if kept in a readable and legible format that supports audit; record retention is generally six years from the end of the last tax year. No national e-invoicing clearance platform or fiscal stamp requirement applies.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, zero-rating can be denied retroactively if export invoices, carrier documents, customs declarations, or payment evidence are missing or inconsistent. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal reassessment period (generally four years for CRA and three years for Revenu Québec; longer in the case of misrepresentation or fraud), charge unabated GST/HST and QST at the applicable rate, assess interest, and impose penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, knowingly making a false statement could trigger a penalty equal to the greater of $5,000 CAD and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus criminal prosecution under s. 380 Criminal Code, which may result in fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account (trade, services, interest) and capital-account (equity, debt, dividends, direct investment) transactions are unrestricted in Canada. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity injection or outbound profit remittance. Remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: dividends to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to Part XIII Canada withholding tax at 25%, reduced by treaty to typically 15% or 5% (e.g., US treaty 5% on substantial shareholding); for a Quebec-resident operating corporation, profits must be computed in CAD for Part I/Taxation Act compliance. The Investment Canada Act notification (Form A/New Canadian Business) is required for foreign direct investment establishing a non-related Canadian business; no CFIUS-style FX authorization exists. Non-resident ownership of voting shares of a Quebec Business Corporation is permitted without exchange approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion period on export receipts. Exporters may freely retain USD or other currencies in offshore or onshore foreign-currency bank accounts. For trade compliance, CBSA requires an electronic export declaration (via G7/CDS or Canadian Export Reporting System) for physical goods shipments valued above CAD 2,000; in addition, the Bank of Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) captures cross-border financial flows via reporting agents (banks). Tax-wise, unrealized exchange gains/losses on export receivables are booked under IFRS 9/ASC 830, and realized gains are taxed as ordinary income for the Quebec Business Corporation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with OSFI; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-authority or exchange-control bureau. Currency market is freely floating; the Bank of Canada acts solely on monetary policy, while OSFI supervises Canadian prudential banking conduct. FINTRAC administers AML/KYC obligations under the PCMLTFA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (restricted). Canada does not issue a discrete EMI licence; foreign digital-EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Corpay) operating into Canada must be registered as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC (MSB registration) and are supervised under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once engaged with Canadian end-users. An offshore EMI may be used as a secondary disbursement or vendor-payment rail for low-value cross-border settlements, but it cannot substitute for a Schedule I bank account for statutory KYC credibility, advisory credit facilities, commercial LC issuance, or FINTRAC suspicious-transaction governance. The primary treasury should therefore maintain at least one Tier-1 domestic account and designate EMI rails exclusively for marketing/ platform disbursements not exceeding an aggregate daily settlement threshold mutually agreed with the corporate bank.
