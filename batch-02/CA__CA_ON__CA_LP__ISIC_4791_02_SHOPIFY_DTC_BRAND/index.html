Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Ontario limited partnership; a nil capital fund may be declared at inception. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, administered nationally for the GST/HST Program and the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) in Ontario; registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/register-your-business/business-registration-online.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance provides provincial tax information at https://www.ontario.ca/page/taxes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign person or partnership carrying on business in Canada, including a non-resident-owned Ontario Limited Partnership operating a Shopify DTC brand, must first complete any required Ontario limited partnership registration with the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, then apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online. The BN functions as the Canadian tax identification number; the GST/HST registration account is identified by the 'RT' program account suffix. The application must disclose the legal name, jurisdiction of formation, foreign address, date commercial activity began in Canada, the general partner and authorized representative, estimated annual worldwide taxable revenues, and the GST/HST, payroll, import/export or other CRA program accounts required. Non-resident suppliers may register remotely without a Canadian incorporation or physical office; CRA will then issue the GST/HST registration number for invoicing and return filing purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box or elective entity classification regime. An Ontario Limited Partnership is statutorily classified as a pass-through partnership for Canadian income tax purposes, with no election to be taxed as a corporation. The Excise Tax Act similarly treats the partnership itself as a separate registrant for GST/HST without an elective classification mechanism. Available elections relevant to a foreign-owned LP include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration election by a non-resident small supplier; (ii) an election under Income Tax Act subsection 216(4) for a non-resident partner to file a net-basis Part I income tax return for specified Canadian-source rents or royalties instead of gross-basis Part XIII tax; and (iii) the mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act section 18.18, which are not elective but apply automatically to deny inappropriate deduction mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies (not limited to Canada-source revenue) in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this amount makes GST/HST registration mandatory. The same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of digital products and services, including Shopify DTC brands selling remotely to Canadian consumers; Canada does not maintain a separate higher threshold for foreign sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, tangible personal property supplied by a registrant to a non-resident customer is zero-rated where the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier, or delivered to a common carrier for export, and are not consumed or used in Canada before export. Digital products and downloadable intangible personal property are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient, provided the supply is not for consumption in Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated if the service is performed entirely outside Canada or otherwise falls within the export provisions of Schedule VI; services that relate to Canadian real property or to goods used in Canada remain taxable. For a Shopify DTC brand, if goods are fulfilled from a Canadian warehouse to a foreign consumer address with proper export evidence, the supply is zero-rated; if goods are delivered to a Canadian consumer, Ontario HST at 13% applies.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A / Canadian customs export report) where the value of the exported goods is CAD 2,000 or more or the goods are controlled or regulated
  • Transport document, bill of lading, or airway bill evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract identifying the non-resident buyer and the international commercial terms (e.g., FOB, DAP)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/payment-provider settlement record showing receipt of payment from the foreign customer in the agreed foreign currency
  • Destination-country customs clearance or import declaration evidencing physical admission of the goods into the foreign market

Canada has no national mandatory electronic invoicing scheme, no real-time fiscal clearance platform, and no digital stamping or pre-approval of invoices. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically, and mandatory electronic filing currently applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million. A compliant tax invoice must state the vendor name, CRA Business Number and RT account, date of supply, description of goods/services, taxable consideration, GST/HST rate, and tax charged or zero-rated status. Electronic transaction records, including Shopify order and payment logs, are acceptable source records if retained in a readable and auditable format for at least six years.

The CRA conducts risk-based audits matching zero-rated export amounts reported on GST/HST returns against CBSA export declarations, shipping records, and payment-provider data. If the required export documentation is missing or insufficient, the CRA will re-assess the zero-rated supply as taxable at the Ontario HST rate of 13%, assess net tax, charge arrears interest at the prescribed rate, and apply late-reporting penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act equal to 5% of the unpaid amount plus 1% per month, up to 12 months. False declarations or omissions made knowingly or with gross negligence attract a civil penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax misstated; refunds can be retroactively clawed back within the normal four-year reassessment period, and that period is unlimited where fraud or misrepresentation is established. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion may result in fines of up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized free-floating exchange rate regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Both current account (trade in goods/services, royalties, e-commerce settlements) and capital account (direct investment, loans, repatriation of capital, shareholder distributions) transactions are fully convertible without prior exchange control approval. Non-resident limited partners can repatriate profits, capital, and loan repayments through Schedule I banks without exchange control permits. The only practical submission is enhanced AML/KYC documentation and CRS/FATCA reporting for U.S. persons.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion timeline applies to export proceeds in Canada. Receipts from cross-border DTC sales can remain in foreign currency accounts indefinitely. However, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or above in a 24-hour period must be reported to FINTRAC. Additionally, export invoices supporting the entry of goods are filed through the Canadian Commercial Corporation or the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) customs reporting requirements, but no fx conversion mandate exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Foreign exchange oversight and administrative enforcement under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with FINTRAC as the financial intelligence unit for cross-border monetary transactions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with legal caveats. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or UK/EU EMIs) can be used as auxiliary receivables collection nodes for Shopify DTC sales, but these entities do not hold Canadian banking licenses and are not members of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). Legal constraints require that all settlement balances above the PCMLTFA thresholds be moved into a Schedule I bank account within one business day to ensure segregation and OSFI-compliant deposit insurance. Direct payroll, corporate tax payments, or escrowed partner capital should never transit via an unlicensed EMI because such funds fall outside Canadian insolvency protections. Shopify Payments itself is the underlying merchant acquiring rail, which settles to a Canadian bank account; the use of an external EMI should be documented as a sub-agent receivables sweep.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level corporate tax for Limited Partnerships (pass-through taxation); partners are subject to personal income tax rates

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

N/A (LP distributions not dividends); for non-resident partners, withholding tax on distributions from Canadian-source income at 25% statutory rate, reducible under tax treaties

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Issuance of T5013 slips to partners by March 31 following the tax year-end; no entity-level filing deadline

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific monetary threshold for LPs; however, if related party transactions exceed certain levels or if required under GAAR/Part XVI.1, transfer pricing documentation may be required (Local File if annual related party transactions > CAD 1 million or similar CRA guidance)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like some jurisdictions; instead, applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), transfer pricing rules under section 247 ITA, and CFC rules for controlled foreign corporations

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to CAD 5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties for negligence

Director personal liability: N/A (LP structure); however, general partners may have personal liability for partnership obligations, including tax
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under GAAR; potential denial of deductions, interest and penalties up to 50% of tax owing

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable for tax liabilities; possible director liability if misclassified as corporation
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 10% of transaction value or more; reassessment of income

Director personal liability: General partners may face personal liability for unpaid taxes
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines under Proceeds of Crime Act or Income Tax Act; potential criminal charges

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable
