Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA); Ontario's HST is collected by CRA, while the Ontario Ministry of Finance retains only residual provincial levies (none applicable to standard data-center colocation supplies).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership must (1) confirm it carries on business in Canada and register for GST/HST when its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (the small-supplier threshold); (2) apply online via CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), providing the partnership agreement, addresses, and partner particulars; (3) receive a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RT' program account for GST/HST; (4) if a non-resident registrant, post security if demanded by CRA under ETA s. 241; and (5) file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96); partners are taxed on their shares of Canadian-source partnership income. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is a separate 'person' and a registrant (ETA s. 123(1)); the LP is therefore an entity-level registrant with flow-through income tax consequences. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income is taxable to resident and non-resident partners on a source basis; Ontario HST applies where the supply is consumed or used in Ontario. — A foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership must (1) confirm it carries on business in Canada and register for GST/HST when its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (the small-supplier threshold); (2) apply online via CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), providing the partnership agreement, addresses, and partner particulars; (3) receive a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RT' program account for GST/HST; (4) if a non-resident registrant, post security if demanded by CRA under ETA s. 241; and (5) file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96); partners are taxed on their shares of Canadian-source partnership income. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is a separate 'person' and a registrant (ETA s. 123(1)); the LP is therefore an entity-level registrant with flow-through income tax consequences. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income is taxable to resident and non-resident partners on a source basis; Ontario HST applies where the supply is consumed or used in Ontario.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada. Relevant available elections include (i) the GST/HST Quick Method election (eligible where taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 400,000), (ii) the ETA s. 156 election to supply among closely related registrants at nil consideration, and (iii) the non-calendar partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) where the partnership has no specified member. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.5) are not elective; they automatically neutralise deductible or double-deduction outcomes created by the LP's transparent status versus offshore opacity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no general Foreign Exchange Control Act, and capital movements, including partnership capital contributions, loan on-lending, collateral placements, and profit remittances, are not subject to exchange control approval. Non-resident limited partners may freely remit Canadian-dollar distributions after satisfying Canada Revenue Agency reporting and Part XIII withholding obligations, including T5013/NR4 and Form T106, and CRS/FATCA disclosures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender period or conversion ratio applies to export or service proceeds. Canada abolished compulsory exchange surrender in 1951. Entities may hold foreign currency in domestic and offshore accounts. Cross-border movements of physical currency or bearer instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, and international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period, must be reported to FINTRAC. Business transactions must be recorded and retained for tax and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Department of Finance Canada (exchange policy); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI, prudential bank supervision); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, AML/CFT supervision and cross-border reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary receivables and disbursements; Low as sole clearing infrastructure. Legal constraints: any non-bank electronic money institution carrying on money services business in Canada must register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Foreign EMIs are generally not authorized to accept deposit liabilities in Canada, are not CDIC insured, and cannot directly access the Bank of Canada large-value transfer system; clearing depends on a Canadian partner bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million; Master File required if total cross-border transactions exceed CAD 10 million; Country-by-Country Report required if consolidated group revenue ≥ CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 and substance-over-form principles apply. Partnerships must demonstrate genuine business purpose and economic substance to avoid reassessment

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and seizure of assets
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of tax benefits, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for negligence; criminal charges for fraud if intentional
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if not made with reasonable efforts), plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they authorized or participated in the non-compliance
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for individuals and CAD 5 million for corporations; imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for criminal penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to comply
