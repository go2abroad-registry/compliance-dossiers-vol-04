Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST, Business Number (BN) and federal income tax administration, portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency and Business Registration Online (bro.businessregistration.gc.ca); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial income tax and Numéro d'entreprise du Québec (NEQ), portal: revenuquebec.ca and clicSÉQUR Affaires; the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca) issues the NEQ and registers the société en commandite (limited partnership). Non-resident intake channel: CRA International Tax Services Office (1-800-959-5525) and the Non-Resident Business Account program.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the 10-digit NEQ (Quebec enterprise number), appointing a nominal representative if the LP carries on business in Quebec. Step 2: Apply for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online, activating the GST/HST program account (RT), and where relevant payroll (RP) and income tax (RC) accounts. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec for QST through clicSÉQUR Affaires, either upon exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or immediately where the LP has a permanent establishment in Quebec and makes or intends to make taxable supplies to non-residents. Step 4: File the annual partnership information return (T5013) with CRA and issue T5013 slips to resident and non-resident partners; a foreign general partner must also complete the CRA non-resident registration and, for treaty-protected income, file Form NR301 to reduce Part XIII withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96; the partnership itself pays no federal or Quebec income tax and each partner is allocated its pro rata share of income or loss for the fiscal period. The Specified Investment Flow-Through (SIFT) partnership rules would impose entity-level tax only if the LP's units were listed on a designated stock exchange, which is not the case for this private design-subscription LP. For GST/QST the LP is treated as a separate 'person' registrant carrying on a commercial activity, and for Quebec civil law the LP has separate patrimony for creditor claims.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; classification follows the Income Tax Act and Quebec civil law. Available statutory elections relevant to this CA_LP: (1) single-registrant election for qualifying partnerships under Excise Tax Act section 272.1, with the mirror election under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax, consolidating GST/HST and QST returns of the partners and the partnership; (2) non-resident partner net-basis election under Income Tax Act section 216 to replace 25% gross Part XIII withholding with tax on net Canadian-source income; (3) partnership fiscal period election under Income Tax Act section 249.1; (4) group or joint election under ETA section 273 or 274 for closely related registrants where the corporate general partner qualifies; (5) hybrid mismatch rules: Canada imposes the Part I.2 25% excessive entitlement tax and Income Tax Act section 18.4 rules on hybrid mismatch arrangements involving a foreign tax-indifferent partner; a US LLC partner cannot extend its check-the-box classification to the Canadian LP, and treaty tie-breaker rules govern dual-resident status rather than the US Section 7701 election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA section 240(1) and QSTA, calculated over the current calendar quarter plus the four preceding calendar quarters on taxable (non-zero-rated) supplies made in Canada/Linked Quebec supplies. Mandatory registration without reliance on the threshold applies when the LP is a non-resident partnership or carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment in Quebec and makes taxable supplies; registration is also required for a registrant that makes zero-rated export supplies, because the LP must be registered to claim input tax credits / input tax refunds on its Canadian inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The UI/UX design subscription (ISIC 7410 / NAICS 541430, design-as-a-service) qualifies for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 10 of the Excise Tax Act where: (1) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, not an individual present in Canada at the time the supply is made; (2) the service is not in connection with real or immovable property situated in Canada; (3) the service is not in connection with corporeal moveable property situated in Canada; and (4) the service is not supplied to an individual for personal consumption while in Canada. Services performed partly or wholly in Canada for a non-resident client are zero-rated as supplies made to a non-resident; services rendered wholly outside Canada are additionally zero-rated on a territorial basis. The QST applies the same 0% rate for supplies of services and intangible property made to persons that are not Quebec residents and that are not recipients in Quebec. Because the export supplies are taxable at 0% (not exempt), the registered LP may claim full input tax credits and input tax refunds on Canadian software, payroll, hosting and professional-fee expenses. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-currency repatriation window: export proceeds may be received in CAD or foreign currency, converted under CRA published exchange rates, and the CRA verifies place of supply, non-resident status and provision of the service rather than the date of funds receipt.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system — mandatory only if physical design deliverables or prototypes are exported; pure digital design subscriptions are intangible supplies outside the customs declaration regime
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for couriered physical prototypes, design kits or printed deliverables, evidencing physical departure from Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or Canadian bank credit confirmation) or the Stripe_Billing / Wise foreign-currency payout ledger evidencing inward cross-border payment and the non-resident payer identity
  • International design subscription agreement (MSA / SOW / terms of service) identifying the non-resident recipient, place of supply, Canadian design performance scope, and absence of Canadian real-property and movable-property nexus, together with French-language zero-rated invoices

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory tax-clearing API regime is currently in force in Canada or Quebec for the 2025 filing period. Canada has adopted a voluntary Peppol-based Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (pilot from 2024-2025 with the federal government as anchor buyer); Quebec enacted Bill 37 (LQ 2024, c. 18) amending the Tax Administration Act and the QSTA to introduce mandatory B2B/B2C e-invoicing and e-reporting, to be phased in by government regulation, with large taxpayers expected around 2026-2028 and SMEs later. Current compliance obligations: GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE or the GST/HST return XML/API; QST returns and remittances via clicSÉQUR Affaires web services; invoices to Quebec-based clients must be in French under the Charter of the French Language (as amended effective 1 June 2025) and must show the BN, the QST registration number, and the zero-rating annotation TPS/TVQ 0% for export supplies to non-residents.

Canada has no 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating, but CRA and Revenu Quebec can reassess a zero-rated export claim if the supply is re-characterized as domestic, causing clawback of unpaid GST at 5% and QST at 9.975% plus daily compounded prescribed arrears interest (approximately 9% regular and 14% corporate arrears in 2025). Penalties include: (i) ETA section 285 false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax understated; (ii) ETA section 280 willful-evasion penalty of 50% of the total tax sought, fines up to CAD 25,000 per count on summary conviction or CAD 100,000 on indictment, and imprisonment up to two years; (iii) Quebec gross-negligence penalty under the Tax Administration Act (Quebec) of 50% of unreported QST; (iv) Income Tax Act section 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% applied to the partners' understated income where the export zero-rating substantiation is fraudulent or false; and (v) CRA Export Verification Program and Revenu Quebec post-export audits, which may suspend or claw back input tax credit / input tax refund claims. The CRA may also impose Director/Officer liability and joint-and-several liability on the general partner. Mitigation requires retention of the full documentation bundle for at least six years, attested non-resident status certification (Form NR301 where applicable), and French-language invoicing compliant with Quebec Bill 96 amendments effective 1 June 2025.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating currency; there is no exchange-control law, no licensing regime, and no central-bank pre-approval for profit repatriation, capital contributions, or partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners. CAD/USD/EUR subscription receipts (Stripe and Wise settlements) and equity injections may be retained in foreign-currency accounts without mandatory conversion. Operational hurdles are limited to (i) FINTRAC large-value reporting obligations imposed on the bank for cross-border electronic fund transfers at or above CAD 10,000, and (ii) federal and Quebec income-tax withholding on the Canadian-source taxable allocation to non-resident partners; neither constitutes an FX convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canada. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation quota for export proceeds derived from design-subscription services (ISIC 7410 / NAICS 541430). The CA_LP may hold foreign currency indefinitely in USD/EUR-denominated treasury accounts, subject only to an income-tax functional-currency election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act if the primary record-keeping currency is other than CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; exchange-control and monetary policy coordination rests with the Minister of Finance and the Department of Finance Canada; AML/transactional supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), while the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates Quebec-regulated money-services businesses and Desjardins.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for settlement aggregation and low-value disbursements; Low for primary treasury custody. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) is registered as a money-services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, for Quebec activity, with the AMF; it is not a Schedule I/II bank and deposits are not insured by CDIC or the Fonds de sécurité des dépôts du Québec. Legal constraints: (i) no EMI passport exists in Canada, so Wise may not offer deposit-taking, credit, or constructed ISDA FX lines; (ii) funds held at Wise are not protected by a statutory trust or insolvency ring-fence equivalent to bank depositor preference; (iii) Wise executes FX as principal at its own mid-market spread, creating counterparty price risk inconsistent with audited treasury policy. Wise Business is nonetheless operationally viable as a secondary rail for aggregating Stripe payouts and paying small Canadian contractors below CAD 25,000 per transaction; it must not hold capital escrow funds or partnership distribution proceeds above the large-transaction monitoring threshold.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% and Quebec 11.5% active business income rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation, before refundable dividend tax treatment; general federal rate 15% plus Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Canadian withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is 25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. For a limited partnership distributing income to non-resident partners, the partnership must withhold 25% on the non-resident partner's share of partnership income that is taxable in Canada but not distributed, and on actual distributions of Canadian-source income, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty. Quebec does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax; the federal Part XIII rate applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes transfer pricing documentation requirements on transactions between a Canadian resident taxpayer and non-resident related parties. The threshold is CAD 1,000,000 in gross revenues, gross expenditures, or both, for the taxation year, for transactions involving goods or services (including cross-border subscription and design service transactions). For transactions involving intangible property, the threshold is CAD 500,000. The documentation must include a Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country Report (for which the local entity threshold is CAD 10,000,000 in revenue and related group revenue of CAD 1,050,000,000 for CBCR filing). Quebec adopts the federal transfer pricing rules under the Taxation Act; Revenu Québec does not impose a separate TP documentation threshold, but references federal TP rules. The partnership must prepare contemporaneous documentation if its related-party cross-border transactions exceed the CAD 1,000,000 threshold, and must ensure that all non-arm's-length transactions are priced at arm's length. Failure to provide documentation underlines a penalty: 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, with a minimum penalty of CAD 5,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or BVI frameworks, but the Canada Revenue Agency applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec GAAR under section 1079.2 of the Quebec Taxation Act to deny tax benefits from transactions or arrangements that are primarily tax-motivated and that lack substantive business purpose. The limited partnership must demonstrate genuine business activity in Quebec (employees, offices, client contracts, and decision-making). Non-resident partners cannot use the Canadian limited partnership solely to access treaty benefits without substantial economic presence; the CRA may apply the treaty-shopping limitation (beneficial ownership and principal purpose test) under Canada's treaties, and the OECD multilateral instrument (MLI) where applicable. A limited partnership that lacks economic substance in Canada may be regarded as a conduit and the income recharacterized, exposing the non-resident partners to full 25% Part XIII withholding without treaty relief, or the Canada Revenue Agency may deny treaty benefits under anti-abuse provisions. The partnership must also comply with corporate veil requirements, maintain a registered office in Quebec, and file annual registrations (Registraire des entreprises) to reinforce economic substance. For transfer pricing, the partner's foreign entity must be afforded adequate economic substance; the documentation should confirm that any related-party cost-sharing arrangements (e.g., intercompany design services, licensing of design assets) reflect actual functions, assets, and risks in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Federal Income Tax Act: severe penalties of 50% of the federal tax evaded (gross negligence penalty), plus interest and civil penalties. Revenu Québec: penalty of 50% of the tax evaded for false statements or omissions; criminal prosecution for tax evasion (wilfully evading tax) is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of 50% to 200% of the amount evaded (federal), and up to 18 months' imprisonment and fines up to 100% of the tax evaded under the Quebec Taxation Act. Civil fraud penalties may also apply. For a corporation, fines may be substantial; a false statement under the Income Tax Act carries a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax for the year.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner of the limited partnership are personally liable for unpaid withheld taxes (payroll deductions, GST/QST, Part XIII withholding) and for corporate tax debts if the corporation grossly neglects to remit. Criminal tax evasion may expose controlling minds to imprisonment up to 5 years, court-ordered restitution, and travel bans or seizures of assets. Civil liability also arises for participation in a fraud that causes loss to the Crown; the CRA may assess directors personally up to the amount of unremitted payroll or withholding tax. Quebec Revenue may similarly hold directors responsible for unpaid amounts and may pursue an investigative order against the corporate veil (lifting) when it is proved that the corporation is a sham or conduit.
Shell Company / Asset Stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA can disallow loss allocations, deny non-resident treaty benefits, recharacterize the arrangement as a sham, and assess unreported income at 25% Part XIII withholding on gross distributions. GAAR penalties (section 245) impose a 10% penalty on disallowed tax benefits in addition to reassessment. Civil penalties: up to CAD 12,500 for schedule filing omissions. In extreme cases, the partnership could be dissolved under Quebec civil law for nullity of a legal act made with illicit purpose. For non-resident partners, the CRA may impose an 25% withholding tax on all distributions and a 10% penalty. The Canada Revenue Agency and Revenu Québec routinely exchange information on entities lacking substance.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of a shell limited partnership may be liable for the company's tax liabilities under the Income Tax Act (director liability for GST/HST and payroll, section 227.1) and under Quebec laws (section 51 of the Taxation Act, and the Civil Code of Quebec). Courts may disregard the general partner's separate identity if the general partner is a mere puppet or is using the partnership to evade obligations; this permits principals to be held personally liable for all unpaid tax, penal interest, and fines. Travel bans under the Seized Property Management Act or the Criminal Code may apply if a criminal charge of fraud or money laundering is filed.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Criminal Code of Canada and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: money laundering is an indictable crime with imprisonment for life (maximum 10 years under section 462.31, with life for trafficking proceeds under related narcotics offences) and fines in the court's discretion; the Proceeds of Crime Act imposes administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for non-compliance by reporting entities (recent amendments allow FINTRAC to impose penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 for non-compliance and CAD 500,000 for failure to submit a suspicious transaction report (STR)). In Quebec, the Financial Markets Tribunal may impose administrative penalties. Suspicious transactions connected to corruption or fraud can trigger seizure of property and restraint orders. For the corporate entity, a conviction may result in significant fines (unlimited for indictable offences) and restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors may face criminal liability for laundering proceeds of crime if they knowingly aided or directed transactions. Personal liability includes freezing orders over personal assets, confiscation of moveable/immovable property, travel bans restricting departure from Canada (under certain federal/Quebec orders), and imprisonment up to 10 years (life for related financing offences).
