Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) as the national tax authority, online portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); in Quebec, CRA jurisdiction is exercised in parallel with Revenu Québec (RQ) which is the single administrator for both GST/HST and Quebec Sales Tax (QST) registrations, filings and audits under the Canada-Quebec Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA); RQ operates the 'CLIC' business portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) and the Quebec Enterprise Register (Registraire des entreprises) issues the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) which serves as the consolidated tax identifier.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is never pass-through for federal or Quebec tax; it is a resident corporation under ITA s. 2(1) and QSTA Part I taxed on worldwide income, with federal general rate of 15% (9% effective on the first CAD 500,000 for a Canadian-controlled private corporation, if applicable) and Quebec general rate of 11.5% (3.2% SME reduced rate subject to provincial limits); territorial scope is worldwide-unitary, with attribution rules for foreign affiliates under ITA s. 95; the factory zone in Quebec confers no federal/provincial tax holiday unless a specific provincial tax holiday (e.g., new technology investment incentive) is separately obtained; non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% or lower treaty rate on dividends, royalties and interest.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; no corporate entity can be disregarded. Available statutory elections relevant to a foreign-owned Quebec frozen-food manufacturer: (i) ITA s. 85 / QSTA s. 372 rollover election to transfer qualifying assets (plant, machinery, frozen-food IP) into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) s. 83(2) capital dividend election to distribute tax-free the Capital Dividend Account; (iii) s. 216(1) election for a non-resident parent to file an income tax return on net income instead of Part XIII gross withholding where Canadian real estate or equipment rentals are earned; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA Part XVIII (ss. 212.3(20)) which require a prescribed information return (s. 237.2 ITA) where a foreign entity is fiscally transparent or a hybrid financial instrument is used; and (v) treaty-based elections to claim reduced withholding, including the Canada-United States enhanced treaty MAP/limitation-on-benefits provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: registration becomes mandatory when the corporation's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident manufacturer with a Quebec permanent establishment must register once it solicits orders or makes taxable supplies in Canada; a separate mandatory QST registration applies to 'large enterprises' with group Quebec revenues above CAD 10,000,000 regardless of the small-supplier threshold; export sales are excluded from the threshold computation, but voluntary registration is strongly recommended to recover input tax credits (GST) and input tax refunds (QST) embedded in plant construction, freezers, packaging machinery and freight.

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, no API stamping, and no national e-invoice clearance mandate: the CRA accepts any paper or electronic invoice that contains the prescribed information under ETA s. 221/223 and GST/HST Regulations, and requires records to be retained for six years. Quebec-specific mandate: under QSTA arts. 40.1-40.14 and the Regulation respecting electronic invoicing and electronic payment, large enterprises (Quebec taxable turnover above CAD 10M) must receive electronic invoices and make electronic payments from fiscal years beginning in 2025, all Quebec undertakings must do so from fiscal years beginning in 2026, and issuance of e-invoices and transmission of structured invoice data to Revenu Québec are phased in by 2027-2028 using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format; no digital stamps are required, but large enterprises must also continue filing the annual invoice-summary return (Form M-4 / SAF-T in XML) prescribed under s. 34.6 QSTA; non-compliance triggers QSTA administrative penalties per day of default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control approval is required for equity injections, trade settlements, dividend remittances, or shareholder loan servicing. Profit remittance, royalty outflows, and capital repatriation are unrestricted; an ordinary withholding tax on payments to non-residents and transfer-pricing documentation apply under the Income Tax Act. Financial institutions must file FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for inbound/outbound wires of CAD 10,000 or more, and Statistics Canada may require balance-of-payments direct investment survey reporting. A new foreign-controlled manufacturing subsidiary is subject to Investment Canada Act notification, with national security pre-clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, retention period, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore bank accounts and may convert at freely negotiated market rates. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Exports must be documented for customs, tax, and sanctions compliance, but no central-bank repatriation rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payment corridor; Low as a primary treasury rail. Foreign electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMIs are not chartered banks in Canada; they cannot take deposits, issue letters of credit, or provide settlement-grade sweep facilities. To operate in Canada they require FINTRAC money services business registration and are increasingly subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act licensing and Quebec AMF approval when offering payment services in the province. Such EMIs may be used for small-value fragmented disbursements or cross-border payroll flows, but the entire capital structure and trade settlement must run through a Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million (total value of transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation. Additionally, if the corporation is part of a multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 50 million, a Country-by-Country Report may be required.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules require that transactions have genuine economic substance. For treaty benefits, the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause in certain treaties (e.g., US) requires substantive business operations in the residence country. A shell company with no real activity may be denied treaty relief and face penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Taxation Act (Quebec). Penalties: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest. Imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals, and for corporations, fines up to CAD 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions (e.g., payroll taxes). Civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, and application of GAAR. Penalties: 50% of the tax avoided, plus interest. The CRA may also impose a 10% penalty for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. Civil liability for restitution, and potential criminal charges for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to arm's length, plus penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 1 million). Interest on underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length pricing may be held personally liable for the penalty portion. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed during investigation.
