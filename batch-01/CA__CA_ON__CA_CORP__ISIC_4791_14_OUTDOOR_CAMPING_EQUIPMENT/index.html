Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; business and GST/HST registration portal: Canada Revenue Agency My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, or Form RC1A for non-resident registrants, and simultaneously register for a separate GST/HST account. The application is submitted electronically through the CRA's register or My Business Account portal or by mail/fax. Ontario provincial registration is completed separately through the Ontario Business Registry for extra-provincial status when the corporation has an Ontario nexus. The BN is the statutory tax identification number and the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the RT/RM program code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A corporation is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available elections relevant to restructuring and intercompany transactions include Income Tax Act section 85 rollover elections, section 156/157 associated corporation elections, and Excise Tax Act elections for closely related corporations (for example section 157). Hybrid mismatch elections are limited and must comply with the OECD hybrid mismatch rules adopted under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the small supplier rules; mandatory registration is triggered once the threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods exported from Canada and for certain services supplied to non-residents where the property is delivered outside Canada or the service is for use outside Canada, under sections 21, 23 and 25 of the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must obtain and retain evidence of export within prescribed conditions; the supply is zero-rated if all statutory conditions are met regardless of the purchaser's residency.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A or equivalent electronic export reporting)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Carrier certificate or customs control evidence showing physical exportation
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance records

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance operates in Canada. E-invoices and EDI are acceptable, but CRA does not require pre-clearance or real-time API submission. Specific GST/HST information requirements are enforced on invoices issued by registrants.

CRA may reassess the GST/HST return, deny zero-rating, and impose interest and penalties under the Excise Tax Act where export documentation is false or substantiation is insufficient. Gross negligence penalties of up to 25% of the amount of tax could be assessed for false statements or omissions, and refunds may be withheld. Fraudulent claims may additionally lead to prosecution and restitution orders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Currency Act and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating currency with no exchange control restrictions, no surrender requirements, and no prior approval for dividend, interest, or royalty remittances. Profit repatriation to non-residents is subject only to Part XIII withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate, typically reduced by treaty) and to OECD CRS / FATCA reporting obligations, not to FX approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export receipts. Exporters may retain funds in any convertible currency in Canadian-chartered bank branches or foreign bank accounts. For income tax purposes, Section 261 of the Income Tax Act allows reporting in CAD or an eligible functional currency, and unrealized foreign exchange gains/losses are recognized on an accrual basis for business income.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the sole monetary and central banking authority; foreign exchange administration is delegated to the Department of Finance and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for reporting, with no separate exchange-control regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — Foreign electronic money institutions and payment-service providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) can operate as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC and, where applicable, as Registered Payment Service Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) (in force for registration since November 2024). They cannot accept deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts, and they are not eligible custodians for treasury liquidity. For a Low_Risk_Standard e-commerce retailer, such fintechs are viable only for collection/settlement optimization, not as the primary operating bank. Stricter Canadian guidance also restricts holding client funds without a trust arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or shareholder-loan funds must be remitted in freely convertible CAD or USD via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 6400 (capital contribution) or 6500 (intercompany financing); funds settle through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Fedwire for USD corresponding accounts. All incoming international electronic funds transfers (IETFs) of CAD 10,000 or more within 24 hours trigger a FINTRAC report by the receiving Canadian bank. Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year. No extension is available for the T2 filing deadline.

Line 300 - Taxable Income Compute net income under Part I, add or deduct applicable amounts, and then subtract the maximum carryover claims, including the non-capital loss carryforward, restricted farm loss, and other adjustments, to arrive at taxable income. Non-resident corporations compute taxable income on Canadian-source business income as determined under sections 115 and 116 of the Income Tax Act. Inter-company payments such as management fees and royalties to non-arm's length parties may be recharacterised under section 247 if not supported by documentation. Schedule 4 - Allocation of Taxable Income to Ontario Calculate and report the share of taxable income allocated to Ontario based on the corporation's permanent establishment locations, gross revenue attributable to Ontario, and the applicable allocation formula for a multi-jurisdictional corporation. For a corporation resident in Ontario with no other permanent establishment, 100% of taxable income is allocated to Ontario. A non-resident corporation with an Ontario permanent establishment may be subject to Ontario corporate income tax on the taxable income allocated to that permanent establishment. The allocation schedule must be supported by factual evidence of where products are sold, where contracts are concluded, and where the operating personnel are located. Late filing of the T2 attracts an immediate penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% for each complete month of delinquency, up to 12 months. A second late filing within a three-year period increases the base penalty to 10% and the monthly surcharge to 2% per month, up to 20 months. CRA compound interest is also charged daily on late tax instalments, assessed taxes, and penalties at a prescribed rate equal to the 90-day treasury bill rate plus 4%.
Line 350 - Part I Federal Tax and Part IV Tax Calculate the basic Part I federal tax at 15% of taxable income, apply the small business deduction or other credits, and then add any need for Part IV dividend tax. The Ontario tax is calculated on a separate schedule and entered into the T2 tax summary. Non-resident corporations may claim treaty relief from Part I tax only through an applicable income tax treaty. The T2 must contain the relevant treaty schedule and an entity-level declaration of eligibility under the DTA. Reduced withholding on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is separate and does not eliminate the requirement to file a T2. Late filing of the T2 attracts an immediate penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% for each complete month of delinquency, up to 12 months. A second late filing within a three-year period increases the base penalty to 10% and the monthly surcharge to 2% per month, up to 20 months. CRA compound interest is also charged daily on late tax instalments, assessed taxes, and penalties at a prescribed rate equal to the 90-day treasury bill rate plus 4%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000. The T106 Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents must be filed where the aggregate value of transactions with a given non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for a taxation year. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including a functional analysis and OECD-aligned Local File, is expected for all controlled transactions and must be produced if requested by the CRA; global groups meeting the country-by-country threshold are also required to maintain the Master File and CbC report.

Economic substance scope: Ontario and Canada do not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in UAE or Barbados. However, the corporation must maintain genuine substance, including a registered office, board minutes, bank accounts, employees and physical or operational infrastructure in Ontario, where applicable. Arrangements lacking real economic substance and carried out primarily to avoid tax are vulnerable to the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, which can recharacterise the transaction and impose reassessments. CRA may also deny treaty benefits where the entity lacks substance and is a mere conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion or Gross Negligent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: A gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax owing on the misreported income may be assessed under subsection 163(2). Criminal prosecution under section 239(1) may result, upon summary conviction, in fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to two years; upon indictment, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false return or participate in the evasion may be jointly and severally assessed for the penalties under the corporate attribution rules; they may also face personal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraud, which carries a maximum of 14 years' imprisonment. Courts may order travel bans, passport seizure, or asset seizures while prosecution is pending.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the shell structure, reassess the Canadian-source income under section 247(2) or the GAAR, impose a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the aggregate adjustment where applicable, and assess the non-resident shareholder's tax on a look-through basis. The corporation may also be administratively dissolved under the Ontario Business Corporations Act for failing to file required annual returns.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under section 160 of the Income Tax Act for transfers to non-arm's length shareholders if the shell transferred property without consideration. They may be assessed for unremitted withholding taxes under section 227.1, and an insolvent shell corporation does not shield directors from fraud or culpable conduct causing third-party claims or class actions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under section 247(2), CRA may adjust the transfer price to an arm's-length amount; subsection 247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment amount where the adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and the transfer pricing documentation requirements are not met. Failure to file T106 carries a separate penalty of CAD 25 per day, capped at CAD 2,500, and an extended reassessment period of six or more years for undisclosed transactions.

Director personal liability: If the corporation is unable to pay the resulting reassessment, directors who approved or signed the deficient transfer pricing schedules may be liable for the tax, penalties and interest under the applicable director liability provisions. Reckless signing of a false corporate tax filing may expose directors to criminal sanctions and civil restitution; travel bans may be imposed in the course of an proceeds-of-crime restraint order if money laundering is also shown.
AML Failure, False Beneficial Ownership Reporting, and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, non-compliance with beneficial ownership, record-keeping, and reporting requirements may attract administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations. Unlawful activity-related transactions may lead to Criminal Code money laundering offences under section 462.31, punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years; Ontario's corporate statute separately penalises false filing of required records.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly launder profits from tax evasion or offshore fraud can be charged personally under the Criminal Code and be subject to asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition proceedings. The corporate veil is pierced where the director knowingly exercises control over illicit funds, and civil restitution orders can attach to personal accounts and real property.
