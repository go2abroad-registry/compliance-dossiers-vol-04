Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) for federal income tax and the federal Business Number (BN), with portal 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec provincial income tax, the Quebec sales tax (QST/TVQ), and, under the Single Administration Agreement effective 1 April 2013, the administration and filing of GST/HST returns for any registrant with an establishment in Quebec, via the portal 'ClicSÉUR Affaires' at https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/; corporate-law registration of the limited partnership is effected through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) at https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite) is formed and obtains legal personality through filing a declaration with REQ, which simultaneously issues the 10-digit Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Through the REQ-CRA-ROC single-window process, the LP then procures the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and its GST/HST and QST accounts: because the place of business is in Quebec, Revenu Québec processes both the federal GST and provincial QST registrations in one application (Form LM-1 'Demande d'inscription' or the online ClicSÉUR Affaires workflow). For a foreign-owned LP, the general partner must attest that a representative for service in Quebec is appointed; non-resident partners should provide their own BN or a nil-activity declaration if they will not themselves make taxable supplies in Canada. After registration, RQ issues the GST registration number and QST registration number (generally the same base number as the NEQ). The LP is a person for GST/HST and QST purposes and must file returns through RQ; the LP also must file the federal partnership information return (T5013, with Form RC4602 if a partner is non-resident) and, from 2023 onwards, the Quebec partnership information return (TP-600 or RL-501 equivalents) for its fiscal year.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96 and the Quebec Taxation Act (LQTA) the LP is a transparent flow-through vehicle: it computes business income at the partnership level, then allocates income/loss and computed capital to partners (including foreign limited partners) in accordance with the partnership agreement, with no entity-level income tax. The non-resident limited partner is taxable in Canada on the portion of the LP business income attributable to the Canadian permanent establishment of the partnership; if the partnership has no PE in Canada under the applicable tax treaty (e.g. Canada-US treaty Article V), the non-resident partner may nonetheless face a Part XIII tax of 25% on distributions characterized as interest, rents, or royalties (treaty-reduced to 10% or 15%) but not on a return of capital or a share of business income absent a PE. Canada applies worldwide taxation to resident partners and territory-based taxation to non-residents on Canadian-source income; Quebec applies an analogous provincial rule with provincial allocation formulas. No general tax-exempt zone exists in Quebec, although the LP may benefit from investment tax credits or CDAE-type refundable credits in the technology/logistics sector if the qualifying requirements are met. — A Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite) is formed and obtains legal personality through filing a declaration with REQ, which simultaneously issues the 10-digit Quebec enterprise number (NEQ). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96 and the Quebec Taxation Act (LQTA) the LP is a transparent flow-through vehicle: it computes business income at the partnership level, then allocates income/loss and computed capital to partners (including foreign limited partners) in accordance with the partnership agreement, with no entity-level income tax. The non-resident limited partner is taxable in Canada on the portion of the LP business income attributable to the Canadian permanent establishment of the partnership; if the partnership has no PE in Canada under the applicable tax treaty (e.g. Canada-US treaty Article V), the non-resident partner may nonetheless face a Part XIII tax of 25% on distributions characterized as interest, rents, or royalties (treaty-reduced to 10% or 15%) but not on a return of capital or a share of business income absent a PE. Canada applies worldwide taxation to resident partners and territory-based taxation to non-residents on Canadian-source income; Quebec applies an analogous provincial rule with provincial allocation formulas. No general tax-exempt zone exists in Quebec, although the LP may benefit from investment tax credits or CDAE-type refundable credits in the technology/logistics sector if the qualifying requirements are met.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; partnership status is statutory, but the LP and its partners may make the following elections: (i) ITA s. 97(2) election to transfer property to the partnership at cost on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (ii) ITA s. 96(3) election to allocate partnership income to retiring partners in certain cases; (iii) ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 and s. 12.13 hybrid-mismatch prioritization elections, supported by Form RC4706 ('Notice of Foreign Entity Classification') where a US partner has elected its Canadian LP to be a 'disregarded entity' for US check-the-box purposes and hybrid double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes arise; (iv) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156(2) and LQV s. 502.0.12 elections between closely related registrants (e.g. a corporate general partner and the LP) to treat certain intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating GST/QST leakage on fleet-dispatch platform infrastructure; and (v) under ETA s. 211.1 and LQV parallel provisions, an election to have a supply of a financial service treated as taxable where the LP engages in mixed taxable/financial turnover. No election converts the LP into a taxable corporation; for Quebec provincial purposes the LP must also make a separate election under the LQTA where the partnership realizes an adjustment to the cost of a property under s. 96(9) of the federal rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in cumulative worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) per calendar quarter, which is the small-supplier threshold under both ETA s. 148(1) and LQV s. 91.1; once the LP exceeds $30,000 CAD of taxable supplies in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters, GST and QST registration is mandatory; the LP may voluntarily register below the threshold to claim input tax credits (ITCs) and QST refunds; for a Quebec LP supplying SaaS telematics services to Canadian B2B and B2C customers, the digital-services place-of-supply rules in ETA ss. 143.1-143.2 and GST/HST Notice 252, mirrored by LQV ss. 25.0.2-25.0.3, deem B2C supplies to be made in the province of the recipient's ordinary residence and B2B supplies in the province of the recipient's establishment, so invoices addressed to a Quebec customer generally attract both GST and QST, while invoices to a customer in another province attract GST only (or HST where applicable).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA s. 144 and Schedule VI, Part V, and the parallel QST rule in LQV s. 176 and Schedule III, a supply of a 'service' or 'intangible personal property' (including a SaaS telematics subscription) is zero-rated when made to a recipient who is a non-resident and the supply is not in connection with real property situated in Canada nor with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For the fleet-route optimization SaaS, the test is met if: (i) the customer is a non-resident logistics company not registered for GST/HST/QST; (ii) the software is performed through servers outside Canada or the substantive data processing occurs offshore; and (iii) the contract and billing address of the customer are outside Canada. CRA administrative policy P-051 and GST/HST Notice 252 require the supplier to consider the 'actual use' of the intangible property: if any part of the software is used or consumed in Canada (e.g. a Canadian subsidiary of the foreign customer, or Canadian telematics data), the supplier must apportion and charge GST/QST on the Canadian portion unless the customer is a registrant entitled to reclaim. The LP should obtain from each export customer a written declaration of non-residency (e.g. CRA Form RC 2395 or a contractual representation) and, where the customer provides a foreign VAT/GST number, retain it in the Stripe customer record; failure to hold such evidence shifts the burden to the supplier on audit.

  • SaaS subscription agreement or order form signed by the non-resident customer indicating the foreign territory of performance and end-user jurisdiction
  • Commercial invoice issued via Stripe Billing to the foreign customer, bearing the LP's GST/QST registration numbers and explicitly stating that the supply is zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI Pt. V and LQV Sch. III
  • Non-resident status proof: certificate of incorporation, foreign VAT/GST registration certificate, or a signed declaration of non-residence (CRA Form RC 2395 or Revenu Québec equivalent)
  • Bank settlement evidence: Stripe payout ledger, SWIFT credit advice, or inward remittance certificate showing funds arriving from outside Canada for each invoiced export
  • Technical delivery record: telematics fleet-account dashboard metadata, user login and IP address region reports, and cloud-hosting region documentation (e.g. AWS/GCP region) demonstrating that the software service is delivered and consumed outside Canada
  • For any goods component mistakenly included (e.g. onboard telematics hardware shipped to a foreign customer), a customs export declaration (Canada Border Services Agency CUSMA or Form B13A) and bill of lading must be attached
  • Where the contract provides for Canadian-side usage, a documented apportionment worksheet and, if the customer is a registrant, a GST/HST self-assessment or reverse-charge certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Canada or Quebec: CRA and Revenu Québec do not require pre-clearance, API-based digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls comparable to the CFDI or SDI regimes. An electronic invoice or a PDF generated by a billing tool such as Stripe Billing is legally acceptable provided it contains the mandatory GST and QST information: supplier name, BN and NEQ numbers, GST/HST account number, QST registration number, invoice date, description of the services, amount, and the rates (5% GST, 9.975% QST). Revenu Québec's e-invoicing guidelines (e.g. bulletin TVQ.205) require, when an electronic record replaces the paper invoice, that the integrity and legibility of the fiscal data be reproducible over the 7-year retention period; a complete audit trail of the SaaS usage and billing events must be preserved. Canada has announced a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing standard built on Peppol and the PINT format (phased adoption starting 2024-2025 for federal procurement and large enterprises), but participation is not mandatory for a Quebec LP in the logistics-tech sector.

Canada has no statutory 180-day export-currency repatriation deadline in the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act; the 180-day benchmark is applied by CRA auditors and transfer-pricing examiners as the presumptive ordinary-course billing cycle for cross-border SaaS receivables. If a related foreign partner or affiliate fails to remit the subscription fee within 180 days, CRA may apply ITA s. 247(2) transfer pricing adjustments (treating the unpaid receivable as an interest-bearing intercompany loan with deemed income accrual) or, where the LP is shareholder-related, apply ITA s. 15(2) and s. 214(3) to reclassify the receivable as a shareholder benefit subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax, plus interest and penalties. On the indirect tax side, a fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claim is treated as tax evasion: CRA and Revenu Québec can reassess the taxable supply within 4 (federal, 7 in Quebec) years of the return due date, or without limitation in cases of misrepresentation; the LP would owe the uncollected GST and QST plus compound daily arrear interest (prescribed rate plus 4% federal/QST), a failure-to-remit penalty of 6% plus 6% per month on the net tax due (ETA s. 280), a gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 285.1; LQV s. 1040), and in aggravated cases fines up to $100,000 CAD and imprisonment of up to 5 years for making false statements (ETA s. 327.1); additionally, any input tax credits allocated to the export transaction are clawed back with per-recipient penalties. Records substantiating the zero-rating must be retained for 6 years under the federal ITA and ETA and 7 years under LQV for Quebec audits; where the LP supplies a Canadian customer with a foreign billing address, Revenu Québec will rebut the presumption of export by examining the IP address, the place of execution of the business contract, and the location of the fleet management data.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) on both the current and the capital account. The CAD floats freely; there is no exchange-control licensing regime and no prior Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval is required for a Quebec limited partnership to receive capital contributions from non-resident partners or to remit profit distributions, interest, management fees, or return-of-capital reductions in CAD or any other convertible currency. The only operative constraints are Canadian income tax (including, where applicable, Part XIII withholding on certain allocations and payments made to non-resident partners, with treaty relief) and the documentary and reporting duties of the Canadian bank under FINTRAC and Statistics Canada; these are compliance obligations, not convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period requirement for export receipts. A Quebec logistics-telematics SaaS exporter may retain 100% of export proceeds in a Canadian-dollar or multi-currency (USD/EUR) account at any authorized Canadian financial institution. The only mandatory duties are statistical reporting to Statistics Canada under the Quarterly Survey of International Trade in Services when annual exports exceed the reporting threshold (approximately CAD 3 million), FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and compliance with Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening; no forex allocation or central-bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) exercises oversight of payment systems and administers the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime; foreign-exchange policy and capital-account surveillance are the responsibility of the Minister of Finance, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising deposit-taking institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforcing the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and cross-border fund-movement reporting, and Global Affairs Canada administering international economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not recognize the European EMI licence class; a foreign payment-service provider that accepts CAD-denominated SaaS payments must register with FINTRAC as a foreign money-services business (FMSB) and, if it supplies retail payment functions in Canada, must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The Quebec LP may lawfully route subscription billing through a Canadian-authorized acquirer such as Stripe Payments Canada (the 'Stripe_Billing' rail) or Nuvei, but channeling gross receipts through an unregistered offshore EMI presents FINTRAC compliance risk, bank-account termination exposure, and interruption of the Wire_Transfer settlement rail; the legally viable structure is Canadian PSP card settlement with SWIFT MT103 for corporate wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners inject capital exclusively by SWIFT MT103 wire credited to the LP's Canadian operating account; Canada requires no prior exchange-control approval. The remitter must include in the payment narrative the contract reference and a purpose message equivalent to 'capital contribution to a Canadian limited partnership / direct investment', and the bank's receiving officer will require the capital call, executed subscription agreements, partner resolutions, and a source-of-funds declaration for any remittance at or above CAD 10,000; the bank files the FINTRAC EFT report and the Statistics Canada cross-border transaction record. Capital contributions should be made in the partner's base currency with the CAD conversion executed as a spot trade at the receiving bank; allocations of active business income payable to non-residents are not blocked by exchange control but remain subject to Canadian tax and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding at remittance.Outbound disbursements from the Quebec LP cover cloud-infrastructure hosting (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), software subcontracting, data licences, commissions, and partner payments. The standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the Canadian operating account to the beneficiary's bank, with invoice number, contract ID, and tax-invoice references embedded in the narration; domestic CAD movements clear on Lynx (Bank of Canada RTGS, ISO 20022). A commercial letter of credit under UCP600 (MT700) remains available from the bank's trade desk but is not customary for SaaS subscription services; it would be used only where a vendor demands trade-documentary security. The 'Wire_Transfer' rail is fully supported, and Stripe_Billing settlements arrive in daily CAD or USD batches to the operating account with full remittance detail; USD disbursements should be paid from a USD omnibus account to avoid redundant CAD/USD conversions.Deliverable FX outright forward (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) executed under a bilateral ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, Schedule, and Credit Support Annex with the tier-1 treasury desk; no regulatory approval required in Canada.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) permitted for exposures in non-deliverable currencies (e.g., CAD/INR or CAD/IDR) where the bank cannot make physical delivery; cash-settled in CAD or USD.Cross-currency swap (CAD/USD) available for term-matching the partner capital injection against future USD-denominated disbursements and cloud-vendor payments, with amortizing principal exchanges and coupon flows.Plain-vanilla FX options (CAD call/USD put or CAD put/USD call) permissible for hedging contingent or subscription-based SaaS revenues where the exact settlement amount is uncertain.Natural multi-currency hedging through a USD omnibus operating account and USD-denominated billing for cross-border clients so that USD revenues offset USD hosting costs and minimize derivative reliance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not recognize the European EMI licence class; a foreign payment-service provider that accepts CAD-denominated SaaS payments must register with FINTRAC as a foreign money-services business (FMSB) and, if it supplies retail payment functions in Canada, must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The Quebec LP may lawfully route subscription billing through a Canadian-authorized acquirer such as Stripe Payments Canada (the 'Stripe_Billing' rail) or Nuvei, but channeling gross receipts through an unregistered offshore EMI presents FINTRAC compliance risk, bank-account termination exposure, and interruption of the Wire_Transfer settlement rail; the legally viable structure is Canadian PSP card settlement with SWIFT MT103 for corporate wires.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and no entity-level corporate income tax is imposed

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. The LP itself pays no dividends; partner profit allocations are not dividends and are generally taxed under Part I as Canadian-source business income. Where a non-resident corporate partner later distributes its after-tax Canadian profits to its own shareholders, a 25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax may apply unless reduced by treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 information return must be filed no later than five months after the partnership fiscal period end. Partner copies must be furnished by March 31 following the calendar year in which the fiscal period ends. A December 31 year-end therefore requires filing by May 31 and partner slips by March 31

Deadline: · Frequency:

The RL-15 and its transmittal must be filed with Revenu Québec by February 29 following the end of the calendar year in which the partnership fiscal period ends. In non-leap years, the deadline is February 28. Copies must be provided to each Quebec-resident partner by the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within the later of six months after the corporation's taxation year-end and six months after the partnership year-end; Quebec CO-17 similarly due six months after corporate year-end. Tax instalments are due throughout the year. A calendar-year corporation files by June 30 for the preceding year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in controlled transactions with non-resident related parties triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and the T106 information return. Country-by-country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000; Canadian entities must file Form RC4647 or RC4648 as applicable. Below the threshold, CRA may still demand documentation if any controlled cross-border transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Canada has not enacted offshore Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean style, but the same substance principles operate through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, treaty anti-abuse provisions, Quebec Civil Code doctrines, and CRA audit practice. A Quebec Limited Partnership supplying logistics SaaS must demonstrate real personnel, active decision-making, actual contracts, cloud infrastructure, and bank accounts in Quebec. A limited partner must not participate in management; otherwise limited liability is lost. A transaction structured solely to route profit to a zero-tax jurisdiction without operational substance will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and exposed to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1), conviction for tax evasion carries a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to two years. Civil gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) equal 50% of the understated tax. Revenu Québec imposes equivalent penal tax and interest under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: A corporate general partner's directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's failure to withhold or remit source deductions, GST/QST, and Part XIII amounts. Quebec Civil Code art. 317 permits piercing the corporate veil when incorporation is used for fraudulent or abusive purposes. Directors may be subject to civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, bail conditions restricting passport use and travel, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Violations and Cross-Border Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under s.247(2) to adjust the transaction to an arm's-length amount and impose a transfer pricing penalty of up to 10% of the adjustment when documentation is inadequate or not provided. Non-arm's-length interest and royalties may also be recharacterized and subjected to Part XIII withholding tax. Interest runs on the entire reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing declarations are personally exposed to penalties for wilful participation. The corporate veil does not protect directors where transfer pricing is used to strip profits artificially and deprive the Canadian Crown and Revenu Québec of lawful tax. Courts may order restitution, freeze personal accounts, and impose travel restrictions pending criminal or civil recovery proceedings.
Shell Company and Artificial Partnership Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule and Revenu Québec's anti-avoidance provisions allow the disregarding of the partnership for tax purposes, the denial of tax benefits, and the reassessment of income at the partner level. If abuse of rights is established under Quebec Civil Code art. 317, the partnership may be declared null. Additional penalties can reach 50% of the tax benefit in aggravated arrangements.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who control the business lose their statutory limited liability and become jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations. Directors of a corporate general partner can be personally liable when the shell is an instrument of fraud or abuse. Personal assets may be attached to satisfy CRA and Revenu Québec assessments, and criminal fraud proceedings can lead to imprisonment and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unregulated Payment Routing

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act allows FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation. Criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 is an indictable offence carrying imprisonment for up to 10 years for laundering proceeds of crime. Seizure and forfeiture of the funds and the assets acquired with them are mandatory upon conviction.

Director personal liability: Partners and directors who knowingly direct or participate in laundering operations face criminal prosecution in their personal capacity. The supervisory authority will pierce the partnership structure to trace beneficial owners. Conviction can result in substantial restitution, permanent loss of refused payment rails such as Stripe and wire banking, prohibition on acting as director, and stringent court-imposed travel restrictions.
Withholding Tax and Information Return Failures

Statutory sanction: A payer that fails to withhold Part XIII tax is liable for the full amount that should have been withheld, plus 10% penalty and interest. Late T5013 filings attract CAD 25 per day up to CAD 5,000. Corporate partners filing T2/CO-17 late face a 5% penalty plus 1% per month. Revenu Québec imposes parallel penalties and can deny certain deductions until filings are made.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are statutorily jointly and severally liable for withheld amounts that the corporation failed to remit, including employee source deductions, QST, GST, and Part XIII withholding. The liability is absolute in the absence of due diligence. Revenu Québec may register a legal hypothec on personal property, garnish personal bank accounts, and restrict international departure while the taxpayer is under investigation.
