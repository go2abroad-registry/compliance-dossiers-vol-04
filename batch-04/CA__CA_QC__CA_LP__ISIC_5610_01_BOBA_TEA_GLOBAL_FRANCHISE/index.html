Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership; contributions may be in money, property, or services; no escrow account or bank attestation is required by law. Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (CA_LP) must first register the partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and disclose the general and limited partners; it then opens a single Revenu Québec registrant file for GST/HST plus QST through Revenu Québec's online portal, or with CRA's Business Registration Online using the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN). A non-resident LP without a physical establishment in Quebec must appoint a service representative/mandatary in Quebec and file the partnership identity declaration with CRA and Revenu Québec. Revenu Québec issues the 16-character QST registration identifier (e.g., 1234567890 TQ 0001); the LP must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return and the Quebec equivalent (CO-17) annually.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box election. A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax: partners, not the LP, bear tax on partnership income. Available domestic elections include: (i) non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(8), mirrored in Quebec; (ii) ITA s. 216 election permitting a non-resident partner to compute Part I tax on active business income instead of Part XIII withholding; (iii) hybrid mismatch and dual consolidated loss limitations under ITA s. 18.4; and (iv) for U.S. federal purposes, a Form 8832 check-the-box election to treat the LP as an association taxable as a corporation, the resulting entity classification being recognized for Canada-U.S. treaty purposes in accordance with CRA administrative entity-classification guidance, while preserving fiscally transparent treatment where the LP is not an electing eligible entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide taxable supplies for the small-supplier exception); no registration threshold for non-resident suppliers of digital services or intangible property to Canadian/Quebec consumers effective GST/HST July 1, 2021 and QST January 1, 2019; franchise royalty and syrup B2B supplies to non-residents require standard registration once the CAD 30,000 turnover threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada where the supplier exports or causes the export under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1 and 14, with mirrored QST provisions under the LTVQ; syrup inventory shipped to foreign franchisees qualifies where the goods leave Canada and the franchisee obtains possession and use outside Canada. Services and royalties supplied to non-resident franchisees are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 7 and 23 when the recipient is a non-resident not present in Canada at the time the service is performed, the franchise licence is not exploited in Canada, and no Canadian goods or services are transferred in connection with the supply; Revenu Québec applies the same zero-rating tests for QST purposes. The master franchise and trademark licence contracts must be structured and invoiced to offshore franchise entities so that Canadian nexus (Canadian employees, Canadian customers, Canadian locations) is not incidentally created, which would disqualify zero-rating.

No mandatory B2B real-time clearance, API fiscal reporting, or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada or Quebec; GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA NETFILE (GST34) and QST returns through Revenu Québec; customs and export reporting is digital via Canada Border Services Agency using the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED), CERS, and the Single Window Initiative; Revenu Québec mandates electronic filing of RL/T4/CO-17 information slips and requires large businesses to transmit certain data via EDI/XML; all books, records, invoices, and e-invoicing documents must be retained for six years from the end of the relevant period, or longer with CRA consent, for audit purposes.

CRA may reassess within four years for ordinary adjustments, extended to seven years where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is shown (ETA s. 298); Revenu Québec applies the same general windows and a six-year window for unreported earnings. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA and Revenu Québec impose retroactive GST/HST plus QST of 14.975% on the assessed taxable receipts, with prescribed daily compound interest and civil penalties under ETA s. 280 (false entries) and s. 285.1 (25% of any overclaimed refund or rebate); gross negligence and wilful evasion attract penalties of up to 50% of tax understated (ITA s. 163(2) and QST Act s. 526), criminal fines up to double the tax evaded, and imprisonment under ETA s. 327 (maximum five years). CRA and Revenu Québec may also recompute franchise, syrup, and royalty transfer prices under ITA s. 247, triggering a 10% adjustment penalty, and may deny input tax credits or refunds until the complete documentary export bundle is produced. Evidentiary sufficiency includes receipt and banking of export proceeds within the 180-day window; failure to satisfy this evidentiary protocol can cause the export zero-rating to be clawed back retroactively with full interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely convertible currency with no exchange control regime for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, royalty payments, and capital outflows are permitted without prior central bank approval, subject to anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds, tax withholding compliance, and financial information reporting. For a Quebec limited partnership, the repatriation of distributable profits to non-resident partners is unrestricted but may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; no hard convertibility or remittance approval hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory currency conversion ratio applies to Canadian export receipts. Exporters may earn, hold, and disburse foreign currency freely. For statistical purposes, transactions exceeding C$10,000 or certain thresholds may require reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and/or Statistics Canada under the Banking Act and the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS); repatriated funds are not subject to any conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), with prudential regulation of banks and clearing settlement systems overseen by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administration and policy enforcement under the Financial Administration Act, the Currency Act, and the Export and Import Permits Act, administered through the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium for directly licensed EMIs; no EU-style EMI passport exists in Canada. Foreign fintechs acting as money transmitters or payment service providers must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, for activity in Quebec, may require additional licensing from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). A fully regulated Canadian chartered bank remains the highest-viability treasury backbone; offshore digital EMIs can be used as ancillary collection or disbursement tools only where the underlying funds flow is transparent and all Canadian reporting obligations are satisfied.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable at entity level; limited partnership is fiscally transparent. Corporate partners face a combined Canadian federal + Quebec headline rate of 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec) on allocated income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII); not applicable at LP level – the partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay dividends. Non-resident partners are taxed on their share of Canadian-source income and may face Part XIII withholding on distributions of passive income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File required for fiscal periods if the partnership or its franchise group has gross revenue exceeding CAD 15 million or non-arm's length cross-border transaction value exceeding CAD 5 million. Below thresholds, a simplified TP policy memorandum and contemporaneous benchmarking report are strongly recommended to support arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for LPs. However, the federal GAAR (Income Tax Act s.245) and Quebec GAAR (Tax Administration Act ss.1079.14-1079.18) deny tax benefits from transactions without genuine economic substance. For an LP franchisor, CRA/Revenu Québec may recharacterize intercompany royalty streams or expense allocations if the partnership lacks real operational authority, physical presence, or actual decision-making over franchise operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Federal: prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239(1) – fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec: Tax Administration Act penalties and criminal sanctions for false declarations.

Director personal liability: General partners and controlling directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties; civil restitution orders may freeze personal assets; travel bans can be imposed under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: Revenu Québec may annul LP registration and reassess income at the highest marginal rate; application of Canada’s GAAR to disregard transactions; administrative penalties up to CAD 10,000 per false statement.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil under Civil Code of Québec art. 317; partners become personally liable for the partnership’s tax debts; directors face disqualification and up to 3 years imprisonment for fraudulent corporate activities.
AML / Suspicious Transactions

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million per violation for businesses; criminal money-laundering offense under Criminal Code s.462.31 – imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers personally liable for failure to report; assets subject to civil forfeiture; travel bans and arrest warrants can be issued; partnership property may be seized pending investigation.
