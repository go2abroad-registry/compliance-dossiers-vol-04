Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; Quebec: Revenu Québec — Clic Revenu portal and Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for provincial corporate registration.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned or non-resident-controlled corporation: (1) incorporate federally or provincially and, if carrying on business in Quebec, register extra-provincially with the REQ; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory corporate TIN for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts; (3) open a GST/HST program account (BN + 'RT') mandatorily once taxable worldwide supplies exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive quarters, or voluntarily if supplying zero-rated exports; (4) register with Revenu Québec for a QST file number (BN + 'TQ'), completing Form LM-1-V or using the online service; (5) where employees are present in Quebec, register for the DAS (deduction at source), CNESST, and SDL accounts. Online TIN issuance is typically completed within 1–5 business days for standard filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian Business Corporation is a resident corporation subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income; no US-style check-the-box pass-through election is available — the corporation is the separate taxpayer; dividends are subject to a separate integration regime. For non-resident-owned corporations, Part XIV branch tax rules apply in addition, and Quebec levies a provincial corporate income tax as a distinct tax on the same entity). — Foreign-owned or non-resident-controlled corporation: (1) incorporate federally or provincially and, if carrying on business in Quebec, register extra-provincially with the REQ; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory corporate TIN for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts; (3) open a GST/HST program account (BN + 'RT') mandatorily once taxable worldwide supplies exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive quarters, or voluntarily if supplying zero-rated exports; (4) register with Revenu Québec for a QST file number (BN + 'TQ'), completing Form LM-1-V or using the online service; (5) where employees are present in Quebec, register for the DAS (deduction at source), CNESST, and SDL accounts. Online TIN issuance is typically completed within 1–5 business days for standard filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian Business Corporation is a resident corporation subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income; no US-style check-the-box pass-through election is available — the corporation is the separate taxpayer; dividends are subject to a separate integration regime. For non-resident-owned corporations, Part XIV branch tax rules apply in addition, and Quebec levies a provincial corporate income tax as a distinct tax on the same entity).

Available election forms

Available elections include: subsection 85(1) rollover election for intercorporate asset transfers; capital dividend account election under subsection 83(2); functional-currency election under subsection 261(1) for eligible corporations reporting in Canadian dollars; GST/HST joint election under subsection 273(1) and QST joint election for closely related corporations; quick-method GST/QST accounting elections; small-supplier voluntary registration election; and non-resident section 216 election for net rental income remittance. There is no check-the-box entity classification regime in Canadian federal or Quebec law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies per calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier rule) — applicable to both GST/HST and QST; the CMRA virtual-office and mail-scanning provider must register once the threshold is exceeded; foreign suppliers of digital services to Quebec consumers may also be deemed to have a QST registration obligation regardless of the threshold in specific non-resident digital-services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available: (i) for goods exported from Canada under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supported by export documentation; (ii) for services supplied to a non-resident recipient under section 9 of Schedule VI where the service is not rendered to a person in Canada, is not in connection with real or tangible personal property located in Canada, and for the CMRA mail-scanning/digital-delivery service, requires that all recipients be non-residents and that the service be performable entirely by remote electronic delivery; (iii) mirror QST rules apply under sections 28 and 29 of the Quebec Sales Tax Act, with Revenu Québec requiring the non-resident status of the client be demonstrated by contract, proof of billing address outside Canada, and evidence of foreign payment rails; the service provider must also verify that no Canadian resident acquires the benefit of the service.

  • Export Declaration filed electronically with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods exceeding C$2,000 or controlled goods
  • Commercial invoice and signed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the scope of the digital mail-scanning deliverable
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or international courier consignment note evidencing outbound movement of physical mail items
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/FX settlement record documenting payment from abroad
  • Non-resident declaration or client self-certificate, plus delivery and IP-address logs showing the service was supplied from Canada to a foreign destination

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or block-chain-enabled e-invoicing is currently in force in Canada or Quebec; e-invoicing is voluntary and aligned to the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 standard; however, electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns is mandatory for all registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million, and XML/EDI application programming interface transmission is supported through the CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's transmission services; the CMRA service line must maintain digital records and provide electronic invoices upon tax-audit request.

CRA and Revenu Québec apply strict audit protocols to zero-rated export claims; unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating results in full reassessment of net tax, reversal of input tax credits, penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act of the greater of C$250 and 25% of the amount claimed where gross negligence is established, daily compounded interest at prescribed rates, and criminal liability under subsection 327(2) of the ETA for deliberate evasion; the Quebec QST Act imposes parallel clawback provisions, a further administrative penalty of up to 15% for repeated false statements, and a 4-year normal reassessment window extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating and freely convertible currency under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There is no general exchange-control legislation restricting capital or current account transactions. Profit remittances, including dividends and branch profits payable to non-resident shareholders, are freely transferable in any permitted currency, subject only to income tax withholding under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Canada-Quebec tax allocation framework. No prior regulatory clearance from the Bank of Canada is required for share capital injections, intercompany loans, or repatriation of earnings. Subordinate restrictions flow exclusively from sanctions, anti-money laundering (AML) due diligence by financial institutions, and large-trade reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation timeline for Canadian resident exporters. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency indefinitely in Canadian chartered bank accounts, foreign-currency accounts, or offshore accounts, subject to Canadian transfer-pricing rules and FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for physical movements or transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. The commercial mail receiving and scanning services model relevant to CA_CORP does not generate export proceeds in the traditional goods-shipment sense; therefore no Bank of Canada conversion mandate or CBSA export-currency declaration applies. Compliance remains centered on accurate transactional labelling and source-of-funds documentation for recurring cross-border payments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating within the federal Department of Finance framework; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforce cross-border currency and anti-money laundering reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability and medium-to-high legal caution. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and virtual account providers are not licensed banks in Canada and cannot provide primary clearing-account status comparable to a chartered bank. An entity acting as a payment service provider in Canada generally must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, depending on Quebec activities, satisfy the Act respecting the distribution of financial services and provincial consumer-protection rules. For CA_CORP's virtual-office and mail-scanning operation, offshore EMIs may be used only as ancillary collection/remittance channels for low-value recurring fees, never as the principal treasury rail. Funds held with unregulated EMIs are not CDIC-insured and may create heightened sanctions and beneficial-ownership diligence burdens for Canadian directors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders or intercompany lenders must be effected by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent international wire to CA_CORP's Canadian operational or escrow account in CAD. The remitting financial institution must reference the Canada SWIFT BIC of the beneficiary bank, the beneficiary account number, and clear payment narrative such as 'Share capital contribution — CA_CORP' or 'Intercompany funding — CA_CORP'. Although Canada does not prescribe a national purpose-code taxonomy, the bank's electronic onboarding and transaction screening will classify the payment under the source-of-funds and purpose categories. The customer must provide a board resolution, share-subscription agreement, or loan agreement matching the exact amount and remitter. Transactions at or above CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC as large virtual-currency/cross-border transactions where applicable; the bank may request the Fund Sources and Purpose of Funds (L-4) form or equivalent. Any remittance routed through an intermediary must include the ordering institution's identifiers and must not obscure the beneficial owner.Outbound settlement for trade payables, operating disbursements, and commercial obligations is cleared primarily through payments Canada's Lynx system for high-value CAD payments, the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for pre-authorized debits and electronic cheques, and SWIFT MT103 for cross-border foreign-currency payments. For commercial letter of credit transactions, the Canadian issuing bank may transmit an SWIFT MT700 to the beneficiary's bank, with settlement on maturity through MT202/MT103 using the payment terms of the LC. USD disbursements can be cleared through a Canadian-dollar/US-dollar 'due to' account or correspondent banking relationships at major Canadian Schedule I banks. The virtual-office and mail-scanning business will predominantly use MT103 for vendor payments and card-scheme clearing through Stripe for recurring client subscriptions.
