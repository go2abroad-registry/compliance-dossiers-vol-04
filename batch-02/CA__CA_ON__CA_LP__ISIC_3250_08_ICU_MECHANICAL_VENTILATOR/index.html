Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada), Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/registration.html; Ontario limited partnership registration is effected through ServiceOntario (Ontario Business Information System), Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry

E-government filing portal

A non-resident investor establishing a Canadian limited partnership must first register the Ontario limited partnership with ServiceOntario (Form 1 under the Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16); second, the partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number (GST/HST) Account' via CRA's Business Registration Online portal, which creates the GST/HST program account (RT); third, the partnership must open an RM (Corporation Import/Export) program account for Customs and export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) if goods exceed CAD 2,000 per shipment; fourth, if the partnership hires staff, an RP (Payroll) account is required; each non-resident limited partner with sufficient Canadian-source income must separately file Canadian income tax returns and obtain a CRA Individual/Corporation Number or domestic entity BN as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership is not a taxable entity, and its income or loss is computed at the partnership level (s. 96) and allocated to the partners for inclusion in their own income; for GST/HST purposes the partnership is a distinct 'person' under the Excise Tax Act, and a non-resident partner's Canadian-source business income may be exposed to Part XIV withholding under s. 212.1 if the partnership engages in a Canadian business through a permanent establishment; Ontario does not impose a separate partnership-level income tax, and the Ontario HST component is collected by CRA.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the LP is statutorily flow-through for income tax but may make (a) a s. 216.1 election to defer non-resident partner withholding where the partnership earns only passive rental income, (b) a s. 98(3) election to roll a sole proprietorship into the partnership on a tax-deferred basis, (c) an s. 96(9) fiscal-period election to align the partnership's fiscal period with a partner's taxation year, and (d) undertakings or agreements with CRA under s. 116 to reduce withholding on disposition of future taxable Canadian property; in addition, Budget 2021 and the OECD hybrid mismatch provisions (s. 18.2/18.3 proposals, MLI Article 3) require documentation of an arm's-length economic substance arrangement, preventing a determination that the Canadian LP is treated as a hybrid by its foreign partner jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada, including the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter; once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded the supplier must register effective the first calendar day following the quarter in which it is exceeded; non-resident vendors with no Canadian permanent establishment may still be obliged to register if they make taxable supplies in Canada, and exporters making only zero-rated supplies may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits (ITCs) for Canadian input costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under s. 142 of the Excise Tax Act applies to a supply of tangible personal property (the invasive ICU ventilator firmware and hardware) where the supplier ships the goods to a non-resident consignee by its own conveyance or via a common carrier at the supplier's direction, or where the recipient is a non-resident and the goods are delivered directly to the non-resident's address outside Canada. Schedule VI, Part II, zero-rates medical and assistive devices (including critical care ventilators) supplied to a Canadian public hospital authority, health care facility, or eligible institution, provided the purchaser certifies eligibility and the device is used exclusively for medical or rehabilitation purposes. Schedule VI, Part V, zero-rates services supplied in respect of goods that are exported by the recipient, provided the services are directly linked to an export document trail. Any exempt Canadian sale to an end-user patient (e.g., a private individual buying a ventilator without a prescription) will remain exempt at 0.00% for the provincial component but may expose the seller to ITC recapture if the GST was claimed; in all cases, commercial exporters must maintain Proof of Export documents sufficient to demonstrate physical exit within a reasonable period.

  • Commercial invoice (English or French) with the ventilator's proper tariff classification (H.S. 9018.90.00 or 9019.20.00 as appropriate)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill naming the Canadian supplier as shipper and the non-resident buyer as consignee, with the named foreign port of discharge

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous controls, or digital stamping system. CRA requires electronic books and records retention under s. 286 of the Excise Tax Act and mutat regulation respecting electronic records (TSZ format, audit trail, six-year retention). The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) e-invoice interoperability standard is adopted on a voluntary basis by major Canadian suppliers and the Government of Canada. Export invoices may be issued in a foreign currency, but the GST/HST reporting periods must convert foreign-currency consideration to Canadian dollars using CRA's published exchange rates. Ontario does not separately mandate e-invoicing; the Ontario Business Registry provides digital registration data but not tax clearance for B2B invoices.

CRA routinely audits export zero-rating claims on a documentation-discovery basis. A lack of contemporaneous Proof of Export (customs declaration, bill of lading, and foreign remittance evidence, each aligned in substance and timestamp) leads to: (a) denial of the zero in the audit window and reassessment of uncollected HST at the full 13% on the export invoice principal; (b) assessment of interest and arrears penalties under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act; (c) a 20% gross negligence penalty plus a 5% penalty and a 7% repeat-penalty under s. 285.1 for making false statements or knowingly causing an omission; (d) failure to retain export documentation gives rise to a penalty up to CAD 2,400 for each omission and possible court order to comply; (e) for deliberate falsification, criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years, and for the Ontario 8% HST component, a provincial recovery action may be pursued by the Ontario Ministry of Finance as the same CRA-assessed amount includes a provincial share clawback with interest at the prescribed rate plus 6% for large corporations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate without surrender requirements, conversion mandates, or profit-remittance licensing. Practical compliance gates: FINTRAC electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting applies to any cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more, filed by the financial institution within the statutory period; CBSA Form E677 (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report) is required for physical currency or bearer instruments above CAD 10,000 entering or leaving Canada; the Investment Canada Act (ICA) applies to foreign investments that acquire control of an existing Canadian business (greenfield LP formation is generally exempt; non-cultural WTO-investor review threshold was approximately C$1.336B for 2024). Profit distribution to non-resident partners of the Ontario LP is not blocked by FX controls, but tax compliance applies: the LP passes through Canadian-source business income via T5013 allocations; non-resident partners remain taxable under Part I on allocated income; payments characterized as interest, rent, royalties, or deemed dividends attract Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25%, treaty-reduced (e.g., US-Canada treaty to 5/15%; China-Canada treaty to 10%). No obligation exists to convert foreign-currency export receipts into CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no minimum surrender ratio, and no mandatory conversion of export proceeds. The Ontario LP may retain 100% of USD, EUR, CNY, or other export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks. Mandatory ancillary filings: (i) the bank submits a FINTRAC EFT report for each wire of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) CBSA export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) is required for commercial exports valued above CAD 2,000 per shipment; (iii) Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under S.44 of the Medical Devices Regulations must accompany each export clearance; (iv) end-user and destination screening must exclude OFAC/CFSI and Canadian sanctions (Special Economic Measures Act, Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act) prohibited parties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) serves as the central bank, responsible for monetary policy, banknote issuance, financial system stability, and FX reserve management. Prudential supervision of banks is vested in OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) under the Bank Act; AML/ATF regulation and cross-border transaction reporting are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); CBSA enforces cross-border currency reporting; the Minister of Finance retains statutory authority over the Exchange Fund Account. Canada maintains no administrative foreign exchange control regime, no currency licensing, and no advance approval requirement for current or capital account transactions.

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium for core treasury; acceptable only for peripheral payables. Canada does not license standalone EMIs. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined 25% federal/Ontario headline for M&P income: federal 15% (non-CCPC general corporate rate) + Ontario 10% Manufacturing & Processing rate; Ontario LP is fiscally transparent, so the tax is assessed on the partner and the LP itself is exempt from corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII (s. 212(2)) on Canadian-source dividends paid to non-residents. LP distributions of active business income to a non-resident partner are NOT dividends and are not subject to Part XIII; the partner instead pays Part I net tax on the income allocated through the LP (subject to treaty PE protection). Where the LP receives and allocates dividends from a Canadian corporation, 25% Part XIII applies to the non-resident partner's share.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a December 31 fiscal period: on or before March 31 of the following calendar year. For any other fiscal period end: within 5 months after the fiscal period end. Each corporate partner files its own T2 return within 6 months of its tax year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no fixed Local File/Master File monetary threshold; however, Form T106 is mandatory when aggregate FMV of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a non-resident exceeds C$1 million in the fiscal period. The s. 247(3) TP penalty applies to taxpayers with gross revenue above C$5 million where the net TP adjustment exceeds C$5,000. Country-by-Country reporting (CBCR) filings are triggered at consolidated group revenue of C$750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Ontario has no statutory ESR framework. The Minister relies on the GAAR (s. 245), the sham doctrine, and the Canada-US Treaty beneficial-ownership and PE tests. An Ontario LP that carries on no real business activity, has no employees or premises, and exists solely to stream dividends/royalties out of Canada will be disregarded or re-characterized, resulting in full Part XIII re-assessment and denial of treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): summary conviction - fine of 50%-200% of the tax evaded and/or up to 2 years imprisonment; indictable - fine of 100%-200% and/or up to 5 years imprisonment; plus 50% gross-negligence penalty (s. 163.2) and non-deductible interest.

Director personal liability: General-partner directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholding taxes and GST/HST; individuals who authorize the evasion are subject to criminal prosecution, travel bans, passport surrender under the Passport Program, and civil restitution orders.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR s. 245(2): the avoidance transaction is denied and the full benefit re-assessed; the 25% Part XIII rate is restored; a 50% misrepresentation penalty under s. 163.2 may apply; CRA can also apply s. 247(2) to re-price any related-party component flows.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the LP veil where the general partner is a mere nominee; directors face derivative-liability claims under the Ontario Business Corporations Act s. 246, Criminal Code s. 465 conspiracy charges, and personal tax assessments where they have signed false declarations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) permits the Minister to re-price all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions; s. 247(3) imposes a 10% penalty on the net adjustment for large taxpayers (gross revenue > C$5 million); failure to file T106: $100/day (min $100, max $2,500) plus 10% of reportable amounts for knowingly failing.

Director personal liability: Directors who dictated non-arm's-length pricing can be reassessed personally for the LP's resulting tax shortfall through the culpable-director doctrine; criminal exposure arises under s. 239(2) for counselling a false tax filing; a US partner's Canadian PE finding can surge into personal worldwide reporting obligations.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties: up to C$1,000,000 per violation; Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA): up to C$1,000,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment for unauthorized export of controlled medical goods; Criminal Code s. 462.31 (laundering): up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AMPs and prosecuted for money laundering; travel bans and asset freezes are available under the Criminal Code and the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act s. 34 where the LP's transactions finance organized crime or sanctioned state programs.
