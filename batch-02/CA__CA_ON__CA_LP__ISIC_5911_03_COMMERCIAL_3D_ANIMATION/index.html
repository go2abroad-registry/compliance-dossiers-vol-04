Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permitted with restrictions. Ontario law requires the limited partnership to maintain a registered office with a physical, street-address location within Ontario where the records and ISC/UBO register are kept; a post office box is prohibited. A serviced or virtual office is acceptable if it is a genuinely staffed physical address in Ontario, is used for service of process, and the partnership's statutory records are physically maintained there. However, for an operating commercial 3D animation and VFX business, a pure mail-drop virtual address materially increases the risk of adverse findings on carrying-on-business in Ontario and of retroactive denial of limited liability; a short-term serviced office lease supplemented by a formal records-management agreement is the recommended minimum commercial substance arrangement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Business Number registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO), accessible at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting commercial activities in Ontario must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via CRA's Business Registration Online portal, by fax, or by mail. A non-resident partnership must provide its partnership constituent documents, the Canadian resident agent's name and address, and a completed Form NR1 (CoRD), Form RC471 (Non-Resident Registry), or equivalent CRA non-resident business registration package. The BN is issued immediately upon successful online registration; the GST/HST account is assigned simultaneously when the entity indicates taxable supplies exceeding the small supplier threshold or voluntary registration is requested. Ontario provincial tax accounts (e.g., Employer Health Tax, Workplace Safety and Insurance Board) are registered separately with the Ontario Ministry of Finance or the WSIB where applicable.

A non-resident partnership must provide its partnership constituent documents, the Canadian resident agent's name and address, and a completed Form NR1 (CoRD), Form RC471 (Non-Resident Registry), or equivalent CRA non-resident business registration package. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. A limited partnership is a fiscally transparent pass-through arrangement for Canadian income tax; an election to have the partnership treated as an eligible Canadian partnership for GST/HST purposes permits the partnership to file a single GST/HST return when all members are registered, under the 'Eligible Partnership' election (Form GST25). For non-resident partners, no deemed Canadian entity classification election is available; however, treaty-based structural hybrid mismatch elections may be relevant under the OECD Multilateral Instrument where double-dip depreciation or deductibility mismatches arise, and CRA administrative positions require the partnership to be recognized as the person making supplies for GST/HST purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies (excluding zero-rated exports and financial services) over a 12-month calendar or quarterly period for a non-resident small supplier; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, exports of tangible personal property and services supplied to a non-resident (including commercial CGI, VFX, 3D animation, and related intellectual property services) are zero-rated when supplied to a person who is a non-resident of Canada and not registered for GST/HST, and the service is performed or the property is delivered outside Canada or is specifically excluded from taxable supply. Documentary proof must establish the non-residency of the recipient and the offshore delivery/performance of the service; subcontracting services performed for another GST/HST registrant remain subject to 13% HST unless the services are zero-rated as a supply of intangible personal property or export service under Schedule VI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian border export documentation or proof that the goods were shipped from Canada, including B13A Export Declaration for non-regulated exports or Carrier's B3 Customs Entry)
  • Commercial Invoice identifying the foreign buyer and 3D animation/CGI deliverables
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/Wise B2B payment record showing receipt of funds from outside Canada
  • Export contract and scope-of-work agreement for offshored animation services
  • Certificate of non-residency of the recipient (e.g., foreign trade registry extract or tax residence certificate)

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime in Canada. The CRA requires that invoices issued by all registrants contain prescribed information and that electronic invoices and records be retained in a readable and auditable electronic format. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and the Canadian Revenue's e-invoice interoperability specifications (PEPPOL-based, led by the Canada Revenue Agency and the Digital Interoperability Program) are being progressively adopted, but participation remains voluntary.

The CRA may reassess an exporter to deny zero-rating and claw back input tax refunds if export documentation is incomplete or the supply is found to be taxable in Canada. Penalties include a false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated net tax, a penalty under section 281.2 of the Excise Tax Act of up to CAD 25,000 for failure to provide required export documentation, and interest on the assessed net tax under ETA section 280. Where export zero-rating is abused or fictitious exports are discovered, criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment. For non-arms-length offshore arrangements, transfer pricing adjustments may also apply under the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate with unrestricted convertibility for current account transactions. Capital account transactions are liberalized subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is permitted without prior approval, subject only to Canadian tax withholding and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. Exporters must repatriate foreign exchange proceeds through authorized dealers within 90 days. All cross-border receipts exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC suspicious transaction reporting and source-of-funds verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs due to Canadian Payment Services Regulations and strict AML/KYC requirements. Traditional correspondent banking via Tier-1 banks remains the only compliant channel for cross-border settlements.
