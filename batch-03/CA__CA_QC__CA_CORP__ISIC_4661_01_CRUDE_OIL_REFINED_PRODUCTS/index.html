Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal corporate income tax (T2) and GST/HST outside Quebec; Revenu Québec (Agence du revenu du Québec, RQ) administers both the 5% federal GST for Quebec-based registrants and the 9.5% Quebec Sales Tax (QST) under the Canada-Quebec integrated tax administration agreement, with filing through the clicSÉUR Affaires portal; statutory business registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ); federal online portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canada/Quebec business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a per se taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Business Corporations Act; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (ITA subsection 2(1), section 250(4)); no pass-through or fiscal transparency election is available; no tax-exempt zone applies in Quebec; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment under Part I and may be subject to Part XIV branch tax on after-tax earnings.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; a Canadian business corporation cannot elect partnership or disregarded status. Available elections relevant to this energy-commodity trading dossier: (i) GST/HST section 150 election between closely related Canadian-resident corporations to treat otherwise taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA section 156 election for nil-consideration supplies; (iii) ITA subsection 216 election permitting a non-resident to file a net-basis Canadian return for rental income; (iv) ITA section 219 election procedure for computing Part XIV branch tax on remitted branch profits; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.4-18.6 and Quebec equivalent) are self-executing effective 1 July 2022 for structured arrangements and 1 January 2023 for cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements, with no elective entity classification; cross-border mismatches involving a transparent foreign entity or hybrid instrument trigger deduction deferral, deemed dividend treatment or primary/secondary adjustment consequences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 small supplier threshold measured on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over four consecutive calendar quarters; registration for both GST and QST becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted and commercially advisable for exporters to recover input tax credits (ITCs); the same C$30,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers with no permanent establishment in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST/QST rate applies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Sales Tax mirror legislation to: (i) supplies of tangible personal property (crude oil, refined products and marine fuels) exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export, provided the goods actually leave Canada within 60 days of the time of supply unless the Minister extends the period, and documentary proof of exportation is obtained within two years of the supply; (ii) supplies of marine bunker fuel loaded as ships' stores on a vessel departing Canada on an international voyage, which are treated as exports at the time of bunkering; (iii) brokerage and commission services supplied to a non-resident in respect of exported petroleum goods where the service recipient is not a GST/QST registrant and the underlying goods are not situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; services relating to goods still physically in Canada (storage, blending, inspection, terminal handling) do not qualify for zero-rating and remain taxable at 14.975%. A registered exporter claims the zero rate and recovers input tax credits through the periodic GST/QST return; excess refunds are payable by Revenu Québec or CRA within approximately 30 days of filing, with statutory interest on late refunds.

Quebec mandates B2B electronic invoicing in phases: fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2023 for enterprises with annual turnover of at least C$500 million; after 31 December 2024 for turnover of at least C$50 million; and after 31 December 2025 for all enterprises. Invoices must be issued electronically in a structured format (XML/EDI or PDF with an advanced electronic signature) that guarantees authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility, and must be retained for six years. Canada operates no real-time fiscal clearance, API validation or digital stamping system; there is no continuous transaction control (CTC) government clearinghouse. Remittance of GST/QST returns is electronic through clicSÉUR Affaires (Revenu Québec) with EDI or direct-debit options, and Canada has adopted the PEPPOL network for Pan-Canadian B2G e-invoicing and public procurement.

Canadian law imposes no express statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is adopted as the risk-rated settlement window for this dossier and aligns with the documentary cycle expected by CRA and Revenu Québec in export verification examinations. Mandatory compliance windows are: 60-day physical exportation rule (ETA Schedule VI), 2-year documentary-evidence window, and 6-year record retention requirement. If zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA or Revenu Québec reassesses the supply at 14.975% and claws back refunds with arrears interest from the filing due date; the normal reassessment window is four years, with no limitation period where misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default is established. Civil penalties include ETA false-statement and gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax avoided, ITA section 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax, and Quebec equivalents under the Tax Administration Act. Criminal exposure arises under ETA sections 327/328 and ITA section 239 for tax evasion, with fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to two years on summary conviction or five years on indictment; Customs Act export reporting offences carry fines of up to C$25,000 per occurrence, and CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) penalties apply to false or incomplete B13A declarations. Fraudulent export zero-rating also triggers mandatory disclosure reporting, revocation of registration, denial of input tax credits, and retrospective clawback of refunds with compound interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains no exchange controls; conversion, holding, borrowing, lending, and repatriation of currency are unrestricted. Profit and dividend remittances are governed exclusively by tax law: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% statutory, treaty-reduced under USMCA and CETA, with no FX approval required. A Quebec business corporation may maintain its books in USD for tax purposes under section 261 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). Non-resident equity investment is screened only through the Investment Canada Act net-benefit notification or review procedure, never through currency convertibility rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no holding-period constraint on foreign currency receipts. Marine fuel and distillate export proceeds may remain indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible foreign currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. The only compliance frictions are FINTRAC-reportable electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and the usual income-tax recognition of export revenue under the accrual method.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and payment-system oversight is co-distributed to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation, Payments Canada for clearing and settlement infrastructure (Lynx RTGS), FINTRAC for AML/CFT surveillance, and the Department of Finance Canada for sanctions and foreign investment policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Non-Canadian EMI and digital-bank platforms (Wise Business, Revolut Business, MultiPass) are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot provide commercial SWIFT MT103 settlement, documentary credit advising, standby LC issuance, or Lynx clearing. They are not participants in the Canadian high-value payment system and hold client funds through banking-as-a-service correspondents, creating principal concentration risk for a High_Risk_Special_License energy merchant. Their permitted role is confined to pre-funded disbursement cards, travel and small-vendor payments, and incidental FX conversion. A Canadian-based digital money service provider would require FINTRAC MSB registration and, under the new retail-payments oversight regime, authorization by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; none of these vehicles can substitute for a Schedule I treasury relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing protocol: inbound funds from a non-resident parent or strategic investor are wired by SWIFT MT103, or by CHIPS/Fedwire for USD from US counterparties, to the Quebec corporations designated CAD or USD operating account at a Schedule I bank. Canada has no notification-based external purpose-code table comparable to a capital-control regime; remittance instructions must still carry accurate SWIFT fields: Field 50 ordering customer, Field 59 beneficiary, Field 70 remittance information identifying equity subscription, shareholder loan, or advance against a specific trade contract, and Field 72 bank-to-bank instructions. For equity injections, board resolutions, a notarized shareholder subscription agreement, and source-of-wealth statements are standard conditions precedent. FINTRAC large electronic funds transfer reports are mandatory for all incoming or outgoing transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and transfers exceeding CAD 100,000 are ordinarily scrutinized through the banks enhanced due diligence unit for consistency with the approved business plan. Where the non-resident investor acquires control of the Quebec entity, the Investment Canada Act notification must be filed before implementation if the enterprise value is below the annual WTO threshold, or an application for review if above that threshold (approximately USD 1.179 billion in 2024 terms).Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for supplier and broker disbursements in USD, CAD, or third currencies; (ii) SWIFT MT700 issues and MT707 amendments of commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 for marine fuel and distillate purchases, with documents comprising commercial invoice, bill of lading, Certificate of Quantity and Quality, shore-tank or barge delivery proof, and INCOTERMS 2020 delivery terms; (iii) SWIFT MT760 standby letters of credit under ISP98 issued as performance, bid, and payment guarantees to physical suppliers and terminal operators; (iv) domestic CAD high-value disbursements are cleared in real time over Lynx, the Payments Canada RTGS system with final settlement at the Bank of Canada; (v) USD legs clear through the Canadian banks US correspondent on Fedwire or CHIPS with OFAC and SEMA sanctions screening applied at every hop. Non-Canadian EMI and digital-bank platforms (Wise Business, Revolut Business, MultiPass) are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot provide commercial SWIFT MT103 settlement, documentary credit advising, standby LC issuance, or Lynx clearing. They are not participants in the Canadian high-value payment system and hold client funds through banking-as-a-service correspondents, creating principal concentration risk for a High_Risk_Special_License energy merchant. Their permitted role is confined to pre-funded disbursement cards, travel and small-vendor payments, and incidental FX conversion. A Canadian-based digital money service provider would require FINTRAC MSB registration and, under the new retail-payments oversight regime, authorization by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; none of these vehicles can substitute for a Schedule I treasury relationship.
