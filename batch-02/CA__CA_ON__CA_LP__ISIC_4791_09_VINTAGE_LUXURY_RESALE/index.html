Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE portals. The Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) is administered federally by the CRA, including the Ontario provincial component; the Ontario Ministry of Finance does not administer HST.

E-government filing portal

The Ontario Limited Partnership applies for a federal Business Number (BN) online through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by filing Form RC1 with the CRA (International and Large Business Directorate for non-resident LPs). The LP must open a GST/HST program account ('RT' program identifier) under the same 9-digit BN and elect a standard filing and remittance frequency. Non-resident LPs must submit a certified partnership agreement, evidence of foreign registration, and the identity of the general partner; an authorized Canadian representative is strongly recommended for service of process. One BN per entity is issued; the partnership itself is registrant for GST/HST and is assigned its own BN distinct from the partners' BNs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario LP formed under the Limited Partnerships Act is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes: it is not a taxpayer under the Income Tax Act but a fiscal unit that files a T5013 Partnership Information Return; each limited and general partner is taxed on its agreed share of income. Limited partners enjoy limited liability but are subject to at-risk limitations on loss deductibility. For GST/HST purposes the LP is a separate 'person' and, once registered, is the registrant making taxable supplies of secondhand luxury goods.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; partnership transparency is mandatory. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) the partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4), available where all partners are corporations; (ii) a reasonable partner income allocation agreement under ITA s. 96(1); (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election while below the small-supplier threshold; (iv) the ETA s. 156 election for nil consideration between registrants at the same location; and (v) the ETA s. 273 closely-related election where corporate partners are involved. Hybrid-mismatch recharacterization rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21) may disallow deductions in payment-hybrid arrangements, and no protective hybrid election is available to override them.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the preceding three consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST small-supplier threshold); voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax credits and apply zero-rating to exports, after which the LP must collect 13% HST on all taxable domestic sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act where the registered LP ships the vintage luxury goods from Ontario to an address outside Canada, or makes them available outside Canada, pursuant to a supply to a non-resident buyer. Consignment commission and platform facilitation services supplied to non-resident consignors may be zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V, Schedule VI if the service is not performed on goods physically located in Canada for a resident recipient. When the LP acquires used goods from non-registrant individual consignors, the deemed input tax credit under ETA s. 176 (tax fraction of 13/113 of the payout) arises at acquisition and remains claimable even where the goods are subsequently exported at zero rate; such deemed ITC is fully refundable only upon registration and net-filing through GST/HST NETFILE.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) or approved courier export manifest for each parcel shipped from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or house air waybill showing the named foreign destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange credit advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Consignment contract and payout statement to the consignor substantiating the ETA s. 176 deemed input tax credit
  • Carrier tracking history confirming delivery to the foreign address within the export deadline

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate exists in Canada or Ontario. The CRA requires electronic remittance of GST/HST net tax through GST/HST NETFILE or a recognized software vendor API with XML/JSON validation; the LP must issue ordinary electronic invoices (PDF/HTML/EDI) bearing its BN. Payment rails such as Stripe and Escrow Pay may operate under a tax-in-transit basis with the CRA. A voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability framework (PEPPOL) has been piloted for government procurement but is not yet a statutory mandate.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period under ETA s. 298(1), extended to 10 years for misrepresentation or neglect, and without any time limit for fraud. A zero-rated export claim requires the complete documentary bundle; missing or inconsistent export evidence re-characterizes the supply as taxable HST at 13%, reverses previously claimed ITCs, including the s. 176 deemed ITC, and imposes arrears interest. Penalties include a gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the net understated net tax, a false-statement penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties of 1% to 6% per month, and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 with fines equal to 100% to 200% of the evaded HST and imprisonment of up to five years for deliberately fraudulent export zero-rating schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, distributions, management fees, and capital redemptions payable to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (normally 25% before treaty relief) and banking-source-of-funds verification. FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to transfers at or above CAD 10,000, but no administrative convertibility license or central-bank permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline on export proceeds. A CA-LP luxury reseller may lawfully retain export receivables in any foreign currency and convert them at market rates at its discretion. Statutory obligations are confined to (i) CBSA and Statistics Canada export reporting for exported goods (B13 declarations; CAD 2,000 per-shipment threshold), (ii) six-year record retention under the Income Tax Act, and (iii) PCMLTFA cross-border currency reporting above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border funds-transfer and AML/CFT reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control licensing authority similar to SAFE, and the Bank of Canada exercises no prior-approval power over foreign-exchange transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operating collections and settlement-disbursement rails; Low for capital escrow and core treasury concentration. Canada does not license EU-style Electronic Money Institutions; non-bank payment service providers must register with OSFI under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's operational and capital-subscription accounts at a Schedule I bank, with the beneficiary line matching the registered name '[ENTITY NAME] Limited Partnership' and the remittance-information field stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LP AGREEMENT [DATE]'. Canada does not prescribe statutory payment-purpose codes, but the Details-of-Charge field should reference the partnership-agreement clause and the contributor's full name. Verification bundle: capital-call notice, executed subscription agreement, updated LP register, and source-of-funds documentation from the contributing partner's bank; the receiving bank files FINTRAC EFT reports for inflows at or above CAD 10,000 and may apply enhanced due diligence to inflows above CAD 100,000. USD, EUR, and GBP contributions may be credited to multi-currency sub-ledgers, converted at the bank's commercial rate, or held in a USD clearing account; value dating is confirmed through Lynx (Bank of Canada RTGS).Clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cross-border supplier, auction-house, and consignor payouts in USD, EUR, GBP, and JPY; (ii) Lynx and Payments Canada Real-Time Rail for domestic CAD disbursements; and (iii) a UCP600 Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Canadian bank for high-value inbound vintage luxury inventory procurement, payable at sight against commercial invoice, certificate of authenticity, packing list, insurance certificate, and carrier waybill. Consignor settlements follow the Escrow_Pay batch schedule and are executed as referenced MT103s tagged with seller ID; chargeback and refund flows are settled through the operating account and never commingled with the segregated escrow sub-account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operating collections and settlement-disbursement rails; Low for capital escrow and core treasury concentration. Canada does not license EU-style Electronic Money Institutions; non-bank payment service providers must register with OSFI under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may lawfully provide multi-currency receivables accounts and cross-border settlement, but they are not banks and cannot hold CDIC-insured deposits; they are unsuitable as the primary treasury anchor and cannot satisfy OSFI-supervised escrow segregation expectations. Optimal structure: Tier-1 Schedule I operating account as the statutory banking nexus, with licensed fintechs acting solely as settlement conduits sweeping Stripe and Escrow_Pay proceeds into the Canadian account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario). The Ontario limited partnership itself is not subject to income tax; income flows through to partners at their applicable corporate or personal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder. For the Ontario LP, profit distributions are not corporate dividends; no Part XIII withholding applies on allocations to partners, as the income is taxed directly at partner level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: aggregate consideration per related non-resident triggering Form T106 disclosure. CRA does not impose a fixed statutory dollar threshold for the Local/Master File; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared for all material cross-border related-party transactions, and penalties attach if not delivered within 90 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not enforce a codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the income tax framework requires real commercial substance. The GAAR (s. 245 of the Income Tax Act) allows CRA to deny a tax benefit from an artificial or sham transaction; an Ontario LP must maintain a functioning business, a valid general partner, and a physical Ontario address to preserve limited liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or direct evasion are jointly and severally liable for penalties and tax, may face 10-year border/exit bans, and can be subject to asset seizure.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may disallow loss carry-forwards, reassess under GAAR (25% penalty on the disallowed benefit), and Ontario may involuntarily dissolve the LP for failing to file the annual declaration.

Director personal liability: Where the limited-liability shield is abused, general partners and controlling officers can be personally liable for the LP's unpaid taxes and may lose the protection of the Limited Partnerships Act.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment plus a penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount (minimum CAD 5,000) under s. 247(3); unpaid non-resident withholding taxes under Part XIII become additionally collectible.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate officers are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and can be charged with a director's liability assessment equal to uncollected amounts.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1,000,000 for entities; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for obstruction or false reporting.

Director personal liability: Senior management who were willfully blind or knowingly enabled the non-compliance are subject to personal fines, prohibition orders, and personal criminal liability.
