Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 (approx. USD 112,000). At least 20% of the nominal capital, i.e. CHF 20,000, must be fully paid up and deposited in a blocked (liberation) account with a Swiss bank, to remain blocked until the Commercial Register issues the registration extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate and inspection by the Cantonal Fire Insurance (Gebäudeversicherung Zug) required for the registered office and office premises; workplace clearance from Suva (Swiss National Accident Insurance) for office-based staff. No civil-defense construction clearance applicable.

At least 20% of the nominal capital, i.e. CHF 20,000, must be fully paid up and deposited in a blocked (liberation) account with a Swiss bank, to remain blocked until the Commercial Register issues the registration extract.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV – Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung), Bern; online e-services portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch. Cantonal and communal tax authorities (e.g., Kantonales Steueramt) administer direct taxes, while the commercial register authority (Handelsregisteramt) issues the UID business identification number. The Swiss Customs Authority (FOCBS/BAZG) administers customs declarations and export proofs.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) must: (1) execute the notarial deed of incorporation and appoint the board of directors; (2) register the AG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) of the canton of registered office; on entry, the Federal Statistical Office automatically assigns the 9-digit Swiss Business Identification Number (UID, format CHE-123.456.789), which serves as the corporate tax ID; (3) for VAT purposes, complete VAT registration with the ESTV through the e-services portal (ePortal/eService) once annual taxable turnover from Swiss-domestic supplies reaches CHF 100,000 (effective 1 January 2025, lowered from CHF 250,000); (4) obtain the MWST/VAT number (CHE-123.456.789 MWST) and file quarterly or semi-annual returns electronically. Foreign entities not domiciled in Switzerland and registering for Swiss VAT must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (domiciliary representative) and submit the ESTV's 'Registration of a foreign company' form, including articles of incorporation, board/power-of-attorney evidence, and ISIC business activity codes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss AG is a separate legal person subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability at the federal, cantonal, and communal levels based on registered office (Sitz) or place of effective management in Switzerland. It is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss AG is a separate legal person subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability at the federal, cantonal, and communal levels based on registered office (Sitz) or place of effective management in Switzerland. It is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions. Territorial scope: worldwide income of the AG is taxable, with double-tax-treaty relief and the Swiss domestic carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate; no tax-exempt zone treatment is available.

Available election forms

Swiss law contains no US-style check-the-box election, but provides the following legally binding elections/reliefs: (i) participation deduction (Beteiligungsabzug) election for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income from substantial shareholdings (≥10% or fair-market-value threshold CHF 1 million); (ii) patent box and R&D super-deduction elections available in most cantons; (iii) mandatory tax ruling requests with cantonal and federal authorities to confirm transfer pricing, permanent-establishment avoidance, and financing structures; (iv) hybrid mismatch outcome adjustments (Swiss rules effective 1 January 2020 implementing BEPS Action 2) disallow deductions for hybrid arrangements resulting in double non-taxation and deny election benefits; (v) VAT optional net-rate method (Saldomethode) election for taxpayers with turnover below CHF 5,005,000 (subject to requirements); (vi) election to file Swiss VAT Group (Gruppenbesteuerung) for qualifying affiliated entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover from Swiss-domestic supplies, effective 1 January 2025 (previously CHF 250,000). The threshold applies cumulatively across the AG's total Swiss taxable supplies; foreign suppliers of electronically supplied services (including voice-streaming and digital services) to private consumers domiciled in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT even below the threshold, unless the electronic interface operator (Apple/Google) is deemed the supplier under the 1 January 2025 Schnittstellengesetz platform framework.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) export of movable goods abroad substantiated by a Swiss customs export declaration; (ii) services the recipient uses outside Switzerland (export of services) under Article 23(2) and place-of-supply rules of Article 18 MWSTG, including electronically supplied services (voice streaming, digital content) supplied to business or private recipients domiciled abroad; (iii) ancillary transport services linked to exported goods; and (iv) certain international services. For the voice-streaming social app, in-app purchases settled through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing to foreign end customers may be zero-rated if Swiss supply-side rules apply and substantiation proves recipient residence abroad (billing address, IP geo-location, payment-card BIN, and subscriber contract data). Supplies to residents of Switzerland are subject to Swiss VAT at 8.1%; where the App Store/Google Play operator is deemed the supplier under the 2025 electronic-interface rules, the platform is liable for Swiss VAT on B2C digital supplies, requiring the AG to structure contract flows accordingly.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / déclaration en douane) accepted by the FOCBS/BAZG
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or payment-provider credit confirmation showing FX repatriation of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export/service contract
  • Recipient-domicile evidence for services (IP log data, payment card BIN, business registration extract, or signed recipient confirmation)

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax stamping, or API-based invoice clearance regime for private documents. Mandatory invoice elements include supplier name and MWST/UID, recipient name and address, date, description of supply, consideration, and applicable VAT rate/amount. Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) acceptance has been mandatory for domestic B2B/B2C invoice payments since 30 June 2020. A structured B2B e-invoicing mandate (eBill/Peppol-similar XML format) becomes generally binding on 1 January 2026 (voluntary adoption since 2024; EDI legacy formats remain acceptable). VAT returns are filed digitally via the ESTV e-service portal under a self-assessment system.

The ESTV may audit export zero-rating substantiation at any time within a 10-year VAT assessment limitation period. Unsubstantiated zero-rating claims are retrospectively recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies, with VAT assessed at 8.1%, plus interest applied from the filing date. Under Articles 103–105 MWSTG, penalties for tax evasion are imposed: up to 15% of the tax evaded for negligent conduct, up to 25% for intentional conduct, and in aggravated cases or for VAT fraud (Abgabebetrug), a fine of up to CHF 30,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years may be imposed on responsible directors; clawback of input VAT credits previously taken is also mandatory.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Switzerland maintains no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no import deposit schemes, and no central bank approval for cross-border fund movements. Dividends, royalties, interest and loan repayments may be freely remitted abroad, subject only to the 35% statutory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) or applicable treaty relief. Capital repatriation is unrestricted, but all payments must comply with Swiss sanctions law, anti-money laundering (AMLA) requirements, and the transparency obligations of the Swiss Financial Market Supervision. In practice, banks treat crypto-origin funds, non-verified beneficial owners, or high-risk counterparties as the principal friction points rather than any FX restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A Swiss AG may hold foreign currency in its accounts freely, use it for offshore trade settlements, or defer conversion, as the national treatment under the SNB's free capital movement principle presumes unrestricted foreign exchange handling. Banks will nonetheless record the current-account underlying and may request export invoices to evidence the commercial purpose of an incoming payment.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF), with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervising banking and payment institutions for compliance purposes

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium as a supplemental treasury rail but Low as the primary clearing route. An EU/UK-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) cannot passport into Switzerland; it may offer a CHF settlement through a Swiss correspondent bank, yet such an arrangement does not grant direct SIC membership and fails the Swiss-payment prudential test. FINMA-registered Swiss fintech firms or SRO members can serve as secondary wallets for small user refunds, loyalty payouts, and cash-flow ring-fencing, but the core cross-border treasury should remain on tier-1 Swiss banks to guarantee direct CHF clearing, foreign exchange pricing, and repo-style liquidity. A pure digital EMI opening without physical Swiss nexus is not acceptable for a medium-risk app revenue treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or domestic SIC payment in CHF from the shareholder's bank. The transaction narrative must contain the ISO 20022 purpose code appropriate to the inflow, typically 'DIMG' (Direct Investment - Foreign) for equity or 'COMC' for intra-group commercial funding, and must reference the executed shareholder resolution, articles of association or capital increase deed. The receiving Swiss bank requires a sworn source-of-funds declaration for the amount and will place a ring-fenced credit hold until the Commercial Register ('Handelsregisteramt') confirms the capital encumbrance release. For foreign-currency injections, the bank may require an intra-group treasury confirmation and a reason for the foreign currency; in all cases exchange-rate risk is contemporaneously hedged via FX forward.Outbound trade disbursements use SWIFT MT103 / PACS.008 for ordinary supplier and tax payments, MT202 for bank-to-bank reimbursements, and confirmed irrevocable commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) for high-value, documentary trade settlements. CHF-denominated instructions are cleared via SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing), EUR payments via EURO SIC, and USD through correspondent banks with UBS/OCBC or another major New-York clearing correspondent. The treasury beneficiary of a payment gateway must maintain segregated sub-accounts for (i) platform fees, (ii) content royalties, (iii) VAT / Swiss WHT settlements, (iv) payroll and (v) litigation reserves; each sub-ledger should have its own MT101 instruction batch and reference number to enable straight-through reconciliation.FX Forward (USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, GBP/CHF) for anticipated commodity and trade flowsFX Swap (combined spot and forward operation) to bridge between CHF funding and USD/EUR disbursement cyclesNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on a limited set of non-convertible emerging-market currencies, operating strictly on a cash-settlement basis under ISDA terms with a Swiss/G7 bank counterpartyCross-Currency Swap (CCS) to fix long-term USD or EUR revenue streams against CHF liabilities in the parental capital structureOTC European-Style FX Options (put/call) bought for hedging downside on large tender receivables; premium payment is limited and no leveraged speculative sale is permitted

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating is Medium as a supplemental treasury rail but Low as the primary clearing route. An EU/UK-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) cannot passport into Switzerland; it may offer a CHF settlement through a Swiss correspondent bank, yet such an arrangement does not grant direct SIC membership and fails the Swiss-payment prudential test. FINMA-registered Swiss fintech firms or SRO members can serve as secondary wallets for small user refunds, loyalty payouts, and cash-flow ring-fencing, but the core cross-border treasury should remain on tier-1 Swiss banks to guarantee direct CHF clearing, foreign exchange pricing, and repo-style liquidity. A pure digital EMI opening without physical Swiss nexus is not acceptable for a medium-risk app revenue treasury.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal direct tax 8.5% on net profit (effective 7.83% after deductibility of federal tax itself) plus cantonal and communal corporate income tax; combined all-in statutory headline rate for an AG in Canton Zug is approximately 14.6%, while the national cantonal blended range is approximately 12%–21% depending on the canton of residence.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% on any dividend, profit share, constructive distribution and economically equivalent distribution made by a Swiss AG, regardless of shareholder residence. In-kind distributions, including platform credits, virtual benefits or creditor waivers, are also within scope if capable of monetary valuation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due no later than six months after the company's fiscal year-end. For calendar-year taxpayers, the usual cantonal deadline is 31 March of the following year, with an administrative extension to 31 October commonly granted when a qualified representative is engaged. In Zug, the standard deadline for calendar-year accounts is 30 June unless an extension is filed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF; administrative practice applies materiality thresholds in CHF. A Master File is broadly expected where the consolidated group's revenue exceeds CHF 1 billion, and a Local File is expected where the Swiss entity's annual turnover exceeds CHF 50 million or where individual related-party transaction categories exceed CHF 1 million per annum. Exact thresholds follow Swiss Tax Conference guidance and FTA enforcement practice under OECD standards.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain offshore-style ESR codes. Instead, Swiss tax law and treaty practice impose an economic-substance requirement through beneficial ownership, actual place of management, substantive business purpose and profit-allocation standards under the OECD Guidelines and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. For a voice-streaming app, the Swiss AG must demonstrate genuine management, contracted operating personnel, product-specific decision-making, platform/system hosting in Switzerland and real administrative substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and intentional false reporting

Statutory sanction: Simple tax evasion is punishable by a penalty up to three times the evaded tax, plus interest on the underpayment. Aggravated tax fraud is a criminal tax offense and carries fines calculated from the evaded amount and, in serious cases, imprisonment for up to five years. Tax authorities may reopen prior years, disallow loss carryforwards and issue discretionary assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and shadow managers having actual control who sign or facilitate false returns are personally subject to evaded-tax penalties, civil restitution and criminal conviction. Travel bans, asset seizure, entry restrictions and imprisonment may be imposed against natural persons even if the corporation remains liable.
Shell company and abusive treaty structure

Statutory sanction: The FTA and cantons may disregard the corporate form, allocate income to the true economic owner, deny DTA exemption and treaty benefits, cancel protective rulings, reclassify transactions as constructive dividends and impose penalties up to three times the evaded tax. The commercial register authority may also order the company's liquidation if the required registered seat and actual administration are not maintained.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who lack authority, fail to carry out management functions or accept instructions from undisclosed principals are jointly and severally liable for tax debts. Foreign beneficial owners may be subjected to extradition-backed investigations, entry bans and personal civil judgments for the AG's non-payment of taxes.
Anti-money-laundering and payment-scope breach

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years in qualified cases, plus confiscation of assets. FINMA may issue enforcement orders, terminate unlawful payment activity, impose fines and prohibit individuals. Administrative fines of up to CHF 500,000 per person and corporate penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, money-laundering officers and beneficial owners are personally prosecutable. Arrests, travel bans, freezing of personal assets and imprisonment are possible. Jurisdiction over remotely located founders may be asserted through Swiss mutual legal assistance and international arrest requests.
Withholding tax failure on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Federal Tax Administration may enforce collection of the full 35% WHT from the AG, with late-payment interest at 5% per annum and administrative fines. If the transaction is recharacterized as a hidden distribution, the AG cannot recover the tax from the shareholder, and the tax liability becomes a direct corporate debt.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or failing to withhold are personally liable for the unpaid amount. Willful non-collection accompanied by false accounting constitutes tax fraud and subjects the responsible directors to criminal fines and imprisonment, including where ownership is held through foreign holding companies.
Transfer-pricing documentation and arm's-length violation

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax authorities may adjust income upward to an arm's-length position, deny deduction of the excessive part, reclassify the excess as a dividend subject to 35% WHT and issue a default assessment. Procedural fines up to CHF 10,000 apply; where contemporaneous documentation is absent, the taxpayer bears the burden and evidentiary losses.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign intercompany agreements with artificial prices are jointly and severally liable for resulting tax deficiencies, interest and penalties. Where a parent-company controller forces the Swiss entity into loss-making transfer prices, that individual can be treated as an accomplice to tax evasion and penalized personally, including by imprisonment.
