Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Canadian and Ontario law treats the Limited Partnership as fiscally transparent. The partnership is not an income tax taxpayer; each partner, including non-resident limited partners, is liable on the allocated share of Canadian-source business income. Relief may be available under relevant tax treaties.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election. The LP is conclusively flow-through. However, partners may elect under Income Tax Act s. 96(3) to allocate partnership income otherwise than in proportion to capital or contributed capital where the agreement is in writing. A non-resident partner may elect under s. 216 to compute Canadian tax on certain rental or business income on a net basis. For GST/HST purposes, an election under Excise Tax Act s. 273 is available to treat certain supplies between closely related registrants as deemed consideration for nil, provided all conditions are met.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar. There is no exchange-control approval, surrender, or licensing requirement for inbound or outbound remittances, including profit distributions, dividend payments, interest, royalties, intercompany loan servicing, or liquidation proceeds from a limited partnership. Repatriation of profits from an Ontario LP is governed by taxation rules, chiefly Part XIII non-resident withholding tax and Ontario limited partnership return obligations, rather than by currency controls. Transactions of C$10,000 or more in a single day, or deliberately structured equivalent transactions, must be reported to FINTRAC, and Canadian financial institutions apply heightened documentation standards for pharmaceutical-sector settlement flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. An Ontario-resident pharmaceutical exporter may retain foreign currency in a Canadian-dollar functional account or a Foreign Currency Account at a domestic Schedule I bank, use it directly for trade settlements, convert it at market rates, or hold it as working capital. The only binding obligations are FINTRAC large-transaction reporting, Statistics Canada trade data declarations, and the business-receipts documentation requirements of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); external administration of exchange controls is not centralized because Canada operates without a statutory foreign exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplementary treasury utility for small-value payments, corporate card issuance, and vendor disbursements. A non-resident fintech EMI or electronic-money institution should not be used for primary capital escrow, LC-backed trade settlement, or CRA/OSFI sensitive accounts because it lacks a deposit-taking bank charter, is not a member of Payments Canada, and its holdings are not protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or the Canadian Association of Payments Insurers. The operating treasury structure must remain anchored at a Canadian Schedule I bank, with the EMI used only as a controlled disbursement sub-ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be wired directly by the registered LP partners through their home-country clearing banks to the Ontario-based beneficiary account using SWIFT beneficiary identification, with the remitter name, account number, fund origin, and partnership capital-call notice aligned to the LP agreement. Canada does not issue a government currency-control purpose code for inward investment; the commercial purpose is evidenced by the capital-call subscription letter, the limited partnership agreement, a board or general-partner distribution resolution, and the partner's source-of-funds bank statement. For transfers exceeding C$10,000, the receiving Schedule I bank must complete a large EFT report to FINTRAC and may place a clearing hold until the partner supplies a sworn statement of lawful origin and proof of tax identification in the partner's jurisdiction. The entity should pre-position its CRS/FATCA status and Ontario Business Information Number to avoid delay.The primary clearing rail for supplier settlement is an international SWIFT MT103 payment for operational disbursements and raw-material spot purchases up to the bank's exposure threshold, with SWIFT GPI tracking. For significant crude-heparin supply contracts with new or cross-border counterparties, the LP must use a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Canadian Schedule I bank and advised through a correspondent or negotiating bank under UCP600, with a 60-to-120-day deferred-payment tenor matched to certification sampling and container delivery. CAD-denominated domestic and treasury payments train through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system or batch EFT; US-dollar payables clear through the Canadian bank's US-dollar clearing node or its US correspondent relationship. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent, so partners include allocable income in their own tax returns.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, ordinary profit distributions are capital distributions of previously taxed income and are not subject to dividend withholding; however, any Canadian-source rents, royalties, interest, or deemed dividends allocated to non-resident partners attract 25% withholding unless reduced by an income tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a statutory dollar threshold for maintaining transfer-pricing documentation; CRA requires contemporaneous documentation under ITA s.247(4) for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions. Form T106 must be filed when aggregate reportable transactions with any one non-resident exceed C$1,000,000 per taxation year. Country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of C$750,000,000 or more. Best practice is to prepare and retain Master File and Local File whenever the Canadian LP's cross-border raw-material purchases, management fees, or financing transactions exceed C$1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) to disregard transactions or structures that lack a bona fide business purpose and artificial economic substance. For a heparin fractionation LP to sustain treaty protection and avoid recharacterization as a taxable Canadian entity, the partnership must maintain real substance in Ontario: operational premises, GMP-grade fractionation equipment, laboratory staff, raw-material inventory, scientific protocols, local decision-making, and continuous manufacturing activity. A nominee general partner without operational autonomy is insufficient and invites GAAR reassessment, treaty-benefit denial, and possible reclassification of the partnership as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus interest; criminal conviction under ITA s.239 carries a fine of between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax, or imprisonment for up to two years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner who direct or acquiesce in false filings can be prosecuted under ITA s.242; the court may impose personal restitution, asset freezing, and travel bans; imprisonment up to five years is possible under Criminal Code fraud provisions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to disregard the LP structure, disallow treaty benefits, reassess within extended limitation periods, and impose interest, gross-negligence penalties, and other administrative sanctions; criminal conspiracy charges can follow.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the partnership veil where limited partners exercise control and the general partner is a mere puppet; directors-directors of the GP may be held personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes, restitution under Ontario partnership law, and may face travel restrictions pending criminal or extradition proceedings.
Money Laundering / AML Inadequacy

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties can reach C$500,000 per violation for entities; FINTRAC may suspend or revoke compliance registration; Criminal Code money-laundering sanctions include imprisonment for up to ten years and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer and the general partner's directors are personally liable for knowingly failing to file suspicious-transaction reports; civil restitution can attach to director assets; the court can impose travel bans, enforceable asset freezes, and extradition to jurisdictions with parallel laundering charges.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing & Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA can restore the arm's-length price under ITA s.247(2) and impose a s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment whenever contemporaneous documentation is absent; T106 late-filing penalties up to C$500 per month for up to 24 months also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation or participate in the evasion can be personally assessed for unremitted Part XIII withholding on intercompany royalties and interest; the same directors may be pursued under director-liability regimes for any penalty that the partnership cannot pay.
Non-Filing / Withholding Misuse

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties of C$25 per day up to C$2,500, plus automatic arrears interest; unreported T5013 slips invite CRA matching assessments and penalties under the information-return schedule; unremitted withholding taxes are collectable by the CRA as an assessed debt against both the partnership and the withholding agent.

Director personal liability: Under ITA s.227.1, directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the full amount of unremitted withholding taxes, plus interest and penalties; civil recovery may extend to foreign personal assets, and the court may issue a travel ban while criminal charges for wilful non-remittance are pending.
