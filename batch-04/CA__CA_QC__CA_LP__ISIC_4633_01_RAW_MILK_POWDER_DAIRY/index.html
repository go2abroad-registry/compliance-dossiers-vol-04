Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal GST/HST and Income Tax; Revenu Québec – Quebec QST and Provincial Tax; Federal portal: GST/HST NETFILE/My Business Account; Quebec portal: Revenu Québec CléRC

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must first apply for a federal GST/HST registration number via Form RC1, and for a Quebec QST registration via Revenu Québec Form LM-1-V, providing the partnership agreement, non-resident ownership details, Canadian business address or agent information, and anticipated Canadian taxable supplies; a Quebec Business Number (NEQ) may be required for provincial registration, and the CRA may issue a federal Business Number (BN) which is then coordinated with Revenu Québec.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must first apply for a federal GST/HST registration number via Form RC1, and for a Quebec QST registration via Revenu Québec Form LM-1-V, providing the partnership agreement, non-resident ownership details, Canadian business address or agent information, and anticipated Canadian taxable supplies; a Quebec Business Number (NEQ) may be required for provincial registration, and the CRA may issue a federal Business Number (BN) which is then coordinated with Revenu Québec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A limited partnership is generally a fiscally transparent pass-through entity for Canadian income tax purposes; no equivalent of a US 'check-the-box' election exists, but a Canadian partnership may elect under section 96 of the Income Tax Act to compute income at the partnership level only for administrative convenience; for GST/HST and QST, a qualifying partnership may elect under the 'partner as agent' rules (section 273 of the Excise Tax Act and equivalent QST rules) to have one partner act as the registrant on behalf of the partnership; hybrid mismatch rules under the BEPS Action 2 framework may require disclosure and adjustment where a foreign partner claims an exemption or deduction inconsistent with Canadian tax characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide annual taxable supplies for GST/HST registration; CAD 30,000 worldwide annual taxable supplies for QST registration with Quebec nexus; once registered, registration remains mandatory even if threshold no longer exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported, or services supplied to a non-resident in respect of exported goods, where the exporter maintains documentary evidence of exportation within the prescribed time frame; for GST/HST, exports are zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, and for QST under the Quebec Taxation Act; raw milk powder traded cross-border must be physically exported from Canada by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export, and the supplier must hold sufficient export evidence (e.g., customs export declaration, bill of lading, freight documents) to substantiate zero-rating.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / CERS or Form B13A)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean/Air/Truck) evidencing physical export
  • Customs brokerage entry confirmation and transit documents
  • Delivery confirmation from the foreign consignee or import customs authority
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from the foreign buyer (bank wire, SWIFT MT103, letter of credit settlement)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or national electronic invoicing mandate; CRA accepts electronic invoices and records in standard XML/EDI or PDF formats; Quebec requires that all invoices issued by a QST registrant contain specific prescribed disclosures and be retained electronically if issued electronically; no digital stamping or real-time API clearance is currently required; however, businesses must provide electronic invoices in readable format upon audit request, and Quebec has implemented mandatory electronic billing for certain public-sector suppliers, which is not generally applicable to dairy commodity exporters.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated shipments without sufficient export documentation; penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a penalty of up to CAD 250 for each failure to file an information return, and gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest; fraudulent export zero-rating claims can result in seizure of goods, fines of up to CAD 20,000 or double the tax evaded, and imprisonment under tax evasion provisions of the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act; retrospective clawbacks (reassessment and disallowance of input tax credits) may extend back to the date of registration, and Quebec QST assessments carry similar interest and penalty provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is a fully open capital-account jurisdiction. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital account; there is no exchange-control approval framework for dividend, royalty, loan, or partnership-profit remittance to non-residents, and no permit is required for cross-border transfers. The only operative constraints are bank-level KYC and FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, plus, for non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership, the notification and review requirements of the Investment Canada Act (ICA) where control of a Canadian business is acquired.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not mandate surrender, conversion ratios, or statutory repatriation timelines for dairy export receipts. Proceeds may be retained in USD, EUR, or CAD at the trader's option. Regulatory overlay: exports of whole/skimmed milk powder from Quebec are subject to Canadian Dairy Commission (CDC) supply-management oversight, Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) export certification, and, where applicable, Customs Tariff quota administration for the U.S. market (CUSMA/USMCA); these affect documentation but not FX control.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and fiscal agent; the Minister of Finance / Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account and foreign-exchange intervention policy under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52); OSFI supervises Schedule I banks and FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services businesses (e.g., Wise Business, OFX) and retail-payment licensees under the Retail Payment Activities Act can be onboarded remotely and used for low-value collections, but they cannot issue or discount dairy trade Letters of Credit, access LVTS high-value CAD clearings, or serve as the LP's statutory settlement account for Quebec Revenue and REQ bookkeeping. Offshore unregulated EMIs are legally disfavored for the primary treasury account due to substantiation risk on EFT reporting and trader verification; they may be configured only as a secondary collection rail with daily sweep settlement to a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner subscriptions are routed via SWIFT international transfer (or Fedwire/CHIPS in USD through the bank's U.S. correspondent) into the LP's CAD or USD account. Canada has no statutory SWIFT purpose-code registry, but the bank's compliance system requires the narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [LP NAME]' referencing the LP's REQ number, the capital-call identifier, and the LP agreement; the instruction letter from the paying entity must include the bank's BIC/ABA and account number. Transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are auto-reported to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Where the injection gives a non-Canadian partner control of the Quebec business, an ICA notification or application must be concluded before funds are cleared; same-day USD/CAD conversion is executed under a standing treasury mandate at the bank's dealing desk.Trade disbursements for whole/skimmed milk powder use: (1) SWIFT MT103 (TT) wires for low-value or open-account settlement; (2) commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT798, confirmed or unconfirmed) for supplier payment against presentation of Bill of Lading, invoice, CFIA health certificate, and weight/quality certificates; (3) domestic CAD high-value wires via LVTS (Payments Canada) for Canadian clearing, including GST/QST settlements; (4) USD correspondent clearing (CHIPS/Fedwire) through the Canadian bank's New York agent; and (5) documentary collections (DP/DA) as a secondary rail.
