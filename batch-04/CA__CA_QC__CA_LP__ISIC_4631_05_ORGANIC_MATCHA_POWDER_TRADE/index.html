Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and income tax administration (portal: CRA Business Registration Online, www.canada.ca); Revenu Quebec (RQ) - Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial tax administration (portal: RQ CLIC, www.revenuquebec.ca); Registraire des entreprises du Quebec - mandatory extra-provincial partnership registration (portal: www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File a declaration with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec within 60 days of starting activities to obtain the NEQ enterprise number. Step 2: Apply via CRA Business Registration Online (or Form RC1) for the 9-digit Business Number and GST/HST program account; a limited partnership is a deemed registrant person under ETA s. 272.1 despite income-tax transparency. Step 3: Register for QST with Revenu Quebec via CLIC or Form LM-101-V; registration is compulsory where the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded, and a non-resident LP must appoint a representative for service (ETA s. 268). Step 4: File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and Quebec RL-15, allocate income to partners, and meet any Part XIII/Part I withholding obligations regarding non-resident partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian LPs are income-tax transparent by default (ITA s. 96) but are separate notional registrants for GST/HST/QST. Material elections: (i) ETA s. 272.1(5) election to treat the partnership or the partners as the taxable person for input tax credit purposes; (ii) drop-shipment zero-rating election for matcha delivered in Canada to a non-resident recipient for export (ETA s. 179); (iii) fiscal-period and late-filing elections (ITA s. 96(13), s. 220(3)); (iv) hybrid-mismatch election and ordering rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.8. Canada has no US-style check-the-box entity classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (small-supplier limit; ETA s. 148(1) and QSTA equivalent): worldwide taxable supplies of C$30,000 or less in a calendar quarter or in the four preceding calendar quarters; the matcha wholesale distribution operation will exceed the threshold and must register for both GST/HST and QST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) organic matcha powder physically exported by the LP as a registrant supplier (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1; mirroring QST Act rules), (ii) supplies of goods delivered in Canada to a non-resident recipient for export (drop-shipment, ETA s. 179), and (iii) services performed and supplied wholly outside Canada. Goods must be exported as soon as reasonable and generally within 60 days after ownership transfer; contemporaneous shipping, customs, and payment evidence must be retained. Plain matcha sold domestically may separately qualify as zero-rated basic groceries under Schedule VI, Part III.

  • Customs Export Declaration (G11 / Canadian Automated Export Declaration; mandatory above C$2,000 or for controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Cargo Control Document / customs carrier entry
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-currency inward payment confirmation matched to the invoice
  • USMCA/CUSMA Certificate of Origin (recommended for US-destined matcha to obtain preferential US duty treatment)

None: Canada and Quebec do not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for B2B supplies. Voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian B2B E-Invoicing Framework (2024) is optional, and conventional invoices are not rejected. Invoices must still satisfy ETA ss. 223-224 and QST invoice-content rules (vendor identity, BN, QST registration number, date, description, tax amounts), with six-year record retention.

CRA and Revenu Quebec jointly audit export zero-rating claims; missing documentation triggers reversal of the zero-rating and reassessment of the full 14.975% combined GST/QST plus statutory daily compound interest. Penalties: false-statement penalty of 25% of the improperly claimed refund (ETA s. 285); gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax evaded; the normal 4-year reassessment window extends to 12 years for misrepresentation and is unlimited for fraud. General partners and directors face joint and several liability for unremitted net tax (ETA s. 323; Quebec equivalent), and fraudulent refund schemes may attract criminal prosecution. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day treasury ceiling is an audit-tested internal control for arm's length settlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a fully convertible currency with no exchange-control restrictions on either current or capital account transactions. The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act impose no prior approval, repatriation, or surrender obligations for CAD or foreign currency. Profit remittance from a Quebec Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners is not FX-restricted, but may attract Canadian non-resident withholding tax (Income Tax Act Part XIII) on distributions that are taxable Canadian-source income, with treaty rate reductions available; CRS/FATCA reporting and NR4/T5013 schedules are required. Foreign currency accounts may be held freely; the Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rate is the standard conversion benchmark for Canadian-dollar functional-currency accounting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender regime exists in Canada. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no fixed statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at any Canadian Schedule I bank. Compliance obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, the Canada Revenue Agency functional-currency rules, and supporting documentation for the Canada Border Services Agency and the Canadian Organic Regime if product-level claims are advertised.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervisors: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) for Quebec payment services; exchange intervention authority rests with the federal Minister of Finance assisted by the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury custody and capital escrow; Medium as an auxiliary collection and payout rail. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU electronic money institutions) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act. Quebec's Payment Services Act and the AMF licensing regime require a Quebec payment service provider licence for money-transfer activities. An unlicensed offshore EMI must not hold the LP's permanent operating balances; it may only serve as a supplementary FX/collection conduit, subject to FATF-compliant originators and bank-backed settlement into the Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions arrive by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated operational or escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, or domestically through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) where the remitting partner holds a Canadian account. The remitter name must exactly match the limited partner's KYC records; field 70 must state the capital contribution reference and the LP agreement clause; field 50K/56A/57A must be pre-validated to avoid rejection. For any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, the receiving bank automatically files an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (eFTR) with FINTRAC; for CAD 100,000 or more, the bank may require a documented source-of-funds declaration, including the remitter's bank statement and the GP's capital call notice. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not subject to federal or Quebec corporate income tax on allocated partnership income. Tax is imposed at partner level. For an incorporated partner, the federal general corporate rate is 15% and the Quebec general corporate rate is 11.5%, producing a combined general rate of 26.5%. The federal small-business rate is 9% and the Quebec small-business rate is generally 3.2%, subject to eligibility and business-limit rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A limited partnership distribution is generally not a dividend and is not automatically subject to dividend withholding tax. A Canadian corporation paying a dividend to a non-resident is generally subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax, reduced under an applicable income tax treaty, commonly to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on recipient ownership, beneficial ownership, and treaty conditions. A partnership must separately analyze withholding on allocated income and payments to non-resident partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not provide a universal transaction-value threshold that eliminates the statutory requirement to maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. Form T106 reporting is generally triggered for aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1,000,000, subject to statutory exclusions and filing rules. A Canadian taxpayer must maintain records sufficient to support arm's-length pricing, and CRA may request contemporaneous documentation. Canada generally does not impose a separate mandatory OECD-style Master File filing requirement equivalent to some jurisdictions, although a Master File may be requested or be prudent for multinational groups. Quebec generally relies on the federal transfer-pricing framework.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the United Arab Emirates or certain other jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant through the Income Tax Act, the transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty principal-purpose and beneficial-ownership concepts, sham and agency doctrines, partnership allocation rules, debt characterization, and common-law anti-avoidance principles. The partnership should maintain real procurement and distribution functions, commercially justified pricing, inventory and logistics records, decision-making evidence, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, banking records, and documented risk control. A non-resident partner or related supplier lacking substantive functions may face recharacterization, denied deductions, treaty relief denial, or transfer-pricing adjustments.
