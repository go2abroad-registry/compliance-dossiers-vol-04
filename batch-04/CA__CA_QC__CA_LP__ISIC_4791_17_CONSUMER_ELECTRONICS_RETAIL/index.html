Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Canada qualify for zero-rating of GST/HST and QST if the goods are exported and proper proof is provided to the tax authority.

  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping document
  • Proof of export (e.g., customs clearance if applicable)
  • Evidence of payment to non-resident customer

GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices for taxable supplies. No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required under current Canadian law, but registrants may be subject to audit and digital reporting initiatives.

Fraudulent claims for zero-rating may result in penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including fines of up to $10,000 per false statement, interest charges, and potential criminal prosecution. Tax may be clawed back with applicable interest if zero-rating is disallowed upon audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada's legal tender is the Canadian dollar; the currency is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada does not maintain exchange control legislation requiring central bank approval for cross-border fund transfers, trade payments, profit distributions, or capital repatriation. Non-resident partners of a Quebec limited partnership may freely receive their distributive share of operating profits and capital distributions, subject to applicable Canadian withholding taxes (e.g., Part XIII tax on passive income such as interest or certain dividends) and to information reporting filings such as T5013 slips and the section 116 tax clearance certificate for dispositions of Canadian taxable property. No foreign-exchange surrender or conversion certificate is required by the federal or Quebec authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion timeline, or mandatory percentage for export proceeds. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in a Canadian bank account, converted at will, or used directly for cross-border trade settlements. However, every exchange transaction of CAD 10,000 or more (or equivalent) must be reported to FINTRAC in accordance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; unusual or suspicious patterns in export-related sales flows may give rise to a mandatory Suspicious Transaction Report. Export proceeds are taxable as business income to the limited partnership and must be accounted for under Canadian GAAP in CAD equivalents.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential oversight and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transactional AML/ATF reporting.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the Limited Partnership as a fiscally transparent pass-through entity. Partners pay tax at applicable progressive marginal rates; a partner that is a Canadian Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) faces a combined Federal+Quebec effective rate of approximately 12.2% on active business income up to CAD 500,000 (Federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%), and a general corporate partner faces approximately 26.5% (Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). A non-resident corporate partner is liable for Part I tax on its share and may be subject to branch tax under Part XIV.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at the LP level because the LP is fiscally transparent and cannot pay dividends. A cash distribution to a non-resident partner is not a dividend; it is simply a return of allocated earnings already taxed under Part I (or Part XIV for a non-resident corporation). Where a non-resident corporate partner eventually pays a dividend out of the LP's distributed earnings, the ordinary Part XIII rate of 25% applies, subject to an applicable tax treaty (reduced to 15% for US residents; 5% if the US shareholder corporation owns at least 10% of the voting shares).

Schedule D – Information on Corporate Partners Identification of all corporate partners, including their Quebec tax account numbers and effective percentage of partnership interest. Foreign corporate partners must provide their home jurisdiction registration number and a written confirmation that they are not resident or deemed resident in Canada; treaty claims require Form TP-1016-REL. Quebec: Under the Taxation Act and Tax Administration Act, late filing penalty for failure to transmit TP-920 is CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; a partner may be assessed a 10% late-filing penalty plus interest on any unpaid Quebec tax from the statutory due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's length transactions in a taxation year triggers mandatory filing of T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents). There is no statutory threshold for contemporaneous documentation, but CRA's administrative practice expects a Local File and, for transactions exceeding CAD 1 million, a Master File when the MNE group has total revenues above CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulation' omnibus like the UAE, but the doctrine is imposed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the 'de facto and de jure' control tests. A Quebec LP used solely as a tax-avoidance vehicle (e.g., parking e-commerce inventory and routing profits through a shell affiliate) may be disregarded as a sham, and the CRA/Revenu Quebec will recharacterize the arrangement. The LP must have: (a) a real physical establishment in Quebec; (b) employees or contractors conducting the retail operations; (c) commercial substance in the purchase and sale of consumer electronics; and (d) business purpose independent of tax reduction. For limited partners, the relationship must not be a disguised financing arrangement; otherwise the partner's share may be reclassified as interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (deceitful filing)

Statutory sanction: Federal ITA s.163(2) penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; on conviction, imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax. Quebec's Tax Administration Act s.57 imposes a similar 50% civil penalty and a criminal fine up to 200%. Criminal records are published on CRA's 'Named Persons' list.

Director personal liability: The general partner (or managing partner) is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts, including taxes, penalties, and interest under s.18.2 of the Tax Administration Act (Quebec). Officials who signed the false return personally may be subject to a civil penalty of CAD 1,000 to CAD 25,000, a travel ban for continued non-payment, and, in case of conviction for tax evasion, a prison sentence of up to 5 years.
Sham Arrangement / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR (ITA s.245) to disregard the LP for tax purposes, reassess the partner's income on the basis of the 'real' taxpayer, and apply a 50% penalty under s.163(2) for false statement. Under Quebec, s.1079.1 of the Taxation Act mirrors GAAR and permits Revenu Quebec to add 10% of the amount of the avoidance benefit as a penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers are liable for the full tax amount (including interest) if they authorized the sham, and may be assessed personally under s.227.1 of the ITA for unremitted amounts. Travel bans may be issued under the Tax Collection Act against directors who refuse to pay; a director may also be sued for breach of fiduciary duty to third-party creditors.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), a transfer-pricing adjustment may be imposed at the higher of the arm's-length price; a separate penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (up to a maximum of CAD 1,000,000 for a partnership) applies if the taxpayer did not use reasonable efforts to quantify the correct price. CRA may also apply the s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty if the mispricing arises from intentional disregard.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner may be jointly and severally liable for the 10% penalty if they signed the T106 or T5013 with knowledge of the mispricing. The general partner's personal assets are exposed for the partnership's unpaid penalty; for a non-resident director, a failure to attend a CRA investigation may lead to a bench warrant and travel restriction.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty (AMP) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to CAD 1 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals, plus a penalty of up to CAD 100,000 for each day of non-compliance. Criminal penalties may include imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate partner or of the general partner may be prosecuted if they directed, authorized, participated in, or failed to reasonably prevent the AML breach. Directors may be personally fined up to CAD 500,000, subjected to a compliance order, or, upon conviction, to imprisonment of up to 5 years. The CRA may also cross-reference FINTRAC's data with the T5013 to trigger an income tax audit.
