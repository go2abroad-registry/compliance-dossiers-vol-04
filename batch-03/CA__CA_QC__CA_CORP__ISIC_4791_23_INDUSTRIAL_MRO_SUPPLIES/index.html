Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital applies under the QBCA; authorized capital may consist of shares without par value issued for cash, property, services or promissory notes (s. 46 QBCA). No escrow account is required at incorporation; the full subscription price is reflected in the share register.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory fire safety inspection certificate issued by the municipal fire safety department (Service de securite incendie) before opening; building and racking designed to the Code de construction du Quebec and NFPA 13 sprinkler standards where required; CNESST employer registration, first-aid kit, emergency response plan, and workplace clearance certificate for the warehouse; security plan, WHMIS pictograms, and SDS binders for any controlled MRO products.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital applies under the QBCA; authorized capital may consist of shares without par value issued for cash, property, services or promissory notes (s. 46 QBCA). No escrow account is required at incorporation; the full subscription price is reflected in the share register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and federal income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec provincial corporate income tax, and employer source deductions; Registraire d'entreprises du Québec for provincial business registration. Primary online portals: CRA Business Registration Online, CRA My Business Account, and Revenu Québec Mon dossier d'entreprise.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor typically forms a Canadian corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act. The corporation then obtains a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online service, providing articles of incorporation, registered office address, director information, and board authorization. GST/HST program account (RT), payroll, and other program accounts are activated in the same process. A separate Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) is issued by the Registraire d'entreprises du Québec, and the corporation must register for QST and provincial payroll accounts through the Revenu Québec online registration service. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Canada may apply for a simplified non-resident registration using CRA Form RC4591 and, for Quebec, the equivalent Revenu Québec registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable legal entity. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Resident Canadian corporations are subject to Canadian federal and provincial income tax on worldwide income. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source income and to Part I Part XII.1 branch tax where applicable. No tax-exempt zone applies. A small supplier may be exempt from mandatory registration, but this is a registration threshold, not an entity-level tax exemption.

Available election forms

No check-the-box tax classification election exists under Canadian tax law. Corporate tax status follows the entity's legal form. Available elections include the section 85(1)/(2) asset rollover election for incorporation or business restructuring; the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status election; the GST/HST Quick Method election for small suppliers; the annual GST/HST filing election for non-resident registrants; the closely related corporation election to relieve certain GST/HST obligations; the section 216(4) election for non-residents to compute Canadian rental income on a net basis; and corresponding Quebec tax elections administered by Revenu Québec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. Under both the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) and Quebec's QST Act, a person is a small supplier unless the total value of its worldwide taxable supplies from Canada and elsewhere exceeds CAD 30,000 in a particular calendar quarter and in the four preceding calendar quarters. Non-resident vendors making taxable supplies of digital services or intangible property to Canadian consumers may be required to register if the threshold is met or exceeded. Quebec applies substantially the same CAD 30,000 threshold for QST registration. There is no monetary threshold for a non-resident that carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to GST/HST and QST when goods are exported from Canada and ownership in the goods passes to a non-resident buyer, provided the goods are exported as part of the supply, or are delivered to a common carrier for export, or otherwise removed from Canada in accordance with Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding Quebec QST rules. Services are zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident and the service is performed wholly outside Canada, subject to exceptions for services relating to real property in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada, or services performed in Canada that are not expressly listed as zero-rated. The supplier must retain documentary evidence of export; without that evidence the supply becomes taxable at the standard rates, and the supplier must account for GST/HST and QST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or Canada Customs coding form)
  • Bill of Lading (original transport document or equivalent ocean/air/rail/road waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 proof of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods
  • Cargo control document or carrier-issued export evidence
  • Customs broker exporter summary or freight forwarder invoice
  • Proof of foreign destination and delivery, where applicable

Canada and Quebec do not currently operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government digital stamping, and no API pre-clearance requirement for standard B2B invoices. Suppliers must issue invoices containing the prescribed statutory information: supplier and recipient identities, the supplier's GST/HST registration number and Quebec QST registration number where applicable, invoice date, description of goods/services, VAT/GST/QST amounts, rates, and total amount. Electronic invoices in PDF, EDI, or other machine-readable formats are accepted, but the issuer must retain an auditable copy in Canada for at least six years. Quebec has been advancing mandatory electronic invoicing in phases for established large enterprises, but as of the current stage no QR-code or real-time fiscal approval requirement applies to an industrial MRO electronic-catalog seller.

The CRA and Revenu Québec actively verify zero-rated export claims, especially for high-volume e-commerce sellers. If the exporter cannot produce satisfactory written evidence of export and payment, the tax authority will reassess the supply as taxable, disallow the zero-rating, and assess unreported GST/HST and QST plus statutory interest. For fraudulent or grossly negligent claims, the Excise Tax Act authorizes a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the net amount of tax sought to be evaded, and Revenu Québec imposes a comparable civil penalty. Criminal tax evasion is also prosecutable. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended to eight years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Refund offsets and criminal fines may apply in the most serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free convertibility regime for current account transactions pursuant to the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions are convertible subject to reporting obligations to Statistics Canada. Profit remittances by Canadian corporations are unrestricted for most sectors, subject only to tax withholding, FINTRAC compliance, and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank is required. Canadian exporters must file export declarations with Statistics Canada through the Export Reporting System within 45 days of shipment. Proceeds may be retained offshore or repatriated freely without central bank conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

High feasibility for cross-border digital payment platforms and electronic money instruments via licensed providers such as Wise Business or Revolut Business. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration for money services businesses and OSFI oversight for any EMI activities; offshore digital EMIs require full compliance with Canadian AML/KYC standards and may face remittance restrictions.
