Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administrator and GST/HST authority for harmonized Ontario HST; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (Business Registration Online, My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE). Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) for provincial HST harmonization oversight; ServiceOntario/Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry) for Limited Partnerships Act registrations, annual returns, and agent-for-service filings.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors forming an Ontario Limited Partnership (CA_LP) must: (1) file the limited partnership declaration (Form 1LP) with the Ontario Business Registry — no minimum capital is prescribed and non-resident limited partners are permitted, but a resident attorney or agent for service may be required where no Ontario-resident general partner exists; (2) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or through Business Registration Online; (3) activate a GST/HST program account (BN suffix RT) upon exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in Canada, or voluntarily to recover input tax credits; (4) register the exporter/importer identifier with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) using the same BN with an IM program account and enroll in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for B13A export declarations; (5) file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return within five months of fiscal year-end and issue T5013 slips to partners; (6) ensure non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada obtain a tax account and file Part I returns for their allocated share of Canadian-source income; the LP should also verify whether partner property dispositions constitute 'taxable Canadian property' requiring CRA section 116 certificates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Ontario tax purposes; partnership income is computed at the LP level under ITA section 96 but taxed to each partner (resident and non-resident) on their allocated share. Because non-resident partners are contemplated, the LP will generally not qualify as a 'Canadian partnership' under ITA section 102, which limits rollover elections while preserving flow-through treatment for business income (ITA s. 2(3) for non-residents). The LP itself files an information return only, not an income tax return.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate an IRS-style check-the-box entity classification election; tax status follows legal form and provincial partnership law. Relevant statutory elections and mechanics include: (i) ITA s. 96 partnership allocation computations and the s. 97(2) joint rollover election on contributions of property, available only where the partnership is a 'Canadian partnership' under ITA s. 102 (all members resident in Canada) — unavailable to this cross-border LP unless structured accordingly; (ii) ITA s. 216(1) net-basis election for non-resident partners deriving Canadian rental or timber income; (iii) GST/HST nil-consideration and fair-value elections among closely related registrants for group supply planning; (iv) joint capital cost allowance elections to transfer undepreciated capital cost between partners and the partnership. The OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 et seq.) apply automatically and are not elective; no election exists to re-characterize a transparent LP into a taxable entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current account and capital account. No exchange-control approvals, central-bank licensing, prior remittance permits, or compulsory conversion rules apply to capital injections, trade settlements, profit repatriation, partner distributions, or royalties. Canada complies with the OECD Codes of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and the CUSMA (USMCA) investment disciplines. The principal gatekeeping instrument is the Investment Canada Act (ICA): a WTO investor, including a U.S. limited partner, establishing a new Canadian business (the Ontario LP's wholesale operation) must file a single online ICA Notification within 30 days after implementation; the operation is exempt from 'net benefit' review because it is not a cultural business. Fiscal hurdles are limited to (i) Part I taxation of the LP's Canadian-source business income passed through to non-resident partners under Income Tax Act (ITA) ss. 96 and 115(1)(a), with distributions of that already-taxed income not re-subjected to withholding, and (ii) Part XIII withholding at 25% statutory on certain LP payments to non-resident partners (interest, management/administration fees, guarantee fees) under ITA s. 212, generally reduced to 15% for arm's-length interest paid to U.S. residents under Article XI(2) of the Canada–US Tax Treaty (1980, as amended through the 5th Protocol). There are no invoicing-currency restrictions, no import/export price controls, no minimum holding periods on proceeds, and no obstacles to remitting liquidation-sale proceeds to non-resident partners other than standard tax reporting on T5013 and NR4 information returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. The LP may retain USD, EUR, or CNY proceeds indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts maintained with Schedule I banks; conversion to CAD is a purely commercial decision. Books and records must be kept and financial statements reported in CAD, or, upon an election under ITA s. 261, in an eligible functional currency (including USD) available to qualifying partnerships carrying on business in Canada, on a five-year renewable term. Foreign-exchange gains and losses on capital account are recognized under ITA s. 39(1) and allocated to partners; income-account FX differences are ordinary income or loss. The CBSA export filing (via Canada's single-window portal and the G13/EI reporting regime) records that goods have left Canada, but no exchange-control document is required alongside the customs entry.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central monetary and settlement authority operating the Lynx high-value payment system and the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration function for non-bank payment service providers. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency analogous to a SAFE-style exchange-control bureau: exchange controls were suspended in 1951 and no Foreign Exchange Control Act remains in force. Legal and prudential responsibilities are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada (monetary policy interface, Investment Canada Act administration, and tax treaties), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential supervision of Schedule I–III banks), FINTRAC (AML/ATF and cross-border currency-transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act), and Payments Canada (clearing and settlement). The Currency Act establishes the Canadian dollar (CAD) as legal tender; the Bank of Canada publishes the daily 12:00 ET official exchange rate used for tax and financial-reporting conversion.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate applicable to corporate partners (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Ontario corporate income tax). The Ontario limited partnership itself is a pass-through entity; no corporate income tax is assessed at the LP level. Non-resident corporate partners carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment are also subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% on after-tax income not reinvested (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% under Canada-US DTA, where eligible).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source dividends paid to a non-resident beneficial owner under section 212(2) of the Income Tax Act. For the Ontario LP, distributions to non-resident partners are not typically dividends but partnership distributions of business income; those allocations are subject to Part I / Part XIV branch tax rather than Part XIII. If the LP earns passive income (interest, royalties, rents), Part XIII withholding may apply to the non-resident partner's share.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no monetary de minimis threshold for transfer-pricing documentation. A contemporaneous transfer-pricing file (equivalent to Local File) is expected for every non-arm's-length cross-border transaction, regardless of amount, where the transaction is material in the context of the taxpayer's business. Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting is mandatory under Canadian rules for multinational groups with consolidated annual group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000. For a mid-sized off-price wholesale LP, every related-party inventory purchase must be documented under section 247(4); the absence of any transaction-level exemption is a common audit issue.
