A pure mailbox or virtual office is not acceptable as the sole registered office (Sitz) of an operating Aktiengesellschaft engaged in TIR trucking. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under Swiss law. AG is subject to statutory corporate income tax at federal and cantonal/municipal levels. Optional elections include: federal and cantonal participation exemption for substantial dividends and capital gains; holding company or mixed company status at cantonal level; VAT fiscal unity (tax group) election for 100% affiliates; and group allocation of profits under the cantonal tax law. Hybrid mismatch rules follow OECD BEPS recommendations in Swiss tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover for universal VAT liability; foreign suppliers of intangible services and e-services are subject to the same threshold on Swiss-derived turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of goods is zero-rated where the goods physically leave Swiss territory (or go into a customs warehouse or free zone), and the supplier substantiates the export with customs and transport documents. Cross-border transport services (including trucking under TIR) are zero-rated when they are directly addressed to export goods, if the recipient is a taxable person abroad, or when the place of supply is outside Switzerland in accordance with the OECD place-of-consumption rules (Art. 23e Swiss VAT Act). The zero rate also applies to logistics, handling, and ancillary services that are part of the export supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (file: A/ERPDS via e-dec)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • TIR Carnet (international customs transit document)
  • Signed CMR consignment note

Switzerland does not mandate universal e-invoicing. However, the circulation of standardized e-invoices (eBill/EX, ZUGFeRD 2.7) is recognized for VAT deduction. All customs transactions, including TIR transit and export declarations, must be transmitted electronically via the e-dec / e-vits (NCTS) system. Real-time fiscal clearance is not required, but VAT payer data must be reported electronically under the e-SAFT framework upon request.

Substantiation documents must be retained for 10 years. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient proof at the time of the FTA audit, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 8.1% with default interest and penalties of 50% to 300% of the evaded VAT. Intentional evasion triggers criminal fines up to CHF 250,000 and imprisonment terms under Swiss VAT Act Art. 91; fraudulent TIR transactions additionally are referred to the Federal Office of Customs and Border Security and to the jurisdiction of the criminal courts.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised currency regime: the Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible on both current and capital account, with no foreign exchange control law, no central bank approval requirement for cross-border payments, and no restrictions on inbound capital, profit repatriation, or dividend remittance to non-residents. Legal tender status of CHF is defined under the CPIA, but payment in foreign currency is contractually permissible. The sole practical obstacle to remittance of distributable profits is the 35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends and similar distributions, which is refundable upon recipient tax residency disclosure or reducible to the applicable double tax treaty rate; no additional cantonal or federal FX or transfer permit is required. For a TIR bond-carrying trucking AG, fuel and toll expenses incurred in EUR and other currencies are freely payable; hedge accounting and foreign-currency bank balances are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation requirement on export proceeds, no mandatory conversion into CHF, and no time limit for the holding of foreign currency receipts. Exporters operating under TIR carnets may retain EUR, USD, or CHF balances in domestic or foreign banks, apply them directly to outbound trade payments, or convert at market rates. Switzerland's only residual control is the obligation to truthfully declare cross-border cash movements of CHF 10,000 or more to customs authorities pursuant to the Money Laundering Act, which applies to physical cash, not wire transfers.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera, Berne and Zurich; jointly with the Swiss Federal Department of Finance (FDF) administering the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) and banking ordinances. The SNB is the sole issuer of banknotes and operator of the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury, Medium for ancillary rails. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are not regulated by FINMA and cannot hold statutory capital escrow, issue Swiss IBAN accounts, or provide SIC access; they operate only under acquiring or partner-bank arrangements and are acceptable solely as supplementary fuel-card or wage-distribution channels. Domestic Swiss FinTech licensees under the Banking Act (FinTech licence, public deposits up to CHF 100 million without interest or securities business) and licensed payment service providers may provide operational accounts, but a full banking relationship with a SIC participant remains mandatory for a TIR trucking AG's primary settlement and commercial credit needs. Use of any non-FINMA-regulated EMI for statutory shareholder capital injection would be rejected by the Swiss Commercial Register office and notaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing of the minimum registered capital (CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 fully paid at formation) is executed by domestic SWIFT MT103 payment to a Swiss SIC-participant bank's blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) designated for capital formation. The remittance must reference the company's Commercial Register file number and be accompanied by an Einzahlungsschein (payment certificate) naming each subscribing shareholder and the precise CHF amount. Banks require the payer (ultimate beneficial owner) to match the notarised subscription declaration; third-party payments are prohibited. For post-formation inbound share premium, shareholder loans, or intercompany advances, use SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose codes 'DETI' (equity injection) or 'DISA' (direct investment in non-financial enterprise); after receipt, present the bank's confirmation to the notary and the Commercial Register office. The bank must verify no cash, no commingled funds, and a clear audit trail from the shareholder's bank account; escrow release occurs only upon registration of the AG. For funds arriving in EUR or USD, conversion to CHF is executed at the bank's published spot rate; no SNB approval is required.Swiss treasury clearing rails for a TIR trucking AG comprise: (i) domestic CHF disbursements via SIC instant or batch clearing with ISO 20022 pain.001 messages; (ii) euro-denominated settlements via SEPA Credit Transfer from a Swiss SEPA participant bank, using the same IBAN/BIC corridor; (iii) non-SEPA cross-border trade payments, fuel-card top-ups, and subcontractor settlements via SWIFT MT103 with full purpose code and supporting customs/TIR references; and (iv) documentary trade instruments for fleet and capital equipment purchases through irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by MT700 to the beneficiary's bank, frequently on 90-day usance terms. TIR carnets themselves are discharged through the guaranteeing association rather than the clearing system; LSVA tolls are debited directly via SEPA direct debit or fuel-card networks. CHF/EUR conversion for euro corridor expenditures is consolidated through standing FX conversion orders to minimise spread. All outbound disbursements above CHF 15,000 require stated purpose codes and, where triggered, underlying invoice/contract storage to satisfy Swiss bank re-verification duties under the AMLA; payments to subcontractors abroad must be accompanied by properly executed consignment and customs records to avoid unjust enrichment claims and maintain bonded cargo compliance.Deliverable OTC FX forwards in CHF/EUR and CHF/USD for up to 12 months, commonly executed under a bank master FX agreement without ISDAFX swaps (swap contre change) for simultaneous spot-forward restructuring of EUR and CHF liquidity, permitted for cash-flow matching of fuel, toll, and payroll cyclesCross-currency swaps (currency interest rate swaps) under ISDA/CSA for long-term financing alignment on asset-backed fleet loansNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on currencies without SNB-issued freely deliverable CHR reference, but only with a FINMA-regulated counterparty and strictly for hedging genuine TIR corridor operating exposure (e.g., RUB, TRY, KZT)European-style EUR/USD futures listed on NYSE Euronext or Eurex for anticipatory hedging of intermodal revenue, settled in cashFX options (plain vanilla call/put) for asymmetric protection on large capital expenditures, permissible without SNB notification; speculative option writing on the corporate account is prohibited by Swiss banking practice and would breach the company's treasury policy

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% statutory Swiss federal corporate income tax rate (effective federal rate approx. 7.83% after deduction of the federal tax itself); combined federal + cantonal + communal ordinary AG burden in Zug approx. 11.5%–11.9%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends paid out of Swiss movable capital; no additional cantonal/communal withholding tax. The 35% withholding is refunded to Swiss resident shareholders and reduced under DTA for non-residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Cantonal filing notice normally requires submission within 6 months after the fiscal year-end (for a 31 December year-end: by 30 June of the following year); the Federal Tax Administration may set an independent federal deadline; extensions up to 12 months are obtainable on reasoned application, particularly for cross-border groups; a final default assessment (Ermessensveranlagung) may be issued if the return is not filed within the granted term

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 10,000,000 (annual aggregate of cross-border intercompany transactions) is the de facto threshold derived from Swiss Federal Tax Administration audit practice requiring contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File); intra-group service charges exceeding CHF 2,000,000 trigger functional and cost-benefit analysis documentation; a Master File should be prepared for MNE groups with consolidated turnover above CHF 1,000,000,000, aligning with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Switzerland imposes no statutory TP filing obligation but documentation must be submitted within 60 days of tax-administration demand; burden of proof for arm's-length compliance shifts on failure to provide documentation.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland enforces economic substance through (i) substantive anti-abuse doctrine under Article 6 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax allowing disregardia of artificial arrangements; (ii) the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) minimum standards, including the Principal Purpose Test in all Swiss DTAs concluded or modified after 2024; (iii) the Swiss STAF 2020 requirement that treaty benefits, participation relief, and patent box apply only to an enterprise with genuine economic activity, own premises, qualified employees and business decision-making in Switzerland; and (iv) the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) anti-abuse rules. A Swiss AG acting as a pure invoicing or letterbox vehicle for TIR foreign transport operations without Swiss operational activity will be reassessed as having a permanent establishment abroad or a permanent establishment in Switzerland and the nominal Swiss entity will be denied treaty benefits and may be liquidated for non-compliance with the TIR licensing requirement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine under Art. 175 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax up to the tax evaded; in aggravated cases (Art. 176, e.g., repeated evasion or false documents of substantial scale) a fine up to three times the evaded tax; VAT evasion under the Swiss VAT Act carries comparable penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and executive officers who knowingly participated in the evasion are personally criminally liable; additionally, joint and several civil liability to the corporation under articles 752–754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations; recovery actions may target personal bank accounts; revocation of the vehicle operating licence and prohibition on maintaining residence in Switzerland are possible measures in the context of major VAT and fuel-excise fraud.
Tax Fraud and Forgery (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Custodial sentence of up to three years, or monetary penalty for negligent commission, under the Swiss Criminal Code and the customs and tax legislation; confiscation of vehicles, fuel stock and criminal assets; exclusion from Swiss public procurement; per route, the customs evasion fine may reach five times the duties evaded plus the payable duties.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal imprisonment, travel bans within Switzerland pending extradition cooperation, and civil restitution orders; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud to attach the assets of the signatory directors and beneficial owners; European arrest warrant and Interpol red notices may issue for cross-border TIR offences.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the artificial structure under Art. 6 DBG; denial of participation relief and treaty refunds; assessment at the place of effective management (often the foreign seat of the parent); 5% deemed profit uplift plus penalties; annulment of the holding privilege and possible revocation of the TIR transport licence by the Swiss federal office of transport if the entity is no longer the operator.

Director personal liability: The managing director and each director residing in Switzerland are jointly liable for the unpaid taxes and penalties; foreign directors may be subject to Swiss administrative assistance requests with criminal referral; personal bank guarantees and director liability policies are often void in cases of deliberately abusive structures.
Non-Remittance of Withholding Tax and Social Contributions

Statutory sanction: Mandatory simple interest on outstanding withholding tax; negligence penalty of up to 20% of the unremitted tax; deliberate evasion from withholding tax payment increases the penalty to three times the evaded amount; social security contributions carry contribution surcharges and administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Article 37 of the Federal Withholding Tax Act imposes direct personal liability on statutory representatives and managing officers of the company for unrecovered withholding tax; civil liability and criminal fines at personal level; travel restrictions are common in cross-border transport cases where directors are non-residents; social security collection authorities may enforce against the private assets of directors who signed the payroll.
Money Laundering, AML and Customs/TIR Fraud

Statutory sanction: Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis money laundering: custodial sentence up to three years and a fine; aggravated laundering (professional/band) up to five years; failure to report to the Money-Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (Art. 305ter) or negligence in the banking-AML context leads to fines and closure of bank mandates; customs offences under Art. 118 of the Swiss Customs Act: repayment of duties, confiscation, penalty up to the amount evaded and, in serious cases, disqualification from TIR guaranteeing procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are individually criminally liable; corporate bank guarantees are frozen by the reporting obligation; personal travel bans and asset freezes are available under Swiss legal assistance; the corporate veil is pierced for civil recovery of the laundering proceeds; for foreign beneficial owners, extradition to the protected state of the laundering offence is maintained under Swiss extradition law; the TIR guarantee chain (IRU/ATIT) may seek indemnification from the directors' personal assets under guarantee agreements.
