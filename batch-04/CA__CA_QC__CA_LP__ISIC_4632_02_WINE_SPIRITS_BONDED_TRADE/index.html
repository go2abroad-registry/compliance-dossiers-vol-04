Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Provincial: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) which administers Quebec Sales Tax (QST), corporate/partnership filings, and coordinates with the Registraire des entreprises for the Enterprise Number (NEQ). Customs matters fall under the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign Limited Partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and the associated GST/HST program account (RT) by filing Form RC1 or registering online through the CRA's Business Registration Online, using the non-resident partnership registration flow. If the LP has a place of business or employees in Quebec, it must concurrently register with the Registraire des entreprises for a NEQ (Quebec enterprise number) and then register with Revenu Québec for QST and source-deduction accounts. For bonded wine/spirits trade, the LP must also obtain a CBSA importer/exporter identifier (RM account), a licensed excise warehouse permit under the Excise Act, 2001, and a Quebec liquor permit from the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux. A foreign LP without Canadian permanent establishment may nonetheless register voluntarily for GST/HST and QST to claim input tax credits on pre-export purchases, and must file an RC372 election if part of a corporate group.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; the LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 96, with income computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners for inclusion in each partner's own return. Available elections include: (1) partnership fiscal period end election under ITA 249.1; (2) mandatory/elective allocation rules for non-resident partners electing under ITA 216 to file on net taxable income instead of Part XIII withholding; (3) joint election under ETA 273 for closely related entities to treat supplies as non-supplies (rarely applicable to an LP); and (4) the RC372 election for GST/HST groups when the LP is a member of a qualifying group. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA 18.4-18.6 and ETA overriding provisions apply, with no separate election available to neutralize cross-border payment-timing mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold over a consecutive four-calendar-quarter period for both GST/HST and QST; registration is mandatory once a non-resident LP makes taxable supplies in Canada above this threshold, but voluntary registration is advisable for exporters claiming input tax refunds on inputs used in zero-rated export supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of wine and spirits qualifies for zero-rated treatment under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the Excise Tax Act where the LP exports the goods in accordance with the ETA export evidence rules: the goods must be supplied to a buyer outside Canada, be safely exported within 60 days of the invoice date (or a longer period allowed by the Minister), and the LP must obtain documentary evidence within two years of the supply. The bonded warehouse transfer itself is not a taxable supply; duty is suspended on the Excise Duty (Excise Act, 2001) and the Quebec specific tax on alcohol until export, provided the transfer is made directly from the licensed bonded warehouse to the export carrier. Zero-rating also covers offshore back-to-back sales where title passes outside Canada and goods are shipped directly from the bonded warehouse to a foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A form) or CARM electronic export declaration showing commodity code 2208, Canadian exporter account, port of exit, and destination country
  • Bill of Lading (airway bill or sea waybill) issued by the carrier with the bonded warehouse premises as point of loading and a foreign port or airport as destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire instruction evidencing currency settlement from the foreign buyer to the LP's Canadian bank account within the applicable repatriation period
  • Bonded Warehouse Withdrawal Certificate signed by the licensed warehouse keeper under the Excise Act, 2001 confirming duty-suspended removal
  • SAQ/RACJ endorsement or Quebec liquor permit export rider authorizing the removal of wine and spirits to a foreign consignee

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing National Standard (PEPPOL-bis BIS 3.0) adopted in 2022; however, CRA and Revenu Québec require that all invoices contain specified data fields (ETA section 3 and QST rules), including BN, transaction date, clear description, amount and tax calculation, and be preserved in original format for six years. For bonded warehouse withdrawals to export, CBSA requires prior electronic ACI/eManifest filings for goods leaving Canada by road, rail, air, or marine modes, and the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform processes the export declarations digitally via API or portal.

CRA may reassess within four years of an assessment, extended to ten years for misrepresentation; any zero-rating claim lacking adequate export evidence is fully reversed, with net tax reassessed, interest charged at the prescribed rate plus a 6% arrears penalty under ETA section 280, and a false-statement penalty under ETA section 281.1 equal to the greater of CAD 4,000 and 15% of the claimed net tax, with additional penalties up to 25% for gross negligence under ITA section 163.2. Criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and Excise Act, 2001 section 217 for fraudulent zero-rating carries fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years; Revenu Québec may additionally claw back QST refunds and cancel the enterprise's NEQ and liquor permit, and CBSA may suspend the importer/exporter license on evidence of diversion of bonded goods into domestic distribution.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account lines. No central-bank permit or prior approval is required for capital contributions, profit repatriation, partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners, or liquidation proceeds. Practical frictions are fiscal and transactional: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, generally treaty-reduced) on remitted partnership distributions, FINTRAC reporting for cross-border electronic-funds transfers and large cash transactions in excess of CAD 10,000, and mandatory screening against OFAC, UN, EU, and federal Canadian sanctions lists. For a Quebec société en commandite (LP), conversion friction arises only from the administrative need to maintain a valid immatriculation number issued by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and a valid CRA excise-warehouse registration for bonded wine and spirits inventory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules apply. There is no requirement to convert export receipts into CAD, no obligatory repatriation window, and no mandated minimum CAD balance. The Quebec LP may retain USD, EUR, or GBP receipts in foreign-currency operating accounts at domestic Schedule I banks. Residual reporting obligations include: (i) filing under the Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) for cross-border receipts and payments, (ii) FINTRAC EFT transaction reports for transfers over CAD 10,000, and (iii) CRA Form T106/T5013 disclosure for any management fees, commissions, or interest paid to non-residents, together with Part XII.2 investment-partnership rules where passive income is allocable to non-resident partners.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Bank of Canada Act with supervisory coordination by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for domestic bank conduct and by Global Affairs Canada for federal economic-sanctions administration. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange-control administration analogous to a SAFE regime; FX policy authority is vested in the Currency Act and the Minister of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail; Medium as a secondary disbursement rail only. Canada does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences; non-resident fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and clear through Canadian Schedule I partner banks. For a Quebec LP engaged in bonded wine and spirits wholesale trade, such platforms cannot support CRA excise-duty payment rails, customs bonded settlements, or UCP 600 documentary credits. Non-resident capital commitments must be received by SWIFT MT103 with the payment narrative coded 'EQUITY INJECTION - PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL' and the LP immatriculation number as reference, classified per IMF Balance-of-Payments Manual (BPM6) as direct investment into the partnership. The remitter's name on the MT103 must match the LP register exactly; a corporate remitter requires submission of a control-ownership chart proving its LP holding. Funds exceeding CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC and supported by a deposit anticipation letter executed by the General Partner prior to the value date. No Bank of Canada licence or FX conversion approval is required. CAD funds settle over the Lynx high-value system; USD and EUR funds settle via the correspondent nostro accounts of the receiving Schedule I bank. The bank performs a source-of-funds match against the capital-commitment notice for each tranche.Primary clearing rails comprise: (i) SWIFT MT103 open-account transfers and ISO 20022 pain.001 equivalents for supplier settlements relating to Scotch whisky and Bordeaux wine on EXW, FCA, or CFR terms; (ii) commercial sight or usance Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 and documentary collections under URC 522 for large containerised shipments, with presentation of bills of lading, certificates of origin and age (e.g., Scotch Whisky Association certificate of authenticity, Bordeaux AOC/IGP vintage certificates), and CFIA import/export documentation; (iii) CRA excise-duty payments on removal of product from bond, which must clear exclusively over Canadian payment systems via ACSS or Lynx using the partnership's CRA program account and may not be routed through commercial L/C or MSB rails; and (iv) domestic bonded-warehouse transfers, which settle via Lynx real-time inter-bank transfers and are recorded on the CRA excise-duty schedule.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards with tenors up to 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank FX Master Agreement, for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP underlying trade exposures.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) for matching long-dated supplier payment liabilities, requiring an ISDA Credit Support Annex and a duly adopted General Partner hedging resolution.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to exotic supplier-country currencies (e.g., UAH, MDL) where no deliverable forward market exists; CAD itself is always settled on a deliverable basis, so a CAD NDF is neither permissible nor commercially necessary.FX spot options and zero-cost collars for open-account receivable and payable cycles, subject to the bank's derivatives-appropriateness review and the LP's documented internal hedging policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary clearing rail; Medium as a secondary disbursement rail only. Canada does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences; non-resident fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and clear through Canadian Schedule I partner banks. For a Quebec LP engaged in bonded wine and spirits wholesale trade, such platforms cannot support CRA excise-duty payment rails, customs bonded settlements, or UCP 600 documentary credits. Open-account supplier payments below CAD 50,000 may be routed through MSBs, but the principal treasury relationship must remain a licensed Canadian bank for compliance and credit reasons.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined statutory headline rate for a general Canadian corporation in Quebec (15% federal + 11.5% provincial); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not subject to Canadian corporate income tax at the entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident recipient. In the context of a Limited Partnership, the LP itself does not pay dividends; profit allocations flow to partners. Where a corporate structure is used for repatriating LP profits to non-resident investors, the distribution would be characterized as a dividend subject to the 25% statutory rate unless treaty-reduced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – CRA requires the filing of Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for each non-arm's-length cross-border transaction or series when the aggregate value of such transactions in a taxation year exceeds CAD 1,000,000. For high-value multinational enterprises, CRA expects a consolidated Master File for transactions of CAD 50 million or more and a Local File for transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000, although no separate statutory Local/Master File threshold exists in the Income Tax Act.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the manner of the UAE or Bermuda, but substantive economic presence is an implied requirement for treaty entitlement and is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and through judicial doctrines of sham and substance. A Quebec limited partnership engaged in bonded warehouse wholesale trade must maintain physical inventory, qualified staff, decision-making functions, banking, and tax records in Canada; a shell LP with no local substance will attract CRA reassessment, treaty-benefit denial, GAAR penalties, and possible criminal prosecution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, on summary conviction, fines of 50% to 100% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fines of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civilly, subsection 163(2) imposes a penalty equal to 50% of the unreported tax where false statements or omissions are made knowingly or due to gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for taxes withheld but not remitted and for evaded taxes where they participated. CRA can issue a director's liability assessment, garnish personal bank accounts, and, where assets are hidden, apply for travel bans through Federal Court. Limited partners lose their liability shield if they take part in the commercial management of the partnership.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) of the Income Tax Act: penalty of 10% of the amount of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CAD 1,000,000, unless the taxpayer made reasonable efforts and prepared contemporaneous documentation. Late T106 filing penalties amount to CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500 per occurrence. Reassessed tax will also carry compound interest.

Director personal liability: Insolvency, asset-freeze orders and director liability are available to CRA where the corporate veil is crossed; in LP structures, the general partner becomes personally liable for the partnership's debts, including transfer pricing reassessments, unless it is a shell corporation with no assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 permits CRA to deny the tax benefits arising from an avoidance transaction; penalties of 50% of the denied benefits may be applied under section 163(2). Revenu Québec can revoke the partnership's registration and impose provincial penalties. Courts may disregard the shell entity under the sham doctrine, and treaty benefits may be rejected under the OECD's principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be assessed personally for unpaid taxes and penalties if the corporate form is disregarded. In egregious situations, CRA may issue a 'failure to appear' warrant and freezing orders against the director's personal assets, while travel restrictions are imposed under the Canada Revenue Agency's enforcement powers for serious non-compliance.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for failure to report large cash transactions. Criminal Code money-laundering provisions impose imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering proceeds of tax evasion or customs fraud; seizure and forfeiture of assets under the proceeds-of-crime regime are additional sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally prosecutable if they authorized the AML breach; Quebec's anti-money-laundering oversight also applies administrative penalties to both the LP and its officers. Travel bans may be requested by authorities where the person is a principal of a criminal money-laundering network.
Withholding / Profit Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Sections 215 and 227(8) of the Income Tax Act: the payer is liable for the full unreported 25% withholding tax, compounded daily arrears interest, and a 10% penalty on the unremitted amount. CRA may assess the partnership even if the funds have already left Canada and can garnish assets without prior judicial authorization.

Director personal liability: Section 227.1 imposes joint and several personal liability on directors and the general partner for all unremitted Part XIII withholding. CRA issues a personal liability certificate, allowing seizure of personal assets; criminal prosecution may follow where the director knowingly participated with the intent to evade the tax, carrying with it imprisonment under section 242.
