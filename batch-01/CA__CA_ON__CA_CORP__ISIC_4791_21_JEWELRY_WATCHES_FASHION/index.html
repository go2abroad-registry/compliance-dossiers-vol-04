Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — administered through the 'My Business Account' portal and the GST/HST NETFILE system

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Business Corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online, Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or through the provincial Ontario Business Registry. The registration includes issuance of a corporation income tax account, GST/HST program account, and, if applicable, payroll and import/export accounts. Non-resident corporations using a Canadian address or representative may also complete Form RC1 or use CRA's non-resident registration gateway; a Canadian agent or nominee is not mandatory for GST/HST but strongly advisable for service of process and statutory notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Worldwide taxation for resident Canadian corporations; CA_CORP incorporated federally or provincially is deemed tax-resident in Canada if its central management and control is exercised in Canada, thereby subject to Part I tax on worldwide business income with foreign tax credit relief. The entity is not eligible for US-style pass-through check-the-box treatment.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. Elections available include: (a) GST/HST election under Section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations; (b) income tax Section 85 rollover election for transferring property to a corporation on a tax-deferred (rollover) basis; (c) Section 44 or 45 elections for deferral of capital gains; (d) explicit annual salary-dividend elections by corporate directors; and (e) hybrid-mismatch-attribute notices under the OECD Multilateral Instrument where cross-border structures implicate Canada–US or Canada–EU treaty provisions. All elections require prescribed forms and filing through CRA's electronic or paper channels.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in taxable supplies (GST/HST-includable revenue) over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, measured at any point after a quarterly threshold test. For non-resident e-commerce sellers of good in Canada using a Canadian fulfillment warehouse or marketplace model, threshold waiver does not apply if the supply is made through a distribution platform; however, a resident Business Corporation is subject to the standard $30,000 small-supplier threshold. The Ontario HST rate is 13% on all qualifying taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to goods exported directly from Canada, or services supplied to a non-resident client where the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is ancillary to exported goods. For online retail of jewelry and watches, export zero-rating is available only if the seller maintains documentary evidence proving physical removal within 60 days of sale, title passed outside Canada, and no taxable Canadian use by the vendor. Supplies made to non-residents that are electronically delivered (e.g., warranty services) are generally zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident and is not registered for GST/HST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or the Canada Border Services Agency eExport system declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / despatch note evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with terms of sale (CIF/FOB), HS code, and description of the article
  • Inward remittance proof (SWIFT MT103, bank transfer confirmation, or PayPal/Stripe settlement records) confirming payment in Canadian or foreign currency
  • Purchase order, contract, or order confirmation with the non-resident customer and ship-to address outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or country-specific e-invoicing application at the federal level. CRA requires electronic records to be retained and made available on request; GST/HST returns must be remitted electronically老 via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE if annual revenue exceeds CAD $1 million, and use of Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary. Ontario Revenue Authority audits rely on point-of-sale records, payment-gateway transaction logs (Stripe/PayPal), and platform-generated tax invoices. No prior CRA approval or encrypted QR code is required for invoices issued by the CA_CORP.

CRA and CBSA conduct export-verification audits; where zero-rating is claimed without customs documentation or where proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (or supported by a contractual deferral), the exporter is liable for reinstatement of GST/HST at the Ontario HST rate of 13% on the full invoiced value, plus interest calculated from the return filing date and a penalty of 10% to 20% of the net tax assessed under Section 285 of the Excise Tax Act for false or incomplete information. Fraudulent claims, forged shipping documents, or sham export invoices constitute wilful evasion, invoking criminal penalties under the Excise Tax Act (up to CAD $50,000 fine and/or imprisonment of up to two years), additional corporate tax clawbacks under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, and denial of corresponding income-treaty credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; no exchange-control permits, no capital repatriation limits, no prior central-bank approval, and no non-resident deposit restrictions exist for Canadian-dollar transactions. Profit and dividend remittances from an Ontario business corporation are lawful and unlimited; however, Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) applies at 25% (generally reduced to 10-15% for dividends, and 5-15% for interest and royalties under the Canada-U.S. and Canada-China tax treaties). Thin-capitalization rules (ITA section 18(4)-(6)) limit deductible interest where shareholder debt-to-equity exceeds 1.5:1, and excessive debt-equity structures may be recharacterized as deemed dividends under ITA section 80.4 and 214.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no mandated repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export proceeds or e-commerce revenue derived from online merchandising. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained indefinitely in any bank-account denomination; the only applicable compliance obligation is FINTRAC EFT reporting for each transfer or aggregate transfers of $10,000 CAD or more within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial-system authority; prudential oversight of federally incorporated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Cross-border capital administration, foreign-exchange monitoring, fund-transfer surveillance, and anti-money-laundering enforcement are carried out by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), with reporting obligations for all electronic funds transfers (EFT) of $10,000 CAD or more.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot lawfully hold CAD-denominated settlement accounts in Canada unless they are registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA and, where they engage in retail payment activities, are authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Non-registered foreign EMIs may only route transactions via SWIFT pass-through arrangements with client funds held in segregated trust accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank; such structures are operationally viable for this e-commerce treasury but trigger enhanced due diligence by Canadian clearing banks and require explicit disclosure of the pass-through beneficiary structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with explicit payment-purpose codes: 'Equity Injection / Capital Contribution' for share-subscription proceeds and 'Shareholder Loan' for intercompany debt. The receiving Ontario corporation must ensure its bank records the source of funds and, for all EFTs of $10,000 CAD or more, the bank or entity must file a FINTRAC EFT report. Funds are settled into a CAD-denominated capital escrow or operational account; conversion to USD, if needed for wholesale inventory purchases, is executed by the bank at prevailing spot rates, or via a forward contract if the exposure exceeds CAD 500,000. Return flows to non-resident shareholders in the form of dividends or interest are disbursed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with Part XIII withholding deducted at source (25% statutory, reduced to 10% or lower under applicable treaty), and any non-arm's-length cross-border payments in excess of the statutory threshold require a T106 Information Return filed with the CRA.The treasury clearing-rails architecture comprises three tiers: (1) for low-value fashion-jewelry procurement and operational vendor payments, direct SWIFT MT103 payments from the CAD operating account settled same-day via LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) and international correspondent banking; Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5% = 26.5% combined general rate; Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) with an Ontario permanent establishment may apply the Small Business Deduction: 9% federal + 3.2% Ontario = 12.2% combined rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian tax-resident corporate head entity to non-residents, applicable to ordinary cash dividends, deemed dividends on share redemptions, and synthetic dividends routed through hybrid instruments.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due date is six months after the fiscal year-end; balance-due payable date is two months after year-end, extended to three months for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction in the preceding tax year. Quarterly instalments are due where estimated Part I tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions in the fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation and T106 reporting; CAD 50,000 applies for specified transactions (loan guarantees, subordinated loans, debt subordination, and performance undertakings). Documentation must include a local file, master file, and functional/economic analysis.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies no standalone Economic Substance Regulations analogous to offshore hubs; however, the enhanced General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) enacted by Bill C-59 (June 2024) statutorily incorporates economic substance as an interpretive criterion for avoidance transactions entered into on or after April 1, 2024, and the Supreme Court's Copthorne Holdings decision confirms substance-over-form analysis. For ISIC 4791 online retailing, CRA functional analysis requires demonstrable Ontario substance—employees, inventory risk, order fulfilment, and decision-making authority—before treaty benefits will be honoured; an Ontario shell intermediate with negligible local personnel is exposed to GAAR re-characterization and OECD Multilateral Instrument principal-purpose denials.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax. Criminal sanction under s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 2 years' imprisonment; CRA may freeze accounts via s.231.3 jeopardy orders and issue s.231.6 foreign information demands.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders are jointly and severally assessable under s.160(1) for property transfers to non-arm's length parties; convicted officers face imprisonment, passport surrender, and court-imposed travel bans as bail conditions pending appeal.
Shell Company / Sham / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Enhanced GAAR reassessment (applicable to transactions after April 1, 2024) restores income to the Canadian entity, adds a 25% penalty on the avoided tax, and imposes interest; treaty relief is denied under the principal purpose test; CRA can apply s.247 transfer-pricing adjustments of 10% of the net understated amount where documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T106 declarations face s.239 criminal prosecution; the corporate veil is pierced when a force-shaving sham is proven, subjecting directors to civil restitution, frozen travel through immigration hold/exit-embargo mechanisms, and extradition risk in cross-border fraud investigations.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Failure (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for money-laundering-related non-compliance and CAD 1,000,000 for terrorist-financing violations; Criminal Code section 462.31 (laundering proceeds of crime) carries up to 10 years' imprisonment; FINTRAC compliance orders may suspend merchant processing rails.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in the reporting failure are jointly exposed to indictment under the party-liability provisions of the Criminal Code; upon conviction they face imprisonment, board disqualification, and pre-trial travel restrictions including passport seizure and supervised release conditions.
Unremitted Source Withholding and Payroll Trust Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Unremitted amounts are deemed held in trust for the Crown under s.227, attracting daily compounded interest and a 15% late-remittance penalty; CRA issues a Director's Liability assessment for 100% of the unremitted trust balance without the need for court action.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s.227.1 unless they prove the strict due-diligence standard; the Crown seizes personal bank accounts, garnishes salaries, and enforces judgments outdoors Canada through mutual collection treaties; active criminal investigation can trigger judicial travel bans and detention pending trial.
