Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Québec limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed solely by the limited partnership agreement. Limited partners may not withdraw their contribution if such withdrawal impairs creditor rights (CCQ arts. 2245–2246). No escrow account is mandated by law, but the partnership may contractually choose a trust or escrow arrangement. The LP must always have at least one general partner with unlimited liability.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Federal GST/HST and income tax administration, portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec (Quebec Revenue), QST and Quebec income tax administration, portal: revenuquebec.ca; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar), statutory limited partnership registration, portal: registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Quebec limited partnership: (1) file the declaration of registration / constitution of the société en commandite with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ); (2) apply to the CRA for a 15-character Business Number (BN) using the business registration online service, registering the CA_LP as the GST/HST registrant if it is required or wishes to be a registrant; (3) apply via Revenu Québec, using the NEQ and BN, for the QST registration; (4) complete the non-resident registration supplement where any partner is not resident in Canada, providing the foreign entity's name, country of incorporation, and foreign tax identification number; (5) keep the partnership's BN, NEQ, GST/HST account number, and QST number on all invoices, import/export documents, and returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent as a matter of mandatory domestic tax law: the partnership computes income but is not an income tax taxpayer; each partner is taxable on its share. Relevant statutory elections include election of a non-calendar partnership fiscal period under Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(9) and, for GST/HST, the available elections respecting filing periods and related-party supplies under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules may apply to recharacterize transparency where a foreign jurisdiction treats the LP as opaque, but there is no elective domestic entity-status change.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST. A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada through a Quebec establishment should generally register even below the threshold if registrant status is necessary to claim input tax refunds; an exporter that makes only zero-rated supplies may register voluntarily to obtain input tax credits and QST refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property, including pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot base bricks, is zero-rated for GST/HST and QST where the supplier sells the goods for export, the property is delivered outside Canada by the supplier or by a common carrier/forwarder on behalf of the supplier, and the supplier retains documentary proof that the goods were exported. The goods must leave Canada without significant use in Canada and the supplier must have no reason to believe the export is not genuine. Services supplied to non-residents and performed entirely outside Canada also qualify for zero-rating where separately invoiced. The food product may independently qualify as a basic grocery zero-rated supply, but the exporter must nevertheless satisfy the Schedule VI export-evidence requirements if the exemption is claimed as an export.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA export report or carrier-generated export documentation for the shipment
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill showing foreign destination and date of export
  • Commercial invoice with complete HS commodity description of the pre-packaged hotpot soup base
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Delivery and transport records confirming the goods left Canada
  • CFIA or other federal food export documentation where applicable to pre-packaged soup base

No mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal API pre-authorization currently applies in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST and QST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars in French and/or English, may issue paper or electronic invoices, and may use EDI/PEPPOL voluntarily; CRA and Revenu Québec audit invoice records rather than clearing invoices in real time.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit export zero-rating claims and require the exporter to substantiate each shipment for at least six years. Failure to maintain adequate export proof results in reassessment of GST/HST at 5% and QST at 9.975%, plus arrear interest and late-filing penalties. False or fraudulent claims attract a 25% gross-negligence penalty under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec taxation rules, unlimited reassessment periods in cases of misrepresentation, and criminal liability. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day inward FX repatriation deadline; however, the 180-day benchmark is used by the compliance dossier as documentary settlement evidence, since absence of export proceeds may undermine the commercial reality and validity of the export zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada imposes no general exchange control permits or unqualified conversion restrictions. Capital contributions, profit distributions, and repatriation of limited partnership capital may flow freely, but remain subject to documentary anti-money laundering controls, CRA Part XIII withholding on non-resident income, Quebec provincial GST/QST obligations, CRS/FATCA tax transparency, and standard bank source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters are not required to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any statutory timeline. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in Canadian chartered bank foreign-currency accounts. The principal obligation is FINTRAC's reporting rule requiring every international electronic funds transfer of CAD $10,000 or more within a 24-hour period to be reported, with supporting records retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; FINTRAC is the financial intelligence unit administering Canadian cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting and money services business registration. Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign exchange control authority or an FX licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium - constrained. Canada does not license offshore electronic money institutions as banks. A non-resident EMI may only operate as a FINTRAC-registered money services business or as a payment agent of a Schedule I Canadian bank. It cannot accept deposits in Canada or issue Canadian depository accounts. Offshore EMIs may be useful for API payment collection and payout corridors, but they are not viable as the primary treasury or trade clearing rail for a Quebec limited partnership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investors transmit CAD, USD, or other convertible currencies by SWIFT MT103 through their banking institution to the Canadian Schedule I bank's correspondent account, then onward to the Quebec LP's operating or capital-escrow account. The beneficiary account name must exactly match the registered partnership legal name. The remittance details must identify the transfer as a capital contribution, include the investor name and LP identifier, and state a clearly defined purpose, such as Contribution to capital of CA_LP. The receiving Canadian bank performs standard funds-transfer verification; international EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC. Funds are then credited to the partnership capital ledger only after source-of-funds and sanctions clearance.Trade settlements to upstream fat, seasoning, and packaging suppliers are executed through SWIFT MT103 remittances with reference to the underlying commercial invoice and purchase order. Large-value or letter-of-credit-backed settlements use irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600); inboard export receipts may be routed as documentary collections under URC 522 or as direct SWIFT credit transfers. Domestic Canadian-dollar disbursements may traverse Payments Canada's Lynx or Real-Time Rail, while all cross-border payments must pass through the bank's SWIFT-connected correspondent network. No Canadian surrender or exchange-control approval applies, but the bank will require commercial invoices, packing lists, insurance documentation, and applicable CFIA export certificates to process trade-related flows.Outright foreign exchange forward contracts for documented commercial export or import exposureCross-currency swaps under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement to hedge long-term CAD versus foreign-currency working capital positionsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where the counterparty currency has no accessible onshore deliverable forward marketFX swaps used to roll short-term settlement balances without speculative leverage

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not itself subject to federal or Quebec income tax on ordinary partnership income. Taxable income is allocated to the partners. For a taxable Canadian corporation, the general federal corporate rate is 15% and the general Quebec rate is 11.5%, producing a combined general rate of 26.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A limited partnership distribution is generally not a dividend and is not subject to dividend withholding merely because it is distributed. Canadian Part XIII withholding generally applies at 25% to specified amounts paid or credited to non-residents, including many royalties, management fees, rents, pensions, and certain interest amounts. Dividends paid by a Canadian corporation are generally subject to 25% withholding, subject to treaty reduction. Under the Canada-China income tax treaty, qualifying dividends are generally reduced to 10% where the beneficial owner is a company meeting the direct ownership threshold and 15% in other treaty cases, subject to the treaty text and anti-abuse rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 and applicable Quebec partnership information returns are generally due within five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period. The fiscal period generally cannot exceed 12 months. T5013 slips must be provided to partners by the applicable information-slip deadline, generally the last day of March following the calendar year for calendar-year reporting. T106 is generally due with the reporting entity's income tax return, or by the applicable prescribed deadline where no income tax return is required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For T106 reporting, a commonly applicable threshold is aggregate non-arm's-length non-resident transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 during the year. Canadian contemporaneous-documentation rules under section 247 of the Income Tax Act do not operate solely as a universal transaction-value threshold; the taxpayer must generally maintain documentation sufficient to demonstrate arm's-length pricing. Canada generally does not impose a separate statutory Master File requirement in the manner used by some jurisdictions, but multinational groups may have Country-by-Country Reporting obligations where the consolidated group meets the applicable EUR 750 million threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the United Arab Emirates or certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty principal-purpose analysis, beneficial-ownership doctrine, transfer-pricing rules, foreign affiliate and foreign accrual property income rules, and judicial tests concerning residence, permanent establishment, sham transactions, and abusive tax avoidance. The partnership should maintain Canadian commercial substance proportionate to its activities, including genuine inventory functions, personnel or outsourced operational capability, premises or logistics arrangements, decision-making records, inventory risk, credit risk, and contemporaneous governance evidence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment of tax, interest, and gross-negligence penalties that may reach 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit. Criminal tax evasion can result in fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years; aggravated fraud-related conduct may attract more severe Criminal Code consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and responsible officers may face prosecution. A limited partner normally has limited liability, but a person who controls operations, commits the offence, acts as a de facto general partner, or participates in fraud may incur personal criminal and civil liability. Canadian law does not generally impose an automatic tax-evasion travel ban, but criminal release conditions, warrants, passport consequences, and international enforcement may restrict travel.
Failure to Remit Payroll, GST/HST, QST, or Non-Resident Withholding

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the unremitted amount, arrears interest, late-remittance penalties, failure-to-file penalties, and potential director liability. The CRA may issue director-liability assessments for source deductions and certain GST/HST amounts, subject to statutory due-diligence and limitation rules. Revenu Quebec may impose parallel assessments and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner can be jointly and severally liable for specified unremitted trust amounts if statutory conditions are met. General partners are directly exposed under partnership law. Persons who knowingly participate in diversion or obstruction may face criminal charges and asset-recovery proceedings.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement and Related-Party Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust income under section 247, deny deductions, impose transfer-pricing penalties generally equal to 10% of the applicable adjustment where statutory thresholds are met, and assess interest. Quebec may make corresponding adjustments. Repeated or intentional conduct may trigger GAAR or criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: The primary liability is generally imposed on the partnership or partner taxpayer rather than directors personally. Personal exposure may arise from fraud, knowing participation, director-liability assessments for associated trust amounts, or criminal obstruction. Ordinary commercial transfer-pricing error does not by itself pierce the corporate veil.
Shell Company, Sham Partnership, or Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment under GAAR, beneficial-ownership and residence doctrines, penalties, interest, withholding at domestic rates, partnership recharacterization, and potential criminal charges for fraud, laundering, or false statements.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard sham arrangements and impose liability on persons who controlled or benefited from the conduct. Veil piercing is exceptional and fact-specific; it is not an automatic consequence of low substance. Criminal convictions can result in imprisonment, fines, restitution, forfeiture, and court-imposed release or travel restrictions.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC reporting, record-keeping, client-identification, and compliance failures may result in administrative monetary penalties. Sanctions breaches under Canadian sanctions legislation may result in substantial fines and imprisonment. Proceeds-of-crime offences may result in imprisonment, forfeiture, and criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, compliance personnel, and general partners may be prosecuted where they authorized, directed, consented to, or knowingly ignored the conduct. Corporate status does not protect a person who personally commits laundering, sanctions, obstruction, or false-reporting offences.
Unlicensed Food Distribution and Product Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Canadian Food Inspection Agency and other authorities may detain, recall, seize, destroy, or prohibit sale of products and may impose administrative or criminal penalties under applicable food, customs, consumer-protection, and Quebec language laws. Tax deductions may be challenged where transactions are fictitious or unlawful.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be prosecuted where they authorized, directed, or acquiesced in offences. General partners may be directly liable for operational violations. Civil claims may include product liability, recall costs, consumer damages, and restitution; imprisonment depends on the specific offence and facts.
