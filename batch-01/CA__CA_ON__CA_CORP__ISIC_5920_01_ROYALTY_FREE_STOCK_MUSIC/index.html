Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal administration of corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll and the Business Number (BN); Business Registration Online (BRO) and the My Business Account portal are the operative digital gateways; Ontario corporate income tax is also administered by the CRA under the Canada-Ontario Tax Collection Agreement, with the Ontario Ministry of Finance retaining provincial tax-credit administration.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Ontario Business Corporations Act or Canada Business Corporations Act; no minimum capital is required. 2) Apply to the CRA for the 15-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the BN is the sole corporate TIN, with program accounts appended (RC for corporate income, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll). 3) Register the RT account prior to commercial operation and select Ontario as the province of head office to be charged the 13% HST. 4) Non-resident equity ownership up to 100% is permitted, but CRA may require an authorized Canadian representative to sign registrations and filings. 5) Publish the BN on invoices, contracts and export records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Ontario business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; federal and Ontario corporate income taxes are imposed at the entity level, and shareholder dividends are taxed again when distributed; no pass-through, look-through or S-corporation analogue is available to an OBCA/CBCA corporation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; entity classification follows Income Tax Act legal principles. Relevant elections for this structure: voluntary GST/HST registration for a small supplier; the quick method of accounting (ETA s. 259) to simplify HST remittances; ITA s. 85 rollover elections on asset transfers; ITA s. 216 election for any non-resident royalty or rental income; hybrid-mismatch deductions are mandatorily disallowed under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, so no elective hybrid-saving mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148): GST/HST registration becomes mandatory only once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated exports do not count toward this threshold; voluntary registration is nonetheless recommended for the stock-music platform to recover input tax credits and to contract cleanly with foreign licensees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies to the supply of intangible personal property — the royalty-free stock-music licence — when made to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the licence is for use wholly outside Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23). If the foreign recipient is GST/HST registered, zero-rating applies only where the intangible is acquired for use solely in the recipient's offshore commercial activity. Licences that grant in-Canada performance (e.g., audio at Canadian commercial premises) are taxable at 13% HST. Substantiation requires contractual territory restrictions, non-resident identification, and evidence of foreign use or consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) — required only where tangible media is physically exported; waived for pure digital delivery
  • Signed licence or click-wrap subscription agreement identifying the non-resident licensee and defining the licensed territory
  • Commercial invoice stating '0% GST/HST — ETA Schedule VI, Part V' and citing the applicable zero-rating paragraph
  • Digital delivery/dispatch log and usage records evidencing offshore download or streaming
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT, Stripe or PayPal settlement reports reconciling to invoices and the sales ledger
  • GST/HST return schedules and bank statements supporting the zero-rated supply classification

Canada does not currently operate a statutory real-time e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping or API mandate. CRA requires electronic filing and remittance (GST/HST NETFILE plus electronic payment) for most registrants and a six-year retention of books and records; the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing initiative is moving toward the Peppol BIS standard, but adoption remains voluntary/planned. Until then, suppliers must issue invoices containing the ETA s. 223 prescribed information (vendor name and BN, date, description and applicable tax).

CRA may reassess erroneous zero-rating within the four-year normal reassessment period, or beyond it in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, and will charge compound daily interest on the net HST understated. Penalties include false-statement and negligence penalties under ETA s. 280, the gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated (minimum CAD 100) under ETA s. 285.1, and criminal tax-evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327. Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, so the 180-day benchmark is treated as a CRA audit-documentation expectation; export books and evidence must be preserved for six years under ETA s. 286.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior approvals apply to current-account or capital-account transactions. An Ontario business corporation may lawfully receive subscription proceeds from non-resident shareholders and remit dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees abroad without Bank of Canada clearance. The material remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than regulatory: Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents at a 25% default rate, treaty-reduced to 15% (5% for qualified substantial shareholdings) for United States residents under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention and to 10%-15% for most other treaty partners. Practical obstacles are limited to (i) CRS and FATCA due diligence performed by the receiving institution, (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting at CAD 10,000 or more, (iii) the requirement to recognize all foreign-currency items in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or the CRA annual average rate, and (iv) bank-level source-of-funds verification for inbound capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Export receipts derived from commercial soundscape licensing, royalty-free stock music subscriptions, and audio sample distribution may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP, or any other freely convertible currency. For Canadian corporate income tax purposes, the CRA requires that the exporter compute revenue and gains in Canadian dollars using a consistently applied Bank of Canada exchange rate, ordinarily the daily rate for individually priced transactions or the annual average rate for recurring subscription revenue. Electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more into or out of Canada are reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC, and physical currency or bearer monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more crossing the border require a CBSA cross-border currency report. All payout records from payment facilitators such as Stripe and PayPal, executed licensing agreements, and bank statements must be retained for at least six years per CRA record-keeping standards.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), the Canadian central bank and sole banknote issuer; prudential supervision of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act. Canada now has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration authority; exchange-control functions were abolished in 1951. Cross-border currency and AML/CFT reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, with border declaration enforcement by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). The Minister of Finance retains residual emergency powers under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act to impose temporary foreign-exchange restrictions in a national crisis; the Canada Revenue Agency operates the CRS and FATCA reporting infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An Ontario business corporation may lawfully hold settlement funds in offshore digital e-money institution (EMI) accounts located in FCA-regulated, EU-regulated, or comparable GAFI-compliant jurisdictions, and Canadian exchange-control law will not prohibit such accounts. However, an offshore EMI that routinely solicits Canadian clients or processes payments for Canadian beneficiaries may be required to register as an authorized foreign money services business or retail-payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act and PCMLTFA as applicable. The primary constraints are commercial rather than legal: EMI balances are not eligible for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage, do not participate in Payments Canada clearing systems such as the Large Value Transfer System or the Automated Clearing Settlement System, and may receive elevated audit scrutiny by Canadian Schedule I banks and the CRA when used as the principal treasury vehicle. The recommended architecture is a hybrid one: maintain CAD/USD operating and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank for primary liquidity, FX execution, and payroll, and use a single regulated EMI only as a supplementary settlement rail for high-volume, low-ticket merchant payouts originating from platforms such as Stripe and PayPal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection must follow a fully documented wire protocol: (i) the board adopts a resolution authorizing a specific share issuance and designating the operational account or a capital escrow account as the receiving account; (ii) the subscribing shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from a bank account held in the shareholder's own name, using a purpose code acceptable for cross-border equity investment, such as Capital Investment, Equity Injection, Direct Investment Equity, or Business Investment, depending on the receiving bank's classification taxonomy; (iii) the receiving Schedule I bank performs standard KYC, sanctions screening, and source-of-wealth checks; and (iv) the company issues share certificates, updates the Ontario share register, and files any required Form 1 notification under the Business Corporations Act if applicable. FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies to transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but no prior central-bank authorization is required. Verification evidence to be retained for at least six years includes the authenticated MT103, the bank credit advice, the signed directors' statutory declaration on purpose of funds, the share certificate stub, and a reconciliation of Canadian-dollar value using the Bank of Canada exchange rate on the value date. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. An Ontario business corporation may lawfully hold settlement funds in offshore digital e-money institution (EMI) accounts located in FCA-regulated, EU-regulated, or comparable GAFI-compliant jurisdictions, and Canadian exchange-control law will not prohibit such accounts. However, an offshore EMI that routinely solicits Canadian clients or processes payments for Canadian beneficiaries may be required to register as an authorized foreign money services business or retail-payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act and PCMLTFA as applicable. The primary constraints are commercial rather than legal: EMI balances are not eligible for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage, do not participate in Payments Canada clearing systems such as the Large Value Transfer System or the Automated Clearing Settlement System, and may receive elevated audit scrutiny by Canadian Schedule I banks and the CRA when used as the principal treasury vehicle. The recommended architecture is a hybrid one: maintain CAD/USD operating and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank for primary liquidity, FX execution, and payroll, and use a single regulated EMI only as a supplementary settlement rail for high-volume, low-ticket merchant payouts originating from platforms such as Stripe and PayPal.
