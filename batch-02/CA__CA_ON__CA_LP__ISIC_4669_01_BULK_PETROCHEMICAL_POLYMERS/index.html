Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Ontario law. Capital contributions are determined solely by the limited partnership agreement; the partnership must have at least one general partner with unlimited liability and one or more limited partners whose liability is capped at their agreed contributions. All contributions must be recorded in the partnership records as at admission.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing authority mandates a permit for wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules in Ontario; Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA) jurisdiction arises only if the partnership adds bulk storage of regulated flammable petroleum products or operates harsh-environment processing equipment.

If the partnership later installs transloading, blending, or bulk liquid storage, a TSSA Fuel Petroleum Product Handling Permit or a municipal industrial operating permit would become required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and federal tax registration; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for provincial portions of HST administered federally); CRA portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships must register for a Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account with the CRA before making taxable supplies in Canada. Apply online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by completing and submitting Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'. Non-resident applicants must provide: legal entity proof (certificate of limited partnership, registration statement from home jurisdiction), partnership agreement, a list of partners (with their TINs), a Canadian contact/agent, and an estimate of Canadian taxable supplies. The CRA generally issues the BN/GST/HST account within 5–10 business days. Registration is mandatory once taxable sales exceed CAD 30,000 annually (worldwide threshold for non-residents) or as a voluntary registrant for input tax credit recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA) for income tax; no check-the-box election exists in Canada. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and must register as such. Optional elections available: (a) joint election for exempt supplies (ITA s. 156) is available for certain supplies among related registered persons; (b) s. 21 election to eliminate tax on capital property transfers; (c) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 212.3 and 258 apply to deny deductions in cross-border arrangements. Ontario permits the Limited Partnership to file an Ontario Registration form under the Limited Partnerships Act but this is provincial corporate/commercial registration, not a tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies per calendar year; for non-residents not carrying on business in Canada, registration may be optional even when supplies are made, but a threshold-based nexus applies to supplies facilitated by a Canadian agent or if the supply is of 'qualifying tangible personal property' delivered in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for both GST/HST applies when the bulk petrochemical polymers (virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules) are exported from Canada to a foreign destination. For sales by a Canadian-registered limited partnership to a non-resident customer, the goods must be delivered by the supplier to a common carrier, mailed, or shipped by the supplier (or the supplier’s agent) to a destination outside Canada, or the supply must be made under a contract of carriage to a destination outside Canada. The goods must be exported in accordance with s. 6(1) and s. 6(1.1) of the ETA; no commercial value threshold applies. Services that are directly attributable to exported goods (e.g., freight, handling) may also be zero-rated if separate conditions are met. Proof of export must be obtained within two years of the invoice date, and the exporter must have a 'superficial domestic use' prohibition. 'Offshore services' connected to exported goods require the recipient to be a non-resident and the services to be performed primarily outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (A1/A6 form via Canada Border Services Agency or via the Canadian Export Reporting System – CERS, or equivalent electronic declaration; required when goods exceed CAD 2,000),
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing a Canadian port of loading and foreign port of discharge, with evidence of actual export, including carrier container tracking or vessel manifest, indicating the overseas destination of the cargo;
  • Commercial invoice showing the full sale consideration (in USD/ CAD / other currency), product description, ISIC 4669 / HS 3901-3902 classification (polyethylene and polypropylene primary forms), and terms of sale (EXW / FOB / CIF),
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank advice of the international wire transfer showing receipt of payment from the foreign customer within 180 days of shipment (common due diligence to substantiate the foreign-dealing nature of the sale); for trade via SWIFT / LC payment, the bank payment confirmation and final LC negotiation documents (presentation, dispatch and receipt) also serve as remittance evidence.

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. However, CRA requires electronic GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE for all registered persons with digital filing obligations. Invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST information (section 3 of the GST/HST Regulations). E-invoicing (EDI, PDF, XML) is permitted if records are complete and accessible; CRA does not require pre-authorization or certified invoicing software. Records for export zero-rating must be maintained electronically and available on request.

CRA scrutinizes export zero-rating claims closely. Failure to substantiate exportation results in reassessment of the unreported HST/GST (13% of the supply amount), plus interest and penalties. Specific penalties: (a) under ETA s. 280.1, any person who knowingly, or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, makes a false statement in a GST/HST return is liable to a penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; (b) under the Criminal Code and Tax Rebate Integrity provisions, fraudulent export claims can trigger criminal prosecution; (c) misstatements due to negligence or lack of reasonable care attract a 5% penalty plus 5% per month in some cases. Where the same transaction is claimed as an export by both the Canadian seller and the foreign customer, CRA may apply the 'invalid export' rule and claw back input tax credits. The period of clawback extends 4 years from the date of the assessment (or 10 years in cases of misrepresentation attributed to fraud, neglect, or wilful default). Ontario HST portions are clawed back in the same manner as federal GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act and Bank Act impose no approval requirement, quota, deposit limitation, or prior-authorization condition on trade settlements, capital injections, or partnership distributions. Profit-remittance hurdles for an Ontario Limited Partnership are limited to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced to as low as 0-15%, e.g., under the Canada-US Tax Treaty) on income allocations credited to non-resident limited partners unless a CRA waiver or exemption certificate is secured; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for every cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) transfer-pricing (s.247) and interest-deductibility (s.17 / thin-capitalization) rules when related-party funding crosses the border. No currency-conversion mandate applies to retained earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio (de facto 0% surrender / 100% retention permitted). Polymer export receivables may be held in CAD or foreign currency in any Canadian or foreign account. Compliance duties are limited to: (i) filing a CBSA electronic export declaration for shipments of polyethylene/polypropylene granules exceeding CAD 2,000 per shipment (HS 3901/3902; NAICS 424690); (ii) reporting to Statistics Canada under the Balance of Payments program when requested; and (iii) preserving FINTRAC records for all proceeds received by cross-border wire.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with adjacent regulatory authority exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank FX-prudential supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border wire surveillance and MSB registration, Payments Canada for clearing-rail administration, and Global Affairs Canada / Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export-licensing and export-declaration control. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration and no convertibility-licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for core treasury operations and NOT viable for LC settlement. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Ebury, WorldFirst) may legally hold CAD and route domestic ACH/EFT payments through a Canadian money-service business or banking partner, but none can issue a SWIFT MT700 documentary credit or a confirmed irrevocable LC, which is the contractual settlement rail for ISIC 4669 bulk polymer trades. Legal constraints under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: any non-bank EMI servicing the LP must be a FINTRAC-registered MSB (unless it qualifies as a foreign bank under the Bank Act Part XII), must maintain client funds with a Schedule I bank, and remains subject to the same CAD 10,000 EFT-reporting obligations. Recommended use is limited to incidental payments (freight, demurrage, brokerage), not trade-finance or capital-escrow flows.
