Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (Registrar of Enterprises), operating through the Entreprise Quebec government portal and the electronic 'Mon entreprise' business registration service; federal Business Registration Online (CRA) used for the federal Business Number.

Governing statute

Primary: Business Corporations Act (Quebec), CQLR c S-31.1, ss. 10-12 (articles of incorporation and certificate of constitution), s. 26 (declaration of directors), s. 116 (registered office), ss. 124-128 (officers); complemented by the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-45, ss. 16-18 (enterprise registration and initial declaration). Federal alternative: Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c C-44, s. 8, with extra-provincial registration in Quebec under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (statuts de constitution) filed electronically with the Registraire des entreprises, giving rise to the Certificate of Constitution (certificat de constitution) and the enterprise number (NEQ); accompanied by the initial declaration of registration (declaration initiale).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Quebec Business Corporations Act; shares without par value may be issued for such consideration as the board determines under ss. 36-38; no mandatory escrow account requirement for a restaurant corporation; non-resident (overseas) direct ownership is permitted without limitations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canadian federal or Quebec tax law. A corporation is taxed as a legal person at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act and Part IV of the Taxation Act (Quebec). There is no federal income tax consolidation election; electing to be a co-tenancy or partnership may be available only if legal requirements are met, but a business corporation cannot elect to be disregarded for income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules under Canada's Income Tax Act (ss. 18.4–18.6) apply to deem deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, but no prophylactic classification election is provided.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small supplier threshold under ETA s.148; CAD 30,000 — QST small supplier threshold under Quebec Excise Tax Act (QETA); registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed this worldwide/Canadian threshold, with special attestation in Quebec for permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA if the supplier exports them in accordance with regulations (e.g., shipment by common carrier, customs-examined export, delivery to a common carrier for mailing to an address outside Canada) and retains evidence within documentary requirements. Services are zero-rated when performed wholly outside Canada or when invoiced to a non-resident for an activity outside Canada. For a hotpot restaurant exporting condiments and packaged food, zero-rating applies to physical export of goods; restaurant meals sold in Canada are taxable at 14.975%; offshore franchise/brand service income may be zero-rated or may be outside GST/HST if no supply in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13 / CAED)
  • Bill of Lading (marine or air waybill)
  • Commercial invoice with seller and purchaser names
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt proving foreign consideration
  • Foreign customs entry or proof of arrival at destination outside Canada
  • Purchase order/contract with export terms and incoterms

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. CRA accepts electronic invoices if they contain the supplier's BN, date, name, and amount; Revenu Québec has published an electronic invoicing standard and interoperability roadmap, but e-invoicing remains voluntary at tax administration level. There is no API tax stamping for domestic restaurant sales; receipts must meet prescribed information requirements. Export invoices must be cross-referenced to communications/customs data on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec can reassess within 4 years (or later for misrepresentation) and claw back zero-rated treatment, making the supply taxable at 14.975%, with interest. Gross negligence penalties under ETA s.285.1 and Quebec QST Act parallel provisions can be 50% of the net tax understatement; knowingly making false statements may lead to enhancement and, for evasion, criminal prosecution. Unsupported export documentation will result in immediate disallowance; officers may be liable for directors' liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a free-floating currency regime with no general exchange control legislation. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances by foreign shareholders are not subject to prior exchange-control authorization; however, they are subject to Canadian federal and Quebec withholding tax, transfer-pricing documentation, and must be supported by audited or reviewed financial statements, board-approved dividends, and a solvency test. Non-resident-controlled Quebec enterprises are generally permitted to fully repatriate dividends, management fees, royalties, and repayments of shareholder loans, provided all Canadian tax obligations are settled. An additional regulatory review threshold may apply under the Investment Canada Act if the investment is subject to net-benefit review or involves cultural businesses, which could indirectly burden profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or conversion requirement for export proceeds. Export receipts, including franchise royalties or condiment-supply revenue from overseas direct-operated units, may be retained in foreign currency in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank accounts. For financial and tax reporting, such amounts must be valued in CAD under applicable accounting standards, but no mandatory conversion timeline, percentage ratio, or central-bank deposit rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy coordinates with the Department of Finance Canada and the Exchange Fund Account.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium direct viability. Foreign digital EMI/fintech payment institutions are not licensed to accept deposits or carry on banking business in Canada without a bank, trust, or provincial/loan license. They can operate merely as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) and are therefore suitable only as auxiliary payment channels for low-value disbursements, card issuance, or cross-border FX execution. For a full-service hotpot restaurant chain with merchant acquiring, POS settlement, payroll, and trade payments, the primary treasury node must remain a tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account; fintech EMIs cannot replace the clearing, credit, and correspondent-banking privileges of a domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the Canadian beneficiary bank, denominated in CAD or USD/CNH. The beneficiary instruction must contain the full Canadian account number (institution and transit number), the operating or capital-es row account title, and a precise description line: 'Paid-in capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement'. Large incoming cross-border electronic funds transfers are systemically reported to FINTRAC by the bank when at or above CAD 10,000. The remitter must obtain an advance AML clearance package consisting of the source-of-funds evidence, board resolution, share subscription agreement, and tax identification numbers. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

For a corporation carrying on business in Quebec, the general combined corporate income tax rate is 26.5%: 15% federal plus 11.5% Quebec. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may obtain a combined rate of approximately 12.2% on eligible active business income within the applicable federal and Quebec small-business limits: 9% federal plus 3.2% Quebec. Eligibility, business limits, taxable-capital reductions, and Quebec-specific conditions must be tested separately; non-resident control generally prevents CCPC status.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII withholding tax is generally 25% on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident. A tax treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for qualifying corporate parent-shareholder dividends, subject to the particular treaty's ownership, beneficial-ownership, limitation, and anti-abuse requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Both federal T2 and Quebec CO-17 returns are generally due within six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. Tax balances are generally due two months after year-end; qualifying CCPCs meeting the applicable small-business conditions may have three months to pay. Instalments are due on the applicable monthly or quarterly schedule. A return deadline extension does not extend the balance-due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general statutory CAD threshold that triggers OECD-style Local File and Master File preparation. Under Income Tax Act section 247, contemporaneous documentation is required for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions and must generally be provided within three months of a written request. A separate T106 reporting threshold generally applies when aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million. Country-by-country reporting and master-file requirements may apply to qualifying multinational groups under their separate consolidated-revenue and other statutory tests.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a general offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Canadian tax outcomes remain subject to residence, permanent-establishment, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, treaty anti-abuse, general anti-avoidance, and other applicable rules. The corporation should maintain credible commercial operations, records, personnel, decision-making, and contractual conduct consistent with its claimed functions and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing and false statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 247 permits transfer-pricing adjustments and, where the statutory threshold and conditions are met, a penalty generally equal to 10% of the relevant net adjustment. Interest, reassessment, and other penalties may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate income tax or transfer-pricing penalties solely because of office. Personal exposure may arise under statutory director-liability provisions, participation in an offence, knowing authorization, or applicable civil and criminal law.
Failure to remit non-resident withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unremitted tax, interest, and statutory penalties. Director liability may apply under Income Tax Act section 227.1, subject to its statutory defences and limitation period.

Director personal liability: Potential personal liability is limited to applicable statutory conditions; it is not automatic. Criminal liability requires the elements of the relevant offence.
Corporate income tax return and instalment defaults

Statutory sanction: Federal and Quebec late-filing penalties, prescribed interest, instalment charges, and potential reassessment apply. Deliberate evasion or false reporting can lead to substantially more severe civil or criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Director liability may arise for specified unremitted source deductions or other statutory amounts, subject to statutory conditions and defences; ordinary corporate income tax debt does not by itself create automatic personal liability.
Tax evasion, fraud, or wilfully false records

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act offences can result in fines, imprisonment, or both; a conviction may include a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, in addition to the tax, interest, and civil penalties. The precise offence and sentence depend on the facts and statute.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, participate in, or assist an offence may face personal prosecution and imprisonment. Corporate status does not shield an individual from liability for personal criminal conduct.
Undisclosed foreign assets or offshore reporting

Statutory sanction: Failure-to-file penalties can accrue under the applicable provisions; gross negligence or knowing conduct can attract higher penalties and extended reassessment exposure.

Director personal liability: The corporation and responsible individuals may face separate consequences where they personally participated in false reporting or an offence. A reporting failure does not automatically create director imprisonment or travel restrictions.
AML, proceeds of crime, and sanctions

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code, Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and sanctions legislation may provide for substantial fines, asset seizure or forfeiture, and imprisonment. Applicable obligations depend on the entity's activities and regulated status.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal civil or criminal exposure for their own participation, authorization, or legally sufficient failure to act. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty and require a separate lawful basis.
Quebec restaurant sales-tax and payroll non-compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec may assess tax, interest, and penalties; failure to remit source deductions can trigger statutory director-liability provisions and, in serious cases, prosecution.

Director personal liability: Director liability for source deductions and certain other amounts is subject to statutory conditions, due-diligence defences, and limitation periods. Personal criminal liability requires proof of the relevant offence.
