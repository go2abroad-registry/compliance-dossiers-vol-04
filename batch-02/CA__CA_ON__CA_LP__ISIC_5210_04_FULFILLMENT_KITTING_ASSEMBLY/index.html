Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canadian law. An LP is statutorily transparent; income, gains, credits and liabilities flow through to partners in accordance with the limited partnership agreement. Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies where the kitting/packaging supplier exports the tangible personal property as part of fulfilling an order, or delivers it directly to a destination outside Canada. The eligible exporter must hold documentary evidence establishing export within prescribed shipping and delivery timelines. Supplies of services related to exported goods may also qualify as zero-rated if provided directly in respect of the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or AELP equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Proof of export (port terminal receipt, carrier certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / payment evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through NETFILE or approved intermediated channels; CRA requires contemporaneous transaction records and supports e-invoicing on a voluntary basis.

CRA can reassess within four years of the original return, or indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Improper export zero-rating claims may result in penalties up to 25% of the net tax under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, additional false-statement penalties of up to 50%, interest, and criminal prosecution for knowingly issuing false tax documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized current and capital account. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible in a market-determined managed float; no central-bank approval or administrative authorization is required for capital inflows, operating remittances, profit distribution, or foreign-currency account maintenance. The Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, leaving no convertibility bar under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Income Tax Act. Profit remittance from an Ontario Limited Partnership is unrestricted as an FX matter; however, Canadian tax gates apply: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on their allocated share of the partnership's Canadian-source business income (sections 2(3) and 115), a 15% withholding may apply to income distributions credited to non-resident partners (subsection 153(1.1)), and interest paid to non-resident lenders is subject to Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) plus thin-capitalization limits (subsection 18(4), 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio). These are Income Tax Act compliance gates, not currency-convertibility restrictions. For treasury valuation and CAD-functional accounting, the Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rate is the authoritative conversion reference.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export- proceeds surrender obligation exists in Canada. Export receipts, including proceeds from cross-border fulfilment, warehousing, and kitting services performed in Canada, may be retained abroad, converted into any currency, or remitted at the exporter's discretion. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no export-licence approval step. Reporting is limited to (i) statistical classification by Canadian banks under Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), (ii) FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (IETR) for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) ordinary accounting/tax recognition of revenue under Canadian GAAP and the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act, responsible for monetary policy and financial stability; Department of Finance Canada retains policy authority over international financial transactions; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises federally regulated banks and trust companies; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for cross-border AML/CFT reporting. Canada has no exchange-control or foreign-investment-approval agency for routine commercial FX operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex is rated Medium-High as an operational payment facilitator, but Low-Medium as a primary settlement depository. Feasibility: Airwallex can support multi-currency collection from e-commerce marketplaces, invoice matching, and expedited supplier disbursements in USD, EUR, and CNY, and it aligns with the target payment rails 'ACH_Direct' and 'Airwallex'. Hard legal constraints: Airwallex (Canada) Inc. is not a Schedule I bank under the Bank Act; it cannot accept Canadian deposits as a bank, does not have direct Lynx settlement membership, and is not CDIC-insured. Funds held with Airwallex sit in omnibus accounts at a Canadian chartered bank or foreign partner bank. The entity must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and comply with PCMLTFA EFT reporting, sanctions screening, and UBO verification. Treasury design: use Airwallex as a controlled conduit for cross-border payables and marketplace receivables, but maintain all permanent balances and Lynx-capable clearing through a Schedule I bank account held by the Ontario Limited Partnership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the non-resident partner's bank to the LP's operational or capital-escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with an MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover where an intermediary is used. Payment field 70 should state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO [ONTARIO LP NAME] PER LP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]'. The receiving bank classifies the flow as direct-investment other capital for Statistics Canada ITRS; no Bank of Canada, Finance Canada, or OSFI pre-approval is required. Compliance gates: (i) wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more require the bank to file a FINTRAC IETR; (ii) equity and partnership capital contributions are not taxable and are not subject to Part XIII withholding; (iii) if the contribution is structured as a partner loan, Part XIII interest withholding (treaty-reduced) and thin-capitalization limits (ITA subsection 18(4), 1.5:1 ratio) apply, and the loan agreement must be documented; (iv) the bank will verify the LP registration, the General Partner resolution, and the UBO schedule. The Bank of Canada daily noon rate is used for CAD booking where the contribution is made in a foreign currency.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) Lynx, Payments Canada's real-time gross settlement system, for large-value CAD payments via the Schedule I bank's direct membership; (2) ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) for domestic ACH batches covering payroll, rent, and vendor settlement, aligned with the target 'ACH_Direct' rail; (3) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier wires denominated in CAD, USD, EUR, or CNY, supported by invoice references in the payment narrative; (4) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600, irrevocable and optionally confirmed, issued by the Schedule I bank when procuring kitting inputs from foreign suppliers, with the advising/confirming bank located in the supplier's jurisdiction; (5) Airwallex multi-currency payment rails for expedited small-to-mid value supplier disbursements and marketplace payouts, controlled via sub-accounts and invoice-matching tools. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario) applied to corporate partners on allocated active business income; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not separately taxed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII) on Canadian-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; for the Canadian LP, profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are taxed as partners’ shares of Canadian-source active business income and are subject to default Part XIII withholding of 25% unless the non-resident partner submits a Form NR6 guarantee/elective return and pays Part I instalments.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the partnership fiscal period end (or earlier if the corporate partner’s T2 filing due date falls first); under CRA administrative practice, a calendar 2025 fiscal period (Dec 31, 2025) requires the T5013 return by June 30, 2026. Individual T5013 slips must be delivered to each partner by the last day of February following the calendar year in which the fiscal period ends.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within six months after the corporation's taxation year end (June 30 for a Dec 31 year-end partner). Balance-due day is two months after the year-end for most non-CCPC corporate partners, or three months for a CCPC entitled to the small business deduction; instalments are due monthly if estimated tax exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 reportable transaction threshold (12-month aggregation) for Form T106 and for preparation of a Canadian Local File; a Master File is required where the MNE group has consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1 billion and the Canadian partner’s specified cross-border transactions with related non-residents exceed CAD 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no codified 'Economic Substance Regulations' (as in Caribbean/Irish contexts), but CRA heavily applies the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245), sham transaction doctrine, and DTA limitation-on-benefits provisions to disregard shell/letter-box arrangements lacking business purpose and substance. The Ontario LP must maintain substantive decision-making and operational activity in Ontario (registered office, resident agent, general partner authority, employee headcount, real warehouse/kitting facility) or risk the CRA (i) recharacterizing the LP as a conduit, (ii) attributing income to the non-resident partner under Part I, or (iii) denying treaty benefits under the Anti-Treaty-Shopping rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239: criminal fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded, jail up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2)/(3) of 50% of the understatement of tax; full reassessment plus prescribed interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and executive officers of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for any source deduction failures and can face travel bans, deportation/civil-communities restrictions, and imprisonment in fraud cases.
Failure to Withhold / Remit Part XIII on Non-Resident Partner Distributions

Statutory sanction: Payer (general partner undertaking) is personally liable for the entire unremitted Part XIII amount plus interest and a 10% s. 227(8) penalty; the liability can be imposed despite the absence of bad faith.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unwithheld source amounts under s. 227.1; the CRA may issue director liability assessments before exhausting corporate recourse and may seek a court order to seize personal assets.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance (Aggressive Tax Avoidance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 denies the DTA or Canadian-resident benefits and reassesses the non-resident partner directly; penalty under s. 163.2 up to CAD 100,000 for providing an accountant/tax opinion without reasonable basis; treaty LOB clauses may deny lower withholding; CRA may also issue a part IV/XIV.4 reassessment.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard the LP veil and treat the offshore parent as carrying on business in Canada; directors who schemed the conduit arrangement may be assessed as promoters under s. 163.2 and face criminal liability for culpable misrepresentation.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: s. 247(5) penalty equal to 10% of the net TP adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) where adjustment exceeds staff? plus recalibration of losses; inadequate contemporaneous documentation removes the burden of proof and subjects the taxpayer to a 100% gross-up of any adjustment; late T106 filings may be subject to s. 162(11.3) penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading T106/T2 declarations may face s. 163.2 penalty of up to CAD 100,000 and a 50% negligence penalty; those who are CRA 'tax service providers' can be cited for abetting fraudulent reporting.
