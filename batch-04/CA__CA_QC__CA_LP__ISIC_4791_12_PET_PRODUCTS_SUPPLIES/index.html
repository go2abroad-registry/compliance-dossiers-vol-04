Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec. Québec administers both the federal GST and the provincial QST for registrants established in Québec. Registration portals: Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' (clients.revenuquebec.ca) and CRA Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html).

E-government filing portal

(1) File the declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the 10-digit NEQ; this automatically triggers creation of the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with CRA. (2) Access the Revenu Québec business portal with the BN/NEQ and activate the GST/HST (RT) account and QST account. (3) If the partnership hires employees, also activate the payroll (RP) and QPIP accounts through the same portal. (4) The LP is registrant from the day it ceases to be a small supplier (when worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000) or from the effective date stated in its voluntary registration application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Québec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under ITA s. 96: no entity-level federal or provincial income tax is paid by the LP; each limited and general partner reports its allocated share of income or loss. For indirect taxes, the LP is itself a separate 'registrant person' under ETA s. 272.2 and the Québec QST Act, and must collect GST and QST on its domestic taxable supplies of pet products unless the small-supplier exemption applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; pass-through treatment is automatic. Relevant elections available to the LP: (i) voluntary GST/QST registration under ETA s. 240(1) to recover input tax credits on exclusively zero-rated export activities; (ii) Quick Method of Accounting election for GST/QST remittance simplification; (iii) joint election under ITA s. 96(3) to establish or change the partnership's fiscal period; (iv) hybrid mismatch neutralization election in respect of a foreign partner that treats allocated Canadian-source income as entity-level taxed income in its residence jurisdiction, per Canada's BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148 small-supplier rule; identical threshold under QST legislation). Once exceeded, the LP must register effective the day the threshold is exceeded and file returns monthly or quarterly thereafter. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold; an exporter making only zero-rated supplies may nevertheless elect voluntary registration to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: ETA s. 22 zero-rates tangible pet supplies exported by the LP where (i) ownership and possession pass to a non-resident purchaser and (ii) the goods are exported within 60 days of delivery to a common carrier. Services: offshore services relating to the exported goods or supplied to non-residents for performance entirely outside Canada may be zero-rated under ETA ss. 7–9, provided the service is not performed for a person resident in Canada. The LP must evidence a genuine cross-border sale through foreign-address shipping data, incoterms, and foreign-currency settlement rails.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (electronic G13 / Canada's International Trade Data System declaration) for shipments valued over CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification for pet products, incoterms, consignee details, and foreign payment reference
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier waybill evidencing physical export movement from Canada
  • Carrier delivery proof (tracking history, proof of delivery signature, foreign customs release or US customs entry)
  • Bank FX receipt or card-acquirer settlement record confirming inward remittance of export proceeds

Canada and Québec impose no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital fiscal code regime. CRA and Revenu Québec accept paper, PDF, EDI, and XML invoices; a Peppol-based Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard is being introduced voluntarily from 2025. Québec mandates electronic invoicing only for suppliers with annual sales of CAD 25 million or more when invoicing Québec public bodies; the subject e-commerce LP is below this threshold and has no e-invoicing obligation.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/QST and input tax credits for up to four years, with no time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, and may retroactively claw back all export zero-rating and associated refunds where documentary proof is deficient. Administrative penalties include ETA s. 280 (failure to register: greater of CAD 250 and 1% of net tax), ETA s. 285.1 (false or misleading statement: greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the understated tax), plus statutory interest, and under Québec's Tax Administration Act art. 19.1 a penalty of up to 50% for knowingly false statements. Criminal prosecution for export-credit fraud under ETA s. 327 and Québec tax legislation may result in fines, seizure of assets, imprisonment, and permanent debarment from duty-free export programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; Canada accepted the International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations on 25 March 1952, meaning the currency is convertible without balance-of-payments restrictions on either the current or capital account. No exchange-control approval, license, or administrative certificate is required for a Quebec limited partnership (LP) to receive foreign capital contributions, pay foreign suppliers, or remit profits. Profit remittance is governed by taxation instead of foreign-exchange law: distributions to non-resident limited partners trigger Canadian Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate under Income Tax Act section 212 unless a treaty rate applies (the Canada-United States treaty, for example, reduces certain dividend and interest rates to 5-15% and 0-10% respectively); a non-resident partnership may apply for a rate-reduction authority using the CRA Form NR302, and a parallel provincial withholding obligation is administered by Revenu Québec under the Taxation Act in respect of Quebec-source payments. Banks standardly require 5-10 business days for wire clearance, and the LP must produce a CRA or provincial clearance certificate or an undertaking for future remittance before the first qualifying distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation, conversion ratio, or filing deadline applicable to export proceeds in Canada. Receipts from the electronic retailing of pet supplies (ISIC 4791) may remain in CAD or in permitted foreign-currency accounts held at a Canadian Schedule I bank without time limit. The only operational requirements are: (i) every cross-border electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution; (ii) cash or certain monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); and (iii) the LP must retain underlying commercial invoices and settlement records for five years from the transaction date.

Central bank supervision: The central bank is the Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act; exchange control and prudential banking administration are jointly applied by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act. In Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) administers provincial registration of money-services businesses and over-the-counter derivatives conduct rules. The Bank of Canada does not operate exchange controls; the Minister of Finance may issue directives under the Currency Act and the Bank Act for prudential reserve purposes only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partners fund the Quebec LP using SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) in CAD or USD. The wire must specify the LP's name and NEQ in Field 50K, use Field 70 (Remittance Information) with the structured notation 'LPCAP-CA-LP-ISIC4791-<LP_NAME>-<CAPITAL_COMMITMENT_REF>', and identify the ultimate remitter rather than an intermediary. The receiving Canadian Schedule I bank will clear through its correspondent network (for USD, the U.S. correspondent ABA; for EUR, the target banking channel or SWIFT EUR payment) and must file a large EFT report with FINTRAC where the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. The treasury must have ready, within 48 hours of the credit, the executed partnership agreement, the capital-contribution notice, the ownership-organisation chart, and the tax-compliance letter, and, if the capital is to be immediately converted from USD to CAD, a 1- to 3-month forward contract should be executed at the same moment to fix the functional-currency conversion cost.Outbound disbursement is split by ticket size: (i) for supplier invoices below CAD 250,000, a SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 showing the commercial invoice number ('INV-2025-XXXX') and the country-of-origin tariff code; payments to the People's Republic of China should use the Canadian bank's direct RMB clearing line where available to avoid correspondent delay; (ii) for purchase agreements at or above CAD 250,000, an irrevocable, confirmed commercial letter of credit governed by the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600) is recommended, with transport documents, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin attached; (iii) merchant settlement to Stripe/PayPal is by ACH-style daily sweeps in CAD to a dedicated acquiring sub-ledger, then swept once daily to the master operating account. Every outbound payment of CAD 10,000 or more generates a FINTRAC EFT report; the LP's treasury must preserve a sanctions-screening waiting time of 1-2 business days for payments naming pet-care product descriptions where the underlying goods could trigger export-control classification (dual-use electronics in smart pet devices).Outright deliverable CAD/USD and CAD/CNY foreign-exchange forwards (standard maturities 1-24 months; extension by forward swap is permitted without crystallizing spot gain)FX swaps (swap of principal and interest across two currencies for maturity-matching of the capital escrow deployment)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — permitted in Canada, used almost exclusively for CAD/onshore-CNY and CAD/CNH positions where onshore deliverability is not possible; executed under ISDA definitions through the bank's offshore Renminbi desk with optionality to settle in CAD equivalentCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for the term conversion of a USD capital contribution into a CAD-equivalent liability positionVanilla European-style foreign-exchange options and zero-premium collars (regulated under the Quebec Securities Act and CSA derivatives rules; the LP must execute an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement plus a Quebec-law opinion where the partnership is a Quebec civil-law entity)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium as an ancillary settlement and payment-acquiring rail; Low as the exclusive primary treasury account. Canada does not issue a distinct electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence; a fintech provider must either hold a Canadian payment service provider registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC (mandatory since 1 June 2020), or, if operating in Quebec, hold a registration under the Act respecting money-services businesses administered by the AMF. Foreign EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business transact through custodial accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks and therefore cannot replace the domestic bank relationship for the LP's statutory escrow or for Part XIII withholding flowbacks. The e-commerce rails Stripe and PayPal, when offered as local acquiring solutions, are treated as payment facilitators; their settlement is routed into the LP's principal operating account at a federal bank, and the settlement contracts must separately certify compliance with PCMLTFA and the Quebec Consumer Protection Act in respect of prepaid card or digital-wallet overlaps.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the limited partnership level (pass-through entity); combined federal and Quebec corporate rate of 26.5% for a corporate general partner (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay dividends; for a non-resident partner, Canada imposes Part XIII withholding of 25% on the partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income when distributions are made or deemed to be made. If the partnership were a corporation, the domestic dividend withholding rate would also be 25%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Form T106) is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of transactions with non-resident related persons exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; Master File requirements apply for large multinational groups with revenues above CAD 800 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a named Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and Quebec's anti-avoidance provisions require genuine commercial substance. A limited partnership must maintain a valid partnership agreement, separate bank accounts, real business activity, and operational evidence in Quebec to preserve pass-through status, avoid GAAR reassessment, and prevent denial of treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act: fine equal to 50%–200% of tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment up to five years, plus civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and compounded interest

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unremitted source deductions and deemed trust amounts; personal criminal liability, travel bans, property seizure, and restitution orders are available where the individual knowingly participated in evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec may invoke GAAR to recharacterize the partnership structure, deny treaty benefits, reassess partners on a look-through basis, impose a 10% avoidance penalty plus interest, and the Quebec enterprise registrar may strike the registration for inactivity

Director personal liability: Controlling partners, especially the general partner, risk personal assessments for taxes, penalties, and interest; courts may issue injunctions freezing bank accounts and order asset seizures against individuals who deliberately maintain a sham partnership
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 for businesses and CAD 500,000 for individuals; FinTRAC may revoke an MSB registration, and Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers may impose additional administrative sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for AML failures if they knowingly or negligently caused non-compliance; courts may prohibit them from acting as directors and impose criminal imprisonment of up to five years for knowingly being a party to a money laundering offence
