Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) for the federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account; Revenu Quebec (Quebec Revenue Agency) - clic RQ portal and Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ) for the Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and QST registration.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership (LP) carrying on a virtual-office/CMRA service in Quebec: (1) appoint a designated representative or agent in Canada; (2) obtain the federal BN using form RC1 or via CRA's Business Registration Online, including the GST/HST program account (RT prefix); (3) register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec to obtain the NEQ and satisfy mandatory legal publicity (the statutory license for carrying on an enterprise in Quebec) using forms RQ-1/RQ-2 and, where acting through an agent, the power of attorney form LM-101-V; (4) register for QST with Revenu Quebec through clic RQ, obtaining the TQ account, because the LP makes taxable supplies in Quebec; (5) file annual GST34 / TPZ-14 returns online and, as a fiscally transparent partnership, file T2042 / TP-600 partnership information returns so each partner (resident or non-resident) is allocated income; (6) non-resident partners should obtain Canadian individual/corporate TINs where required and apply under treaty for withholding rate reductions; FINTRAC registration is also assessed because virtual office services may be incidental to money transmission or specified services.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident limited partnership (LP) carrying on a virtual-office/CMRA service in Quebec: (1) appoint a designated representative or agent in Canada; (2) obtain the federal BN using form RC1 or via CRA's Business Registration Online, including the GST/HST program account (RT prefix); (3) register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec to obtain the NEQ and satisfy mandatory legal publicity (the statutory license for carrying on an enterprise in Quebec) using forms RQ-1/RQ-2 and, where acting through an agent, the power of attorney form LM-101-V; (4) register for QST with Revenu Quebec through clic RQ, obtaining the TQ account, because the LP makes taxable supplies in Quebec; (5) file annual GST34 / TPZ-14 returns online and, as a fiscally transparent partnership, file T2042 / TP-600 partnership information returns so each partner (resident or non-resident) is allocated income; (6) non-resident partners should obtain Canadian individual/corporate TINs where required and apply under treaty for withholding rate reductions; FINTRAC registration is also assessed because virtual office services may be incidental to money transmission or specified services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. Available elections include: ITA s.249.1(3) fiscal-period election for the LP to use an off-calendar year-end; ITA s.216(1) election for a non-resident partner receiving Canadian-source rental or service income to file a Canadian return instead of being subject to full Part XIII withholding; treaty-based re-characterization of the LP as a non-entity (component member) under the Canada-US Treaty, with Form NR301/TP-1016R to claim reduced withholding; GST/HST joint election under ETA s.273 for closely related entities, and the corresponding QST election under QSTA s.557.2 where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters for federal GST/HST small-supplier exemption; for QST, CAD 50,000 over four consecutive quarters where the supplier is not resident in Quebec and has no establishment there. Once the virtual-office and mail-scanning fees exceed either threshold, the LP must register with CRA and Revenu Quebec; registration can be voluntary below the thresholds but is advisable to claim input tax refunds (ITC/QST-R) on start-up costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/HST/QST applies to goods physically exported from Canada by the LP and delivered outside Canada to a non-resident (Schedule VI Part I of the Excise Tax Act; QSTA s.107.1). For services such as virtual-office mail scanning supplied to a non-resident client, zero-rating applies under the offshore services rules of ETA Schedule VI Part V (s.7 and s.9) and equivalent QSTA provisions where the service is provided otherwise than to persons resident in Canada, is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and is for the recipient's use or enjoyment outside Canada. The LP must establish the non-resident status of each client (residence certificate, jurisdiction of incorporation) and demonstrate the service is not taxable in Canada under the place-of-supply rules; zero-rated export supplies also preserve ITC/QST-R eligibility for Canadian inputs.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or CERS e-certified report) for any tangible goods shipped; for mail-scanning services, the equivalent service contract, statement of work, and scan/delivery activity log evidencing offshore provision for the non-resident recipient
  • Transport document (bill of lading, air waybill, or courier receipt) where goods are exported; for digital mail-scanning services, technology logs documenting server location outside Canada or delivery outside Canada
  • Inward remittance and foreign-exchange evidence (bank SWIFT MT103 or Canadian Schedule I bank exchange record) proving proceeds are received from the non-resident, retained with the LP's non-resident status file (residence certificate, Form NR6, CRS/KYC records)

Canada and Quebec have no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (NETFILE/XML via CRA) for registrants with over CAD 1,000,000 in annual revenue and for QST large enterprises through clic RQ. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing follows the pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework. Invoices must meet ETA s.221 and QSTA s.444 requirements: vendor name, BN and QST registration number, date, description, consideration, and tax charged. Electronic invoices and source documents must be retained for six years.

CRA and Revenu Quebec can reassess within 4 years of a normal assessment, and without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud exists. If zero-rating is denied, the LP is liable for 5% GST plus 9.975% QST on the full consideration, with daily compound interest. Additional penalties include: 5% late-filing penalty plus 1% per month (max 10%) under ETA s.280(1); 25% penalty for false statements or omissions (s.285(1)(a)); 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.285(1)(c); and for willful evasion under s.327(1), a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment. Revenu Quebec applies mirror sanctions under QSTA ss.1021.13-1031 and may cancel QST registration, deny refunds, and refer criminal prosecutions in export-fraud schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control approvals, no counterparty or tenor restrictions, and no restrictions on profit repatriation apply to a Quebec limited partnership. Remittances of Canadian-source business income to a foreign limited partner are not FX-gated, but the partnership must withhold 15% of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income under subsection 227(8.3) of the Income Tax Act as prepayment of Part I tax, and 25% under Part XIII (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% to 0% under the Canada-US and Canada-UK Treaties where applicable) on interest, rents, royalties, and management-type fees remitted to non-resident partners; NR4 information returns and accompanying remittances are due to the Canada Revenue Agency. Current-account payments for the CMRA services (virtual-office and mail-scanning subscription fees remitted offshore) are unrestricted and require no governmental permit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada abolished statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. A Quebec LP may retain service and export receipts in any freely convertible currency in Canada or abroad. However, every Canadian financial institution must report cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC, individuals and entities must declare physical currency of C$10,000 or more at the border under the CBSA regime, and all remittances must be screened against Canada's sanctions framework (Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act, and Criminal Code terrorist-financing lists).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), the central monetary authority established under the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange-control administration; the Currency Act establishes a free-floating Canadian dollar. Cross-border currency transaction surveillance is effected by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for physical currency reporting. Prudential supervision of domestic systemically important banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM as primary treasury domicile; MEDIUM as auxiliary collection rail. Canada does not operate an 'electronic money institution' (EMI) licensing category. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) may transmit funds and issue payment instructions through licensed Canadian partners, but they cannot open deposit accounts in the LP's name, issue Canadian institution/transit numbers, or provide Schedule I banking services without a Canadian bank charter. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force since November 2024, supervised by the Bank of Canada), payment service providers — including Stripe Canada in respect of the target rails (Stripe Recurring, Credit Card) — must register; using such providers to collect subscription revenue is structurally viable, but the LP's statutory treasury, capital accounts, and Part XIII withholding obligations must remain anchored at a Canadian Schedule I bank or at Desjardins.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners are executed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank transfers) to the LP's Canadian account at National Bank of Canada or Desjardins. The wire must carry the LP's full legal name and NEQ number, and the remitter must complete the purpose-of-payment field with clear capital-contribution language (e.g., 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' in SWIFT Field 70) consistent with the receiving bank's balance-of-payments classification and the partnership's capital-call notice. The LP must maintain the partnership agreement, capital-call notices, subscription documents, and the partner's source-of-funds evidence; the Canadian bank will verify these documents for FINTRAC EFT reporting (C$10,000-or-more threshold) and sanctions screening. No Bank of Canada authorization is required. Foreign-currency receipts may be held as-is, converted through a spot FX trade booked with the bank's treasury desk, or hedged by forward; booking records must be preserved to support the LP's capital ledger and to preclude recharacterization as taxable income.Domestic vendor payments clear through Payments Canada (the Automated Clearing Settlement System, ACSS, and the Real-Time Rail, RTR). Cross-border supplier disbursements use SWIFT MT103 wires containing the beneficiary's SWIFT/BIC and account details (or US ABA routing for USD). Commercial letters of credit, issued under UCP600 by the LP's bank, are used only for physical goods purchases (e.g., scanning hardware or paper) and require presentation of conforming invoice, packing list, and transport documents. The target merchant-collection rails (Stripe Recurring and credit card) settle through Stripe's Canadian licensed payment entity into the LP's CAD account via ACH/EFT credit. All outbound wires must undergo OFAC/CFT and Canada sanctions screening. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Quebec provincial (11.5%) and federal (15%) corporate income tax rate for a general limited partnership deemed to be a corporation for Canadian tax purposes, where applicable; otherwise, the LP is fiscally transparent and tax is levied at the partner level. For non-resident corporate partners receiving Canadian-source income through the partnership, the applicable federal Part I rate is 15% plus Quebec provincial levy on allocated Quebec-source income, subject to treaty relief.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For a Canadian limited partnership, profit distributions are not dividends; distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25% (federal) plus Quebec withholding of 5.75% (total 30.75%) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Under the Canada-United States tax treaty, the rate is 15% on actual 'dividends' but for partnership distributions the rate depends on the character of the underlying income; business profits earned through a permanent establishment are taxed on a net basis (Part I) and not subject to withholding. For a pure passive distribution of non-business income, the treaty rate is 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Canadian transfer pricing rules (section 247 of the Income Tax Act), contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File) is required for any transaction with a non-resident non-arm's length party where the total value of the transactions exceeds CAD 1 million (aggregate for all such transactions in a taxation year). For a partnership, the threshold is applied at the partner level for transactions allocated to each partner. Revenu Québec adopts the same threshold for Quebec tax purposes (CAD 1 million). If the aggregate cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million, a Country-by-Country Report may be required for an ultimate parent entity with consolidated revenue over EUR 750 million; however, a virtual office/CMRA entity typically falls below this. Master File is mandatory for transactions above CAD 5 million; Local File is mandatory above CAD 1 million. Additionally, the CRA form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) must be filed for each transaction in excess of CAD 100,000 with a non-resident related party.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of Quebec do not exist as a separate code; Canada applies the 'general anti-avoidance rule' (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the 'economic substance' doctrine under Quebec's anti-avoidance provision (Tax Administration Act, section 1079.2). A virtual office/mail scanning LP must demonstrate real economic substance: physical office in Quebec, employees engaged in mail reception, scanning, data processing, and client communication, substantive local decision-making by the general partner, no artificial transactions to shift income to zero-tax jurisdictions. The CRA's CRA Folio S4-F9-C1 and the OECD's BEPS Action 5 minimum standard on harmful tax practices are applicable. If the LP is a 'shell' with no employees, no physical presence, and only passive mail forwarding, the CRA may apply GAAR to reallocate income to the actual controller, impose a 10% penalty on the tax benefit, and deny treaty benefits under the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) package. For Quebec, the 'substance' requirement also affects the entitlement to the lower 11.5% provincial rate, as a corporation or partner must not be a 'specified corporate partner' without substantial Canadian presence. A partnership must also comply with the Canada Revenue Agency's beneficial ownership reporting requirements under the Canada Business Corporations Act (as amended) if it has a federal incorporation; under Quebec's registry of entreprises, a declaration of registrant (immatriculation) must list the general partner and any limited partners with 25% or more of the partnership, and the registry is publicly accessible.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Failure to Report Partnership Income

Statutory sanction: Under the Canadian Income Tax Act (sections 238 and 239), tax evasion is an indictable offence punishable by a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years. Revenu Québec under the Tax Administration Act (sections 47 and 69) imposes a civil penalty of 50% of the extra tax for gross negligence and up to 100% for wilful evasion, plus daily interest at prescribed rates (currently 10% per annum). Conviction for tax evasion also results in publication under the CRA's 'Name and Shame' program.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, withheld taxes, GST/QST, and employee source deductions under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and section 498 of the Quebec Taxation Act. For an LP with no corporate general partner, the limited partners who participate in management lose limited liability and become jointly and severally liable for partnership debts and penalties. Personal liability extends to civil restitution orders, a travel ban under the Income Tax Act section 238(2) (suspension of passport for non-payment of taxes exceeding CAD 10,000), and imprisonment for up to five years.
Thin Capitalization / Excessive Interest Deductions

Statutory sanction: Under section 18(4) of the Income Tax Act, interest deductions are denied in proportion to the debt that exceeds 1.5 times the 'equity' of the partnership or partner. A penalty of 10% of the disallowed amount under section 247(3) applies if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to document the transfer pricing. Revenu Québec may additionally reassess and impose a 20% penalty under section 1078.2. The CRA may apply the 'earnings stripping' rules under section 18(5) for excessive interest paid by a Canadian entity to a specified non-resident shareholder, disallowing the interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for the reassessed tax, interest, and penalties only if the underpayment was a result of a misrepresentation that they knew or should have known. Criminal liability for counselling non-arm's length interest stripping can result in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA can deny the treaty benefits and recharacterize the Canadian entity as a permanent establishment of the U.S. parent, resulting in Part I tax at 15% (federal plus 11.5% Quebec) on a deemed net income computed by the CRA, plus a 10% GAAR penalty, and gross-negligence penalties of 50%. Revenu Québec can dissolve the registered partnership for failure to maintain the registered office in Quebec. Under Quebec's Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, a partnership that fails to disclose the ultimate beneficiary may be struck from the register of enterprises (Registraire des entreprises), and its directors may be personally liable for debts up to CAD 10,000 per month of unlawful operation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner who signed the false declaration of registrant may be fined up to CAD 5,000 (per offence) and imprisonment for up to two years for perjury under the Quebec Legal Publicity Act. The CRA's GAAR can impose liability on the partnership itself, and if the partnership lacks assets, the general partner and its directors are liable to the extent of assets; limited partners may be held liable if they acted as agents of the partnership or allowed their names to be used in the business.
AML / Money Laundering through Mail Scanning and Virtual Office

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an unregistered MSB can be subject to an administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 for a first offence and up to CAD 2,000,000 for a subsequent offence for an entity; individuals are liable to fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. If the business is not an MSB but a 'transaction-based' business, may still be a non-financial designated business; failure to report a suspicious transaction (STR) can lead to a penalty of up to CAD 500,000. FINTRAC can publish or suspend the business.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for FINTRAC penalties if they 'direct, authorize, or participate' in the contravention. Seizure of corporate bank accounts, travel bans, and extradition for money laundering under the Criminal Code (sections 462.31) can result in imprisonment of up to 10 years for individuals, and if convicted, the court may order restitution to victims and forfeiture of property. Directors of the corporate general partner may be subject to proceeds-of-crime confiscation orders.
Failure to Register or Hold Statutory Licence for Commercial Mail Receiving Agency

Statutory sanction: Under Quebec's Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, any person carrying on an enterprise in Quebec must be registered; failure carries a fine of CAD 300 to CAD 5,000 for each day of non-compliance for a legal person, and the right to institute legal proceedings is suspended. Under the Canada Post Corporation Act, using a post office box or mail forwarding address to misrepresent a place of business is an offence punishable by up to CAD 10,000 per offence for a corporation and CAD 2,000 for an individual. Revenu Québec may deny tax deductions for unregistered enterprises.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, or agents who knowingly authorized or acquiesced in the violation are liable to the penalties, and may be subject to directors' liability for any taxes, charges, or penalties under the Legal Publicity Act, including civil liability for obligations incurred while the entity was not registered.
