Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV / FTA / AFC), portal: https://www.esty.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are required to register with the competent cantonal tax authority for corporate income tax, VAT, and social security withholding through the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's central business register (UID Register). The procedure involves: (1) obtaining a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the official UID portal or the commercial register entry; (2) if VAT taxable, submitting an online VAT registration form (e-services) to the ESTV with statutory corporate documents and place-of-management confirmation; (3) appointing a Swiss fiscal representative if the foreign entity has no fixed place of business in Switzerland, and providing proof of foreign VAT registration; (4) receiving a separate VAT number (in addition to the UID) for indirect tax compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss taxation operates under territorial and prorated taxation at the cantonal and federal levels. Available elections include: group taxation under the Swiss Tax Reform (Mantelerlass) for Swiss resident companies forming a tax group; election for partial taxation of capital gains on substantial shareholdings; election for deemed liquidation on relocation; and VAT group registration (Art. 14 VAT Act) allowing closely linked persons to form a single VAT entity. Hybrid mismatch elections do not exist under Swiss domestic law, but Switzerland follows OECD BEPS recommendations, with hybrid financial instrument rules limited to abuse-tested provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 for main VAT rate; CHF 100,000 for reduced rate supplies; lower thresholds apply to certain services including digital services from non-resident suppliers (CHF 100,000 worldwide turnover for electronic services)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for zero-rated VAT where proof of export is retained and the goods are physically delivered to a foreign destination within the statutory deadline. B2B exports of services to foreign customers may be reverse-charged to the recipient where the recipient is a taxable person outside Switzerland; certain direct digital services to non-taxable persons outside Switzerland are outside Swiss VAT scope. Parcel consolidation hub services performed in Switzerland for overseas customers are generally place-of-supply in Switzerland and subject to VAT at the standard rate, unless the services constitute directly connected to exported goods and the export requirement is fully documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollausfuhranmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading or similar transport document evidencing exit from Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or banking evidence of foreign payment
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Insurance certificate (where applicable)
  • Sales contract or purchase order

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate. E-invoicing is optional for B2B under Swiss Peppol/XML standards; QR-billing is mandatory for all Swiss invoices in CHF or EUR payable via QR IBAN. No digital stamping and no continuous transaction controls are imposed by the ESTV.

VAT refund claims for export zero-rating are subject to retrospective audits by the ESTV and cantonal tax authorities. Failure to produce complete export evidence within the audit period triggers reversal of the input VAT deduction, additional VAT assessments, default interest of up to 4% per annum, and administrative fines up to CHF 100,000 under Art. 184 VAT Act. Intentional fraud with forged export documents constitutes tax evasion and may lead to criminal prosecution, confiscation of proceeds, and unlimited civil liability plus exclusion from export refund regimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) for both current and capital account transactions. There is no exchange control law, no prior authorization for foreign direct investment, and no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, interest, or liquidation proceeds. However, dividend remittances are subject to Swiss anticipatory withholding tax at 35%, which may be reduced or refunded under applicable double taxation treaties and qualified ownership criteria; ordinary Swiss corporate taxation is levied at federal, cantonal, and communal level. The SNB may apply negative interest on sight deposits as a monetary-policy tool, but this does not impair convertibility or payment mobility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Entities freely retain receipts in original foreign currency or convert to CHF/EUR/USD subject to normal bank settlement. The only regulatory obligations are statistical data filings to the SNB for balance-of-payments purposes, tax reporting under the Swiss Tax Ordinance, and VAT considerations under the Federal Act on Value Added Tax; no exchange-control clearance or foreign-exchange licensing is triggered by export settlements.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank, SNB), acting in coordination with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking/AML oversight and the Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA) for withholding tax and reporting duties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs without FINMA banking or fintech license cannot validly serve as the primary treasury settlement account of a Swiss-resident Aktiengesellschaft. Swiss banking law requires licensed banks or FINMA-authorized fintech deposit institutions to accept funds from the public and provide payment accounts. Global EMIs such as Airwallex may be used as auxiliary collection and FX-routing rails, provided the EMI relies on European or local partner-bank sponsorship and maintains segregated client funds; however, the core CHF clearing and regulatory-settlement backbone of the parcel hub must reside with a systemically relevant Swiss bank or Swiss cantonal bank to satisfy statutory audit, creditor protection, and FINMA expectations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct tax on the after-tax net profit (equivalent to approx. 4.4% on a pre-tax basis); combined federal, cantonal and communal headline rate in the Canton of Zug approx. 11.9% for fully taxable companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) levied on every dividend distribution by the CH AG under Art. 1 VStG; the 35% is a cash-withholding cost that is fully recoverable by the shareholder only via treaty refund or the information-reporting procedure for foreign shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within six months after the statutory balance-sheet date; for a strict calendar-year AG the standard deadline is 30 June of the following year. Written extension requests are routinely granted by the Zug Tax Administration; a formal summons after expiry triggers an estimated assessment.

Box 1 (Ertrag / Turnover) Gross revenue from cross-border parcel consolidation, sorting, direct-injection linehaul and co-loading services, net of VAT-relevant items and foreign permanent establishment turnover. Foreign-shareholder groups must convert all foreign-currency revenue at the year-end mid-rate and attach a schedule separating Swiss-source turnover from turnover re-invoiced to the foreign parent outside the Swiss tax net. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.
Box 19 (Reingewinn / Net Profit) Net commercial profit before tax taken from the statutory financial statements prepared under Swiss Code of Obligations Art. 957 et seq.; includes parcel-handling margin, linehaul income and ancillary logistics fees. Non-resident shareholders do not complete this line personally; the Swiss AG reports the full domestic net profit, while the foreign principal must separately attribute any Swiss permanent establishment results to the return. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.
Box 22 (Steuerlich massgebender Gewinn) Tax-adjusted profit: add-back of non-deductible expenses such as 50% of attendance fees for board meetings, excessive director compensation, and hidden profit distributions; deduction of loss carryforwards, IP box relief, participation relief and NID. The AG with a non-resident parent must reconcile every add-back with a transfer-pricing adjustment file; intercompany recharges from the foreign parent are entered on the same line only if arm's length. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.
Box 25 (Beteiligungsabzug / Participation Relief) 100% exclusion of dividends and qualifying capital gains on shares held at 10% or more of the target's capital for more than one year; the relief is computed on the net dividend after foreign withholding taxes. The foreign parent must submit a share-register excerpt evidencing the holding threshold and holding period, even where the parent itself is not a Swiss taxpayer, to justify the Swiss AG's deduction. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.
Box 27 (IP-Box-Abzug / License Box) Deduction of up to 90% of qualifying net royalty and intangible income (Zug rate) derived from self-developed patents, software and similar rights, conditional on patent registration and the presence of R&D substance in Switzerland. Where the IP is owned by the non-resident parent, the license box deduction is unavailable to the Swiss parcel AG; the Swiss entity may only deduct arm's length royalties paid for the use of the IP. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.
Box 30 (Eigenkapital / Taxable Equity) Declaration of taxable equity for the Zug cantonal capital tax (approx. 0.08% statutory rate), based on the opening balance-sheet equity less qualifying participation holdings and IP box assets. Non-resident paid-in capital is only taxable to the extent employed in the Swiss business; shareholders should include the back-to-back loan structure proving that shareholder debt is not disguised equity. Late filing triggers a procedural fine (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000 per Art. 174 DBG and default interest at 3.25% per annum on unpaid amounts; deliberately deficient returns attract a surcharge of 100% of evaded tax, rising to 150% for fraudulent false documentation; repeated defaults can lead to enforced liquidation of the AG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss TP documentation rules (effective for tax periods from 2020): mandatory Master File and Local File are triggered when the multinational group records consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more, or where the Swiss entity's intercompany transactions exceed the de-minimis substantive thresholds per transaction type; the CH parcel AG must in all cases maintain a comparability and functional analysis under the OECD TP Guidelines 2022 for transactions with the foreign parent, including sorting fees, linehaul rates, and back-office recharges.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not enact Caribbean-style ESR regulations, but substance is enforced through the effective-place-of-management doctrine under Art. 50 DBG: the AG's strategic decisions, board meetings and operational management must be situated in Zug or the profits are reattributed to the foreign principal; the MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) denies treaty benefits where the Swiss structure lacks genuine economic activity; the license box additionally requires autonomous R&D, local employees and management oversight; banks and tax administrations apply substance checks when the AG claims DTA refunds on the 35% anticipatory tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Direct and Cantonal Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Per Art. 175 DBG: a surcharge equal to 100% of the evaded tax, increased up to 150% where deliberate fraud or false documents are used; repayment of the evaded tax with default interest at 3.25% p.a.; publication of the penalty decision; indefinite suspension of loss carryforward; in severe cases criminal imprisonment of responsible individuals for up to three years under Art. 186 DBG.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign false tax returns face personal fines, confiscation of private assets, travel bans via international judicial assistance, and imprisonment up to three years; the corporate veil is pierced under Art. 754 CO where the board caused loss to the company, making each director jointly and severally liable for restitution to shareholders and creditors.
Failure to Account for 35% Verrechnungssteuer

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Art. 187 VStG: fine up to three times the tax amount evaded; forcible seizure of bank accounts and enforcement of the unpaid balance with interest; denial of the refund for the foreign shareholder; in cases of false refund claims, an additional surcharge of up to 80% of the claimed amount.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally personally liable for the anticipatory tax withheld or withholdable; personal assets may be attached; individuals may face imprisonment and travel restrictions, and after a criminal conviction they may be barred from acting as directors of any Swiss company.
AML and Money Laundering Breaches (GwG / StGB)

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to CHF 5 million under Art. 102 StGB, individual criminal fine up to CHF 1 million, confiscation of the laundered assets and profits; administrative revocation of any financial-market license or association status; AML registration cancellation by the Federal Supervisory Body.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently process tainted funds face personal imprisonment up to three years for money laundering under Art. 305bis StGB, restitution obligations, asset freezing orders, travel restrictions, and director disqualification following the publication of the sanction in the Swiss Official Gazette.
Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance (MLI-PPT)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test with retroactive revocation of refund claims and default interest; reclassification of the AG as a shell company leading to reattribution of profits to the foreign parent; cancellation of the license box relief; anti-abuse surcharge up to 150% of the evaded tax; the entity may be struck from the Commercial Register if no real seat can be proven.

Director personal liability: Directors maintaining a paper-only company may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate debts under veil-piercing jurisprudence, face administrative fines, and risk driving-ban measures; if the nominal seat is permanently ineffective, bankruptcy of the AG triggers personal liability examinations of the board, asset seizure and possible extradition in international fraud proceedings.
