Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (BRO); in Quebec, additionally Revenu Québec (RQ) through the Registraire des entreprises / Services Québec portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign corporation obtains a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online using either a Canadian-based director or a certified agent under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act; concurrently registers with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial payroll accounts via the province's enterprise registration system; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Quebec may have simplified QST registration but remain subject to federal Part I filing obligations

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through eligibility for a business corporation; non-resident shareholders subject to Canadian branch tax and withholding regimes; no territorial classification for active business income earned in Canada — Foreign corporation obtains a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online using either a Canadian-based director or a certified agent under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act; concurrently registers with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial payroll accounts via the province's enterprise registration system; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Quebec may have simplified QST registration but remain subject to federal Part I filing obligations

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through eligibility for a business corporation; non-resident shareholders subject to Canadian branch tax and withholding regimes; no territorial classification for active business income earned in Canada

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; available statutory elections include section 85 rollover, section 219 deemed-residence election, section 216(4) rental income election, functional currency election under section 261, GST/QST quick-method and streamlined-rebate elections, and hybrid-mismatch limitation rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 (Budget 2021 implementation)

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four trailing calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST and QST registration become mandatory

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of dental zirconia blocks exported by the manufacturer qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI Part V (section 1) and corresponding Quebec QST zero-rating provisions; the exporter must establish that title and risk of loss passed to a non-resident purchaser and that the goods were physically delivered from Canada via an export transaction; closely related services directly invoiced with the export goods are also eligible

  • Customs Commercial Invoice and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) cargo-control documentation
  • Export Declaration (B13A / CAED; or ACI eManifest filing where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or sea/air waybill evidencing departure from Canada
  • certified freight-forwarder or customs-broker certificate of export
  • Inward remittance / SWIFT-wire or TT transfer records as proof of consideration in settlement of the sale

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing obligation is currently in force; CRA and Revenu Québec accept conventional paper/PDF and optional PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices; electronic issuance is permissible but the issuer must preserve an auditable chronological set of data and source documents for six years

Canada imposes no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation ceiling for exporters; nevertheless, CRA and Revenu Québec retain a six-year audit look-back under which any export zero-rating claim lacking documentary substantiation will be reversed, resulting in reassessed GST/QST, daily compounding interest under ETA section 280.1 and QST equivalent provisions, penalty equal to 50% of the grossed-up understated tax under ITA section 163(2) / ETA section 280, additional Revenu Québec negligence penalties, and possible civil fraud prosecution under the Criminal Code for intentional misrepresentation

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free current account convertibility regime under IMF Article VIII, permitting unrestricted repatriation of export proceeds, royalty payments, and profit remittances for manufacturing corporations. Capital account transactions remain subject to prior review by Finance Canada or OSFI for investments exceeding CAD 100 million or involving certain real estate or portfolio securities. Quebec-based dental ceramic manufacturers face no material convertibility barriers for core trade settlements or intercompany funding when supported by verifiable commercial documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in offshore or domestic foreign currency accounts and convert to CAD at prevailing market rates via authorized dealers. All transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency Customs and Trade Online (CBSA CTOL) system. Profit remittances to foreign parents require supporting documentation for Income Tax Act compliance but are not subject to mandatory conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for regulated fintech platforms including Wise Business and Mercury for cross-border payables and receivables. Legal constraints arise from FINTRAC registration requirements for money services businesses and Quebec provincial AML rules limiting offshore digital EMIs. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 500,000 for intangibles (per transaction or aggregate); Master File required for transactions exceeding CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits. A corporation must have real business operations, employees, and assets in Canada to claim treaty relief and avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties: 100% of tax amount plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for civil restitution and criminal fines; personal assets may be seized.
AML

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals including fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be fined and imprisoned; personal liability for non-compliance with AML obligations.
