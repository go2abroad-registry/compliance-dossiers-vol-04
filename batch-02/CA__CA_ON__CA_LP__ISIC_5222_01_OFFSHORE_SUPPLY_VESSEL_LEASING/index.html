Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Federal tax administration, including GST/HST, customs duties, and business number registration; Ontario Ministry of Finance — Provincial tax and benefit administration, with the HST provincial component collected by CRA under the HST harmonization arrangement. Principal registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) / My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) through the CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'. The partnership registers for a GST/HST program account if it makes taxable supplies in Canada (other than zero-rated supplies) in excess of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits and to charge GST/HST on taxable supplies. The BN is the statutory corporate and partnership tax identifier; Ontario does not issue a separate provincial TIN for HST purposes, as the HST is collected by the CRA on behalf of the Province of Ontario. For excise and customs matters, the partnership may also require a Canadian Customs Business Number and a registered importer/exporter account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A limited partnership is tax-transparent under the Canadian Income Tax Act and the Ontario Taxation Act; income, gains, and losses flow through to partners in proportion to their partnership interests. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a separate person from its partners. Non-resident partners are subject to Canadian tax only if the partnership carries on business in Canada and the partner is a member of the partnership. The partnership is not subject to entity-level federal or Ontario corporate income tax unless a specific election applies to treat it as a corporation for foreign tax purposes under domestic law.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. A Canadian limited partnership is pass-through by default; no election exists to convert it to a taxable entity for domestic purposes. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) an election under subsection 97(2) of the Income Tax Act to defer recognition of gain or loss on property contributed to the partnership; (ii) a partnership election under section 10 of the Income Tax Act with respect to inventory of a business; (iii) for GST/HST purposes, a partnership and a partner may jointly elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to deem certain supplies by the partner to be made by the partnership; and (iv) foreign tax and hybrid mismatch elections may be required under the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 2 framework, as reflected in the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules in sections 94.8 to 94.26 of the Income Tax Act. No domestic election permits a limited partnership to be taxed as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under section 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act), computed on taxable supplies made in Canada, other than zero-rated supplies, in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident engaged in commercial activity in Canada must register when the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods (including a marine vessel) that is exported from Canada by the supplier, or supplied to a non-resident for removal from Canada, may be zero-rated if the supplier retains satisfactory documentary evidence of the export and the goods are actually exported within a reasonable time. For an offshore supply vessel leased by a Canadian limited partnership to a non-resident owner/operator, the lease supply may qualify for zero-rating as (i) a supply of goods for export, (ii) a supply of a vessel for use outside Canada, or (iii) a supply of a transportation service if the vessel is used predominantly in international transportation. If the vessel is leased for use within Canadian territorial waters, continental shelf operations, or in the exploration/exploitation of Canadian natural resources, the supply will generally be subject to HST at 13%. The supplier must obtain a documented declaration from the non-resident recipient confirming that the vessel is being exported or used exclusively outside Canada, and must retain export documentation and evidence of consideration conclusively linking the supply to export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or EDI approved exporter report)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill showing exporter, foreign consignee, vessel identification, loading port, and foreign discharge port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank documentation confirming receipt of foreign currency consideration within the prescribed repatriation period
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Booking confirmation or charter party agreement in case of vessel leasing

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices for GST/HST purposes. Electronic invoices and supporting records are accepted provided they are intelligible, reliable, and retain data integrity. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, and all registrants may file electronically through the CRA's My Business Account. Ontario does not impose separate e-invoicing clearance obligations beyond the federal electronic filing regime.

If the CRA determines that a zero-rated export claim is unsupported or that the vessel was not genuinely exported, the supply is reassessed as taxable at 13% HST, and the partnership is liable for the tax plus statutory interest from the original filing date. A false statement or omission on a GST/HST return that reduces the net tax may attract a civil penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the amount sought to be evaded. In cases of wilful evasion or fraudulent export zero-rating, criminal prosecution may result in fines of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also audit the partnership's export documentation and, under the joint and several liability provisions applicable to partnerships, any partner may be assessed for the partnership's tax, penalties, and interest. Retrospective clawbacks can extend for a statutory limitation period generally four years after the filing date, or ten years in the case of misrepresentation attributable to neglect or carelessness.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, so no exchange-control approval or profit-remittance licence exists for any current or capital transaction. This Ontario Limited Partnership (CA_LP) may receive equity contributions from non-resident partners and remit charter-hire profits or partnership distributions in CAD or USD without Bank of Canada or OSFI clearance; the only legal gates are Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on non-resident distributions (treaty-reduced where applicable), FINTRAC reporting obligations, and Canadian/OFCAC sanctions screening. CAD/USD conversion executes at interbank spreads with no surrender ratio or conversion quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable: Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for export or charter proceeds. Offshore supply-vessel charter revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD in a Canadian bank account. The sole compliance requirement is FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers and cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more, with the Canadian financial institution filing the report automatically; the bank will require a stated commercial purpose in every wire instruction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control bureau. FX administration is coordinated among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential FX risk, FINTRAC for AML and cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions, and the Department of Finance Canada for Currency Act administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI providers are not authorised deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot hold regulated maritime escrow, issue standby or commercial letters of credit, or provide fiduciary clearing services. A foreign EMI must register with FINTRAC as a money-services business and, if engaged in retail payments, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; it may serve only as an ancillary low-value collection or disbursement rail and may never replace a Schedule I bank account for statutory escrow or primary treasury operations.
