Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (Income Tax Act s. 96: a partnership is a computation vehicle, not a taxpayer; each partner reports its allocated share). Territorial_Scope: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source business income. For GST/HST purposes the LP is a separate 'person' and registrant; no Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows legal form. Available elections: (i) the s. 96(3) Income Tax Act 'alternative method' election for corporate partners to align partnership fiscal-period income with their taxation year; (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration for small suppliers (Form RC1/BRO) to obtain input tax credits; (iii) hybrid mismatch framework (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.5 and complementary provisions) overrides flow-through outcomes for structured cross-border hybrid financing/licensing arrangements and carries no opt-out election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully freely convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for remittances of profits, royalties, dividends, or interest, and no currency rationing or priority lists. Non-resident investors may repatriate capital and profits without material currency-based restrictions; the outstanding compliance friction is limited to tax withholding (Part XIII for non-residents) and AML/ATF reporting of large electronic transfers (over CAD 10,000). Inward investments that acquire Canadian control are notifiable/reviewable under the Investment Canada Act only if above the financial thresholds, not under foreign-exchange rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline. Export receipts in any foreign currency may be retained in foreign currency accounts, converted freely, or used to settle overseas payables. The only statutory requirements arise under the PCMLTFA: financial institutions must file Large Cash Transaction Reports and Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, and must adhere to the FATF Travel Rule provisions for inter-institution transfers. No central-bank surrender ratio or analogous capital-control obligation applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is distributed among the Bank of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the PCMLTFA. The Investment Canada Act (via ISED) oversees foreign investments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Canada is not an EU EMI passporting jurisdiction; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) must either obtain a Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC or partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank through a paid-agent arrangement to access domestic clearing. An EMI with MSB registration may hold a settlement account at a Canadian bank and provide cross-border payment services, but it is not a bank and cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits or provide ACH-direct debit origination without a sponsor. For an Ontario LP, using an EMI is legally viable for outbound royalties and inbound subscriptions provided (i) the EMI holds an active FINTRAC MSB registration, (ii) the LP maintains a direct bank account at a Schedule I bank for principal treasury operations, and (iii) all transaction records are reconciled against the bank's statement to avoid being treated as an unlicensed PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the Ontario LP must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's home bank to the LP's Canadian bank account. The remitter's full legal name and address must match the LP records; the bank beneficiary must be the LP itself (not the general partner personally); and the payment reference must state: 'Capital contribution under Ontario Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date], Investment Canada Act clearance not required' or similar clear description. Use the appropriate settlement currency (CAD or USD) and include an MT103 purpose/remarks code such as 'Third-party currency conversion' or 'Direct investment' where the field exists. For amounts above CAD 10,000, the Canadian bank will file an EFT report with FINTRAC; for capital injections above CAD 1 million, expect enhanced due-diligence requesting audited financials and source-of-wealth evidence. Since the Ontario LP has no minimum statutory capital requirement, no escrow rules apply, and funds become creditable partner capital immediately upon receipt and verification. The bank may require a confirmation letter from the LP's counsel confirming that the transfer represents a permitted capital contribution under the LP agreement and not an operating loan.For royalty disbursements to global licensors and content owners, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for individual payments of CAD 10,000 or more, with precise reference codes (e.g., 'ROYALTY UNDER LICENSE AGREEMENT #...'). For high-volume, sub-threshold disbursements, an aggregated clearing model using a registered FINTRAC MSB payment provider (Payoneer, Wise, or cross-border Stripe Connect) is standard, with funds funded from a CAD/USD clearing account at a Schedule I bank. The LP must ensure that each payment is documented with an e-invoice, a remittance statement, and a corresponding license agreement in the evidentiary file to satisfy both AML and tax audit requirements. For transaction sizes where banks require documentary support, a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) may be issued by the LP's Canadian bank in favor of a production-music counterparty, though such LC usage is rare for royalty-free stock-music licensing. An irrevocable standby L/C (SWIFT MT720) may be used when a platform or content-aggregator requires a performance guarantee. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million gross revenue for Local File; CAD 50 million gross revenue for Master File (per CRA guidelines). For partnerships, thresholds apply to the partnership's gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The partnership must have genuine economic activity and business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny deductions, impose penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided, and refer to the RCMP for money laundering charges under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Director personal liability: Partners may be jointly and severally liable for corporate obligations if the veil is pierced. Directors may face civil restitution and criminal charges.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust the transaction, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation is inadequate), and charge interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the non-compliance. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.
