Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) (www.canada.ca/business-registration) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario corporate income tax is administered by CRA under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation acquiring Canadian tax identity must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA. The standard procedure is: (1) prepare entity documentation (certificate of incorporation, registered office, directors/officers, and Canadian resident agent where applicable); (2) file through Business Registration Online (BRO) or submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts' by mail or fax; (3) select the account types required by the Canadian operations, including at a minimum the GST/HST account and, if taxable Canadian employment income arises, the payroll source deductions account; an import/export account (RM) is required if importing or exporting goods; (4) for non-resident registrants, CRA may require security under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act prior to issuing a GST/HST registration number. The BN serves as the federal tax identification number and as the Ontario account identifier for harmonized sales tax; the account is activated immediately upon approval and the taxpayer must file based on the assigned reporting periods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a CA_CORP incorporated under federal or Ontario law is a taxpayer separate from its shareholders. It is liable for Part I federal corporate income tax on worldwide income if resident (central management and control in Canada); if non-resident, liability is limited to Canadian-source business income and capital gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property. There is no flow-through designation for a standard Business Corporation.

Available election forms

Canada has no IRC-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; corporate residence is determined by law and central management and control. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) the non-resident election under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act, which applies only to Canadian rental real property income and is not relevant to marine survey service income; (ii) hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act for deductions subject to foreign hybrid treatment; and (iii) corporate rollover elections under section 85 for property transfers to a corporation on incorporation or reorganization. GST/HST registrants may elect simplified accounting methods (e.g., the quick method) and, for non-residents, may elect under sections 239 and 240 of the Excise Tax Act with CRA consent. For the present marine claims survey corporation, no entity-classification election is available; tax status is statutory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold). A person making taxable supplies in Canada must register with CRA if its worldwide taxable supplies, excluding financial services, exceed CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or in the four consecutive calendar quarters following a fiscal quarter. For Ontario supply nexus, a marine insurance surveyor is required to register once the threshold is met; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, subject to CRA security requirements for non-residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Canada are zero-rated if the goods are exported in accordance with the Customs Act and the supplier retains proof of export. Services, including marine insurance damage surveys, supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act if the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not an excluded service. Exclusions include services rendered to an individual present in Canada, services performed in respect of real property situated in Canada, and certain services performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada unless the property is being exported to a non-resident destination. To claim zero-rating for a marine cargo or hull survey service, the surveyor should obtain and retain: the non-resident client's status and non-registration evidence, the written inspection contract naming a non-resident principal (e.g., an overseas hull or cargo underwriter), proof that the service is performed for use outside Canada, and, where the vessel or cargo inspected is subsequently exported, customs evidence. The supply is claimed as zero-rated on the GST/HST return, which preserves input tax credit entitlement and supports net tax refunds.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or electronic CBSA export report where the inspected goods are exported separately
  • Commercial invoice / marine surveyor statement of charges
  • Ocean or air waybill (Bill of Lading / Master Airway Bill) showing shipper, consignee, and export port
  • Customs Cargo Control Number / highway carrier S10 waybill documentation
  • Proof of vessel or cargo export (port departure record, CBSA customs export report, or carrier proof of clearance)
  • Service agreement and scope-of-work with the non-resident underwriter or insurer
  • Certificate of insurance or claims assignment confirming the non-resident risk
  • Foreign remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103, wire transfer confirmation, or inward remittance certificate) showing payment received from a non-resident

Canada does not operate a national continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system. No real-time tax authority pre-clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or API invoice authorization is mandated at the federal or Ontario level. Suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (vendor identity, Business Number, date of invoice, description of supply, applicable tax rates and amounts). CRA accepts electronic invoicing in standard formats (e.g., PDF, EDI) provided the invoice content complies with the Excise Tax Act and records are retained for six years. CRA receives customs export and import transaction data electronically through Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) systems; no separate federal invoice clearance is required.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit exporters and zero-rated service providers. If documentation fails to prove physical export or a supply to a non-resident, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at the applicable rate (13% HST in Ontario), collects the net tax, and may claw back input tax credits, with interest. The ordinary reassessment period for GST/HST is four years; it may be extended indefinitely where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or where amounts are omitted from filing. Civil penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act for knowingly making, participating in, assenting to, or acquiescing in a false statement include a minimum CAD 100 penalty and, where tax is at issue, up to 25% of the amount sought to be evaded. Criminal offences under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines and imprisonment for summary conviction or indictable offences; directors and officers may be held personally liable for corporate remittance and penalty obligations in cases of gross negligence. Retrospective clawback also triggers payment of 13% HST on the zero-rated supply previously reported; the registered person remains liable for the tax even if the foreign principal cannot be charged.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no licensing requirements, no prior-approval regimes, no central-bank notification duties, and no quantitative limits on dividend remittances, interest payments, management-fee repatriation, or equity liquidation proceeds for non-residents. The sole constraints are treaty-based withholding taxes (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act) and asset-freezing measures under the United Nations Act and Special Economic Measures Act applicable to sanctioned persons/counterparties. For a marine insurance surveyor receiving USD claim-inspection fees, receivables may be held offshore or repatriated freely; FINTRAC and the bank will only require the standard wire-transfer report (EFTR) for cross-border transfers over C$10,000 and, for larger inflows routed to an operational account, the bank may seek a source-of-funds attestation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for repatriating proceeds of exported services. Marine surveying fees invoiced to foreign underwriters may be retained in USD/EUR accounts without restriction. Remaining compliance duties are informational only: (i) bank-filed FINTRAC EFTR on cross-border wire transfers over C$10,000; (ii) Statistics Canada trade-in-services reporting (EBOPS) for export earnings above the reporting threshold; and (iii) CRA foreign-income and T1135 foreign-asset disclosures where applicable. No Bank of Canada or Finance Canada authorization is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority, with Payments Canada operating the Lynx and ACSS settlement rails; OSFI as prudential regulator of Schedule I/II banks; FINTRAC as AML/CFT supervisor; and Global Affairs Canada as sanctions administrator. Canada maintains no general statutory exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary clearing rail; Low as a primary bank substitute. Canada does not licence EU-style electronic money institutions; Wise (TransferWise) and Payoneer operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not as banks. They can settle low-value foreign-currency surveyor payouts and provide efficient USD-to-CAD conversion, but they cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, issue credit or overdrafts, or hold funds in escrow/trust under an Ontario insurance-adjuster framework. Therefore, the marine claims surveyor must maintain its principal operational and capital accounts at a Schedule I chartered bank; fintech rails are acceptable only for sub-C$10,000 disbursements to international sub-contractors, subject to the company's own sanctions screening duty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from foreign shareholders must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the company's CAD operational account at a Schedule I bank, clearing on Lynx (Canada's high-value gross settlement system). Field 70 must state 'Share subscription - [Company legal name]' to ensure the credit is posted to issued capital rather than ordinary revenue. The bank files a FINTRAC EFTR on any transfer over C$10,000. Corporate-side perfection: board resolution approving the share issuance, executed share subscription agreement, filing of the corporate change reflecting issued capital in the Ontario Business Registry or Corporations Canada, and, for a non-Canadian investor, an Investment Canada Act notification when the enterprise value threshold is exceeded. Any USD/EUR-to-CAD conversion must be executed as a documented spot contract with the rate recorded in the treasury ledger; application of passive income or debt-versus-equity characterization under the Income Tax Act should be confirmed with tax counsel.International service payments (subcontracted surveyors, loss-adjusting counsel, reinsurance premiums, field-expense reimbursements) are effected by SWIFT MT103 through Lynx with EBOPS service codes attached, or by commercial documentary letter of credit (UCP600) for equipment purchases requiring transport-document presentation. Domestic recurring vendor payments clear via ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) or Interac e-Transfer in CAD. Where claim remittances must be escrowed pending litigation or coverage opinion, the bank's trade desk operates a segregated trust account under a documented escrow agreement; standby letters of credit can back performance guarantees issued to clients.Outright deliverable forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) to fix conversion of contracted survey-fee receivables; a verifiable underlying export-service exposure is required by the bank's treasury policy.Short-dated FX swaps to roll foreign-currency claims deposits into CAD without introducing open market risk.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for foreign-denominated intercompany loans or acquisition debt, requiring an ISDA Master Agreement and collateral annex.Purchased OTC FX options and zero-premium collars to protect minimum USD/EUR conversion rates on anticipated claim-inspection fees.Deliverable contracts only: NDFs (non-deliverable forwards) are not applicable to CAD because the Canadian dollar is freely convertible; no NDF market or regulatory authorization exists for Canadian-dollar trades.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary clearing rail; Low as a primary bank substitute. Canada does not licence EU-style electronic money institutions; Wise (TransferWise) and Payoneer operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not as banks. They can settle low-value foreign-currency surveyor payouts and provide efficient USD-to-CAD conversion, but they cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, issue credit or overdrafts, or hold funds in escrow/trust under an Ontario insurance-adjuster framework. Therefore, the marine claims surveyor must maintain its principal operational and capital accounts at a Schedule I chartered bank; fintech rails are acceptable only for sub-C$10,000 disbursements to international sub-contractors, subject to the company's own sanctions screening duty.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for willful participation in tax avoidance

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties and restitution if non-compliance is deliberate
