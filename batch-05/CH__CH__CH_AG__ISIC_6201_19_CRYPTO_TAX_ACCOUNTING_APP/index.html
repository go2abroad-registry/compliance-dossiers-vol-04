Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum issued share capital for a Swiss AG is CHF 100,000. At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal capital (CHF 20,000) must be paid in, but in any case no less than CHF 50,000 must be paid in cash and deposited into a blocked Swiss bank escrow account (Sperrkonto). The bank must issue written blocking confirmation before notarisation; the escrow release only after commercial register registration.

Funding & unlock path: The premises require a municipal fire safety certificate, a structural load and building occupancy approval from the local inspectorate, and workplace health clearance under Art. 6 and Art. 16 of the Swiss Labour Law, including first-aid readiness, evacuation planning, and electrical equipment inspection. Occupational accident prevention and workplace ergonomic compliance are supervised by SUVA or the cantonal labour inspectorate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions, ESTV/AFC) for Swiss VAT and federal direct tax withholding; cantonal and communal tax administrations for corporate income and capital tax registration; centralized online portal 'ePortal ESTV' (TaxMe) at https://eportal.estv.admin.ch, plus the Federal Statistical Office UID portal (Central Business Name Index 'Zentraler Firmenindex').

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity with Swiss indirect-tax nexus must first obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the federal UID portal; VAT registration then proceeds through the ESTV ePortal using the 'Registration/Adjustment for VAT-liable persons' form (MWST/TVA). The Swiss AG (CH_AG) obtains its UID automatically upon commercial-register entry in the canton of incorporation and receives a separate MWST-Nr. (VAT ID) when VAT registration is perfected. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the share capital and exercise full control. A company without Swiss commercial-register entry but performing taxable activities in Switzerland must complete the ePortal non-resident registration flow, designate a Swiss notification address/electronic service domicile, and will by default file on a six-month settlement or annual reporting basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through elections; an AG is mandatorily subject to entity-level taxation at federal, cantonal and communal levels. Available statutory elections include: (i) the small-business VAT exemption election where worldwide turnover is below CHF 100,000 and turnover excluding real-estate services is below CHF 250,000; (ii) optional ordinary group taxation (effective 1 January 2025) allowing a financially integrated group to elect aggregate tax-unit treatment, and the special tax-status group regime for financial institutions; (iii) cantonal tax elections for patent box/QRD deductions and equity-reduction allocation. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Switzerland follows the OECD hybrid mismatch design within its GAAR framework, which must be respected in the crypto tax-accounting product logic.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from non-exempt supplies (Art. 10 VAT Act); non-resident suppliers making supplies used or performed in Switzerland are also liable at the CHF 100,000 threshold, with certain services supplied by non-residents to Swiss recipients subject to reverse-charge transfer of liability below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the CH_AG qualify for the 0% Swiss VAT rate where the goods physically leave Switzerland and the dispatch is conclusively evidenced. Services, including SaaS/cloud-based crypto-tax calculation and bookkeeping software, qualify for 0% when the recipient has its place of business/effective seat abroad and the service is economically used or beneficially enjoyed outside Switzerland, and the service is not for a Swiss permanent establishment of the foreign recipient. The commercial model must document the foreign subscriber's residency and the absence of Swiss in-country benefit to sustain zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (Swiss export declaration/SED, or NCTS declaration where required by customs procedure)
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical dispatch outside Switzerland
  • Inward remittance certificate or payment-track evidence showing settlement by the foreign buyer (including USDC on-chain payment verification where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice compliant with Art. 27(3) VAT Act
  • Export-transaction ledger reconciling e-invoicing data to the customs declaration

Switzerland does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API reporting to tax authorities. A VAT invoice must contain the vendor's VAT number and Swiss UID, transaction and invoice dates, sequential invoice number, supplier and recipient identity, nature and quantity of supplies, tax rate, tax amount, and total amount. B2B e-invoicing commonly uses the SIX 'Paynet' and Swiss QR-Bill (Swico/Swiss Implementation Recommendations) interchange formats. Foreign and domestic suppliers must retain complete invoicing records; the ESTV does not provide fiscal pre-encryption APIs, but the crypto tax SaaS must document the CHF reference or the foreign-currency conversion method on every invoice for audit integrity.

The ESTV subjects software exporters, including crypto-SaaS platforms, to heightened audit scrutiny on zero-rating claims. If export or foreign-benefit evidence is defective, the 0% treatment is retrospectively reversed, VAT is reassessed at the standard 8.1% rate, and default interest of 5% per annum runs from the original invoice due date. Administrative fines for tax contraventions (such as incomplete invoice data) amount ordinarily to up to CHF 8,000; intentional tax evasion triggers penalties up to three times the evaded tax and criminal proceedings under VAT Act Arts. 57-60 and 47. Repeated or flagrant zero-rating misuse can additionally result in retrospective revocation of the VAT registration and the imposition of security obligations for future liabilities under Art. 55 VAT Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a full convertibility regime grounded in constitutional economic freedom (Art. 27 Federal Constitution) and adherence to the OECD Capital Liberalisation Code. Both capital and current account transactions are liberalized; no exchange control authorization, mandatory licensing, or central bank approval is required for cross-border payments, capital injections, or profit repatriation applicable to a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The Swiss franc (CHF) is a freely floating, fully convertible reserve currency. Software export and SaaS subscription revenues may be invoiced and retained in USD, EUR, or USDC without restriction. Profit remittance (dividends, royalties, licence fees) is unrestricted from an FX perspective; the only fiscal friction is the Swiss 35% anticipatory withholding tax on dividends, which is refundable for Swiss-resident recipients or reduced under the applicable US-Swiss double taxation treaty (typically 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding substantial participation and 15% otherwise), and which does not constitute a currency control hurdle. There is no exchange-rate approval, no compulsory repatriation, and no surrender-of-export-proceeds requirement for a Swiss corporate treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Swiss law. The SNB imposes neither a mandatory conversion ratio nor a statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts or cross-border service income. A Swiss AG may hold 100% of its foreign-currency export proceeds (USD, EUR, or stablecoin) indefinitely in non-CHF accounts. The only indirect obligation is statistical: banks participating in the Swiss Payment Convention (SPC) must report cross-border payment instructions with structured remittance information for SNB balance-of-payments and direct-investment statistics; this requires the company to designate correct ISO 20022 purpose codes (e.g., EQUI for equity injection, SUPP for supplier payments, ROYA for royalties) and to respond to the SNB's annual direct-investment survey where material cross-border positions exist.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, acting in coordination with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF), the State Secretariat for International Financial Matters (SIF), and the Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal direct corporate income tax at 8.5% of net taxable profit plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; combined effective headline rate ranges from approximately 11.5% to 21.0% depending on the canton of residence of the Swiss AG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) applies to dividends distributed by a Swiss AG to shareholders, including foreign corporate and individual shareholders. Cantonal and communal withholding taxes do not apply to dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law imposes no general mandatory Local File or Master File threshold based solely on entity size; however, the Federal Tax Administration expects contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence upon request for any material related-party transaction. For practical compliance, a Local File is advisable for aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceeding CHF 1 million, and a Master File is recommended for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CHF 50 million. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of zero-tax jurisdictions do not apply to Switzerland. Nevertheless, Swiss constitutional and treaty practice requires every Swiss AG to have genuine business substance at its registered seat and place of effective management: physical offices, qualified staff, board meetings, independent decision-making, and real economic activity. A crypto tax SaaS company must employ actual technical, compliance, and development functions within Switzerland. Where substance is absent, tax authorities may ignore the Swiss entity, deny treaty benefits, recharacterise revenue as belonging to the foreign parent, and expose the structure to BEPS and exchange-of-information scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is punishable by an administrative fine of up to three times the tax evaded. Tax fraud committed by falsification of vouchers or books carries a monetary penalty of up to CHF 250,000 and imprisonment of up to three years. The underpaid tax itself remains collectible with default interest.

Director personal liability: The chief executive and financial officers are individually indictable as the organs responsible for filing accuracy; upon conviction they face imprisonment, criminal fines, civil damages under Article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, and travel restrictions during criminal investigation. A court may order restitution of the evaded tax from personal assets.
Withholding Tax Violation

Statutory sanction: The Swiss AG becomes liable for the full withholding tax, statutory interest, and recovery costs. In aggravated cases, the concealment of one's tax-collection obligation constitutes tax fraud punishable by a fine of up to CHF 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to three years.

Director personal liability: Withholding obligations are imposed on the company's organs; directors who knowingly cause the company to pay dividends without withholding can be personally assessed with the tax and interest. The Swiss tax authority may impose a travel ban and initiate asset seizure where there is a risk that the funds will be moved abroad without payment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the Swiss entity, reallocate profits to the actual economic owner, impose a constructive dividend at the recipient level, and apply 35% withholding tax on deemed distributions. The AG may thereafter be reported to the commercial register for bankruptcy or involuntary dissolution if it cannot demonstrate ongoing business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held civilly liable for damages caused to the company and tax authorities by being a mere mailbox entity; willful violation of substance requirements is treated as a sham arrangement and may support a finding of criminal fraud, leading to personal restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML and Digital Asset Compliance

Statutory sanction: Although the software entity is not automatically a supervised financial intermediary under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act, acts of providing financial services or facilitating cryptocurrency payments may trigger FINMA oversight. Violations can lead to administrative fines, confiscation of digital assets, and criminal penalties under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 305bis and 305ter), including imprisonment of up to three years.

Director personal liability: A director who knows or should know that crypto subscriptions are being used to launder tax fraud proceeds may be prosecuted as an accessory to money laundering. Conviction results in imprisonment, monetary penalty, confiscation of proceeds, travel bans, and disqualification from holding director positions; personal civil liability is available to affected creditors and state authorities.
