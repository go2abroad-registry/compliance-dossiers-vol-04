Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal administrator of the GST/HST, income tax, and business number systems; registration portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and the GST/HST Registry (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-register.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) administers the provincial Employer Health Tax and corporate tax coordination, but the Ontario 8% HST component is assessed, collected, and refunded exclusively by the CRA under the Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (CITCA).

E-government filing portal

Sequence for the foreign limited partnership: (1) Determine whether the LP is engaged in a taxable commercial activity in Canada; a non-resident supplier of specified digital supplies to Canadian consumers must register when its worldwide revenue from such supplies to recipients in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in the preceding 12 months. (2) Submit CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), identifying the limited partnership by its own legal name, and concurrently open the GST/HST program account (RT suffix); non-resident registrants may file electronically or by mail using a Canadian agent/intermediary. (3) Register as non-resident importer of record and exporter with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) portal, authorizing a licensed customs broker under a client/bond arrangement for any goods shipped into Canada; the CRA Business Number (BN) serves as the CBSA import/export identifier. (4) Elect the partnership's GST/HST reporting period (monthly, quarterly, or annual, with annual generally requiring a CAD 500,000 taxable-supply threshold absent a non-resident election) and remit net tax electronically through CRA My Payment. (5) For Ontario: no separate HST registration is required because the provincial component is harmonized, but the LP must open an Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) account with the Ontario Ministry of Finance if it has Ontario employees; absent employees, no separate provincial account is required. (6) If the LP makes only zero-rated export supplies, registration remains mandatory only if the LP exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or voluntarily registers to recover input tax credits (ITCs).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96); the LP files only an information return (T5013) and the CRA BN is issued in the partnership's name; for GST/HST purposes the partnership is statutorily deemed a separate 'person' (Excise Tax Act s. 272(1)) and is the registrant, with each member jointly and severally liable for unremitted net tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 96); the LP files only an information return (T5013) and the CRA BN is issued in the partnership's name; for GST/HST purposes the partnership is statutorily deemed a separate 'person' (Excise Tax Act s. 272(1)) and is the registrant, with each member jointly and severally liable for unremitted net tax. Entity-level taxation applies only if the LP becomes a SIFT (Specified Investment Flow-Through) partnership under ITA s. 197, for example by listing its units on a stock exchange; a privately held cross-border LP remains pass-through for Canadian federal and Ontario provincial purposes, although the home jurisdiction may classify it as opaque (U.S. default check-the-box rules).

Available election forms

(i) Voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(2), waiving the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold to recover ITCs on export-related inputs; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 and a corresponding GST/HST fiscal-year election to align returns with the LP's non-calendar or foreign fiscal year; (iii) ETA s. 273 partnership liability election under which a designated general partner or specified member may assume joint and several responsibility for the LP's net tax; (iv) reverse-hybrid structuring election — Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election, but a non-resident owner may file U.S. Form 8832 to elect or terminate opaque classification of the Canadian LP for U.S. federal tax purposes, and the LP's constitutive documents should be reviewed against the BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) regime under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21, which disallow double-dip deductions without a discretionary administrative election; (v) the GST/HST Quick Method is generally unavailable or inadvisable for a non-resident exporter whose annual revenue exceeds CAD 400,000 and for zero-rated exporters seeking full ITC recovery; (vi) a non-resident registrant may elect to file annual GST/HST returns where quarterly instalments are made if the CRA accepts the Foreign-Based Information Return criteria, but monthly or quarterly filing is standard for an export-heavy LP seeking refunds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on the LP's worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada together with supplies of associated persons in the four preceding calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers of specified digital supplies (services and intangible personal property) to Canadian non-registrants must register once worldwide revenue from such supplies to Canadian recipients exceeds CAD 30,000 over the preceding 12 months, without the usual associated-person aggregation relief for foreign vendors; imported commercial goods are always subject to GST/HST at the border on importation regardless of the vendor's registration status, with the purchaser as importer of record unless the LP elects a duty-deferral/import-as-registrant model.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act only where the LP delivers the goods to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada, transfers possession of the goods to a non-resident recipient outside Canada, or exports the goods under a contract of carriage; possession and control must pass outside Canada before any use in Canada, and the LP must hold contemporaneous proof of export before claiming 0%. Services: exports and offshore services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada, goods situated in Canada at the time of performance, or a right to admission to a place in Canada; for the LP's online merchandising activities, digital platform services, referral commissions, authentication and valuation certificates, warranty administration, and fulfilment coordination supplied to non-resident counterparties may be zero-rated only if the recipient's non-resident status and non-registration are documented. If the LP sells goods from a third-country drop-ship location to a Canadian consumer, the supply is made outside Canada and no GST/HST is collectible by the LP, but the consumer pays GST/HST at importation; the LP must not erroneously collect 13% HST in that scenario.

Canada does not impose Latin-American-style real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory pre-clearance e-invoicing. GST/HST law operates on documentary self-assessment: every registrant must provide a 'sales invoice' containing prescribed information where consideration exceeds CAD 30 (ETA s. 169(1) and s. 223), including the supplier's name and BN, date of invoice, recipient's identity, nature of the goods/services, consideration, applicable tax rate, and tax payable, issued within 15 days of a recipient request; simplified invoices are permitted below CAD 30 and electronic invoices are accepted. For B2G transactions, the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework is voluntary at the federal level and in Ontario procurement, with no fiscal clearance function. For imports and exports, the CBSA CARM platform, eManifest/ACI electronic cargo reporting, and PARS/PARS release constitute the operational electronic clearance mandate; the LP should maintain API/EDI integrations with its licensed customs broker and ensure electronic invoice data reconciles with CARM declarations to sustain zero-rating evidence.

CRA's Exports and GST/HST Audit Program cross-matches the LP's zero-rated declarations against CBSA export cargo data and banking/payment-settlement flow; insufficient documentary evidence triggers reassessment of the full 13% HST (5% federal plus 8% Ontario) with compound daily arrears interest at CRA's prescribed rate from the return filing due date, plus the ETA s. 285(1) gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax understated, including clawed-back refunds and denied input tax credits. The LP and its partners are jointly and severally liable under ETA ss. 272-273. Deliberate export-fraud or fabricated zero-rating schemes attract criminal prosecution under ETA s. 289 and the Criminal Code, customs false-declaration civil monetary penalties under the Customs Act s. 109.1, and forfeiture/ascertained-excise valuation remedies. The CRA normal reassessment period is four years but is unlimited for misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default; the LP must retain all export substantiation records for six years under ITA s. 230 and ETA s. 286, and foreign-source currency proceeds should be repatriated and traceable within the 180-day treasury-control benchmark, as delayed repatriation substantially increases transfer-pricing audit risk under ITA s. 247 even though Canadian law imposes no statutory 180-day export-receipt deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility. Canada has been an IMF Article VIII member since 1952, meaning no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions. Capital and current account flows are free of exchange-control approval, quotas, or dual-rate mechanisms. Profit and capital remittances by an Ontario limited partnership to non-resident partners carry no statutory approval hurdle; the only frictions are tax-related: Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reducible often to 15% or less) on certain Canadian-source payments, and banking AML due diligence for wires above CAD 100,000. E-commerce settlements via Stripe/PayPal are converted to CAD at PSP-applied wholesale rates; CRA reporting requires CAD functional-currency records using the Bank of Canada noon rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Foreign Exchange Control Act (repealed 1951) eliminated all surrender and repatriation mandates; the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act impose no conversion ratio, no timeline, and no requirement to repatriate export receipts. Online jewellery/watch revenues may be held indefinitely in USD or EUR at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Regulatory duties: (i) FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting for each transfer ≥ CAD 10,000 (record and report within 5 business days); (ii) Customs Act has no exporter-proceeds declaration for online sales; (iii) Income Tax Act s.261 requires CAD reporting absent a functional-currency election. Recommended treasury policy: sweep USD PSP settlement balances on a 10-business-day cycle when the USD/CAD rate exceeds a 10-day moving average.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority; prudential banking regulation by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); economic sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) and United Nations Act. Canada has no standalone foreign-exchange control agency; the Foreign Exchange Control Board was abolished in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as a settlement/top-up rail with a Schedule I bank as final treasury anchor; LOW as the primary treasury bank. Canada does not license 'EMIs' per se. Functionally equivalent providers are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (e.g., Wise Payments Canada Inc., Payoneer Canada Inc.) and, from September 2024, Retail Payment Activity Providers licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and supervised by the Bank of Canada. Legal constraints: (i) PSP/RPAA balances are not CDIC-insured deposits and must be swept to a chartered bank for operational liquidity; (ii) PSPs cannot extend credit, guarantees, or overdrafts; (iii) jewellery/watch merchants attract enhanced chargeback scrutiny, prompting rolling reserves of 5–10%; (iv) Stripe and PayPal settlements in CAD must be withdrawn to a Canadian Schedule I bank account, making domestic bank presence unavoidable. Wise may be used for small vendor payouts (≤ CAD 5,000) in 30+ currencies at mid-market rates, but must not be used to obscure source-of-funds for capital contributions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general federal-Ontario corporate rate (federal 15% after general rate reduction plus Ontario 11.5%); the Ontario Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and files an information return, with Part I tax imposed only at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders; the Canada-US treaty reduces the rate to 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for a US corporation beneficially owning at least 10% of the voting shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed where aggregate transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceed C$1,000,000 in a fiscal year or where outstanding debt exceeds C$1,000,000; contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is expected under CRA IC 94-4R and OECD guidance; the s. 247(3) penalty applies where the transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and documentation is deficient.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a BVI-style Economic Substance Regulation; economic substance is enforced through the sham transaction doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245), and partnership validity law requiring a genuine commercial partnership. An Ontario LP with no employees, premises, or real business functions will be disregarded, resulting in recharacterization of income, denial of treaty benefits, and potential 25% Part XIII withholding on imputed dividends.
