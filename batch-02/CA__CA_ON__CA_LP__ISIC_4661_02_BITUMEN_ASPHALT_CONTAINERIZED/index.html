Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); My Business Account portal at canada.ca; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for Ontario HST coordination and provincial tax administration support)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Ontario Limited Partnership must register the LP under Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act and obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Form RC1. Where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada in excess of the small supplier threshold, it must also obtain a separate GST/HST registration number (RT). The LP is a pass-through for Canadian income tax purposes and must file an annual partnership information return (Form T5013) with CRA and issue T5013 slips to resident and non-resident partners. A non-resident partner without a Canadian BN may rely on the LP's BN for compliance but should request a separate BN if it carries on its own Canadian business activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope applies because Canadian-source partnership income is allocated to resident and non-resident partners and is not subject to entity-level income taxation. However, the LP is a 'person' for GST/HST purposes and is registrable as a supplier of taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; a limited partnership is mandatorily treated as a pass-through. Available elections include: ITA s. 249.1 fiscal-period election for the partnership; ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations; ETA s. 273 joint venture election; ITA hybrid mismatch and treaty-boosted non-resident partner elections under ss. 18.2/18.3 and s. 250(5). Ontario accepts CRA-determined classifications for provincial purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000, being the GST/HST small supplier threshold: the LP is not required to register unless, within any calendar quarter and its immediately preceding four quarters, total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. Non-resident suppliers operating in Canada in the course of a Canadian business and making taxable supplies without a physical presence may also owe simplified registration duties, especially for cross-border digital supplies, and may elect registration even below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including containerized drummed or bagged bitumen) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the supplier exports the goods in the course of its commercial activities and retains proof that the goods were shipped out of Canada within 60 days of delivery to a carrier for export. Offshore services provided to a non-resident in respect of exported goods may also be zero-rated if the services are for use entirely outside Canada. For a wholesale trading model, the export must be a genuine sale to a non-resident that results in physical movement of the bitumen out of Canada to the foreign destination; mere transfer of title will not sustain zero-rating without concurrent export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 or electronic Canada Border Services Agency export declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice (identifying vendor, purchaser, containerized goods, and zero-rated export status)
  • Bill of Lading or Through Ocean Waybill evidencing export from Canada to the named foreign destination
  • Carrier Certificate of Exportation (issued by the transporting carrier, with customs cargo control number)
  • Packing List and Container Manifest (maritime container numbers and seals)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT acknowledgement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Canadian or correspondent bank account
  • Freight invoice and customs brokerage confirmation showing movement of containerized cargo from the Ontario point of origin to the Canadian port of exit

Canada does not mandate any real-time e-invoicing, fiscal API clearance, or digital stamping. CRA requires invoices issued by GST/HST registrants to display the vendor's Business Number and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description, tax amount, and tax rate. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for operators with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million, and CRA requires that books and records, including export documentation, be kept electronically or in paper format for six years. No CRA clearance is required before issuing invoices.

CRA and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) may deny zero-rating if the LP fails to produce the full export documentation bundle within a reasonable period after an audit request. The LP may then be reassessed to collect the 13% HST on the full invoice amount, plus interest and penalties. Fraudulent or knowingly false zero-rating claims attract penalties under ETA ss. 280 and 281, including a penalty of up to 25% of the unreported HST and fines up to CAD 25,000 plus up to 200% of the tax attempted to be evaded, and imprisonment for criminal evasion. CRA has a four-year normal reassessment window, which is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Furthermore, clawback may extend to the general partner of the Ontario Limited Partnership, who is jointly and severally liable for GST/HST obligations, including any export-related reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII. There is no registration, licensing, quota, or repatriation approval for a foreign-owned Ontario limited partnership; CAD/USD exchange is executed at market rates without central bank intervention. Profit and capital remittances to non-resident limited partners face no FX restriction; the only monetary friction is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced) imposed on Canadian-source income allocations, whereas returns of contributed capital are free of tax and remittance controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. A wholesale supplier of penetration-grade bitumen (HS 2713.20 / NAICS 424720) may retain 100% of USD proceeds in a Canadian USD clearing account or offshore. Compliance duties are monitoring-only: FINTRAC reportable international EFTs at or above CAD 10,000, accurate CBSA export valuation, and confirmation that the goods are not on the EIPA Export Control List.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority; Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. FX-related oversight is distributed: OSFI supervises clearing banks, FINTRAC enforces AML/ATF surveillance over cross-border EFTs under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Global Affairs Canada administers export controls under the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA).

Airwallex Fit: High

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (federal general corporate rate 15% + Ontario general corporate rate 11.5%); the Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent, so the headline rate applies at the partner level, not to the LP itself

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII (s. 212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation; treaty-reduced: 15% general rate under the Canada-US treaty (5% for a US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting stock) and 15% general / 10% for a 10% corporate shareholder under the Canada-China treaty. LP profit distributions to non-resident partners are not legally dividends: active business income allocated to a non-resident partner is taxed under Part I and is not subject to the 25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory where the aggregate market value of transactions with each non-arm's-length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods/services or CAD 5,000,000 for intangibles/shares; Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 (approximately CAD 1.1 billion); Canada does not statutorily require separate Local File or Master File filings, but the CRA administratively requires contemporaneous documentation to avoid transfer pricing penalties

Economic substance scope: Canada has no stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE ESR; however, economic substance is decisive in (a) determining whether a non-resident partner carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment under an applicable tax treaty, (b) applying the s. 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule where the Ontario LP structure is artificial, and (c) denying treaty benefits under the principal purposes test implemented through the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) in most of Canada's tax treaties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal Offence)

Statutory sanction: Under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act: on indictment, a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; on summary conviction, the same fine range and imprisonment for up to 2 years less a day

Director personal liability: The general partner and every director/officer who directed or accepted the evasion is jointly and severally liable for the full tax owed; the CRA may apply for a departure prohibition order under s. 240 preventing the individual from leaving Canada; imprisonment applies on conviction.
Gross Negligence and False Statements

Statutory sanction: s. 163(2) penalty equal to 50% of the additional tax payable; third-party penalties under s. 163.2 of up to CAD 100,000 for preparing false statements; CRA may reassess beyond the normal 3-year review period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default

Director personal liability: Civil restitution to the CRA for the 50% penalty; a director who signed or approved the false return can be personally assessed under s. 227.1 for unremitted amounts; travel bans and asset freezes apply where collection is at risk.
Failure to Withhold Part I / Part XIII on Non-Resident Allocations

Statutory sanction: The LP is personally liable for the unremitted withholding under s. 227(8) plus daily interest and a 10% late-remittance penalty; the unobtained amount is also disallowed as a deduction to the payer

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors are jointly and severally liable without limitation for the unremitted amounts under s. 227.1; the obligation survives bankruptcy, permits asset seizure, and supports a court-ordered departure prohibition (travel ban).
Shell Company / Sham Structure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the LP as a sham or re-characterize the arrangement under GAAR (s. 245) and deny treaty benefits under the principal purposes test; full tax, interest, and penalties are re-assessed; under s. 13 of the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act a limited partner taking part in control forfeits limited liability and becomes a general partner

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable without limitation for all partnership obligations; limited partners exercising management control become personally liable as general partners; civil restitution, travel bans, and, where fraud is proven, imprisonment of up to 5 years follow.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 100,000 or 10% of gross revenue and the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to document the arm's length price

Director personal liability: Directors who approve related-party transactions are personally assessable for the penalty where they directed the false documentation; additional s. 163(2) gross negligence penalties apply if records are materially false.
Anti-Money Laundering / Unstructured Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Although wholesale bitumen trading is rated 'Low_Risk_Standard', FINTRAC obligations attach to the payment institutions processing Swift_LC/TT_Wire rails; AML administrative penalties can reach CAD 500,000 per violation for financial intermediaries, and the LP's assets may be frozen under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Director personal liability: General partners can be prosecuted for money laundering under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment for up to 10 years; a sham or criminal finding exposes partners to civil restitution, departure prohibitions, and confiscation orders.
