Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV; Administration fédérale des contributions AFC; Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); central eServices portal at https://www.esty.admin.ch; company and UID search via the central business register Zefix at https://www.zefix.ch/en/; VAT-specific e-service portal for registration, returns and account status (ESTV eServices / SuisseTax).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity acting as a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) first incorporates under Swiss law and obtains a company identification number (UID / Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the cantonal commercial register with automatic central registration in the Swiss Business Index (Zefix). For VAT purposes, the UID is converted into a VAT identifier by the ESTV by assigning an MWST/TVA suffix, e.g., CHE-123.456.789 MWST. The application for VAT registration is submitted electronically with the UID, description of the business activity, expected Swiss turnover, proof of place of business, and, where the entity is not established in Switzerland, appointment of the entity itself or a Swiss VAT representative authorised to receive communications. Registration must occur before taxable supplies begin; the ESTV normally issues the VAT number within 5–20 working days. There is no separate federal income tax registration certificate for non-residents; the cantonal tax administration processes income tax registration when a permanent establishment or Swiss domiciled parent AG exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss AG is a capital company with full legal personality subject to federal, cantonal and communal corporate income tax and annual cantonal capital tax on its worldwide income if resident, without any fiscal transparency or pass-through to shareholders. For direct and indirect tax purposes, the company is a standalone taxable person subject to Swiss VAT on all taxable supplies (standard rate 8.1%) unless an express exemption applies. Individuals, general partnerships and sole proprietorships may elect transparent treatment, but a Swiss AG cannot elect to be disregarded as separate from its shareholders.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for corporations. Statutory elections available under Swiss tax and VAT law include: (i) VAT group registration for closely integrated entities under art. 13 of the VAT Act, allowing one tax subject and intra-group supplies to be outside the scope of VAT; (ii) option to exempt small businesses from VAT upon turnover not exceeding CHF 100,000 (art. 34 VAT Act, but unavailable to non-residents); (iii) election to make a Swiss property sale taxable under VAT (option to waive the real-estate exemption); (iv) cantonal tax consolidation election for an affiliated Swiss group where the parent holds at least 80% and group-oriented financial accounting is used; and (v) recognition of Switerland’s OECD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules with no elective exception—hybrid instruments or entities producing mismatch outcomes are automatically neutralised. There is no check-the-box election analogous to the United States; capital-company classification is mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) from Swiss supplies for Swiss-resident businesses and enterprises with a Swiss permanent establishment; a foreign-based enterprise with no place of business in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT from the first taxable supply in Switzerland (zero-de-minimis threshold). Exemption for small businesses under art. 34 VAT Act is not available to non-resident suppliers.

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, negative-audit-file, or government digital stamping system. There is no continuous transaction control; VAT is declared periodically (usually quarterly) through ESTV e-services. Mandatory e-invoicing exists for invoices issued to the Swiss federal public administration (B2G), which must be submitted as structured e-invoices (XML according to eCH-0110 or PDF/A-3 with embedded XML) and include the Swiss QR invoice payment slip. The Swiss QR-bill (Swiss QR Code) itself is compulsory for payment-slips issued by Swiss commercial parties from 30 June 2020; B2B e-invoicing by the Swiss eBill channel or EAN/XML exchange is permitted and increasingly standard, but not mandatory outside the public sector. Archiving of invoices and customs documents for 10 years is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is freely convertible. Switzerland maintains no general foreign exchange control legislation; both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted. Dividends, profits, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted abroad without prior approval. No restrictions exist on non-resident ownership of shares or on funding of a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The only statutory checks are anti-money-laundering, sanctions screening, and the SNB's statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligation. For rail-freight forwarding entities, trade-related payments for transportation and logistics services are treated as ordinary current account transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applicable to export receipts. Proceeds from cross-border rail-freight and intermodal transport invoices may be retained in any currency, held on deposit, or converted into CHF at the counterparty bank's discretion. Swiss banks are required to report cross-border payment flows for monetary statistics, but no surrender, repatriation, or conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High, subject to proper licensing. Swiss-registered fintech companies holding a FINMA FinTech Licence (Art. 1b Banking Act), or full banking licence, may open accounts and provide cross-border payment and FX services. Non-Swiss electronic money institutions (EMIs) from the EU/EEA do not enjoy passporting rights into Switzerland; an offshore EMI may not on-board a Swiss AG for regulated payment services unless it establishes a Swiss branch, acquires a Swiss licence, or operates through a Swiss-licensed partner bank. For a low-risk rail-freight forwarding enterprise using SWIFT wires and commercial LC, the pragmatic route is a tier-1 Swiss bank account with a fintech overlay for remote treasury visibility and API-based reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or subordinated shareholder loan funding must be wired via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to a Swiss resident bank account. Switzerland imposes no exchange-control license or approval. Use ISO 20022 purpose code CAPT (Capital Transfer) or CCLT for shareholder loans, with the remittance field referencing the notarised capital contribution resolution. The receiving bank will require: a board resolution resolving the capital issuance, updated shareholders' register, source of funds statement, and the standard AML-KYC pack. For statistical reporting, the bank records the cross-border payment on behalf of the SNB. The CHF account may be linked to a capital escrow / capital payment sub-account, after which the capital becomes freely available for intermodal logistics operations.Preferred clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for settlement to carriers, rail operators, terminal handlers, and European/Asian freight partners; (2) SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank liquidity movements; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710) for documentary trade settlements on container block-train transactions, with SWIFT MT754/MT756 for confirmation and payment; (4) SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) for domestic CHF transfers and Euro SIC/SEPA for EUR outflows. For high-value urgent disbursements, RTGS through SIC and SWIFT gpi track-and-trace functionality minimises float. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on net profit after tax (Art. 10 DBG); equivalent effective federal burden of 7.83% on pre-tax profit; combined with cantonal and communal profit taxes, the typical ordinary burden for a Zug-registered AG ranges between 11.5% and 13.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal anticipatory tax on gross dividends, bonus shares, monetary benefits in kind and liquidation distributions from a Swiss AG (Art. 4 VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within the period fixed by the cantonal tax authority under Art. 124 DBG; for a calendar-year AG the standard due date is 31 March of the following year, automatically extended to 30 June where filed electronically, with further extension available on written request; for a non-calendar-year accounting period the deadline is six months after the close of the business year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return and payment are due within 30 days following the end of each calendar quarter, or within 30 days of the date the dividend distribution is resolved and paid.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax practice applies OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines without a statutory filing threshold; a complete OECD-compliant Local File and Master File is expected where the Swiss AG belongs to a multinational group with consolidated global revenue above CHF 50 million or where annual cross-border controlled transactions exceed CHF 5 million per category; smaller freight-forwarding structures must maintain a contemporaneous functional analysis, a comparability table and the method applied for each intermodal service.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a substance-over-form general anti-abuse doctrine developed in Federal Supreme Court case law and embodied in the abuse-of-law principle of Art. 210 DBG; the disregard of legal form operates where the rail-freight AG has no operational staff, no physical business premises, no local decision-making and merely invoices intermodal margins while the commercial substance is conducted by the non-resident parent; such a structure can be recharacterized as a foreign permanent establishment, denied treaty benefits, refused notional interest deduction and made subject to withholding-tax recovery on all distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements to Tax Authorities

Statutory sanction: Restitution of all unpaid federal and cantonal tax plus default interest at 5% per annum; administrative fine up to three times the tax evaded; where the taxpayer uses falsified books and records, the matter escalates to criminal tax fraud with monetary penalties in CHF amounts calculated on culpability; underlying document falsification is separately punishable under Art. 251 StGB with imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the officer responsible for tax filings are individually liable for restitution and fines where their wilful or negligent conduct caused the under-dedaration; Swiss debt enforcement may issue travel bans on directors for pending tax debts exceeding the statutory threshold, and civil restitution suits can pierce the corporate veil against intentionally depleted assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the Swiss AG as a conduit: all freight margin allocated to the foreign related operator; denial of treaty residence and participation deduction; automatic recapture of undeclared residual income; assessment of accumulated taxable base plus 5% annual interest; the structure is void for withholding-tax relief purposes under the abuse-of-law provisions of Art. 21 VStG.

Director personal liability: Board members who approved the artificial invoicing structure are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax arrears; sanction is enforced as personal liability for unpaid withholding tax where distributions from the hollow treasury occurred; directors face exclusion from board mandates and seizure of personal assets in the canton of domicile.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Add-back of the excess intercompany deduction with default interest; reclassification of the excess as a constructive dividend triggering 35% withholding tax and penalty procedure; in aggravated cases, criminal tax-evasion proceedings with fines up to three times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The CEO and CFO who signed and priced the controlled transactions are personally exposed to criminal fines and civil restitution for the tax shortfall; the Swiss fiscal authority may issue formal chargeable notices to directors, and constant violation leads to disqualification from office under Swiss corporate law.
Withholding Tax Misappropriation on Dividend Distributions

Statutory sanction: Compulsory recovery of the 35% gross withholding tax from the company; default interest at 5%; monetary penalty up to the amount of the evaded tax; the distribution remains taxable even if the dividend could later not be recovered from the shareholder.

Director personal liability: The entire board of directors is jointly and severally liable for the remittance of withheld anticipatory tax under Art. 12 VStG; this liability attaches to personal assets of each signatory of the distribution resolution and is not limited by liquidation of the AG, it also extends to criminal prosecution for misappropriation of tax monies.
Money Laundering and AML Breach in Freight Invoice Flows

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Art. 305bis StGB carrying imprisonment up to three years, or up to five years in aggravated cases, plus confiscation of laundered assets; additional fines under the Swiss AML Act; the logistics business, although classified as Low_Risk_Standard, forfeits the low-risk threshold instantly when deliberate laundering attempts are proven.

Director personal liability: Directors are prosecuted as principals where they procured the instruments of crime; personal asset freezes and travel bans are ordered by the investigating magistrate; the company's license-independent status cannot shield the directors from extradition requests based on predicate offences in source countries.
