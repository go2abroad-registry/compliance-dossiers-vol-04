Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including its Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca/business-registration, GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account; provincial corporate registration and Ontario tax matters are administered by ServiceOntario and the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with Canadian nexus first obtains a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online, or by filing Form RC1 if applying on paper; the BN is the sole federal tax identifier for corporate income tax and GST/HST, with no separate national VAT identification number. If the entity is carrying on business in Ontario under a federal or extra-provincial structure, it must complete the applicable extra-provincial registration through ServiceOntario before filing tax accounts. A non-resident supplier of SaaS or telematics-related digital services whose Canadian taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold must open a CRA GST/HST account through the simplified foreign supplier registration pathway; registration may be required even without a Canadian permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election or pass-through election is available for a Canadian Business Corporation; the entity is categorically subject to federal Part I and Ontario corporate income tax at the entity level. Relevant statutory elections for cross-border structures include the section 85 rollover for transfers of eligible property, the section 216(1) election for net-basis Canadian-source rental income, and allocation rules among associated corporations under section 256. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by Income Tax Act section 18.4 to 18.6 and OECD BEPS Action 2 principles; there is no elective regime or opt-out mechanism, and CRA may also apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; a person ceases to be a small supplier and must register for GST/HST once total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident digital suppliers of SaaS and related services must register when Canadian-source taxable digital-service revenues exceed CAD 30,000 on an annual basis; once registered, HST at 13% applies to Canadian taxable supplies unless a zero-rating provision applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, goods exported from Canada are zero-rated if the exporter maintains contemporaneous proof of exportation and the goods are delivered outside Canada. For logistics-technology SaaS and telematics platform access, a supply made to a non-resident recipient is eligible for zero-rating when the recipient is outside Canada at the time of supply, the supply is not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment of the recipient, and the supply is not primarily for use in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The exporter must retain commercial, shipping, and payment evidence to substantiate the zero-rated treatment on the GST/HST return and underlying input tax credit claims.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or Canada Border Services Agency electronic export report) or equivalent inward/outward export proof
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the non-resident buyer, terms of sale, and nature of the digital service or goods
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (original or certified carrier copy) showing movement of goods outside Canada or, for digital services, server and user-location evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT MT103/credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign or Canadian currency

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API submission, or digital stamping at the federal or Ontario level. Electronic invoices are legally valid and must contain the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, description of the supply, amount of consideration, and applicable GST/HST rate and tax payable. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or CRA-approved API channels. SaaS billing via Stripe or wire transfer is acceptable provided the statutory invoice particulars and supporting records are preserved for the required six-year retention period.

CRA may deny zero-rating and claw back input tax credits and refunds where the export document bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or fails to demonstrate that the recipient was outside Canada or that the supply was not connected to Canadian property or users. Penalties include a gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the misstated amount subject to a CAD 100 minimum, daily compound interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, failure-to-file and false-return penalties, and criminal liability for fraudulent export claims. The normal four-year reassessment period may be extended or made unlimited where false statements or neglect, carelessness, or wilful default are established, allowing retrospective clawback of HST previously zero-rated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully free-floating, with complete current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, capital-repatriation permits, or profit-remittance licences. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends, interest, royalties, and disposal proceeds, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutorily 25%, reduced under income tax treaties such as the Canada-US Convention) and to standard Income Tax Act compliance, including T1135 foreign-property reporting when foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000 in cost. No import or conversion of foreign currency is mandated at point of entry; CAD/USD multi-currency operating accounts are standard at all Schedule I banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory holding or liquidation timeline. Export and services revenue may remain indefinitely in a foreign-currency operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Statutory conversion ratio: 0%; required timeline: none (0 days). For goods exports, an export declaration must be filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments above CAD 2,000; telematics and SaaS services exports require no customs declaration. Arm's-length transfer pricing and T106 information-return obligations apply to non-resident related-party transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and sole bank-note issuer. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority; prudential regulation of chartered banks resides with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/CTF supervision with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and sanctions administration with Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. Payments infrastructure (LVTS and ACSS) is operated by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium - legally permissible but ring-fenced. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) may operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses and, where applicable, must also obtain registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act, supervised by the Bank of Canada. They cannot accept deposits or provide CDIC-insured deposit protection in the manner of a chartered bank, and their offshore fund-safeguarding structures are not eligible for the CDIC guarantee. Viable supplementary use: aggregating US and European customer receivables from Stripe Billing, plus low-value cross-border vendor payouts. The primary settlement hub - capital injection, payroll, creditor payments, and FX hedging - should remain at a Schedule I chartered bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal plus Ontario headline for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC): 12.2% for qualifying active business income up to the CAD 500,000 small business deduction limit (federal 9% plus Ontario 3.2%); 26.5% for general active business income above the small business deduction limit (federal 15% after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction, plus Ontario 11.5%). Manufacturing and processing income in Ontario is taxed at a reduced Ontario rate of 10%, producing a 25.0% combined rate.
