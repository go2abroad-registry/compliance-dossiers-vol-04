Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal tax registrar and GST/HST administrator; digital portal: My Business Account at canada.ca. Ontario HST (8% provincial component) is administered by CRA; Ontario provincial tax filing by partners is handled through the Ontario Ministry of Finance ONT-TAXS portal at the partner level, not by the partnership.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the LP is resident in Canada or, if non-resident, whether it is carrying on business in Canada. Step 2: Complete Form RC1 (Canadian entities) or RC1A (non-resident entities) with the partnership agreement, Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration confirmation, and FSRA captive-insurer licensing evidence. Step 3: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and open program accounts: 'RT' for GST/HST where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding the small-supplier threshold or elects to register; 'RP' for payroll if applicable; 'RC' is not required because the LP is fiscally transparent. Step 4: Provide a Canadian agent address if the LP is a non-resident registrant. Step 5: File T5013 Partnership Information Return annually and allocate income to partners; the LP itself pays no federal Part I income tax. Step 6: Maintain Ontario extra-provincial limited partnership registration and the FSRA licence (or exemption order) under the Ontario Insurance Act before underwriting reinsurance risk.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The LP is fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level and taxed in each partner's hands. There is no entity-level federal or Ontario income tax on the LP. Canada generally taxes worldwide income of resident partners; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source business and property income. Part XIII withholding does not apply to partnership allocations but may apply to certain gross insurance premiums paid to non-resident insurers.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; legal form determines tax classification. Relevant elections: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations and partnerships to treat intercompany supplies as nil consideration for GST/HST; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(9) in limited circumstances; (iii) selected listed financial institution (SLFI) special attribution method elections under ETA Part IX, Division IV; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules (enacted by the Fall Economic Statement Implementation Act, 2023, applying to taxation years beginning on or after September 30, 2023) operate automatically without an elective check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD per calendar year (worldwide taxable supplies threshold under ETA s. 240(1)); exceeding it triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. However, a captive reinsurer whose supplies are exclusively exempt financial services is not a registrant unless it is a listed financial institution, elects registration, or makes taxable supplies. Captive reinsurers covering Canadian risks should assess SLFI status under ETA Part IX, Division IV.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Reinsurance is an exempt financial service under ETA Schedule V, Part VII, s. 1 (the definition of 'financial service' includes the providing of insurance and reinsurance). A supply of reinsurance to a non-resident recipient may be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part VII, s. 7 only where the service has no connection to Canadian real property, tangible personal property in Canada, or Canadian-situs financial instruments. Reinsurance of Canadian risks written for an affiliate will ordinarily remain exempt rather than zero-rated. Documentation must establish the recipient's non-residence and the foreign situs of the underlying risk.

  • Certified copy of the reinsurance treaty or slip evidencing risk situs and non-resident party
  • FSRA captive-insurer licence or exemption order under the Ontario Insurance Act
  • ETA-compliant tax invoice stating 'exempt financial service' or the Schedule VI, Part VII, s. 7 zero-rating citation
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank wire confirmation evidencing premium receipt from the foreign reinsured
  • OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation supporting the arm's length premium
  • Actuarial loss-reserve statement evidencing genuine risk transfer
  • Note: Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, and Inward Remittance Certificate are not applicable because reinsurance is an invisible service export, not a goods export.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax-stamp mandate exists in Canada. A GST/HST registrant must issue tax invoices containing the vendor's BN, date, description, consideration, GST/HST rate, and tax amount (ETA ss. 223-224.1). Invoices for exempt supplies must state the exemption; zero-rated supplies require the relevant Schedule VI citation. E-invoicing is voluntary and may follow the Pan-Canadian PDF standard or PEPPOL exchange; records must be retained for six years.

If a zero-rating or exemption claim is rejected, CRA reassesses the premium as taxable at 13% HST and assesses net tax under ETA s. 296 with interest. Gross-negligence penalties may apply under ETA s. 280(1) (greater of CAD 100 or 25% of understated net tax) and under ITA s. 163.2 (up to 50% of the income-tax benefit). Recipient input tax credits are clawed back because the invoice is invalid; the normal 4-year reassessment period is extended to 10 years for misrepresentation attributable to negligence. Captive arrangements that lack genuine risk transfer may also be recharacterized under ITA s. 245 (GAAR), with additional penalties under ITA s. 163(2) for repeat misreporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with no material distinction between capital and current account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely tradable on global markets, and there are no exchange control regulations requiring case-by-case approval for foreign investment, profit repatriation, dividends, interest, or return of capital. Profit remittance hurdles arise principally from tax compliance (withholding tax, Part XIII), transfer pricing documentation, and anti-money-laundering reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). No material convertibility risk exists for CAD-denominated captive reinsurance operations, provided the underlying instruments are bona fide and arm-length priced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Export receipts, insurance premiums, reinsurance recoveries, and other proceeds may be retained in CAD or foreign currency without forced conversion. Financial institutions must still file Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) and Large Cash Transaction Reports where applicable, and cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC. Captive reinsurance SPVs should maintain complete documentation of the underlying risk transfers to demonstrate that premium flows are legitimate reinsurance transactions rather than unregulated deposit-taking or financial intermediation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) are not directly regulated as banks in Canada and cannot offer deposit-taking services under the Bank Act. A captive reinsurance SPV may use an EMI for payment facilitation if the EMI is a registered Money Service Business (MSB) under PCMLTFA and holds a valid FINTRAC registration, or operates through a regulated Canadian bank sponsor. However, due to the higher-risk classification of captive reinsurance, most Canadian account-opening banks restrict the use of unregulated offshore EMIs for core escrow, reserve, and settlement accounts. A registered Canadian MSB or a bank-owned payment subsidiary is the more compliant and operationally viable route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the Ontario LP captive SPV should be routed via standard SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages from the parent/cedent's home jurisdiction correspondent bank to the Ontario SPV's CAD or USD operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire must include purpose codes consistent with the Bank Act and FINTRAC EFT reporting: for a capital contribution use code 'Capital Contribution for Reinsurance Captive', and for premium flows use 'Reinsurance Premium Payment under Master Policy XXX'. The receiving bank will verify the legal entity name, account number, the remitter's AML status, and alignment of the stated purpose with corporate registry filings. To prevent wire blocks, provide the Ontario LP's business number (BN), provincial registration, and certified incorporation/registration certificate. Commercial LCs for captive reinsurance are less common but are permissible where contractually required; they may be issued by the captive's bank on behalf of the SPV as a beneficiary or applicant. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.00% combined (federal 15.00% + Ontario provincial general 10.00%) applied to corporate partners' allocable income from the Limited Partnership; the LP itself is flow-through and pays no corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII domestic rate (s.212(1)); not directly applicable to a partnership distribution — profit allocations are taxable on the partner, no withholding applies to the allocation itself. If the LP is reclassified as a 'taxable Canadian corporation' (sham), the rate would apply to dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate FMV of non-arm's length cross-border transactions in a fiscal period; Master File: CAD 20,000,000 in gross revenue and specified reconciliations (CRA Form RC4723 and RC4054).

Economic substance scope: Although Canada has no domestic ESR law similar to Cayman, the CRA requires real insurance risk transfer, Canadian management, and an actuarial function under the Insurance Companies Act (Canada). Ontario's captive insurance licensing regime (Ontario Insurance Act, Part II) imposes substance conditions on captives — the LP must maintain books, records, and risk-management protocols in Ontario; permission for non-resident based LPs is conditional on the operation being directed from Ontario. The GAAR under s.245 can recharacterize a captive without economic substance, and an anti-treaty-shopping rule in the Canada-U.S. tax treaty denies treaty benefits where a Main Purpose Test is not satisfied. For a captive LP, substance includes a valid reinsurance agreement, actuarial pricing, loss reserves, settlements, and an independent board.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (ss.163, 239 ITA)

Statutory sanction: On indictment: imprisonment not exceeding 5 years per count; fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax adjustment (s.163(2)).

Director personal liability: The Ontario limited partnership shield does not protect a knowingly participating general partner; directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable to the CRA for taxes withheld, and may be personally served with a 'lien' and travel restraint order under the Federal Crown Liability and Proceedings Act; imprisonment is applicable after a first-class conviction.
Shell Company / Sham Entity

Statutory sanction: CRA may collapse the partnership as a sham, tax the foreign parent or offshore beneficiary directly, and apply s.247(3) transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the adjustment plus interest; the Ontario Financial Services Commission may revoke the captive licence and impose administrative monetary penalties.

Director personal liability: Where the parent is a foreign corporation, the CRA can permit collection from the Canadian general partner as 'absconding debtor'; directors may face civil restitution orders and a discretionary travel ban under the Extradition Act if funds are hidden in another jurisdiction.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC can issue an administrative penalty up to CAD 500,000 for a serious contravention; individuals who intentionally launder assets through the captive are liable on summary conviction to a fine of CAD 500,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 6 months; indictment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who commit or authorize the transactions are parties to the offence; the limited partnership veil is a legal fiction that does not shield a complaint against 'directing mind' when funds have been layering; courts can order restitution to FINTRAC and prohibit travel pending trial.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure (s.247)

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess and allocate a profit adjustment of 20%–30% of premium income; the 10% s.247(3) penalty accrues on the adjustment where the taxpayer has failed to make reasonable efforts to determine an arm's length result.

Director personal liability: Signing officers of the corporate general partner may be assessed a separate administrative penalty of CAD 1,000–25,000 under the Tax Court of Canada Rules for knowingly false documentation; in egregious cases, the CRA will issue a compliance order against the directors personally and lien their assets.
