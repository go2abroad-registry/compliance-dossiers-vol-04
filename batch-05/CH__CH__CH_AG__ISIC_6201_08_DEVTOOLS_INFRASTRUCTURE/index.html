Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug)

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (OR/CO), Articles 620-731 (Aktiengesellschaft), specifically Articles 629-637 (incorporation, capital, and organization); Commercial Register Ordinance (HRegV), Articles 2 and 102 ff.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Handelsregistereintragung) together with notarized articles of association and bank capital confirmation; submitted via the cantonal registry portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up before registration. The paid-in capital must be placed on a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) in the company's name and confirmed as being at the unrestricted disposal of the board.

Funding & unlock path: No mandatory civil defense or structural clearance applies to a non-industrial software office. The lessor must furnish a valid fire safety certificate for the office premises; the employer must register with the Swiss accident insurer (Suva or authorized private insurer) and comply with federal occupational safety requirements contained in the Labour Act (ArG) and accident prevention ordinances.

Paid-in capital rule: Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up before registration. The paid-in capital must be placed on a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) in the company's name and confirmed as being at the unrestricted disposal of the board.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC). Official portal: https://www.esty.admin.ch; UID/VAT e-service portal: https://www.gate.e-service.admin.ch. Cantonal tax offices (for an AG seated in Zug: Kantonales Steueramt Zug) are the competent authorities for cantonal and communal corporate income tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarially incorporate the AG and enter it in the Commercial Register; the registration authority procures the Swiss Business Identifier (UID/IDE/IDI, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) from the Federal Statistical Office. Step 2: The UID functions as the universal tax identification number and VAT number; Switzerland does not issue a separate company TIN. Step 3: Register for VAT via ESTV e-service (form 1065, Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuerpflicht) within 30 days after becoming taxable; mandatory when gross taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000 per business year (art. 10 VAT Act). Step 4: Corporate income tax registration is automatic through the Commercial Register notice to the cantonal tax authority; annual federal and cantonal tax returns follow. Step 5: Non-resident providers of DevTools/cloud services may instead use the simplified e-services registration at ESTV without establishing a Swiss registered seat.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Swiss AG is an opaque legal entity; no pass-through election exists. The AG pays federal income tax at 8.5% on after-tax profit (approximately 11% pre-tax effective) and cantonal/communal profit tax (Zug about 14.6%; Zurich 19.7-21% combined), i.e. an aggregate effective profit tax of roughly 11.9%-21%. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation for the Swiss-resident AG, with exemption/credit for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Swiss AG is an opaque legal entity; no pass-through election exists. The AG pays federal income tax at 8.5% on after-tax profit (approximately 11% pre-tax effective) and cantonal/communal profit tax (Zug about 14.6%; Zurich 19.7-21% combined), i.e. an aggregate effective profit tax of roughly 11.9%-21%. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation for the Swiss-resident AG, with exemption/credit for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. No tax-exempt zones apply to software/cloud companies; the historical privileged cantonal statuses are grandfathered under the 2020 reform.

Available election forms

(1) US-style check-the-box election is unavailable; entity classification is statutory. (2) Voluntary VAT registration election for small businesses with turnover below CHF 100,000 (art. 11 VAT Act). (3) Binding advance tax rulings from the cantonal authority and ESTV for SaaS/DevTools revenue qualification, transfer pricing and financing. (4) Participation relief (Beteiligungsabzug) for dividends and capital gains from participating interests of 10% or more. (5) Patent box election and additional R&D super-deduction (up to 150%) under the TRAF/OSTA 2020 reform. (6) Group taxation (ORG) election for consolidated Swiss groups with at least 80% cross-shareholdings and aligned fiscal years. (7) Hybrid mismatch rules are statutory (OECD/ATAD-aligned); elective treatment is not available, but an alternative characterization can be secured by advance ruling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 gross taxable turnover per business year from Swiss supplies for an established Swiss AG (art. 10 VAT Act); registration is voluntary below the threshold and for pure exporters. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register when worldwide e-services turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 and Swiss-derived turnover exceeds CHF 40,000 per year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% (zero) rate if they physically leave Switzerland and a Swiss customs export declaration (NCTS) is provided (art. 23(1) VAT Act). Services for foreign B2B recipients are zero-rated when the place of supply is at the recipient's seat under art. 18 VAT Act. A Swiss DevTools/SaaS exporter must retain (a) the foreign customer's contract, (b) its UID/VAT evidence, and (c) system/API delivery logs showing the service is provided from Switzerland to the foreign recipient. B2C digital services are outside the Swiss tax scope when the consumer is habitually resident abroad. The supplier keeps proportionate input VAT deduction, giving a structural net VAT refund position on export-heavy SaaS businesses.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (NCTS / PL-Exit summary declaration; required for tangible exports, not applicable to cloud services)
  • Commercial invoice with ISIC 6201 DevTools/API monitoring service description and foreign customer UID/VAT ID
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / airway bill (for physical exports only)
  • Executed software services agreement evidencing the foreign taxable person (B2B place-of-supply proof)
  • Platform/API delivery logs and access records demonstrating foreign location of the customer
  • SWIFT inward remittance certificate evidencing the foreign fund flow

No real-time fiscal clearance, no fiscal stamping and no SAF-T/API tax-clearance mandate exists. The legal e-invoice standard is the Swiss QR-Rechnung (QR-bill) aligned with the EN 16931 profile (CH-EN16931); federal suppliers must be invoiced electronically. Mandatory invoice content includes supplier name, address and UID, date, recipient, description and extent of the supply, consideration in CHF, and VAT rate. Records must be kept for 10 years.

The taxpayer bears the full burden of substantiating export/zero-rating facts (art. 69 VAT Act). Missing evidence triggers retroactive assessment at 8.1%/2.6% plus default interest at 4.5% p.a.; procedural infringements are fined up to CHF 30,000. Deliberate falsification is VAT evasion, subject to fines up to three times the tax evaded (art. 102/103 VAT Act); aggravated fraud involving forged documents is punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment (art. 104 VAT Act). The criminal limitation period for clawback is 10 years; fraudulent invoicing chains create joint and several liability (art. 76 VAT Act). ESTV audits substance in intra-group cross-border service flows and denies zero-rating for circular financing or substance-less shell arrangements. Note: Swiss law does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter reflects the standard international export invoice/settlement benchmark accepted in Swiss tax audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible under both current and capital account transactions. Switzerland maintains no general foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on cross-border capital movements, and no licensing requirements for the transfer of dividends, interest, or service fees to non-residents. Profit remittances are subject only to ordinary corporate income tax on the distributing entity and, where applicable, withholding tax at the statutory rate of 35% (reduced under applicable double tax treaties, often to 5% or 0% depending on the treaty partner and shareholding level). There are no capital controls, no approval from the SNB for most inbound/outbound payments, and no mandatory investment or conversion requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland does not prescribe any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain any currency and to hold such proceeds in foreign currency accounts at any domestic or cross-border bank. The SNB does not impose any requirement to surrender, repatriate, or convert export receivables into Swiss francs. The only applicable framework concerns anti-money laundering transparency: the receiving Swiss bank must have the underlying commercial invoice and shipment documentation on file for due diligence purposes, but this does not trigger mandatory FX conversion.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for fully offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) without a Swiss FINMA license or passport. Legal constraints: a foreign EMI can only hold Swiss franc accounts at Swiss banks if it is itself a regulated client of the Swiss bank. The entity must either (a) hold a FINMA-recognized banking or securities license in its home jurisdiction and undergo the Swiss bank's cross-border due diligence, or (b) open a direct account with a Swiss-licensed fintech company (FINMA fintech license) or a tier-1 bank. Unregulated EMIs are generally rejected by Swiss banks because of the prohibition on accepting deposits from the public without a license and the strict AMLA (Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act) requirements. For a low-risk IT company, using a regulated Swiss bank as the primary clearing hub and a FINMA-compliant fintech for fast API-based treasury operations is more viable than relying on offshore EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: the Swiss AG issues a board resolution and an equity subscription agreement (Zeichnungsschein). The foreign investor remits the CHF or EUR equivalent via SWIFT MT103 to the Swiss bank, using payment purpose code 110 (Gewährung von Darlehen und Krediten) or 111 (Einlagen und Kapitalanlagen) if it is a loan; for a capital increase without notarial act, use code 100 (Transfer within the company group) or code 510 (acquisition of shares). The bank requires the investor to submit the purpose code and the underlying contract; the Swiss bank will additionally ask for a written confirmation from the company that the funds are not criminal proceeds. For non-EU investors, the bank will perform a full source of wealth assessment and may require the foreign bank's SWIFT confirmation or the actual account statement showing provenance. The transfer must not take place via an unregulated crypto exchange unless the final source is fully bank-verified; otherwise the receiving bank will freeze the funds until a chain-analysis report is provided.The primary clearing rail for international trade settlements is the SWIFT network, using the customer credit transfer message MT103 for full detail and straight-through processing. For larger disbursements or intra-bank liquidity funding, MT202 COV may be used when the ordering customer is different from the beneficiary's bank. For commercial trade with foreign vendors, an irrevocable Letter of Credit (LC) may be opened via the Swiss bank, typically in accordance with UCP 600 (Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits). The Swiss bank will also support SEPA instant credit transfers (SCT Inst) in EUR and Swiss QR-bill for domestic payments. For cross-border disbursements exceeding a franc equivalent of CHF 1 million, the bank performs an enhanced due diligence check on the payee and the underlying contract; the payment must carry a valid trade purpose code (e.g., 1001 for hardware and software licences, 2041 for cloud service platform fees, 5012 for marketing services).Outright FX forwards (fixed date and FX forward swaps) in major currencies (USD, EUR, CHF, GBP, JPY)Currency swaps (cross-currency swaps) for treasury refinancing or long-term operational FX managementCurrency options (European-style plain-vanilla options) for downside protection on large trade settlements, with no exotic barrier conditions without special FINMA derivatives approvalNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for illiquid emerging-market currencies where a deliverable forward is unavailable in the Swiss onshore market; for CHF pairs, only deliverable forwards are standard because CHF is fully convertibleSynthetic FX forwards via a combination of spot and forward deposits at the same Swiss bank are permissible as an internal treasury optimisation product

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 1 million in related party transactions or CHF 50 million in total turnover

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but requires actual substance for tax residency and to avoid treaty abuse. The company must have a physical office, employees, and decision-making in Switzerland. For holding companies, substance requirements include adequate premises, personnel, and board meetings in Switzerland.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to CHF 250,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years; additional penalties for repeated offenses

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participate in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 3 years
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and penalties up to CHF 100,000; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes and penalties, and may be subject to travel bans and imprisonment for fraud
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CHF 500,000 for the company; criminal prosecution of responsible individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for money laundering; civil liability for damages
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, interest on underpaid tax, and penalties up to 30% of the additional tax assessed

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for negligence if they fail to ensure proper TP documentation; civil penalties and potential criminal charges for tax evasion
