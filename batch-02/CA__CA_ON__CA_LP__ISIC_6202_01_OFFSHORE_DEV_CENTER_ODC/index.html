Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — official portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration-online; My Business Account (MyBA) for GST/HST filings; Ontario Ministry of Finance (ONT-TAXS Online) for Ontario provincial tax programs; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export customs declarations and clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990, c. L.7) with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services via the Ontario Business Registry, obtaining the Ontario registration number and firm name reservation. Step 2 - Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number'), disclosing the Ontario LP registration number, registered office address, full legal names of all general and limited partners, each partner's jurisdiction of residence, ownership percentages, principal business activity (NAICS 541512 - Computer Systems Design Services), and expected taxable supplies. Step 3 - CRA issues the BN with program account suffixes: 'RT' (GST/HST registrant), 'RP' (payroll deductions if Canadian employees are hired), and 'RC' (corporate income tax; normally not required because the partnership is fiscally transparent and files T5013 instead). Step 4 - Non-resident partners who themselves carry on business in Canada must obtain their own BN/GST/HST registration through the same RC1 process. Step 5 - Ontario-specific: register for Employer Health Tax (EHT) via ONT-TAXS only if Ontario payroll exceeds the C$1,000,000 exemption threshold; no separate provincial sales tax registration is required because the Ontario 8% provincial portion is administered by CRA under the Harmonized Sales Tax. Step 6 - Commence filing obligations: annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and periodic GST/HST net-file returns (monthly/quarterly per assigned cycle).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax purposes; income, losses, credits, and taxable capital gains flow through to partners at the partner level, and the LP itself is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Exception: a 'publicly traded partnership' can be deemed a corporation under ITA subsection 197(1). For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' (registrant) and must account for net tax on its own RT account.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification regime; tax status follows the juridical form. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) ITA subsection 249.1(9) partnership fiscal-period election — permits a partnership to elect a non-calendar fiscal period, subject to conditions; (ii) Excise Tax Act subsection 156(2) closely-related registrant election — treats qualifying intercompany supplies between the LP and related registrants as not being a taxable supply, simplifying GST/HST administration; (iii) Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention Article IV(7) fiscally transparent entity recognition — permits U.S. LLC/limited-partner members of a Canadian LP to be taxed as partners where substantiated, and supports same treatment for non-resident partners from treaty states; (iv) Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) under ITA subsection 247(4) with CRA to prospectively bind transfer-pricing methodology for the ODC's cost-plus cross-border service arrangements; (v) anti-hybrid rules under ITA sections 18.2, 18.4, 20.2, 21.2, and 91.3 apply automatically and cannot be elected out; compliant dual-character tax documentation must be maintained to avoid deduction denial.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (small-supplier threshold). A person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when worldwide taxable supplies — including zero-rated export supplies — exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over any four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident vendors of software/digital services are subject to the same C$30,000 registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of software engineering, development, QA, maintenance, and support services provided by the Ontario LP to a non-resident client is zero-rated where the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and is not carrying on business in Canada, and the service is not supplied in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Physical export of development hardware/servers is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 1-3 upon satisfactory evidence of export. For intercompany ODC arrangements, the cost-plus transfer price must satisfy arm's length standards, and the non-resident recipient must annually provide a non-registrant declaration and certificate of tax residence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CAED, Form B13A) filed electronically with CBSA for physical goods/equipment exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment of goods to the non-resident consignee (goods exports only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency receipt from the non-resident payer within the settlement window
  • Executed offshore development agreement, statement of work, and itemized commercial invoices naming the non-resident recipient and describing the software services supplied (required for pure-services exports where no customs declaration exists)
  • Non-resident status proof: foreign charter/formation documents, foreign tax residency certificate, and signed GST/HST non-registrant declaration

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no government pre-approval of invoices in Canada. CRA accepts paper and electronic records; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) and the PEPPOL e-invoice network are voluntary B2B interoperability standards. GST/HST books and records supporting zero-rated supplies must be retained for six years and made available to CRA electronically on request. Ontario does not operate a separate SAF-T, clearance, or e-invoicing approval system; the Ontario 8% provincial HST component is remitted to CRA on the consolidated federal/provincial return.

CRA reassessment windows: 4 years for normal reassessment, 7 years for misrepresentation/negligence, and unlimited for fraud. Unsupported zero-rating claims are reversed to 13% HST, assessed with prescribed-rate arrears interest, and subject to penalties: ETA section 285 false-statement penalty (approximately 10% of the misstated net tax), ETA section 280 failure-to-file penalties, a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the partner-level income tax under ITA subsection 163(2), and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA section 327 and ITA section 239. Intercompany pricing misstatements additionally trigger the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under ITA subsection 247(3), and CBSA may separately adjust custom values of exported goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; there are no restrictions on the debit or credit of foreign currency for ordinary business. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners that constitute dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees may be subject to Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty reduction; partnership income allocations are generally taxable at the partner level under Part I, and non-resident partners should file Canadian returns or otherwise rely on treaty protection. Inbound capital contributions into an Ontario limited partnership are not subject to FX conversion, exchange control approval, or monetary sterilisation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist in Canada: export proceeds may be freely retained in USD or converted to CAD without mandatory timeline or conversion ratio. For a Canadian software and services exporter, invoicing in USD and maintaining a USD operating account is permissible. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC by the bank or money services business; the underlying export proceeds themselves carry no surrender obligation and no central bank conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control policy oversight resides jointly with the Minister of Finance and the Foreign Exchange Revolving Fund, with prudential supervision of banks exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority; the de facto enforcement framework for cross-border payment integrity is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High as a payment rail, but not a substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank. Platforms such as Wise Business or Payoneer may offer faster cross-border settlement and multi-currency sub-accounts; however, any entity carrying on a money-services business in Canada must be registered with FINTRAC and must comply with Canadian AML/KYC and large-transfer reporting rules. Foreign domiciled EMIs without a Canadian registered presence are legally constrained from holding operating cash for a Canadian LP and cannot provide CDIC deposit insurance. Recommended structure: maintain a primary high-tier bank account for settlement integrity and capital escrow, with an EMI used only for vendor disbursement productivity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: originating foreign parent issues an SWIFT MT103 from its own bank to the LP's Canadian Schedule I bank account. The beneficiary must identify the Ontario LP and designated account number. Field 70 of the MT103 should clearly state: INBOUND CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO ONTARIO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP, NOT A REIMBURSEMENT OR MANAGEMENT FEE. Canada does not mandate a public purpose-code system; when the receiving bank uses ISO 20022 purpose fields, the bank may map the transaction to its internal capital-contribution code or 'AGCM' for capital-market activity if accepted. The bank will perform OFAC/EU/UN sanctions filtering and FINTRAC large-EFT reporting if the contribution is at or above CAD 10,000. The LP should provide a capital contribution resolution, bank instruction letter, and the general partner's KYC pack at least 5 business days before funding to avoid unnecessary hold. Funds may be received in CAD or USD; if received in USD and CAD is required, use a wire-linked spot conversion at the receiving bank rather than an unlinked FX trade.Use Canadian bank-hosted SWIFT MT103 for cross-border settlements to contractors and remote engineering hubs. If the LP invoices and holds export proceeds in USD, matching USD disbursements avoid FX conversion; otherwise execute a same-day spot FX trade and remit in CAD. Payments Canada Lynx and the Real-Time Rail serve domestic CAD clearing and cannot clear cross-border obligations; a SWIFT MT103 is the standard cross-border clearing instrument. Commercial letters of credit are generally unnecessary for software engineering services; where contractually required, an irrevocable documentary credit via SWIFT MT700 payable at sight may be used only against conforming invoices and deliverables. For every outbound disbursement, retain the contract, statement of work, invoice, acceptance certificate, and counterparty beneficial-ownership evidence to support the bank's transaction monitoring. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15.0%) and Ontario (11.5%) general corporate rate applied to corporate partners; the Ontario Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and does not pay corporation tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax applies to any dividend paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; however, the Ontario Limited Partnership does not pay dividends per se. Profits distributed to non-resident limited partners are normally allocated Canadian-source active business income subject to Part I tax at the partner level, not Part XIII, unless the structure elects to treat the LP as a corporation under relevant Canadian tax attributes or passes through investment-type income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer-pricing law requires a business to prepare contemporaneous, complete transfer-pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) when non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate for the taxation year; additionally, Form T106 (Information Return on Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be electronically filed for aggregate transactions above that threshold. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000 must file Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting through CRA.
