Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; Revenu Québec for Quebec corporate income tax, Quebec Sales Tax (QST), and provincial payroll levies. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Revenu Québec Business Portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/business/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated or newly formed Canadian Business Corporation must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by filing Form RC1; (2) activate the GST/HST account (RT), payroll account (RP), and import/export account (RM) as applicable; (3) register with Revenu Québec for the QST number (RN), Quebec Pension Plan (QPIP) and Health Services Fund (QHSF) accounts, and the Quebec corporate income tax file using the applicable LM-101-V registration package; (4) provide articles of incorporation, certified English/French translations if executed abroad, evidence of registered office in Quebec, director identification, and completed Canada-Quebec Revenue Agreement forms to authorize coordinated CRA-Revenu Québec registration; (5) complete extra-provincial registration under the Quebec Business Corporations Act if incorporated under another jurisdiction before tax accounts are activated; and (6) appoint a Canadian resident agent for service if the directorate is non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is a resident corporation taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act and under the Quebec Taxation Act; no look-through or pass-through treatment is available to foreign shareholders; the corporation is subject to Canadian federal and Quebec provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, subject to the foreign affiliate surrender rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' classification election; the domestic legal form of the entity controls federal and Quebec income tax characterization. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 12.1, 12.2, 18.4, 18.5, 18.6 and Quebec counterparts) restrict double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes in cross-border financing. Available statutory elections include: (a) section 216 election for non-residents earning rental income from Canadian real property to be taxed on a net basis; (b) section 217 election for non-residents receiving participating employment remuneration; (c) section 85 rollover election for incorporating property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (d) subsection 256(2) associated corporation elections to allocate the small business deduction; and (e) GST/HST section 156 election to treat supplies between closely related registrants as made for nil consideration, with the corresponding QST election under the Quebec Sales Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (Canada) and CAD 30,000 (Quebec) – annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies of goods and services, calculated exclusive of GST/HST and QST, with aggregation rules for associated persons; a person who is not a 'small supplier' must register, and a non-resident carrying on business in Canada that exceeds the threshold must register; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec GST/HST and QST registrant zero-rates exports of the digital X-ray flat-panel detectors (tangible personal property classified under ISIC 3250 / NAICS 334517) when title and risk pass to a non-resident purchaser and the goods are physically exported from Canada: delivered by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, consigned by the supplier to a common carrier for direct export, or delivered to a licensed customs broker/forwarder for same-day export under ETA Sch. VI Part V and the corresponding QST zero-rating rules. Services rendered in respect of the exported detectors, including installation and commissioning performed outside Canada, are eligible for zero-rating under the exported-goods and non-resident-recipient service tests. The zero-rated amounts must be reported as taxable supplies at 0% on GST/HST and QST returns, with export evidence retained to substantiate the claims.

  • CBSA export declaration electronically filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management portal for shipments over CAD 2,000 in value
  • Commercial invoice / OEM sales contract stating HS classification, Incoterms, unit value, buyer identity, and destination
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or cargo manifest (truck/rail) evidencing the physical international movement of the goods
  • Proof of export such as a signed carrier receipt, terminal export stamp, or foreign customs entry confirmation
  • Delivery acknowledgement / proof of delivery signed by the foreign buyer or offshore logistics warehouse
  • SWIFT MT103 or TT wire credit confirmation, or Letter of Credit settlement documents, demonstrating receipt of foreign consideration
  • Exporters' ledger reconciliation linking each zero-rated invoice to the corresponding export declaration and payment record

As of the 2025 assessment cycle, Canada and Quebec do not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or tax-authority pre-approval of e-invoices. Registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information under ETA s. 223 and the QST regulations, file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, remit QST electronically for large businesses, and retain books and records for six years in Canada. A voluntary pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS-3.0 e-invoice framework is being rolled out for government and business interoperability, but it remains a standards-based supply-chain measure rather than a statutory tax-clearance mandate.

The CRA and Revenu Québec subject export zero-rating claims to intensive audit; where the requisite export documentation is deficient, the supply is recharacterized as a taxable domestic supply and the GST/HST and QST become payable at 14.975%, with arrears interest and penalties computed retroactively from the original filing date. Reassessment windows are generally four years, but remain unlimited where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Civil penalties include the GST/HST gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated, comparable QST penalties under Quebec law, and transfer-pricing adjustments under Income Tax Act s. 247 where offshore pricing or prolonged non-repatriation of proceeds exceeding 180 days is present. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may be prosecuted as tax evasion, exposing the corporation and its directors to fines of up to CAD 100,000 on summary conviction (with enhanced fines up to 200% of the tax evaded) or CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment, public naming by the tax authorities, director personal liability, and cross-border information exchange under the Canada-US Tax Convention and OECD frameworks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current account and capital account convertibility; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and convertible; there are no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital for non-residents, provided that all applicable Canadian federal/provincial taxes and withholding taxes are satisfied; filings under section 116 of the Income Tax Act may be required for disposition of certain taxable Canadian property; no FX licensing or exchange control approval is required for cross-border trade settlements or capital flows

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert export receipts into CAD; exporters may maintain foreign currency bank accounts in Canada and retain balances in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies; no mandatory repatriation timeline exists; ordinary record-keeping and reporting obligations apply under the currency reporting regime for cross-border monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no dedicated foreign exchange control authority, as Canada maintains no exchange control regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility: foreign digital EMI entities (e.g., UK/European e-money institutions) are not authorized to provide deposit-taking or settlement services in Canada without registration as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and membership in FINTRAC reporting; licensed Canadian EMIs/MSBs exist but generally lack full clearing/in-house account infrastructure; direct global EMI onboarding acceptable only as supplementary collection/payment gateway, not as primary account or clearing rail

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (Federal 15.0% + Quebec 11.5%); federal general rate is 38% reduced by 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under ITA subsection 212(2) on any taxable dividend paid or credited by a corporation resident in Canada to a non-resident beneficial owner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (goods, services, royalties, loans) exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated gross revenue greater than CAD 100,000,000; Form T106 – Summary of Non-Arm's Length Transactions must be filed when the aggregate value of reportable transactions exceeds CAD 100,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to offshore-financial-centre ESR regimes; however, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the Quebec General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Tax Administration Act, s.1079.1.1) invalidate transactions without genuine commercial substance, including conduit royalty structures and shell invoicing. CRA and Revenu Québec require substantive undertaking in Quebec: employees, physical premises, R&D documentation, board minutes, order-taking, and risk management. A Quebec subsidiary that is merely an invoice-through entity will be re-characterized, its intercompany deductions denied, and the parent exposed to Part XIII withholding reassessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1) and Quebec Tax Administration Act s.56, conviction carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment for up to 5 years on indictment (2 years on summary conviction), plus CRA gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and interest from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false return are personally criminally liable; courts routinely order restitution, a freezing of assets, passport surrender, and a travel ban while on bail; conviction creates a criminal record and bars the director from serving as officer of any Canadian licensed medical device entity.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Unsupported Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price under ITA s.247(2), apply a penalty under s.247(3) (documentation penalty of CA$1,000 per month up to 24 months plus 10% to 50% of the gross negligence adjustment), reassess the Part I tax, and deny the related deduction; late interest runs from the original due date of the underpaid instalments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false intercompany pricing can be prosecuted for gross negligence; while the primary liability remains corporate, CRA may assert director liability for unremitted withholding on the reassessed deemed dividends and seek civil restitution from the foreign parent under the doctrine of economic compulsion or agency.
Artificial Shell Structure / General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: GAAR vacates the transaction: the corporate income is re-attributed to the parent, SR&ED and M&P credits are denied, Part XIII withholding is assessed at 25% on deemed dividends, and Quebec may impose a 20% penalty on the avoidance benefit; limitation periods are waived for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Where a director is the architect of the artificial structure, courts may pierce the corporate veil under Quebec Civil Code oppression and CRA's director liability provisions; civil restitution can extend to personal assets, and the CRA can seek an international travel prohibition pending criminal fraud proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering & Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Excise Tax Act s.323 and Quebec Tax Administration Act s.92.10 impose automatic civil liability on directors for unremitted GST/QST and source deductions; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative penalties can reach CA$500,000 for a corporation; money laundering prosecutions carry imprisonment up to 10 years and forfeiture of the imaging equipment and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable without proof of intent for unremitted source deductions and GST/QST unless they exercised a standard of care (due diligence); a federal arrest warrant for money laundering triggers an Interpol Red Notice travel ban, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from holding corporate office in Canada.
