Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account, administers federal corporate income tax and GST/HST registration. Revenu Québec administers Québec corporate income tax, Québec sales tax (QST), and related Québec tax registrations through its online services. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers commercial import and export reporting through the Canada Border Services Agency Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

E-government filing portal

A corporation must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts, including an RC0001 GST/HST account and, where relevant, an RM import-export account. Registration is generally completed through CRA Business Registration Online, by telephone, or through applicable incorporation and registration channels. A non-resident corporation may register by filing the prescribed CRA registration information and providing incorporation documents, legal name, registered address, directors, ownership and business-activity information, and authorized-representative details. A corporation carrying on business in Québec or making supplies subject to QST generally registers with Revenu Québec for a Québec identification number and QST account, unless a statutory small-supplier or other exception applies. Importers and exporters must establish the applicable CBSA CARM client account and obtain any required importer/exporter program identifiers. Canada does not issue a standalone EU-style VAT number; the GST/HST account number is generally the relevant indirect-tax identifier and is used with the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxable entities and do not have a general United States-style check-the-box election to be treated as partnerships or disregarded entities. A corporation may qualify for the small business deduction, associated-corporation allocation, capital-cost allowance elections, scientific research and experimental development incentives, and other statutory tax elections, subject to eligibility and prescribed filings. Certain qualifying Canadian-resident corporations may elect under specific provisions to alter the treatment of transactions, reorganizations, amalgamations, wind-ups, or foreign-affiliate arrangements. Canada has anti-hybrid mismatch rules, including restrictions on deductions and foreign tax-credit or dividend treatment for specified hybrid mismatch arrangements; these rules are not an elective entity-classification regime. A Canadian corporation generally remains subject to entity-level taxation even where it has non-resident shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. For GST/HST, a person is generally a small supplier if taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to sector-specific rules. Québec generally uses a CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST purposes, subject to statutory rules and exceptions. The Québec standard combined consumer rate is 14.975%, comprising 5.000% GST and 9.975% QST. Registration may be required earlier or independently for non-residents, specified supplies, taxable real-property or admissions activities, platform-based supplies, or other prescribed circumstances. A corporation that voluntarily registers may be required to collect tax and may claim eligible input tax credits or input tax refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST, goods supplied in Canada and exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or transfer of possession outside Canada or shipment by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, depending on the applicable rule. Goods supplied to a non-resident for export may qualify only if the specific regulatory conditions are met, including documentary evidence and restrictions on use in Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where they satisfy the statutory exported-service rules; services relating to tangible personal property situated in Canada, real property in Canada, or activities performed in Canada may remain taxable, subject to specific exceptions. For QST, exports of movable property and qualifying services may be zero-rated where the Québec statutory place-of-supply and export conditions are met. A fulfillment or kitting provider must distinguish its own taxable logistics service from the sale or export of customer-owned goods. Input tax credits for GST/HST and input tax refunds for QST generally require valid supporting documentation, commercial-use nexus, and prescribed records. Zero-rating can be denied where goods are consumed, used, or retained in Canada, where the recipient is not eligible, or where export evidence is incomplete.

  • CBSA export declaration or applicable electronic export reporting record, including the required export reporting reference where reporting is mandatory
  • Commercial invoice or equivalent sales and shipment documentation identifying the exporter, consignee, description, quantity, value, destination, and contractual terms
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier document evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Packing list and warehouse or fulfillment records linking the exported goods to the relevant customer order
  • Proof of delivery or carrier confirmation showing receipt or delivery outside Canada
  • Customer purchase order, contract, sales agreement, or written export instructions establishing the commercial purpose and destination
  • Import entry, customs clearance, or equivalent destination-country evidence where reasonably available
  • GST/HST and QST tax invoices or credit notes, as applicable, showing zero tax and the statutory basis or transaction description
  • Payment records, bank statements, merchant settlement reports, or other commercial evidence supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not generally a Canadian statutory document
  • Inventory, serial-number, lot, and goods-movement records demonstrating that the goods were not used or consumed in Canada before export

Canada and Québec do not generally impose a nationwide real-time electronic-invoice clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system for ordinary B2B fulfillment, kitting, packaging, warehousing, or logistics invoices. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information when required to support GST/HST input tax credits or QST input tax refunds, including supplier identity and registration number, invoice date, recipient information where applicable, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount. Electronic records are generally acceptable if integrity, accessibility, retention, and audit requirements are satisfied. GST/HST and QST returns are filed through CRA and Revenu Québec portals, and certain registrants may be required to file electronically. Import and export declarations are submitted through CBSA systems, including CARM where applicable. Québec-specific mandatory e-invoicing requirements may apply to particular public-sector or government-procurement arrangements, but no general private-sector real-time clearance requirement applies to this business model.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange-control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days, and no general statutory inward-remittance certificate is required solely to substantiate GST/HST or QST zero-rating. Tax authorities may nevertheless request contracts, customs records, carrier evidence, proof of destination, inventory records, customer identity, payment evidence, and operational records. If zero-rating is unsupported or incorrectly claimed, the supplier may be assessed the applicable GST/HST or QST, related interest, and penalties, and may lose associated input tax credits or input tax refunds. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess prior reporting periods within statutory reassessment periods and may extend or reopen periods in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful blindness, fraud, or other statutory exceptions. GST/HST and QST penalties can apply for false statements, gross negligence, failure to file, failure to remit, failure to maintain records, or failure to provide information. Deliberate tax evasion or fraudulent documentation may trigger civil fraud penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under applicable federal or Québec law. Customs non-compliance may result in administrative monetary penalties, seizure, accounting adjustments, reassessment of duties and taxes, and enforcement action by CBSA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act. Canada has not invoked foreign-exchange controls since the 1950s; the Canadian dollar floats freely and there is no central-bank approval requirement for capital movements. Equity subscription, shareholder loans, dividends, royalties, intercompany trade payments, and repatriation of proceeds are all unrestricted. Profit remittance from a Quebec corporation is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced to 5%-15% by tax treaties) and to solvency tests under the Business Corporations Act (Quebec) or the Canada Business Corporations Act; no exchange clearance or central-bank permit is required. The Investment Canada Act may apply only to large acquisitions or control-of-business reviews, which are not engaged by the creation of a small kitting-and-fulfilment subsidiary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export or collection receipts. An ISIC 5210 cross-border kitting/fulfilment entity may denominate invoices freely in CAD, USD, EUR, or other currencies and may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or foreign-currency bank accounts. The Bank of Canada does not require conversion, and exporters are not obligated to repatriate funds within any period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); primary federal bank regulator is the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the Department of Finance Canada has policy authority over the currency; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with material legal constraints. Deposit-taking in Canada is a regulated activity reserved for banks licensed under the Bank Act (Schedules I/II/III), federal trust and loan companies, and provincial credit unions. Offshore digital EMIs, including Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer, may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered money-services businesses (MSBs); they may facilitate cross-border payment collection, FX conversion, and vendor disbursements but cannot serve as the primary Capital Escrow Account because funds held with an unlicensed EMI are not bank deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and are held in pooled omnibus accounts at chartered banks. Recommended treasury architecture: maintain a Schedule I bank account as the legal head treasury and escrow account, and deploy Airwallex solely as a payment-facilitation rail for low-cost cross-border settlements with explicit reconciliation controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the equity injection as a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank) into the Canadian Capital Escrow Account with the beneficiary account listed on the Lynx/RTGS rail. Payment details must reference the shareholder resolution and state 'Equity subscription / authorized share capital' to avoid mischaracterization as taxable consideration. The bank will require a board resolution, a direction letter, a source-of-funds declaration, and the executed subscription agreement. For incoming USD wires, explicitly instruct the receiving bank to settle without auto-conversion to preserve the USD balance; for CAD conversion, apply a separately negotiated spot rate. US-sourced transfers initiated as cross-border ACH will clear at a less favorable rate and are acceptable only for operational cash, not for capital injection.Supplier and contractor payments: SWIFT MT103 wire instructions for global non-CAD disbursements; Canadian-dollar transactions clear via Payments Canada Lynx (large-value real-time gross settlement) or ACSS (retail batch for lower-value payments); USD disbursements settle through U.S. correspondent banks such as BMO Harris, RBC/US, or TD Bank, N.A., via Fedwire or CHIPS, or via Cross-Border ACH for recurring small payments. For high-value kitting subcontracts, use an irrevocable confirmed commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) or standby letters of credit for performance guarantees. Real-Time Rail (RTR), Canada's modern real-time payment system, is permissible for immediate low-value CAD disbursements but should not replace MT103 for auditable cross-border trade settlement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility, with material legal constraints. Deposit-taking in Canada is a regulated activity reserved for banks licensed under the Bank Act (Schedules I/II/III), federal trust and loan companies, and provincial credit unions. Offshore digital EMIs, including Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer, may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered money-services businesses (MSBs); they may facilitate cross-border payment collection, FX conversion, and vendor disbursements but cannot serve as the primary Capital Escrow Account because funds held with an unlicensed EMI are not bank deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and are held in pooled omnibus accounts at chartered banks. Recommended treasury architecture: maintain a Schedule I bank account as the legal head treasury and escrow account, and deploy Airwallex solely as a payment-facilitation rail for low-cost cross-border settlements with explicit reconciliation controls.
