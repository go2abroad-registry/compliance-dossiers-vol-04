Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), with corporate account administration through the federal My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO); export declaration filing is effected through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Ontario corporate tax and the Ontario HST component are collected by the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or required to collect GST/HST must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and the applicable program accounts. Procedure: (1) submit Form RC1 Request for a Business Number via mail, fax or online through the CRA Business Registration Online portal; (2) provide certified copies of foreign incorporation documents, constitutional documents, and any applicable non-resident declaration forms; (3) CRA issues a nine-digit BN with program-account suffixes, e.g., RC0001 for corporate income tax, RT0001 for GST/HST and RP0001 for payroll; (4) a GST/HST registration is mandatory where the non-resident makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or falls within the non-resident digital-services mandatory registration framework; (5) a payroll deduction account is required if the corporation employs crew or administrative staff resident in Canada; (6) no separate Ontario TIN exists, as Ontario corporate income tax is administered federally through the same BN. The ETA registration is the sole indirect tax identifier, and the Canada Customs invoice number / export reporting number performs a supplementary identification role for physical exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian-resident Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income. There is no statutory flow-through classification for a business corporation, and Ontario generally conforms to the federal entity-level model.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box pass-through election; corporate classification is statutory. Available elections relevant to this entity include: (1) the s. 256(2) Income Tax Act election to deem corporations associated for the purpose of sharing the small-business deduction; (2) the GST/HST Quick Method election (s. 156 Excise Tax Act) and other net-tax calculation elections; (3) the s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a Canadian return on net rental or royalty income; (4) the s. 216(4) election for a non-resident corporation to compute Part I tax on Canadian-source resource or real property income on a net basis; (5) the s. 89(11) capital dividend account designation; and (6) the s. 45(3) foreign-currency translation election. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are governed by the automatic disallowance and deemed-deduction rules in Income Tax Act ss. 18.4 to 18.6 and ss. 20.1 to 20.2, with no elective mechanism available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). Registration is mandatory when taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident supplier whose Canadian-sourced supplies within the digital or intangible-property framework exceed the threshold may be required to register regardless of the small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services made to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not a service of acting as agent or arranging for a taxable supply in respect of such property. Crew recruitment, maritime seafarer placement, payroll administration and vessel crew management services invoiced by the Ontario corporation to a non-resident shipowner or manning principal qualify as zero-rated export services where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not supplied for consumption in Canada. The zero-rating of exported goods is available under Schedule VI, ss. 6(1), 7 and 16, subject to proof of export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System) filed with CBSA for physical export of goods; not required for pure service exports but recommended for mixed service-and-supplies contracts
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Air Waybill or cargo manifest evidencing shipment from Canada where tangible goods accompany the crew management service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of foreign-exchange settlement (bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident principal
  • Signed crew management or seafarer recruitment agreement identifying the non-resident shipowner, vessel name, flag state and IMO number
  • CRA-compliant zero-rated GST/HST invoice (Form GST/HST invoice) containing the prescribed statutory particulars and the non-resident recipient identification

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or government pre-approval e-invoicing regime. GST/HST invoices are self-issued and must contain the prescribed particulars under Excise Tax Act s. 223(2) and Regulations 211 and 212, including the supplier name and Business Number, recipient name, date of invoice, consideration and tax charged. Supporting records must be retained for six years. The CRA supports voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0), but issuance remains contractual and no digital stamp or clearance API is required.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims with particular focus on services supplied to non-residents. Failure to substantiate the export or non-resident status results in reassessment charging uncollected GST/HST at the Ontario HST rate plus interest. Civil penalties include Excise Tax Act s. 280 (false statements or omissions in a return: 5% of the understated tax for the first occurrence and 5% for each subsequent occurrence) and s. 285 (failure to provide required information: fines up to CAD 500). Criminal evasion under s. 327.01 exposes the corporation and its officers to fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. Retrospective clawback is not limited to the usual four-year reassessment period where negligence or misrepresentation is established, and separate penalties under the Customs Act may apply to false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada maintains no exchange-control law; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no statute imposes limits on the availability of foreign exchange, the conversion of CAD into other currencies, or the cross-border movement of funds. Non-resident shareholders may freely invest in, capitalize, and receive distributions from an Ontario corporation without Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval. The only transactional constraints are (i) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting obligations applicable to international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) Canadian autonomous sanctions and United Nations asset-freeze obligations, and (iii) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, which imposes non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, and certain deemed payments (statutory rate 25%, generally reduced to 5%-15% under tax treaties such as Canada-U.S. and Canada-U.K.). Profit remittance by a ship-crew recruitment agency is unrestricted in substance, but the Ontario corporation must withhold and remit under subsection 215(1), file NR4 information returns, and instruct the paying bank with accurate SWIFT remittance coding so the bank can verify withholding where the payment is characterized as a dividend.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement on export proceeds. An Ontario ship-crew recruitment agency may invoice foreign shipowners and vessel managers in USD, EUR, GBP, SGD, or any freely convertible currency and may retain those receipts indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I Canadian bank or in an approved offshore treasury account. There is no minimum conversion ratio, no mandatory settlement timeline, and no requirement to file an export-proceeds declaration with the National revenue authority or the central bank. For income-tax purposes, the corporation must express its taxable income in CAD, either through normal CAD functional-currency accounting or through an Income Tax Act section 261 foreign-functional-currency election, and foreign-exchange gains and losses are recognized on the revenue account in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles. For GST/HST purposes, the export of crew-recruitment services to non-residents can qualify for zero-rating only where the services are supplied to a non-resident and satisfy the documentary requirements of the Excise Tax Act, including the maintenance of contracts, invoices, and evidence that the service is performed outside Canada or is an exported service.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), headquartered in Ottawa, is the central bank and the sole issuer of Canadian banknotes. There is no standalone foreign-exchange control administration in Canada; the foreign-exchange framework is governed by the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and market-based exchange-rate determination. The Department of Finance Canada sets financial-sector policy, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally registered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Global Affairs Canada administers Canadian autonomous sanctions and the UN Act sanctions regimes. No Canadian corporate treasury operation requires a foreign-exchange permit from the Bank of Canada or any exchange-control license.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (B+). Digital cross-border money-service businesses and electronic-money institutions, including Wise, Airwallex, Corpay (Western Union Business Solutions), Convera, and Payoneer, can open multi-currency wallets for an Ontario Business Corporation using the Ontario Business Registry extract, Articles of Incorporation, and a directors' resolution, and are viable as secondary disbursement rails for seafarer payroll and small-balance supplier payments. Legal constraints: (i) a foreign EMI holding or transmitting CAD for residents is generally required to be a FINTRAC-registered money-service business (MSB) or to act through one; the Ontario corporation must confirm the EMI's Canadian registration before relying on it for CAD funding. (ii) Funds held with an offshore EMI are not covered by Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and constitute unsecured credit exposure to the EMI. (iii) The EMI, as the reporting entity, will file FINTRAC international EFT reports where the transaction equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, but the Ontario corporation remains legally obliged to provide accurate beneficiary data and to ensure no sanctions breach. (iv) If the Ontario crew-agency itself aggregates seafarer wages and redistributes them, it may independently qualify as an MSB and must register with FINTRAC; to avoid that, the agency should use a bank or licensed payroll provider as the executing institution of record, with the EMI used solely as a payment-execution sub-rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (B+). Digital cross-border money-service businesses and electronic-money institutions, including Wise, Airwallex, Corpay (Western Union Business Solutions), Convera, and Payoneer, can open multi-currency wallets for an Ontario Business Corporation using the Ontario Business Registry extract, Articles of Incorporation, and a directors' resolution, and are viable as secondary disbursement rails for seafarer payroll and small-balance supplier payments. Legal constraints: (i) a foreign EMI holding or transmitting CAD for residents is generally required to be a FINTRAC-registered money-service business (MSB) or to act through one; the Ontario corporation must confirm the EMI's Canadian registration before relying on it for CAD funding. (ii) Funds held with an offshore EMI are not covered by Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and constitute unsecured credit exposure to the EMI. (iii) The EMI, as the reporting entity, will file FINTRAC international EFT reports where the transaction equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, but the Ontario corporation remains legally obliged to provide accurate beneficiary data and to ensure no sanctions breach. (iv) If the Ontario crew-agency itself aggregates seafarer wages and redistributes them, it may independently qualify as an MSB and must register with FINTRAC; to avoid that, the agency should use a bank or licensed payroll provider as the executing institution of record, with the EMI used solely as a payment-execution sub-rail.
