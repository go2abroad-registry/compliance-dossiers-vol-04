Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime. The Swiss franc is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior permit for capital inflows or outflows, no restrictions on profit remittance, loan repayment, or share-capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. The only applicable formalities are the statutory 35% anticipatory withholding tax on dividends (reclaimable or reduced under double taxation treaties), CRS/FATCA reporting obligations for non-resident account holders, and SNB balance-of-payments statistics reported indirectly by the mandated bank. Sectoral restrictions such as the Lex Koller on real estate acquisitions do not affect a specialty tea-salon trade operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no mandatory surrender, no statutory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may hold receipts indefinitely in any convertible currency, onshore or offshore. Cross-border payment data is aggregated by banks for SNB statistical reporting, but individual treasury decisions are unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank, Banque nationale suisse, Banca nazionale svizzera), located in Bern and Zurich, operating under the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank and the Federal Act on Currency and Legal Tender; the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) is the licensing and AML supervisory counterpart.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary settlement channel, Low as the primary treasury relationship. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) may support low-balance merchant settlements and small cross-border supplier payments, but they cannot issue the mandatory capital confirmation for a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft, cannot hold the statutory blocked share-capital escrow, and are not a substitute for a licensed Swiss bank under the Federal Act on Banks. Deposit-taking from Swiss-resident customers without a banking or FinTech licence (Art. 1, 1a, 1b Banking Act) is prohibited; unlicensed cross-border deposit-taking is a criminal offence enforceable by FINMA. Recommended structure: principal banking at UBS Switzerland AG or ZKB, plus a controlled EMI float strictly for payment-card settlement under an explicit internal treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders remit the CHF 100,000 minimum share capital (at least CHF 50,000 paid-in at incorporation, per Art. 632 para. 2 of the Swiss Code of Obligations) via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to a bank-designated blocked capital account (Sperrkonto), referencing 'Capital Contribution - Einlagezahlung' and the company registration name. The bank issues a formal capital confirmation (Bankenbestätigung) attesting that the funds are paid in and will be at the free disposal of the board upon commercial register registration. Foreign-currency remittances must be explicitly converted to CHF at an agreed spot rate, with the SWIFT narrative showing the original currency, conversion rate, and resulting CHF amount. No capital import permit, FDI registration, or settlement approval is required. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal CIT; combined cantonal Zug statutory rate 14.83% (2024)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed by a Swiss AG; under the participation exemption the 35% rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying group dividends (≥10% shareholding and holding period ≥1 year); DTA rates for non-residents generally range 0%–15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law does not impose statutory local-file monetary thresholds; in practice the tax administration orders contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation when cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 500,000 per annum. A CbC report is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no separate ESR regime (unlike UAE). Economic substance is nonetheless assessed through Swiss GAAR and treaty anti-abuse provisions; taxpayers must demonstrate local management, physical premises, real staff, and commercial purpose to retain treaty and participation benefits. Shell or mailbox companies are blocked, and the principal-purposes test (PPT) under the MLI may deny DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Direct Federal and Cantonal)

Statutory sanction: Additional payment up to 100% of evaded tax plus interest; procedural fines up to CHF 10,000; aggravated fraud (e.g., forging records) carries imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to CHF 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are civilly jointly liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; criminal conviction triggers personal fines, travel bans, extradition risks, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, participation exemption, and patent box; retroactive re-assessment of taxable income; fines up to the tax advantage; potential deregistration by the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the tax adjustments if they implement a sham structure; travel bans and imprisonment can be imposed in cases of intentional fraud.
AML / Money Laundering via Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to CHF 500,000; confiscation of laundered assets; violation of the Swiss AML ordinance (SRO) can trigger license revocation and strengthened oversight.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as compliance/de facto AML officers may be prosecuted personally; civil restitution, court-imposed travel bans, prohibition from serving as board members, and imprisonment are all available.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment increasing taxable profits; interest at 5% per annum; penalty of up to 30% of the deficiency in negligent cases and up to 100% for wilful evasion; CbC or OECD exchange of information automatically flagged.

Director personal liability: Financial directors and responsible officers are personally liable for false documentation; intentional TP fraud can lead to criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus personal claims for restitution.
