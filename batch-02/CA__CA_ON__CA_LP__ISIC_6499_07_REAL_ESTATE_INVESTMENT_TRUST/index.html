Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE at canada.ca. Provincial: Ontario Ministry of Finance (ONT-TAXS) for residual Ontario tax items; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export customs and CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident Ontario limited partnership (LP): (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via BRO or through an authorized Canada Post agent using the LP legal name; CRA issues the 9-digit BN with program account identifiers (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RZ for non-resident Part XIII withholding). (2) Register for GST/HST under the RT account once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold), or before the first taxable supply in Canada; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits. (3) Provide full name, address, and TIN (Canadian BN or foreign tax ID) of each partner at registration and on T5013 partnership information returns. (4) No separate Ontario tax identity number is required because the 8% Ontario HST component is federally collected and income of an LP is taxed at partner level; Ontario administrative obligations (e.g., Employer Health Tax) arise only if the LP has Ontario payroll. (5) Where rental income is allocable to non-resident partners, the LP or its Canadian agent must open a Part XIII withholding account and remit 25% gross withholding (or net-basis under a s. 216 election).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. An Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96) but is treated as a separate person for GST/HST (Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 272). Applicable elections and statutory exclusions: (a) SIFT 'real estate investment trust' exclusion (ITA s. 122.1(1)) - a qualifying REIT whose units are listed and whose assets are substantially real estate is not taxed at the entity level as a SIFT and retains pass-through treatment; no election is required, only factual qualification. (b) ITA s. 216 election - non-resident partners of the LP may elect to file net-basis Canadian rental returns for real property income instead of being subject to 25% gross Part XIII withholding. (c) Non-calendar fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1) where all partners of the LP are non-residents. (d) ETA s. 156(2) election for nil consideration on taxable supplies between closely related group members including the LP and related trusts/corporations. (e) Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) operate mandatorily and non-electively to disallow deductions for hybrid arrangements achieving deduction/no-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) measured on total worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters plus the current calendar quarter; registration becomes mandatory immediately upon the first taxable supply made in Canada or when the threshold is exceeded. Non-resident landlords of Ontario real property must register before charging commercial rents (taxable at 13% HST); long-term residential rents are exempt under Schedule V, Part I, s. 6.1 and do not count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (i) goods are zero-rated when exported by the supplier or on the supplier's direction and shipped to a destination outside Canada (s. 1), or when sold to a non-resident person for use exclusively outside Canada and exported within 60 days (s. 3); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under s. 7 where the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada, does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not in respect of a Canadian resource; (iii) for this Ontario REIT LP specifically, commercial rental income from Canadian real property is taxable at 13% HST (not zero-rated) and residential lease income is exempt; management fees paid by foreign unit holders or by the REIT for Canadian property administration cannot be zero-rated merely because the recipient is a non-resident. Zero-rating is available only for bona fide export-linked or offshore services identified under Schedule VI; the supplier must retain documentary proof that the supply was exported or the benefit of the service was realized outside Canada.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A or CARM e-declaration for 'Export Goods') endorsed by the exporter or licensed customs broker
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Cargo Control Document (CCD) showing destination outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list describing the goods or services and the export transaction terms
  • Bank credit advice or foreign exchange remittance record evidencing receipt of export proceeds (Canada does not statutorily require an Inward Remittance Certificate)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary; Canada is adopting the Pan-Canadian PINT (Peppol) framework on an opt-in basis through certified service providers. The CRA mandates electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE and T5013 partnership returns above prescribed thresholds) with failure-to-file penalties. The Ontario 8% HST component is administered through the federal CRA system and does not impose a separate provincial clearance mandate.

Canada imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds; 180-day terms are purely commercial. CRA audit exposure for the REIT LP includes: (i) false statements or omissions penalties under ETA s. 285 (greater of $250 and 25% of unremitted tax, rising to 50% for knowing or gross-negligence filings); (ii) criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 with fines from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; (iii) arrears interest at prescribed CRA rates compounded daily; (iv) normal assessment window is four years, unlimited for misrepresentation, fraud, or abandonment (ETA s. 298); (v) all general partners and limited partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations (ETA s. 272); (vi) CRA may retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment and deny input tax credits where export documentation is incomplete, fictitious, or inconsistent with physical movement of goods or offshore service delivery, shifting the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no residual approvals or surrender obligations exist. Profit remittance from an Ontario Limited Partnership real-estate REIT vehicle faces no FX convertibility hurdle; the only practical constraints are tax-related, namely Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced as applicable), deemed-dividend characterization under subsection 96(6) of the Income Tax Act for non-resident limited partners, and CRS/FATCA documentation obligations. Distributions must be sourced from accumulated operating income, capital gains, or returning capital and should be documented in the partnership's books to ensure the correct withholding classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory repatriation period, and no required conversion ratio for export receipts or any other foreign-currency inflows. A REIT LP may retain USD or other foreign-currency balances in authorized dealer bank accounts indefinitely. The only border-related requirements concern physical currency and monetary instruments: a written report to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is required for cross-border movements of $10,000 CAD or more, and financial institutions must report international electronic funds transfers exceeding $10,000 CAD to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy and administration are coordinated through the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF and cross-border transaction reporting. There is no single SAFE-style exchange-control regulator; Canada relies on a decentralized, market-based system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail; viable only as a narrow settlement adjunct. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) must navigate the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider; the transitional registration window began November 2024. EMIs cannot accept deposits or extend credit absent a bank license, so they are inappropriate for capital-escrow, custody, or debt-servicing functions. For an Ontario REIT LP, the compliant architecture is a domestic Schedule I bank as principal clearing and escrow counterparty, with a regulated EMI (e.g., Wise Business or an RPAA-registered PSP) used strictly for low-value cross-border disbursements and administrative conversions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wires to the LP's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, using the partnership's registered legal name and the account mandate on file. The payment narrative should unambiguously identify the transaction, for example 'CALL #4 CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [REIT NAME] LIMITED PARTNERSHIP'; there is no mandatory Canadian purpose-code regime, but the narrative, together with the underlying capital-call notice and subscription agreement, constitutes the compliance record. International EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more are automatically reported to FINTRAC by the bank; the LP itself is not required to pre-authorize the transfer. No Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required for capital inflows. The receiving bank will verify source-of-funds documentation, UBO declarations, and the consistency of the remitter name against the LP register before crediting the capital escrow account.High-value CAD payments settle through Lynx, the Bank of Canada's real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada; USD disbursements are cleared through SWIFT MT103 via a Canadian bank's U.S. branch or U.S. correspondent to the Fedwire system. Commercial letters of credit for construction and procurement obligations are issued under UCP600 using SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707. Recurring and smaller distributions to unit holders or partners can be cleared through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the U.S. ACH network, as applicable, with the bank acting as originator. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Ontario general corporate income tax rate of 26.5% (federal 15% plus Ontario 11.5%), subject to Ontario capital tax exemptions for real estate investment trusts structured to comply with the SIFT trust rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends and certain real estate investment trust distributions paid by Canada-source instruments to non-resident holders, unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end; the unpaid balance is due no later than two months after the fiscal year-end for most corporate filers and three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires timely transfer-pricing documentation where the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1 million in the taxation year; a contemporaneous Local File and, where applicable, Master File must be maintained to OECD standards; Form T106 and Schedule 9 must be filed for reportable transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act. While no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime exists, Ontario real estate investment trusts must demonstrate genuine business purpose, administrative substance, and non-avoidance of Canadian tax otherwise CRA may recharacterize transactions, impose withholding deficiencies, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act with fines up to 200% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessments apply retroactively with daily compounding interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the full amount of unremitted tax, interest, and penalties; travel bans and seizure of personally held assets may be ordered where culpable intent is found.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the allocable income and apply a 10% penalty on the amount of transfer-pricing adjustment exceeding C$5 million, plus interest on the underpayment; unadjusted transactions may also trigger Part XIII gross-up liability.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner or corporate manager are liable for penalties attributable to knowingly making false statements; civil restitution and recapture of personal distributions may be enforced.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR reconstruction, denial of treaty benefits, disallowance of deductions, and penalty under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act for third-party civil penalties; fines up to C$100,000 or 200% of reported income omissions may be asserted.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing in tax court may expose general partners to personal assessment for Part XIII withholding taxes; personal asset freezing orders and director liability under provincial corporate statutes are applied.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Unregistered Investment Business

Statutory sanction: Provincial securities enforcement fines up to C$5 million per contravention; CRA may disallow the partnership's income entitlement for non-compliant investors; OSC orders for disgorgement, cease trade, and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors knowingly assisting illegal capital solicitation are liable for investor restitution, administrative penalties, and in aggravated cases imprisonment under the Ontario Securities Act.
