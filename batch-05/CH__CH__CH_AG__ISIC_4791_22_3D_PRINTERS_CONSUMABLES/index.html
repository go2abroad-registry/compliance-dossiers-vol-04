Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce) of the canton of the registered office, with federal coordination by the Swiss Federal Commercial Register Office (EHRA) in Bern; electronic filing through the federal eChange portal.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Arts. 620–763 on the Aktiengesellschaft; registration duty under Art. 640 OR; articles of association and incorporation deed formalities under Arts. 626 and 629 OR; audit opt-out under Art. 727a OR; beneficial owner disclosure under Art. 934o OR.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial incorporation deed (Errichtungsurkunde) with certified Articles of Association (Statuten), submitted electronically via eChange and published in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SHAB).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Articles must state a nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000; before registration at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be paid in cash and deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank; the escrow confirmation must accompany the registration application; contributions in kind must be fully paid in and substantiated in an incorporation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety acceptance certificate (Feuerpolizeiliche Abnahmebescheinigung) issued by the cantonal fire prevention authority in accordance with VKF guidelines; structural load inspection and building clearance for warehouse racking; occupational safety inspection by SUVA confirming safe manual handling of filament spools, sealed chemical storage lockers, emergency eyewash stations, and first-aid equipment under Ordinance 3 on Occupational Health and Safety.

Paid-in capital rule: The Articles must state a nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000; before registration at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be paid in cash and deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank; the escrow confirmation must accompany the registration application; contributions in kind must be fully paid in and substantiated in an incorporation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni ATC); official portal for VAT registration and filing: Swiss Federal Tax Administration VAT e-services (www.estv.admin.ch)

E-government filing portal

Swiss AGs automatically receive a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the Swiss Federal Statistical Office upon commercial register entry; the UID serves as the VAT number. Foreign entities not domiciled in Switzerland must mandatory register for Swiss VAT with ESTV if performing taxable supplies in Switzerland, irrespective of turnover, and must submit an online application (or written application) including company excerpt, certificate of incorporation, board/power of attorney, and a designated Swiss fiscal representative (if required). The ESTV then assigns a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr.), typically congruent with the UID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Swiss AGs automatically receive a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the Swiss Federal Statistical Office upon commercial register entry; the UID serves as the VAT number. Foreign entities not domiciled in Switzerland must mandatory register for Swiss VAT with ESTV if performing taxable supplies in Switzerland, irrespective of turnover, and must submit an online application (or written application) including company excerpt, certificate of incorporation, board/power of attorney, and a designated Swiss fiscal representative (if required). The ESTV then assigns a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr.), typically congruent with the UID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

VAT group election under Art. 15 LTVA allows closely linked Swiss enterprises to be treated as one VAT person. Small business election (Art. 11 LTVA) permits exemption for turnover under CHF 100,000. No US-style check-the-box election exists in Switzerland. For direct taxes, hybrid entity classification follows the Swiss legal classification under Art. 50 LIFD; special mixed-status foreign entities are reclassified under Swiss domestic law, and hybrid mismatch rules under Art. 61a LTVA apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover for Swiss-domiciled legal entities; non-established foreign legal persons have no registration threshold and must register for VAT upon first taxable supply in Switzerland

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to foreign destinations qualify for zero-rating (0% VAT) under Art. 6 para 1 lit a LTVA if the goods are physically transported out of Switzerland by the supplier or a third party. Services performed directly for foreign customers (recipient abroad) and certain offshore goods-related services are also zero-rated under Art. 7/18 LTVA when the recipient's seat or permanent establishment is abroad and the service is actually used abroad. The supplier must hold documentary evidence of the export or offshore nature of the supply; otherwise VAT at 8.1% must be charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Transit-declaration per e-dec or e-déc), Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR, Inward Remittance Certificate (bank transfer confirmation evidencing foreign payment)

No national real-time fiscal clearance, API field-level tax control, or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing (Peppol, eBill, PDF) is permitted provided the invoice satisfies the content requirements of Art. 26 LTVA (vendor, recipient, description, consideration, date, separate VAT rate/amount). Tax authorities may require digital submission via the Swiss VAT system (eVI), but there is no per-invoice pre-clearance approval.

ESTV audits export documentation with high scrutiny. If export proof is missing, incomplete, or false, the previously zero-rated supply is reclassified as taxable in Switzerland, subject to VAT at 8.1% plus default interest (currently 4.75% pa). For fraudulent claims (wilful tax evasion), surcharges up to three times the evaded tax may be imposed, and criminal prosecution under Swiss VAT law may be initiated. Limitation periods: 5 years for ordinary assessment; 10 years for undeclared and fraudulent transactions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control restrictions apply. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties and internal capital movements are freely permitted; however, payments that constitute taxable Swiss-sourced income may be subject to Swiss withholding tax at 35% unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty, and the recipient must provide the necessary tax residence attestation. No profit repatriation licensing or prior approval regime exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland does not impose any statutory timeline for the repatriation or surrender of export proceeds, nor any mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any permitted currency and freely converted into CHF or other currencies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); no separate foreign exchange administration authority exists in Switzerland.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMI onboarding: Low. Swiss financial market law requires any entity conducting deposit-taking, payment processing or e-money issuance on a professional basis to obtain a FINMA license (banking licence, FinTech licence, or payment service provider authorisation). Offshore digital EMIs cannot passport EU/EEA licences into Switzerland absent a specific FINMA recognition. A low-risk e-commerce operator may use a regulated Swiss PSP or a FINMA-licensed FinTech service for payment processing. Registration with the Swiss registry of beneficial owners and AML KYC obligations remain mandatory.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) appears on adverse media or is a politically exposed person (PEP)

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) appears on adverse media or is a politically exposed person (PEP)

Defense evidence: Enhanced due diligence package comprising a PEP/UBO explanatory memorandum, tax office clearance certificates, audited source-of-wealth report, source-of-funds evidence, existing bank reference letters, official beneficial ownership register excerpt and a sworn declaration of lawful origin of capital.
Cash-intensive, fragmented or unexplained third-party payments

Root cause: Cash-intensive, fragmented or unexplained third-party payments

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements, detailed invoice ledger, logistics and carrier proofs, customs declarations, inventory valuation report, VAT filing documentation and a bank-level reconciliation confirming that all payment flows map to commercial underlying transactions.
Newly incorporated AG with complex corporate structure or recent total change in shareholders

Root cause: Newly incorporated AG with complex corporate structure or recent total change in shareholders

Defense evidence: Organisational chart, notarised share register, capital verification statement from the Swiss Commercial Register, board minutes authorising the banking relationship, lease agreements and material contracts, plus reference letters from previous financial institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from foreign shareholders must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using CHF as the settlement currency. No Swiss foreign-exchange approval or conversion licence is required, but the bank must receive the notarised capital increase resolution, the Commercial Register entry confirming the new capital, and a precise payment purpose reference stating 'Capital increase / share subscription'. The remitter must match the registered shareholder and the declared UBO. Funds may be held in CHF or in permitted foreign currencies, and are subject to standard Swiss AML checks rather than exchange-control authorisation.Clearing rails for trade settlement include SWIFT MT103 for priority international wires, SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated payments within the EEA, and documentary letters of credit under ICC UCP 600 for high-value or risk-mitigated supplier settlements. For a low-risk e-commerce buyer paying 3D printer resin and filament suppliers, MT103 transfers with complete beneficiary bank details are operationally most reliable. SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) is available for real-time EUR payments within participating European banks.
