Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum issued capital for an Aktiengesellschaft is CHF 100,000; paid-up capital must be at least 20% (CHF 20,000). In the case of contributions in kind, a sworn auditor's report (Sachgründungsbericht) is required. The capital must be deposited in a Swiss cantonal bank or licensed bank in the name of the company and confirmed via bank blocking confirmation (Blockierungsbestätigung / Sperrkonto) until registration in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: A computerized service operator has no physical manufacturing plant. Business and office premises in Zug must comply with cantonal fire safety assurance (Feuerpolizei, feu control certificates), structural load safety, and occupational health inspection for continuous work at workstations. If the company sets up a physical office in the Canton of Zug, it must obtain a General Compliance of Works procedures and LIFE Safety Evacuation certificate from the municipal authorities, complete Workplace Lighting/Noise/LCD Screen Compliance, and ensure first-aid procedures per Swiss workplace security ordinances. The building owner must submit a fire safety concept and, where VDP-NL (video data processing and non-licensed office), the workspace contractor must provide a workstation risk assessment before hiring employees.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum issued capital for an Aktiengesellschaft is CHF 100,000; paid-up capital must be at least 20% (CHF 20,000). In the case of contributions in kind, a sworn auditor's report (Sachgründungsbericht) is required. The capital must be deposited in a Swiss cantonal bank or licensed bank in the name of the company and confirmed via bank blocking confirmation (Blockierungsbestätigung / Sperrkonto) until registration in the Commercial Register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Switzerland maintains no exchange-control legislation and draws no legal distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Dividends, profit repatriation, repayment of shareholder loans, royalties, interest and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad freely and without prior approval. However, sanctions and embargoes administered by SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs), OFAC extraterritorial enforcement, and FINMA AML obligations can materially delay or block payments where a sanctioned person, entity, or jurisdiction is involved or where the underlying funds are suspected to be illicit. For a cross-border video-match social app, treasury planning must therefore centre on sanctions screening, revenue-source traceability, and robust documentation rather than exchange-control pleading.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland does not impose any statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement. Export receivables may be held indefinitely in any freely convertible currency, and the Swiss AG may elect to keep foreign currency with a Swiss licensed bank or an offshore bank treasury account without SNB authorisation. For ISIC 6311 data-processing and video-match service exports, invoices may be issued in EUR, USD, CHF or any other currency, and converted to operating currency only when required by commercial need.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, acting together with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) in the areas of monetary policy, payment-system oversight, and banking supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for primary treasury use; Medium-High only as an ancillary collection and payout rail. A foreign/offshore EMI such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or a UK/US-licensed EMI can be used to receive Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlements in major currencies, but the lawful and practical limitations for a Swiss AG are consequential. Such EMIs do not have a Swiss banking licence, cannot issue a Swiss clearing number, and cannot provide the notary-required blocked-capital certificate. They also do not fall within the Swiss deposit protection scheme, expose the AG to second-tier counterparty risk, and may not be recognised by FINMA as an acceptable AML-compliant custodian. If the EMI actively acquires Swiss clients or executes payments on behalf of the AG into Swiss bank accounts, it may trigger FINMA licensing under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act or the FinSA/FinIA requirements. The compliant blueprint is to maintain a large-liquidity buffer in a FINMA-licensed commercial bank and use EMIs solely for low-balance international card collection and sub-1% cash-balance settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the Swiss AG's blocked escrow account at a FINMA-licensed bank. The sending bank must use a purpose code identifying the credit as a capital contribution (e.g. 'A020' under ISO 8583, or the Swiss bank's internal Einlagezahlung keyword) so that the receiving bank may release the no-debit confirmation to the notary. For a modern treasury setup, the preferred rail is SWIFT gpi (global payment innovation) with unique end-to-end transaction reference (UETR) and OPIC (optional payment instruction code) to avoid correspondent delays. The verified capital certificate issued by the bank, the notarial deed, and the Commercial Register excerpt must be retained in the treasury file. Amounts exceeding CHF 1 million may trigger a wealth-source questionnaire requiring personal balance sheet and tax documentation; capital in crypto is only acceptable if liquidated through a FINMA-regulated securities dealer with a full transfer trail and then delivered through normal fiat wire.Outbound disbursements for trade settlement should use the Swiss Payment Standards (SIC5) and SEPA Instant rails for euro-denominated flows, and SWIFT MT103/MT202 for USD, GBP and other non-SEPA currencies. For structured trade in the video-match vertical with potential licensing or content-conduit counterparts, a UCP600-compliant commercial letter of credit issued by UBS/ZKB and confirmed by an international bank provides documentary security. The bank will require the underlying commercial contract, tax invoice, and for cross-border service payments, a transfer pricing control certificate. Royalty, dividend and management fee outflows from the Swiss AG to the foreign parent should be tagged with the relevant OECD transaction purpose code on the SWIFT MT103, accompanied by a board minute, a cost-allocation statement, and the Swiss withholding tax certificate if relief is claimed under the DTT. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory fixed threshold in CHF; mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is endorsed by the OECD TP Guidelines and ordinance (e.g., when cross-border related-party transactions exceed CHF 500,000 annually or involve high-risk intangibles)

Economic substance scope: Swiss ESR rules applied through the existing substantial economic presence requirements for tax treaty benefits: activities must be performed, assets must be held, and risks must be managed in Switzerland for the benefit to be recognised; video-matching platform operators must demonstrate actual operation of matching algorithms, content moderation personnel, servers or relevant contracted infrastructure, and business decision-making in Switzerland

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CHF 250,000 (Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax Art. 175-186); recovery of unpaid tax plus compound interest; exclusion from participation relief

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false filings are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; may face disqualification as directors and, in aggravated cases, custodial sentence from 3 months to 2 years
Shell Company and Abusive Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits, reassessment of income at full Swiss corporate tax rate, retroactive withholding at 35% on deemed distributions, and fines for failure to keep commercial books

Director personal liability: Civil piercing of the corporate veil under Swiss Code of Obligations Art. 754 (liability for breach of fiduciary duties) and Art. 757; travel bans under the Federal Act on Debt Collection, and prison sentences up to 5 years for fraudulent bankruptcy or misuse of the corporate form
AML and Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (VStrG/AMLA): registration with the Anti-Money Laundering Authority, administrative fines up to CHF 500,000, licence revocation for licensed financial intermediaries, asset freezing orders, and confiscation of illegally acquired corporate assets

Director personal liability: Directors and operational officers may personally face criminal proceedings for money laundering (Art. 305bis StGB, imprisonment up to 3 years), aggravated money laundering (Art. 305quater StGB, imprisonment from 1 to 10 years), and civil compensation to damaged parties under Article 41 of the Swiss Code of Obligations
Withholding and Repatriation Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine, mandatory payment of the withheld amount, penalty interest additionally payable at 5% per annum, and loss of refund or relief already granted

Director personal liability: The board members and responsible finance officers are personally liable for wilful failure to collect the tax, may be arrested upon leaving Switzerland, and can be sanctioned with imprisonment not exceeding 1 year (VStrG Art. 177)
