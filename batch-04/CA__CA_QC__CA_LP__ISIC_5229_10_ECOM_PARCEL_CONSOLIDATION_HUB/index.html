Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST, income tax, and Business Number administration (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), provincial income tax and payroll (www.revenuquebec.ca); Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) – statutory legal publicity and enterprise number (NEQ) registration (www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca). Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier d'entreprise'.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Register the extra-provincial limited partnership (société en commandite) for legal publicity at the REQ to obtain the NEQ (Canada-Quebec enterprise number). Step 2 – Apply to CRA via BRO or Form RC1/RC1A for a 9-digit Business Number (BN); open the RT (GST/HST) program account and, if applicable, RP (payroll) and RN (non-resident withholding) accounts. The transparent LP files GST/HST under its own BN/RT; no RC (corporate income tax) account is needed for the LP itself, but every corporate or non-resident partner must maintain its own income tax account. Step 3 – Register for QST with Revenu Québec through the online business portal using the NEQ; obtain the QST/TVQ registration number. Step 4 – Appoint a resident representative (mandataire) with power of attorney if the LP has no fixed establishment in Canada. Step 5 – File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return (and Quebec counterpart TP-600.13) whenever the partnership has 7 or more members, any corporate member, or any non-resident member; include Schedule 50 partner allocations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – mandatory fiscal transparency under Income Tax Act s. 96: the limited partnership is not an income taxpayer; each partner's allocable share is computed and taxed at the partner level. For GST/QST, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' and is the registrant of record; Canada has no optional check-the-box classification election, so transparency is statutory and non-waivable. A non-resident partner carrying on business through the Quebec permanent establishment is taxable on its share of Canadian-source income (with Part XIV branch tax exposure for non-resident corporate partners).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists. Available elections: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(6) aligning the partnership year-end with the majority partner's year-end; (ii) GST/HST joint venture or co-ownership election under Excise Tax Act s. 273 if the parcel hub is operated as undivided interests rather than a partnership; (iii) mirror QST joint-venture election under the Quebec QST Act; (iv) designation of a responsible partner/agent for GST/QST filings and remittances of the non-resident LP; (v) for U.S.-resident partners, a U.S. check-the-box election (Form 8832) may cause hybrid classification mismatches requiring documentary evidence under Canada's OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch neutralizing provisions, which disallow deductions or impose inclusion adjustments where the same expenditure is treated inconsistently in Canada and a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annually – the GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold, measured on worldwide taxable supplies of the LP and all associated persons over the preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter (ETA s. 148; QST Act equivalent). A parcel consolidation hub with non-resident clients should voluntarily register immediately nonetheless, because zero-rating of exports cannot practically be claimed without a valid registration and the ability to recover input tax credits through quarterly net-filings.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the supplier (or its agent) delivers the parcels to a destination outside Canada in the ordinary course of business, or the purchaser exports them as part of a continuous journey with no diversion, and the supplier retains evidence satisfactory to the Minister (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.2 – Proof of Export). Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 7 where linehaul, sorting, and direct-injection services are supplied to a non-resident person and are not performed in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; where the services physically handle Canadian-situated parcels destined for export, zero-rating remains available if the complete documentary chain shows the goods departed Canada within the export window and were delivered to the non-resident consignee. Quebec grants parallel 0% treatment under the QST Act Schedule IV, with substantiation per Revenu Québec guidance mirroring the federal rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or electronic CERS filing with CBSA, including the GCA reference and date of departure)
  • Commercial invoice / contract reflecting ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510 linehaul services and matching all HS values and descriptions
  • Master or House Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or certified surface-carrier international BOL evidencing cross-border in-transit movement
  • Destination last-mile carrier Proof of Delivery (POD) with signature, geotag, and timestamp in the destination jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent: bank wire confirmation / SWIFT MT103 and beneficiary bank credit advice matching invoice value
  • Non-resident customer identity and beneficial ownership file (UBO declaration, AML/KYC record) supporting the 'supply to a non-resident' test

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API approval exists in Canada at either federal or provincial level. CRA and Revenu Québec operate on self-assessment and post-filing audit; electronic invoices are permitted if they contain the prescribed information (ETA s. 236(1) and QST Act equivalent) and source records are retained in readable digital form (ETA s. 286.1; CRA IC03-1). Quebec is progressively phasing in mandatory electronic invoicing for business-to-government procurement under its public tendering platform (SEAO), but there is no B2B or export-consolidation e-invoicing clearance mandate. The operator may adopt PEPPOL or EDI bilaterally for commercial efficiency.

CRA and Revenu Québec audit zero-rating on a document-first basis; failure to produce the full export evidence bundle results in automatic reassessment imposing GST/QST at 14.975% on the entire invoice amount plus daily compounded prescribed interest from the date the tax was deemed collectible. Penalties: gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 5,000 and 25% of the net tax involved for knowingly or willfully false statements (ETA s. 285(1) and mirror QST Act penalty provisions); criminal evasion (ETA s. 327.5) carries fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment; the normal 4-year reassessment window is extended to 12 years where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default (ETA s. 298(4) and equivalent QST limitation rules). Parallel customs exposure: CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) fines up to CAD 25,000 per occurrence for false or omitted export declarations, plus seizure and forfeiture risk under the Customs Act. Any input tax credit previously claimed on the exported goods is clawed back, and the LP must issue a corrected invoice in writing to preserve a refund path within the statutory limitation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control restrictions on profit remittance by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident limited partners; remittances are subject only to Canadian tax withholding requirements under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and applicable treaty relief. The Bank of Canada does not impose pre-authorization for inward capital contributions or outward trade payments, but large or unusual transactions may be reviewed under the Investment Canada Act and FINTRAC reporting rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion timeline applies to export proceeds. A parcel-consolidation hub may retain export revenue in the currency of receipt or convert it at market rates through a Canadian authorized bank. FINTRAC filing obligations arise for single transaction receipts exceeding CAD 10,000, but this is a monitoring threshold, not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Payments Canada administers the clearing and settlement systems, and FINTRAC oversees cross-border monetary and wire-transfer reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a principal treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI entities may support ancillary FX and cross-border payout workflows, but they cannot clear through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or serve as the primary settlement bank for a Canadian-regulated treasury hub. Any EMI activity involving Canadian residents is subject to FINTRAC money-services business registration, and the phrase 'banking' or 'deposit' is legally constrained in Quebec. The limited partnership should maintain a Tier 1 domestic bank account and use an EMI solely for operational disbursement acceleration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at LP entity level (pass-through); combined federal and Quebec headline rate for a taxable corporation is 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% provincial)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; ordinary LP distributions of Canadian business profits are not treated as dividends for Part XIII purposes — a non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on the allocated net income, and the LP may be required to withhold 15% of the partner's share of Canadian business income under section 194 of the Income Tax Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are triggered when annual cross-border non-arm's-length transactions with each related non-resident exceed CAD 1,000,000 for goods, services, or intangibles, and CAD 5,000,000 for loans or advances. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the Canadian-partner multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is judicially enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the sham-transaction doctrine, and treaty PE / beneficial-ownership rules. A Quebec LP engaged in parcel consolidation must maintain physical premises, employees, operational decision-making, and real risk management capacity in Canada; otherwise CRA may treat the LP as a conduit, deny partnership allocations, impose Part XIII withholding on payments that circumvent the partnership, and invoke the hybrid mismatch rules in s. 18.4 if the LP is treated as opaque in the foreign partner's jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239: fine of not less than 50% of the tax evaded and not more than 200%, plus imprisonment of up to five years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated tax, plus interest and late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted amounts; in criminal cases, responsible individuals face personal fines, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: GAAR under Income Tax Act s. 245 denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the transaction; CRA may disregard the partnership entirely, treat the business income as earned directly by the foreign partner, and apply Quebec penalties under the Taxation Act for misdeclaration. Repeated sham arrangements attract gross-negligence penalties of 50%.

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the partnership veil and assess foreign partners directly; directors of the corporate general partner face personal liability for penalties and restitution if they authorized the sham structure, and may be subject to director travel bans under collection procedures.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247 transfer pricing adjustment, plus a 10% penalty on the net adjustment where contemporaneous documentation is absent; T106 late-filing penalties of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500; interest accrues on adjusted tax. CRA may also apply a 50% gross-negligence penalty if the mispricing is deliberate.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner's directors are exposed to personal liability for the unpaid penalties and interest if they signed non-arm's-length contracts without commercial justification; crystallization of the penalty can also trigger insolvency-based director liability for creditor claims.
Non-Resident Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(1) makes the partnership liable for the full withholding amount, plus interest and penalties of 10% of any unremitted amount under s. 227(8); Part XIII amounts can be grossed-up under s. 212(1.1) to the extent no withholding was made.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholding amounts; Revenu Québec may also pursue directors under the Taxation Act for unpaid source deductions; severe non-compliance triggers travel bans and personal collection actions.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; criminal money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment of up to ten years; Customs Act false-declaration penalties up to CAD 15,000 per count, plus forfeiture of goods.

Director personal liability: General-partner directors and senior officers face personal prosecution, fines, debarment from federal contracts, and travel bans; UPAC and RCMP may seize corporate and personal assets tracing the laundered funds, and the LP's registration may be suspended by the tax authorities.
