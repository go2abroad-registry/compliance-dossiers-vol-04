Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/businesses.html). Provincial counterpart: Revenu Québec (www.revenuquebec.ca) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial registration obligations.

E-government filing portal

A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, including a GST/HST program account, by submitting Form RC148 or using Business Registration Online (BRO) with a 'partnership' registration. Registration is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold. Revenu Québec registration for QST must be completed separately via the Quebec business registration service (Inforoute/Données), requiring the BN, partnership contract, and authorized representative details. Non-resident partners triggering Canadian nexus must provide their foreign registration particulars and, if demanded, designate a Quebec agent for service.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, including a GST/HST program account, by submitting Form RC148 or using Business Registration Online (BRO) with a 'partnership' registration. Registration is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold. Revenu Québec registration for QST must be completed separately via the Quebec business registration service (Inforoute/Données), requiring the BN, partnership contract, and authorized representative details. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists. The limited partnership's pass-through classification is non-elective for federal and Quebec income tax. Available elections include: (a) Income Tax Act s. 97(2) partnership rollover on contributed property; (b) s. 249.1(6) alternative fiscal-period election; (c) Excise Tax Act s. 156(2) election for nil consideration on qualifying intra-group supplies; and (d) hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act ss. 12.7 and 18.6 which limit deduction/non-inclusion outcomes for partnership interests.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in GST/HST and QST taxable supplies over any single calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of tangible personal property, including designer blind box toys, when the goods are exported by the supplier or by a non-resident purchaser through a common carrier within a reasonable time after delivery (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6). Quebec QST provides an equivalent zero-rating on exports (An Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax, relevant exemption for goods exported outside Canada). The supplier must hold documentary evidence that the goods left Canada and that the purchaser is a non-resident. Services, such as design or finishing services supplied in connection with export-bound goods, may also be zero-rated if performed for a non-resident and the goods are subsequently exported.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or eManifest export record for commercial shipments; for low-value courier shipments, the courier's export manifest and shipment reference.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note demonstrating physical departure of the goods from Canada and destination outside Canada.
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract identifying the non-resident purchaser and the international delivery terms (e.g., Incoterms), confirming title transfers only after export.
  • Proof of payment received from the non-resident buyer, including SWIFT/MT103, Star-Settle/Stripe/Shopify settlement records, or credit-card vouchers showing the foreign-currency remittance.

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory continuous controls, or digital stamping for invoices. Both CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoicing and e-records, provided the invoice clearly states the supplier name and Business Number, the amount of GST/QST charged, and the nature of the supply. Taxpayers must retain all books and records in machine-readable form and provide audit access on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec closely scrutinize zero-rating substantiation. Deficient documentation leads to reassessment of unremitted GST/QST plus prescribed interest and a civil penalty of up to 25% of the net tax amount for false statements or gross negligence (Excise Tax Act s. 280; Taxation Act corresponding provisions). Retrospective assessments may extend to four years, or without time limit in cases of fraud or misrepresentation. Repeated or willful disregard can attract criminal prosecution. Although Canada imposes no statutory forex repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is applied in audit practice; prolonged non-repatriation of export sales proceeds increases fraud-risk indicators and may negate zero-rating eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under an IMF Article VIII regime providing full convertibility for current account transactions. Capital account transactions face no general restrictions for resident limited partnerships. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are permitted without prior approval, subject only to standard Income Tax Act withholding at source and automatic reporting via T5 slips. Quebec provincial entities benefit from the same federal convertibility framework with no additional capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds is required under Canadian law. Exporters may freely negotiate settlement terms with their banks. All export receipts must be recorded for Canada Revenue Agency tax compliance and may be reported through normal banking channels without prescribed conversion ratios or timelines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
