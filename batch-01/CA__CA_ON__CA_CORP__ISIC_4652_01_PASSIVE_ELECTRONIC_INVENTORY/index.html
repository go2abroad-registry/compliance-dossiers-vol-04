Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal; Business Registration Online (BRO) at cra-arc.gc.ca

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a Business Number (BN) and associated GST/HST program account (RT) by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). Submission options: online via Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail, or by fax to the CRA. Applicants must provide the legal entity name, business address, directors/officers, a Canadian representative or registered agent, non-resident details, and expected taxable revenue in Canada. CRA issues the BN and GST/HST account effective as of the requested registration date. A foreign entity not carrying on business in Canada may still register voluntarily if it desires input tax credits on Canadian expenses.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. For income tax, corporations are taxable entities; no pass-through elections except for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations (SBC, not simplified). For GST/HST, elections are available under the Excise Tax Act: section 156 (closely related corporations election to treat certain supplies as non-taxable on nil consideration), section 150 (deemed supplies for property transferred within a closely related group), and the Quick Method election for small business registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 and 18.5 apply independently to cross-border arrangements; they are not elective but operate mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter also triggers mandatory registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier or delivered by the supplier or a common carrier to a destination outside Canada. Services performed wholly outside Canada or services reasonably related to exported goods (e.g., international transportation, freight handling) may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident. To claim the 0% rate, the supplier must retain documentary evidence proving the goods were exported within a reasonable time, per CRA administrative policy (GST/HST Memorandum 7-1-5 The Export of Goods).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration, Form B13A, or Automated Customs Declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document
  • Freight forwarder or carrier certificate of delivery outside Canada
  • Customs proof of export (electronic confirmed shipment status or CBP export documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire transfer evidence or payment proof from the foreign buyer)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists at the federal or Ontario provincial level. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed standard invoices (Excise Tax Act section 237) containing the vendor's name, address, BN/GST/HST registration number, date of invoice, description, and tax amounts. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose gross annual revenue exceeds CAD 1,500,000; filing is accepted via GST/HST NETFILE or approved EDI/XML. E-invoicing is optional but must be presented to the CRA in an accessible and readable format upon audit.

CRA may assess unremitted GST/HST, deny zero-rating, and recover tax deemed unpaid. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include section 280 penalties for false statements or omissions (up to 25% of the amount sought to be evaded), section 285 penalties for failing to file or knowingly reporting false information, and daily penalties for failure to comply with audit requests. Interest applies retroactively on assessed amounts. Criminal prosecution under section 327 (tax evasion) is available where there is intent to misrepresent exports. Clawback periods: CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax within 4 years, or indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud, neglect, or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account items; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls, no administrative approvals for inward or outward capital movements, and no restrictions on payment for imported electronic components or on repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or loan repayments. Practical obstacles to profit remittance are fiscal rather than FX-based: Part XIII of the federal Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.) imposes a default 25% withholding tax on dividends and certain payments to non-residents, with treaty-based reductions (commonly 5-15% on dividends and 0-10% on interest under Canada's double-taxation conventions, including the Canada-US treaty); the Ontario distribution corporation must file NR4 returns and remit withholding to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Additional constraints include thin-capitalization rules under ITA s. 18(4), which limit deductible interest paid by a Canadian subsidiary to its foreign parent, and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations attaching to non-resident beneficial owners. No exchange-control permission is required for the retention of foreign currency or for dividend remittance in CAD or USD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Trade receivables and export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD, CAD, or any freely convertible currency. For CRA and Ontario corporate assessment purposes, revenue is measured in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rate or another consistently applied reasonable rate, and foreign-exchange gains or losses are recognized under ITA s. 39(1), s. 95 rules for foreign affiliates, and IFRS 9 accounting standards.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as legal-tender issuer and monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises authorized banks, foreign-exchange dealers, and trade-finance providers in Canada; FINTRAC administers cross-border funds movement reporting, KYC/AML obligations, and international EFT reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a complementary payment rail, not as the primary treasury account. An offshore digital EMI is not a 'bank' under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and, absent an OSFI banking licence or a provincial credit-union licence, cannot establish a transferable deposit-taking bank account; its balances are characterized as e-money or payment float. To Canadian customers, an EMI must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA unless it operates under an exemption, and must comply with KYC/KYB, EFT reporting above CAD 10,000, sanctions screening, and transaction monitoring. Once fully in force, Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) will additionally require registration with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider. The Ontario distribution entity may lawfully use a compliant offshore EMI for low-value, time-critical trade disbursements to overseas component vendors, but CDIC deposit insurance does not cover EMI balances, and concentration of liquidity in an unlicensed foreign entity could breach a Schedule I bank's covenant on downstream funds movement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for large intercompany treasury transfers) from the foreign parent's bank, denominated in CAD or USD, credited to the Ontario operating account at a Schedule I bank. The payment narrative must state the true purpose as 'equity contribution to Canadian subsidiary' or 'intercompany shareholder loan', referencing the corporate investment resolution. The receiving bank must verify the remitter's identity and source of funds; FINTRAC reporting applies to any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada, with the purpose-of-payment narrative recorded; the narrative code should identify 'capital/equity contribution' or 'intercompany loan from related company'. No exchange-control approval is required and no CAD conversion certificate is necessary because Canada operates a floating-rate regime. The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal period end (I.T.A. s.150(1)). For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year.
