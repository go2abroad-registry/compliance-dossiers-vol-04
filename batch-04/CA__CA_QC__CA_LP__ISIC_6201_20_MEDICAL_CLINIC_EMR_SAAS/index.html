Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership; capital contributions are set by the limited partnership agreement. No escrow account is imposed by the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises for SaaS/EMR limited partnerships.

Funding & unlock path: The partnership must obtain a municipal occupancy permit for the physical office or server-room premises. Workplace fire safety clearance under the Fire Safety Act (CQLR c S-3.4) and the Quebec Building Code is required, including smoke alarms, emergency exits, extinguishers, and evacuation plans verified by the local fire department or preferred by a certified fire-safety inspector. If the establishment provides any server room with raised floors or heavy infrastructure, a structural load inspection by an engineer licensed in Quebec and, where applicable, an RBQ permit or certification is required. Occupational health and safety clearance is obtained by registering with the CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail) and just reaching compliance with the Act respecting occupational health and safety, CQLR c S-2.1, including electrical safety, ergonomics, first-aid kits, and an evacuation warden arrangement.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership; capital contributions are set by the limited partnership agreement. No escrow account is imposed by the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises for SaaS/EMR limited partnerships.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec — Quebec provincial income tax, QST, and payroll administration. Portals: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account; Revenu Québec Mon dossier entreprise.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must first determine whether it is carrying on business in Canada/Quebec. If required to register, obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA’s Business Registration Online, or by filing Form RC1. For Quebec, register with Revenu Québec for a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and a QST account, typically using the BN. Non-resident applicants must provide the partnership agreement, home-jurisdiction registration evidence, and identification of all partners. Quebec law may require designation of an attorney for service of process. After approval, the partnership receives its BN/QST account and must file periodic GST/HST and QST returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box entity classification election. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax and is treated separately as a person for GST/HST and QST. Procedural elections available include simplified accounting methods for GST/HST, such as the Quick Method, and certain elections under the Excise Tax Act respecting input tax credit allocation. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD annual worldwide taxable supply threshold for GST/HST small supplier status; $30,000 CAD equivalent threshold for QST. Non-residents carrying on a commercial activity in Quebec or supplying digital services in Canada may be required to register regardless of this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when exported from Canada and evidence shows delivery outside Canada. Services, including EMR SaaS and cloud software, supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act unless the service relates to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada or is otherwise excluded. QST zero-rating follows analogous export principles. The supplier must be registered and must retain evidentiary documents demonstrating the non-resident recipient and actual delivery or use outside Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency B13 Export Declaration (for physical goods; generally not applicable to pure SaaS services)
  • Customs or commercial invoice identifying the non-resident purchaser
  • Bill of lading or alternate evidence of export delivery where goods are bundled with services
  • Master Subscription Agreement / SOW for the EMR SaaS platform
  • Non-resident status evidence such as foreign registration certificate or registered office address
  • Service delivery logs, server records, IP addresses, or remote access evidence proving use outside Canada
  • Bank remittance or inward payment records confirming receipt of cross-border consideration

No mandatory real-time transaction clearance or digital stamping regime exists for ordinary B2B supplies in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and Peppol standards are permitted. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed GST/HST and QST information and retain electronic records for at least six years. Tax filings and remittances are submitted through CRA and Revenu Québec online systems and APIs.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST and QST at 14.975% where export substantiation is inadequate. Interest is compounded daily. Under the Excise Tax Act, knowingly or grossly negligently making a false or omitted statement can attract a penalty of 25% of the tax amount involved, plus additional administrative penalties. Reassessment periods extend indefinitely where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established. Criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment. QST penalties under Quebec tax administration law are similarly severe.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an unconditional IMF Article VIII jurisdiction: both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible, and the Canadian dollar is free-floating with no official convertibility ceiling, no prior-approval regime, and no repatriation or licensing hurdles for non-resident capital. For this Quebec Limited Partnership, the only practical convertibility frictions are: (i) Part XIII/Quebec withholding tax at source on distributions to non-resident partners (mitigated by tax treaties and by structuring distributions as loan repayments or guaranteed payments where commercially defensible); (ii) Canadian bank KYC/AML friction on inbound contribution wires, particularly where the remitter is a non-resident partner; and (iii) documentation of the LP's functional currency for IFRS/CTA purposes under CRA administrative practice. There are no exchange-control surrender or licensing requirements affecting the SaaS revenue stream.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender, deposit, repatriation, or mandatory conversion of export receipts. EMR SaaS subscription proceeds invoiced in USD or CAD by the Quebec LP may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, swept offshore, or converted through any registered Canadian dealer without CRA or Bank of Canada approval. The only binding duties are (i) accurate trade-in-services reporting under Statistics Canada's survey framework; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting by the financial intermediary for transactions at or above C$10,000; and (iii) the LP's obligation to furnish source-of-funds documentation to its clearing bank to avoid anomalous-activity flags on recurring USD inflows.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole issuing and monetary authority, acting as fiscal agent and manager of Canada's official international reserves. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; prudential oversight of banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while transaction-level cross-border surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The Minister of Finance holds formal control of the Exchange Fund Account, but no exchange-control statute restricts currency movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary rail; Low as a primary treasury repository. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK EMI licence category; a foreign electronic money institution may only operate in Canada as a registered money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA, with mandatory FINTRAC registration, and it may not take deposits or extend credit without a Canadian bank charter. Wise Business, Payoneer, and similar providers are viable for low-value payroll and contractor disbursements and for multi-currency receive accounts, but they lack: (i) CDIC deposit insurance, (ii) direct access to Lynx (Canada's high-value RTGS), and (iii) relationship stability for an LP holding capital contributions. Stripe Billing is retained as the merchant acquiring rail for clinic credit/debit card payments, settling in CAD to the National Bank operating account, while all treasury liquidity and committed capital remain in Schedule I bank accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol for non-resident LP capital contributions: (1) remitter executes a SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (capital payment) or 'EQUI' (equity investment contribution), designating the Quebec LP's name, the National Bank/RBC account number, and institution/transit codes in field 57D, with field 70 reading 'LP capital contribution'; (2) USD wires clear through the bank's US correspondent (e.g., BMO Harris or RBC's New York branch) while CAD wires achieve same-day finality through Lynx, Canada's SWIFT-enabled high-value RTGS; (3) the first contribution must be pre-announced to the bank's trade-surveillance desk to prevent an automatic compliance hold; (4) the LP delivers on demand the executed subscription agreement, partner identification, and source-of-funds bank statements; (5) FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting applies at or above C$10,000 per transaction, with the LP obligated to provide accurate remitter/beneficiary particulars; (6) funds remain uninvested in the Capital Escrow Account until post-receipt compliance review is completed and the bank confirms the capital can be deployed to operating or escrow accounts.For EMR SaaS operating outflows (cloud infrastructure, licensing, subcontractors, international payroll): (a) domestic CAD supplier payments use Lynx via the bank's host-to-host channel, providing irrevocable real-time finality;
