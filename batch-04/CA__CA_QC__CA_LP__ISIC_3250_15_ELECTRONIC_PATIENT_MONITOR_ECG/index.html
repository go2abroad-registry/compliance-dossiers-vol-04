Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Quebec limited partnership; contributions are fixed by the partnership contract and must be made in money or property only, since a limited partner may not contribute work or services (art. 2247 C.C.Q.). No statutory escrow account is mandated; called capital may be held in a Quebec bank account pending use, and the limited partners' liability is limited to their unpaid committed contribution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada – canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec – revenuquebec.ca/en (provincial fiscal authority for QST and Quebec income tax)

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships carrying on business in Quebec must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or online via the Business Registration Online portal. For QST, registration with Revenu Québec is mandatory, completed via Form LM-1 or the Registraire des entreprises (Quebec Business Registry). Required supporting documents include the partnership agreement, certificate of foreign registration, identification of all general and limited partners, and a statement of permanent establishment in Canada. If the LP is a non-resident without a permanent establishment but makes taxable supplies in Canada, it must still register for GST/HST and QST once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; the CRA may require security as a condition of registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A limited partnership under the Civil Code of Québec (arts. 2231 et seq.) is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; income and losses flow through to partners on an annual basis. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and is the registrant in its own right.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a general 'check-the-box' entity classification election; partnership status is statutorily imposed. Available elections relevant to an LP include: (i) the joint election by a partnership and its members under subsection 177(1) of the Excise Tax Act to deem members' supplies to the partnership as made by the partnership; (ii) a section 216 election for non-resident partners with Canadian rental income; (iii) a subsection 96(3) election for reasonable allocation of partnership income and losses; and (iv) equivalent elections under the Quebec Taxation Act and Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Income Tax Act and may require specific disclosure elections where cross-border disputes exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide taxable supplies other than financial services; small supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST, triggered on taxable supplies made in Canada/Quebec)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for the 0% rate of GST/HST under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods in the ordinary course of business, owns the goods at the time of export, and retains satisfactory evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated where the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real estate or tangible personal property located in Canada. The QST zero-rating provisions in the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax mirror the federal rules. To claim zero-rating, the LP must be a registrant, the export must occur within a reasonable time (generally 60 days), and the complete documentation bundle must be maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of B2B invoices. The CRA permits electronic invoices and records if they are accurate, readable, and accessible; Revenu Québec requires businesses to keep electronic sales records in a standardized format where applicable. For federal government procurement, suppliers must transmit invoices via the CanadaBuys PEPPOL e-invoicing platform. API-based GST/HST NETFILE is available for electronic transmission of returns; no mandatory XML or digital seal is imposed.

CRA and Revenu Québec may disallow zero-rating and issue a reassessment for uncollected GST/HST and QST plus interest and penalties. The gross negligence penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act is up to 50% of the net tax amount; false statements can incur penalties ranging from CAD 250 to CAD 25,000. Criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion may result in fines up to CAD 200,000, imprisonment for up to eight years on indictment, or both. The statutory limitation period for reassessment is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud attributable to the registrant.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible, freely floating currency. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls, capital-account approval regimes, surrender quotas, repatriation licences, or golden-currency restrictions on current or capital account transactions. A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) may therefore receive non-resident capital contributions, settle trade payables, distribute profits, and redeem limited partners' interests in CAD or any freely usable foreign currency without Bank of Canada or Department of Finance authorization. The practical constraints are documentary and tax-driven: the remittance narrative must accurately distinguish capital contributions, loan advances, and distributable profits to preserve the intended character under the Income Tax Act, and all cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC. Outbound payments of interest, royalties, management fees, and similar amounts to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax, with treaty-reduced rates available, so treasury instructions must preserve payment character to avoid recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio. The Quebec LP may retain, invest, or hedge 100% of its USD/EUR export receipts in domestic or foreign bank accounts in accordance with the limited partnership agreement's treasury policy. Export proceeds arising from NAICS 334510 medical-monitoring device sales remain subject only to ordinary FINTRAC record-keeping (five-year retention), MSB registration thresholds where applicable, and large-value EFT reporting; no central bank filing or conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada; BoC) is the central bank responsible for monetary policy, financial stability, and the national high-value settlement system (Lynx). Prudential supervision of federally incorporated deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while in Quebec the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates provincial securities and derivatives conduct. Canada does not operate a standalone foreign-exchange-control administration analogous to SAFE; the Minister of Finance holds residual statutory powers under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border wire monitoring and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary banking layer; MEDIUM only as a supplementary payment and FX rail. Foreign electronic-money institutions (UK FCA-authorised EMIs, EU/EEA e-money institutions, and similar digital banks) are not banks under the Bank Act and cannot open domestic CAD deposit accounts or provide CDIC-insured deposit services to a Quebec LP. To transact in Canada they must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, and any derivatives activity must respect AMF and CIRO registration rules. Most global EMIs will decline to act as the settlement bank for a partnership treasury because of correspondent-bank de-risking and the pass-through ownership opacity of limited partnerships; the practical deployment is therefore limited to virtual-card issuance, small-value supplier payments, or metadata routing, while the true liquidity and clearing layer must remain at a Schedule I bank.
