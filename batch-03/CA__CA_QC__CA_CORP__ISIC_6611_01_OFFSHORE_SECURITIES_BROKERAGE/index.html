Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA), the federal tax administrator for the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act; and Revenu Québec (RQ), the Quebec provincial tax administrator, which under the Canada-Quebec Tax Collection Agreement administers both the federal GST/HST and the provincial QST (Quebec Sales Tax) for Quebec-based registrants, plus provincial corporate income tax under the Quebec Taxation Act. Primary self-service portals are CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec Espace client (CliENT) (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/), with the Business Number (BN) and the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) as the operative tax identifiers.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate: file federal articles under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec articles under the Business Corporations Act (CQLR c S-31.1) and obtain the NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. Step 2 - Federal tax ID: apply for the 9-digit Business Number with CRA account identifiers (RC0001 payroll, RC0002 GST/HST, RC0005 corporate income tax) using Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, online through CRA Business Registration Online, or through the joint federal-Quebec one-stop filing. Step 3 - Quebec tax ID: register for QST and provincial income tax accounts through the Registraire des entreprises single window integrated with Revenu Québec; the NEQ and CRA BN are cross-referenced. Step 4 - Non-resident specifics: a foreign-incorporated securities broker must appoint a representative authorized to deal with the CRA and Revenu Québec (power of attorney via Form RC59 or a Quebec mandat), may rely on treaty relief by filing Form NR301 with Schedule A, and cannot obtain a BN solely to claim treaty benefits without Canadian nexus. Registration for GST/HST and QST is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive quarters or on the first taxable supply in Quebec not covered by the small-supplier exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is an independent taxable entity under Canadian and Quebec law, subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Quebec provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income if resident in Canada, with foreign tax credits and the BEPS-aligned CFC/foreign affiliate rules (ITA s. 91-95) governing offshore brokerage subsidiaries; there is no corporate pass-through or partnership election, and the small business deduction is available only to Canadian-controlled private corporations on active business income capped by the business limit.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime; corporate status is derived from the incorporating statute (per se classification) and cannot be re-elected. The operative elections for cross-border structures are (1) ITA s. 216(1) election to file a Canadian income tax return for rental/royalty income in lieu of Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA s. 115.1 election by a non-resident to compute tax at Canadian rates and reduce withholding; (3) hybrid mismatch elections under ITA s. 18.2(3)-(5), 18.3(4) and 18.4 electing to forgo deductions on hybrid instruments or hybrid transfers and implementing the OECD BEPS Action 2 outcomes; (4) GST/HST elections including the closely related corporations election (ETA s. 156), the direct payment/zero-rating elections for financial services to non-residents, and the ETA s. 150(2) group registration election; and (5) the Quebec QST parallel elections administered by Revenu Québec. Partnerships and trusts remain fact-based classifications with mandatory rules (ITA s. 102, 104), not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST and QST: registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies (excluding exempt financial services) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive quarters. A securities broker whose supplies are almost exclusively exempt financial services (Schedule V Part VII) need not register for GST/HST/QST unless it makes taxable supplies above the threshold, but may voluntarily register to recover input tax credits; non-resident persons are registrable as non-resident registrants where they carry on a taxable commercial activity or make taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GOODS: a supply of goods is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V s. 21-28 where the registrant exports the goods by commercial shipment within 60 days of the sale, causes delivery outside Canada, or supplies goods to a non-resident who immediately removes them from Canada; supporting carriage documents and the export declaration must be kept. SERVICES (offshore brokerage): the core commission, execution, clearing and custody services supplied by the retail broker are financial services ordinarily exempt under ETA Schedule V, Part VII, not zero-rated; zero-rating is available only for a supply of a financial service to a non-resident person under ETA Schedule VI, Part VI s. 7, subject to exclusions where the recipient is a specified financial institution carrying on business in Canada, the service relates to a financial instrument issued or sold by or on behalf of a Canadian resident, or the service finances property situated in Canada. An offshore-located retail client acquiring agency brokerage for non-Canadian-listed securities can therefore receive those services at 0% GST/HST/QST only if the non-resident status, the non-Canadian nature of the financial instrument, and the offshore settlement trail are documented contemporaneously; QST zero-rating mirrors the federal rules under the Quebec Taxation Act and Revenu Québec interpretation.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A or electronic export report transmitted through the Canadian International Trade Single Window) for any physical securities certificates, coins, or goods exported
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier proof of export evidencing removal from Canada within the statutory 60-day window
  • Commercial invoice and executed order-matching/trade confirmation identifying the non-resident offshore client and the securities brokerage service
  • Client onboarding file with Form NR301 or CRS self-certification establishing non-resident status and beneficial owner identity
  • Non-resident financial instrument documentation (ISIN, issuer domicile, listing exchange) demonstrating the instrument is not issued or sold by a Canadian resident
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 outward wire, or clearing-house settlement statement evidencing offshore settlement and FX repatriation within 180 days
  • Segregated client account statements and clearing/IIROC audit trail files supporting the agency basis of the brokerage service
  • GST/HST and QST rebate/credit substantiation package (CRA Form GST287 and Revenu Québec Form VD-11 equivalents) for any input taxes sought on export-related expenses

No mandatory real-time or near-real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance API regime applies to GST/HST/QST today. Canada is transitioning to voluntary Peppol-standard e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework, and Revenu Québec has piloted voluntary B2B e-invoicing; public sector suppliers increasingly face mandatory electronic invoicing obligations contractually. Tax registrants with taxable revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million per GST/HST year must file their returns electronically (including through Revenu Québec for QST). Under the securities regulatory framework, IIROC/CSA National Instrument 23-103 imposes mandatory electronic trade confirmation and record-keeping obligations for retail brokerage, requiring the preservation of electronic audit trails, order-execution records, and client identity files; these indirect records serve as the factual basis for GST/QST audit and zero-rating substantiation.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess an export zero-rating claim or the exemption basis within four years of the reporting period, with no limitation period where fraud, wilful misrepresentation, or gross negligence applies; denial triggers immediate assessment of the uncollected GST/HST/QST plus maintained daily compound interest on arrears at prescribed rates and a retrospective clawback of input tax credits. The gross-negligence penalty is 25% of the tax understated (ETA s. 285(1)); prosecutions for false statements or wilful evasion subject directors and senior officers to fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 24 months on summary conviction and up to five years on indictment (ETA s. 327), with parallel Quebec Taxation Act penalties. Export and zero-rating books must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286); failure to produce the custom documentation bundle on demand is an independent offence attracting penalties of at least CAD 8,000 for repeated failures, in addition to disqualification of the zero-rating. Regulated firms exposed to CRA sector projects targeting broker-dealers must expect SAS audits of non-resident certifications, FX reconciliation, and the 180-day repatriation trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII; Canada imposes no exchange-control law, no capital-account authorization regime, no foreign-currency surrender requirement, and no restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance for a Canadian corporation to a foreign shareholder is effected as dividends, subject to federal Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25%, reduced to 5%–15% under tax treaties (e.g., Canada–US treaty). Quebec does not superimpose a separate provincial withholding on non-resident dividends. Practical filters are limited to FINTRAC large-EFI reporting (CAD 10,000+), sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA account reporting, Statistics Canada FDI/International Investment Position reports, and Investment Canada Act notification thresholds for acquisitions of control or cultural-sector new businesses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable – no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank repatriation mechanism for export or brokerage proceeds. All Canadian export, trading, and client-fund receipts in foreign currency may be retained freely in CAD or foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks; conversion to CAD is at the bank's prevailing market rate without a purpose-code or prior-approval requirement. For the brokerage treasury, the only overlay is that CIRO dealer members must maintain client trust (segregated) funds in designated currencies and perform daily segregation calculations; this is an asset-integrity rule, not an FX-surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority; however, Canada operates no exchange-control agency. Banking prudential oversight rests with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); financial-intelligence and AML/ATF supervision rests with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). For a Quebec securities dealer (ISIC 6611/NAICS 523120), securities conduct, licensing, and client-asset protection are regulated by the AMF (Autorité des marchés financiers) and the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO, which succeeded IIROC in 2023).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for core client-asset segregation, clearing, and card settlement; MODERATE only for ancillary operating liquidity and multi-currency administrative wires. Domestic and foreign fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Ebury, Global Reach, OFX) are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime supervised by the Bank of Canada (phased-in 2024–2026), but they are not chartered Schedule I banks, are not CDIC members, and are not approved 'qualified institutions' to hold CIRO Rule 3200 segregated client funds. A foreign-domiciled EMI without Canadian MSB/EMD registration is categorically blocked from receiving client-funding wires for a retail securities broker. Therefore, the Segregated_Client_Wire and Visa/Mastercard settlement rails must be anchored at a Schedule I bank; EMIs may be used solely for vendor payments and low-value FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent-shareholder equity is injected as an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire from the offshore parent to the dealer's operating account at the Schedule I bank. Canada imposes no mandatory wire purpose code; however, the remitter reference must state 'Share capital contribution' and reference the board resolution authorizing issuance of shares. The receiving bank screens the UBO, the counterparty bank, and the wire against OSFI-administered sanctions lists; value is credited immediately after confirmation, and an FINTRAC large-EFI report is generated for amounts ≥ CAD 10,000. No exchange-conversion permit is required; CAD/USD conversion is at the free-market spot rate. Concurrently file Statistics Canada FDI reporting, confirm Investment Canada Act notification thresholds (new Canadian business/cultural-sector analysis), and complete CRS/FATCA self-certification (W-8BEN-E / Canada Schedule A) for the parent. Gain on injection is recorded as paid-up capital; ensure Quebec's share-capital and registration filings (Registraire des entreprises) are updated.Trade and client disbursements settle primarily over Payments Canada's Lynx real-time large-value system using SWIFT MT103/MT202 messaging for cross-border legs; intra-Canada CAD settlements clear via Lynx or ACSS. Securities transactions clear and settle through CDSX (CDS Clearing and Depository Services Inc.), with Continuous Linked Settlement (CLS) for the FX legs of securities trades to eliminate PvP settlement risk. Commercial letters of credit are not standard for equity/CFD/derivative brokerage; cash flows are executed via MT103 (customer disbursements) and MT202 (bank-level cover). Client payouts are debited from the CIRO Rule 3200 segregated client trust account and wired only to the client's own named bank account pursuant to FATF travel-rule data and bank sanctions screening; no Visa/Mastercard rails are used for outbound cash disbursements (card rails are deposit-only). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for core client-asset segregation, clearing, and card settlement; MODERATE only for ancillary operating liquidity and multi-currency administrative wires. Domestic and foreign fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Ebury, Global Reach, OFX) are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime supervised by the Bank of Canada (phased-in 2024–2026), but they are not chartered Schedule I banks, are not CDIC members, and are not approved 'qualified institutions' to hold CIRO Rule 3200 segregated client funds. A foreign-domiciled EMI without Canadian MSB/EMD registration is categorically blocked from receiving client-funding wires for a retail securities broker. Therefore, the Segregated_Client_Wire and Visa/Mastercard settlement rails must be anchored at a Schedule I bank; EMIs may be used solely for vendor payments and low-value FX conversion.
