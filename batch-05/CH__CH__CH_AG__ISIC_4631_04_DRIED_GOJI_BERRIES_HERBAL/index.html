Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid in cash before registration, deposited with a Swiss bank under a blocked capital confirmation (Sperrkonto) or an escrow/escrow account, and documentation of free availability must be presented to the notary and Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Feuerpolizeiliche Nutzungsbewilligung) issued by the cantonal Fire Prevention Inspectorate, including fire-protection compartmentation of the storage area, hydrant and extinguisher layout, loading dock lane safety, and electrical installation certificate. Structural load inspection of warehouse racks/storage heights by a recognised civil engineer in accordance with cantonal construction codes must be filed. Occupational clearance is mandatory under the Federal Ordinance on Accident Prevention (VUV), with a low-risk warehouse declaration to the Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund (SUVA) or an approved private occupational insurer, confirming statutory accident-insurance coverage for employees, ergonomic handling instruction for pallet movement, and audible/fire-exit signage.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid in cash before registration, deposited with a Swiss bank under a blocked capital confirmation (Sperrkonto) or an escrow/escrow account, and documentation of free availability must be presented to the notary and Commercial Register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Eidgenoessische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Administration federale des contributions (AFC) / Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA); VAT Main Division, Schwarztorstrasse 25, 3003 Bern; central UID (business identification) register portal at www.uid.ch; official e-services portal at www.estv.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft obtains its Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID-CH) ex officio upon entry in the Cantonal Commercial Register; the UID-CH simultaneously serves as the Swiss VAT ID (MWST-Nr.). A foreign entity without a Swiss commercial register entry must apply to the ESTV VAT Main Division for a UID-CH and VAT registration before performing taxable supplies if cumulative worldwide turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. Non-resident applicants must appoint a Swiss service address and, in practice, a Swiss fiscal representative; a non-resident exporter with no Swiss nexus and no domestic taxable supplies has no Swiss VAT registration obligation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the Swiss AG is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to direct federal tax (8.5% statutory rate on net profit; approximately 4.33% effective after pro-rata relief), cantonal and communal profit taxes, net wealth tax on equity, and Swiss VAT on taxable supplies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Swiss AG is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to direct federal tax (8.5% statutory rate on net profit; approximately 4.33% effective after pro-rata relief), cantonal and communal profit taxes, net wealth tax on equity, and Swiss VAT on taxable supplies. Residence-based worldwide taxation applies, tempered by the situ-territoire principle exempting foreign permanent establishment profits and foreign immovable property; pass-through or check-the-box treatment is not available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Swiss law. Available elections relevant to an AG exporter: (i) group VAT unit election (Steuergruppe / unite fiscale) for economically integrated Swiss entities; (ii) participation exemption / reduction (Beteiligungsabzug) election on qualifying dividend and capital gain income; (iii) cantonal notional interest deduction (NID) in participating cantons; (iv) election of the Swiss VAT reporting period (quarterly, semi-annual or annual); (v) small-business category election with renunciation of input VAT deduction; (vi) election to treat the AG as a holding company under cantonal holding status principles only for older tax regimes (grandfathered cases); (vii) option to claim foreign PE income exemption under the ordinary exemption method.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (exclusive of exempt supplies and real estate sale costs) triggers mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Enhanced thresholds apply to certain categories: CHF 250,000 for donation-collecting non-profits and CHF 150,000 for agricultural and healthcare activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies where the supplier proves, with export documentation, that goods were physically removed from Swiss territory (or from a Swiss customs free zone) to a foreign destination for a foreign-domiciled buyer or a foreign permanent establishment of a Swiss entity. Directly related transport, logistics, commission, and packaging services provided in connection with the export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals equally qualify for 0%. The supplier must receive the consideration from the foreign buyer and retain a coherent file demonstrating the commercial identity of goods, the dispatch route, and the foreign recipient. Qualifying export transactions preserve the full input VAT deduction and are reported in the periodic Swiss VAT return.

  • Validated customs export declaration (ARD / Ausfuhrdeklaration lodged via GemDat) or transit and end-use customs documentation for removals from customs bonded warehouses
  • Commercial invoice with HS tariff classification and Incoterms for dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, including the foreign consignee details
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR international consignment note evidencing the physical border crossing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank transfer credit confirmation showing receipt of foreign consideration in the Swiss settlement account
  • Packing list and phytosanitary certificate demonstrating botanical identity and compliance with food or herbal supplement export controls

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API filing, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B and public-sector exchange uses the SwissPeppol (eBill/Peppol BIS) standard; the Swiss QR-bill is mandatory for all payment slips issued to Swiss residents from 1 July 2020. Invoices must satisfy the statutory content requirements of Art. 26a of the VAT Ordinance in any format or medium, with a 10-year electronic archiving obligation for all export substantiation records.

The ESTV audit and refund units examine export zero-ratings for formal dossier completeness and economic substance, including the reality of the foreign buyer and the Swiss bank receipt. Deficiencies trigger reclassification as a domestic taxable supply at 8.1% VAT, plus default interest of 4.5%-5% per annum; tax omission penalties reach up to three times the tax evaded (Art. 175 MWSTG). Fraudulent zero-rating schemes attract criminal fines and potential imprisonment, with assessment limitation periods of 5 years for negligent omissions and 10 years for intentional evasion or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no licensing requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of share capital, dividends, interest, or trade proceeds. The Capital Account is fully open for non-resident investors, and the current account is unrestricted. Profit remittance is subject only to standard Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividends unless reduced by treaty or participation relief; no additional cantonal or federal gates apply. Non-resident shareholders may freely hold and transfer AG shares, subject to entry into the Swiss commercial register for qualified participations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts in any currency may be retained offshore, converted at freely negotiated market rates, or held in domestic multi-currency accounts. Receipts should be documented in the Swiss customs electronic export declaration system (NCTS) and in the company's statutory accounts; Swiss GAAP requires accrual recognition at the spot exchange rate at transaction date.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF), with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) coordinating cross-border financia affairs

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI entities are not authorised banking institutions under Swiss law and cannot open resident operational bank accounts in Switzerland. They are only viable for auxiliary payment processing, not for the primary liquidity or escrow rail. Swiss FINMA-licensed fintech companies (e.g., bank-like payment firms under the Fintech Licence regime) may hold client funds up to CHF 100 million on interest-bearing accounts, but they do not provide commercial credit or documentary credit facilities. Foreign EMIs such as UK-licensed or EU-passporting EMIs may clear CHF only through Swiss SIX-member banks, exposing the principal to ring-fenced counterparty risk. Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury account; Medium as an ancillary low-value collection channel for non-core markets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders may inject capital via SWIFT wire in CHF, EUR, or USD into the AG's capital escrow account at a Swiss tier-1 bank. There is no exchange control approval; the bank will request the SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) to state 'Contribuzione Capitale' or 'Capital Contribution' and may require a copy of the notarised capital payment instruction. For CHF transfers, the rail is SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) operated by SIX Interbank Clearing. EUR transfers use SEPA or SWIFT; USD transfers use correspondent banks via CHIPS or Fedwire. The bank will verify that the sending institution holds a valid SWIFT BIC and that the source of funds corresponds to the UBO declaration. The account should be flagged as 'Capital Escrow' until the commercial register confirms the share capital increase, after which the account can be automatically transmuted into the operational account. The bank will provide a payment acceptance certificate (Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) for regulatory evidence.For open-account terms, the company issues SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers to international suppliers through the SIC/SEPA or correspondent banking network. For intra-Swiss payments, QR-bill or ISO 20022 payment instructions via SIC are standard. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on after-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal + cantonal + communal effective rate for a Zug-headquartered AG approximates 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on all dividend distributions, constructive dividends and similar distributions by the Swiss AG (Art. 4 VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the close of the fiscal year; calendar-year AGs must file by 30 June of the following year. Written extension requests of one to three months are generally granted by the Zug tax administration; a non-calendar fiscal year requires prior approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for Swiss groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million; Master File and Local File preparation follows OECD standards where cross-border related-party transactions of the AG exceed approximately CHF 10 million annually (no codified Swiss statutory threshold, but documentation must be contemporaneous and produced on ESTV demand).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations as in other holding jurisdictions, but the substance-over-form doctrine and OECD BEPS minimum standards apply strictly: the AG must maintain real business premises, qualified local employees, active management and decision-making in Switzerland, and Swiss bank accounts; letterbox entities are denied treaty benefits under the MLI/PPT, face recharacterization of income as constructive dividends (Art. 21 VStG), and risk revocation of the statutory wholesale authorization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Art. 175 DBG: fine of up to 1.5 times the evaded federal tax for a first offense and up to 3 times for a repeat offense, plus default interest at 4.5% p.a.; tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG carries imprisonment of up to five years and additional fines; the unpaid tax remains fully collectible and cantonal penalties apply in parallel.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories who knowingly caused the under-declaration face criminal conviction, passport seizure and travel bans under the Swiss Code of Criminal Procedure, removal from the commercial register, and civil restitution claims by the tax administration and creditors under Art. 754 OR; culpable participation also triggers anti-money-laundering reporting.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Verrechnungssteuer (35% WHT)

Statutory sanction: The AG is jointly and severally liable for the grossed-up unpaid WHT (Art. 14 VStG), compounded with 4.5% default interest (Art. 13 VStG) and a procedural fine of up to CHF 10,000 (Art. 60 VStG); the WHT claim enjoys statutory priority in bankruptcy and any under-withholding on benefits-in-kind is reclassified as a further taxable distribution.

Director personal liability: Board members who ordered or tolerated unauthorized distributions are personally liable for the shortfall; culpable violation of the WHT obligation is a crime under Art. 62 VStG punishable by imprisonment of up to three years, and the administration may sue directors directly for restitution of the uncollected amounts.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Profit correction under Art. 57-58 DBG raising the taxable profit at the effective Zug rate of about 11.9%, reclassification of the shifted amount as a constructive dividend subject to 35% WHT plus 4.5% interest, penalty of up to 1.5 times the additional tax, and denial of corresponding relief under MAP/APA procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified false transfer pricing documentation or instructed external advisors to prepare misleading benchmarks are criminally exposed under Art. 186 DBG and become personally liable for the uncollectible tax under Art. 218 DBG in case of company insolvency; professional advisors face license and disciplinary sanctions.
Shell-Company Substance Deficiency and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits and domestic deductions under the MLI/PPT anti-abuse rules, retroactive reclassification of accumulated profit as a 35% WHT-charged constructive dividend, revocation of the wholesale food and botanical license under the Swiss Foodstuffs Act, AML fines of up to CHF 500,000 (Art. 37 AMLA) with higher FINMA enforcement measures, imprisonment of up to three years under Art. 305bis StGB, and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal money-laundering convictions with imprisonment and fines at the same scale, travel restrictions and asset freezes during investigation, disqualification from future board service, and joint and several civil liability to the company, tax authorities and wronged third parties under Art. 754 OR.
