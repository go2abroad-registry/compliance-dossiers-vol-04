Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration, GST/HST registration and audit; online portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — export declarations via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS). Ontario Ministry of Finance / Ontario Business Registry — provincial HST component and Ontario LP registration registry.

E-government filing portal

An Ontario Limited Partnership is not automatically issued a tax identifier; active application to the CRA is required. Procedure: (1) Maintain current Ontario Business Registry registration, identifying the general and limited partners; the LP must have at least one general partner resident in Ontario to qualify as a 'Canadian partnership' under ITA s.102. (2) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via CRA MyBA or by fax/mail to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre, requesting the 'RT' program account for GST/HST; a foreign-owned LP must designate an agent with a Canadian care-of address and disclose non-resident partner status. (3) For export of goods, register the 'RM' customs/export program account with the CBSA, linked to the same 9-digit BN. (4) The LP itself files no T2 corporate return, but must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually and allocate flow-through income/loss to each partner; each partner remains responsible for their own T1/T2 filings. (5) The CRA typically issues the BN immediately online or within 5-10 business days on paper.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act s.96, the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax is imposed, and each partner (resident or non-resident) includes allocated income or loss. For GST/HST purposes, however, the Excise Tax Act s.248 defines 'person' to include a partnership, so the LP is a distinct registrant that must charge, collect, remit, and claim input tax credits for HST. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act s.96, the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax is imposed, and each partner (resident or non-resident) includes allocated income or loss. For GST/HST purposes, however, the Excise Tax Act s.248 defines 'person' to include a partnership, so the LP is a distinct registrant that must charge, collect, remit, and claim input tax credits for HST. Territorial scope: Ontario supplies of surveying services are within the place-of-supply rules (ETA s.143.2 and related regulations) and attract the 13% HST (5% federal GST plus 8% Ontario component). No tax-exempt or free-zone regime applies to ordinary Ontario survey operations.

Available election forms

Canada does not employ a US-style check-the-box election; classification is fixed by provincial LP law and ITA s.102. Available elections include: (i) ITA s.96(3) partnership fiscal-period election, aligning the LP's fiscal year-end with all partners; (ii) ETA s.240(2.2) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a non-resident or below-threshold supplier engaged in commercial activity in Canada; (iii) ETA s.156/s.273 elections for closely related corporations and partnerships to treat inter-party taxable supplies as nil-consideration where conditions are met; (iv) for the U.S. owners, an IRC 7701 Form 8832 classification election can treat the Canadian LP as a corporation for U.S. purposes, though this does not alter Canadian pass-through status; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4 and s.163.2 restrict deduction benefits for hybrid arrangements; and (vi) 'SIFT partnership' anti-avoidance rules under ITA s.197 may apply only to publicly-listed partnerships, not a private marine-survey LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148(1), measured against the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any single calendar quarter and in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; associated persons and partnerships are aggregated for threshold purposes. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory (ETA s.240(1)); below the threshold, voluntary registration (ETA s.240(2.2)) is available to claim input tax credits on Ontario survey-related drydock, inspection equipment, and fuel costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is governed by Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. (A) Goods: a supply of goods physically exported by the registrant supplier (s.1) or delivered to a common carrier or consignee outside Canada (s.2) qualifies for 0% GST/HST. (B) Services: s.7 zero-rates a supply made to a non-resident person, but the zero-rating is excluded where the service is rendered to an individual in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, or is directly connected with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time it is rendered. Consequently, an on-site marine hull or cargo survey performed on a vessel or goods located at an Ontario port is taxable at 13% HST even if billed to a foreign underwriter; only desk-based loss assessment or pre-claim services concerning property located outside Canada can qualify for zero-rating under s.7. (C) A 'necessary part' of a zero-rated international transportation service may be zero-rated under s.9, but insurance-loss surveying is not a necessary transport service. (D) Marine insurance claim surveying is not a 'financial service' under Schedule V, Part VII and is therefore a taxable supply. The LP must be a GST/HST registrant to claim zero-rating or refunds of paid HST on expenses.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) or an authorized customs service provider for exported goods exceeding CAD 2,000 or regulated goods, showing the LP's 'RM' customs BN, value, exporter reference, and foreign destination.
  • Transport proof: original or certified Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or intermodal cargo manifest identifying the consignee outside Canada, shipment dates, and foreign discharge port; the CRA treats the Bill of Lading as the primary export-continuity evidence.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: SWIFT MT103, bank advice, or Canadian account statement evidencing receipt of payment in CAD or convertible foreign currency from the foreign insurer/underwriter, cross-referenced to the survey contract and insurance claim file; for zero-rated services, the written contract with the non-resident insurer and the surveyor's declaration that the property was outside Canada at performance are also required.

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or CRA tax-authority invoice validation is currently in force in Canada. A GST/HST registrant must issue a 'GST/HST invoice' no later than 30 days after the supply, showing the vendor BN, the 'RT' account, date, description, consideration, and HST amount or a 'tax included' statement. E-invoicing on a voluntary, interoperable Peppol/PAN-Canadian framework is acceptable but not mandatory; CRA consultation on compulsory e-invoicing for large enterprises is underway with potential implementation around 2028-2029. The CRA retains full electronic audit powers, including Requests for Information and Data Retrieval/Analysis under ETA s.286.

CRA limitation and scrutiny: GST/HST reassessments must generally be issued within 4 years (ETA s.298), extendable without limit where a taxpayer has waived in Form RC231 or where fraud, misrepresentation, or gross negligence is present. Income-tax reassessments of partners follow the 3-year (individual) or 4-year (corporate) normal reassessment windows under ITA s.152(4), equally unlimited for misrepresentation. Penalties: (i) ETA s.285 — 50% of the tax evaded for knowingly or through gross negligence making false or misleading statements in an application, return, or export invoice; (ii) ETA s.280 — failure-to-register penalties; (iii) ETA s.281 — failure-to-file penalties; and (iv) criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 and Criminal Code s.380(1) for intentional evasion. Retrospective clawbacks disallow the zero-rating, re-assess HST at 13%, deny input tax credits, and impose daily compound arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate. A marine surveyor claiming zero-rating on on-site Ontario port inspections is a high audit-risk profile because s.7 expressly excludes services connected with Canadian-situated tangible personal property; the CRA applies dedicated export-verification reviews and requires contemporaneous dock, vessel, and travel records. The 180-day benchmark reflects the CRA's customary expectation that foreign consideration be repatriated within approximately six months; slower realization may trigger transfer-pricing and residency scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital transactions. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound LP capital subscriptions, outbound trade disbursements, dividend-like distributions, or profit repatriation by non-resident limited partners. Capital account and current account convertibility are equally unrestricted, subject only to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering compliance, CRS/FATCA reporting, and beneficial-ownership verification. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore tax-informational and bank-compliance matters rather than FX regulatory controls; Canada does not impose surrender requirements, repatriation quotas, or repatriation taxes at the FX level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender export receipts to the Bank of Canada. Marine survey and inspection export revenues may be retained in Canadian dollars or in any freely convertible foreign currency. Exporters and service providers remain subject to general reporting duties: FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, customs export documentation for physical cargo where applicable, and Canadian income-tax reporting of foreign-source income. No surrender or conversion mandate exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; co-administrators: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), FINTRAC, Global Affairs Canada sanctions administration, and Department of Finance Canada. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (Wise Canada Limited) is viable as a secondary payment rail only, not as the primary corporate bank account for the Ontario limited partnership. Wise operates as a Canadian money-services business registered under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not as a Schedule I bank; it is not CDIC-insured and cannot hold partnership capital escrow, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide domestic Canadian clearing equivalent to Lynx. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC MSB registration, provincial money-transfer or licensing obligations, mandatory beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds verification for account openings, and enhanced monitoring of high-value corporate transactions. Wise is suitable for small outbound global surveyor disbursements, reimbursement of overseas inspection expenses, and low-value invoices, but all statutory capital injections and underwriter settlements should clear through a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscriptions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the general partner or contributing limited partner's home bank to the beneficiary account held with a Canadian Schedule I bank. The remittance information field should state 'SUBSCRIPTION — CA_LP — [capital-call reference or LP agreement number]'; Canada has no statutory purpose-code requirement, but the narrative must align exactly with the account name and KYC profile. Beneficiary account details must include the payee bank's SWIFT BIC plus the Canadian institution number, transit number, and account number; for USD wires, the correspondent chain must be identified by the Canadian receiving bank. High-value CAD payments settle over Lynx, the Payments Canada large-value clearing system; the receiving bank will perform source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening, and title/UBO checks before crediting. For capital injections above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC EFT reporting is mandatory for the financial institution; the LP must be able to produce the original capital-call notice, subscription agreement, beneficial-ownership declaration, and proof of foreign exchange conversion if the subscription was converted into CAD.Outbound disbursements to overseas sub-surveyors, insurers, and marine trade counterparties should use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments, with the beneficiary's SWIFT BIC and account number or clearing code in the payment instructions. Domestic CAD disbursements should clear through Payments Canada systems where Lynx is used for high-value/time-critical payments and the Retailer Settlement Service for lower-value batch payments. For transactions where marine-cargo inspection certificates are required, a Commercial Letter of Credit can be used through the tier-1 bank's trade desk: the LP may act as the nominated surveyor or inspection beneficiary under an LC, but the LC proceeds must be supported by a conforming certificate of survey, signed damage survey report, and presentation documents. Wise can be used as a low-value auxiliary rail for small international reimbursements, but it must not be used as the sole clearing platform for underwriter claim wires or large trade settlements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million in gross revenue from transactions with non-residents (including cross-border services, royalties, and interest) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation under CRA's transfer pricing documentation requirements.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. Partnerships must demonstrate real economic activity, including physical presence, employees, and business purpose, to avoid recharacterization of transactions or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Ontario Corporations Tax Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest and penalties; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the evasion; civil restitution orders, travel bans, and potential seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership structure under GAAR, reallocate income to the partners, and impose a 25% penalty on the tax benefit; treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in tax treaties.

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities if the partnership is deemed a sham; directors of corporate partners may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal charges for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be personally fined and imprisoned (up to 5 years) for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations; civil liability for damages arising from money laundering activities.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price, impose a 10% penalty on the net adjustment amount (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000), and apply interest on the additional tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be liable for the penalty if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing misstatement; personal liability for the penalty amount and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
