Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec (RQ) - Mon dossier d'entreprise portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca). Both authorities administer the federal GST/HST and Quebec QST (TVQ) registration regimes under the federal-provincial GSTRQ single-registration cooperation for establishments in Quebec.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the nine-digit NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. Step 2: Register online through CRA Business Registration Online for the 15-character Business Number (BN); CRA issues the RC program account (corporate income tax) and the RT program account (GST/HST) once the registration obligation or voluntary election is triggered. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec for the QST (TVQ/TQ) account; Quebec-payroll accounts are administered by Revenu Québec rather than CRA, so no federal RP account is required for a Quebec-only workforce. For foreign non-resident digital suppliers: simplified GST/HST and QST registration is available electronically without Canadian legal presence once taxable supplies to Canadian/Quebec recipients exceed CAD 30,000; the same Business Number and a QST number are issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian or Quebec business corporation is a separate legal and tax taxpayer subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income under Part I of the federal Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act; there is no pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification for corporations. For indirect tax purposes, the entity is a GST/HST and QST registrant above the small-supplier threshold and a non-small supplier of taxable digital services.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canadian federal or Quebec tax law. Available elections: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier and voluntary QST registration by a QST small supplier; (ii) election under the Excise Tax Act for supplies between closely related corporations to be deemed made for nil consideration; (iii) GST/HST fiscal-unit election enabling qualifying closely related resident corporations to be treated as a single registrant; (iv) GSTRQ combined GST/QST registration election streamlining filings with CRA and Revenu Québec. Hybrid-mismatch rules based on BEPS Action 2 apply to cross-border arrangements and do not provide any domestic opt-out election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in any four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST small-supplier status; parallel CAD 30,000 threshold for QST small-supplier status, including the digital-economy simplified registration rule for supplies to Quebec consumers over a 12-month period. Revenues from zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold calculation; in-app subscriptions and virtual items sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to Canadian or Quebec consumers are taxable and count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating (0%) applies under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act to supplies of services and intangible personal property made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and where the supply is for consumption wholly outside Canada; QST mirror zero-rating applies under the Quebec Sales Tax Act and is administratively harmonized with the federal rules. Voice-streaming subscriptions and virtual-gift sales qualify for zero-rating only where the corporation retains evidence that the end-user is a non-resident of Canada and the service is consumed outside Canada (e.g., ordinary residence metadata, foreign IP/delivery records, contract terms, and payment origin). Zero-rating is denied where the digital supply is deemed to be made in Canada, where the recipient is a GST/QST registrant for use in Canada, or where an App Store platform acting as merchant of record collects Canadian GST/QST under the platform supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A export declaration with Unique Identifier or C21 form) - required only if physical promotional merchandise or hardware is shipped; for pure cloud voice-streaming services no Canadian customs exit filing is generated, and the documented export invoice set serves as the equivalent evidentiary record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill with carrier-signed proof of export - required for physical goods consignments; for digital delivery, substitute Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console settlement reports and disbursement advices evidencing non-Canadian end-user download metadata and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - evidenced by bank credit advices, SWIFT MT103/MT950 records, and App Store/Google Play payout statements confirming foreign-currency repatriation to the corporation's Quebec bank account within the compliance treasury window

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API validation, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Canada or Quebec. Invoices must contain the prescribed GST/HST and QST registration numbers and particulars required under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Sales Tax Act, and supporting books and records must be retained for six years. Electronic invoicing is lawful and acceptable to CRA and Revenu Québec; Quebec has announced a phased roadmap toward mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing, but no binding implementation date applies to this business at the compliance date.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where a zero-rating claim is supported by misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Unsupported export claims are clawed back retroactively at the combined 14.975% rate (5% GST plus 9.975% QST) together with prescribed-rate remittance interest, and may attract the gross-negligence penalty (minimum CAD 500 and generally 25% of the understated net tax). Knowingly making false statements or participating in fraudulent export zero-rating is a criminal offence under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and parallel Quebec provisions, carrying fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years upon indictment. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing merchant-of-record settlement records are high-visibility audit data points, and CRA and Revenu Québec cross-match platform payout statements against declared zero-rated export revenue to expose mismatches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose prior approval, capital controls, repatriation restrictions, or compulsory conversion for foreign investment, shareholder loans, dividends, service fees, royalties, or ordinary trade payments. Profit remittance remains subject to available corporate law authority, solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing support, treaty eligibility, thin-capitalization and interest-deductibility rules, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, and bank anti-money-laundering review. Canadian-resident corporations must maintain adequate books and records and may have cross-border reporting obligations, including specified foreign-property reporting, related-party transaction reporting, and information returns where applicable. Non-resident ownership is generally permitted, but federal or provincial corporate-law director-residency rules and beneficial-owner disclosure requirements may apply depending on the incorporating statute and corporate profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual settlement terms, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and bank monitoring. Canadian banks may request invoices, contracts, payment explanations, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and beneficial-owner information for unusual or high-value receipts. Cash or monetary-instrument movements of CAD 10,000 or more across the Canadian border are subject to reporting by the relevant carrier or traveller under applicable customs and proceeds-of-crime rules; this threshold does not create an export-receipt conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration or mandatory exchange-control authority for ordinary commercial transactions. Applicable oversight may also involve the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Autorité des marchés financiers for Quebec-regulated derivatives and financial-market matters, and Global Affairs Canada for sanctions and export controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collection, payout, and foreign-currency functionality; low as the sole Canadian treasury bank. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, payment, foreign-exchange, or money-services functionality to Canadian residents without the required Canadian regulatory basis, registrations, exemptions, safeguarding arrangements, and consumer or payment-law compliance. A foreign platform may be usable for corporate treasury only after confirming its licensing jurisdiction, permitted cross-border services, Canadian solicitation status, funds safeguarding, sanctions controls, data handling, and settlement access. It should not replace a Canadian bank account for Canadian payroll, tax remittances, Apple or Google settlement reconciliation, operating expenses, or regulated payment activity. Any Canadian money-services-business activity may trigger registration and compliance obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a regulated Canadian bank by SWIFT or correspondent-bank wire to the corporation's Canadian account. Use a clear payment reference such as 'equity subscription', 'capital contribution', or 'shareholder loan', consistent with the legal documents; Canada does not generally use a mandatory nationwide FX purpose-code system for ordinary commercial wires. Before receipt, provide the bank with the subscription agreement or loan agreement, board or shareholder approval, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, remitter details, expected amount and currency, and the intended use of funds. The corporation should record the receipt consistently in its minute book and accounting records, issue shares or recognize debt as legally documented, reconcile the bank credit to the remittance advice, and assess thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, interest deductibility, and related-party reporting consequences. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, anonymous wallets, or unexplained payment intermediaries. Incoming wires may be screened, held, returned, or reported where AML, sanctions, fraud, or correspondent-bank concerns arise.Use Canadian-bank domestic EFT, wire transfer, or SWIFT MT103 for ordinary foreign supplier, contractor, cloud-hosting, advertising, platform, and professional-service payments. Each payment file should include the legal payee name, verified bank details, invoice or contract number, service description, currency, tax treatment, and sanctions screening result. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate: 15% federal (Income Tax Act, Part I, s. 123) plus 11.5% Quebec provincial (Taxation Act, s. 771). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) benefits from a combined 12.2% rate on the first C$500,000 of active business income (9% federal small business deduction + 3.2% Quebec small business deduction), but this is unavailable where the corporation is controlled, in law or in fact, by non-residents (e.g., a US parent).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (s. 212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Quebec imposes no separate dividend withholding tax on non-residents, as the federal Part XIII tax is shared with the province. Under the Canada-US tax treaty (Article X): 5% for dividends paid to a US corporation that beneficially owns at least 10% of the voting shares; otherwise 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year (e.g., December 31 year-end is due by June 30). The balance of federal corporate tax is due two months after year-end for a non-CCPC and three months after year-end for an eligible CCPC; Quebec balance payment follows the parallel calendar.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 Information Return is mandatory where the aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (the CRA 'Local File' standard per Information Circular IC 87-2R) should be prepared for all material intercompany transactions to sustain the 'reasonable efforts' defence against the s. 247(3) penalty. A mandatory Master File applies to fiscal years beginning after 2021 for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$1,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting obligations arise for fiscal years beginning after 2015 where the MNE group's consolidated revenue is at least C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR regimes are specific to offshore financial centres such as the UAE and BVI). The functional Canadian equivalents are: (i) the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) invalidating abusive avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance; (ii) judicial substance-over-form and 'sham' transaction doctrines; (iii) the corporate-residence test based on central management and control; (iv) anti-treaty-shopping jurisprudence and OECD BEPS minimum standards applied by CRA; (v) permanent establishment risk: a Quebec voice-streaming operation with local staff, servers or editorial control may create a Canadian PE for the foreign parent, exposing profits to Part I/Part XIV tax. A Quebec corporation holding nominal staffing, no board activity and all IP offshore will attract GAAR re-characterization of intercompany royalties and management fees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fine of between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to five years; s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of tax avoided or misclaimed; GAAR reversal under s. 245 and reassessment within the normal or extended statutory periods; Quebec Tax Administration Act penalties of 50% to 150% and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who signed or directed false filings are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax arrears, penalties and interest; courts pierce the corporate veil on fraud; criminal conviction may trigger travel bans pending sentencing, asset seizure and forced sale of personal property, and extradition exposure where offshore proceeds are concealed.
