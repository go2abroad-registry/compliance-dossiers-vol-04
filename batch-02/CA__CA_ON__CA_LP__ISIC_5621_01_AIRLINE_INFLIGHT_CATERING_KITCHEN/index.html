Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Ontario limited partnership (minimum_capital_requirement: false). Contributions are set under the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be money, property, or services, per s. 4 of the Limited Partnerships Act. No escrow or capital deposit account is mandated by statute; capital is payable to the partnership bank account upon drawdown in accordance with the Agreement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; registration and compliance portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO). Ontario's HST is administered federally by the CRA; the Ontario Ministry of Finance retains provincial records under the Ontario Taxpayer Protection Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign Limited Partnership (LP) carrying on business in Ontario must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Standard SOP: (1) Ensure the LP is validly registered under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act and obtain the filed certificate of limited partnership; (2) apply for a BN by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with CRA, or through the Business Registration Online service, identifying the entity as a partnership; attach the partnership agreement and Ontario certificate; (3) register for a GST/HST (RT) account if taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or if voluntary registration is desired; (4) open a payroll (RP) account for any Canadian employees; (5) open a customs import/export account with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for imported foodstuffs and packaging; (6) designate a Canadian resident agent for service of documents and maintain a Canadian address for CRA notices; (7) file annual Form T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (with Territorial_Scope for non-resident partners) - The Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under Part I of the Income Tax Act; the partnership itself is not a taxable entity and only files an information return. Each partner is taxable on its allocated share of Canadian-source income; non-resident partners are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source business income through the partnership and may rely on applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by legal form, and an LP is automatically treated as a partnership. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.2(2) to align the partnership's fiscal year-end; (ii) ETA elections, including the s. 156 election to treat supplies among closely related registrants as non-taxable, although generally not available to partnerships with unrelated partners; (iii) where applicable, ETA s. 226 non-resident registrant election to be governed by simplified GST/HST rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 apply to cross-border partnership mismatches without any elective relief; taxpayers are limited to substantiating 'reasonably comparable income inclusion' or excluding the mismatch amount.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - worldwide taxable supplies of the LP in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, or in any single calendar quarter, determine 'small supplier' status. Below this threshold, registration is not mandatory; above it, registration is compulsory once the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada, with special rules for non-residents carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (0% GST/HST). For airline inflight catering, meals and ancillary supplies prepared in the Ontario kitchen and delivered to an international air carrier for consumption onboard an outbound international flight qualify as zero-rated exports. Services supplied to a non-resident air carrier are zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident recipient and is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is rendered, subject to the GST/HST place-of-supply rules.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A and corresponding export reporting record filed with CBSA)
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing international carriage of the catering shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank records evidencing foreign-currency remittance received from the airline

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government API validation applies to GST/HST in Canada or Ontario. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for most registrants), maintain machine-readable books and records, and comply with CRA e-invoicing readiness. Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary for business-to-business transactions and not yet operationalized as a clearance model.

CRA export verification audits target zero-rating claims by inflight caterers. Failure to retain prescribed export evidence triggers reassessment at the full 13% HST rate with interest, a four-year retroactive clawback (with an indefinite reassessment period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud), and civil penalties, including the ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the net tax involved. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for wilful evasion can result in fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment; parallel fraudulent-evasion charges under ITA s. 239 may also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada does not operate exchange controls requiring administrative approval for cross-border payments, foreign investment, or profit remittances. Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners and distributions to them are freely transferable subject to Income Tax Act (Canada) withholding obligations, applicable tax treaty withholding relief for non-resident partners, and standard financial institution KYC review. An Ontario limited partnership is a pass-through entity; non-resident partners may face Part XIII withholding on allocated Canadian-source income unless treaty relief is obtained, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications are required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory foreign exchange conversion timeline. Exporters, including airport in-flight catering operators serving international air carriers, may retain export receipts in any currency and are under no obligation to repatriate proceeds to Canada. For banking compliance, all incoming international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are automatically reportable by financial institutions to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and exporters should maintain invoices, airline catering agreements, and customs/waybill documentation to demonstrate legitimate trade origin.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), with supervisory involvement from the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Ontario Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA); cross-border financial intelligence and anti-money laundering reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for established foreign electronic money institutions that are registered with FINTRAC as money service businesses and licensed under the Ontario Money Services Business Act, 2021; Low for unregulated offshore EMIs without a Canadian presence or FINTRAC registration. Legal constraints: a non-bank EMI is not an authorized Canadian deposit-taking institution and cannot offer CDIC-insured accounts or direct membership in Payments Canada. Offshore EMIs may operate as payment facilitators through a Schedule I bank sponsor, but they cannot provide core treasury clearing, commercial letters of credit, or FX forwards. Treaty and regulatory compliance requires the EMI to maintain an agent or office in Canada and to submit to provincial securities/money-services oversight. For high-volume aviation contracts, a Schedule I chartered bank is the recommended treasury vehicle.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: General partner is a non-resident private company with no permanent operational presence in Canada.

Defense evidence: Certified corporate registry extract from the general partner’s home jurisdiction, apostilled certificate of incorporation and good standing, general partner board resolution authorising account opening, certified passports and utility bills for all directors, a signed bank reference letter from the general partner’s home bank, detailed source-of-funds statement for the LP capital account, and proof of the contemplated aviation catering operating premises and contracts in Ontario.
Opaque or multi-tiered beneficial ownership structure of the limited partnership.

Root cause: Opaque or multi-tiered beneficial ownership structure of the limited partnership.

Defense evidence: Completed FINTRAC individual corporate KYC form, consolidated beneficial ownership schedule identifying every individual holding 25% or more of partnership interests, documentary evidence of each owner’s identity and tax residence, CRS and FATCA self-certification forms, partnership capital account ledger from inception, and a taxpayer-identification-number certificate for each principal owner. If no individual controller exists, a signed certificate of senior managing official under the PCMLTFA regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing of foreign direct investment into the Ontario limited partnership is conducted through a Schedule I bank’s SWIFT-assisted clearing network using a standard MT103 message. The beneficiary field must show the exact legal name of the Ontario LP and the remittance information field must include a cleared narrative: (i) sender legal name and location; (ii) the LP’s full registered name and Ontario LP file number; (iii) a capital call reference number; and (iv) an explicit purpose string such as 'Capital Subscription – Limited Partner Contribution’ or 'Non-Resident Limited Partner Loan’ as applicable. The receiving bank will validate the incoming payment against the executed capital call notice, partnership agreement, and KYC schedule of each non-resident limited partner. For transactions of CAD 10,000 or above in any single wire, the bank automatically files an Electronic Funds Transfer report (EFTR) with FINTRAC. No foreign exchange conversion is required for CAD-denominated injections; USD injections may be booked into a chargeable USD account or converted into CAD using the bank’s wholesale FX desk on a value-dated forward or spot contract.Outbound trade settlements are executed through: (1) SWIFT MT103 payments for international equipment, ingredients, and packaging imports, with structured remittance advice referencing the supplier invoice number, catering production order, and bond entry number; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) issued by a Canadian chartered bank for large capital acquisitions of kitchen freezers, ovens, plating conveyors, and international warehouse fit-out; (3) Payments Canada’s EFT/ACH rails for monthly Canadian trade disbursements, subcontractor catering payroll, and Ontario utility payments; and (4) cross-border ACH/Global ACH for recurring low-value settlements with U.S. airline vendors where the monthly aggregate does not justify wire transfer costs. USD/CAD conversion for all outbound trade payments should be centrally executed on the bank’s wholesale FX desk, never through correspondent-account overnight balances, to maintain tight bid-offer spreads and complete trade-date audit trails.Deliverable over-the-counter CAD/USD foreign exchange forwards executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or a short-form FX forward master with an OSFI-regulated Canadian Schedule I bank, based on notional amounts supported by forecast airline settlement receipts and imported supplier invoices.Cross-currency swaps for medium-term capital injection or equipment financing, converting CAD floating-rate liabilities into USD USD-LIBOR/SOFR-linked fixed streams, permitted only for documented underlying exposure with a maximum tenor aligned to the bond kitchen equipment lease or commercial loan.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/CAD where a covered deliverable forward is not administratively available for a specific settlement jurisdiction; permitted only for the amount of a documented invoice/receivable exposure and settled payable in CAD on an evidenced hedging basis.Exchange-traded currency futures and options on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) for tactical management of temporary USD receivables exposure, subject to the LP’s internal treasury policy limitsOTC European-style FX options with Canadian chartered banks, used to hedge downside on a large foreign-currency capital transaction while preserving participation in a favourable CAD rate movement, with premiums amortised into the catering project’s finance cost.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% combined (15% federal + 10% Ontario) for corporate partners; LP itself is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory 25% for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. An Ontario LP's distributions to partners are not dividends and generally do not attract Part XIII withholding; non-resident partners are instead taxed under Part I on allocated business income. If the LP structure later incorporates and pays dividends to foreign shareholders, the statutory 25% applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under ITA s. 247(4), contemporaneous documentation is required where aggregate non-arm's-length non-resident transactions (goods/services) exceed CAD $1 million, or where controlled intangible/guarantee transactions exceed CAD $1 million. For taxation years beginning on/after January 1, 2023, Canadian TP documentation has been expanded to include OECD-aligned Local File and Master File obligations; Local File is mandatory at the same CAD $1 million threshold; Master File and Country-by-Country reporting apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million (approx. CAD $1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Ontario has no economic-substance-regulation (ESR) statute of the type seen in UAE/BRC regimes, but CRA applies the OECD-guided 'economic substance and business purpose' analysis through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the sham doctrine, and partnership at-risk rules. For an airport bonded inflight catering LP, economic substance is demonstrated by real kitchen staff, physical plant at the airport/airside, procurement and safety procedures, bank accounts, and active management; a 'letterbox' LP with no operational substance may be dissolved, reassessed at partner level in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: On indictment under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 100%–200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: 50%–100% fine and up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of up to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Director/officer personal liability for trust-fund amounts; CRA may pursue civil restitution and seize personal assets; criminal conviction may include imprisonment and, at bail, travel restrictions pending trial.
Unremitted Source Deductions / GST-HST

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227.1 director/general-partner liability for 100% of unremitted amount, compounded daily interest, 10% + 2% per month penalty for repeat failure; garnishment of aviation contract receivables and seizure of the kitchen facility.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for the whole unremitted balance unless they demonstrate the required degree of care, diligence, and skill; personal bankruptcy does not discharge a director from trust-fund liability.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment to arm's-length price under ITA s. 247; 10% transfer-pricing penalty on net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the $5 million or 10% threshold; T106 penalties; interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner or foreign parent can be held personally liable if the reassessment coincides with insolvency or asset-stripping; false TP documentation can result in criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
