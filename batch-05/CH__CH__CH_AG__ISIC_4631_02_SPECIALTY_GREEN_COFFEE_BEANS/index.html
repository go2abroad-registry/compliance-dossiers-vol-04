Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) – access via www.estyv.admin.ch and the 'Suisse eTax' portal for VAT filing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Swiss business identification number (UID / Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) by registering with the Federal Statistical Office or, if establishing a branch or company, through the cantonal commercial registry. For VAT purposes, the taxpayer must file a VAT registration application with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration, in writing or via the official portal, indicating the effective date of liability. Registration is required before commencing taxable activities if the annual worldwide turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; non-resident entities may appoint a tax representative if no Swiss domicile or fixed establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Switzerland. A Swiss AG is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Available elections include VAT group taxation (for closely linked persons), the option to allocate specific corporate tax attributes to capital contributions, and certain participation relief elections for qualifying dividend income under the participation deduction regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration may be permitted under certain conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods to a foreign country, including specialty green coffee beans dispatched or transported by the supplier to a destination outside Switzerland, provided the export is evidenced by customs documents. Services directly connected to exported goods, such as freight and insurance, may also qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is established abroad or where the service strictly supports the cross-border movement of the goods. The exporter must record the export in the VAT return and maintain proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs-approved proof of export)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (sea, air, road, or rail)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence demonstrating the export proceeds received from the foreign buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate is currently enforced. Switzerland uses bilateral QR-bill and Swiss eBill interoperable standards for electronic invoicing. A valid VAT invoice must contain statutory particulars; electronic transmission is acceptable, but the taxpayer remains responsible for archival integrity and audit-ready storage.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration can retrospectively reclaim zero-rated VAT plus statutory interest if export documentation is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent. In cases of fraud or negligent misrepresentation, the limitation period extends to 10 years (ordinarily 5 years), and civil penalties may be imposed up to 50% of the evaded tax or up to CHF 500,000 for willful VAT offenses, without prejudice to separate customs penalties and criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible with no exchange-control regime and no legal distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions for residents or non-residents. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends may be repatriated freely subject to the 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which is refundable or reduced under applicable double-tax treaties, including the Swiss-EU withholding tax agreement and unilateral Swiss-EU dividend tax relief. No prior Central Bank authorization is required for capital contributions, trade settlements, shareholder loans, or distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. Exporters may freely hold and deploy foreign-currency proceeds. The only obligations are statistical and registration-type: threshold-based SNB balance-of-payments surveys, FINMA reporting for regulated entities, and Federal Customs Administration declarations where the commodity transit regime is used; none requires converting foreign exchange into CHF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse), with prudential and AML supervision by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), trade policy coordination by the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF), and balance-of-payments statistics administered by the SNB.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High as a supplementary treasury layer: Swiss FINMA-supervised fintech payment firms operating under the Art. 1b Banking Act fintech license, or AMLA-registered financial intermediaries, offer API-driven multi-currency accounts, automated FX execution, and straight-through processing. Low as the sole primary banking rail for offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Estonia, Lithuania, UK): they lack direct SIC/SNB settlement membership, rely on correspondent nesting, and are not passportable into Switzerland; they are acceptable only as ring-fenced non-CHF wallets for minor origin-country payments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Swiss federal withholding tax on dividends is 35%. For a non-resident shareholder that is not entitled to treaty relief, 35% is final and must be withheld and remitted by the AG. Under an applicable double-taxation agreement, the rate may be reduced to 0%, 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on the ownership structure and specific treaty; the most common corporate participation relief requires beneficial ownership and a direct shareholding of at least 10% of voting rights.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss transfer-pricing documentation is required where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds CHF 50 million or where the Swiss entity's cross-border intercompany transactions exceed CHF 5 million per year; OECD Country-by-Country Reporting applies when group consolidated revenue is CHF 900 million or more. A coffee distribution AG below these thresholds must still evidence the pricing in its invoices, contracts, purchase confirmations, bills of lading, and financing agreements if challenged.
