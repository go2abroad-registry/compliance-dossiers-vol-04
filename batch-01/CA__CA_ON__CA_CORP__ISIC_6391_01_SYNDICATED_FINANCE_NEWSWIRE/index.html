Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary web portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; business registration at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/tax-registrations.html (Business Registration Online).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and, if engaged in taxable supplies to Canada, a GST/HST program account (RT). Non-residents register by filing Form RC160 (Non-Resident Registration Request) with the CRA, either through Business Registration Online, by mail, or by telephone, and must provide: legal name and jurisdiction of incorporation, Canadian address or authorized representative/agent, director/officer particulars, description of commercial activities, and projected revenue from Canadian and worldwide taxable supplies. GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 148 small-supplier threshold). Voluntary registration is available to non-residents under ETA s. 240(2.1), enabling input tax credit recovery even where no Canadian sales occur. Ontario does not issue a separate indirect tax ID; the CRA-administered HST account is the sole provincial-federal instrument.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity classification election comparable to U.S. Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3; a Business Corporation (federal or Ontario-incorporated) is statutorily taxed as a separate legal person under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 2 and s. 142, subject to Part I federal tax and Ontario Part III.1 corporate tax. Available identity-affecting elections: (i) ETA s. 156 election among closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate supplies as nil-consideration supplies, eliminating cash GST/HST float; (ii) ETA s. 240(2.1) voluntary non-resident registration; (iii) ITA s. 111.4 deemed dividend election in amalgamations/wind-ups; (iv) Ontario provincial allocation elections via the Canada-Ontario Comprehensive Tax Agreement (COTA) for allocation of taxable income among provinces; (v) hybrid mismatch limitation rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 (in force for cross-border arrangements after December 31, 2023) restrict deduction arbitrage involving non-resident financial investors, but do not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold) in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; threshold applies to aggregate worldwide revenue, not just Canadian-source revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for a syndicated finance newswire (ISIC 6391) is governed by Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The primary rule is s. 23(1) ETA read with Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5: a supply of a service made to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated unless the service is (a) performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, (b) performed in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or (c) rendered to an individual physically present in Canada at the time of performance. A digital financial-market data feed delivered via API to non-resident subscribers, with no Canadian real property involvement and no Canadian-resident benefit, qualifies for the 0% rate. Additionally, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 zero-rates 'financial services' supplied to non-residents; a newswire/financial-information service may fall within the exempt financial-services definition under ETA s. 123(1) where it involves financial messaging or data integral to financial intermediation, providing a second statutory basis. The supplier must apply the 'non-resident recipient' due-diligence rule: obtain and document the non-resident address, verify the counterparty is a non-resident, demonstrate that the service is received outside Canada, and maintain a clear audit trail (contracts, invoices, payment remittance, IP/API access logs). Supplies to an Ontario-resident financial institution or any Canadian customer attract 13% HST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (filed via the Canadian Customs Automated Data Exchange System (CAED) or an authorized service provider) for any tangible goods component
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export for tangible media/hardware
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank statement evidencing collection of payment from the non-resident counterparty
  • Executed commercial contract / unconditional service agreement naming the non-resident subscriber and specifying delivery jurisdiction
  • GST/HST invoices (readable paper or electronic) applying the 0% rate with non-resident recipient details
  • API usage logs, delivery metadata, or electronic consumption records demonstrating that the financial data feed is received and consumed outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API reporting of invoices. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and harmonized under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service, which is based on the Peppol PINT (Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine) interoperability framework administered by the CRA and the Digital Governance Council. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE (or Form GST34) once annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Invoices must be retained in an acceptable electronic or paper format; there is no centralized CRA invoice clearance API. Books and records must be kept for six years (ITA s. 230; ETA s. 286).

CRA applies robust audit scrutiny to zero-rated cross-border services: where documentation is insufficient, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Ontario, and CRA assesses unremitted 13% HST plus prescribed interest under ETA s. 280. If misrepresentation was grossly negligent, ETA s. 280.1 imposes a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the additional tax payable. For false statements without gross negligence, ETA s. 285 permits a penalty equal to 20% of the net tax evaded. Willful tax evasion is a criminal offence under ETA s. 327, carrying fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to eight years. CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year window from the filing date (ETA s. 298); the limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is demonstrated, permitting retrospective clawback of prior zero-rating claims, input tax credit reversals, and interest compounding. Practical caution: for goods exports, CRA policy expects physical export generally within 60 days; for services, the 180-day FX repatriation deadline reflects prudent commercial settlement policy and documentary evidentiary expectations rather than a statutory ETA deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no general foreign exchange control regime under the Currency Act or the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations (historically expired). The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance via dividends is unrestricted, subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaty relief. No advance approval is required for foreign direct investment, capital repatriation, or dividend distributions, except for sector-specific national security review under the Investment Canada Act for acquiring control of a Canadian business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts or convert them at a licensed financial institution at market rates. No surrender requirement to the central bank exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign exchange control authority, although the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium with legal constraints. Non-resident fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) can provide ancillary cross-border disbursement and FX services, but they cannot serve as a primary operating account for a Canadian federal/provincial corporation under the Bank Act if they are not CDIC-insured. To collect CAD in Canada or process payments on behalf of Canadian clients, a non-resident EMI generally requires registration as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and appointment of an AML compliance officer, unless the activity is carved out. A dual-bank approach is recommended: a schedule I bank account for CDN clearing and an EMI for small-value or FX-optimized cross-border trades.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound wires should be sent via SWIFT to the Canadian bank account using the bank's intermediate or receiving branch. The preferred message is SWIFT MT103 with purpose code indicating capital contribution or commercial invoice, e.g., 'FDI Capital Injection' or 'AP/AR settlement'. Supporting documentation — including the certificate of incorporation, investment resolution, loan or share-purchase agreement — must be retained to survive CDD and FATCA/CRS reviews. For CAD-funded operations, a local wire (EFT, Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)) or Interac/Rails is acceptable, but for non-CAD the incoming wire should mirror the beneficial owner's name to avoid OFAC/FINTRAC reverse-AML flags. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax rate (Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. Treaty reductions may apply, such as 5% Canada-U.S. withholding for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of voting shares and 15% for portfolio investors.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's taxation year-end; for a December 31 fiscal year-end, the return must be filed no later than June 30 of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 – T106 Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents is mandatory for corporations with gross revenue, total assets, or paid-up capital exceeding C$1,000,000 that engage in cross-border related-party transactions. A contemporaneous transfer pricing Local File/Master File should be maintained where the aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions exceeds C$1,000,000; Country-by-Country Report filing is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of €750 million or more (approximately C$1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf-style sense, but the common law doctrines of substance over form, sham, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245) allow the CRA to deny tax benefits derived from artificial transactions or shell arrangements lacking genuine business purpose. For treaty relief, the non-resident beneficial owner must demonstrate real commercial substance, not merely hold a corporate shell, to be entitled to reduced withholding rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act subsection 239(1), evading or attempting to evade corporate income tax is an indictable offence punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to two years. The CRA will also reassess the tax, impose a 50% gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2), and charge statutory interest from the date the amount should have been remitted.

Director personal liability: A director or officer who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the fraudulent filing is jointly and severally liable for civil penalties and may be subject to criminal prosecution. In cases of fraudulent evasion, courts allow piercing the corporate veil to seize personal assets, impose travel bans, and order restitution or imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Penalty / T106 Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment under ITA section 247 reallocates income and deductions. The section 247(3) penalty is 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000 or 10% of the gross adjustment. T106 late-filing penalties accrue at C$100 per day per form, minimum C$1,000, maximum C$25,000, plus prescribed-rate interest on the underlying reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in setting artificial intra-group prices can be held personally liable for corporate penalties where the transactions are part of a sham or fraudulent scheme. Criminal exposure arises if the conduct constitutes tax evasion; the veil may be pierced to demand civil restitution and subject directors to travel bans pending court proceedings.
Abusive Tax Avoidance / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245) to deny the tax benefit entirely, request back-documents, and impose a 25% penalty under subsection 245(8) where the avoidance transaction was not disclosed. A common-law sham recharacterizes the arrangement and validates a reassessment with gross negligence penalties and interest. Repeated shell misrepresentation may trigger a criminal tax evasion investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit an empty-shell structure to operate with no commercial purpose can lose limited liability protection. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the shell is used to defraud the CRA, making directors personally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and trust obligations; targeted travel bans may also be ordered where fraud is established.
AML / Unregistered Payment Services

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) imposes fines of up to C$500,000 for a company and C$100,000 for an individual for failure to register, report, or implement a compliance program. Criminal prosecution under the Canadian Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime carries imprisonment for up to five years plus forfeiture of the property concerned. Administrative monetary penalties may be separately levied by FINTRAC.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct or allow payments to be used for money laundering or terrorist financing face personal criminal liability. The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent asset transfers and AML violations, allowing seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and extradition worldwide.
