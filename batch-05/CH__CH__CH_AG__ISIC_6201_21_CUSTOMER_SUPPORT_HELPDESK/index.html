Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), acting under the legal supervision of the Federal Commercial Register Secretariat (Handelsregisterfachstelle) of the Swiss Federal Office of Justice (FOJ)

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft/stock corporation), art. 621 (minimum capital CHF 100,000), art. 632 para. 2 (minimum paid-up capital), art. 640 (registration in the Commercial Register), art. 643 (registered office and purpose), art. 626 (articles of association), art. 727a (audit opt-out); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV), arts. 2-20; Cantonal Notarial Act of the Canton of Zug.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified deed of incorporation (Errichtungsakt) containing the Articles of Association (Statuten/Statuts), together with the official Commercial Register application form (Anmeldung) and the bank's confirmation of paid-up capital, submitted through the Canton of Zug electronic commercial register portal

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000 (OR art. 621). At incorporation, at least 20% of the par value of each share must be paid in cash and the aggregate paid-in amount must reach at least CHF 50,000 (OR art. 632 para. 2). The paid-up capital must be deposited on a blocked bank account in the company's name (escrow arrangement) and is released only after registration; contributions in kind require an audited formation report by a sworn Swiss auditor.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Feuerpolizei of the Canton of Zug covering escape routes, extinguishing equipment, signage, and alarm systems; structural load clearance of the leased office floor issued by the Municipal Building Inspectorate; and the employer's workplace-safety notification to SUVA complying with the Swiss Accident Insurance Act (UVG) and Ordinance on Safety at Work (VAM), including electrical installation inspections and ergonomic workstation assessments.

The Board confirms that one half of the nominal capital (CHF 50,000) has been paid on the company's blocked bank account, adopts the registered domicile at [Street], CH-6300 Zug, and appoints [Name] as Chair with individual signing power, [Name] as CEO with individual signing power, and [Name] as Money-Laundering/Data-Protection Compliance Officer. The Board waives the ordinary statutory audit pursuant to art. 727a CO and instructs the CEO to submit the commercial register filing.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial licensing institution applies to ISIC 6201 helpdesk and SaaS activities in Switzerland. For physical office operations, the authorities concerned are the Cantonal Fire Inspectorate of Zug (Feuerpolizei), the Municipal Building Inspectorate (Bauinspektorat), the Cantonal Labor Inspectorate, and SUVA for occupational health and safety.

Category: Swiss law does not provide an operating license for helpdesk/cloud software businesses; the only administrative clearances relevant to the rented office premises are the Certificate of Occupancy (Benutzungsbewilligung) issued by the municipality and the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (Brandschutzkontrolle) issued by the Feuerpolizei.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA) / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Administration fédérale des contributions (AFC); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities not established in Switzerland but exceeding the VAT threshold must apply for a Swiss Value Added Tax number (MWST-Nummer) via the FTA's electronic VAT registration portal. Entities already entered in the Swiss commercial register receive a Business Identification Number (UID) from the Central Business Name Index (Zefix). The application requires identification documents, proof of business seat or service address, description of activities, turnover estimates, and, if applicable, a Swiss fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities not established in Switzerland but exceeding the VAT threshold must apply for a Swiss Value Added Tax number (MWST-Nummer) via the FTA's electronic VAT registration portal. Entities already entered in the Swiss commercial register receive a Business Identification Number (UID) from the Central Business Name Index (Zefix). The application requires identification documents, proof of business seat or service address, description of activities, turnover estimates, and, if applicable, a Swiss fiscal representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

VAT group taxation election under Art. 52 of the Swiss VAT Act for closely linked persons/entities; election for taxation of foreign entities with Swiss permanent establishment; retrospective VAT registration election within 30 days of exceeding threshold; no US-style check-the-box pass-through election under Swiss federal tax law; hybrid mismatch elections limited to specific OECD BEPS-aligned rules for transparent entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 annual worldwide turnover (effective 1 January 2025; previously CHF 100,000, with special registration obligation for non-resident businesses supplying digital services in Switzerland).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Art. 53 of the Swiss VAT Act applies to exported goods, international transport services, and certain cross-border services where the recipient's place of business is abroad. For exported goods, the supplier must provide proof of export by Swiss customs declaration and commercial documentation. For services, zero-rating applies if the recipient is situated outside Switzerland and the service qualifies as B2B cross-border supply; low-value goods and certain e-commerce supplies are subject to specific rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss customs export declaration / Ausfuhrdeklaration)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Payment Confirmation
  • Sales Contract or Service Agreement
  • Proof of Physical Exit or Foreign Recipient Business Status

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API validation, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is permitted under Swiss eBill/QR-bill standards, and paper or PDF invoices are accepted. VAT invoices must contain prescribed mandatory fields including Swiss VAT identification number, supplier/buyer name and address, date, description, consideration, and applicable rate. Continuous transaction controls are not implemented; periodic VAT returns are filed electronically via the FTA portal.

Swiss tax law imposes fines up to CHF 10,000 for tax irregularities, and imprisonment for intentional tax evasion or fraudulent documentation. In case of incorrect zero-rating claims, the FTA may retrospectively assess the VAT due plus interest and claim refunds of input VAT incorrectly credited. The statutory limitation period for tax evasion is 10 years for deliberate concealment, while ordinary assessment periods are 5 years. Swiss courts may also impose penalties for forged customs declarations and export evidence. No specific FX repatriation deadline exists under Swiss VAT law; the 180-day benchmark reflects treasury and anti-money laundering documentation expectations but is not a statutory VAT condition.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has full current- and capital-account convertibility under Article 3 of the Swiss Federal Constitution's economic freedom principles and the Foreign Investment and Business Residence regime. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible without exchange controls or mandatory approval. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds can be transferred abroad without FX approval, subject only to withholding tax on dividends (35% federal, reduced under treaty) and to standard bank KYC/source-of-funds checks. No capital controls, no surrender requirements, and no FX licensing thresholds apply to inbound or outbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Switzerland. Swiss exporters may retain proceeds in any currency, convert them freely, or maintain multi-currency accounts with a Swiss bank. The SNB only publishes balance-of-payments statistics based on domestic bank reports; individual companies are not required to surrender export receipts or obtain approval for conversion.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) together with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and AML oversight; the Foreign Exchange Act is not a separate instrument, as Switzerland maintains a liberal free-market FX regime.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on net profit after tax (plus cantonal and municipal profit taxes; combined statutory rate typically 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax ('Verrechnungssteuer') is levied on distributions of profits and dividends from a Swiss AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return for a calendar-year-ended AG must be filed within 6 months following the end of the fiscal year (i.e., by 30 June of the following year for most taxpayers). Cantonal authorities may grant extensions of 2 to 6 months upon written request with justification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss entities of a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of CHF 80 million or more must prepare and submit the Master File. A Local File is required when the Swiss entity's net sales exceed CHF 10 million and intercompany transactions exceed CHF 200,000 per transaction category. For a Helpdesk SaaS AG, the threshold applies to all cross-border intercompany service fees, cost recharges, and IP royalties.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a formal 'Economic Substance Regulations' (ESR) regime akin to onshore tax havens, but the Federal Tax Administration enforces substance-over-form and OECD BEPS principles. A Helpdesk AG must demonstrate sufficient board governance, local employees with decision-making authority, physical premises (or appropriate home-office arrangements), and actual performance of core income-generating activities. A letterbox company without substance may lose treaty benefits, trigger legal rewrites of revenue, and face harsher penalties under tax evasion and money laundering statutes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Non-Resident Ownership

Statutory sanction: The Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax (DBG) and Substantive Tax Law impose an administrative fine equal to the evaded tax, plus a penal surcharge up to three times the evaded amount. Unpaid withholding tax is recoverable with default interest of 4.5% per annum; the Swiss tax authority can render the foreign recipient jointly liable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for deliberate or grossly negligent tax underpayments; Swiss board members face criminal imprisonment up to three years or civil restitution of withheld taxes, and under the Federal Criminal Act, can be subject to travel bans and repatriation of funds.
Shell / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The FTA may disregard the AG's tax residence, attribute the profit to the economic owner abroad, or apply the 'direct offset' rule. Penalties include denial of treaty benefits, an assessment at the higher federal effective rate plus cantonal penalties, and legal voidance of the AG's corporate existence if actual commercial activity has ceased.

Director personal liability: Board members and beneficial owners who orchestrate a sham vehicle face administrative fines of up to CHF 500,000, criminal confiscation orders, and potential extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties for tax fraud offences.
AML / Money-Laundering (Stripe / PayPal Recalibration)

Statutory sanction: The Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) applies to financial intermediaries; if the helpdesk platform begins to hold client funds, a FINMA license might be required. Sanctions reach a fine of up to CHF 500,000 for institutions and CHF 100,000 for individuals, plus withdrawal of the operating licence and asset freezing orders by the Money Laundering Reporting Office (MROS).

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally liable for failing to report suspicious activity; severe cases trigger imprisonment of up to three years. Civil liability requires restitution of laundered assets, and international cooperation measures can impose travel bans and seizure of personal bank accounts.
International Tax Evasion / Fiscal Labyrinth

Statutory sanction: Swiss courts may apply the 'substance-over-form' doctrine to recompute the taxable profit at the full arm's length value; interest on the adjustment at 4.5% per year, plus a penalty of up to 30% of the extra assessed tax. Under the OECD Multilateral Instrument, treaty benefits for abusive structures are denied retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations or endorse fraudulent transfer pricing reports face criminal proceedings for forgery, imprisonment up to five years, personal liability for civil damages to minority shareholders, and a ban from serving as board members of any Swiss corporation for up to ten years.
Failure to Withhold Dividend Tax

Statutory sanction: Under the Federal Withholding Tax Act (VStG), the unpaid withholding tax is a priority claim against the AG; the FTA can impose a penalty equal to 5% of the gross dividend for each commenced month of non-collection, plus interest and a late-payment surcharge of up to 150% of the statutory tax.

Director personal liability: The directors become personally and jointly liable for the uncollected withholding tax if they knowingly failed to register as tax-exempt or omitted to deduct; Swiss law also allows forced liquidation and a personal ban on leaving the country until the outstanding tax debt is settled.
