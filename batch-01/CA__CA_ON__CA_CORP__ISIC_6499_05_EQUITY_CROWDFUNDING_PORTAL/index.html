Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An OBCA registered office under ss 20 and 21 may be a corporate service provider's street address in Ontario so long as it is not a post office box and service of process is accepted during normal business hours; a virtual mailbox therefore technically satisfies the statutory registered-office requirement for the corporate file layer. However, for an OSC-regulated equity crowdfunding portal under OSC Rule 45-108 and a FINTRAC-supervised money services business, a hollow virtual office is NOT acceptable as the principal place of operations: the portal must demonstrate real commercial substance — a physical head office in Ontario, a designated chief compliance officer and responsible officers, physical books-and-records and data rooms, employment or consultant arrangements, active board oversight, and an operational escrow, ACH, and bank wire settlement function. OSC compliance examinations and initial licensing interviews verify this substance. Verdict: virtual registered office permitted for the registry-file layer only; the operating business layer requires a physical office and personnel.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN is the master tax identification; GST/HST, payroll, and import/export program accounts are appended to the BN. Non-residents must provide a Canadian registered agent/address and may designate a Canadian representative. GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single or consecutive four-quarter period, except for zero-rated supplies. Voluntarily registration is available before crossing the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law. Canada applies entity-level taxation based on legal incorporation status. Available statutory elections include the Income Tax Act section 85 rollover for transfers of qualifying capital property, section 216 election for non-resident rental income, and section 150 elections for associated corporations. For GST/HST, closely related corporations may elect nil consideration, and non-residents may elect to self-assess. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by mandatory OECD BEPS Action 2 rules (sections 18.2-18.4 ITA) with no elective option to reclassify entity status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding zero-rated supplies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when exported from Canada within prescribed timeframes and supported by evidence of export (Schedule VI, Part V, ETA). Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident with the service performed wholly outside Canada, provided the service is not related to Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. An equity crowdfunding portal's fees charged to non-resident issuers or investors qualify as zero-rated non-resident service exports if all platform functions are executed outside Canada on behalf of the non-resident, with no Canadian situs of taxable supply. Supporting contract and documentation should specify foreign purchaser location and offshore performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration / Export Report) or written export permit for controlled goods
  • Commercial invoice and proof of sale
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire confirmation from the foreign purchaser showing consideration received in Canadian or foreign currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government pre-approval of e-invoices is mandated. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE. Tax invoices must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, but there is no digital stamping requirement. CRA accepts electronic records and invoices for substantiation.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim retroactively for up to four years from the original reporting date, or indefinitely in case of fraud or misrepresentation. Incomplete export documentation, missing foreign currency conversion records, or failure to prove delivery to a non-resident triggers denial of the 0% rate, resulting in GST/HST reassessment at 13%, plus interest and penalties. Penalties under Excise Tax Act s.280 include a 5% negligence penalty and 50% of unreported interest, or 15% gross negligence penalty for false statements. Criminal prosecution may apply for deliberate fraud, carrying jail time and unlimited fines. CRA audit practice requires clear bank confirmations of inbound remittance within a reasonable commercial period; absence may lead to clawback of previously claimed input tax credits and foreign tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no exchange controls; both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible for residents and non-residents. Profit remittances are unrestricted, subject only to applicable non-resident withholding taxes (statutory 25% on dividends, potentially reduced by tax treaty) which are withheld and remitted by the payer. No prior governmental authorization is required for equity crowdfunding investment inflows or outbound distributions; however, all cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds exists in Canada. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings abroad or convert them domestically at market rates. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, timelines, or repatriation requirements. Canadian banking law only requires transaction-level reporting for transfers over CAD 10,000 and strict compliance with AML/KYC record keeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; primary compliance oversight for FX and AML reporting is shared with FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) and OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for prudential banking regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a primary counterpart. Offshore digital EMI institutions must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) to lawfully provide services to Canadian residents or maintain Canadian-facing operations. Even when registered, their ability to clear cheques, issue remote payment cards, or hold settlement balances is narrower than tier-1 banks. Use them primarily as auxiliary payment gateways, never as the sole treasury settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity investment remittances must be routed through a Canadian chartered bank account using SWIFT wire transfers. The remitter must include in the SWIFT MT103: full corporate legal name (matching the registered CBCA/OBCA name), bank account number and Canadian institution number, the Ontario jurisdiction identifier, and a purpose detail such as 'Equity Crowdfunding Investment — Capital Contribution' or 'Share Capital Deposit'. For amounts above CAD 10,000, the receiving bank is obliged to report to FINTRAC; therefore the entity must maintain a validated beneficial ownership file. Funds from non-resident shareholders should be tagged with cross-border receipt codes to enable automatic regulatory reporting and ensure no violation of provincial securities exemptions.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for high-speed wire payments and commercial letters of credit (L/C) where counterparties require documentary assurance. For Canadian dollar and US dollar settlements, banks use the Canadian dollar interbank CLS or large-value transfer system (LVTS) for CAD, and correspond through CHIPS/CHIPS-Fedwire for USD. Disbursements related to startup investments (e.g., acquiring shares, paying platform fees) must be executed through the same segregated escrow rail and documented with proper counterparty invoices and contracts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (15% federal general corporate income tax plus 11.5% Ontario non-manufacturing general corporate income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder of Canada, unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty. Under Canada-US DTA, the rate is reduced to 15% for portfolio shareholders and to 5% for a US resident corporation beneficial owner holding at least 10% of the voting stock.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (required under ITA s.247 to avoid a 10% transfer-pricing penalty) is mandatorily expected when aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations apply to an MNE group with consolidated annual group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more; Ontario has no separate provincial TP documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on licenses, but enforces economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245), which denies tax benefits from transactions lacking a genuine business purpose, a real economic substance, or any arm's-length commerciality. For an Ontario equity crowdfunding portal, economic substance is further compelled by securities regulation: the OSC requires a physical presence in Ontario, accountable individual approval, records of management and control in Canada, and trust segregation of investor funds. A pure shell company with no actual staff, premises, or decision-making in Canada will be treated as resident where central management and control is exercised and may be recharacterized for tax and securities purposes; directors may face personal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s.163(2); interest accrues daily at prescribed rates; CRA may audit through to the OSC/FSRA filings and impound business records

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in evasion can be charged personally under ITA s.239; civil director's liability under s.227.1 for unrepaid withholding taxes limited to the amount of the withholding plus interest and penalties; courts may impose travel bans pending investigation; criminal sentences may include significant restitution orders and imprisonment
Unregistered Crowdfunding Portal / Securities Breach

Statutory sanction: Ontario Securities Act ss.25(1), 28(1): registration prohibition; administrative penalty up to CAD 1,500,000 per contravention; maximum civil monetary penalty of CAD 5,000,000; cease-trade orders and asset freezes; order for restitution to investors; criminal fines up to CAD 5,000,000 for corporate offenders and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors of the portal operator may be jointly and severally liable for improper distributions; OSC may impose director cease-trade orders; personal liability extends to restitution of investor funds misapplied; criminal exposure includes jail for failing to register or making false statements in prospectus exemption filings
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties: up to CAD 1,000,000 for a money services business, plus compliance orders and suspension of authorization; Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; forfeiture of assets; crowdfunding portal may lose its OSC license

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or authorize an AML breach may face criminal complicity charges and imprisonment; civil liability for failure to implement a FINTRAC compliance program; travel bans may be imposed pending money-laundering investigation; personal assets can be restrained under proceeds-of-crime legislation
Transfer Pricing Abuses and Shell-Company Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment plus a 10% penalty under ITA s.247(3) on the net adjustment (threshold CAD 1,000,000); GAAR under s.245 can disallow the tax benefit and impose interest and reasonable penalties; provincial reassessment by Ontario; public naming in CRA tax evasion lists; seizure of assets to satisfy reassessed tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for corporate tax debts where transfers were made with intent to defeat creditors or the Crown; s.227.1 director's liability applies to withholding taxes, unpaid GST/HST, and other statutory liabilities; directors may be subject to civil restitution, prohibition on leaving Canada in cross-border tax-evasion investigations, and imprisonment for conspiracy to evade taxes
