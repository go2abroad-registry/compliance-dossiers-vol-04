Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, administered through the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial (Quebec): Revenu Québec, administered through the CLIENT portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/), with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) as the corporate/legal registry.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec must obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online, by telephone, fax, or mail, and must open a GST/HST account coded RT. Concurrently, the société en commandite must register with Revenu Québec for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and, as a legal entity, must file an immatriculation with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, designating a natural person resident in Quebec as representative for service of documents and providing a fixed Quebec place of business for the tea salon lounge. The partnership itself, not the partners, becomes the GST/HST and QST registrant for the taxable business activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is a legal person under the Civil Code of Québec but is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax applies and partners are taxed on their allocated shares. Available elections: (1) a partnership may elect to register for GST/HST and QST as a single registrant under Excise Tax Act s. 240(1) and the QST regime rather than having each partner register separately; (2) a non-calendar fiscal year-end election under Income Tax Act s. 96(12) for partnership years; (3) an allocation election under s. 96(5) ITA determining how partnership income/loss is distributed among partners; (4) under the Quebec Taxation Act, elections concerning the attribution of the establishment's taxable base between partners with a permanent establishment in Quebec; (5) anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4-18.6 ITA and equivalent QST provisions) restrict arrangements that would exploit partnership classifications between Canada and foreign jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital assets) measured over four consecutive calendar quarters for small-supplier status under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1) and the QST Act; however, a non-resident limited partnership carrying on a business in Canada or making commercial supplies in the course of a business at a peace-officer-identified place in Quebec is generally required to register regardless of the threshold, and the tea salon with a physical lounge will normally exceed CAD 30,000 in sales, triggering mandatory registration from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate GST/HST and QST on supplies of tangible tea goods and cultural ceramic merchandise delivered to export markets when the goods are shipped by common carrier, mail, or courier to a foreign destination, provided the supplier retains proof that the goods were delivered outside Canada within the specified documentation window. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient (e.g., wholesale procurement brokerage, tea-culture consulting, or overseas event design performed from Quebec for foreign recipients who are not registered for GST/HST and are not present in Canada) are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 7 of the Excise Tax Act and corresponding QST rules, subject to carve-outs for services in connection with Canadian real property or performed on tangible personal property located in Canada for a recipient that is a consumer. Retail on-premises lounge sales to domestic customers do not qualify for zero-rating.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000, or alternative carrier-generated proof of export for low-value parcels
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract evidencing the supply of tea goods/ceramics, the Canadian vendor (LP), the foreign buyer, and the consideration expressed in currency
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming a foreign consignee, foreign port or delivery address, and date of export, plus customs or carrier stamps showing departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/credit-card settlement record confirming receipt of proceeds from the foreign purchaser in a foreign or Canadian currency account

Quebec does not impose a continuous transaction control (CTC) electronic clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory API invoice-clearance regime comparable to Latin American jurisdictions. However, Revenu Québec mandates the use of a certified Sales Recording Module (SRM / module d'enregistrement des ventes) or a registered cash register system for operators of restaurant-type establishments, including tea salons serving beverages and prepared food for on-premises consumption, once annual gross turnover exceeds CAD 30,000; the SRM must record all sales transactions with digital integrity, and invoices must be produced in accordance with QST regulations. The CRA operates a voluntary e-invoicing pilot without any current statutory obligation for the federal GST accounts.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive CRA and Revenu Québec audit scrutiny; if the exporter fails to produce the documentation bundle, the zero rating is reversed and unreported tax is assessed with interest. Civil penalties for false statements or omissions under Excise Tax Act s. 285 and QST equivalent amount to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax, while gross negligence penalties may reach 50% of the net tax; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s. 239 can impose fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess beyond the standard four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, and retroactive clawbacks apply to the full amount of zero-rated refunds plus interest compounded daily; records must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange controls, and neither current account payments (tea inventory imports, service fees, royalties) nor capital account transactions (capital contributions, distributions, profit remittances by non-resident limited partners) require Bank of Canada authorization. Practical hurdles are limited to AML/KYC onboarding, CRS/FATCA beneficial ownership documentation, Quebec civil-law notarial requirements, and, for payments to non-resident partners, Part XIII non-resident withholding under the Income Tax Act (normally 25% unless reduced by treaty, reported on NR4 forms). Profit remittance is lawful, but the corporate treasury must classify remittances as partnership distributions rather than salary or royalty to avoid over-withholding and preserve capital account integrity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain receivables in USD, EUR, CNY, or other currencies and may operate foreign-denominated bank accounts. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation or conversion. For Canadian tax accounting, Section 261 of the Income Tax Act requires foreign currency amounts to be translated into Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate, or an acceptable alternative rate, at the time income is recognized. GST/HST zero-rating for qualifying exports does not displace the recordkeeping requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act. Foreign exchange administration in Canada is decentralized: the Department of Finance Canada oversees sanctions and trade policies; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally authorized financial institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces cross-border transaction reporting and AML/ATF obligations. No single Canadian FX-control authority exists and no central bank approval is required for ordinary capital or current account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment rail, Low as a sole treasury solution. Offshore digital EMIs, including fintech platforms with multi-currency virtual accounts, may support inbound remittance collection and low-value supplier disbursements, but they are not members of Payments Canada and cannot provide Lynx/ACSS clearing, CRA payroll tax debits, or fully compliant Quebec payroll infrastructure. Legal constraints arise under the Bank Act, which reserves deposit-taking and payments in Canada for authorized institutions; an EMI acting as a money-services business must be registered with FINTRAC and may require provincial licensing from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) in Quebec. Use EMIs only for pre-funded, low-value cross-border payments; the primary operational and capital accounts must remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank.
