Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance and ServiceOntario / Ontario Business Registry for provincial business registration. Primary digital portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/cra-business-account) and Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - an Ontario LP is not a taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; income, gains and losses flow through to partners proportionately. The LP files a federal T5013 information return only. Canadian-source partnership profits of non-resident partners are generally subject to Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) unless the partner files a Part I return under section 216 and pays tax under section 218. The general partner acts as the statutory representative for filings and assessments.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canadian federal tax law; pass-through treatment is mandatory once partnership status is established under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act. Available statutory elections include: (a) section 216 election - non-resident partners file a Canadian return to report Canadian-source partnership income on a net basis instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (b) section 218 election - non-resident partnership elects for its non-resident members to compute tax on the partnership's taxable income as if the partnership were a resident taxpayer; (c) joint GST/HST election under section 178 of the Excise Tax Act for closely-related corporations/partnerships; (d) optional quick method and other administrative GST/HST elections. Hybrid mismatch rules: Canada has enacted OECD BEPS hybrid rules (sections 18.2-18.4 ITA); partnerships must flag deduction/non-inclusion outcomes on T5013 supporting schedules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST registration is mandatory for the partnership once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or trailing four quarters; as a revenue-generating international trucking fleet, mandatory registration applies immediately; the same threshold governs a non-resident partnership registering for GST/HST).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Part V and Part VII of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (1) 'exported goods' physically removed from Canada by the taxpayer or a common carrier; (2) supplies of road transportation services that are part of an uninterrupted freight movement of goods from a location in Canada to a location outside Canada, including bonded cross-border movements under CBSA authority; (3) airport-feeder services performed on goods already in customs-bonded status are zero-rated where the immediate destination is an international airport or preclearance area on the Canada-US boundary; (4) a supply of customs bonded warehouse handling or inter-airport transfer service qualifies; (5) the ETA zero-rating certificate (Form GST11) and supporting commercial evidence (bill of lading, drayage tickets, OBL) must be retained by the carrier. Services on a purely domestic Ontario leg are HST-taxable unless the goods remain in a continuous bonded export movement.

  • Canadian Export Report (B13A or electronic G7 Canadian Export Reporting System transmission) with Exporters' Business Number and destination country
  • CBSA ACI e-manifest / pre-arrival customs data (conveyance and cargo) for the bonded movement
  • Original through Bill of Lading or Carrier Waybill (air waybill for the airport-to-airport sector to which the truck feeder connects)
  • Customs Bond (CBSA surety bond / bonded carrier licence number)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance / wire transfer confirmation from the foreign booking party or IATA CASS statement for airfreight payments

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, live tax-authority e-invoicing or digital stamping. The CRA permits compliant electronic invoices under GST/HST Memorandum 17-1, requiring legible reproduction, secure third-party storage and audit-readability for six years. The federal government is actively promoting interoperable business-to-business e-invoicing (Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Strategy and PEPPOL-based infrastructure), but issuance remains voluntary and customer consent is required. For customs, CBSA mandates electronic transmission for export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (G7 e-manifest) and the Advance Commercial Information (ACI) e-manifest regime; paper B13A remains accepted for low-volume filers, but the bonded fleet will submit G7 electronic cargo and conveyance data in real time to CBSA.

The CRA and CBSA jointly audit zero-rated export claims; the exporter must substantiate the claim within six years with evidence of physical export and payment. If a service is misclassified as zero-rated, the taxpayer is liable for the uncollected 13% HST plus retroactive interest and penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, including a 50% 'false statements' gross-negligence penalty where the error was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to culpable conduct. CBSA may also suspend or revoke the bonded-carrier licence and impose administrative monetary penalties (up to CAD 25,000 per contravention) under the Customs Act AMPS. CRA can require foreign-exchange repatriation documentation under ITA section 17(1): export- or loan-related amounts outstanding to a foreign affiliate may trigger imputed income if not repatriated within 180 days of the taxation year-end or loan advance date, reinforcing the fiscal evidence folder.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control licensing, approval, waiting period, or monetary ceilings. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating ISO 4217 currency, and Canada accepts International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations, so no restrictions apply to payments and transfers for international current transactions. Profit remittance from an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners is structurally unrestricted; however, Canadian tax law imposes the following functional constraints: (i) non-resident partners are taxable on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income and must file a Canadian non-resident income-tax return; (ii) amounts characterized as dividends, interest, rent, or royalties may attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, although true partnership income allocations are not distributions subject to Part XIII withholding; and (iii) a non-resident partner may be required to obtain a section 116 or section 215 certificate, or to authorize an agent inside Canada, when funds are repatriated from designated types of Canadian property. Banks independently apply AML/CFT controls that require identification of the beneficial owner and the source of funds for every inbound capital contribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada abolished mandatory surrender requirements and exchange controls in 1951 and has never reintroduced statutory surrender timelines, forced conversion ratios, or export-license requirements. Export invoices for GPS-sealed bonded road-feeder service may be denominated in CAD or any freely usable foreign currency, and export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad without repatriation or conversion deadline. The only binding overlay is regulatory: (i) any cross-border transportation of monetary instruments valued at CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the CBSA (Form E677), and (ii) the receiving Canadian financial institution must file a FINTRAC Large Value Transaction Report where a single deposit or series of linked deposits during a 24-hour period amounts to CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act, is the monopoly issuer of Canadian banknotes, the operator of the national large-value settlement systems (LVTS and Lynx), and monetary-policy agent for the Government of Canada; Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration, and residual exchange-control powers vest in the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52); prudential supervision of authorized deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border currency-movement reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the PCMLTFA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary cross-border payment services and LOW for primary treasury custody. Legal constraints: (i) deposit-taking in Canada is, subject to finite exceptions, limited to Schedule I / Schedule II banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and to credit unions under provincial legislation, so a non-resident electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot lawfully take CAD deposits or issue CDIC-insured accounts; (ii) an offshore EMI serving Canadian clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must maintain Canadian compliance infrastructure for client due diligence, transaction monitoring, and reporting; (iii) although no Ontario provincial MSB licence currently applies (in contrast to Quebec and British Columbia), the EMI's Canadian settlement account must be held at a chartered bank or an authorized payment-service provider able to clear through LVTS/Lynx or ACSS; and (iv) EMIs generally lack CBSA customs-bond facilitation, commercial LC issuance, and fleet asset-financing capability, rendering them unsuitable as the sole treasury rail for an ISIC 5222 customs-bonded truck-fleet operation. Recommended architecture: use a Big Six bank as the primary liquidity host and use a FINTRAC-registered EMI solely for small-value cross-border disbursements and international fuel-card settlements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary cross-border payment services and LOW for primary treasury custody. Legal constraints: (i) deposit-taking in Canada is, subject to finite exceptions, limited to Schedule I / Schedule II banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and to credit unions under provincial legislation, so a non-resident electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot lawfully take CAD deposits or issue CDIC-insured accounts; (ii) an offshore EMI serving Canadian clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must maintain Canadian compliance infrastructure for client due diligence, transaction monitoring, and reporting; (iii) although no Ontario provincial MSB licence currently applies (in contrast to Quebec and British Columbia), the EMI's Canadian settlement account must be held at a chartered bank or an authorized payment-service provider able to clear through LVTS/Lynx or ACSS; and (iv) EMIs generally lack CBSA customs-bond facilitation, commercial LC issuance, and fleet asset-financing capability, rendering them unsuitable as the sole treasury rail for an ISIC 5222 customs-bonded truck-fleet operation. Recommended architecture: use a Big Six bank as the primary liquidity host and use a FINTRAC-registered EMI solely for small-value cross-border disbursements and international fuel-card settlements.
