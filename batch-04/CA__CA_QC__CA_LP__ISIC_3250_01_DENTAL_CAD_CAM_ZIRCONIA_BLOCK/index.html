Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account is required for a Quebec limited partnership. The partnership agreement determines initial capital contributions, and the REQ declaration must state the maximum amount of capital committed by limited partners. Under C.C.Q. art. 2240, a limited partner who withdraws contributed capital or participates in management may become fully liable to partnership creditors.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal income tax, GST, and Business Number (BN) administration; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Revenu Québec (RQ) - Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial source deductions, and enterprise registration via the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ; https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / G7.

E-government filing portal

1) Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with CRA (online via My Business Account or by mail; a non-resident LP may act through an authorized Canadian tax representative). 2) Register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the 10/11-digit NEQ; a Quebec limited partnership must file a registration application in the enterprise register and, if it has no establishment in Quebec, appoint a mandatary for service of process. 3) Using the NEQ, open the GST/HST account and QST account through Revenu Québec (which administers both taxes under a single-administration agreement), together with any CNESST workplace-assessment and source-deduction accounts. 4) Obtain a CBSA importer/exporter program account for customs export reporting. 5) For the dental ceramic consumable line, obtain and maintain a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) for the distribution/export entity. 6) File the annual T5013 partnership information return and ensure each partner (resident or non-resident) reports its allocable share on its own T1/T2 return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96: no entity-level income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocable share of income, gains, losses, and credits. The LP is, however, a distinct person for GST/QST purposes and must register, charge tax on taxable supplies, and file the T5013 return. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxable under Part I of the ITA on their Canadian-source share (with treaty relief available under the Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7)) and are subject to the limited-partner at-risk loss rules (ITA ss. 96(2.1)-(2.7)). Canada applies a residence/territorial hybrid system: residents are taxed on worldwide income, non-residents only on Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

No federal or Quebec 'check-the-box' entity-classification election exists; the LP's transparency is mandatory. Available elections and mechanisms relevant to this structure include: (i) the ITA s. 97(2) rollover election where a partner contributes manufacturing equipment or technology to the LP on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) the ITA s. 216(1) election allowing a non-resident partner to compute Canadian tax on net Canadian-source income rather than suffer gross-basis Part XIII withholdings (LP business income is in any event allocated and taxed under Part I); (iii) the ETA s. 273 joint-venture election for undivided-interest co-manufacturing consortia (useful for contract milling capacity but not applicable to a true LP); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (effective 1 July 2022) contain no election but permit structural 'same treatment' and OECD-compliant carve-out alignments to prevent deduction-denial on cross-border payments to US limited partners; (v) Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7) transparency relief prevents double taxation of US limited partners receiving income through the fiscally transparent LP; and (vi) Revenu Québec simplified non-resident vendor registration and the small-supplier registration waiver (a registrant may apply to cancel QST registration when the small-supplier threshold is no longer exceeded).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (GST and QST small-supplier thresholds, harmonized). A non-resident without a Canadian establishment is tested on Canadian-source taxable supplies; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is tested on worldwide taxable supplies. Registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or when a non-resident makes taxable supplies in Canada. The export-oriented LP should nonetheless register immediately and voluntarily even below the threshold in order to recover input tax credits (ITCs) and provincial input tax refunds (ITRs) on raw zirconia, milling tools, equipment, and engineering services used to manufacture the dental blocks.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (CAD/CAM zirconia blocks and milling discs) are zero-rated under ETA s. 22 and Schedule VI, Part V, section 1, with mirror treatment for QST, provided: (a) the supplier is a GST/QST registrant selling goods by way of sale; (b) the goods are delivered by the supplier to a point outside Canada or delivered to a common carrier for direct export to the non-resident buyer; (c) export occurs as soon as reasonable after the supply and in all cases within 60 days of the supply (unless the Minister authorizes a longer period); and (d) contemporaneous records evidence the physical export. Domestic supplies of zirconia blocks to Canadian dental laboratories are standard-rated at 5% + 9.5% unless the product qualifies as a Schedule VI, Part II 'medical device' under the Food and Drugs Act analysis (finished custom prostheses are more likely to qualify than raw milling blocks). Related CAD/CAM design services exported to non-residents may be zero-rated under ETA s. 9(2) where no part of the service is performed in respect of Canadian real or tangible property.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / G7 platform
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, HS classification, unit value, currency, and foreign buyer identity
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or forwarder's cargo receipt)
  • Packing list and weight/cube manifest
  • CBSA export confirmation or carrier proof of departure, plus foreign customs import/clearance record from the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or TT credit advice evidencing payment by the non-resident buyer
  • Non-resident purchase order/contract and, for dental devices, the Health Canada MDEL / ISO 13485 certificate of conformity

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamp regime exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is voluntary and PEPPOL-based (Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework; CRA-accredited service providers in operation since 2024). Invoices must satisfy the content rules of ETA s. 169(4) and the QST equivalents: supplier name, BN and QST registration number, date, description, consideration, and tax amounts. Electronic filing is compulsory: GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE/EDI) when annual taxable supplies exceed CAD $1M; QST returns must likewise be filed through Revenu Québec's online portal; remittances above CAD $10,000 per period must be made by electronic funds transfer.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline under the ETA, ITA, or Customs Act; 180 days is recorded as the prudent audit benchmark for monitoring export receivables. The binding statutory shipping deadline is 60 days under ETA s. 22(1) (extendable by the Minister); if goods are not exported within that window, zero-rating collapses and GST/QST at 14.5% is assessed on the sale. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years (unlimited where misrepresentation is attributable to negligence or fraud) to deny zero-rating, disallow all ITCs/ITRs on export inputs, and claw back refunds already paid. Consequences: 25% gross-negligence penalty on additional net tax under ETA s. 285 (plus the 50% ITA s. 163(2) penalty on any partner-level income misstatement); prescribed interest compounded on arrears; false-statement penalties of CAD $100-$25,000; and criminal tax-evasion sanctions up to 200% of evaded tax and five years' imprisonment (ETA s. 327, ITA s. 239, and the corresponding AQST provisions). Revenu Québec may also suspend or cancel the QST registration number and CRA may hold refunds until all export documentation and returns are produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no foreign exchange controls or residual convertibility restrictions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances from a Quebec Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are freely permitted, subject to applicable withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and any relevant tax treaty. No exchange control approval or Bank of Canada permit is required for dividend or distribution remittances. The capital account is open; inbound equity contributions and intercompany loans can be repatriated without boilerplate restrictions, though supporting documentation must evidence the arms'-length nature and legal substance of the transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada to surrender export proceeds to the central bank, nor any mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency accounts with Canadian authorized financial institutions or remitted directly through correspondent banking channels. The only compliance duty is to maintain records for income tax and FINTRAC reporting thresholds, and to ensure that any large cash or wire transactions are monitored for suspicious transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability with legal constraints. Foreign fintech and digital EMI providers generally require registration or licensing under Canadian payment-services regulations if they have an operational presence or solicit Canadian clients. For a Quebec Limited Partnership issuing cross-border trade payments, a non-resident EMI may be used only as a supplementary payment rail, provided the EMI is not a Canadian bank and does not hold client funds in Canada without a valid money-services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC. Direct Vault or cash-management services for treasury liquidity are better held with a tier-1 Canadian chartered bank to avoid banking-day engulfment and to preserve access to CDBC clearing (via Payments Canada) and USD/CAD settlement.

Include the beneficial-ownership declaration filed with Corporations Canada or Quebec's Registraire des entreprises, confirming no sanctions nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via direct SWIFT MT202/MT103 corporate credit or Fedwire (USD) to the partnership's Canadian operating bank using a clearly defined purpose code such as DLVS for capital contribution or INTR for intercompany loan. The receiving bank should be provided with a capital-contribution resolution, partnership agreement, and a signed KYC pack before the first wire. A commercial invoice and a written capital-injection certificate must accompany each inbound wire to avoid misclassification as revenue for tax purposes. Beneficiary account details must match the registered LP legal name; the general partner shall execute a funds-receipt acknowledgement to complete the lane.Settlement rail for supplier payments is SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers (cost and time efficient for high-value CAD/USD transactions), and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for large or recurring zirconia-block raw-material purchases where counterparty risk mitigation is required. For routine disbursements below 50,000 CAD, use Payments Canada's Lynx wire system for same-day CAD clearing, or a direct bank-draft / ACH-equivalent EFT for domestic suppliers. For USD-denominated payments, use the bank's correspondent USD clearing rail with ISO 20022 message standards wherever the counterparty bank provides it. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with legal constraints. Foreign fintech and digital EMI providers generally require registration or licensing under Canadian payment-services regulations if they have an operational presence or solicit Canadian clients. For a Quebec Limited Partnership issuing cross-border trade payments, a non-resident EMI may be used only as a supplementary payment rail, provided the EMI is not a Canadian bank and does not hold client funds in Canada without a valid money-services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC. Direct Vault or cash-management services for treasury liquidity are better held with a tier-1 Canadian chartered bank to avoid banking-day engulfment and to preserve access to CDBC clearing (via Payments Canada) and USD/CAD settlement.
