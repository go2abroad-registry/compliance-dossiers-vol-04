Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — national tax authority; GST/HST program administrator and federal TIN issuer; e-portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/taxes/business-number.html). Ontario provincial counterpart: Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Business Registry for provincial registrations such as Employer Health Tax.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership (LP): (1) complete extra-provincial registration in Ontario through the Ontario Business Registry if the LP carries on activities in Ontario; (2) obtain a CRA federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by using the CRA Business Registration Online service; (3) activate the GST/HST program account (RT) linked to the BN when the LP is required or elects to register; (4) designate an authorized representative or agent resident in Canada for CRA service and tax administration; (5) maintain a physical or registered office address in Canada for records and service of documents. The BN is the statutory federal TIN; the RT program account number is the indirect tax registration identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: income, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and flow through to partners under subsection 96 of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST, the LP is treated as a separate person under the Excise Tax Act and is capable of being a registrant for supplies made in Canada. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income and certain allocations may be subject to Part XIII withholding. Not a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; classification is governed by the legal form of the partnership under provincial law and CRA administrative interpretation. Available elections include: (i) a fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act allowing a partnership to adopt a non-calendar-year fiscal period if specified conditions respecting partner fiscal periods are met; (ii) a GST/HST election under subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act designating a partner or agent as the partnership's representative for registrations, filings, and payments; (iii) certain inter-partnership elections for transferring property on a rollover basis under section 85 as applied through subsection 96(2). No statutory hybrid-mismatch election exists; the hybrid mismatch rules under subsections 18.5 and 18.6 apply mandatorily where circumstances prescribe.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A supplier is a 'small supplier' and not required to register for GST/HST unless the total value of taxable supplies made in Canada, in any single calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, exceeds CAD 30,000. Once the threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory. Separate simplified registration rules apply to non-resident suppliers of taxable digital services and qualifying goods, with an analogous CAD 30,000 annual revenue threshold; exceeding it compels GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property (including electric cargo bicycles) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier, or the supplier's agent, exports the goods from Canada and delivers them outside Canada, provided the supplier maintains contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under Part V where the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, not performed in respect of tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed, and not a service of 'consumer' value to the non-resident; services performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated. The manufacturer must have proper export records and, if applicable, a CERS export declaration to support the 0% rate.

  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, destination, and value
  • Packing list describing the cargo ebikes and components
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement from the overseas buyer

None. Canada does not maintain a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, digital invoice stamping, or API-based transaction clearance for GST/HST. Registrants must issue tax invoices retaining prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and keep books and records for a minimum of six years. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is compulsory for most registrants with annual revenues above the applicable threshold (CAD 1.5 million); CRA accepts GST/HST NET filings but does not require pre-clearance or fiscal authorization of individual invoices.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within the normal four-year limitation period, and without time limit where there is misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 13% HST, with interest accrued from the original filing date. Penalties for false statements or omissions in returns under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act can reach the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax evaded; wilful participation in a fraudulent export scheme may lead to criminal prosecution and potential imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks are enforceable for the full period of non-compliance where the exporter cannot produce the mandatory export and remittance documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible, floating currency. Current account transactions, including trade payments, services, interest, dividends and partnership distributions, are not subject to exchange control approval. Capital account flows are likewise unrestricted, but a non-Canadian investor must assess Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds and, where applicable, provincial registration or licensing relevant to the manufacturing activity. Profit remittance may be effected through ordinary banking channels; there is no restriction on holding, converting or transferring foreign currency, although financial institutions must satisfy FINTRAC reporting and internal CDD obligations for property or funds sent into/out of Canada of CAD 10,000 or more. Prudential liquidity requirements, rather than convertibility limits, govern CAD funding in the wholesale market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. A Canadian exporter is not required to repatriate foreign currency, convert export receivables into CAD within any period, or surrender a percentage of proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any foreign exchange authority. Export earnings may be left in foreign currency accounts, sold to a bank at the prevailing market rate, or used for offshore working capital, subject only to general tax transfer-pricing, record-keeping, FINTRAC transaction reporting, and the Government of Canada's economic sanctions and trade controls (e.g., Export and Import Permits Act, Special Economic Measures Act).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), supplemented by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision. Canada has no separate foreign exchange control administration or currency surrender authority for general commercial receipts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border payment fintechs and digital EMI-style platforms may operate in Canada as money services businesses (MSBs) rather than as chartered banks. A foreign EMI cannot legally offer deposit products or clearing services in Canada without entering through a Canadian bank subsidiary or an authorized foreign bank branch or becoming a registered MSB under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the applicable provincial regulatory regime. Viability for the electric cargo e-bike manufacturer is Medium as a secondary payment channel for vendor, payroll or niche cross-border disbursements, but Low as a substitute for Tier-1 bank clearing, liquidity pooling, FX execution, or trade finance under letters of credit. The manufacturer should maintain a domestic bank anchor account and use a licensed MSB/fintech only for discrete, low-value payment paths where correspondent banking costs are demonstrably lower.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are to be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or higher-value MT202 cover as instructed) from the non-resident limited partner's bank into the Ontario LP's CAD or USD operational account at the Tier-1 bank. The beneficiary account must be pre-validated, and the payment must contain a precise narrative such as 'LP capital contribution - Electric Cargo E-Bike Ontario Manufacturing - Invoice/Agreement Number'. Banks may require the underlying contribution agreement, bank reference, and purpose declaration before the first credit is released; any single international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more may generate FINTRAC reportable EFT data in the ordinary course. There is no exchange-control permit, but the bank's compliance team will review adherence to Canadian sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules and the bank's internal 'source of funds' expectations. For investment thresholds, counsel should confirm whether filing under the Investment Canada Act is required, particularly where the capital injection funds the acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business rather than a de novo manufacturing start-up.Trade disbursements for component purchases, contract manufacturing and freight can be executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for small and time-sensitive supplier payments; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank and advised/confirmed by a supplier-side bank for large tooling runs and containerized cargo e-bike component purchases; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for low-risk or repetitive shipments; and (iv) CAD cheques/ACH only for domestic Ontario vendors. USD-denominated disbursements should draw from the LP's USD clearing account held at the Canadian bank or its US correspondent, avoiding unnecessary double conversions. Settlement is through the bank's correspondent network; domestic CAD legs clear through Payments Canada (the Large Value Transfer System for high-value wires) while international legs settle through SWIFT/CHIPS/Fedwire as applicable.
