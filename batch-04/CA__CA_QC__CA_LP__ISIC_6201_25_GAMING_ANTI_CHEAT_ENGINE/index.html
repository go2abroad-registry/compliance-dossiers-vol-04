Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) via My Business Account / Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency); for Quebec: Revenu Québec via Tax Services Online / CLINT (revenuequebec.ca)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts) or registering online through the Business Registration Online service. The BN serves as the base in for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax and rebate accounts. For Quebec, registration must also be made with Revenu Québec using the common data platform to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and QST/employer accounts; a non-resident entity may appoint a Canadian representative and must provide certified constitutional documents, proof of the partner’s identity/address, and a completed appointment of representative (Form RC59 / Quebec Formulaire AD-1) if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act; income or loss flows through to partners according to the partnership agreement. Elections available include: (i) making a section 96(2) Income Tax Act designation that the partnership is a 'taxable Canadian partnership' with a fiscal period; (ii) electing under section 156 ETA to have one member file/report and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership; (iii) electing under Revenu Québec’s simplified QST filing and combined federal/provincial filing when conditions are met; (iv) hybrid mismatch anti-abuse rules under the ETA/Income Tax Act may be addressed through structurally elective documentation but not via a tax return election. No check-the-box election is available to characterize the LP as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.976%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold) and CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in Quebec for QST registration; threshold is computed on annual gross revenue from taxable supplies including zero-rated supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier in accordance with Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the exporter retains documentation proving export from Canada within a prescribed period and the goods are not consumed in Canada. Zero-rating also applies to services supplied to a non-resident, other than services performed in Canada or services in respect of real estate, tangible personal property, or improvements situated in Canada, provided the non-resident does not receive the benefit in Canada. For the gaming anti-cheat engine, licensing of software middleware and anti-cheat services to non-resident gaming operators will qualify for zero-rating as intangible personal property/services supplied to a non-resident if contractual performance, IP delivery, and hosting/service location documentation support the foreign supply. QST zero-rating parallels the GST/HST rules under section 40 of the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax (LQTV).

  • Canadian Export Reporting / CBSA export declaration (Form B13 or CAED via CBSA portal)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic cargo manifest
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer/swift record evidencing foreign currency payment
  • Proof of delivery outside Canada (customs stamps, foreign import documentation, transporter acknowledgment)
  • Service performance records, IP delivery receipts, and access logs for zero-rated offshore services

No general statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Canada or Quebec for B2B supplies. GST/HST and QST taxpayers must file electronic returns through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or Revenu Québec’s CLINT / Saisie de données; XML/API filings are available for high-volume filers and mandatory for specified electronic services. Quebec has an electronic-invoice standard (E-invoice) for public-sector procurement, but private-sector issuance remains non-mandatory. Taxpayers must retain source documents, transaction-level detail, and export evidence in accordance with CRA and Revenu Québec record-keeping rules; the data must reconcile to filed returns on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec actively audit export zero-rating claims. Under the Excise Tax Act, a taxpayer who knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence makes a false statement may be liable to penalties of 25% of the amount previously refunded or credited, plus arrears interest and cancellation of refunds if documentation is found deficient. False export declarations under Canada Border Services law can lead to administrative monetary penalties and seizure of goods. Quebec imposes equivalent penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act, including a penalty of up to 25% plus interest for misrepresentations, and criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion. Unsupported zero-rated claims are reclassified as taxable supplies, resulting in full retroactive GST/HST/QST clawback, reassessment within four years (or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation/fraud), and potential partner or director civil liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD). Canada has liberalised both the current account and the capital account; inward capital injections into a Quebec limited partnership (CA_LP), subscription proceeds, software licence royalties from foreign gaming studios, and profit repatriation are free from exchange-control approval, registration, or surrender. A Quebec LP may simultaneously hold CAD and USD operating sub-ledgers and may denominate contracts in either currency. The sole practical constraint concerns Canadian tax withholding architecture for non-resident limited partners: business income allocated to a non-resident partner and attributable to the LP's Quebec permanent establishment is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act and is thereafter distributable without Part XIII withholding, whereas distributions of property income not attributable to a Canadian PE (e.g., pure royalty income) attract Part XIII withholding at a statutory 25% rate, subject to reduction under the Canada-United States Tax Convention and other bilateral treaties. Pre-first-distribution confirmation should be obtained from the Canada Revenue Agency and Revenu Quebec as to the characterisation of the LP's income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished all statutory foreign-exchange surrender and conversion requirements in 1951. The LP may retain 100% of export and licensing proceeds in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency, may maintain foreign-currency clearing accounts at Schedule I banks, and may convert into CAD at any time and at any commercial bank rate without timeline or ratio restrictions. The only administrative obligations are FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction and Electronic Funds Transfer Reports triggered at the CAD 10,000 threshold, which are filed by the bank as a routine matter and do not constitute custody, surrender, or conversion mandates, plus the Bank of Canada's balance-of-payments statistical reporting captured at the bank-customer transaction level.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border financial intelligence and AML reporting. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration; the Bank of Canada administers a market-determined floating exchange-rate framework under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52).

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (transactions with non-residents) for Local File; Master File required if total transactions exceed CAD 10,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; no specific ESR regime, but substance is critical to avoid treaty shopping and GAAR challenges

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of tax payable

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participate; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 25% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts; personal bankruptcy risk and prohibition from serving as directors
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations, CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal prosecution possible

Director personal liability: Directors and officers personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly fail to comply; imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and seizure of assets
