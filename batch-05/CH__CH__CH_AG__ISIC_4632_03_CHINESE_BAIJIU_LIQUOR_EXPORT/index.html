Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Federal Tax Administration FTA), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern, Switzerland; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; electronic filing and VAT registration portal: https://gateway.estv.admin.ch. Federal value added tax is administered exclusively by the ESTV; cantonal and communal tax authorities separately administer cantonal and communal income and capital taxes on entities resident in their territory.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss Commercial Register entry that becomes liable for Swiss VAT must register with the ESTV by submitting Form 106 'VAT Registration Request' (also available through the federal EasyGov portal and the ESTV e-service). The application must include proof of identity of the entity, foreign VAT/TIN certificate, identification of the authorised signatory, evidence of Swiss economic activity, and an estimate of expected Swiss taxable turnover. The ESTV then issues a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr./No TVA) together with a UID-CH business identifier. For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) incorporated in Switzerland, the definitive UID-CH number is allocated automatically by the Commercial Register at incorporation, and VAT registration is completed separately with the ESTV using the recognised Swiss VAT registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is a distinct taxable corporation subject to federal, cantonal, and communal corporate income and capital taxes on its worldwide net profits, with relief for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. Switzerland does not grant pass-through or transparency treatment to an AG, and the entity classification is not elective.

Available election forms

Swiss tax law does not recognise US-style check-the-box elections and provides no general hybrid mismatch election. Statutory election mechanisms available under Swiss VAT law include: (i) the optional exercise of VAT liability by a small business whose turnover is below the CHF 100,000 exemption threshold (Art. 21 para. 3 Swiss VAT Act); and (ii) the election to form a VAT group among closely linked entities, allowing group-wide treatment as a single taxable person (Art. 52 Swiss VAT Ordinance). At the corporate income tax level, the participation deduction applies automatically without an election, and no elective transparent treatment exists for an AG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of taxable turnover in Switzerland excluding VAT within a calendar year, in accordance with Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act; small businesses below this threshold are exempt unless they elect to become taxable, and foreign suppliers of certain electronic, digital, or intangible B2C services are subject to registration when their Swiss turnover reaches the same statutory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Switzerland to a foreign country are zero-rated pursuant to Art. 23 Swiss VAT Act when the goods are physically transported out of Switzerland by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, the supplier acts in its own name and for its own account, and the export is formally declared to customs. For baijiu exports, the distilled spirits must leave Swiss customs territory under an approved Swiss customs export procedure. The zero-rating also extends to direct domestic supplies to export-oriented purchasers only when the statutory conditions for export are met. The taxable person must retain adequate proof of export to support the zero rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Switzerland does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, official digital stamping regime, or mandatory continuous-control API with the tax authority. Taxpayers are free to issue invoices in paper or electronic format provided the invoice satisfies the content requirements of Art. 26 Swiss VAT Act. Accepted electronic formats include Swiss QR-invoice and standard Swiss e-invoicing formats. Vendors using fiscal or POS systems must ensure data integrity and auditability, but no prior governmental authorisation or clearance of invoices is required. The ESTV performs post-event documentary audits rather than pre-clearance.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration regularly audits export zero-rating claims by inspecting the customs export declaration, transport documents, and payment evidence. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export or if the stated export is found to be fictitious, the zero-rated supply is retrospectively recharacterised as a taxable domestic supply, and the VAT is clawed back at the standard rate together with default interest. Intentional tax evasion or fraud in connection with zero-rating claims may attract criminal fines of up to four times the tax evaded, confiscation of the avoided tax, and imprisonment for serious cases; negligent failure to maintain proper export proof may result in monetary penalties and reassessment. Reputational and commercial consequences include exclusion from simplified export procedures and compulsory prepayment of VAT in future periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility on both the current and capital account of the balance of payments. The Swiss franc is freely convertible into foreign currencies; there is no exchange-control licensing regime, no central bank approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, and no statutory restriction on profit repatriation. Dividend distributions remain subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax, although treaty-based or EU/FTA relief may reduce this rate if the beneficial owner is eligible. Funds transfers are monitored solely under anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and reporting obligations rather than exchange-control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists under Swiss law to surrender export proceeds to the Swiss National Bank, to convert export receipts into Swiss francs, or to repatriate them within a prescribed timeline. Exporters may hold, invest, hedge, or redirect export proceeds freely in any currency. Banks, however, must report balance-of-payments data to the Swiss National Bank consistent with the Federal Act on the National Bank and FINMA reporting circulars, and the underlying fact-pattern must still satisfy Swiss anti-money-laundering and trade-compliance documentation standards.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury and liquidity-clearing use. Switzerland does not recognise EU/UK-style electronic-money institution passports; a foreign EMI licence does not authorise holding Swiss customer accounts or conducting payment business in Switzerland. FINMA banks under the FinTech regime may accept public deposits up to CHF 100 million under a special licence, but such entities are not equivalent to full clearing banks and cannot issue or negotiate documentary credits. Offshore digital EMIs should be treated as auxiliary payment channels only, never as the legal settlement rail for capital escrow, FX execution, or import/export trade finance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal Direct Federal Tax (approximately 7.83% on a pre-federal-tax basis); combined cantonal and communal corporate income tax rate varies by seat, approximately 11.9% to 21.1%, with illustrative Zug combined rate of 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss anticipatory/withholding tax on gross cash and in-kind dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the close of the fiscal year (for a 31 December year-end, generally by 30 June of the following year), unless the cantonal tax authority grants an extension, commonly until 30 September. The Direct Federal Tax assessment is lodged together with the cantonal return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed Swiss statutory Local File/Master File threshold; all material related-party transactions must be demonstrably arm's length when challenged. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900,000,000. Swiss audit practice expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for high-risk cross-border flows involving Chinese Baijiu procurement, licensing, financing and distribution.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, Swiss corporate residence requires statutory seat and effective management; treaty benefits and withholding tax relief require genuine economic substance such as physical office, employees, bank accounts, board meetings and business risk control. Letterbox companies with nominee directors and centralized management abroad risk denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization, withdrawal of tax rulings and retroactive assessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Administrative evasion surcharge of up to three times the evaded tax; orderly fine up to CHF 10,000; criminal fine and imprisonment of up to three years for tax fraud; default interest; retroactive assessment and loss of tax-preferred treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or order concealment face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, personal restitution obligations to the tax authority, travel bans, passport seizure, asset freezing and board disqualification.
Withholding Tax / Anticipatory Tax Violations

Statutory sanction: The distributing company is liable for the unwithheld 35% tax, plus default interest, procedural fines and an evasion surcharge of up to three times the evaded tax; unpaid anticipatory tax is a preferential claim in insolvency proceedings.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve or participate in non-compliant distributions are personally liable for unpaid withholding tax; criminal sanctions include imprisonment, confiscation, travel bans and monetary restitution.
Transfer Pricing and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax authorities recharacterize excess payments as constructive dividends subject to 35% withholding tax and additional Swiss corporate income tax; expenses are disallowed; default interest, evasion penalties and transfer pricing audit referrals apply; double economic taxation may result.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize unfair related-party transactions breach fiduciary duties under Article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations; they may face civil restitution actions, personal bankruptcy liability, criminal penalties, travel bans and director disqualification.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of China-Switzerland DTA withholding tax relief; recharacterization of Swiss tax residence; retroactive taxation of Swiss-source profits; cancellation of favourable tax rulings; forced liquidation under Swiss company law; negative registration with Swiss authorities.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors risk criminal liability for aiding tax evasion, false attestation and money laundering; courts may impose travel bans, asset seizures, personal liability for unpaid taxes and prohibition from serving as Swiss directors.
AML, Trade Sanctions and Customs Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Articles 305bis and 305ter of the Swiss Criminal Code, including fines of up to three times the laundered value and imprisonment; customs and excise penalties up to five times evaded duties; confiscation of goods and proceeds; suspension or revocation of the Swiss spirit export licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to implement adequate AML controls and for wilful participation in unlawful conduct; penalties include imprisonment, personal asset confiscation, travel bans, reputational destruction and permanent exclusion from Swiss management roles.
