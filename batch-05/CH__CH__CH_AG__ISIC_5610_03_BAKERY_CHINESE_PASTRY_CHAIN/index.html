Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch. The ESTV administers Federal Direct Tax, Swiss VAT registration and VAT returns; cantonal and communal profit/capital taxes are administered by the competent cantonal tax administration (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung / Administration fiscale cantonale) at the AG's registered seat.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) in the Swiss commercial register (Handelsregisteramt) to obtain the Swiss company number and the statutory Swiss Business Identification Number (UID / IDE / IDI) issued under the ZEFIX central name index (format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx; verified at www.uid.admin.ch). (2) Register the AG with the competent cantonal tax administration to obtain the cantonal corporate tax file number covering Federal Direct Tax and cantonal/communal taxes, and file the statutory articles of incorporation and board confirmation. (3) Apply to the ESTV for VAT registration (Mehrwertsteuer-Register) through the official Federal VAT e-portal by submitting the commercial register excerpt, founding deed, articles of association, and Swiss registered-office confirmation; the 9-digit UID then serves as the VAT identification number with the suffix MWST. (4) For foreign entities without a Swiss registered seat that nevertheless perform taxable supplies in Switzerland, a Swiss fiscal representative (ficcalischer Vertreter / representant fiscal) must be formally appointed and joined to the VAT registration, unless turnover from Swiss-source supplies remains below the statutory threshold. (5) Final step: ongoing annual filing of Federal Direct Tax returns, cantonal/communal tax returns, and VAT returns (quarterly or semi-annually), with financial statements prepared under Swiss Code of Obligations Arts. 958 ff.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to unlimited (world-wide) tax liability on its profits at Federal, cantonal and communal level (combined effective tax rate approximately 11.9%-21.0% depending on canton and profit volume); profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment and income from foreign real estate are exempt from Swiss tax under the object-based exemption principle. The AG is not a pass-through entity; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies, and capital tax (Kapitalsteuer) is levied on paid-in share capital and statutory reserves. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to unlimited (world-wide) tax liability on its profits at Federal, cantonal and communal level (combined effective tax rate approximately 11.9%-21.0% depending on canton and profit volume); profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment and income from foreign real estate are exempt from Swiss tax under the object-based exemption principle. The AG is not a pass-through entity; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies, and capital tax (Kapitalsteuer) is levied on paid-in share capital and statutory reserves. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the shares without affecting entity-level taxation, subject to substance and arm's-length requirements.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and tax regimes include: (i) VAT group election (Mehrwertsteuer-Gruppierung, Art. 11 Federal VAT Act) consolidating economically, financially and organisationally integrated entities into a single Swiss VAT taxpayer; (ii) participation deduction election (Beteiligungsabzug) for qualifying dividends and capital gains; (iii) Swiss principal company advanced tax ruling enabling the central procurement, financing and risk-bearing functions of a master-franchise group to be compensated on a cost-plus basis after approval by the ESTV and the cantonal tax administration; (iv) reduced input-taxation flat-rate method (Saldosteuersatzverfahren) for smaller taxpayers; (v) de minimis non-subjectivity election for small social/non-profit entities; (vi) shareholder-level partial taxation procedure (Teilbesteuerungsverfahren) for dividend distributions; and (vii) thin-capitalisation safe-harbour ratios accepted as an administrative election. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed through Swiss treaty tie-breaker rules, substance-based anti-avoidance principles and BEPS-aligned deduction limitations; no domestic hybrid-mismatch check-the-box mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 general threshold: compulsory Swiss VAT registration arises when worldwide annual turnover from taxable supplies, exclusive of VAT and duties, exceeds CHF 100,000 in a 12-month measurement period; a CHF 50,000 threshold applies to non-profit, social, public-law and pension-fund taxpayers; foreign entities register on Swiss-source taxable turnover exceeding CHF 100,000. For the bakery/Chinese-pastry chain, takeaway sales of bread, pastries and staple foodstuffs are taxable at the reduced rate of 2.6%, whereas in-premises café or table-service consumption is a restaurant service taxable at the standard rate of 8.1%; voluntary registration is available for Swiss real-estate owners and for businesses requiring input-VAT neutrality.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for the 0% VAT rate where ownership in goods (including central-kitchen product dispatches, fresh pastries, packaged Chinese pastry lines and bakery equipment) transfers to a foreign buyer and the goods are physically transported out of the Swiss customs territory to a destination abroad, evidenced by a release of the Swiss customs export declaration. Services qualify for the 0% rate where the recipient is a foreign master franchisee or affiliate with its business seat outside Switzerland and the place of supply of the service is determined as abroad pursuant to the recipient-location rule; this covers master franchise royalties, licence fees, central-kitchen operation and management fees, technology-transfer fees and logistics services, provided the service is not effectively used or enjoyed in Switzerland. Input VAT incurred on export-related purchases (raw ingredients, packaging, ovens, refrigeration, energy and logistics) is fully deductible against output VAT, and any excess credit is refunded either through the periodic VAT return or, for foreign unregistered entrepreneurs, through the ESTV refund procedure for foreign businesses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) released by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS / BAZG) via e-dec web or NCTS, showing the Swiss exporter, foreign consignee, goods description, weight and exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR international consignment note evidencing physical movement of the goods across the Swiss border to the foreign franchisee
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract / purchase order confirming the supply terms, title transfer and export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/MT202 or Swiss bank credit advice) evidencing repatriation and receipt of export proceeds into Switzerland within the prescribed 180-day period
  • Export schedule / zero-rating listing (Liste Z / Verzeichnis) annexed to the Swiss VAT return reconciling zero-rated sales with customs exit declarations
  • For export of services: written franchise or service agreement, evidence of the foreign recipient's registered seat (commercial-register excerpt), proof of actual service performance and evidence that the service is not used in Switzerland

Switzerland does not mandate real-time fiscal-clearance APIs, transaction-by-transaction tax-authority approval, or digital stamping. Since 30 June 2020 the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory on all payment-slip invoices drawn on Swiss payment accounts. E-invoicing is permitted on a voluntary basis using the Swiss EBill standard, SIX Corporate EBill or Peppol-BIS 3.0 format, provided authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are guaranteed and the invoice meets the mandatory content requirements of Art. 26 VAT Act. Invoices and underlying records must be retained for 10 years in a Swiss auditable archive. Export declarations are processed electronically through the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS / BAZG) e-dec web and NCTS interfaces, which is a customs-clearance function rather than a fiscal-stamping mandate.

The FOCBS and ESTV systematically cross-match customs exit declarations, transport documents, VAT return export listings and inward foreign-exchange remittance records. Missing, incomplete or inconsistent export or FX documentation triggers denial of the 0% rate, retrospective reassessment at the standard rate, input VAT clawback, statutory default interest, and recovery actions against the Swiss AG and its responsible directors. Where fraudulent zero-rating or deliberate misdeclaration is established, criminal prosecution follows under Arts. 104 ff. of the Federal VAT Act with fines of up to three times the evaded tax, possible custodial sentence pursuant to the Swiss Criminal Code in aggravated tax-fraud cases, joint and several liability of the board of directors and the Swiss fiscal representative of any foreign parent entity, and cancellation or suspension of the VAT registration. The statutory retention and reassessment period of 10 years preserves long-tail audit exposure over all export transactions in the contested accounting periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are zero legal restrictions on foreign direct investment, dividend distributions, royalty remittances, loan servicing, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds, provided the transaction is lawful under Swiss private law and bank compliance protocols are satisfied. No Swiss Federal Council ordinance or SNB directive imposes prior approval for convertibility; the only practical gatekeepers are banks' own KYC/AML controls and reporting obligations under the Money Laundering Act (GwG), the Sanctions Act (Embargo Act), and the Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI) framework. Profit remittance of a Swiss AG (Aktiengesellschaft) requires a valid annual general meeting (AGM) appropriation resolution and withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35% on dividends unless reduced under the Switzerland-China double taxation agreement (rate reduced to 10% for qualifying shareholdings, and to 5% where beneficial owner holds at least 25% share capital). Royalties and management fees are subject to no Swiss withholding tax when paid cross-border, provided they comply with arm's-length transfer pricing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio to CHF for foreign-currency receipts. Exporters may retain, invest, or convert foreign currency freely. The only compliance layer arises from tax reporting (safe-keeping of invoice and SWIFT records for transfer-pricing audits) and from bank-level monitoring of large cash flows under GwG due-diligence thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), Berne, acting as the independent central bank under the National Bank Act 2003, with banking supervision delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). Switzerland maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration body; exchange-control policy is fully liberalized within SNB monetary policy parameters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment orchestration, Low as the sole treasury backbone. FINMA-licensed fintech companies operating under the FinTech Licence (Art. 1b Banking Act) or as registered payment institutions under the Financial Institutions Act (FINIA) may provide e-money and payment initiation services, but none currently offer the full commercial banking stack (escrow account, guarantee instruments, LC issuing, and SIC settlement) required by a bakery master-franchise central kitchen. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK- or Lithuania-licensed payment firms) are generally prohibited from holding Swiss Client accounts in CHF unless they obtain a FINMA licence; Swiss banks will not clear mandates to unlicensed EMIs, and such EMIs cannot issue Swiss IBANs (CH32/CH93). Recommended architecture: use a tier-1 Swiss bank for treasury and combine with a regulated EMI (e.g., a FINMA-licensed fintech) solely for customer-facing POS and refund channels after obtaining written approval from the relationship bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from the Chinese franchisor/shareholder must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the Chinese remitting bank to the Swiss beneficiary account, using the payment purpose code 'CAPI' (Capital) or 'ETPP' (Equity Transfer) when routed through the SIC/euroSIC. The routing protocol is: (i) the Swiss bank must be pre-advised with a board resolution and a capital-contribution confirmation signed by the COO; (ii) the remittance should originate from the shareholder's own corporate account and not a personal account to avoid third-party-payment strain; (iii) the advising bank and beneficiary bank SWIFT BIC must match the account relationship; (iv) the credit must be posted by close of business T+1, and the bank will issue a SchKG attestation if requested for commercial purposes; and (v) once credited, the funds are to be booked against the company's share-capital and legal reserves accounts, requiring subsequent shareholder meeting minutes. For trade pre-payments from the Chinese franchisor for central-kitchen equipment, use purpose code 'GOODS' or the ISO 20022 'Purpose' element 'TRAD' and ensure the invoice references the underlying purchase order; the Swiss bank will request the source-of-funds declaration where the amount exceeds CHF 50,000, and EDP application for the capital contribution is irrelevant given no Swiss withholding tax applies on paid-in capital.For central-kitchen procurement from China and royalty/franchise fee disbursements to the Chinese master licensor, the cleanest clearing rail is (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-documentary payments cleared through SIC/euroSIC into CNY via a Chinese clearing bank or a correspondent bank in Hong Kong (e.g., Bank of China (Switzerland) AG as an offshore RMB clearing participant); and (ii) a commercial letter of credit (L/C) at sight (UCP 600) issued by the Swiss relationship bank in favour of the Chinese supplier, with documents to be negotiated through a nominated Chinese advising bank. For recurring royalty payments, we recommend a standing instruction with a direct debit mandate in favour of the Chinese licensor, executed on the 5th business day of each month, each payment limited to the contractually agreed royalty ceiling and the purpose code 'ROYA'. For large equipment procurement, an import documentary collection (D/P sight) provides cost-efficient settlement without bank financing exposure. The bank will require a copy of each underlying invoice, the transportation bill of lading or airway bill, and a certificate of origin for food-grade bakery equipment to satisfy Swiss import and Chinese export compliance; all payments above CHF 50,000 are subject to the bank's transaction monitoring and may trigger a request for the corresponding trade contract.Outright FX forwards (CHF/CNY, CHF/EUR, CHF/USD) with tenors up to 24 months executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a short-form FX Master Agreement with the relationship bank; documenting the underlying commercial exposure (royalties, procurement) is required for bank approval.FX swaps (swap of principal and interest cash-flows between CHF and CNY/EUR) permissible for rolling-over settlement exposures on the central-kitchen procurement cycle, provided the swap is entered for hedging, not speculative open positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CNY where counterparty provides an offshore CNY fixing (CNH) benchmark; permissible for a Swiss-resident company hedging CNY-settled trade flows; the Swiss bank will require evidence that the CNH/CNY mismatch is referenced to a recognized fixing (e.g., China Foreign Exchange Trade System 4.30pm fix) and will run a pre-netting MTM margin.Vanilla FX options (call or put on CHF against CNY/EUR/USD) as premium-based hedging instruments, permitted without prospectus requirements when sold by the bank to a professional client under FINMA's client-classification rules; prohibited strategies include binary barriers and leveraged accumulators unless the company qualifies as per se professional and signs a risk disclosure.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-dated matched funding from China, permitted only with a licensed Swiss bank counterparty and subject to standard collateral arrangements under the Swiss Master Agreement for OTC derivative transactions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ancillary payment orchestration, Low as the sole treasury backbone. FINMA-licensed fintech companies operating under the FinTech Licence (Art. 1b Banking Act) or as registered payment institutions under the Financial Institutions Act (FINIA) may provide e-money and payment initiation services, but none currently offer the full commercial banking stack (escrow account, guarantee instruments, LC issuing, and SIC settlement) required by a bakery master-franchise central kitchen. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK- or Lithuania-licensed payment firms) are generally prohibited from holding Swiss Client accounts in CHF unless they obtain a FINMA licence; Swiss banks will not clear mandates to unlicensed EMIs, and such EMIs cannot issue Swiss IBANs (CH32/CH93). Recommended architecture: use a tier-1 Swiss bank for treasury and combine with a regulated EMI (e.g., a FINMA-licensed fintech) solely for customer-facing POS and refund channels after obtaining written approval from the relationship bank.
