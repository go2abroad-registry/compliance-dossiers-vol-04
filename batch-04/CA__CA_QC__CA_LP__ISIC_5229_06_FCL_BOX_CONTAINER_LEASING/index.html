Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Civil Code of Quebec, CQLR c CCQ-1991, Book Five, Title Twenty-Nine, including articles 2186-2235 governing partnership contracts and limited partnerships; Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-44.1, including registration and updating obligations. A Quebec limited partnership is not incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Capital contributions, partner commitments, allocation percentages, distributions, and any contribution funding account are governed by the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is required; funds should be deposited into the partnership bank account or another account specified in the agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening a yard or depot, obtain applicable municipal zoning and occupancy approvals, confirm fire-access and emergency-response requirements with the local fire authority, and maintain compliant storage, traffic, lifting, stacking, and inspection procedures. Structural or foundation approvals may be required for offices, racks, cranes, or other installations. Register with CNESST where required, implement occupational-health and safety controls, train workers, and document inspections. Container leasing alone does not require a separate Quebec civil-defense certificate.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Quebec limited partnership. Capital contributions, partner commitments, allocation percentages, distributions, and any contribution funding account are governed by the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is required; funds should be deposited into the partnership bank account or another account specified in the agreement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax administration via the My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial tax compliance via the CLICK portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-entity Limited Partnership must obtain a federal Business Number by filing Form RC1 (Partnership Information Return) with CRA and simultaneously register for a GST/HST account using Form RC1A (Request for Registration). It must then register with Revenu Québec for QST and any Quebec-specific payroll or provincial levy accounts (Vendor Registration), using the Revenu Québec business registration forms. Non-resident partners are required to file Canadian partnership information returns and may need a CRA program account even if no Canadian permanent establishment exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-entity Limited Partnership must obtain a federal Business Number by filing Form RC1 (Partnership Information Return) with CRA and simultaneously register for a GST/HST account using Form RC1A (Request for Registration). It must then register with Revenu Québec for QST and any Quebec-specific payroll or provincial levy accounts (Vendor Registration), using the Revenu Québec business registration forms. Non-resident partners are required to file Canadian partnership information returns and may need a CRA program account even if no Canadian permanent establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a US-style check-the-box election; the LP is fiscally transparent by statutory default. A Canadian LP may elect under section 34 of the Income Tax Act to use non-calendar fiscal periods for partnership reporting; non-resident partners may file income tax returns under section 216 of the Income Tax Act and make related elections to be taxed on the partnership allocations instead of relying on treaty protection. For QST, a registrant may elect to use the simplified accounting method or the accrual method for input tax credits and QST refunds, and a qualifying partnership may elect to have a single partner be the QST registrant for the partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; however, a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada is generally required to register regardless of turnover. For QST, the analogous small-supplier threshold with respect to Quebec taxable supplies is also recognized at CAD 30,000, with mandatory registration for non-residents making taxable supplies in Quebec. Container-leasing revenue sourced to Canada or Quebec must be included in that turnover calculation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed where the intermodal ISO container leasing supply is an exported supply under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST zero-rated supply provisions of the Quebec Taxation Act. Generally, supplies of containers used exclusively to transport goods to destinations outside Canada, or supplied to non-residents who do not use them in Canada other than for repair or transportation of goods outward bound, are zero-rated. The supplier must hold documentary evidence that the container leaves Canada and that the leasing vender is a non-resident with no Canadian use beyond outward transport.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13 export declaration or Automated Customs Declaration equivalent)
  • Original Bill of Lading or equivalent sea waybill evidencing foreign destination and physical export
  • Container interchange / terminal yard receipt showing container departure from Canadian port or rail facility
  • Signed lease agreement and commercial invoice identifying the container, lessee, term, and per-diem charges
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing settlement in a foreign currency from the non-resident lessee
  • Vessel outturn report or port authority record confirming container release to a foreign consignee

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction-control stamping regime. CRA and Revenu Québec require registered entities to issue invoices containing the legal name, GST/HST and QST registration numbers, invoice date and number, description of supplies, tax rate, and tax payable. Electronic input and output tax records must be retained in readable digital format for six years and made available to auditors on request. Quebec imposes mandatory electronic filing and electronic payment for large registrants (returns and remittances transmitted via Revenu Québec CLICK), but no mandatory e-invoicing digital clearance API exists.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years from the notice of assessment (six years for gross negligence or fraud) and will disallow zero-rating and input tax credits if documentary proof is inadequate. Retrospective clawback is accompanied by interest at the prescribed statutory rate plus late-payment penalties. Misstatement, false statements, or willful evasion under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act may attract penalties of up to CAD 25,000 for a summary conviction under the ETA, gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the net tax allegedly avoided, and additional fines or imprisonment for criminal evasion. For exported container leasing, failure to repatriate consideration within 180 days can itself trigger CRA suspicion of a non-commercial or artificial arrangement and require the taxpayer to rebut the export claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII, Sections 2, 3 and 4 of the IMF Articles of Agreement and maintains no exchange-control legislation restricting current or capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible both onshore (deliverable) and offshore; no prior treasury approval or central bank authorization is required for capital injections, operational outflows, profit remittances, or distributions to non-resident partners. The operative friction is tax compliance, not convertibility: Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes 25% statutory withholding on income allocated or credited to a non-resident partner, reducible by treaty (e.g., 15% under the Canada-U.S. Convention, 15% under most income tax treaties; container-lease rentals may qualify for reduced treaty rates only if the recipient is the beneficial owner without treaty-shopping). The Quebec Taxation Act mirrors this via form RL-15, and a non-resident partner may require an NR6 waiver or Part XIII undertaking to avoid over-withholding at the source. Return-of-capital distributions are not subject to Part XIII; capital-account repatriation faces no exchange-control hurdle once the partner's contributed capital basis is substantiated on T5013 Schedule K and RL-15. Additional administrative frictions: sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act; FATCA and CRS registration and reporting obligations of the LP with respect to non-resident partners; and the corporate general partner should document that no artificial debt-equity substitution is present.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed the Foreign Exchange Control Act effective 1 October 1951 and has never reinstated export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion timelines, or repatriation quotas. Container lease and sublease receipts earned in USD, EUR, or any other currency may be retained abroad or converted at any time, in any amount, with no Bank of Canada approval. Residual compliance obligations: (1) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies to SWIFT/telegraphic wires of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada; (2) FINTRAC large cash transaction reports apply to any single cash payment of CAD 10,000 or more; and (3) Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System requires periodic reporting of receipts from non-resident lessees where the aggregate exceeds CAD 100,000 per reporting period, using the partnership's Business Number (BN) plus the applicable enterprise code. No conversion ratio or statutory timeline is prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); statutory exchange-control authority rests with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), with monetary instrument surveillance administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and balance-of-payments data collection by Statistics Canada under the Statistics Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary multi-currency overlay. Legal constraints: foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) licensed under the UK Financial Conduct Authority or an EU EMI directive are not banks within the meaning of the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot carry on deposit business in Canada. Any foreign money services business (MSB) that shows signs of carrying on business in Canada must register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers may require registration or a deemed-exemption analysis under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Quebec) and the Quebec Deposits Act, which prohibits acceptance of deposits from the Quebec public without a deposit-institution license. A Quebec limited partnership may therefore use a foreign EMI only where the EMI maintains the underlying pooled funds at a Canadian Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch, and the LP's own funds never constitute a Quebec deposit. Container-leasing payment flows from foreign lessees can be collected through an EMI omnibus account and swept daily to the National Bank operational account; however, the EMI must disclose beneficial ownership to the Canadian bank and accept pass-through KYC under FINTRAC guidance. Overall: legally permissible but compliance-cost-heavy; recommend a direct account at National Bank or RBC Global Banking with an embedded FX overlay rather than a standalone EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: inbound MT103 from the contributor's bank to National Bank of Canada, Montreal (institution number 006, branch transit as assigned), with a USD leg cleared through National Bank's U.S. correspondent bank, preferably Bank of America, New York, using the LP's USD account at National Bank. Field 50K (ordering customer) must show the partner's bank name and country; field 59 (beneficiary) must show the exact legal name 'CA_LP' as registered with the REQ to avoid a name-mismatch hold. Payment purpose: use the bank's designated BOP/EFT purpose code for equity injection (commonly 'Equity Capital', purpose code '3000' under the bank's payment-purpose menu) and reference 'ISIC 5229 container fleet capital escrow' in field 72; do not use vague descriptors such as 'consulting' or 'general business'. Verification: complete the bank's foreign-source-of-funds questionnaire; submit the capital-call notice and subscription agreement; if funds are routed by a third-party payer, file the third-party declaration of interest to avoid FINTRAC suspicion; maintain a monthly sub-account reconciliation ledger for the escrow account. For large or time-critical injections, schedule same-day settlement via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD, or via correspondent wire for USD, and notify the bank's treasury desk at least 48 hours in advance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary multi-currency overlay. Legal constraints: foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) licensed under the UK Financial Conduct Authority or an EU EMI directive are not banks within the meaning of the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot carry on deposit business in Canada. Any foreign money services business (MSB) that shows signs of carrying on business in Canada must register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers may require registration or a deemed-exemption analysis under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Quebec) and the Quebec Deposits Act, which prohibits acceptance of deposits from the Quebec public without a deposit-institution license. A Quebec limited partnership may therefore use a foreign EMI only where the EMI maintains the underlying pooled funds at a Canadian Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch, and the LP's own funds never constitute a Quebec deposit. Container-leasing payment flows from foreign lessees can be collected through an EMI omnibus account and swept daily to the National Bank operational account; however, the EMI must disclose beneficial ownership to the Canadian bank and accept pass-through KYC under FINTRAC guidance. Overall: legally permissible but compliance-cost-heavy; recommend a direct account at National Bank or RBC Global Banking with an embedded FX overlay rather than a standalone EMI.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15.0% after the general rate reduction plus Quebec provincial 11.5%, combined statutory headline rate of 26.5% for active business income in Quebec; federal rate before reduction is 38.0% less provincial abatement of 10.0 percentage points and general rate reduction of 13.0 percentage points.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident shareholder; Quebec does not levy an additional provincial dividend withholding tax as it is collected federally.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada's transfer pricing rules under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act require contemporaneous documentation (Local File / Master File equivalent) for transactions between related persons where the aggregate value of the transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; the CRA Form T106 (Information Return for Transactions with Non-Resident Related Persons) is required for transactions with a total value exceeding CAD 1,000,000. A Master File is required for a Canadian taxpayer with total gross revenue over CAD 50,000,000 (or where the multinational group's revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and the filing threshold is met). Quebec applies the same federal documentation standards and does not impose a separate Quebec-specific threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act as the economic substance doctrine; Quebec imposes its own anti-avoidance rule under Section 1063 of the Taxation Act. For limited partnerships, the CRA requires that the partnership be carried on for profit, with real business purpose, and will disregard a general partnership arrangement where the sole purpose is to shift income to a non-resident or to claim treaty benefits. Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as native to OECD base-erosion regimes do not apply verbatim in Canada, but the Canada Revenue Agency may recharacterize transactions lacking commercial substance and apply the 'anti-avoidance' rule. A Quebec LP engaged in shipping container leasing must demonstrate staff, assets, risk-taking, and decision-making in Quebec/Canada to be respected as a tax pass-through entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. The court may also order full restitution of evaded taxes plus interest and penalties under Section 163.1 (gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax) and Section 162(2) late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of a Quebec LP may be jointly and severally liable under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties where they are directors of a corporate partner; civil restitution orders for the tax liability; travel bans under the Canada Revenue Agency's insolvency and serious non-compliance program; imprisonment terms apply where willful tax evasion is established, with no limitation period for fraud.
Failure to file / Failure to comply

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months; for a failure to file within 3 years, an additional 10% penalty; failure to file T5013: CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; CAD 100 per partner per day up to CAD 2,500; Quebec penalties equivalent to 10% of unpaid tax plus 1% monthly, and costs.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be liable for the corporation's failure to deduct, withhold, remit, or pay amounts; no penalty waiver where failure was attributable to neglect, and civil liability for the unremitted amounts plus interest.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Section 247)

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income to arm's-length amounts; penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment (CAD 30,000 minimum? no — the penalty is 10% of the amount by which the taxable income is adjusted, reduced for the part attributable to offsetting adjustments); interest accrues on the adjustment; gross negligence penalty 50% of the understated tax where the taxpayer knowingly participates in the non-arm's-length pricing.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for corporate penalties where they caused the corporation to participate in the non-arm's-length transaction and signed false or misleading declarations, and in egregious cases they face civil assessment under the same rules as tax evasion, including director liability under Section 227.1.
GAAR / Anti-Avoidance Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Under Section 245, the CRA may deny the deduction, inclusion, or tax benefit; re-assess the taxpayer retroactively; apply interest and penalties; a 50% gross negligence penalty in addition to reassessed tax; transaction may be recharacterized and the non-resident partner may become subject to Part XIV branch tax on deemed Canadian income.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who signed a false statement in the T2 can be criminally prosecuted; civil liability for the corporate tax deficiency plus interest where they signed as directors and knew of the arrangement's false shelter; the court may prohibit travel and impose a fine equal to double the benefit obtained.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 1,000,000 for administrative monetary penalties per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; for serious criminal money-laundering indictable offences, imprisonment up to 14 years; Quebec's civil forfeiture regime may freeze and confiscate the container assets as proceeds of unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a general partner who knowingly assist in money laundering face criminal liability with imprisonment up to 14 years; civil suits under Quebec's civil code for restitution; travel bans and asset freezes under FINTRAC reporting violations or police investigative orders.
