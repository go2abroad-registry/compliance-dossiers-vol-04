Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for GST/HST and federal income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec corporate income tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html) and Revenu Québec Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/business/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Quebec must obtain a Federal Business Number (BN) through CRA, a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises, and register for GST/HST and QST accounts. Non-resident corporations may apply by mail or online using Form RC460 (CRA) and Revenu Québec’s registration request, often requiring a Canadian representative or agent. Registration must be completed before carrying on taxable activity; potential retroactive registration and liability arise from the date of first supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not permit US-style check-the-box elections. Available elective/tax-planning mechanisms include amalgamation elections under s.87 and winding-up elections under s.88 of the Income Tax Act, the s.256 associated-corporation grouping rules, and GST/HST elections under s.156 (closely related corporations) and s.157 (qualifying branch). QST rules provide analogous elections under the Quebec Taxation Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital property) for GST/HST; CAD 30,000 for QST. Small-supplier threshold may be exceeded by a single taxable supply. Non-resident suppliers of digital services or goods supplied through an e-commerce platform may be subject to mandatory simplified registration without the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier, and for certain services supplied to non-residents. To qualify, the supplier must retain evidence that the goods were physically exported within a reasonable time, directly or through a commercial carrier, and that services are due to a non-resident. Absent adequate export evidence, CRA will treat the supply as taxable and may reassess.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Automated Export Declaration / GCA)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of lading or air waybill (third-party carrier evidence)
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of foreign proceeds

No mandatory real-time digital clearance or fiscal stamping currently applies in Canada. GST/HST returns must be electronically filed (GST/HST NETFILE) when using certain reporting formats. Revenu Québec requires certain data exchanges electronically but does not mandate Peppol or a continuous transaction control (CTC) system. Voluntary electronic invoicing standards (e.g., Peppol) are supported on a pilot basis.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating if export documentation is incomplete. Penalties under s.280(1) of the Excise Tax Act for false statements or omissions can reach 25% of the tax quantum; interest accrues from the reassessment date. Gross negligence may trigger additional penalties of 50% of any tax collected or credit claimed. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims can result in fines and imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks include reassessments of previously claimed input tax credits and QST refunds plus accumulated interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with an open capital account. Both current and capital account transactions are free from exchange controls; profit repatriation of distributable after-tax reserves is permitted without central bank approval, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (generally 25% domestic rate, reduced under tax treaties). No restriction exists on non-resident ownership or remittance of capital, dividends, interest, or trade proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation applies to export proceeds. Canadian resident exporters may retain foreign currency in offshore accounts, unless such accounts trigger Canadian tax reporting requirements (Form T1135). All corporate reporting is executed in Canadian dollars, and the Director of Taxpayer Services at the Canada Revenue Agency uses average/spot exchange rates for income computation; no timeline exists for repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting with the Department of Finance Canada (and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for AML/ATF supervision)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Stripe Treasury) may operate as Canadian money services businesses (MSBs) only after registering with FINTRAC and complying with Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (fall 2024) supervision as payment service providers. They are not chartered banks and do not offer bank-level deposit insurance or substantive escrow/ESV services; their use is limited to payment collection and sub-account treasury waterfalls. Legally they cannot hold capital escrow in a way that satisfies creditor-priority requirements for a Quebec corporation without an underlying Canadian chartered bank partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with the detailed purpose code DEF9 (equity capital contribution) or TRF density dividend (if a return of capital). The routing instruction must identify the receiving Canadian bank's SWIFT/BIC branch (e.g., ROYCCAT2), and the customer must pre-notify the commercial banking officer with a board resolution declaring the contribution. To maintain perfect legality, the bank may require a statutory declaration under the Taxation Act (Quebec) specifying the funding source and confirming the investor does not hold any security in exchange. For investments above C$1,000,000, the investor must file under the Investment Canada Act post-closing notification; no pre-merger clearance for an online retail business.For supplier and vendor payments, the primary rails are standard corporate SWIFT MT103 for low-value/LED-to-medium accounts payable, and commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) for purchases exceeding USD 250,000 equivalent. The Canadian bank's trade finance desk will issue the L/C confirmed by a foreign correspondent bank.
