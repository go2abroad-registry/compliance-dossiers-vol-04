Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; GST/HST and corporation tax filings via CRA My Business Account. Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax measures.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated entity obtains a Canada Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through BRO. If carrying on business in Canada, it must register for GST/HST under Section 240 of the Excise Tax Act and for corporate income tax if earning Canadian-source income. Required documents include foreign incorporation certificate, evidence of Canadian permanent establishment or place of business, and designation of a tax representative if non-resident. Ontario extra-provincial registration is done through the Ontario Business Registry.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign-incorporated entity obtains a Canada Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or through BRO. If carrying on business in Canada, it must register for GST/HST under Section 240 of the Excise Tax Act and for corporate income tax if earning Canadian-source income. Required documents include foreign incorporation certificate, evidence of Canadian permanent establishment or place of business, and designation of a tax representative if non-resident. Ontario extra-provincial registration is done through the Ontario Business Registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. A Canadian Business Corporation is automatically a separate taxable taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Specific anti-hybrid elections apply under ITA ss. 94.1 and 94.2 (foreign investment entities) and ITA s. 18.4 (hybrid mismatch arrangements). Non-resident digital suppliers may elect the simplified GST/HST registration regime under ETA ss. 239.3–239.6.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in cumulative worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident digital suppliers, CAD 30,000 over a 12-month period (small supplier threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supplies of services and intangible personal property (including software, digital subscriptions, and educational content) made to a non-resident are zero-rated when the recipient is outside Canada and the supply is not for use or consumption in Canada. The supplier must establish that the supply is not for use 'in furtherance of a business carried on in Canada' as defined in ETA s. 123(1).

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA export report for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill showing consignee outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and sales contract describing the zero-rated supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation proving receipt of funds from non-resident

No statutory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance regime exists. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information and retain electronic books and records for CRA audit. For physical exports, CBSA electronic export reporting (e-manifest/B13A) is mandatory. No digital stamping or API clearance is required for digital services.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims within the normal 4-year reassessment period, with no time limit for fraud or wilful misrepresentation. Penalties for false or omitted statements under ETA s. 280(1) include up to 25% of net tax amounts, interest charges, repeated-failure penalties, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible, free-floating regime for both current-account items (trade, services, dividends, royalties, interest) and capital-account items (equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, treasury placements). No prior approval, licensing, or quantitative limits apply to conversion or repatriation. Non-resident ownership up to 100% is permitted; the Investment Canada Act imposes only notification/review above current enterprise-value thresholds (CAD 1.334B for WTO-non-state investors; CAD 5.339B for trade-agreement investors in 2025) and national-security screening for sensitive industries - not applicable to an early-stage EdTech. Profit remittance is restricted only by Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate; treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%-15% on dividends under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention). No minimum holding period or conversion condition applies to foreign-currency receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - sole issuer of Canadian dollar legal tender under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52) and monetary policy authority under the Bank of Canada Act. Canada operates no foreign-exchange control authority; the Minister of Finance administers the Exchange Fund Account, with the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent, for official FX intervention only. Treasury-flow supervision for AML/CTF is vested in FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Ontario 11.5% general rate; reduced to 12.2% for Canadian-controlled private corporations on first CAD 500,000 active business income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (subject to reduction under applicable tax treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (e.g., if year-end is December 31, filing due June 30 of the following year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services, interest, royalties). Master File also required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and filed within 3 months of a CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. For treaty benefits, the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause in the Canada-US treaty requires substantive business operations. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial substance, and the OECD's BEPS actions (e.g., Action 6) are incorporated into domestic rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil reassessment with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit (e.g., payroll deductions) or if they knowingly participated in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 10% of the adjustment, and potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they directed or acquiesced to the sham arrangement; may face civil penalties and criminal prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if gross negligence) plus interest; reassessment of taxable income; potential double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant transfer pricing policies; personal liability for unpaid corporate tax if they failed to exercise due diligence.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties for individuals up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they consented to or acquiesced in the violation; may face travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
