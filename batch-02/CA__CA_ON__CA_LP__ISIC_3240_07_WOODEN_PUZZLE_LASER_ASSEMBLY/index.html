Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and corporate tax administration portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (or its general partner on its behalf) must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or online through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. The BN is the statutory TIN for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate/partnership income tax accounts. For a limited partnership that is a 'registrant' carrying on business in Canada, the partnership is required to register for a GST/HST account (RT) if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Canada may still be required to register under the 'non-resident registrant' rules if they supply goods or services in Canada in the course of a business, subject to the normal small supplier threshold and any mandatory registration rules for non-residents selling tangible personal property through fulfillment or marketplace platforms. The application must identify the partnership's legal name, general partner details, Canadian resident agent, and the date business activity commenced. Upon approval, the CRA issues a 15-character BN (9-digit base plus 2-letter program account code and 4-digit reference number), with separate program accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and income tax (RC).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Limited partnerships are not separate taxpayers for Canadian income tax purposes; income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners who report their respective shares. No 'check-the-box' entity classification election exists under Canadian federal tax law. However, partnership rules allow for elective fiscal periods (CRA administrative election to use non-calendar year-end under subsection 249.1(6) for Canadian resident partners) and, for cross-border hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada applies the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act (sections 18.4 to 18.6, 20.16 to 20.24) which may disallow deductions or require inclusion where a partnership or limited partner is treated differently in a foreign jurisdiction. A foreign limited partnership that is treated as a corporation in its home jurisdiction may create hybrid entity risk; an election may be available under the Canada-U.S. treaty for certain U.S. LLCs (deemed corporation or disregarded as applicable) but Canadian domestic law does not provide a general elective pass-through classification mechanism for foreign entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers supplying digital services or goods through a Canadian fulfillment warehouse may have mandatory registration requirements regardless of the small supplier threshold, and marketplace rules may deem the marketplace operator to be the supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registrant may zero-rate exported tangible personal property under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods 'by or on behalf of the supplier' and the goods are exported 'as soon after the goods are delivered to the carrier, the consignee, or the supplier's agent as is reasonable having regard to the circumstances.' Supplies of services performed on exported goods are zero-rated under section 11 of Part V of Schedule VI. Zero-rating requires documentary evidence that establishes the goods were exported and the supplier made the supply in Canada. A non-resident limited partnership that acquires goods in Canada for export must ensure title and risk of loss pass to the purchaser outside Canada and maintain contemporaneous documentation. For offshore services (e.g., design or assembly planning) supplied to a non-resident person, zero-rating generally applies under Part V, Schedule VI where the service is supplied to a non-resident client and is not related to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property in Canada at the time the service is performed.

  • Commercial invoice or bill of sale evidencing the sale of goods and terms of sale (Incoterms)

Canada does not have a mandatory continuous transaction controls (CTC) or real-time electronic invoicing clearance system at the federal level. Registered businesses must issue paper or electronic invoices containing prescribed information (Form GST/HST 34) including supplier name, BN, transaction date, description, and tax amount. E-invoicing via EDI or PEPPOL is voluntary and not required for fiscal clearance. Quebec (not Ontario) has a mandatory electronic invoicing and government clearance regime for certain B2B transactions (Revenu Québec's mandatory e-invoicing for large enterprises phased in starting 2024), but Ontario businesses are not subject to real-time fiscal stamping. If the entity has a Quebec nexus, separate provincial requirements may apply, but for Ontario-based supplies no real-time clearance or digital stamping mandate exists.

The CRA can reassess a registrant within the normal four-year reassessment period (and later in cases of misrepresentation or fraud) to deny zero-rating and collect unremitted GST/HST, plus interest and penalties. A person who knowingly makes a false statement or omission in a return, or who makes or participates in making a false or deceptive entry in records, with intent to evade payment or remittance of tax, is guilty of an offence under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, punishable on summary conviction by a fine of up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to two years, or on indictment by a fine of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil penalties include a 'false statements or omissions' penalty under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount of tax that was or would have been remitted if the false statement or omission had not been made, and a repeated non-compliance penalty under subsection 280.1(1). Additionally, CRA can impose a 10% penalty and interest on large corporations for repeated failure to report income amounts, though for GST/HST-specific export claims, the primary exposure is denial of the zero-rating, reassessment of tax, interest (current prescribed rate, compounded daily), and gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax under the Income Tax Act if the partnership's allocation to a corporate general partner is involved.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime without exchange controls. The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act contain no legal mechanism for restricting conversion of CAD or repatriation of funds. Both current-account flows (trade settlement, service fees) and capital-account flows (capital contributions, intercompany loans, partnership distributions) are fully convertible and require no prior central-bank approval, registration, or quota. Profit remittance from an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners is not blocked at the regulatory level, but tax consequences apply: a non-resident limited partner is generally taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on its share of Canadian-source partnership business income, subject to treaty relief, and Canadian-source passive remittances such as interest may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax. No currency restriction, exchange-control permit, or profit-repatriation license exists under Canadian federal law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. The Ontario LP may freely retain USD or any foreign currency in Canadian or overseas bank accounts. The only compliance obligation relevant to export receipts is FINTRAC's mandatory reporting of large cross-border electronic fund transfers (CAD 10,000 or more) within 24 hours; this is a reporting duty, not an exchange-control restriction.
