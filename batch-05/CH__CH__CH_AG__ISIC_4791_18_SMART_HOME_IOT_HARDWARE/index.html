Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), operating through the Swiss Federal Commercial Register platform (Zefix portal).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (CO, SR 220), Articles 626 to 635 (incorporation procedure), Article 640 (minimum nominal capital and paid-up requirements), Articles 716 to 716b (board governance and non-transferable duties), in conjunction with the Swiss Ordinance on the Commercial Register (HRegV, SR 221.411); for physical premises, applicable cantonal building and trade regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Incorporation Deed and Articles of Association (Statuten) accompanied by the electronic Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) filed with the Commercial Register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 for an Aktiengesellschaft (CO Art. 621); at least CHF 50,000 of the nominal value must be paid up in cash before registration (CO Art. 632 para. 2). The paid-up capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss-regulated bank; the bank's escrow attestation is filed with the Commercial Register and funds remain blocked until registration is completed and entry is effected.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-prevention acceptance certificate (Feuerpolizeiliche Abnahme) from the Municipal Fire Prevention Service of Zug for the retail premises; structural load and occupancy certification in accordance with SIA 260/261 building standards prescribed under the Cantonal Building Act; occupational safety and accident-prevention clearance pursuant to Swiss Labour Law (ArG/ArGV 3) and Suva's workplace inspection requirements before employees commence any storage, loading, or customer-facing retail activities.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 for an Aktiengesellschaft (CO Art. 621); at least CHF 50,000 of the nominal value must be paid up in cash before registration (CO Art. 632 para. 2). The paid-up capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss-regulated bank; the bank's escrow attestation is filed with the Commercial Register and funds remain blocked until registration is completed and entry is effected.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) — https://www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a Swiss VAT registration number (MWST-Nr) and a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the ESTV's VAT registration portal or by submitting Form 755 for foreign registration. The UID serves as the Swiss tax identification number (TIN) for VAT declarations, customs, and business-to-business invoicing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Aktiengesellschaft is a separate legal and tax entity; Swiss companies are subject to broad worldwide income tax, but under the territorial aspect, Swiss-source income is included) — A foreign entity must apply for a Swiss VAT registration number (MWST-Nr) and a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the ESTV's VAT registration portal or by submitting Form 755 for foreign registration. The UID serves as the Swiss tax identification number (TIN) for VAT declarations, customs, and business-to-business invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Aktiengesellschaft is a separate legal and tax entity; Swiss companies are subject to broad worldwide income tax, but under the territorial aspect, Swiss-source income is included)

Available election forms

Check-the-box is not available in Swiss law; a Swiss AG is treated as a corporate taxpayer. Available elections include the formation of a VAT group (art. 14 MWSTG), twin-tier group taxation simplified under Swiss tax reform, and application of the Swiss Participation Relief or Notional Interest Deduction (default pending) to structure financing. Hybrid mismatch elections must follow OECD BEPS Action 2 rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (mandatory regardless of Swiss establishment; lower TTM threshold for foreign digital B2C service suppliers)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported to a non-Swiss destination qualify for 0% VAT (share of exported goods) provided title passes to a foreign purchaser and the goods leave Switzerland. A valid export custom declaration (Ausfuhrschein / export declaration) is the primary proof. Non-resident professional services can be zero-rated under certain conditions; voidable under art. 23 para 1 MWSTG.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss Single Administrative Document SAD / Ausfuhrschein)
  • Bill of Lading or transport evidence (e.g., air waybill, CMR, proof of unloading in foreign destination)
  • Export sales invoice and purchase order with foreign buyer details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from foreign buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; Swiss tax law requires QR-bills (business invoices) for B2B payments; PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing is accepted for federal processes; invoices must contain UID, date, supplier details, and applicable VAT rate.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration can review export evidence retroactively for ten years. If zero-rating is misapplied, the exporter must pay the uncollected VAT plus interest at 5% (per annum) and, for wilful misstatement, penalties up to CHF 30,000 under Art. 173 VAT Act plus criminal fraud sanctions under Swiss tax criminal law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account under IMF Article VIII. There is no general requirement for prior exchange control approval for dividends, capital repatriation, intercompany loans, or investment inflows. Profit remittance in dividends is subject to the Swiss withholding tax of 35% (refundable/reduced under tax treaties) but is not blocked by FX regulation. The SNB has statutory authority to impose negative interest or sterilisation measures in exceptional circumstances, but no capital transfer restrictions are currently in force.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion to CHF, and no designated time limit for the retention of export proceeds. A Swiss AG may maintain foreign-currency accounts abroad or in Switzerland freely. The only obligation is statistical reporting to the SNB under the Balance of Payments Ordinance; companies with quarterly cross-border payments or receivables exceeding CHF 10 million equivalent must file a BOP report via the SNB's eReporting system. This is a statistical requirement, not a surrender or conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) plus the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market and sanctions enforcement. Switzerland has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration; currency policy and external statistics are jointly administered by SNB and the Federal Statistical Office.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: 2.2/5 (Low-Medium). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are permissible as ancillary payment initiation or cross-border payout rails, but they cannot serve as the principal liquidity custodian for a Swiss AG. Swiss law does not recognise the EU-style EMI licence; deposit-taking activities require a banking licence or FINMA-finance licence (Art. 1b BankA) unless a partner bank holds funds in a segregated safeguarding account. For treasury architecture, use such EMIs for API-driven supplier payouts and card acquiring settlements, while all core liquidity, FX custody, and capital escrow must remain at a fully Swiss-licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be wired from the shareholder's corporate bank account via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for EU-source remittances) with a valid ISO 20022 purpose code indicating an equity contribution or capital transfer, typically 'EQUI' (Equity-like instruments) or 'CAPS' (Capital Transfer). The wire must reference the Swiss AG's commercial register number and the corporate account name exactly. The receiving Swiss bank will require a board resolution, a subscription agreement, proof of share capital (nominal and paid-in), and an exemptions certificate (Verrechnungssteuerformular) if the contribution becomes divisible assets. In practice, the capital injection should first flow into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at the Swiss bank, followed by a formal capital increase registration with the Commercial Register Office. For intercompany loans, the routing must use purpose code 'LOAN' and an intercompany loan agreement with arm's-length interest documentation; no prior SNB approval is required.Supplier and trade settlements should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA, cross-border payments, or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU suppliers under EUR 100,000. For bulk funding between the Swiss company and its own foreign subsidiary or treasury center, use MT202 (bank-to-bank reimbursement) after an MT103-level cover instruction has been sent. For large purchase contracts with Asian IoT hardware manufacturers, a Confirmed Irrevocable Letter of Credit under UCP600 is the recommended rail; presentation documents include commercial invoice, clean bill of lading, insurance certificate, packing list, and certificate of origin. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Practice threshold: CHF 500,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for taxpayers with gross revenue exceeding CHF 50 million; Country-by-Country reporting notification threshold is CHF 900 million consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: No UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation in Switzerland; however Swiss anti-abuse law (Art. 56 LIFD), TP Circular 49, and treaty-limitation doctrines require real local substance: office, staff, operational decision-making, economic risk, and adequate equity. Artificial arrangements are recharacterised, denied treaty relief, or reallocated to the parent jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax; fine of 1x-3x the evaded federal tax; separate cantonal penalties; default interest; restitution of wrongfully deducted refunds.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing may result in civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 6 months to 1 year where a director personally signed false returns or directed concealment.
Failure to withhold and remit anticipatory tax

Statutory sanction: ESTV claim against the paying company for the full unwithheld WHT amount; default interest; administrative fine up to CHF 10,000; criminal prosecution in wilful cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorised signatories are personally liable for the unwithheld amount if they knowingly caused the omission; in severe cases personal bankruptcy measures, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to 3 years apply.
Transfer pricing mispricing and absent documentation

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment to arm's length profit; denial of deduction for the excess; documentation fine up to CHF 20,000; wilful mispricing treated as tax evasion with 1x-3x penalty.

Director personal liability: Responsible financial directors who signed inaccurate or fictitious TP files face criminal sanctions under Swiss tax and commercial law, including imprisonment up to 3 years, personal fines, and restitution orders.
Shell company / artificial commercial substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the Swiss entity as taxable principal; denial of foreign DTA relief; 35% final WHT on distributions; commercial-register fines for false declarations of registered office or corporate seat.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes, face travel bans and fines, and be subject to criminal proceedings for false declarations under Swiss criminal law.
AML / financial-crime red flags in IoT payment flows

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act of up to CHF 5,000,000; confiscation of proceeds; enforcement proceedings against the company and payment service providers.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally fail compliance controls can be imprisoned up to 5 years, fined personally, and permanently disqualified from regulated business functions; civil claims for complicity may also attach.
