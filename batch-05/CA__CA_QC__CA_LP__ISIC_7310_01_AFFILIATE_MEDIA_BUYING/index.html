Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership; limited partners' liability is limited to their agreed contribution. Contributions may be cash or in-kind, and escrow is not mandated unless the limited partnership agreement so stipulates (none for digital media-buying). Non-resident ownership is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain an occupancy clearance from the local fire safety department in accordance with the Quebec Fire Safety Code (RLRQ, c. B-1.1, r. 0.2). Structural: maintain a building occupancy permit under the Quebec Construction Code (RLRQ, c. B-1.1, r. 0.4) and applicable RBQ standards. Occupational: register with CNESST and implement workplace health and safety plans under the Act respecting Occupational Health and Safety (RLRQ, c. S-2.1, as amended by LQ 2021, c. 27).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership; limited partners' liability is limited to their agreed contribution. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec QST and provincial income tax administration. Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account; Revenu Québec’s Tax Online Service.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Quebec limited partnership (CA_LP) must first register with the CRA to obtain a federal Business Number (BN) if it carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada; separate GST/HST and payroll accounts are added to the BN as required. The partnership must then register with Revenu Québec to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and a QST account, or use the integrated Quebec business registration service. Non-resident limited partners without permanent establishment status may require a non-resident tax account, and the partnership must file a T5013 partnership information return to allocate income to the beneficial owners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. A limited partnership formed in Quebec is fiscally transparent by default under Income Tax Act section 96, provided it qualifies as a Canadian partnership. Partners are taxed directly on allocated partnership income unless a partnership is treated as a corporation under deemed-association rules. Hybrid mismatch and reasonable-allocations rules under sections 18.4, 56.2 and 96(6) apply, and a partnership may file a joint allocation agreement with the CRA; Revenu Québec generally follows the federal allocation and reporting methods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is mandatory for a person making taxable supplies in Canada in a single calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable sales. The QST registration threshold is also CAD 30,000 for persons making taxable supplies in Quebec; the threshold applies to a vendor that exceeds it in association with Quebec consumption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available for qualifying supplies under ETA Schedule VI, including tangible personal property exported by the supplier and services supplied to non-residents, such as offshore advertising and affiliate media-buying services, where the service is not received or consumed in Canada by the non-resident client. The Quebec QST zero-rating rules align with the GST framework under Revenu Québec legislation. Eligibility requires the supplier to be registered and the transaction to be supported by contemporaneous documentation that links the supply to a non-resident recipient and to export or offshore delivery.

  • CBSA customs export declaration (B13A or approved reporting equivalent)
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / other transport evidence for goods
  • Commercial invoice and advertising services contract
  • Statement of work / media placement report evidencing offshore recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange settlement record
  • Proof of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • T5013 partnership allocation and partner identification records

Canada has no general real-time fiscal clearance, e-stamping regime, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing clearance for GST/HST or QST. Standard electronic invoicing is permitted. The CRA and Revenu Québec require that invoices contain prescribed information and be retained in an auditable electronic format. Certain Quebec vendors must register for electronic services through Revenu Québec’s secure portal, but there is no continuous digital fiscal stamping requirement. CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CléNumérique are used for filings and returns, not for supply-level pre-clearance.

CRA may deny zero-rated treatment if export documentation is deficient, retroactively assess GST/HST and QST at the full combined rate of 14.975%, and collect interest from the original filing date. False or negligent claims may trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act including up to CAD 250 plus 5% of gross tax omission or 10% if knowingly made, and willful misstatement can result in higher penalties and prosecution. Revenu Québec may assert equivalent QST clawbacks, impose administrative penalties, and disapply refunds where the FPNR no longer demonstrates real offshore supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible and Canada maintains no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions. A Quebec limited partnership may remit operational profits to non-resident limited partners without prior approval from the Bank of Canada or the Department of Finance, at freely floating market rates. The civil-law framework of Quebec imposes no capital controls on the CA_LP, and partner capital withdrawals, loan interest, management fees, and profit distributions are freely repatriable once applicable Canadian income tax compliance is discharged: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on their allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source income under the flow-through mechanics of section 96 and may claim treaty relief where available; no Part XIII withholding attaches to the distribution of already-taxed partnership income. Practical friction is exclusively bank-driven—sanctions screening, beneficial ownership verification, and source-of-funds documentation—and does not arise from currency regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for repatriating export receipts. The CA_LP may retain USD and other foreign-currency receipts indefinitely, whether in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian chartered banks or in offshore accounts. No Bank of Canada filing accompanies receipt of the funds; the only recurring obligations are (i) balance-of-payments statistical reporting of cross-border service exports through the Bank of Canada / Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System (bank-facilitated), (ii) filing of the federal T5013 partnership information return and the corresponding Quebec provincial partnership declaration with Revenu Québec, and (iii) FINTRAC's reporting duty for single or linked large transactions equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, including large virtual-currency transactions, within 24 hours. There is no requirement to convert USD into CAD before settling Canadian-dollar liabilities; the partnership may naturally hedge by maintaining both currency legs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF enforcement; Payments Canada operates the national payment clearing and settlement infrastructure (Lynx and Real-Time Rail).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM–HIGH as a secondary disbursement rail; LOW as a primary settlement bank. Wise (TransferWise) can onboard a Quebec-registered limited partnership and provides multi-currency routing through a FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services business operating under sponsorship of regulated banks; it is acceptable for low-value publisher and sub-affiliate payouts but not for the capital escrow relationship. Mercury (US) does not accept Canadian legal entities without substantive US operations and is therefore not viable. EU-passported electronic money institutions (e.g., Airwallex) cannot book a Quebec LP account directly without obtaining a Canadian MSB registration or operating through an authorized foreign bank branch; indirect sponsorship creates correspondent and enhanced due diligence complexity. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners remit capital by SWIFT MT103 to the CA_LP's Capital Escrow Account at its primary Schedule I bank in Montreal, using a payment narrative substantially in the form of 'Capital subscription – Quebec société en commandite, declaration filed with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec' and, for Bank of Canada / Statistics Canada balance-of-payments purposes, the IMF BPM6 category 'direct investment in Canada – equity injection into a Canadian partnership' as transmitted by the receiving bank. Pre-crediting controls include: (i) confirmation that the remitter name matches the subscription schedules of the partnership agreement; (ii) clearance of OFAC, UN, EU, and Canadian SEMA sanctions screening on the remitter and all ultimate principals; (iii) completion of source-of-wealth and UBO dossiers; and (iv) verification that the funds are not third-party pass-through payments. On clearance, CAD is journaled from the Capital Escrow Account to the Operational Account via internal treasury transfer with no FX conversion; USD receipts are parked in a USD sub-account and converted only at treasury direction through the bank's FX desk, never automatically at spot. Non-resident limited partners whose aggregate injections meet or exceed the statutory threshold must file the prescribed notification under the Investment Canada Act, although a passive limited-partner interest in a media-buying partnership below the review threshold normally receives notification-only clearance. Contributions are recorded as contributed capital for Part I tax purposes and are excluded from the LP's annual income allocation on the T5013 and Quebec partnership declarations.Settlement of media-buying disbursements to advertisers, publisher networks, data partners, and sub-affiliates is executed through differentiated clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border USD wires, with US clearing via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent (RBC Bank for RBC, BMO Harris for BMO, and comparable correspondents for other banks); (2) US ACH credits for recurring sub-threshold disbursements to US publishers and affiliates through the bank's US-domiciled deposit network; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit, structurally permissible but deployed only for large committed annual ad-spend contracts where the media owner requires proof of funds; (4) Canadian domestic CAD settlement through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system and Real-Time Rail for instant sub-threshold payments, supplemented by Interac e-Transfer for micro-disbursements below CAD 1,000. Every outbound remittance references the underlying invoice, insertion order, or publisher contract and carries a service-export purpose narrative consistent with the BPM6 category 'advertising and market research services' to suppress the third-party money-mule red flag. The treasury function applies a 95/5 conversion rule: 95% of forecast USD receipts are covered by forward contracts, and the residual 5% is retained unhedged to absorb variable publisher payout spikes.Deliverable CAD/USD over-the-counter FX forwards (one to twelve-month tenors) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with an OSFI-regulated investment dealer, exempt from mandatory clearing as bona fide trade-hedging transactionsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) – legally permissible as OTC derivatives under Canadian securities laws and the Quebec Derivatives Act, but commercially redundant for CAD given full deliverability of the currencyCross-currency swaps (CAD/USD basis swaps) for tenor-matched hedging of long-dated capital exposures, supported by a Credit Support Annex collateral arrangementCAD/USD vanilla FX options, collars, and participating forward structures purchased from banks or investment dealers for asymmetric protection on media-buying marginAverage-rate (Asian) FX forwards providing rolling monthly hedging of recurring USD advertising receipts to smooth conversion rates across publisher payout cycles

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM–HIGH as a secondary disbursement rail; LOW as a primary settlement bank. Wise (TransferWise) can onboard a Quebec-registered limited partnership and provides multi-currency routing through a FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services business operating under sponsorship of regulated banks; it is acceptable for low-value publisher and sub-affiliate payouts but not for the capital escrow relationship. Mercury (US) does not accept Canadian legal entities without substantive US operations and is therefore not viable. EU-passported electronic money institutions (e.g., Airwallex) cannot book a Quebec LP account directly without obtaining a Canadian MSB registration or operating through an authorized foreign bank branch; indirect sponsorship creates correspondent and enhanced due diligence complexity. The recommended treasury architecture is a Canadian Schedule I bank as the sole primary settlement member and Payments Canada participant, with fintechs capped to small-value bulk disbursement rails—for example, less than CAD 5,000 per beneficiary per day—to minimize correspondent risk and keep AML/KYC fully layered.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Quebec 11.5% general corporate rate; note: Limited Partnership is a pass-through entity, partners taxed at their own rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid to non-residents under domestic law (Part XIII of Income Tax Act); reduced to 5% under Canada-US treaty for portfolio dividends, 15% for substantial holdings; for LP distributions, character of income (e.g., business income, interest, royalties) determines WHT, not dividend rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods transactions and CAD 500,000 for services/intangibles with non-residents (per CRA TP documentation thresholds); Master File required if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million; Local File required if transactions exceed CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act can recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance; for LPs, CRA may scrutinize whether the partnership has real business purpose and substance (e.g., office, employees, decision-making in Canada) to avoid treaty shopping or sham arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act Section 239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under Section 227.1 (director's liability) if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes possible under tax evasion investigations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization: denial of tax benefits, reassessment of income, interest and penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential treaty override.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for negligence if they approved sham transactions; CRA can impose director's liability for penalties.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): fines up to CAD 1 million for individuals, CAD 2 million for entities; criminal charges for money laundering under Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined or imprisoned (up to 5 years) for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations; regulatory bans on operating financial services.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust income, impose penalties of 10% of net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), plus interest; double taxation risk.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in TP mispricing; CRA can pursue director liability for unpaid corporate taxes.
Failure to File / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty: 5% of tax payable (if any) plus 1% per month up to 12 months; for information returns, penalties per partner per day (CAD 25-100) up to CAD 2,500 per partner; Quebec similar.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they were responsible for filing and failed to do so; CRA can assess director liability.
