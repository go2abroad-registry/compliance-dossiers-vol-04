Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax and GST/HST administration; Ontario corporate income tax is collected by CRA on behalf of Ontario under the federal-provincial Tax Collection Agreement. Registration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance separately administers the Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) and certain Ontario-specific rebates.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Canadian business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Ontario Business Corporations Act; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA via My Business Account online registration or Form RC1 (same-day BN issuance is standard for online applications); (3) register the BN for program accounts, notably GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC), and payroll (RP); (4) open an Ontario EHT account through the Ontario Ministry of Finance if payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000; (5) a non-resident corporation without Canadian incorporation may apply directly for a BN under Form RC1 and must appoint a Canadian resident representative for service of process; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory for the non-resident once it makes taxable supplies in Canada and ceases to qualify as a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian corporations are separate taxpayers resident in Canada and are taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under subsection 2(2) of the Income Tax Act. Canada has no pass-through or check-the-box regime for corporations. A non-resident corporation is taxable in Canada only on a 'taxable Canadian source' nexus: business carried on in Canada, disposition of taxable Canadian property, or Part XIII non-resident withholding; territorial limitation of taxation therefore applies only to non-resident corporations.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows the incorporating statute. Statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for two closely related registrant corporations to treat taxable supplies between them as made for nil consideration, subject to the financial-services exclusion; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a net-basis return for Canadian rental income; (iii) ITA s. 217 election for a non-resident recipient of support payments; (iv) ITA s. 261 functional currency election for Canadian corporations satisfying the prescribed conditions; (v) hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by mandatory recharacterization rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6, not by elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 123(1). A person making taxable supplies in Canada must register once the total worldwide consideration for taxable supplies (including supplies of associated persons) exceeds CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold once they make taxable supplies in Canada; non-residents not carrying on business in Canada may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits and are required to register when the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/HST applies to: (i) exported goods under Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada and retains documentary proof of export; (ii) basic groceries under Schedule VI, Part I, including unprocessed bulk soybeans and yellow corn sold as raw agricultural commodities; (iii) 'offshore services' under Schedule VII, Part V, s. 7 where the service is performed in Canada for a non-resident person who is not registered under Part IX and the service relates to goods exported or to be exported from Canada, with the benefit of the service not substantially consumed or enjoyed in Canada. A broker's commission for arranging international bulk-vessel sales of grain to a non-resident buyer qualifies as a zero-rated offshore service if the benefit is realized outside Canada; the supplier must maintain the documentary evidence required by CRA GST/HST Memoranda 17-3, 17-5, and 17-8.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS))
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (including Harmonized System commodity codes and origin certification where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air waybill, or rail waybill) evidencing export movement from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Proof of outbound customs clearance, such as CBSA carrier cargo custody reports or marine export reports
  • Settlement evidence: bank wire confirmation and/or Irrevocable Transferable Letter of Credit documentation, together with inward remittance records
  • GST/HST export documentation file (ETA s. 1(d) proof of export; CRA documentary retention period of six years)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or compulsory API-invoice stamping regime. Invoices must satisfy the prescribed GST/HST information disclosure requirements (ETA s. 223 and GST/HST Memoranda 8.1/9.3). Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA XML/portal. Export declarations must be filed electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on Peppol BIS 3.0 remains voluntary.

CRA audit scrutiny focuses on physical export proof (vessel movement records, foreign port clearance, destination customs entries). If zero-rating is denied, CRA reassesses GST/HST at 13% (Ontario HST) on the full export consideration, plus arrears interest. Penalties include: (i) the 25% gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 for false statements or omissions; (ii) failure-to-register and failure-to-file penalties under ETA s. 280 (1% plus 0.25% per month up to 12 months); (iii) retrospective clawback of refunds or rebates if goods are re-imported into Canada or export documentation is falsified; (iv) criminal exposure under ETA ss. 327.1/328 and Criminal Code s. 380 for fraudulent export schemes. The 180-calendar-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is monitored under CRA trade-transparency analytics; a persistent failure to repatriate export proceeds without a valid commercial justification may trigger recharacterization of the outstanding receivable as a shareholder benefit or deemed dividend under ITA s. 15, subjecting the payment to Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced where applicable).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no general restrictions on the remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or liquidation proceeds to non-residents. Dividends and certain other outbound payments remain subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, treaty-reduced). Inbound foreign direct investment in a Canadian bulk grain brokerage may become notifiable or reviewable under the Investment Canada Act, but this is a national security/investment review mechanism, not a currency exchange restriction. No exchange-control approval is required for capital injection, profit repatriation, or trade settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters are not subject to any statutory timeline or mandatory minimum ratio for the surrender or conversion of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, deposited offshore or onshore, and converted at commercial market rates at the exporter's discretion. Reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) apply to cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but do not impose conversion or surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada; no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada does not recognize the European 'EMI' (Electronic Money Institution) banking licence. Non-resident digital payment providers seeking to serve a Canadian grain brokerage must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and typically require sponsorship by, or partnership with, a federally regulated Schedule I bank to access Canadian clearing rails. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently open domestic Canadian bank accounts or route CAD payments through Lynx. Direct participation in Payments Canada is restricted to regulated member institutions, making an independent fintech-only treasury structure non-viable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (15% federal general corporate rate + 10% Ontario general corporate rate; reduced to 12.2% combined on the first C$500,000 of eligible active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on gross dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction to a lower residual rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when controlled cross-border transactions (goods, services, interest, or royalties) exceed C$1,000,000 in aggregate in the taxation year; Master File is required when the multinational group consolidated gross revenue exceeds C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, the GAAR under section 245 denies tax benefits where the principal purpose is a tax avoidance transaction lacking economic substance. The CRA also scrutinizes treaty-based structures through limitation-of-benefits provisions, thin capitalization rules under section 15(3) and 78(2), and anti-hybrid rules, requiring real commercial rationale, personnel, and management in Canada for a trading or brokerage entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties under section 163(2) equal to 50% of the misstatement amount; CRA public disclosure of taxpayer name for conviction; revocation of refunds and offsets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed the evasion are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for unpaid source deductions and GST/HST; courts can pierce the corporate veil to impose restitution, and tax collection measures may include travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Corporation Transaction

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization for the entire scheme; denial of deductions; re-determined Part I tax plus interest; transfer pricing penalty under section 247(8) of 10% of the net deficiency where the fair-market-value adjustment exceeds C$1,000,000 and documentation is deficient; gross negligence penalties and damages for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally assessed under section 160 for transfers of property without adequate consideration; findings of a sham transaction expose them to civil restitution, court-ordered travel restrictions pending collection, and, in severe cases, criminal liability.
Anti-Money Laundering & Financial Record-Keeping

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, corporate administrative penalties up to C$2,000,000 per violation and C$500,000 for individuals; criminal prosecution for false statements up to C$500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; FINTRAC may suspend or deregister a non-compliant entity.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors are liable for due-diligence failures and can be personally fined; asset-freezing orders, cease-and-desist directions, and prosecutions for failing to implement a compliance program can be issued against directors in their personal capacity.
