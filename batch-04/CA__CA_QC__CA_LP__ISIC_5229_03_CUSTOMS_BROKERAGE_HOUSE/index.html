Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Quebec law for a limited partnership; the partnership agreement must fix the capital contributions of each limited partner. Contributions may be cash or property, but limited partners may not contribute services under art. 2243 C.C.Q. No escrow account is required by the enterprise registrar; however, the federal customs broker licence under the Customs Act will require a licensed broker to post a bond or other security in the amount prescribed by the Canada Border Services Agency.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the municipal service de sécurité incendie after inspection of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and electrical panels; structural load clearance issued by a professional engineer for file storage racks or bond-room vaults; and CNESST registration as an employer with confirmation of workplace health and safety compliance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (CQLR c S-2.1).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html); Revenu Québec — CLICK portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming or acquiring an interest in a Quebec LP must first establish a permanent establishment/ taxable presence in Canada under s. 253 of the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. For GST/HST, apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or online filing, providing the LP's partnership agreement, certificate of limited partnership and partner details. For QST, register with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1-V or the CLICK portal. The LLP's general partner (resident) will act as assessable agent. Registration is mandatory within 30 days of commencement of supply activity in Quebec, or when supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a comprehensive check-the-box election. Under s. 96 of the Income Tax Act, an LP is transparent; partners include their proportionate share of income. Partnerships may elect a non-calendar fiscal period (s. 249.1(4) CRA approval). Non-residents may elect under s. 216 (net rental income), s. 217 (elected Canadian-source employment income), or s. 219 (non-resident corporation). For GST/HST, there are elections for the Part IX (Financial Institutions) rules and capacity to register as a branch. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4-18.6, 93, 94) apply to cross-border payments and deny deductions where no economic substance is present. QST law contains mirror provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold); lower or no threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of taxable digital services; for carriers and certain financial services, registration is mandatory regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under GST/HST (Schedule VI Part V) and QST applies to (a) exported goods when the exporter retains evidence of actual export (Form B13A, carrier waybill, customs broker registration), and (b) services supplied to a non-resident where the service is not performed in Canada in respect of tangible property located in Canada and is not supplied to an individual present in Canada. For a customs brokerage house, fees related to an export shipment may be zero-rated if the brokerage service is supplied to a non-resident principal and the goods have already been cleared out of Canada before performance. The broker must keep complete documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) — CBSA
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill with overseas consignee and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of FX proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice describing exported goods or services
  • Packing List and cargo manifest
  • Non-Resident Assessment (NRST) evidence where applicable
  • Supply contract / purchase order confirming non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is mandated. However, all GST/HST and QST filers must file returns electronically via the CRA and Revenu Québec web portals; large businesses (annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5M) are required to file electronically. Electronic records must be maintained and reported via API where prescribed. Voluntary PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing is available.

CRA may reassess up to 4 years from the end of the tax year (7 years in case of negligence/fraud) and claw back zero-rated amounts with interest. Penalties apply under s. 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act for false or misleading statements: an immediate penalty of 50% of the tax claimed. Revenu Québec may impose a parallel penalty (up to 50% of QST) under the Tax Administration Act. Willful evasion is subject to criminal prosecution and significant fines and imprisonment. Zero-rating denial results in full GST/HST and QST liability, plus interest from the due date.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange controls, no prior approval for profit remittances, and no restrictions on dividend or distributive payments to non-resident limited partners. A non-resident LP's Canada-source income is subject only to ordinary tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and applicable treaty relief; funds may be freely repatriated in CAD or any foreign currency after payment of taxes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-period requirement for export receipts. Exporters may invoice and retain proceeds in any convertible currency. Reporting obligations are limited to export declarations under the Customs Act and FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for physical or financial movements exceeding CAD 10,000, including electronic funds transfers treated as large-value transaction reports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists in Canada, as the Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency under the Currency Act and Canada's obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Canada does not issue an 'Electronic Money Institution' licence as a distinct regulatory category; non-resident fintech EMI platforms seeking to serve Quebec clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, obtain a Revenu Québec registration if doing business in Quebec, and comply with Quebec financial consumer protection rules. Offshore EMI accounts without Canadian licensing and no local clearing presence are legally constrained from acting as a primary treasury rail for a Quebec-licensed customs brokerage, but can be used temporarily for pass-through currency transfers and pre-funded disbursements if the EMI operates under a recognized MSB registration and maintains a Canadian correspondent relationship.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial ownership chain in a non-resident Limited Partnership, especially where limited partners are foreign corporates, trusts, or foundations.

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership chain in a non-resident Limited Partnership, especially where limited partners are foreign corporates, trusts, or foundations.

Defense evidence: Registered LP agreement and certificate from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec; funded and updated declaration of ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO); notarized identification and residential address for each partner, director, and authorized signatory; a structured trust/corporate chart showing seniority and control; and a written source-of-funds and wealth explanation with supporting bank statements or audit records.
ISIC 5229 / customs brokerage activity flagged as inherently trade-based money laundering (TBML) risk because the business handles duty payments, import clearances, and third-party disbursements.

Root cause: ISIC 5229 / customs brokerage activity flagged as inherently trade-based money laundering (TBML) risk because the business handles duty payments, import clearances, and third-party disbursements.

Defense evidence: Valid Customs Brokerage Licence issued by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); documented in-house AML/KYC procedure manual; customer due-diligence file for each importer mandate; independent financial and compliance audit report; professional indemnity insurance certificate; and a detailed transaction-flow diagram distinguishing escrow duties, disbursed tariffs, and brokerage fee income.
Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctions-list hit on any limited partner, beneficial owner, or senior customs broker.

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctions-list hit on any limited partner, beneficial owner, or senior customs broker.

Defense evidence: International and OFAC/OSFI/MSB sanctions screening results with a documented adjudication memo; independent PEP risk assessment; source-of-funds declaration supported by audited financial statements; signed risk acceptance from the bank's Compliance Officer; and a legal opinion from a Canadian-qualified attorney confirming the hit is a false positive or low-inherent-risk profile.
Tax classification mismatch between the LP's pass-through treatment and bank expectations for corporate AML due diligence, FATCA/CRS status, and Quebec enterprise registration.

Root cause: Tax classification mismatch between the LP's pass-through treatment and bank expectations for corporate AML due diligence, FATCA/CRS status, and Quebec enterprise registration.

Defense evidence: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) partnership registration letter or T5013 summary for the fiscal period; Revenu Québec GST/QST registration certificate; completed FATCA Form W-8BEN-E and CRS self-certification for each non-resident partner; signed professional compliance representation letter; and a copy of the bank's own due-diligence form cross-referenced to the LP deed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital subscriptions and limited-partner contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the LP's CAD-denominated bank account in Quebec, using the beneficiary name exactly as registered in the enterprise registry and a clear reference code or invoice tying the transfer to a specific capital call. Where the sender uses USD or a third currency, the receiving bank converts at commercial rates; the bank memo should state 'Contribution to Partnership Capital' or 'Capital Injection'. Required verification includes: FATCA/CRS self-certifications, a capital-call notice approved by the general partner, bank source-of-funds verification showing the originating entity or individual, and a certificate of good standing for the LP. The Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) is used for same-country CAD movements but is not appropriate for cross-border capital; wire is mandatory.Trade settlements, customs duties, and broker service payments use a combination of clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers to foreign suppliers and overseas customs agencies; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 and bank-issued guarantees under URDG758 for high-value or condition-based trade payments; (iii) Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for urgent CAD-denominated domestic disbursements above the LVTS threshold; and (iv) the CBSA 'Duty and Taxes' electronic disbursement channel, which is the ABI-equivalent ACH-like gateway for customs tariff remittances, supplemented by white-wire or certified cheque for rejected credits. All outbound disbursements must include accurate payment purpose codes and transaction narratives that match the supporting invoice, import entry, or broker service contract.Deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts between CAD and USD/EUR/GBP, settled in cash or by actual delivery, for trade and capital-flow hedging.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CAD for counterparties located in jurisdictions unavailable for physical CAD settlement, subject to bank credit limits and ISDA documentation.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge long-term CAD/USD liability and capital-structure exposure.Vanilla FX options (call/put structures) to protect against adverse CAD or foreign-currency rate movements while preserving upside.Collateralized currency swaps under ISDA Master Agreements with a Canadian Schedule VIII eligible counterparty.
