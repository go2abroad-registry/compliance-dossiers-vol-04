Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law. Canadian corporations are taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). For Quebec, the Corporation Tax Act imposes separate entity-level tax. Certain ministerial elections are available, such as the election to treat a wholly-owned subsidiary as a branch for foreign accrual property income (FAPI) purposes (s. 218.1), the capital dividend account election (s. 83(2)), and the joint election for wind-up of a subsidiary (s. 88(1.3)). However, these do not alter the fundamental entity-level classification. Additionally, Quebec rules follow the federal classification; no hybrid mismatch election is available for non-resident entities. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4 to 18.7) apply to disallow deductions in cross-border arrangements but do not require an affirmative election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold) – applies when taxable supplies exceed this amount in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; Canadian resident corporations, other than small suppliers, must register. For non-resident digital services providers, a separate economic nexus threshold of CAD 30,000 from supplies to Canadian consumers also applies. Quebec’s QST threshold is the same, administered by Revenu Québec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim a 0% GST/HST (and QST) rate for exported goods, the supplier must be a registrant, the goods must be sold to a non-resident purchaser, and the goods must be delivered or shipped to a destination outside Canada by the supplier (or through an agent) for direct export. Services (including online electronic services) are zero-rated when provided to a non-resident, but only if the service is not performed in Canada or is not otherwise supplied to a person who is resident in Canada at any time, and the service does not relate to real property in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada. In Quebec, parallel QST zero-rating applies under the Quebec Taxation Act s. 403 and following.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods exported from Canada, as per the Customs Act, reporting requirement)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing transportation to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice issued to the non-resident buyer with the seller’s BN and QST registration number, explicit '0% exported' indicator
  • Proof of export (e.g., export packer list, customs broker report, or Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) electronic export data via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES))

No mandatory real-time electronic clearance or fiscal stamping exists in Canada. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices (e.g., PDF, XML) as long as they contain prescribed information (Excise Tax Act s. 223, QST rules under Quebec Taxation Act). For GST/HST, the supplier must issue an invoice or simplified invoice if the taxable supply exceeds CAD 150. Suppliers using electronic systems must retain records in machine-readable format; CRA audits typically require system access via API integrations for data extraction. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing standards (e.g., EDI, Peppol) are recognized, but no government-operated clearance portal is mandated.

CRA and Revenu Québec routinely audit export zero-rating claims, requiring the exporter to maintain full documentation trails. If the 180-day repatriation deadline is missed, or if documentation is incomplete, the CRA will disallow the zero-rating and reassess the supply at 5% GST (plus 9.975% QST in Quebec) and levy 'goods and services tax/HST rebate' recovery plus interest. Penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 280.1 range from 10% to 25% of the net tax amount for false statements or willful participation in a misstatement. For fraudulent export schemes, criminal penalties can include fines up to 200% of the tax attempted to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years, as well as retroactive clawback of all input tax credits claimed on export-related expenses. Revenu Québec imposes similar penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act s. 1129.1.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted convertibility for current account transactions, including trade settlements, service payments, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions require Bank of Canada approval only in narrowly defined cases; for legitimate corporate activities, convertibility is unrestricted. Profit remittance is permitted upon submission of corporate resolutions, audited financial statements, and tax filings, with no material regulatory hurdles for Quebec-incorporated entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists for mandatory surrender of export proceeds under Canadian law. Exporters must file export declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) within 30 days of shipment and retain supporting documentation. Banks may request proof of export (commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading) for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 to satisfy FINTRAC AML obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
