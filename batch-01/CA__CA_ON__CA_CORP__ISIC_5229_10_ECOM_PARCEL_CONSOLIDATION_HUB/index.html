Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Ontario's provincial tax component is administered by the CRA through the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) program. Corporate registry is administered by Corporations Canada (federal CBCA) or ServiceOntario (provincial OBCA). Primary tax portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian or foreign-incorporated entity obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the single national tax identifier. For a CBCA/OBCA corporation, the BN request is integrated into the articles of incorporation; the CRA issues the BN electronically via the corporate registry. For an existing foreign entity entering Canada, registration is completed via Form RC1 (or RC1W 'Request for a Business Number from Non-Resident Businesses') through BRO, by fax, or by mail to the International Tax Services Office (Sudbury). The filing requires a certified copy of the foreign charter/registration, a Canadian agent/power-of-attorney mandate if the non-resident has no place of business in Canada, and the designation of a person resident in Canada for service of documents under ETA s. 272.1. The same application activates the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) and any payroll (RP), corporate income tax (RC), or import/export (RM) accounts. Online registration is immediate; paper filing takes 5–10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a statutory taxpayer separate from its shareholders under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. Resident corporations are subject to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada. A corporation registered for GST/HST in Ontario is a registrant liable for HST on taxable supplies made in Canada unless zero-rated or exempt. No territory-exempt-zone status exists in Ontario; the whole province applies the Harmonized Sales Tax regime.

Available election forms

Canadian tax law does not provide a check-the-box or US-style pass-through election; corporate classification follows the legal form of incorporation. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election allowing a non-resident to pay Canadian tax on net rental income instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (ii) ITA s. 216.1 election for international transportation income to deem no permanent establishment; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 211 election to treat closely related corporations as a single GST/HST registrant; (iv) ETA s. 240(1) voluntary registration as a small supplier before the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; and (v) ETA s. 150 election for the transfer of assets between registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 17.2 (including time-lagged entities) are mandatory, with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (aggregated with associated persons) measured on a trailing four-consecutive-calendar-quarter basis or per single calendar quarter; compulsory GST/HST registration is triggered once the threshold is exceeded. Non-residents are subject to the same threshold, except that non-residents supplying telecommunications, broadcasting, or certain digital services must register immediately without a small-supplier exemption. A parcel consolidation hub that makes only zero-rated export supplies may still be required to register if any taxable domestic supply is made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Goods exported by the supplier (s. 1) and commercial goods exported under customs control (s. 2) qualify for the 0% rate where the exporter retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied in respect of exported goods (s. 5), including freight forwarding, customs brokering, parcel consolidation, sorting, repacking, and line-haul transportation that is part of a continuous freight service for goods ultimately exported, are zero-rated regardless of the recipient's residency. Services to a non-resident recipient performed exclusively outside Canada (s. 7) are also zero-rated, but a domestic parcel hub will primarily rely on the s. 5 exported-goods category. Supporting zero-rating requires the supplier to show with credible documentation that the goods were physically removed from Canada within a reasonable time and that the supply was made for an export transaction.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A) or ACI eManifest cargo report filed in the Canada Export Reporting System (CAED) referencing the exporting customs sub-location code
  • Commercial Invoice (English or French) identifying the Canadian seller, foreign buyer, goods description, and export terms
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or Master/Individual Air Waybill showing Canadian departure and foreign destination
  • Parcel Manifest / Sortation List matching the consolidated shipment to its final foreign consignees
  • Proof of departure: outbound border clearance report, terminal receipt, or carrier certification of cross-border exit
  • Destination-country customs entry documentation or broker clearance report evidencing foreign arrival
  • Bank SWIFT / Inward Remittance Certificate, or Airwallex/local-clearing settlement statement evidencing cross-border payment for the exported goods or offshore-rendered services

No real-time fiscal clearance API, digital stamping, or government transactional clearance regime exists in Canada. The CRA does not mandate e-invoicing for GST/HST; paper, PDF, EDI, and PEPPOL-based invoices are equally valid if they contain the statutory content (supplier name and BN, recipient name, date, description, taxable amount, HST rate and amount). Registrants with total annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,500,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE). Canada is progressively adopting an optional Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability standard aligned with PEPPOL CIUS, and the CRA has authority under ETA s. 286 to demand invoice-level records in an electronic format during audit.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims for false, incomplete, or self-serving documentation; where proof of export is inadequate, the 0% rate is reversed to 13% HST and net tax is reassessed with compound daily interest (prescribed rate plus an additional 5%). Civil penalties under ETA s. 280(1) reach the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2) are 50% of any income tax shortfall arising from false statements; and criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 is an indictable offence punishable by a fine of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years. CRA will also claw back input tax credits attributable to inputs consumed in connection with unsubstantiated exports and may deny future ITC claims, with all supporting export records required to be retained for six years under ETA s. 286. Although the ETA prescribes no fixed 180-day repatriation deadline, CRA treats an extended foreign receivable or unrepatriated proceeds exceeding 180 days as a red flag that the cross-border supply may be fictitious, triggering full reversal of zero-rating and associated penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions. Foreign exchange is deregulated under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada; no general restrictions apply to cross-border capital movements. Profit remittances, including dividends and branch repatriations, are freely transferable, subject to corporate law solvency tests, Canada Revenue Agency withholding tax compliance (Part XIII for non-residents), and anti-money-laundering verification. Non-resident ownership is permitted under the Investment Canada Act subject to net benefit review thresholds. For an Ontario-domiciled e-commerce parcel consolidation hub, no authorization is required for routine trade-related payments or investment inflows, but all transactions must flow through a reporting entity and meet FINTRAC large transaction reporting and record-keeping obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any conversion requirement for export receipts in Canada. All export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, freely held offshore, or converted to Canadian dollars at any licensed financial intermediary. Enterprises must nonetheless comply with corporate record keeping, transfer pricing, and foreign income accrual rules. Export documentation, including customs export declarations and commercial invoices, must be retained, and the underlying transaction must be reported to FINTRAC if it constitutes a large cash transaction or crosses the threshold for cross-border currency reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) with administrative oversight of foreign exchange and payments by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for inbound/outbound payment routing and FX conversion through licensed Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or restricted-purpose fintechs that hold a FINTRAC MSB registration or are regulated under provincial money transfer licensing. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Ontario corporation are performed following the standard Canadian inward remittance procedure: (i) funds are wired via SWIFT from the foreign parent or shareholder account to the corporation's Tier-1 Canadian bank account using the receiving bank's CAD or USD correspondent routing; (ii) for the first injection, the remitter must include a clear payment description referencing the corporate name, the purpose of funds (e.g., 'shareholder loan', 'paid-up capital contribution', or 'intercompany advance for working capital'), and, to the extent requested by the Canadian bank, the underlying board resolution; (iii) in terms of purpose codes, Canada does not require a statutorily mandated purpose code for inward remittances, although the Canadian receiving bank will map the payment to an IMF Balance of Payments or Bank of Canada statistical purpose code category such as 'Direct investment – equity capital' (FDI) or 'Loans – intercompany' for reporting; (iv) the Canadian bank will screen the wire for sanctions and perform a source-of-funds verification; (v) if the injection exceeds CAD $10,000 in equivalent and is executed as an electronic funds transfer by a Canadian-reporting MSB, FINTRAC reporting obligations apply at the remitting institution; (vi) the Ontario corporation must record the contribution in its share capital account or shareholder loan register, issue a board resolution, and file the appropriate CRA T2 schedule (Schedule 50 for shareholder information) to maintain capital and related-party documentation; (vii) for non-resident shareholders, no Exchange Control approval is required, but the corporate treasury must maintain evidence for the bank's periodic compliance reviews and for CRA's thin capitalization and transfer pricing rules under subsection 15(2), section 17, and section 78. For a parcel consolidation hub, recommended routing is a direct SWIFT MT103 from a parent entity's global bank to a Tier-1 Canadian bank, avoiding third-party or unstamped intermediaries.Outbound trade disbursements from the Ontario parcel consolidation hub should use the following clearing rails: (i) for regular supplier payments, cross-border supplier disbursements and foreign customs clearance fees, use SWIFT MT103 through the corporation's Tier-1 bank with corresponding FX conversion under a designated FX contract or at spot; (ii) for confirmed commercial letters of credit applicable to large wholesale parcel or consolidation equipment purchases, use a documentary LC opened at the Canadian bank with the beneficiary's bank as advising bank, routed via SWIFT MT700/MT710, with all fees and tenor aligned to Incoterms 2020; (iii) for low-value, high-frequency payments to locations such as China, Hong Kong, Singapore, EU, and US, the practical clearing options are Airwallex's global payment network or Local Clearing rails (e.g., FedACH in the US, SEPA in the EU, CIPS or local RTGS in China) where the originating Canadian bank account is integrated with the fintech platform via open banking or dedicated API; (iv) each disbursement over CAD $10,000 in an electronic funds transfer must be reported to FINTRAC by the Canadian financial institution or MSB; (v) for internal treasury concentration, the corporation may implement a zero-balance account structure where outbound settlements are swept from a concentration account to a local clearing rail, ensuring that no single account exceeds FINTRAC reporting thresholds without an accompanying TRR (Terrorist Financing) screening; (vi) all outbound wire requests must be authorized by dual signatures in accordance with corporate banking resolution and the bank's access agreement, and where a foreign beneficiary is in a sanctions- or high-risk country, a compliance hold will require supporting commercial contract, pro-forma invoice, and analysis of the end-use against the Export Controls List.Direct deliverable forward contracts booked with a Canadian Schedule I bank for Canadian dollars versus USD, EUR, CNY, HKD, or other major trade currencies, subject to OSFI and dealer credit capacityNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for CAD versus currencies with restricted delivery (e.g., certain Asian or LATAM currencies), through an OSFI-regulated or FINTRAC-registered dealer authorized to trade over the counterCross-currency swaps between CAD and USD/EUR for large intercompany loan or capital injections, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with a regulated Canadian counterpartyFX options (European style) for hedging transactional exposure on high-volatility trade or consolidation routesPlain-vanilla foreign exchange swap/rollover contracts for short-term funding of parcel consolidation working capital, executed under bilateral credit support annexesCommodity-linked or index-linked derivatives are NOT permitted without additional OSFI and CRA mark-to-market reporting, and are excluded as unsuitable for a low-risk parcel consolidation treasury

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal rate on taxable income earned in Canada by a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (general rate reduction applied beyond this threshold, making the federal general rate 15%, subject to the 2.7% federal abatement adjustment for provinces and territories). Combined with Ontario's provincial rate of 10% (for general corporations) or 3.2% (for CCPCs eligible for the Ontario small business deduction, on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income), the combined statutory headline rate ranges from 12.2% (CCPC eligible for small business deduction) to 25% (non-CCPC or general corporate income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder under Section 212(2) of the ITA, unless reduced by a tax treaty.

Failure to Provide Foreign Information Return (Section 162(7)): CAD 24,000 for each month or part month (up to 24 months) for failure to file Form T1134 (Foreign Affiliate Information Return) or Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) when required. 5. Interest Charges (Section 161): Daily compound interest at the prescribed rate (currently 9% for corporate overdue taxes, effective Q1 2025) on unpaid amounts, accruing from the balance-due day, plus penalties and interest on penalties. 6. Director's Liability for Unremitted Withholdings (Subsection 227.1 ITA): Directors are personally liable for a corporation's failure to remit employee source deductions (payroll, GST/HST, or any amount collected from third parties) to the CRA, if they knew or should have known of the failure, and if the CRA has issued a certificate of assessment for the amount and the corporation is unable to satisfy the debt.
