Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or contributed capital for a société en commandite. Limited partners must actually perform their promised contributions in money or property (not services) under CCQ arts. 2247–2248. No escrow or trust account is required by law at formation; the partnership agreement and contribution schedules govern funding. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and CRA My Business Account, for the federal Business Number (BN), GST/HST program account, non-resident income-tax filings, information returns, and partnership reporting. Revenu Québec, including its online services portal, administers Québec Sales Tax (QST), Québec income-tax matters, source deductions, and Québec partnership information obligations. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import and export customs documentation and the Canada Border Services Agency CARM system for commercial importers.

E-government filing portal

A Québec limited partnership carrying on business or earning income in Canada should obtain a CRA nine-digit Business Number through Business Registration Online or the CRA non-resident registration process, using the partnership agreement, legal name, Québec registration or business-registration evidence where applicable, principal business activity, fiscal period, Canadian and foreign addresses, responsible partners, authorized representative, and ownership or control information. The partnership should open the applicable CRA program accounts, including GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP) if employees are present, and information-return accounts where required. A non-resident partnership should appoint an authorized representative or use a Canadian tax intermediary where operationally necessary and maintain evidence of Canadian business activity and its partnership status. Registration for GST/HST is made with the CRA; registration for QST is generally made separately with Revenu Québec. A non-resident partnership supplying taxable services in Québec must assess both GST/HST and QST registration obligations and whether it is carrying on business in Canada or Québec. Registration is not itself a substitute for any Québec enterprise-registration, professional-regulatory, securities, or financial-services licensing requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general US-style check-the-box election for a limited partnership. A valid Canadian partnership is generally fiscally transparent: the partnership computes income and allocates each partner's share under the Income Tax Act, while partners are generally taxed on allocated income. A partnership may elect or make statutory designations where specifically permitted, including elections or designations affecting partnership income allocations, dispositions of partnership interests, and certain transfers under the Income Tax Act; these are not entity-classification elections. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion where a foreign classification or instrument mismatch produces a deduction/non-inclusion or duplicate deduction outcome. The legal characterization of a foreign or Canadian LP must be supported by its agreement, governing law, conduct, and tax attributes; a foreign jurisdiction's entity election does not automatically bind Canada or Québec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, calculated under the small-supplier rules, generally triggers GST/HST registration when exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Québec generally uses a CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST registration, subject to the detailed QST rules and exceptions. Voluntary registration may be available below the threshold. The 5.0% headline rate is the federal GST rate; Québec QST is generally 9.975%, and the combined ordinary Québec consumption-tax burden is generally 14.975%. Special rules apply to financial services, specified listed financial institutions, real property, imported taxable supplies, digital-economy supplies, and supplies made through platforms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, GST/HST and QST zero-rating generally requires that the goods be exported from Canada or Québec in circumstances satisfying the applicable place-of-supply and documentary conditions; the supplier must retain evidence of export, purchaser identity, delivery terms, carrier movement, customs reporting, and destination outside Canada. For M&A advisory and other professional services, a supply to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the recipient is not resident in Canada and the service is not excluded by the statutory rules, including rules for services in respect of real property or tangible personal property in Canada, litigation or dispute-related services concerning Canadian proceedings, services performed in Canada in certain circumstances, and supplies made to an individual in Canada. The supplier must verify the customer's non-resident status, obtain contractual and commercial-use evidence, identify the actual recipient and beneficiary, document where the services are performed and used, and analyze whether the service is connected with Canadian securities, financial instruments, Canadian real property, or another excluded category. Zero-rating is not available merely because the client pays from offshore or the transaction is cross-border. Exported services must be distinguished from exempt financial services, which generally do not carry GST/HST or QST and generally do not generate input-tax-credit or input-tax-refund entitlement.

  • Commercial invoice, engagement or supply contract, purchase order, and written description of the exported goods or services
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, courier manifest, or other carrier evidence showing movement of goods outside Canada
  • Proof of delivery or foreign receipt and destination-country import or customs record, where applicable
  • Recipient's foreign legal name, address, tax-registration details where available, and evidence that the recipient is non-resident for GST/HST and QST purposes

Canada and Québec do not generally require real-time electronic invoice clearance, mandatory government pre-approval, or universal fiscal digital stamping for ordinary M&A advisory invoices. A GST/HST or QST registrant must issue compliant invoices or other prescribed records, retain source documents and electronic books, and provide sufficient information for tax identification, consideration, tax rate, tax amount, place-of-supply analysis, and input-tax-credit or input-tax-refund support. Electronic records are acceptable if they remain accessible, reliable, readable, and capable of audit reproduction. CRA electronic filing is required for many GST/HST registrants, including registrants subject to mandatory electronic-filing rules, and Revenu Québec may impose electronic filing requirements for QST returns or specified registrants. Electronic funds transfer, payment-processor, and platform records should be reconciled to invoices, contracts, engagement letters, and bank receipts. There is no general Canadian or Québec requirement to transmit every invoice through a government clearance API.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess tax, deny zero-rating, deny input tax credits or input tax refunds, and assess the unpaid GST/HST or QST retroactively, generally together with arrears interest and applicable penalties. A supplier that incorrectly treats a taxable domestic supply as zero-rated can become liable for the uncollected tax, subject to statutory purchaser-relief and assessment rules. Penalties may include failure-to-file, failure-to-remit, repeated-failure, gross-negligence, false-statement, and false-return penalties; gross-negligence penalties can generally be 50% of the understated tax or improperly claimed credit, while tax-evasion conduct may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Customs misdeclarations may attract CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System sanctions, seizure, forfeiture, reassessment of duties and taxes, and prosecution. Québec may impose separate QST interest and penalties. Fraudulent or unsupported export claims should be corrected through amended returns, repayment of improperly claimed refunds or credits, and preservation of the full audit trail; limitation periods can be extended or eliminated in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of partnership capital from a Quebec limited partnership; the Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully transferable. Practical frictions are limited to: (i) mandatory FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Part XIII or Quebec withholding tax on distributions to non-resident limited partners, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; (iii) Investment Canada Act review, which is confined to foreign acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses and does not impede outbound advisory fee flows; and (iv) Canadian economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act, which may block transactions involving designated jurisdictions or persons. For the M&A advisory boutique, success fees in USD/EUR are receivable offshore without limitation, and no central-bank approval is required for any conversion or remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime. The former Exchange Control Act requirements were abolished in 1958, and neither exporters nor cross-border service providers such as M&A advisors are required to convert foreign-currency earnings into Canadian dollars within any statutory timeline or ratio. The limited partnership may retain 100% of cross-border success fees in USD/EUR accounts at Schedule I banks or convert them at its sole discretion. The only compliance obligations are transactional: FINTRAC cross-border currency and EFT reports for physical or electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and consistent recording of foreign-currency receipts in the LP capital accounts for Canadian and Quebec income tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration, as the statutory exchange-control regime was repealed in 1958. Cross-border fund surveillance and anti-money-laundering/anti-terrorist-financing oversight of transfer flows are administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Prudential supervision of deposit-taking, escrow, and FX dealing institutions is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Quebec-specific money-services-business licensing is administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Act respecting money-services businesses (CQLR c E-12.000001).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM (conditional / ancillary use only). Legal constraints: (i) any non-resident electronic-money institution (EMI) operating in Canada as a money-services business must register with FINTRAC as an MSB under the PCMLTFA and, if active in Quebec, obtain an AMF permit under the Act respecting money-services businesses; (ii) foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or US-licensed platforms may be used by the LP solely as payment-collection agents for small advisory retainers, not as custodians of client escrow or settlement funds, because they lack CAD clearing through LVTS/Real-Time Rail and cannot provide documentary credit or secured escrow; (iii) the LP must disclose any EMI relationship to its primary banks and include aggregated EMI transfers in FINTRAC EFT reports once the CAD 10,000 threshold is crossed; (iv) reliance on foreign EMIs for material success-fee flows is incompatible with the statutory escrow and trust-account expectations of Canadian M&A counterparties and creates an adverse client-acceptance profile for the boutique.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for partner capital contributions and equity injections: (i) the payer originates a SWIFT MT103 to the LP's collection account at a Schedule I bank, using the bank's BIC, Canadian clearing code (3-digit institution number plus 5-digit transit), and the LP's beneficiary account number; (ii) the payment narrative or purpose code must read 'LP capital contribution / subscription' or 'Investment – advisory equity', and must reference the executed subscription agreement and the LP capital-account identifier; (iii) where the contribution is received in USD or EUR, the bank's treasury desk executes a same-day conversion to CAD at the relevant spot, or the conversion is pre-hedged under a forward; (iv) the receiving institution is obligated to file a FINTRAC EFT report for any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (v) internal verification requires the bank credit memorandum to be reconciled against the executed subscription agreement, the LP capital-account ledger, and a partner instruction letter; (vi) hardening controls: cash deposits exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable, bearer instruments and in-kind contributions are prohibited without an independent valuation, and no third-party nominee may inject capital without full UBO disclosure.Clearing-rail hierarchy for trade settlements and advisory disbursements: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments in USD/EUR, with field 70 narrative stating 'Success fee under engagement agreement dated [X]' and field 71A charged as 'OUR', settling same-day via Fedwire/CHIPS (USD) or TARGET2 (EUR); (ii) for domestic Canadian-dollar disbursements above CAD 25,000, use the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) providing real-time gross settlement finality; below that threshold, use standard EFT or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for instant small-value payments; (iii) for deal-contingent payments to escrow agents, legal counsel, or counter parties requiring documentary security, use a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued or confirmed by a Schedule I Canadian bank, coupled with escrow release instructions authenticated by two approved signatories; (iv) every outbound disbursement is matched to an executed invoice and a general-partner resolution, with FINTRAC EFT reporting where the transaction reaches or exceeds CAD 10,000; (v) prohibited rails for the boutique: virtual-asset transfers, bearer checks, cash couriers, and any unlabelled third-party payment channels that would compromise the audit trail.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards on CAD/USD and CAD/EUR, executed bilaterally with a Schedule I bank under standard ISDA/CSA or master FX agreements, to hedge known success-fee receivables and foreign-currency operating costs.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing emerging-market or restricted currencies (for example CLP, BRL, MXN, or ARS) where a cross-border M&A mandate involves target or buyer cash flows denominated in those currencies, with net settlement in CAD/USD through the bank's offshore FX desk.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (XCCY/CCS) aligned with deferred earn-out receivables or vendor-note liabilities in foreign currencies, fixing the LP's combined FX and interest-rate exposure under ISDA 2002 with CSA credit support.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM (conditional / ancillary use only). Legal constraints: (i) any non-resident electronic-money institution (EMI) operating in Canada as a money-services business must register with FINTRAC as an MSB under the PCMLTFA and, if active in Quebec, obtain an AMF permit under the Act respecting money-services businesses; (ii) foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or US-licensed platforms may be used by the LP solely as payment-collection agents for small advisory retainers, not as custodians of client escrow or settlement funds, because they lack CAD clearing through LVTS/Real-Time Rail and cannot provide documentary credit or secured escrow; (iii) the LP must disclose any EMI relationship to its primary banks and include aggregated EMI transfers in FINTRAC EFT reports once the CAD 10,000 threshold is crossed; (iv) reliance on foreign EMIs for material success-fee flows is incompatible with the statutory escrow and trust-account expectations of Canadian M&A counterparties and creates an adverse client-acceptance profile for the boutique.
