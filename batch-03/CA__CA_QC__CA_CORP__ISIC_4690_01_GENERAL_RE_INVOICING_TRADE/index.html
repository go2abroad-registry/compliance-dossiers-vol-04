Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) / Quebec Enterprise Registrar, Direction de l'immatriculation; supplemental federal registration with Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) only if the CBCA option is used.

Governing statute

Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA), CQLR c. S-31.1, ss. 9-41 (incorporation), ss. 99-147 (management of affairs), ss. 155-170 (by-laws and meetings); alternative federal regime: Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. The composite jurisdiction CA_QC selects the QBCA as the primary incorporating statute.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles de constitution / Articles of Incorporation filed electronically through the REQ Secure E-Services module (Dépôt d'actes); the filing must be accompanied by the name reservation certificate and the prescribed constitutive deposit fee, approximately CAD 250 (USD 190), inclusive of the registration entry and issuance of the first certificate of incorporation; the annual declaration duty is charged separately.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a QBCA or CBCA business corporation; the subscription price for common shares may be nominal, and no escrow account or capital blocking mechanism is mandated in Quebec for a general re-invoicing trade entity. A majority of the directors must be resident Canadians under QBCA s. 107; non-resident ownership is permitted but triggers enhanced banking AML/KYC information requirements, and any transfer restrictions must be stated in the articles.

Funding & unlock path: Office occupancy requires: (i) a fire safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the local fire prevention service, (ii) an occupancy permit (certificat d'occupation) from the municipality, and (iii) registration of the establishment with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) under the Act respecting occupational health and safety, CQLR c. S-2.1. Structural load inspection by a licensed engineer is required only if shelving, racks, or inventory storage is installed; compliance with the Quebec Building Code is mandatory for any leasehold improvements.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a QBCA or CBCA business corporation; the subscription price for common shares may be nominal, and no escrow account or capital blocking mechanism is mandated in Quebec for a general re-invoicing trade entity. A majority of the directors must be resident Canadians under QBCA s. 107; non-resident ownership is permitted but triggers enhanced banking AML/KYC information requirements, and any transfer restrictions must be stated in the articles.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable to a pure commercial re-invoicing intermediary under ISIC 4690 / NAICS 425120; no sectoral industrial licence is issued for wholesale trade intermediaries. Conditional authorities include: the local municipal planning department for a business establishment permit, the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ) for any conversion or structural works, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for an importer/exporter account if physical goods cross the border.

Category: No provincial operating licence is mandated for general re-invoicing operations; a municipal business establishment permit (permis de commerce et d'occupation) is required if a physical office or depot is maintained, and CNESST establishment registration applies where employees are engaged.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration, via the CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec (Agence du revenu du Québec) for Quebec corporate income tax and Quebec Sales Tax (QST) administration, via the CliquezAffaires / Mon dossier d'entreprise portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/); the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) independently issues the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act and register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for the federal Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN for federal tax program accounts (RT corporate tax, RC GST/HST, RP payroll), using Business Registration On-line (BRO) or CRA Form RC1; non-resident applicants use the CRA non-resident registration channel or Form RC171. Step 3: Register for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act once the legal registration threshold is met or when making taxable supplies in Canada. Step 4: Register for QST through Revenu Québec referencing the NEQ; the QST registration number is the NEQ plus a TQ program-account suffix. Step 5: Appoint a service agent in Quebec if required by the enterprise registrar, and maintain books and records at the registered office. Step 6: Validate the entity's banking and remittance identity by linking the Canadian dollar settlement account to the BN such that wire payments (SWIFT M/T, TT, XT-Transfer rails) can be reconciled electronically with GST/QST filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style elective check-the-box regime; a CBCA/BCA business corporation is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Substantive elections available to the re-invoicing hub include: (i) associated-corporations allocation election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s.256(2); (ii) initial fiscal-period election permitting a first taxation year of up to 53 weeks under ITA s.249.1; (iii) capital dividend account election under ITA s.83(2) using Form T2054; (iv) ITA s.216 net-basis election for non-resident recipients of Canadian rental or royalty income; (v) hybrid-mismatch elections under ITA s.258(7), with deduction-denial rules under ITA ss.18.4, 18.5 and 258 applying to reverse-hybrid and imported-mismatch structures; and (vi) the closely-related-corporations election under ETA s.238 and QSTA s.499 to treat intra-group supplies as non-taxable for GST/QST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies, tested over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter (small-supplier exemption under ETA s.148 and QSTA s.159.1). A re-invoicing hub whose goods are never delivered in Canada may make no Canadian supplies at all and therefore have no registration obligation; a hub holding inventory in Quebec must register when taxable Canadian supplies exceed the threshold. Non-residents soliciting orders in Canada may be deemed registrants irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, ETA where tangible personal property is exported from Canada as soon after the supply as is reasonable and the supplier retains documentary proof of export; it also applies where a non-resident holder of the goods (the offshore principal) instructs shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada and the hub acts solely as a disclosed agent or title intermediary. If the re-invoiced goods never physically enter Canada, the supply is outside the scope of GST/QST rather than zero-rated. The 0% rate must be claimed at invoicing time; if tax was charged in error, a credit note under ETA s.232 is required before input tax credit refunds are available on the related acquisition costs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System / CAED report or Form A666 for goods exceeding CAD 2,000)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or cargo manifest / truck carrier CIQI export report (land)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or TT wire proof of foreign currency repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and purchase-ordre chain evidencing the re-invoicing flow and legal title transfer
  • Packing list, customs broker summary, and non-resident consignee declaration
  • Signed zero-rated export-evidence register maintained by the hub, carrier and customs broker

No real-time fiscal clearance system, no digital stamping and no mandatory third-party tax clearance API is in force in Canada. Registrants must issue invoices containing the prescribed information set out in ETA s.221(1) and QSTA s.314 (supplier legal name, BN and QST number, invoice date, tax amounts, or a zero-rated notation for exports). Electronic return filing is mandatory for GST/HST and QST registration accounts through CRA NETFILE and Revenu Québec CliquezAffaires. Source records, including EDI or PEPPOL-format e-invoices, SWIFT MT100/MT103 wire messages and bank statements, must be retained for six years.

Export substantiation documents must be retained for six years (ETA s.286; ITA s.230). The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within four years of the return date (ETA s.298; ITA s.152(4)); the limitation period is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default. An overstated export refund or input tax credit claim attracts a 25% penalty under ETA s.286.1, increased to 50% for a repeat claim within five years, plus a 25% false-document penalty under ETA s.285.1 and, where income tax is affected, a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2). Erroneously refunded amounts are clawed back retroactively with statutory interest (ETA ss.295, 298). Criminal prosecution under ETA s.328 can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Revenu Québec applies mirror penalties and unlimited clawback under the Tax Administration Act (ss.57, 59, 520.1) for QST fraud. In a re-invoicing trade audit, the tax authority will require the hub to reconcile each SWIFT MT103, TT wire or XT-Forex settlement receipt to a specific export declaration and bill of lading; unreconciled foreign currency receipts are recharacterized as taxable Canadian sales, assessed GST/QST on the gross invoice value, and subjected to penalties and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and financial transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted current-account payments, including payment for imports, services, royalties, interest, dividends, and repatriation of lawful profits, subject to applicable tax withholding, corporate-law solvency requirements, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, contractual restrictions, and bank transaction monitoring. Capital-account transactions, foreign ownership, intercompany financing, acquisitions, securities activities, and derivatives are not subject to general exchange controls but may trigger beneficial-ownership, source-of-funds, sanctions, tax, transfer-pricing, securities, competition, or foreign-investment review. A Quebec corporation should maintain board-approved dividend or capital-return resolutions, evidence of distributable capacity, tax-clearance analysis, intercompany agreements, arm's-length pricing support, and payment records. Non-resident shareholders may generally receive dividends, interest, loan repayments, and other lawful distributions, subject to Canadian withholding tax and any treaty reduction. Canadian banks may request invoices, contracts, customs records, tax forms, ownership evidence, and economic-purpose explanations before releasing or converting funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the holder's discretion, subject to contractual banking terms, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, accounting requirements, tax reporting, customs documentation, and accurate revenue recognition. Financial institutions must comply with applicable suspicious-transaction, large-cash, electronic-funds-transfer, sanctions, and record-keeping obligations. Businesses should reconcile export invoices, shipping or delivery evidence, customs filings where applicable, foreign-currency balances, intercompany pricing, and tax reporting. The absence of surrender controls does not eliminate Canadian income-tax, GST/HST, transfer-pricing, customs-valuation, or beneficial-ownership obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting obligations; the Canada Border Services Agency and Canada Revenue Agency administer relevant customs, tax, trade-reporting, and transfer-pricing requirements. In Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers regulates applicable securities, derivatives, and financial-market activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and low to medium as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution may process lawful cross-border payments only within the permissions of its home-country licence and correspondent or partner-bank arrangements. It cannot automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, Canadian payment-account, trust, foreign-exchange, or money-services functionality. Canadian counterparties may reject offshore EMI accounts where the account holder, payer, beneficiary, underlying goods, or transaction purpose cannot be independently verified. Use of an offshore EMI does not remove obligations under Canadian anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, customs, transfer-pricing, record-retention, payment-transparency, or Quebec consumer and financial-market laws. The corporation should use a Canadian-chartered bank for core collections, payroll, tax payments, customer due diligence, and material trade settlements, with an EMI limited to documented ancillary corridors and subject to transaction limits, reconciliation, segregation, and contingency controls.

Defense evidence: Provider licence and regulatory status; ownership and management profile; correspondent-bank information; safeguarding or segregation terms; AML program summary; sanctions controls; transaction limits; settlement and chargeback terms; full funds-flow diagram; counterparty onboarding policy; reconciliation reports; and written explanation of why a Canadian bank is not being used for the relevant corridor.

Defense evidence: Certified ownership chain to each natural person exercising control or owning the relevant threshold; shareholder agreements; trust deeds and trustee or protector details where applicable; control-rights analysis; passports and residential-address evidence; corporate registry extracts; tax-residency self-certifications; politically exposed person screening; and signed beneficial-ownership certification.

Root cause: Foreign-exchange conversion or derivative request unsupported by commercial exposure.

Defense evidence: Underlying invoices and forecast exposures; currency and maturity schedule; hedging policy; board-approved limits; counterparty confirmations; hedge-accounting analysis where used; derivative suitability and appropriateness records; collateral and credit-support documentation; and evidence that the transaction is not speculative or intended to evade reporting or sanctions controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital through a Canadian-chartered bank account held in the corporation's legal name. Use a bank wire through SWIFT, or an approved domestic clearing route where the remitter and beneficiary institutions support it. The remitter should state the legally accurate purpose, such as equity subscription, shareholder capital contribution, intercompany loan, or trade-finance funding; Canada does not impose a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires. Do not label a loan as equity or a commercial payment as capital. Provide the bank with the executed subscription or loan agreement, board approvals, investor or lender ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and currency, repayment or dividend terms, tax analysis, and ultimate-beneficial-owner information before remittance. Confirm whether the bank requires an advance incoming-wire notice, foreign-currency account, correspondent-bank details, or supporting documents. Record the exchange rate, value date, fees, accounting treatment, and intercompany balance. Large electronic transfers, suspicious transactions, sanctions concerns, and other reportable events may be subject to Canadian regulatory reporting by the financial institution. Avoid personal accounts, unexplained third-party remitters, split payments, cash funding, and circular transfers.Use Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency operating accounts with dual authorization, beneficiary validation, invoice matching, sanctions screening, and transaction-level reconciliation. For ordinary supplier payments, use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor message, subject to the sending and receiving banks' current messaging formats. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a reputable bank, with documentary examination against the credit terms and applicable international rules such as UCP 600 where incorporated. Use documentary collections, open-account wires, or supply-chain finance only after counterparty and goods verification. Maintain purchase contracts, commercial invoices, transport documents, customs records, certificates, insurance documents, proof of delivery, and payment approvals. Canada has no general capital-control requirement to obtain central-bank approval for ordinary import payments, but sanctions, export controls, customs valuation, tax, anti-bribery, anti-money-laundering, and bank-risk controls remain applicable. Establish maker-checker controls, approved beneficiary master data, callback verification for changes, payment limits, positive pay or equivalent fraud controls, and daily liquidity reporting. XT or proprietary transfer labels should be used only where supported by the relevant bank or payment provider and should not replace legally accurate payment narratives or required traceability.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a Canadian bank or appropriately regulated dealer, supported by identified or reasonably forecast commercial exposures and documented counterparty credit limits.Non-deliverable forwards where permitted by the relevant dealer, applicable law, and transaction documentation. NDF settlement should be limited to genuine currency exposure, with benchmark, fixing, net-settlement, collateral, close-out, and reporting terms clearly documented.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for documented funding, liquidity, or currency exposures, subject to dealer eligibility, ISDA or equivalent documentation, collateral terms, valuation controls, and applicable derivatives reporting or clearing obligations.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and structured hedges only after assessing suitability, premium or collateral requirements, potential leveraged outcomes, accounting treatment, and applicable Quebec and Canadian derivatives rules.Spot foreign-exchange conversions for settlement and working-capital needs. Maintain a treasury policy prohibiting unsupported speculative positions, setting tenor and notional limits, requiring independent valuation, and requiring board or treasury-committee approval for material derivatives.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment operations and low to medium as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution may process lawful cross-border payments only within the permissions of its home-country licence and correspondent or partner-bank arrangements. It cannot automatically provide Canadian deposit-taking, Canadian payment-account, trust, foreign-exchange, or money-services functionality. Canadian counterparties may reject offshore EMI accounts where the account holder, payer, beneficiary, underlying goods, or transaction purpose cannot be independently verified. Use of an offshore EMI does not remove obligations under Canadian anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, customs, transfer-pricing, record-retention, payment-transparency, or Quebec consumer and financial-market laws. The corporation should use a Canadian-chartered bank for core collections, payroll, tax payments, customer due diligence, and material trade settlements, with an EMI limited to documented ancillary corridors and subject to transaction limits, reconciliation, segregation, and contingency controls.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general rate (federal Part I 15% + Quebec 11.5%); a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) resident in Quebec with active business income not exceeding CAD 500,000 may access the Small Business Deduction, producing a combined effective rate of approximately 12.2% (federal 9% + Quebec 3.2%).
