Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for partnerships; an Ontario LP is statutorily fiscally transparent. Relevant election mechanisms include: (i) a partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(4); (ii) a non-resident partner election under subsection 216(1) to file a Canadian Part I income tax return; (iii) election by a partnership to have tax allocations follow specially-allocated partnership accounting; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments under Bill C-27 amendments (sections 18.2 to 18.7) where an LP interest is used to create an offshore deduction/inclusion mismatch; and (v) GST/HST elections under section 179 of the Excise Tax Act for non-arm's-length services, and section 181 if the partnership elects to treat strategic recovered services as taxable for self-assessment purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000.00: the small-supplier threshold based on gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers of digital products and services, including EdTech subscription platforms, are required to register for GST/HST once their Canadian taxable-supply revenue exceeds this threshold (digital-economy registration regime effective July 1, 2021)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An Ontario Limited Partnership in the EdTech industry may claim 0% GST/HST on (i) digital advisory, tutoring, and automated language-education services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and not present in Canada; (ii) services that are not supplied in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada, per Schedule V, Part V, section 23 of the Excise Tax Act; and (iii) tangible goods exported from Canada as supported by documentary evidence. Digital subscription access delivered online to overseas consumers or foreign corporate customers qualifies for zero-rating if the place of supply is outside Canada and the services are not enjoyed, performed, or consumed in Canada.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canada Border Services Agency export reporting) for tangible goods
  • Commercial invoice describing the EdTech service, subscription period, and consideration
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier certificate of export for physical goods
  • Signed subscription agreement or online order record identifying the non-resident customer's legal address and place of consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange receipt evidencing remittance of subscription fees from foreign buyers to the partnership's Canadian bank account
  • Server access logs, IP-based geo-location data, or user records substantiating offshore service delivery and consumption

None: Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are accepted as valid business records if they are readable, accessible, and retained in a form that can be produced to the CRA. The CRA supports electronic data interchange and XML/PDF invoices, but there is no statutory e-invoicing clearance obligation at the federal level or in Ontario.

The CRA may disallow zero-rating if the partnership fails to provide sufficient evidence of export or non-resident supply, and will reassess GST/HST due plus interest and penalties. Under subsections 285.1(1) and 280.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act, false statements, carelessness, neglect, or wilful default can trigger penalties up to 25% of the tax sought to be avoided or of the refund claimed. Fraudulent zero-rating claims can restart the limitation period and may lead to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. Where an LP is the registrant, the partners and the partnership are jointly and severally liable for the net tax; clawback includes repayment of any input tax credits attributed to the zero-rated export and any unremitted GST/HST, plus statutory interest running from the date the return was due.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no exchange-control legislation; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Current and capital accounts are free from prior approval, including profit remittances, dividend distributions, and foreign capital repatriation. Repatriation is subject only to income tax withholding on payments to non-residents (Part XIII WHT), FINTRAC reporting of large EFTs and cash transactions over CAD 10,000, and bank compliance with sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement under Canadian law to surrender export proceeds or convert hard-currency receivables into CAD. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency bank accounts; no minimum conversion ratio is imposed. FINTRAC requires reports on outgoing/incoming EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more. Exporters should keep invoices, waybills, and customs declarations in their commercial record.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF supervisor; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are viable as a settlement layer only if registered with FINTRAC as a money service business (MSB) or operating through a Canadian-registered payment service provider partner. Ontario applies the Retail Payment Activities Act and NI 31-103 registration requirements to entities providing payment services in the province. A foreign EMI cannot act as a deposit-taking bank, but may be engaged as a technical processor for treasury and disbursement workflows. The LP should maintain primary settlement accounts with a Canadian Schedule I bank and connect EMIs as sub-limits or disbursement gateways, not as the sovereign treasury backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from non-resident limited partners should be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (foreign direct investment) or 'CGPI' (capital contributions), referencing the Ontario LP registration number and the LP's Canadian Business Number (BN). Canada imposes no exchange-control approval for inward capital, but the receiving bank will perform FINTRAC EFT reporting for all transactions at or above CAD 10,000. For each injection, provide the executed Limited Partnership Agreement addendum, capital call notice, LP contribution statement, and source-of-funds declaration. All CAD transactions of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate for a taxable corporate partner (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Ontario provincial corporate rate). The Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) itself is fiscally transparent: no entity-level federal or Ontario corporate income tax is assessed on the LP; each limited partner is taxed on its allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source business income. A non-resident corporate partner carrying on business through a Canadian permanent establishment (PE) may incur an additional 25% Part XIV branch tax on after-tax PE income, reduced by treaty to 5%/15%, producing a maximum combined incidence of approximately 28.25% before treaty relief.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding under ITA s.212(2) on taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident. Because the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent, LP distributions to non-resident limited partners are not dividends; they are Canadian-source business income allocations taxable under Part I. CRA administrative practice requires the general partner to withhold 25% of amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners as a payment on account of the partner's Part I liability (ITA s.226(2)). The withheld amount is remitted to CRA on NR4-related remittance schedules and may be refunded when the non-resident partner files a Canadian return and applies treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 Information Return must be filed within five months after the partnership's fiscal period end (e.g., June 30 for a December 31 fiscal year-end). Each partner's share is reported on T5013 slips issued to partners by the same deadline. Extensions are not automatically available; late filing triggers daily penalties.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed within six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. The balance-due date is the end of the second month after the year-end for most corporations, extended to the third month for a CCPC eligible for the SBD. Non-resident corporate partners with a PE in Canada must file a T2 even where treaty protection exempts Canadian income, within the same six-month rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000. Under ITA s.247(3) and CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R, a taxpayer is exempt from the transfer-pricing penalty (10% of the net adjustment) if the aggregate value of all transactions with each non-arm's-length non-resident does not exceed CA$1,000,000 for the taxation year. Canada does not prescribe formal OECD 'Master File'/'Local File' labels, but CRA requires contemporaneous documentation generally aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, proving comparability, selection of the most appropriate method, and arm's-length outcome. Country-by-Country (CbC) filing obligations arise when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue is CA$750,000,000 or more; the LP or its corporate general partner is responsible for reporting only if the group meets the threshold and Canada is the tax residence of the reporting entity.
