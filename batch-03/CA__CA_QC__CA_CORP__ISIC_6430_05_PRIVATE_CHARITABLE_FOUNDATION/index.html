Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) – Charities Directorate, including the CRA My Business Account and Charities Listing portals; Revenu Québec (RQ) for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec corporate filings, and charitable receipts recognition. Registration with Corporations Canada (federal incorporation) and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) is the upstream corporate registry step.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate as a corporation without share capital (or with share capital) under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; obtain the incorporation number from Corporations Canada or the REQ. Step 2: Apply for the federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; for the charity itself, submit Form T2050 'Application to Register a Charity under the Income Tax Act' to the CRA Charities Directorate, describing the corporation's exclusively charitable purposes and activities. Step 3: Upon approval, CRA issues the 15-digit charitable registration number (BN + 'RR' program account) and lists the foundation in the Charities Listing; the CRA may deem registration effective as of the application date or incorporation date under ITA s.248(2). Step 4: Register with Revenu Quebec for QST and Quebec source deductions using Form LM-1-V; RQ assigns the NEQ and QST registration number. Step 5: For GST/HST and QST, registration is mandatory unless the foundation qualifies as a 'small supplier' (gross revenue from taxable supplies, excluding donations and exempt financial services, below the threshold). Step 6: File the annual T3010 information return within six months of the fiscal year end, and the Quebec equivalent (e.g., Revenu Quebec's co-ordinated return) to preserve registration; update corporate registry data within 15 days in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with statutory exemption: a registered private charitable foundation is a taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act but is exempt from federal income tax under ITA s.149(1)(f) on all of its worldwide income, including investment returns, provided it meets the conditions in s.149.1(3) (disbursement quota compliance, no private benefit, qualified donee status). Territorial_Scope: resident corporation (incorporated in Canada) with worldwide taxation in theory and complete exemption in practice; for QST, a 'charitable person' under the Quebec Taxation Act with comparable exemption and rebate eligibility. The foundation is not a pass-through entity; the donation tax credit is granted to donors, not to the foundation, and entity-level taxes remain possible under ITA Part V (disbursement quota shortfall), Part X.2 (non-qualified investments), and s.188(1.1) on prohibited advantages.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election for entity classification. Available elections/designations for a private charitable foundation include: (i) an application election under ITA s.149.1(6.3) for a foundation that satisfies the governance and funding tests to be designated a 'public foundation', adopting more favorable disbursement-quota and advantage rules; (ii) the deemed-registration-date mechanism under ITA s.248(2), allowing receipts to be issued from the earlier of the application date and qualifying corporate date; (iii) elections respecting fiscal period and cash-basis accounting for GST/QST where Revenu Quebec and CRA agree (charities may elect simplified annual filing in certain cases); and (iv) the mandatory hybrid-mismatch rules of ITA s.18.2, s.18.21 and s.247A, which provide no elective relief and are relevant only if the foundation enters structured cross-border financing. For QST, Revenu Quebec recognizes CRA charitable registration, and no separate substantive entity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for ordinary suppliers, and CAD 50,000 for charities and public institutions, measured on annual gross revenue from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in the preceding fiscal year or four calendar quarters; donations, grants, gifts, and exempt supplies (notably financial services such as investment management) do not count toward the threshold. A charitable foundation whose only income is donations and investment returns will normally remain a small supplier and is not required to register for GST/HST or QST; registration is available voluntarily and is mandatory only when the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available only for genuine supplies for consideration made by the foundation; true donations and grants are outside the GST/QST system. Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V: (i) tangible personal property exported by the supplier to a destination outside Canada in the ordinary course of business and delivered to the purchaser outside Canada is zero-rated; (ii) a supply of a service supplied to a non-resident person is zero-rated where the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, the non-resident is not a GST registrant, and the service is not the right to use a copyright in Canada; (iii) a service performed wholly outside Canada is zero-rated; and (iv) the supply must be documented so that the CRA can verify that the consideration is for export rather than a domestic grant or a taxable Canadian supply. For a private charitable foundation, an administrative or investment-management fee charged to a foreign grantor may be zero-rated if a written contract establishes the supply-for-consideration, the recipient is a non-resident non-registrant, and no benefit accrues in Canada; however, CRA will scrutinize any recharacterization of gratuitous foreign grants as zero-rated fee income.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / G15) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency, indicating exporter, consignee, destination, commodity classification, and export date
  • Commercial invoice / pro forma invoice with full description, HS classification, unit value, currency, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or highway) showing port of loading in Canada and port of discharge outside Canada
  • Waybill or ACI eManifest / PARS cargo control document issued by the licensed carrier
  • Packing slip and transport contract evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Signed purchase order or service agreement with the non-resident recipient stating the supply and consideration
  • Proof of payment: bank wire instruction, foreign exchange record, and Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank letter) showing receipt of proceeds into the foundation's Canadian bank account
  • Certificate of non-residence or foreign tax registration documentation, and a written statement that the recipient is not registered for GST/HST/QST
  • For zero-rated services: detailed service delivery log, time records, delivery reports, and legal/accounting opinion confirming performance outside Canada and absence of Canadian consumption

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies in Canada or Quebec. The federal government and Quebec support the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. Revenu Quebec has announced a future phased mandate for business-to-business e-invoicing for large enterprises, proposed to begin in 2026, but as of the 2025 assessment year no mandatory e-invoicing or electronic clearance obligation applies to charitable foundations. Registered charities with gross revenue above CAD 25,000 must file the T3010 return electronically, and GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically through CRA NETFILE, Revenu Quebec's Clic Revenu portal, or approved software. Official donation receipts must be issued for gifts of CAD 10 or more and must be retained digitally or on paper for CRA and RQ audit.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, which is extended indefinitely where the return contains misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud. If an export zero-rating claim fails, the supply is taxed at 14.975% (GST 5% + QST 9.975%) with arrears interest, and any claimed input tax credits or public-service-body rebates are clawed back with interest. Penalties include: (i) ETA s.285(1) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated GST and overstated rebate/ITC (minimum CAD 100); (ii) ETA s.280(1) false-statement penalties; (iii) criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 for tax evasion, with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years; (iv) ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any unreported income should the charity's exemption be denied; (v) revocation of registered-charity status under ITA s.149.1(3), with deemed disposition of assets under s.149.1(7) and tax on the deemed proceeds; (vi) ITA s.188 director liability for knowingly participating in a violation; and (vii) corresponding QST penalties under the Quebec Taxation Act and the Tax Administration Act, including interest, penalties, and loss of Revenu Quebec charitable-recognition status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised, free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD) regime under the Currency Act and the Exchange Fund Act. Current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without central-bank or ministerial licences, approvals, or quantitative caps. No Canadian exchange-control law impedes profit, grant, or capital remittance by residents or non-residents. The only statutory FX overlay is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), which mandates FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, originator/beneficiary data retention, and compliance with UN, OFAC, and DFATL sanctions. For a Quebec private charitable foundation, the material remittance constraints are tax-governance constraints rather than FX constraints: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on outbound Canadian-source payments, the registered-charity regime under section 149.1 of the Income Tax Act (disbursement quota, prohibition on carrying on a business, change-of-purpose penalties), and the Canada Revenue Agency direction-and-control doctrine requiring the foundation to exercise active supervision over funds disbursed to foreign beneficiary organisations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline under the Currency Act or any foreign-exchange regulation. Export receipts and charitable donations may be retained in any currency. The applicable compliance obligation is FINTRAC reporting: every cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more (or its foreign-currency equivalent) must identify the originator, the beneficiary, and the purpose of payment in accordance with PCMLTFA and FATF Recommendation 16. The foundation should adopt a documentary 'charitable endowment contribution' purpose description for all inbound wires and retain supporting gift instruments in the CRA file for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) established under the Bank of Canada Act, with foreign-exchange administration oversight vested in the Minister of Finance and the Department of Finance Canada (Exchange Fund Account administered by the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent); prudential supervision of banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT transactional supervision is exercised by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Conditional / Low-Medium. The Bank Act and the OSFI regulatory perimeter reserve deposit-taking to Schedule I and Schedule II banks and to authorised foreign bank branches; a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) is not a deposit-taking institution in Canada and cannot lawfully hold the foundation's endowment as a primary treasury rail. A foreign EMI (EU e-money licensee, U.S. money transmitter, or UK EMI) may operate in Canada only as a money service business registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Holding the charitable endowment in an offshore EMI account also conflicts with CRA's requirement that a registered charity exercise direction and control over its funds. Feasibility rating: viable only for de minimis float and small one-off wire executions to foreign grantees (e.g., Wise Business or Payoneer funded from the Schedule I bank account), provided the EMI is FINTRAC-registered or applies equivalent FATF-compliant KYC and sanctions screening, no significant working balance is held offshore, and each payment carries a complete grant-file record. The primary operating and endowment accounts must remain with a Schedule I bank or with Desjardins.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All cross-border capital injections are to be routed as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers to the foundation's Canadian account at a Schedule I bank, citing the bank's SWIFT BIC (representative codes: National Bank of Canada BNDCCAMM, Royal Bank of Canada ROYCCAT2, Toronto-Dominion Bank TDOMCATTTOR, Bank of Montreal BOFMCAM2, Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce CIBCCATT, Bank of Nova Scotia NOSCCATT). The MT103 must carry full remittance information in the originator and remittance-information fields: donor legal name, source institution, and the characterisation 'Charitable endowment contribution' together with the foundation's CRA Business Number. USD-denominated injections should clear through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (e.g., BMO Harris, RBC New York clearing, TD U.S. subsidiary, CIBC New York agency, National Bank U.S. correspondent) with the appropriate USD cover leg. For transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, both the sending and beneficiary banks must retain originator and beneficiary identity data per PCMLTFA and FATF Recommendation 16. Internal control protocol: dual authorisation by two independent signatories not closely related to the foundation's key donors, posting against the executed gift or endowment agreement, and monthly reconciliation to bank statements and CRA T3010 contribution records.Outbound grant and procurement disbursements use four complementary rails: (i) cross-border grants via SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT gpi tracking, including the grant-agreement reference and purpose narrative 'Charitable grant disbursement'; (ii) domestic CAD payments through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the new Real-Time Rail (RTR), with small grants below CAD 3,000 via Interac e-Transfer subject to dual-step approval; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) and documentary collections, used only where the foundation contracts for research equipment, scientific services, or complex procurements from third-party suppliers requiring documentary trade settlement, with the LC issued by the Schedule I bank against a collateralised facility; and (iv) USD-denominated disbursements converted from CAD via a hedged forward or executed from a USD sweep account to minimise conversion spread. Every outbound payment is pre-screened against OFAC, UN, and DFATL sanctions lists, flagged in the CRA T3010 grant schedule, and supported by a grant agreement containing beneficiary undertakings and anti-terrorism covenants. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional / Low-Medium. The Bank Act and the OSFI regulatory perimeter reserve deposit-taking to Schedule I and Schedule II banks and to authorised foreign bank branches; a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) is not a deposit-taking institution in Canada and cannot lawfully hold the foundation's endowment as a primary treasury rail. A foreign EMI (EU e-money licensee, U.S. money transmitter, or UK EMI) may operate in Canada only as a money service business registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Holding the charitable endowment in an offshore EMI account also conflicts with CRA's requirement that a registered charity exercise direction and control over its funds. Feasibility rating: viable only for de minimis float and small one-off wire executions to foreign grantees (e.g., Wise Business or Payoneer funded from the Schedule I bank account), provided the EMI is FINTRAC-registered or applies equivalent FATF-compliant KYC and sanctions screening, no significant working balance is held offshore, and each payment carries a complete grant-file record. The primary operating and endowment accounts must remain with a Schedule I bank or with Desjardins.
