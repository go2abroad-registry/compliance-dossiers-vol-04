Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital or paid-up capital requirement applies. Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The limited partnership has no statutory board of directors or share capital. The general partner shall maintain the partnership books, beneficial-ownership records, accounting records, and statutory registers and may take all actions necessary to commence the partnership’s Québec fashion e-commerce operations.

Category: No sector-specific manufacturing or industrial operating permit ordinarily applies to an online apparel retailer. Municipal occupancy, zoning, signage, warehouse, and business permits may apply based on the premises and municipality.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html); Revenu Québec — mon dossier d'entreprise and CLIC portals (www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/). CRA administers the 5% federal GST and income tax; Revenu Québec administers the 9.975% Quebec Sales Tax (QST).

E-government filing portal

(1) File the Limited Partnership's declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). (2) Apply for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's BRO service (online or 1-800-959-5525), opening program accounts: RP (income tax), RT (GST/HST) and RC (payroll, if employees). (3) Register for QST by submitting form LM-101-V (Demande d'inscription) to Revenu Québec, which links the QST account (NEQ + 'TQ') to the BN; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold. (4) Non-resident-owned LP nuance: the LP registers through the same provincial path; each non-resident partner is reportable on T5013, and withholding on allocations/distributions to non-resident partners is made under Part XIII Income Tax Act via Form NR601 remittances unless the s. 216(1) election applies. CRA may require a mandated representative (Form RC59) for the GST/HST account, and Revenu Québec likewise requires a resident mandated representative for QST when the LP has no establishment in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ss. 96-103 Income Tax Act (Canada) and ss. 599-669 Taxation Act (Québec); no entity-level income tax is imposed, and each partner reports its allocable share of partnership income or loss. A limited partner's deductible losses are capped by the at-risk rules (ss. 96(2.1) ITA) and suspended for 'specified members' (ss. 249.1(1) ITA) who do not actively participate in the business. For GST/QST, the LP registers as a separate 'registrant' (s. 241 Excise Tax Act; ss. 33.1-42 of the QST Act), independent of partner status. A non-resident general partner is generally deemed to carry on business in Canada through the partnership's permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Available elections: (i) calendar-year fiscal-period election for partnerships under ss. 96(1) and 249.1 ITA; (ii) ss. 96(3) ITA election permitting a non-resident partner to file Part I tax on the partnership allocation; (iii) s. 216(1) ITA election for non-resident partners on rental income; (iv) hybrid-mismatch disallowances and elections under ss. 18.2/18.21 ITA and foreign-pool rules under ss. 94.1-94.3 ITA (relevant where US LLC members are present); (v) s. 21(1) ITA election on foreign-exchange gains/losses; (vi) s. 150.1 ITA early-filing election. No election permits GST/HST grouping of individual partners with the LP; however, closely related corporations and partnerships may elect for joint filing under s. 156 Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; there are no exchange-control permits, no prior-approval requirements for foreign direct investment, no restrictions on profit remittances, and no soft-capital repatriation hurdles for a Quebec société en commandite (Limited Partnership). Non-resident limited partners may contribute capital in any freely convertible currency and receive distributions without exchange-control consent. Remittance obligations arise solely under tax law: Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act R.S.C., 1985 (5th Supp.), c. 1 on amounts deemed paid/credited to non-resident partners (statutory 25%, often reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g., 15% dividends/article VI-like partnership provisions under the Canada-US treaty; 10% interest-like allocations), with NR301/NR302 forms and Revenu Québec remittances as applicable. Return-of-capital distributions to non-resident partners are free of withholding if documented against the partners' capital accounts. No balance-of-payments reporting is triggered at the corporate level except Statistics Canada's mandatory enterprise surveys and FINTRAC's international electronic-funds-transfer reports (threshold CAD $10,000 per 24-hour cycle).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement for export proceeds. The CA_LP may hold e-commerce export receivables denominated in CAD, USD, EUR, GBP, and other settlement currencies in any approved account, without any mandatory conversion ratio or remittance timeline. Receipts from Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL settlement cycles may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely. Where CAD funding is operationally required, conversion is performed at arm's-length interbank spot rates through the entity's designated treasury bank, subject only to normal risk-management governance and FINTRAC large-transaction recordkeeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada) holds statutory jurisdiction over exchange-rate policy under the Currency Act R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52 and the Bank of Canada Act R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates all federally chartered Schedule I deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act S.C. 1991, c. 46. The Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises provincially incorporated financial institutions in Quebec. Canada imposes no central-bank exchange-approval mechanism for cross-border capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium standalone viability; Strategic fit conditional. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category. Offshore EMIs cannot passport into Canada or obtain direct OSFI deposit-taking status. They may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or as unregulated payment intermediaries acting solely as sub-processors. For the CA_LP, foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used as auxiliary receiving/settlement rails for Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL waterfalls, provided (i) the EMI is duly registered with FINTRAC or partnered with a Canadian Schedule I bank under the Retail Payment Activities Act S.C. 2021, c. 23 (RPAA), and (ii) all balances are swept daily into the primary Quebec-based operational account to avoid PSP commingling, FINTRAC EFT reporting asymmetries, and audit-trace fragmentation. An EMI must never serve as the principal treasury depository or the sole conduit for non-resident partner capital contributions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner capital contributions must follow a standardized SWIFT MT103 wire protocol: Field 50K/F ordering customer equals the contributing limited partner's memorandum account; Field 59 beneficiary equals CA_LP at the Quebec bank with unique Capital Escrow account number; Field 70 remittance information must read 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – CAPITAL CALL #NN – [PARTNER NAME] – [LP UNITS ISSUED]' to permit unambiguous bank-side allocation. Canada does not mandate SWIFT purpose codes, but banks apply internal purpose-classification: the transaction should be annotated as 'Inward Non-Resident Investment in a Canadian Limited Partnership' for Statistics Canada / BPM6 direct-investment surveys. CAD-denominated injections settle domestically via LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) or SWIFT GPI with correspondent routing through the bank's New York/Canadian correspondents (e.g., RBC Royal Bank of Canada NY branch for USD, BMO Harris or TD Bank America for USD/CAD intercurrency). FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to international wire transfers ≥ CAD $10,000; the bank will file on behalf of the CA_LP. Verification records for Treasury: GP board resolution approving the capital call, partner wire instruction with signed Capital-Call Reply, source-of-funds evidence from the remitting partner, updated REQ capital registry, and a post-settlement capital-account reconciliation ledger.Supplier payments for fast-fashion apparel and footwear manufacturing use dual parallel rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers for orders below USD $250,000, settled in USD through the bank's US correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire) or in offshore CNY (CNH) via Hong Kong clearing; and (ii) Irrevocable Confirmed At-Sight Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LCs) for purchase orders above USD $250,000, issued through the Treasury desk of the primary Schedule I bank, with maturity structured toward supplier presentation of full original shipping documents (commercial invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading, certificate of inspection). The CA_LP will use its CAD operating account for domestic supplier settlements via LVTS or Interac e-Transfer, and its USD cross-border clearing account for garment-factory wires. Each payment must be backed by purchase order, supplier invoice, customs entry, and goods-receipt match. All outbound wires are subject to SWIFT GPI tracking, OFAC/UN sanctions screening, and FINTRAC EFT archiving. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue (or CAD 10 million in transactions with non-residents) to trigger mandatory documentation. The CRA requires a T3 form for certain cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. For treaty benefits, the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause in treaties may require economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they fail to deduct and remit source deductions. Civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess and impose penalties; the company may be deemed a sham, leading to full tax liability and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for negligence or fraudulent conduct, including civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to CAD 2 million for individuals and CAD 10 million for corporations; criminal penalties may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for fines if they knowingly participate in or authorize non-compliance; travel bans may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest; reassessment of income.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they knowingly participate in the non-compliance, including civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
