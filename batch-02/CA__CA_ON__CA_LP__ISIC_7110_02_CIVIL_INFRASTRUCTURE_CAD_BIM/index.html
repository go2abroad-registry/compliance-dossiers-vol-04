Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Ontario, the partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts) through My Business Account or by mail. The BN serves as the primary tax identifier for the partnership under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. If the partnership is required to collect Ontario HST (generally taxable supplies exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in Canada), a separate GST/HST program account under the same BN must be opened. The partnership then files annual T5013 (Return of Partnership Income) for income-tax information purposes; non-resident partners are allocated income items directly and may need their own Canadian tax identification number. Ontario does not maintain a separate corporate tax registration because Ontario corporate tax is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; a limited partnership is statutorily transparent for income tax purposes under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, and partners must include their share of income or loss. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate person; partners may designate one member as the representative partner for net tax reporting. Hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments may apply under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 for cross-border partnership arrangements to prevent double non-taxation. A partnership may also make a joint venture election under subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act for certain designated activities, but this is not a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada over either a single calendar quarter or a period of four consecutive calendar quarters; supplies made by a small supplier generally do not trigger HST registration unless the supplier is a non-resident carrying on business in Canada and exceeds this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of engineering drafting, Building Information Modeling (BIM), and structural CAD services by a Canadian partnership to a non-resident client may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act as a supply of a service to a non-resident person, provided the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, is not a personal-property service performed in Canada, and the service is acquired by the non-resident for consumption or use wholly outside Canada. Where the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and does not carry on a commercial activity in Canada, the provider must maintain documentary evidence to demonstrate the offshore destination and non-resident status.

  • Executed service agreement or contract identifying the non-resident client and describing the BIM/CAD deliverables
  • Commercial invoice denominated in CAD showing the partnership's GST/HST registration number and explicit 'zero-rated' or 'Export' notation
  • Bank remittance record or incoming wire transfer documentation (including Wise payment confirmation) showing foreign settlement proceeds
  • Non-resident status evidence, such as a foreign business registry extract, foreign VAT/GST registration certificate, or a signed declaration from the client
  • Confirmation that no physical goods are exported, thus eliminating any need for a Customs Export Declaration or Bill of Lading

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Invoicing is paper-based or electronic and must be supported by adequate records; returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE. Optional interoperability using the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (PEPPOL) e-invoicing framework is recognized, but there is no mandatory government-side clearance API or invoice mandate for CAD/BIM export services.

CRA may retroactively reverse zero-rating and reassess unpaid 13% HST plus statutory interest if the documentary evidence is incomplete or the non-resident status cannot be substantiated. Deliberate misrepresentation, false statements, or fraudulent evasion of GST/HST attracts civil penalties (including gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the net tax amount involved), monetary administrative penalties, and potential criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. In addition, the CRA may deny input tax credit claims or impose a 10% penalty on excessive refund claims under the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no statutory exchange-control impediments to moving capital in or out of Canada, repaying shareholder loans, or remitting profits from an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners. The only relevant leakages are tax-based: Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-residents (generally 15% under domestic law, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty), and transfer-pricing rules for connected-non-resident cross-border services. Inward foreign investment may trigger Investment Canada Act notification thresholds, but that is not an FX conversion approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None and not applicable. Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation, or conversion-ratio timelines. A CA_LP exporting BIM/CAD engineering services may retain export invoices in any freely convertible currency, hold proceeds in CAD or foreign-currency bank accounts, and choose the timing and method of conversion. No Bank of Canada approval is required for holding offshore currency balances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's central bank, responsible for monetary policy and financial system stability but not a foreign-exchange control authority. Canada maintains no exchange-control approval regime; FX-related compliance is administered through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF purposes and through Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low and restricted to ancillary payment rails. Canada does not recognize a European-style e-money institution (EMI) as a deposit-taking bank; an offshore EMI cannot provide the primary treasury account for an Ontario LP. Wise Business, or a similar cross-border fintech, may be used as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail under a Canadian Money Services Business (MSB) registration or as an authorized foreign-bank arrangement, and must be treated as uninsured e-money rather than a CDIC-insured bank deposit. The primary operating account and capital-escalation account should always sit with a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed through the Canadian banking system using SWIFT MT103 with exact beneficiary identification: Ontario LP (CA_LP), account with a Canadian Schedule I bank, and the bank's SWIFT BIC. CAD settlement will occur via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day finality; smaller CAD credits may clear through ACSS. Canada does not prescribe statutory wire purpose codes, but the bank's transaction-monitoring system will require a meaningful narrative such as 'Capital Contribution — CA_LP' plus a cross-reference to the LP agreement. In practice, the remitting bank may flag a purpose code from its own FCC/FX classification, typically 'C30 — Equity Investment' or 'C01 — Other Investment'. To avoid delay, provide the bank with the executed capital-call notice, source-of-funds memorandum, UBO certificates, and the contributors' KYC package before the first wire, and ensure the remitting originator account is in the same name as the LP's subscribed partner.Outbound settlement for vendor payments, salaries, and trade payables should be executed through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for correspondent transfers) for cross-border transactions, with invoice/contract references in field 70. For domestic Canadian disbursements, use Payments Canada's ACSS or EFT rails for low-value payments and LVTS for urgent high-value CAD wires. Where letters of credit support procurement of heavy civil-infrastructure equipment or software licences, a Commercial Letter of Credit governed by UCP 600 may be issued through the Tier-1 Canadian bank, with confirmation available through a correspondent bank; the reimbursement flow will be supported by SWIFT MT754/MT799/MT756 messages.
