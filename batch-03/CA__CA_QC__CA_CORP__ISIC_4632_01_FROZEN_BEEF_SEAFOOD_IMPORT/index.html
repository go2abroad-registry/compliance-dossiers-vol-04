Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election is available for Canadian corporations. Elective mechanisms include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election for qualifying SMEs (ETA s. 227 reference); (ii) QST simplified accounting method election; (iii) election under ETA s. 273 and analogous QST provisions for zero-rated intercompany supplies between closely related corporations; (iv) treaty-based classification of fiscally transparent foreign entities (e.g., US LLCs) under Canada-US Tax Convention Art. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold, Excise Tax Act s. 148; QST small-supplier threshold, Québec Taxation Act s. 402). Importers of taxable goods into Canada must pay GST/QST at the border regardless of registration status; registration is mandatory above the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/QST applies to tangible personal property (including frozen beef and seafood) exported by the supplier or under the supplier's direction, per ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 5 and the corresponding QST provisions. Services in respect of the exported goods or performed for non-residents may be zero-rated where they do not relate to Canadian real property (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7). The exporter must retain contemporaneous evidence of physical export, transfer of ownership, and payment to substantiate the claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or CARM e-Export Report)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent FX settlement proof)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE; Revenu Québec e-services). Import/export declarations are transmitted electronically to the CBSA through the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) platform, with border GST/QST assessed via integrated broker EDI messaging (ACI/ACKNACK).

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rating claims within four years, with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or gross negligence. Clawback of claimed refunds is accompanied by statutory interest. Penalties include: 25% of the net tax misstatement under Excise Tax Act s. 280(1); 50% gross-negligence penalty under Income Tax Act s. 163(2); criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 with fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment; and analogous QST penalties under the Québec Taxation Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and capital transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted inbound and outbound investment, dividend payments, interest payments, loan repayments, and trade settlements, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax withholding, corporate-law solvency requirements, contractual banking restrictions, and accurate underlying-transaction documentation. There is no routine approval requirement for a Canadian corporation to purchase or remit foreign currency. A Quebec corporation may receive foreign capital through its Canadian bank account, maintain foreign-currency accounts, and remit lawful profits to non-resident shareholders. Dividends must be declared in accordance with applicable corporate law, including solvency requirements, and non-resident withholding tax commonly applies at the Canadian domestic rate of 25 percent unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty. Interest, royalties, management fees, and other cross-border payments may also attract withholding tax and transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, deductibility, and treaty-entitlement review. The Importer of Record must maintain customs, tax, sanctions, origin, valuation, and food-import records for frozen beef and seafood. Canadian banks may require enhanced evidence for high-value related-party funding, unusual jurisdictions, cash-intensive activity, rapid fund movements, or transactions involving sanctioned parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No Canadian statutory rule requires exporters to repatriate or surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert a prescribed percentage of export receipts, or convert receipts within a prescribed statutory period. Export proceeds may generally remain in Canadian or foreign-currency accounts, subject to tax, accounting, sanctions, customs, contractual, and anti-money-laundering obligations. For this business model, which is primarily an importer and wholesale distributor, the principal FX-control issue is payment for imports rather than surrender of export proceeds. Banks may nevertheless impose contractual reporting, documentary, or transaction-monitoring requirements and may request invoices, bills of lading, customs entries, purchase orders, and beneficial-owner information.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls or mandatory conversion of commercial foreign-currency receipts. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing reporting requirements; the Canada Revenue Agency administers tax reporting and transfer-pricing requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment functionality and Low to Medium as a substitute for a principal Canadian operating bank. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide collection, foreign-exchange, and cross-border payout services only within the scope of its home-jurisdiction licence and the Canadian regulatory perimeter. An offshore provider does not automatically have authority to conduct deposit-taking, retail payment services, money services, or payment activities in Canada. The corporation should verify whether the provider is registered with FINTRAC where required, registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act regime if applicable and in force for its activities, permitted to hold client funds, and capable of satisfying Canadian sanctions, source-of-funds, recordkeeping, privacy, and payment-transparency requirements. Customer funds may not have the same deposit-insurance or creditor-protection status as deposits at a Canadian deposit-taking institution. Use of an offshore EMI should be limited to documented treasury corridors, with a Canadian bank retained for payroll, taxes, customs, supplier settlement, and contingency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a Canadian commercial bank to the corporation's designated Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency account. The remitter name should match the shareholder or disclosed funding entity, and the payment reference should identify the legal instrument, such as share subscription, paid-in capital, or shareholder loan. Before remittance, provide the bank with the executed financing documents, board approvals, capitalization or loan schedule, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, and expected amount and currency. Use the bank's accurate payment-purpose classification; Canada does not impose a universal statutory FX purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires, but banks require truthful internal purpose descriptions and may require a SWIFT purpose narrative, invoice reference, or loan and equity classification. Do not characterize a loan as equity or trade payment as an investment. Reconcile receipt to the general ledger, issue share certificates or record the loan as legally appropriate, retain SWIFT confirmation and bank statements, and review withholding-tax, thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, interest-deductibility, and shareholder-benefit implications before any onward payment. Large or unusual transfers may be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC under applicable reporting rules without that report indicating unlawful conduct.For ordinary supplier payments, use a Canadian bank foreign-currency account and a SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly represented by an MT103 message or its ISO 20022 successor, with the exact beneficiary legal name, account or IBAN where applicable, BIC, bank address, invoice number, purchase-order number, goods description, and shipment reference. For new, high-value, or higher-risk suppliers, use an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a Canadian bank, with compliant presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, certificate of origin, inspection or health documents, and insurance documents where required. A revolving letter of credit may support recurring frozen-food purchases if shipment periods, drawing limits, tolerances, expiry, sanctions wording, and discrepancy procedures are clearly defined. Use correspondent banks and payment corridors approved by the bank; screen all parties and vessels where relevant; avoid third-party payments unless documented and pre-approved; and reconcile customs values, invoices, landed costs, GST or HST, duties, freight, and inventory records. Supplier prepayments should be supported by a contract, invoice, advance-payment terms, counterparty due diligence, and evidence of subsequent shipment.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a Canadian bank or appropriately regulated dealer, used to hedge documented forecast or contracted USD, EUR, or other currency payables and receivables.
