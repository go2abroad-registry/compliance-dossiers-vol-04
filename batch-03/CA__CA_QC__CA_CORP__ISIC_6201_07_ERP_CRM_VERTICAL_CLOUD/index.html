Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

QBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital or authorized capital requirement; no escrow account or bank certificate is mandatory. A corporation may be incorporated without issued shares. Initial shares may be subscribed for nominal consideration and issued for money, property, or past services, as authorized by the board in the first resolution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification requires an evacuation plan, portable extinguishers, emergency lighting, alarm testing, and annual inspection by the local fire prevention service. Workplace clearance is obtained via CNESST verifying electrical safety, workspace ergonomics, assembly of first-aid kits, and the posting of occupational health and safety notices. These municipal, structural, and occupational clearances together constitute the required operating permits for the physical office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for the federal Business Number, GST/HST program account and Income Tax Act filings; portal: CRA 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/gst-hst.html). Revenu Québec is the competent authority for the Quebec Sales Tax, Quebec corporate income tax and required provincial tax registrations; portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/business/.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or Quebec obtains a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a two-letter GST/HST program account code (typically 'RT') by applying through CRA Business Registration Online, by telephone, or on Form RC1. Once the entity's taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD $30,000 small-supplier threshold, GST/HST registration and collection are mandatory. For Quebec, the same entity must register with Revenu Québec for QST using the online registration service or Form LM-1-V, and should obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number from the Registraire des entreprises if the corporation has a Quebec establishment. Supporting documents for a non-resident applicant generally include certified constitutional documents, evidence of the registered office and Canadian representative, list of directors, and a description of the ISIC 6201 vertical CRM cloud software activities. CRA online BN issuance is immediate; Revenu Québec QST account activation may take two to four weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian federal or provincial business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxable entity under Part I of the Income Tax Act; it is not pass-through eligible. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, which for Quebec includes allocation to Revenu Québec when the permanent establishment is in Quebec. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian federal or provincial business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxable entity under Part I of the Income Tax Act; it is not pass-through eligible. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, which for Quebec includes allocation to Revenu Québec when the permanent establishment is in Quebec. No tax-exempt zone regime applies to the vertical enterprise software and CRM cloud business.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box pass-through election; corporate classification follows corporate law and tax residence rules. Relevant statutory elections include (i) a fiscal period election under Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(4); (ii) the closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act section 156, and the corresponding QST election, to treat intercorporate supplies as made for nil consideration; (iii) voluntary GST/HST and QST registration before threshold; and (iv) treaty-based relief elections available through the competent authority where an entity is treated as resident in both Canada and another jurisdiction. Hybrid mismatch rules apply compulsorily and do not permit elective pass-through recharacterization; no election can make the Inc./Corp. transparent for Canadian corporate tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000. Both CRA and Revenu Québec apply the small-supplier threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Once the threshold is crossed, mandatory GST/HST and QST registration applies, and a single taxable supply may itself trigger registration obligations. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST and QST registrant may zero-rate (0%) export supplies where (a) goods are exported from Canada in commercial quantities by the supplier or on its behalf, or (b) services, including vertical CRM/SaaS subscription services, are supplied to a non-resident and are not consumed, performed, or exploited in Canada. Cloud CRM subscription fees paid by a non-resident customer located outside Canada are ordinarily zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST rules, provided the supply is not made to a person in Canada and is not used by a recipient located in Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (Form B13A or Canadian Export Reporting System electronic record), where export reporting is mandatory
  • Commercial invoice fully describing the goods or services and terms of sale
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or motor carrier waybill evidencing physical exportation
  • Packing list or shipping manifest for goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment/remittance receipt from the non-resident customer
  • Foreign customs import entry or destination-customs proof of arrival (where obtainable)

Canada and Quebec do not currently impose continuous transaction controls, real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoice clearance APIs, or digital stamping. GST/HST returns are filed through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or by authorized representatives; QST returns are filed electronically through Revenu Québec's CLICK/online services. Registrants must nevertheless maintain full invoice and transaction records in electronic form, support both B2B and export claims with underlying documentation, and, for Quebec, make business records and invoices available in French.

CRA and Revenu Québec treat export zero-rating as high-risk. If the complete export substantiation bundle, including payment evidence and foreign exchange repatriation records, is not available within the expected 180-day examination framework, the zero-rating is reversed and the full combined GST/HST and QST amount is assessed as tax due with retroactive effect plus compound prescribed interest. Penalties under Excise Tax Act section 285 and Quebec's Tax Administration Act include a minimum penalty of $100 plus 25% of the understated tax or overclaimed refund where a knowingly false statement or gross negligence is found, larger penalties for repeat misconduct, and criminal prosecution under section 327 for tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment of up to five years. Erroneous or fraudulent export refunds are clawed back, and director/officer liability can extend to unpaid assessed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with no general exchange controls. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, including profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repatriation. No Bank of Canada approval or government license is required for foreign direct investment, injection of equity capital, or cross-border borrowing by a Quebec business corporation. Inbound and outbound wire transfers are subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction, or twenty-four-hour aggregate), and regulated transactions must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder will remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any other authorized dealer, no mandatory timeline for the conversion of foreign currency receipts, and no compulsory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign currency in offshore or domestic accounts, use it for operational expenditures, hedge it, or convert it at arm's length market rates. For AML/CFT compliance, export transactions must be supported by commercial documentation such as contracts, bills of lading, customs declarations, and corresponding inbound wires.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Department of Finance Canada; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) passport; a non-resident EMI cannot directly hold deposits or settle in CAD through Payments Canada systems unless it obtains a Canadian banking licence, becomes a registered federal credit union/trust company, or partners with a Canadian clearing bank as an agent. A pure offshore EMI may be permissible as a treasury visibility account for foreign currency flows but must register as a money services business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA with FINTRAC, enrol with the provincial enterprise registrar in Quebec, and comply with Quebec's consumer protection and payment service rules. Deposit-taking by an unlicensed entity is prohibited under the Bank Act, and capital-protected escrow functions should remain with a prudentially regulated tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a foreign parent should be routed as SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 credit transfers with the beneficiary being the Quebec corporation's account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. The transaction must include accurate legal name and Canadian bank transit/branch numbers, a clear remittance information field referring to the share subscription agreement or shareholder loan agreement, and the beneficiary address. For balance-of-payments purposes, Canadian banks classify the payment to a business enterprise as a direct investment or intercompany capital transaction; the payer should avoid classifying a true equity injection as a services payment or 'trade settlement'. FINTRAC reporting is triggered on receipts of CAD 10,000 or more, and the bank will validate the corporate existence, the source-of-funds narrative, and if necessary a completed source-of-investment questionnaire. Directors should ensure the underlying transaction is supported by executed corporate resolutions, an updated shareholding register, and either a share certificate or an intercompany loan agreement.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements should occur over a Payments Canada-approved clearing rail. For CAD domestic payments, use Lynx (Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system) or ACSS for batch items. For cross-border payments, use SWIFT MT103/MT202 and correspond through the tier-1 bank's Canadian clearing branch. Commercial letters of credit should be issued in accordance with UCP600 and, where digital documents are submitted, eUCP; payment is typically effected via SWIFT MT754 and MT799/MT202, with the beneficiary bank receiving through its own clearing membership.
