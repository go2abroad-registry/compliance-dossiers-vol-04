Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; Revenu Québec — CliC SÉUR portal for Quebec provincial income tax, QST, and payroll obligations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership entering Quebec must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, either online or by completing Form RC348 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Account). Once the BN is issued, the partnership or its appointed representative must register for the GST/HST account. For Quebec provincial purposes, the same BN or a separate Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) must be obtained from Revenu Québec; the partnership must file Form LE-12 or register through CliC SÉUR. Registration for QST is required if the partnership makes taxable supplies in Quebec exceeding the small-supplier threshold or if it is a non-resident operating a commercial enterprise in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with income, gains, credits, and losses flowing through to partners; however, for GST/HST and QST the partnership itself is treated as a separate registrant for supplies made in its own right. No designated tax-exempt zone applies in Canada or Quebec.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. A limited partnership is structurally characterized as a pass-through under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act. Partnerships may make certain elections for computation and fiscal period purposes, including the election under subsection 249.1(4) to shorten a fiscal period, and may choose between the Accrual and Cash methods for GST/HST only if the small-supplier threshold is met. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 12.6 and 18.4 of the Income Tax Act may disallow deductions for certain payments under hybrid arrangements, but no entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar year in worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST small-supplier qualification; Quebec QST registration mirrors the CAD 30,000 threshold for taxable supplies having a place of supply in Quebec. Non-resident partnerships carrying on a business in Canada generally must register regardless of the threshold once they are not a small supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A limited partnership may claim the 0% GST/HST and 0% QST rate for qualifying exports of tangible personal property delivered to non-residents and removed from Canada, and for certain supplies of services performed for non-residents if the services are not taxable in Canada. The export must be supported by commercial invoices, shipping records, and evidence of export; for offshore services, the contract must clearly indicate an offshore recipient and direct benefit outside Canada. For Quebec QST, the same zero-rating principles apply, but a separate certificate of export may be required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A1 / Canada Customs Invoice or electronic equivalent through the Canadian International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill with proof of physical departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire confirmation evidencing the export sale proceeds from the foreign purchaser

Canada maintains no national real-time e-invoice clearance or fiscal stamping regime for GST/HST. Registrants must issue valid invoices containing prescribed information and retain all records in Canada or make them available electronically to CRA and Revenu Québec on demand. Electronic filing and remittance through CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CliC SÉUR is mandatory for registrants with annual revenues exceeding established thresholds. API-based EDI invoice exchange is permitted, but no government-operated pre-clearance or imprinting is required.

False or exaggerated export zero-rating claims are subject to audit by both the CRA and Revenu Québec. A denied claim results in immediate reassessment, repayment of the unremitted GST/HST or QST, interest compounding daily, and civil penalties under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act for repeated omission or misstatement. Willful fraud or intentional misrepresentation attracts criminal prosecution, fines of up to PDQ 200,000 or an amount equal to the tax evaded, and imprisonment up to five years. For QST, parallel enforcement powers under the Taxation Act and the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax apply, including penalties of up to 200% of the amount evaded in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member: both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible, and no exchange-control licence is required for investor subscriptions, IPO trust deposits, business-combination consideration, or profit/dividend remittances. Practical remittance hurdles: (i) FINTRAC reporting for every incoming/outgoing wire ≥ CAD 10,000; (ii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reduced) on interest, dividends, and certain management fees; (iii) AMF/TSX escrow-release conditions attached to SPAC trust funds; (iv) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review if the de-SPAC target exceeds statutory monetary thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for foreign-currency export receipts. Exporters may retain export proceeds in any currency, subject only to CBSA electronic export declarations for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 and to Income Tax Act record-keeping requirements. The SPAC is not an exporter; its USD IPO/trust proceeds are freely holdable in Canadian, US, or offshore clearing accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada has no single foreign-exchange administration—regulatory authority is shared among the Department of Finance Canada, FINTRAC (cross-border transaction reporting), OSFI (prudential supervision), Global Affairs Canada (economic sanctions), and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for Quebec securities and escrow matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary treasury or SPAC trust custodian; MEDIUM strictly as an ancillary payment-only corridor. Legal constraints: foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are classified as money services businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must register with FINTRAC before serving Canadian clients; they lack CDIC deposit insurance; they cannot act as a qualified custodian or licensed trustee for SPAC escrow/trust proceeds under AMF and TSX rules; and they cannot clear Canadian-dollar high-value payments over Lynx. Therefore, all trust-era funds must sit with a Canadian-resident Schedule I bank or a federally/Quebec-licensed trust company.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate in Quebec (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent, so this rate applies to corporate partners on their allocated Canadian-source income, not to the LP entity.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on dividends, deemed dividends, and certain distributions of Canadian portfolio income paid to non-resident persons. If the LP receives dividends from a Canadian corporation and distributes those amounts to a non-resident limited partner, the LP or general partner must withhold 25% unless treaty relief applies. Under the Canada–US Tax Convention, the reduced rate is 15% generally, and 5% where the beneficial owner is a US company owning 10% or more of the voting shares of the Canadian payor.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions With Non-Residents) must be filed by a partnership when the aggregate fair market value of specified transactions with non-arm's length non-resident persons exceeds C$1,000,000 in a fiscal period. Canada does not prescribe separate Monetary Local File or Master File thresholds; however, the CRA expects OECD-aligned contemporaneous documentation for all material cross-border transactions. Penalty protection requires complete, accurate, and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation even below the T106 threshold where an adjustment is assessed.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the type applied in zero-tax offshore jurisdictions. However, the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 of the Income Tax Act) and the parallel Quebec GAAR (s. 1079.2 of the Taxation Act) permit the CRA and Revenu Québec to disregard transactions or arrangements that lack real economic substance and whose sole purpose is to obtain a tax benefit. In addition, the 'sham' doctrine applies where parties present a transaction that is in fact different from its documented form. A SPAC limited partnership must demonstrate genuine commercial objectives, maintain substantive governance and records in Canada, hold its escrowed funds in a real Canadian trust or escrow account, and ensure its sponsor arrangements are arm's-length and commercially rational.
