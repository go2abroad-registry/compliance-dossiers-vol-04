Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Nominal share capital must be at least CHF 100,000 and paid up to at least 20% of the par value of each share, with an aggregate minimum of CHF 50,000 (Arts. 621 and 632 OR). Payments must be deposited in a Swiss bank and be freely disposable by the company before registration, evidenced by a bank liberation/confirmation certificate under Art. 633 OR (blocked account 'Sperrkonto' is customary).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance issued by the cantonal fire prevention authority (Feuerpolizei / VKF) evidencing compliance with fire-protection classifications and escape routes for the production workshop; structural and load inspections confirmed by the cantonal building inspectorate for machinery placement; occupational workplace clearance from the cantonal labor inspectorate (Arbeitsinspektorat) under Art. 82-84 of the Swiss Labour Act (Arbeitsgesetz, SR 822.11), including noise protection and fume-exhaust verification; compulsory occupational and non-occupational accident insurance contract with SUVA (UVG, SR 832.20), first-aid provision, and workstation certificates.

Paid-in capital rule: Nominal share capital must be at least CHF 100,000 and paid up to at least 20% of the par value of each share, with an aggregate minimum of CHF 50,000 (Arts. 621 and 632 OR). Payments must be deposited in a Swiss bank and be freely disposable by the company before registration, evidenced by a bank liberation/confirmation certificate under Art. 633 OR (blocked account 'Sperrkonto' is customary).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Aktiengesellschaft is a non-transparent legal entity taxed at the federal, cantonal and municipal levels; federal statutory corporate income tax is 8.5% (approximately 7.83% effective after deduction of federal tax), Zug combined effective profit tax is approximately 11.9%, and Zug capital tax is approximately 0.04%-0.05% on taxable equity; no US-style check-the-box or general pass-through election exists; shareholder distributions are subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35% unless paid from qualifying capital contribution reserves properly tracked in a separate reserve account.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) VAT flat-rate method (Saldosteuersatz) election under Art. 34 VAT Act for Swiss businesses with turnover up to approximately CHF 5 million, simplifying export refund substantiation; (2) Art. 11 VAT Act option to waive the small-business exemption and register voluntarily for VAT; (3) STAF 2020 tax reform elections at cantonal level, including the patent-box deduction (relief up to 90% of qualifying IP income) and the notional interest deduction on excess equity; (4) Swiss Merger Act election to preserve the capital contribution reserve account at book value so that returns of paid-in capital can be made free of 35% Swiss withholding tax; (5) VAT grouping election under Art. 20 VAT Act for related Swiss companies, not relevant for a standalone AG; Switzerland provides no hybrid mismatch or check-the-box election, and the Swiss AG is always a separate corporate taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 VAT Act as amended from 1 Jan 2025; previous threshold CHF 100,000). The Swiss-resident CH_AG must register mandatorily when this worldwide threshold is exceeded; below it, VAT registration is optional. A foreign seller without a Swiss establishment is generally required to register only for Swiss-sourced supplies not benefiting from the B2B reverse charge; the CH_AG is Swiss-resident and therefore applies the CHF 250,000 domestic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% export VAT rate applies under Art. 23(2) VAT Act where hard enamel lapel pins are physically exported by the CH_AG or by a third party on its behalf, title and risk pass to the foreign customer abroad, and the goods leave the Swiss customs territory and remain abroad; the export evidence must be in the records when the VAT return is filed. Services directly connected to export goods, including die-struck brass processing, cloisonné enamelling and polishing work performed on movable goods for a foreign principal, are equally zero-rated if the processed goods are subsequently exported. For Etsy/Stripe cross-border sales, zero-rating is substantiated by despatch abroad via Swiss Post, DHL or FedEx with CN22/CN23 customs declarations, the foreign shipping address and the marketplace/bank settlement showing the non-Swiss buyer; supplies delivered to a Swiss address remain taxable at the standard 8.1% rate.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (electronic exit summary via the Swiss customs platform or postal CN22/CN23 customs declaration attached to the consignment as formal export evidence)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight) or postal/courier consignment note with tracking (Swiss Post, DHL, FedEx) evidencing despatch from Switzerland and delivery to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent settlement proof, being Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe payout statements, foreign-currency SWIFT/SEPA credit advices and the foreign buyer's payment and shipping details
  • Commercial invoice expressly stating 'Export - VAT 0%' and including the ISIC 3240 product description, quantity, unit price, Incoterms and destination country
  • Carrier confirmation or delivery receipt showing physical exit of the goods from Swiss customs territory

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance system, no continuous transaction control (CTC), no digital stamping, and no state gateway API for invoice pre-clearance; e-invoicing is voluntary in the B2B/B2C segment but standardised through the eBill/Peppol network and becomes mandatory for suppliers invoicing the Swiss Federal Administration (B2G) in 2026; every VAT invoice must meet Art. 26 VAT Act content requirements (supplier name/address/UID, recipient name/address, date, description of goods, consideration, VAT rate and VAT amount, and for exports the statement of 0% VAT); the Swiss QR-bill with QR-IBAN is the standard payment-invoice format for Swiss payers; Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe settlement records must be preserved as auxiliary evidence of export sales.

The ESTV may review export zero-rating within the ordinary 5-year reassessment period, extended to 10 years where tax evasion is suspected (Art. 42 VAT Act). If the mandatory documentation bundle is incomplete and export evidence is not produced within the accounting period (with the 180-day settlement benchmark applied to final payment evidence), the export exemption is retrospectively reversed, all related input VAT deductions are clawed back, and the VAT deficiency is assessed together with default interest at the ESTV-applicable rate (currently 3.5% per annum). Intentional evasion to obtain a fraudulent export zero-rating carries a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; negligent evasion carries a fine of up to one time the tax evaded (Art. 99 VAT Act); procedural violations such as non-compliant invoices or late registration are fined up to CHF 1,000 per offence (Art. 98 VAT Act). Export documents must be retained for 10 years (Art. 958f Swiss Code of Obligations); Swiss law does not formally require an inward remittance certificate, but payment and settlement evidence must be retained and the burden of proof shifts to the exporter where records are missing or inconsistent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible under both current and capital account headings as codified in the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and the IMF Article VIII. No exchange control restrictions apply to profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Dividend remittances are, however, subject to a 35% Swiss withholding tax unless reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty, with the recipient being required to submit a tax residence certificate and Form 823 to obtain treaty benefits. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted for inward and outward investment, and there are no currency surrender or conversion mandates for non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore accounts without any SNB reporting requirement, apart from standard statistical balance-of-payments reporting applicable to transactions exceeding certain thresholds. Therefore, the statutory surrender ratio is 0% (not applicable) and there is no deadline for conversion into CHF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). An offshore EMI not holding a Swiss banking licence or FINMA-recognised passport cannot lawfully operate as a payment institution in Switzerland or hold client funds in the form of Swiss e-money. For a Swiss AG processing marketplace settlements via Stripe or Etsy, the use of a regulated Payment Service Provider (PSP) under FINMA supervision is preferred. Offshore EMIs may support payout aggregation from marketplaces but present KYC/AML concentration risks and are not viable as a primary treasury rail for maintaining capital escrow funds. Legal constraints include article 1 of the Swiss Banking Act (BankG), requiring a licence for deposit-taking, and the absence of EU/EEA passporting rights for Swiss entities using an EMI domiciled outside Switzerland.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder loans and equity injections must comply with the Swiss anti-money laundering framework, which requires a bank to declare the origin of funds and to file a payment purpose form. A compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated injections) with a clear reference to 'Kapitaleinlage' (Capital Contribution) or 'Aktionärsdarlehen' (Shareholder Loan). The underlying corporate documents must evidence the capital increase or loan agreement, together with a notarised board resolution and, if applicable, the commercial register notification. Higher-value and more complex shipments support the use of commercial letters of credit (documentary credits governed by ICC UCP 600), with issuing bank guaranteeing payment against presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, packing list, certificate of origin, and quality inspection certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). An offshore EMI not holding a Swiss banking licence or FINMA-recognised passport cannot lawfully operate as a payment institution in Switzerland or hold client funds in the form of Swiss e-money. For a Swiss AG processing marketplace settlements via Stripe or Etsy, the use of a regulated Payment Service Provider (PSP) under FINMA supervision is preferred. Offshore EMIs may support payout aggregation from marketplaces but present KYC/AML concentration risks and are not viable as a primary treasury rail for maintaining capital escrow funds. Legal constraints include article 1 of the Swiss Banking Act (BankG), requiring a licence for deposit-taking, and the absence of EU/EEA passporting rights for Swiss entities using an EMI domiciled outside Switzerland.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal corporate income tax, plus cantonal and communal tax (combined effective rate approx. 11.9% in Canton Zug and approx. 21% in Canton Zurich)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on all dividends distributed by the AG; reduced to 0%-15% under applicable double tax treaties (e.g., 5% on dividends paid to a Chinese corporate shareholder holding at least 25% under the CH-CN DTA; 0% on substantial holdings under selected treaties)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss TP documentation duty (Art. 58a DBG and the Swiss TP Documentation Ordinance) applies where the Swiss entity's annual net revenue reaches CHF 80 million in two consecutive business years, or where material cross-border related-party transactions require contemporaneous records; multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CHF 900 million or more must file an OECD Country-by-Country report with the ESTV.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a de facto economic substance requirement through the place-of-effective-management rule (Art. 50 DBG) and the prohibition on sham transactions (Art. 126 DBG): the enamel pin AG must maintain an office, personnel, Swiss bank accounts, board decisions, accounting records and commercial functions in Switzerland. Letterbox companies are denied treaty benefits and 35% dividend WHT relief, and their income may be re-attributed to the ultimate beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Evasion: fine up to the amount of the evaded tax plus default interest under Art. 175 DBG; qualified tax fraud with falsified records: imprisonment up to three years and/or fine up to three times the evaded tax under Art. 186 DBG; additional cantonal fines in Zug

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are criminally liable as perpetrators; civil restitution actions under Art. 754 CO for the company's tax damage; personal asset seizures and travel bans in mutual assistance proceedings
Shell Company / Substantive Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the sham structure under Art. 126 DBG; denial of tax residence status, DTA treaty relief and WHT refunds; 35% Verrechnungssteuer assessed on deemed distributions; retroactive taxation with default interest and administrative fines

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing for unpaid taxes and WHT amounts; directors personally liable for deficiencies realized in liquidation; criminal charges for obtaining treaty benefits by false declarations; possible disqualification from Swiss board positions
AML/KYC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA) administrative fines up to CHF 500,000; qualified money laundering under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to five years and/or fine; forfeiture of illicit funds

Director personal liability: Director appointed as money-laundering reporting officer faces direct criminal liability; personal fines and imprisonment; civil compensation to the company for reputational and regulatory losses
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Upward tax reassessment to the arm's length profit; disallowance of excess royalty and interest deductions; recharacterization of excess payments as constructive dividends subject to 35% WHT; TP documentation fines up to CHF 10,000 per offence

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability of approving directors for reassessed taxes under Art. 754 CO; criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion; freezing orders on personal assets in aggravated cases
