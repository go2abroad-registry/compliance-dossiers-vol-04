Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation and the Articles of Association (Gründungsurkunde und Statuten), notified to the Commercial Register via the official e-Register filing form R01, followed by publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000 fully subscribed (Art. 621 OR). At incorporation at least 20% of the nominal capital and not less than CHF 20,000 must be paid in cash; any issue premium (agio) must be paid in full. The paid-in funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account in the name of the company in formation with a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank, and the bank deposit certificate must be produced to the notary and the Commercial Register; funds are released only upon presentation of the final Commercial Register excerption (Handelsregisterauszug) confirming registration.

Funding & unlock path: Cantonal Fire Police (Feuerpolizei) fire-safety certificate confirming compliance with VKF / AEAI fire-protection directives, including fire compartmentalization, sprinkler or gas suppression design for the pharmaceutical high-bay frozen areas, and evacuation signage. Structural load inspection and static certificate issued by the building inspectorate for the high-rack refrigerated warehouse, verifying pallet-rack seismic load and stored-floor load capacity. Workplace clearance from SUVA (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund) and the cantonal labour inspectorate confirming safe entry / exit for sub-zero personnel zones, thermal work-rule conformity (EN ISO / temperature-workplace ordinance), emergency rescue plan for locked cold-storage cells, and the required safety officers' signage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) in Bern; official VAT and customs portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT e-filing via ESTV e-services platform (Mein ESTV / AFC en ligne).

E-government filing portal

Foreign corporations performing taxable supplies in Switzerland, including cold-storage warehousing at Swiss premises, must register for Swiss VAT before commencing activity. A non-established entity must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (Art. 73 VAT Act) and file the registration application (official form or ESTV e-services) indicating expected Swiss turnover and foreign tax identifier; the ESTV then issues the Swiss business identifier (UID) and the VAT number (MWST-Nr / Numéro TVA). Registration is mandatory where net taxable Swiss turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per calendar year (Art. 10 VAT Act). A Swiss-resident Aktiengesellschaft is automatically enrolled via the commercial registry and receives the UID, which serves as the statutory tax identification number for corporate and VAT purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax (8.5% statutory on profit after federal tax; aggregate federal-cantonal effective rate typically 11-21% depending on canton) plus cantonal/communal profit tax and capital tax at its registered seat (e.g. Zug or Zurich). The AG is taxed at entity level with no pass-through eligibility. Foreign income of a resident AG is generally subject to Swiss taxation on worldwide income, with relief granted for foreign permanent establishments under double taxation treaties and cantonal application of the territorial exemption method. — Foreign corporations performing taxable supplies in Switzerland, including cold-storage warehousing at Swiss premises, must register for Swiss VAT before commencing activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax (8.5% statutory on profit after federal tax; aggregate federal-cantonal effective rate typically 11-21% depending on canton) plus cantonal/communal profit tax and capital tax at its registered seat (e.g. Zug or Zurich). The AG is taxed at entity level with no pass-through eligibility. Foreign income of a resident AG is generally subject to Swiss taxation on worldwide income, with relief granted for foreign permanent establishments under double taxation treaties and cantonal application of the territorial exemption method.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. Available mechanisms include: (i) Swiss group taxation / fiscal unity election for profit and capital taxes under Art. 9 Tax Harmonisation Act, generally available for qualifying 100% group structures from tax year 2025/2026; (ii) participation relief election exempting qualifying income and capital gains from substantial shareholdings (Art. 69 DBG); (iii) deemed liquidation / step-up election upon cross-border migration; (iv) VAT option to tax otherwise exempt supplies such as real property and certain financial services (Arts. 21-22 VAT Act); and (v) hybrid mismatch rules implementing OECD BEPS Action 2, which operate as mandatory disallowances rather than elective mechanisms for the AG and its foreign related parties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual net taxable turnover from supplies in Switzerland (Art. 10 VAT Act). Cold-storage warehousing is a service supplied where the goods are physically located (Art. 8 VAT Act), so a Swiss AG operating cold-chain facilities always has a Swiss VAT nexus. Voluntary registration is allowed below CHF 100,000 where the operator supplies taxable services. Non-established foreign suppliers of electronically supplied services face mandatory registration from the first franc in certain consumer B2C scenarios under ESTV administrative practice.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% with full input VAT deduction applies to: (1) exports of goods where the seller physically transports the goods out of Swiss customs territory and holds valid customs export proof (e-dec / Ausfuhrschein); (2) services directly and specifically connected to the export or import of goods (Art. 23(2)(c) VAT Act), including transport, handling, freight, and for a cold-chain operator, warehousing/storage services rendered in Switzerland for goods destined for export to an overseas principal, provided the direct link to the export transaction is documented; (3) services performed on movable goods (such as repackaging or temperature monitoring) for a foreign customer where the goods are thereafter exported from Switzerland (Art. 23(3) VAT Act); and (4) supplies into customs warehousing under duty suspension (Zolllager). Purely domestic storage for a Swiss-based customer remains taxable at 8.1% at the place of performance. Export must occur through Swiss customs territory, transport documents must evidence exit abroad, and the supplier must retain all proof for a minimum of ten years.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (e-dec) with exit confirmation and unique customs transaction reference (CRC / Ausfuhrschein)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list with Incoterms 2020 terms and legal exporter entity names
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, CMR consignment note, or air waybill (AWB) evidencing physical exit from Swiss customs territory
  • Customs transit document (T1/T2) or Swiss customs warehouse withdrawal form where goods move through bonded logistics
  • Foreign destination customs import declaration of the buyer jurisdiction (where available)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT MT103, or bank statement evidencing actual receipt of foreign currency consideration from the overseas customer
  • Signed warehouse custody and temperature-monitoring records linking the exported pharmaceutical lot to the storage services

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API clearance, or digital stamping regime. Since 30 June 2020, the QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the mandatory payment-slip format in Swiss payment processing. B2B e-invoicing follows Swiss implementation guidelines (eBill, eCH-0113 XML/UBL) but remains voluntary; paper invoices are lawful. All invoices, including zero-rated export invoices, must satisfy the statutory content requirements of Art. 81 VAT Act (supplier name and address, UID/MWST number, date, description of supply, consideration, and VAT rate including 0%) and must be retained for ten years.

Swiss VAT is self-assessed and the ESTV may audit retrospectively for up to five years, with a ten-year period in cases of fraud. Where zero-rating is claimed without substantiated customs or remittance evidence, the tax authority retroactively recharacterises the supply as taxable at 8.1%, issues a tax reassessment (Nachbelastung) with arrears interest (approximately 4.5-5% per annum), and may impose administrative penalties under Arts. 84-86 VAT Act: intentional tax evasion carries a fine of up to four times the evaded tax and criminal prosecution of responsible officers; negligence and record-keeping violations carry fines of up to CHF 8,000 per breach. Export proof must be credible and complete generally within 180 days of invoicing or foreign-currency remittance; missing or fraudulent FX repatriation evidence can trigger full clawback, denial of input VAT recovery, and interest. Repeated qualified VAT fraud is prosecuted under the Swiss Criminal Code and may result in imprisonment; the burden of proving eligibility for zero-rating rests entirely on the taxable person.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible under a free-float regime. Switzerland imposes no exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions, including capital injections, trade settlements, or dividend repatriation. Capital imports and exports are unrestricted and require only statistical reporting for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance is subject to the Swiss dividend withholding tax of 35% at source, with full or partial refund available under domestic participation relief or an applicable double taxation treaty, provided the beneficial owner supplies the requisite Swiss Form 82 / R-request and proof of tax residence. There are no prior-authorization requirements, surrender obligations, or conversion queues for corporate treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland does not impose statutory surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or fixed repatriation timelines for export receipts. A Swiss AG engaged in refrigerated logistics may retain export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely. Export invoices must be settled through the corporate operational account, and individual transactions above CHF 5 million or unusual cross-border flows may be subject to the SNB's mandatory balance-of-payments reporting thresholds. No foreign-exchange approval is required; the sole obligations are accurate accounting and compliance with the Swiss Federal Act on Monetary and Payment Instruments and the SNB's statistical reporting directives for BoP and international investment position surveys.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are of Medium feasibility for this treasury blueprint. A foreign EMI domiciled in the EU/EEA or UK cannot passport its e-money licence into Switzerland; nor does a Crypto Valley-based fintech licence authorise commercial deposit-taking beyond the CHF 100 million threshold without full banking authorisation. Consequently, an offshore EMI may serve only as a low-balance collection corridor or payment initiation layer, with settlement ultimately routed through a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank or a Swiss fintech bank holding a banking licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act. Pure foreign EMI accounts are not suitable for the capital escrow, customs-shielded liquidity reserves, or ISIC 5210 trade disbursement functions. A Swiss-registered, FINMA-licensed financial technology company (e.g., an entity holding a fintech banking licence) is the only viable direct EMI route, and it must be fully integrated into the group's KYC/AML framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for the CHF capital injection relies on the SIC/eSIC system for domestic transfers or a SWIFT MT103 for international wire arrivals. The shareholder must remit from its own account in its home jurisdiction to the beneficiary account at the Swiss Tier-1 bank, referencing the Swiss notarial deed date and the purpose code for capital contribution (OECD's capital-liability classification, typically category 3B12 or the SNB's statistical code for 'Beteiligungskapital' / direct investment equity). The bank will verify that the beneficiary account reflects the exact corporate name, that the remitter is the registered shareholder, and that no deferred-payment or loan structure is concealed. The bank must receive the notarised minutes of the capital increase and the confirmation of the bank's own account-opening documentation before accepting post-incorporation contributions. The SNB requires the receiving bank to report the transaction for balance-of-payments statistics; no exchange-control approval is needed. Funds arriving in foreign currency may be converted into CHF at the client's discretion or held in a corresponding nostro-based foreign-currency sub-account; the credit must not be split into artificial tranches below CHF 10,000 to avoid structuring suspicion.Outbound trade disbursements for the cold-storage and refrigerated logistics operations must be cleared via (i) SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border vendor, energy-trading and tolling payments denominated in foreign currency; (ii) the SIC system for domestic CHF obligations; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EU counterparties; and (iv) a documentary collection or irrevocable commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) for large refrigerated pharmaceutical shipments requiring the presentation of temperature-log evidence, customs documents, and insurance certificates. ACH_Direct is acceptable only for recurring low-value domestic-esque USD flows processed through the Swiss bank's US clearing partner, not for primary trade settlements. All beneficiary names must exactly match KYC records; invoices must be retained to substantiate the commercial underlying of the transfer; transactions exceeding CHF 50,000 must be encoded with the correct payment purpose description (e.g., 'payment for storage and handling services under ISIC 5210'); and the bank's statutory reporting must be discharged without delays. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are of Medium feasibility for this treasury blueprint. A foreign EMI domiciled in the EU/EEA or UK cannot passport its e-money licence into Switzerland; nor does a Crypto Valley-based fintech licence authorise commercial deposit-taking beyond the CHF 100 million threshold without full banking authorisation. Consequently, an offshore EMI may serve only as a low-balance collection corridor or payment initiation layer, with settlement ultimately routed through a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank or a Swiss fintech bank holding a banking licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act. Pure foreign EMI accounts are not suitable for the capital escrow, customs-shielded liquidity reserves, or ISIC 5210 trade disbursement functions. A Swiss-registered, FINMA-licensed financial technology company (e.g., an entity holding a fintech banking licence) is the only viable direct EMI route, and it must be fully integrated into the group's KYC/AML framework.
