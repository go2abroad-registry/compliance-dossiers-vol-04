Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — national tax administration; e-portals: CRA My Business Account, CRA BizReg, and GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario provincial tax compliance is administered through the Ontario Ministry of Finance and the Ontario Business Registry, but the CRA administers federal and Ontario HST and Ontario corporate income tax on behalf of Ontario.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation ('CA_CORP'), the statutory tax identification number is the CRA Business Number (BN), automatically issued after articles of incorporation are filed with Corporations Canada or through the Ontario Business Registry. The GST/HST program account (RT) must be activated separately under the same BN via CRA BizReg (online), Form RC1F (paper), or during registration by an accountant. A non-resident foreign entity acting as a GP may file Form RC1F directly with the CRA to obtain a BN and must register for GST/HST if it is not a small supplier or is carrying on business in Canada; Ontario registration is required through the Ontario Business Registry if the non-resident has a permanent establishment in Ontario.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a standard Inc./Corp. is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Ontario corporate income tax; no pass-through classification is available for a standard CA_CORP entity) — For a Canadian federal/provincial business corporation ('CA_CORP'), the statutory tax identification number is the CRA Business Number (BN), automatically issued after articles of incorporation are filed with Corporations Canada or through the Ontario Business Registry. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a standard Inc./Corp. is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Ontario corporate income tax; no pass-through classification is available for a standard CA_CORP entity)

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box regime for entity classification; corporate status is non-elective. Available statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST closely-related corporations group election under ETA s.156; (ii) GST/HST joint venture election under ETA s.273; (iii) certain non-resident reporting elections for withholding and remittance; and (iv) elections pursuant to hybrid mismatch arrangements may be constrained because ITA ss.18.2/18.3 apply mandatorily with no taxpayer-driven entity recharacterization election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold) for GST/HST registration; for non-residents carrying on business in Canada, the threshold is referenced to Canadian-source taxable supplies unless the non-resident elects to register. Ontario HST is payable on taxable supplies made in Ontario.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered exporter may zero-rate goods exported by the exporter if the export meets the conditions of ETA Schedule VI and the exporter retains sufficient evidence. Services exported to a non-resident may be zero-rated under ETA s.7/Schedule VI Part V when the recipient is a non-resident not registered under GST/HST and the service is not in connection with real property in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada when performed, or another excluded service. For a Venture Capital GP management entity, management and advisory fees supplied to a non-resident offshore fund may qualify for GST/HST zero-rating if those statutory conditions are satisfied; if the services relate to Canadian-resident portfolio companies or are received by a Canadian resident, HST at 13% generally applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / Canadian Export Declaration; CAED for automated declarations)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Carrier export proof
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / equivalent foreign-currency remittance evidence

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal or Ontario level. The CRA requires GST/HST invoices to contain prescribed information (ETA s.223), accepts machine-generated and electronic invoices, and requires records to be kept in readable, electronically-searchable format for six years. Larger registrants may be required to file GST/HST returns and remittances electronically; no continuous transaction control or e-invoice exchange is mandated.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within the normal four-year assessment period (indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud exists). If the exporter fails to satisfy statutory conditions or cannot substantiate the export, CRA will reverse the zero-rating, assess unremitted GST/HST plus interest retroactively, and may deny input tax credits. Gross-negligence penalties under ETA s.285.1 can reach 50% of the amount involved; wilful evasion may trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Export claims made by a VC GP without physical export of goods or credible non-resident service-recipient evidence are subject to heightened CRA export-of-services audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully free-floating currency regime with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible in both spot and forward markets; no exchange controls exist under the Currency Act or remnants of the repealed Foreign Exchange Control Act. Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are permissible without prior central bank approval, subject to solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA), Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at the statutory 25% rate (treaty-reduced typically to 5%-15%), and transfer pricing documentation under section 247 where intercompany services or management fees are remitted. Structural hurdles are limited to maintaining a Canadian registered office, filing annual corporate returns, and ensuring CRA tax clearance certificates are in good standing for payments above prescribed thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to Canadian entities. Export receipts and subscription payments may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency without repatriation deadlines. Reporting obligations are limited to customs documentation under the Customs Act, FINTRAC reporting for cross-border currency transfers exceeding C$10,000, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys for entities with significant cross-border transactions; none of these impose conversion or surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); securities regulatory authority for the venture capital GP is the Ontario Securities Commission (OSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Canadian-domiciled fintechs and payment service providers registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and as FINTRAC Money Services Businesses (MSBs) can support low-volume capital call disbursements, FX conversion, and contractor payments. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding FCA or EU EMI licenses face restricted viability: they are not Canadian chartered banks, cannot hold direct Bank of Canada settlement accounts, and must register as foreign MSBs with FINTRAC to service Canadian clients. For a venture capital GP management entity, the recommended arrangement is a tier-1 Canadian bank for escrow/operational liquidity, supplemented by a single regulated EMI for niche cross-border contractor payouts where an enhanced due diligence file, the EMI's own regulatory license, and a clear transactional rationale are maintained. Standalone offshore EMI clearing is commercially unsuitable for CAD-denominated LP capital calls and fund distributions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident directors and shareholders with opaque ultimate beneficial ownership

Root cause: Non-resident directors and shareholders with opaque ultimate beneficial ownership

Defense evidence: Organisational chart accompanied by a register of directors and shareholders, attestation of ultimate beneficial ownership, certified passport copies, proof of business address of each director, executed Limited Partnership Agreement and GP constituent documents, references from a regulated financial institution, and a written explanation of the GP's fee and carried-interest economics.
High-risk jurisdiction exposure in the LP investor base

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction exposure in the LP investor base

Defense evidence: FINTRAC-enhanced due diligence file including source of wealth and source of funds statements per LP, independent sanctions screening against the OSFI consolidated list and UN sanctions, negative news report, audited financial statements or investor accreditation evidence, and a signed AML compliance declaration by the GP's designated compliance officer.
Frequent capital call wire volumes without a clear commercial rationale

Root cause: Frequent capital call wire volumes without a clear commercial rationale

Defense evidence: Executed Limited Partnership Agreement, detailed capital call notices, watermark-matched LP commitment schedules, projected drawdown timetable, and investment committee minutes confirming deployment milestones and portfolio company closings.
Law firm or trust company acting as intermediary in fund formation

Root cause: Law firm or trust company acting as intermediary in fund formation

Defense evidence: Law firm engagement letter, professional indemnity insurance certificate, the firm's Law Society of Ontario compliance status, client trust ledger source-of-funds entries, and a chain-of-trust undertaking from the intermediary.
Mismatch between registered ISIC 6499 business purpose and expected transaction velocity

Root cause: Mismatch between registered ISIC 6499 business purpose and expected transaction velocity

Defense evidence: Offering memorandum or fund prospectus, GP management agreement, management fee schedule, OSC registration evidence (Investment Fund Manager or Exempt Market Dealer), and the compliance manual demonstrating that capital call sweeps are paced by investment milestones and subject to LP majority consent thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound LP capital contributions are settled via SWIFT MT103 for USD and CAD wires into the GP's capital escrow account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. CAD transfers from foreign banks may also be cleared via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) where the remitting bank holds a Canadian correspondent, or via the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value payments; USD wires are cleared through Fedwire or CHIPS via the Canadian bank's US correspondent. The standard payment reference in field 70 of the MT103 must include the capital call notice number, LP reference, and the BPM6 purpose code 1010 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'; for US-origin wires the remitter should use the purpose code '031600 – Investment in Equity' and the phrase 'Capital Subscription – VC Fund LP'. Verification steps include sanctions screening of the remitter and beneficiary, confirmation that the remitting account carries the LP's exact legal name, a copy of the capital call notice, the LPA commitment schedule, and FINTRAC large transaction reporting where any single wire or aggregate within 24 hours equals or exceeds C$10,000. Where an LP subscribes in USD and the fund operates in CAD, a spot FX conversion is executed under the bank's master FX terms with settlement legs via CAD LVTS and USD Fedwire on the same value date.Outbound wire payments for portfolio investments, management fees, and operating expenses settle via SWIFT MT103 for international transfers and LVTS for domestic CAD same-day clearing. Commercial letters of credit are permissible only where the GP acts as guarantor or co-investor for a portfolio company's trade finance facility; standard portfolio funding is executed as equity subscription wires or convertible note advances, each requiring an executed facility agreement, board resolution confirming investment authority, and an Investment Canada Act compliance assessment for foreign-controlled LP capital. The recommended clearing rail matrix is: (i) CAD operating expenses via LVTS; (ii) USD investment tranches into US portfolio companies via MT103 with Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent clearing; (iii) EUR investments into European vehicles via MT103 with TARGET2 settlement; (iv) carried interest and management fees to non-resident general partners via MT103 with Part XIII withholding screening and applicable tax treaty documentation (generally NR601 waiver or treaty reduction forms); and (v) distributions of capital returns to LPs via MT103 in the LP's base currency, accompanied by NR4 reporting, treaty rate application, and, where required, CRA clearance certificates.
