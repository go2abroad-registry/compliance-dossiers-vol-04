Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000. At formation, at least 20% of the nominal amount (CHF 20,000) must be paid up, but in no case less than CHF 50,000 (Art. 632 para. 2 and Art. 633 para. 2 OR). The paid-up funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss bank and remain blocked until commercial registration; non-cash contributions require a statutory audit report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzbewilligung) by the Zug cantonal fire police together with structural clearance from the building inspectorate for classified cleanroom zones; SUVA mandatory inspection of CNC lathes covering machine guarding, coolant mist extraction and noise protection; occupational health surveillance under the Labour Act (ArG) including work-hour permits and emergency plans; civil defense shelter compliance under the Civil Protection Act where gross floor area exceeds 1,000 m².

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT and TIN registration via Swiss Federal Tax Administration e-service 'E-Steuer/Impots/Tasse di credito' and the UID Register (UID/CIF/IDEL registration for companies).

E-government filing portal

For an Aktiengesellschaft (AG), the statutory Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / IDI / IDE) is automatically allocated by the cantonal commercial register upon incorporation. For foreign entities with a Swiss VAT obligation, registration for VAT is mandatory within 30 days of commencing taxable activities, unless turnover falls below the 100,000 CHF threshold (except for non-established entities that must register under simplified procedure regardless of turnover if engaged in taxable activities in Switzerland). The VAT registration is effected via the Swiss Federal Tax Administration's online portal (e.vat registration), using the QS code or the Temporary UID, and requires submission of legal entity documents, power of attorney, description of activities, and expected turnover. For corporate income tax purposes, the AG files federal, cantonal, and communal tax returns; the cantonal tax office (for incorporated seats in Zug) is the competent assessor, but the Federal Tax Administration coordinates federal tax. Foreign-entity registration for Swiss payroll or customs purposes would follow separate customs registration procedures with the Federal Customs Administration (OZD/FOCBS).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' pass-through election exists for AGs. Swiss corporations are mandatorily taxed at entity level at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Available elections include: (i) participation relief / capital contribution relief for qualifying dividends and capital gains; (ii) transparent taxation election for Swiss investment funds, investment companies, and limited partnerships for collective investments (CISA); (iii) election for notified hybrid mismatch arrangements denied under the Swiss Hybrid Mismatch Rules (Art. 8a ff. VStG/MWSTG), with no unilateral entity election except for treaty-compliant transparent sub-entities (e.g., a Swiss AG cannot elect pass-through status; a foreign hybrid entity may be treated as transparent if so classified under foreign law). Swiss group taxation (allocation of group contribution) is administrative allocation, not a formal election. Holding or mixed finance companies may elect outdated privileged regimes only for cantonal tax until expiration, but such regimes have been largely abolished by tax reform; no new Swiss principal-privilege elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable activities other than exempt activities for Swiss-established businesses; non-resident businesses are required to register regardless of turnover if they supply taxable services/goods in Switzerland, subject to place-of-supply rules. Charitable/HR/other specific thresholds apply separately (e.g., CHF 250,000 for nonprofit entities with remuneration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated (0% VAT) if goods are physically exported from Switzerland to a destination outside the Swiss customs territory (including Liechtenstein customs union), and the supplier retains the necessary proof of export (Art. 23 Swiss VAT Act). Intraocular lenses manufactured in Switzerland and shipped abroad for use in non-Swiss hospitals are eligible if delivered to foreign purchaser and removed from Swiss customs territory; export may be direct or through a Swiss logistics provider. Services relating to exporting goods (e.g., sea/air freight for export) and supplies of services to recipients abroad with their place of business outside Switzerland are also zero-rated under the B2B place-of-supply rule. The mere delivery of goods to a Swiss foreign-purchaser without actual export does not qualify. Zero-rating requires the supplier to hold evidence that the goods entered customs export and left Switzerland, as well as declarations from the recipient for export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via Swiss National Customs Declaration NCD / 'Export declaration attractiva' or 'suministro' electronically in the FOCBS 'e-dec' system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR / Customs Transit Document (depending transport mode)
  • Commercial Invoice and VAT invoice with zero-rated caption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of foreign payment (if available; Swiss VAT law does not require payment receipt as a statutory condition, but it is retained as substantiation against fraudulent claims)
  • Customs export certificate (for machined acrylic lens exports to EU/NATO countries: Submission of Goods Movement Reference (GMR) / customs seal evidence)
  • Certificate of origin / EUR.1 if exported under Swiss-EU customs agreements with preferential rights (not mandatory for VAT zero-rating but useful)

Switzerland does not currently operate a real-time transactional fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoicing is permitted under Swiss VAT law (E-invoice formats QR-Bill, Swiss Business E-Invoice, and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X). E-invoices must contain all VAT mandatory data (date of supply, issuer/recipient UID, rate, net/gross, VAT amount) and are subject to audit; they are transmitted through the VAT statutory retention requirements (10 years). No real-time API fiscalization is required for standard B2B/B2C transactions, but the Swiss Federal Tax Administration provides electronic filing (E-Steuer) for VAT returns and uses automated risk-based clearance for refund claims; 'Quick-Start' PCI or Online services require API integration for VAT refund applications only. No government-authorised digital stamping is in force; SAF-T-style import/export records may be required on audit.

Swiss VAT law imposes strict audit scrutiny on zero-rated export evidence; if the exporter cannot provide the documentary proof within the time set by the tax authority (usually 60 days after request), the VAT is taxed at 8.1% with retroactive effect from the date of supply. Deliberate or false zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion pursuant to Art. 66 ff. MWSTG, with fines up to 100% of the tax evaded (and fines up to 50% in qualified negligence); criminal fines may reach CHF 300,000 or imprisonment for serious fraud. Retrospective clawback period for undisclosed or incorrect VAT refunds/zero-ratings is generally not limited for intentional evasion and at least 10 years for negligence; interest is charged at statutory rate. Customs-related export false declarations are separately punishable under the Customs Act with penalties of CHF 500–100,000 or 1–3 times of the avoided duties. In addition, the Federal Tax Administration may order liquidation of the exporter's VAT registration and demand bank guarantee for future export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised currency regime with complete CHF convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls, no profit-remittance licences, no FDI approval gates, and no restriction on dividend, royalty, interest, or capital-repayment flows. The only fiscal friction is the statutory 35% Swiss dividend withholding tax, which may be reduced by treaty; this is a tax matter, not an FX restriction. Cross-border payments are freely routed, subject only to FINMA-sanction screening, bank AML due-diligence, and SNB balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratio on export receipts. A Swiss exporter may retain proceeds indefinitely in EUR, USD, or other convertible currencies in a free current account. The sole administrative obligation is statistical: cross-border payment flows are reported to the SNB by banks under the Swiss Balance of Payments Reporting Ordinance when volumes cross prescribed thresholds; foreign-currency retention is unrestricted and no compulsory timeline or conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank; French: Banque nationale suisse; Italian: Banca Nazionale Svizzera). Switzerland has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration; monetary and convertibility policy is set by the SNB under the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA), with banking conduct and AML enforcement delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary collection and disbursement rail, Low as a substitute for a licensed Swiss transactional bank account. Legal constraints: foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) with EU or UK e-money licenses may not take public deposits in Switzerland without a FINMA banking license or FINMA fintech license under the Banking Act and FinSO; passporting rights for EEA EMIs are not recognised into Switzerland. An offshore EMI can provide virtual IBANs, FX conversion, and cross-border settlement only where the underlying liquidity sits with a regulated credit institution. The Swiss IOL manufacturer must therefore maintain a FINMA-regulated primary account to avoid deposit-taking breaches and to satisfy Swiss AML Act (AMLA) counterparty-chain due-diligence expectations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory corporate income tax rate: 8.5%; combined federal and cantonal/municipal headline rate for Zug commercial rate at approximately 14.6% to 15.1%, with Geneva up to 21.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends distributed to all shareholders, including non-residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers the ordinary deadline is 31 March of the following year, with automatic extension to 30 June and, upon reasoned application, up to 30 September.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory fixed threshold; in practice, mandatory Local File documentation is required for intercompany transactions above CHF 500,000 and always required for all related-party transactions when consolidated group turnover exceeds CHF 50 million; Master File expected for groups with consolidated revenues exceeding CHF 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a de facto economic substance doctrine through the abuse of tax treaty provision and tax ruling practice. A Swiss AG must demonstrate independent decision-making, qualified staff, operational premises, and entrepreneurial risk-bearing for its intraocular lens manufacturing activities; a purely passive mailbox company is disregarded for treaty benefits and may be requalified as transparent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Misdeclaration of Manufacturing Profit

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG: imprisonment up to 3 years or monetary penalty up to CHF 250,000, plus back-assessment and interest; civil surcharge up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under Art. 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations for the restitution of unpaid corporate tax when the company is insolvent; travel bans can be imposed in the framework of criminal proceedings, and deliberate evasion triggers deprivation of liberty.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's length profit, compensatory fine, default interest, and in wilful cases criminal tax evasion penalties under Art. 175 DBG.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete transfer pricing documentation without substantive verification are jointly and severally liable for resulting tax debts and can be exposed to extradition or travel restrictions in aggravated cases.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate entity for Swiss tax purposes; permanent establishment status imposed on the foreign owner; denial of treaty relief; penalty of up to CHF 100,000.

Director personal liability: Re-qualification triggers piercing of the corporate veil: shareholders and directors are assessed directly on Swiss-source income, and managing directors may face civil restitution and a prohibition on leaving Switzerland pending tax investigation.
Money Laundering and High-Risk Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution for money laundering under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; confiscation of proceeds; asset freezing and administrative penalties by FINMA.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently accept suspicious payments for intraocular lens exports are personally liable; travel bans and pretrial detention are common, and the corporate veil is pierced when the company is a conduit for predicate criminal conduct.
Falsification of Books and Tax Records

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine and imprisonment for falsification of documents under Art. 251 Swiss Criminal Code (up to 5 years), together with tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG and loss of the liability limitation under Swiss law.

Director personal liability: Directors and bookkeeping officers are personally liable for all corporate tax debts if falsified commercial records render the company bankrupt; imprisonment and travel bans are mandatory including during extradition proceedings.
