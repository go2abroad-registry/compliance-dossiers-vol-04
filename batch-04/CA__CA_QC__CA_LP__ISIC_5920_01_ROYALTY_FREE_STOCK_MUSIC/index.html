Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legislated minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement for Quebec limited partnerships. Under CCQ art. 2240, the contribution of each limited partner must be real and may not be withdrawn or reimbursed while the partnership remains in operation if the withdrawal would impair payment to creditors.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required under the Building Act (Loi sur le bâtiment) and the Quebec Fire Safety Code for premises exceeding regulated occupancy thresholds; structural load inspection applies only if heavy audio racking is installed; occupational workplace clearance requires CNESST registration, first-aid services under the Regulation respecting first aid services, and electrical safety approval for audio-visual equipment; the general partner must keep the establishment address current with the CNESST and the municipal fire prevention office.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. No foreign-exchange-control approvals, no repatriation limitations, and no central-bank permits are required for profit remittances by a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident partners. Canadian-source partnership income paid to non-resident partners is governed by withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (or Part I filing obligations), not by FX clearance. The only mandatory reporting is FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or more) and cross-border currency declaration. Distributions of royalty earnings and capital withdrawals may be executed freely in CAD, USD, or any other convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Proceeds from cross-border digital music-licensing services (ISIC 5920 / NAICS 512240) may be retained in foreign currency, held in foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, or converted at the operator's discretion. For Canadian tax purposes, income is reported in CAD using Bank of Canada exchange rates; FINTRAC reporting applies to physical or electronic cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but no repatriation or conversion of export receipts is ever compelled.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Foreign-exchange reporting and AML surveillance are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises prudential FX risk at banks; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a settlement rail, Low as a primary treasury bank. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic-money-institution (EMI) charter; non-resident digital platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used for collection and payout services only if they hold a FINTRAC money-services-business registration and, for Quebec activities, a licence under the Money-Services Businesses Act administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). Such platforms should be confined to low-value royalty collection and mass disbursement; core liquidity and capital escrow must be held at a CDIC-member Schedule I institution for clearing finality and deposit insurance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital contributions are injected via SWIFT MT103 wires to the LP's designated CAD operating account at a Schedule I bank. The wire instruction must state: (i) the LP's full registered name and NEQ, (ii) the account number, (iii) the purpose code 'Capital Contribution - Limited Partnership Interest', and (iv) the contributing partner's name and account. Canadian receiving banks conduct sanctions screening and report the transaction to FINTRAC where it equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. The contribution must be supported by a subscribed capital schedule and capital-account statement; for US partners, source documentation and FBAR/Form 8938 analysis is recommended. A legal opinion confirming equity (rather than debt) treatment preserves the non-resident partner's Part XIII withholding posture and prevents thin-capitalisation issues.Licensor royalties and supplier payments clear via SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cross-border wires; Lynx (Payments Canada high-value system, successor to LVTS) for same-day CAD settlement; the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value instant credits; and Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for contractual documentary settlement. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the LP level (fiscally transparent); combined federal+Quebec general corporate reference rate 26.5% for corporate partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

N/A for LP distributions (no dividends); if the operating company is a corporation, Canadian Part XIII dividend withholding to non-residents is 25% statutory, reduced to 10%-0% under applicable tax treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within five months after the partnership fiscal year-end when any partner is a corporation; otherwise the later of six months after year-end and March 31. For a Quebec LP with a corporate general partner, a December 31 year-end return is due by May 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending December 31, the RL-15 must be issued by February 28 of the following year; the partnership declaration follows the T5013 deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transactions triggers mandatory T106 and contemporaneous TP documentation; CbC/Master/Local file mandatory at C$750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no discrete ESR statute; under Income Tax Act s. 245 (GAAR) and Quebec Taxation Act anti-avoidance rule, arrangements lacking real economic substance are recharacterized. A Canadian LP must demonstrate commercial activity, an office, records, and arm's-length allocations to maintain tax-transparent validity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of the Income Tax Act; criminal: fine of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years under s. 239.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partner who knowingly participate are co-liable; criminal sentencing may include court-ordered restitution, asset freezing, travel bans and passport surrender as bail/sentence conditions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts income and applies a 10% s. 247(3) penalty on the net transfer-price adjustment, plus interest and reinstatement of untaxed foreign-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable, but if the TP adjustment spawns source deductions or unpaid corporate tax, s. 227.1 director liability attaches.
Withholding Tax / Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Payer becomes liable for the full unwithheld amount, 10%-20% penalties (s. 227(8)), and daily interest; repeated failure may attract imprisonment under s. 238(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted withholdings under s. 227.1 and equivalent Quebec law; CRA may file a Director's Liability Certificate and enforce against personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse
