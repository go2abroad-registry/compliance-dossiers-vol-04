Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst/register.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery for provincial registration and administrative filings.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada must: (1) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA's Registration Online Portal or by paper Form RC176 'Request for Non-Resident Registration'; (2) open the GST/HST program account by selecting 'RT' and identifying the partnership as the registrant; (3) provide complete partner identification details, including the non-resident general partner's foreign corporate identifier and any Canadian ITN; (4) if no permanent establishment in Canada, appoint an agent or fiscal representative using CRA Form RC459 to receive service of process and remit net tax; and (5) file the Ontario Business Information Form and pay the Ontario extra-provincial registration levy, where applicable. The BN is the statutory TIN for federal tax and GST/HST purposes; an Ontario-only operation does not require a separate Quebec QST number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Ontario limited partnership is not a separate taxable entity under the federal Income Tax Act; it is a pure flow-through vehicle under ITA s. 96. Income, losses, and gains are computed at partnership level and allocated to each partner on a yearly basis. The GST/HST regime deems the partnership to be a 'person' solely for registration, collection, and filing; this deemed characterization does not convert partnership status into a corporation. Ontario follows the same flow-through classification under the Corporations Tax Act, with the general partner (even a non-resident) exposed to Ontario permanent-establishment threshold reviews.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification system; pass-through classification is mandatory for a limited partnership. Available statutory elections relevant to classification and indirect tax include: (a) election under ITA s. 97(2) to defer recognition of gain on property transferred to the partnership; (b) election under ITA s. 96(3) to compute partnership income in accordance with selected fiscal periods; (c) election under ETA s. 156 for supplies between GST/HST registrants that are closely related; (d) election to use the quick method of accounting (as available); and (e) election by non-resident partnerships without a Canadian permanent establishment to operate through a fiscal representative under ETA s. 273.1. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3) do not permit an overriding election; they automatically disallow deductions to the extent of 'no inclusion' outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — registration is mandatory where a person's total revenue from taxable supplies in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters combined (the 'small supplier' limit). A non-resident partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada must monitor this threshold on a cumulative quarterly basis. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents that only make zero-rated supplies, enabling recovery of input tax credits; once registered, the registrant remains subject to filing until a valid deregistration is granted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods and offshore services is available under Schedule VI (for goods) and Schedule VII (for intangible personal property) of the Excise Tax Act. Post-production audio dubbing, subtitling, and AI voice-cloning services supplied to a non-resident studio qualify for 0% GST/HST where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of payment and does not use the service in Canada; (ii) the service is not performed in relation to any real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (iii) the supply is not rendered to an individual present in Canada; and (iv) the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary evidence. Digitally delivered deliverables (DWAV files, subtitle packs, AI voice models) are zero-rated as intangible personal property when transferred to the offshore client. Any output materially delivered in Canada, or any exemption claimed without adequate export evidence, is subject to reassessment at 13% HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / CAED)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export, where hard-media deliverables are shipped
  • Commercial Invoice stating the zero-rating basis (ETA Schedule VI Part V or Schedule II/III) and the non-resident client name
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/foreign exchange record confirming receipt of CAD funds from a non-resident account
  • Non-Resident Certificate of Status or a written certification from the foreign studio domicile and tax registration number
  • Independent delivery receipt, metadata manifest, or technical delivery log confirming transfer to the offshore client

No federal real-time clearance or fiscal e-stamping mandate exists as of 2025. Electronic invoices are accepted without prior government validation, API clearance, or digital stamping. CRA promotes voluntary B2B electronic invoicing through PEPPOL and EDI standards. Every GST/HST invoice must contain prescribed data: seller legal name, Business Number, invoice date, description, consideration, amount of GST/HST charged, and the 'tax included' indication where applicable. Zero-rated invoices must state 'GST/HST not charged' or show 0% rate. Invoices and all source records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess within four years, or without time limit in cases of fraud or misrepresentation, to reclassify a claimed zero-rated supply as taxable at 13% HST, deny input tax credits used to fund the export, and impose recovery of net tax plus statutory interest accrued daily. Civil penalties include: (i) a late-filing penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax plus 1% per full month, capped at 12%; (ii) a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the assessed tax or income tax amount under ETA s. 293.1 and ITA s. 163(2); and (iii) a 100% penalty for wilful evasion on refund claims. Criminal prosecution for knowingly making false or misleading export statements may result in fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for indictable offences, loss of zero-rating privileges, mandatory audit of subsequent returns, and inclusion in the CRA's named compliance database.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized and convertible currency regime, with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, partnership distributions, and loan repayments to non-resident partners are not subject to foreign exchange authorization. However, such payments may trigger Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). The distinction between current and capital account transactions is irrelevant for FX regulatory purposes, though certain statistical reporting obligations exist under the Statistics Act and the Canadian Payments Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory requirements for Canadian exporters to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds into Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in any currency, subject to internal bank compliance and contractual arrangements. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) mandates accurate export declarations under the Export and Import Permits Act, but these declarations do not include currency conversion or surrender requirements. Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any cross-border EFT of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign exchange administration in Canada is decentralized and not managed by a dedicated exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional / Medium for non-core treasury use. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts may be used only for non-Canadian client collections and outbound disbursements to jurisdictions where the EMI is licensed. To operate as a payment service provider into Canada, an EMI must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and meet provincial regulatory requirements if applicable. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot open proprietary operational accounts for Canadian entities, and major Canadian correspondent banks generally reject wires originating from unregistered EMIs. Wise (TransferWise) holds a valid MSB registration in Canada and can serve as a supplementary rail for low-value cross-border payments, but the primary treasury anchor must be a Schedule I bank for regulatory reliability.
