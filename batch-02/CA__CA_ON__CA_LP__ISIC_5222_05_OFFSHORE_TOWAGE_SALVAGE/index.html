Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification election. A limited partnership is a flow-through vehicle for Canadian tax purposes; the partnership itself is not a taxpayer and each partner is taxed on the partnership's income and capital gains as applicable. Non-resident partners with Canadian-source business income from a partnership may be required to file a Canadian income tax return; certain elections may be available, including the section 216 basic exemption election for rental income and elections under section 94.2 or section 94.3 for foreign investment entities, but no hybrid mismatch election is generally available for non-resident partners except where statutory anti-hybrid rules apply under subsections 18(1), 18(2), and section 94.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full IMF Article VIII convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. Current-account flows (deep-sea towage invoices, Lloyds Open Form salvage awards, charter hire, bunker disbursements, survey and classification fees) may be remitted freely in CAD, USD, GBP, or EUR. Capital-account flows (limited-partner capital contributions, partnership profit distributions, redemption of LP interests, and intercompany loans) are likewise unrestricted and require no exchange-control permit or profit-repatriation license. Practical hurdles are limited to: (i) foreign limited partners being subject to non-resident Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding at a statutory 25% rate, typically reduced to 10–15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention or the Canada-United Kingdom Double Taxation Convention; (ii) FINTRAC mandatory reporting for each cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or its foreign-currency equivalent; and (iii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act for any counterparty touching embargoed ports, designated vessels, or blocked persons.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline, or foreign-currency retention ceiling. The Ontario Limited Partnership may lawfully retain towage and salvage receivables in CAD, USD, GBP, or EUR in any Canadian or offshore account, provided the amounts are accurately recorded on the partnership books and year-end foreign-currency balances are valued in accordance with subsection 95(2) and section 39 of the Income Tax Act to recognize taxable exchange gains or deductible losses. The only quantitative compliance thresholds arise from FINTRAC: all cross-border remittances equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 require a cross-border wire transfer report, and no export-related revenue may be remitted to or from a sanctioned state without an Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) authorization and a permit issued by the Minister of Foreign Affairs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration analogous to a reserve-bank approval body; exchange-control licensing was abolished in 1951. Transaction monitoring and data collection over cross-border funds transfers are performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). For maritime craft, Transport Canada and the Canadian Coast Guard apply separate operational licensing rules that do not affect FX repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial limited-partner capital contributions are to be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign partner's bank to the LP's CAD-denominated operating account at the chosen Canadian Schedule I bank, with the beneficiary account referencing the LP's legal name and ONBIS registration number. Canada does not maintain a national remittance purpose-code system comparable to China's R-codes; however, the wire must include a sufficiently descriptive payment Details field citing the partnership agreement clause and capital contribution purpose (e.g., 'Capital Contribution under Unanimous Agreement of CA_LP — dated YYYY-MM-DD'), the full legal name and address of the originator (satisfying FINTRAC Originator Information Requirements for all cross-border transfers), and the LP's LEI where available. For USD capital injections, the banks settle through the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or U.S. Fedwire via the bank's New York correspondent; for CAD, settlement occurs through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) with immediate finality. If the inbound wire is a loan from the parent rather than a capital contribution, the LP should execute a written non-interest-bearing intercompany loan agreement to avoid Part XIII deemed-dividend taxation and to support substance arguments in any OECD/CRS review.Operational disbursements (bunkering, port and canal fees, crewing services, insurance premiums, and equipment supplies) are cleared by SWIFT MT103 over the correspondent network; CAD-denominated domestic payments settle via LVTS or the Real-Time Rail. Significant salvage-award settlements and purchase-payment obligations involving foreign shipowners and classification agencies should be executed using Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600, particularly where the counterparty demands a guarantee of no-cure-no-pay security funds; the LP's Canadian bank will issue the L/C with a USD or EUR reimbursement draft drawn on a London or New York correspondent, utilizing the Lloyd's-mandated escrow route when required by the salvage contract. For P&I club loss settlements, funds may be diverted to a designated marine-escrow account only under an express escrow agreement citing the International Group of P&I Clubs' standard documentation, and all outbound transfers must carry the sanctioned-purpose code text 'Marine Towage and Salvage Fee — Invoice [reference]' to avoid misclassification by the beneficiary's bank.Deliverable CAD/USD FX Forwards, with a maximum tenor of 12 months, executed under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement together with a Canadian Market Master Agreement (1990/1996) and CSA credit support annex, used to hedge firm towage invoices and salvage-award receivables.Cross-Currency Swaps (fixed/floating and fixed/fixed) for converting long-term USD-denominated hull-financing liabilities into CAD cash flows matching the LP's Canadian partnership profit-distribution schedule.Vanilla Purchased FX Options (call or put on CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP) with premium paid-in-full, used as price-protection on competitive tender bids where the salvage award is priced in USD or GBP.FX Rolling Forwards and swaps that allow unwinding or netting prior to maturity, subject to OSFI-compliant credit-risk limits and recorded at mark-to-market under International Financial Reporting Standards.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are lawful in Canada only when the underlying currency is non-convertible or illiquid (e.g., exposed to emerging-market ports); for CAD/USD, GBP, and EUR, deliverable forwards are standard and NDFs are redundant.

Any offshore EMI that solicits Canadian clients must itself be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC; otherwise, using the EMI as the settlement venue exposes the LP to the risk of refund denials and to OSFI guidance on circumvention of regulated payment rails.
