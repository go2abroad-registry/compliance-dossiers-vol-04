Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Quebec Business Corporations Act. The company may be incorporated with one or more common shares without par value issued for consideration fixed by the board. There is no statutory escrow account rule; subscription funds must be paid and credited before share certificates are delivered. If shares are issued for property, a plan of assets and liabilities must be attached.

Funding & unlock path: No civil defense or fire safety certificate is required for a pure digital publishing operation. If a physical office is leased, the lessee must comply with the Quebec Building Code for fire safety, exits, and public occupancy requirements, and with CNESST workplace health and safety rules for employees. A fire safety inspection report may be requested by the landlord or property insurer.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax rules, securities laws, and bank compliance review. There is no general exchange-control approval requirement for foreign investment, intercompany funding, payment of dividends, or repatriation of capital. A Canadian corporation may remit dividends or other lawful distributions to non-residents after satisfying corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, treaty eligibility and documentation, and any applicable tax-clearance or reporting obligations. Interest, royalties, service fees, and other cross-border payments require appropriate contractual and transfer-pricing support and may attract Canadian withholding tax, generally subject to treaty reduction where available. Payments to related non-residents may also require transfer-pricing documentation and information returns. Banks may request source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, transaction-purpose, and underlying-contract evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Canada and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio for export receipts. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency or offshore, subject to tax, accounting, sanctions, contractual, and reporting requirements. Financial institutions may request invoices, platform settlement statements, distribution agreements, or other evidence supporting the nature and origin of incoming funds. Foreign affiliate, foreign-property, and international transaction reporting may apply depending on ownership, balances, and transaction facts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine capital controls. The Department of Finance Canada administers relevant sanctions and certain financial-sector policies; FINTRAC administers federal anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance requirements. Quebec does not maintain a separate general foreign-exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment and collection services; not a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking institution account where the corporation requires a conventional Canadian operating account, deposit protection, or local credit facilities. Availability, CAD collection details, safeguarding model, permitted business activities, and cross-border transfer limits depend on the provider's Canadian authorization, product terms, and the corporation's residence and ownership profile. Offshore e-money institution accounts do not remove Canadian AML, tax, sanctions, or books-and-records obligations. Confirm that the provider accepts Canadian corporations and the digital-content business model before relying on it for platform settlements or material treasury balances.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unclear beneficial ownership, nominee arrangements, layered foreign holding companies, or missing control-person information.

Root cause: Unclear beneficial ownership, nominee arrangements, layered foreign holding companies, or missing control-person information.

Defense evidence: Current articles and corporate registry extract; ownership chart through all intermediate entities to natural-person beneficial owners; shareholder and director registers; identification and address evidence for directors, authorized signatories, and beneficial owners; trust or nominee instruments where applicable; corporate resolutions; and explanations of any ownership or control complexity.
Non-resident ownership, foreign directors, or remote onboarding that prevents routine identity verification.

Root cause: Non-resident ownership, foreign directors, or remote onboarding that prevents routine identity verification.

Defense evidence: Certified incorporation and good-standing records; constitutional documents; passports and proof of residential address; certified translations where required; Canadian business address and operational evidence; board authorization for account opening; tax identification details; and completion of the bank's identity-verification or interview process.
Large or irregular initial capital transfers, third-party funding, or transfers inconsistent with stated business scale.

Root cause: Large or irregular initial capital transfers, third-party funding, or transfers inconsistent with stated business scale.

Defense evidence: Executed subscription or shareholder-loan documents; board approvals; sender bank statements or other source-of-funds evidence; sender ownership information; wire instructions matching the documented investor or lender; capitalization table; and a concise explanation of the transfer's purpose and expected use.
High-volume, multi-currency receipts from app stores, payment processors, distributors, or foreign counterparties that do not match the stated publishing business.

Root cause: High-volume, multi-currency receipts from app stores, payment processors, distributors, or foreign counterparties that do not match the stated publishing business.

Defense evidence: Apple and Google developer or publisher agreements; platform account ownership evidence; distribution and syndication contracts; invoices or royalty statements; settlement reports; customer geography and currency summaries; website or application details; and reconciliation from gross sales through platform fees, refunds, taxes, and net deposits.
Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, opaque intermediaries, or counterparties with unexplained links to restricted territories.

Root cause: Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, opaque intermediaries, or counterparties with unexplained links to restricted territories.

Defense evidence: Counterparty identification and beneficial-ownership information; contract, invoice, and delivery evidence; payment-chain details; documented sanctions screening; explanation of any geographic nexus; and evidence that the transaction is permitted under applicable Canadian sanctions and other relevant laws. Do not process a transaction while a sanctions prohibition or unresolved match remains.
Related-party royalties, licensing fees, management charges, or intercompany balances without commercial support.

Root cause: Related-party royalties, licensing fees, management charges, or intercompany balances without commercial support.

Defense evidence: Executed intercompany agreements; evidence of rights ownership and services performed; pricing methodology and transfer-pricing support; invoices and payment calculations; withholding-tax analysis and forms; and required Canadian tax filings or information returns.
