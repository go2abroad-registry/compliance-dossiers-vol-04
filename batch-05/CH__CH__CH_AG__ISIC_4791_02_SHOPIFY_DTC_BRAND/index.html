Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Nominal share capital of at least CHF 100,000 is mandatory; no less than CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up in cash before registration and held in a blocked escrow account at a Swiss bank or authorized finance company, verified by a bank confirmation for release only after commercial registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain the Zug fire-police completion certificate (Feuerpolizeiliche Abnahme) and register the premises with the cantonal building insurance. Structural safety: provide a certified static load assessment for the office or warehouse floor where loads exceed 4 kN/m². Occupational clearance: pass the AWA labor inspectorate inspection under ArGV 3 covering emergency exits, lighting, ventilation, sanitary facilities, and rest areas, and confirm SUVA accident insurance coverage for all workers.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Bern. Online portal: ESTV eServices portal (https://www.estv.admin.ch), Swiss VAT e-business portal (eService für Unternehmer), and Swiss Central Business Name/UID register.

E-government filing portal

A domestic Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is automatically assigned a Swiss Business Identifier (UID, CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) upon registration in the Commercial Register; this UID is the principal tax reference for federal, cantonal, and communal tax authorities. VAT registration is filed with ESTV via electronic registration in the Swiss VAT eServices portal, using the UID, extract from the Commercial Register, articles of association, governing body details, and expected taxable turnover figures. For non-established foreign enterprises, a simplified Swiss VAT registration procedure is available through ESTV, with an obligation to appoint a Swiss tax agent only in defined cross-border situations; the VAT identification number (MWST-Nr / IDE / TVA) is issued in electronic form after completeness review.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; legal-form classification under the Swiss Code of Obligations is controlling for direct tax. The relevant optional mechanisms are: (i) VAT small-business election under Art. 10 MWSTG/Art. 10 LTVA allowing otherwise non-taxable suppliers with worldwide turnover below CHF 100,000 to voluntarily register for VAT; (ii) the optional patent box regime and R&D super-deduction under the Swiss Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act 2019; (iii) the qualified participation deduction for dividends/capital gains on qualifying participations; (iv) cantonal tax statuses/rulings for holding, domicile, or mixed companies remain abolished but are grandfathered for limited transitional periods. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under Art. 57a-57e DBG (VStG) only as a defensive provision; there is no elective hybrid-mismatch opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover excluding VAT, under Art. 10(1) MWSTG/Art. 10(1) LTVA. For Swiss-resident DTC e-commerce retailers, turnover from zero-rated exports is counted in determining whether the CHF 100,000 threshold is reached. Separate statutory rules apply to non-established suppliers of electronically supplied services and low-value distance sales, which may trigger Swiss VAT registration with specific de minimis conditions and special facilitation rules as set out in ESTV guidance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Switzerland to a destination outside Switzerland are zero-rated if the supply is made to a foreign purchaser, ownership passes to the purchaser, and the goods are actually transported out of Switzerland by an authorized export procedure; Swiss export documentation must evidence the customs release. In addition, certain services directly connected to exported goods or international transactions may qualify for zero-rating under the Swiss VAT law. For online DTC sales via Shopify, each shipment must be accompanied by a valid Swiss electronic export declaration and transport proof; delivered-at-place terms or equivalent Incoterms must not contradict the export evidence.

  • Validated electronic Swiss export declaration (via the Swiss Single Window, containing the MRN or national export registration number, goods description, quantity, value, customs office and date of release)
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, underlying purchase order or sales contract
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR/House Waybill as applicable showing the shipped quantity and destination outside Switzerland
  • Proof of payment / bank credit statement indicating receipt of the export proceeds, or inward remittance certificate where requested by the tax authority in audit
  • Customs transit or export proof such as the National Transit System (NCTS) declaration text, export list, or customs certified exit confirmation

Switzerland does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) system, real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping obligation. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory for all domestic payment slips issued to Swiss payers, serving as a structured payment instrument rather than a tax clearance. E-invoicing is permissible and commonly used in PEPPOL and FacturaSuite formats, with no prior tax-authority approval. Taxpayers must issue invoices containing the mandatory Swiss VAT invoice content prescribed by Art. 26 MWSTV/Art. 26 OLTVA and retain books, invoices, and supporting documentation for at least 10 years.

Swiss Federal and cantonal tax authorities examine export zero-rating claims on a risk-based and transaction-level basis. If an export declaration, transport proof, or consideration evidence is false, incomplete, or contradicted by payment data, the tax authority retrospectively recharacterizes the supply as taxable in Switzerland and assesses Swiss VAT at the standard rate, together with default interest and late-payment interest. Intentional tax evasion relating to VAT or customs duty is punishable under the Swiss Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR) and the VAT Act with monetary fines of up to at least twice the amount of tax evaded, and in aggravated cases further criminal penalties; negligent underdeclaration triggers monetary penalties. Customs offences may result in separate confiscatory fines and forfeiture of goods. There is no Swiss statutory deadline or obligation for repatriation of FX proceeds; therefore the zero-rating audit focuses solely on lawful export proof and the economic substance of the cross-border transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland applies a fully convertible currency and capital-control-free regime for both current and capital transactions. Residency-based limitations are absent; cross-border fund transfers are unrestricted. Profit remittance in the form of dividends or loan repayments is lawful without foreign-exchange permission. Only the Swiss anticipatory withholding tax (35%) applies to distributions to non-residents unless mitigated by an applicable double taxation treaty. CHF floats freely and may be traded without central bank authorization, although SNB monetary policy interventions can influence exchange-rate conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Swiss law imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency abroad, converted at will, or settled in EUR, USD, or any freely tradable currency. Banks require only a generalized transaction-purpose statement for anti-money-laundering record-keeping under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA). No SNB balancing or reporting obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank (SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts without FINMA authorization are not viable as the main treasury rail for a Swiss AG. They lack Swiss banking license status and therefore do not obtain FINMA prudential supervision, Swiss deposit insurance, or local settlement finality. Feasibility rating: LOW for primary liquidity; they may be used only as auxiliary collection channels with automatic daily sweeping into a FINMA-supervised Swiss bank account. Any payment institution holding client funds for more than 60 days in Switzerland triggers a licensing requirement under the Banking Act or FinTech Ordinance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal tier: 8.5% on profit after federal tax, equal to 7.83% effective on pre-tax profit. Combined federal, cantonal and communal headline burden for an AG domiciled in Zug: approximately 11.9% of pre-tax profit (Zug benchmark; municipalities vary 11.9%–12.9%; higher in Geneva, Zurich, Basel-Stadt where total reaches 18%–21%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) levied at source on any dividend or profit distribution of a Swiss AG, including distributions out of statutory reserves and constructive dividends stemming from non-arm's-length transactions (Art. 4 VStG). The withholding is refundable only upon the shareholder's application; for Swiss resident corporate shareholders the tax is fully creditable; for non-residents it is final unless reduced under an applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six (6) months after the end of the fiscal year for both the federal and the Zug cantonal return; for a calendar-year entity the statutory deadline is 31 May of the following year. Extensions are routinely granted upon written request up to 30 September (federal) and 31 October (Zug). Where the AG is audited by an external tax expert, the canton normally accepts filing by the extended deadline without penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory TP documentation (Local File and, if applicable, Master File) is triggered once the Swiss company exceeds the small-enterprise exemption: more than two of the following three criteria are met — (i) more than 50 full-time employees; (ii) annual turnover above CHF 20 million; or (iii) balance-sheet total above CHF 10 million (per the Swiss Code of Obligations test applied by the ESTV's TP circular of 2012). For the Shopify DTC brand operating through an AG with turnover crossing CHF 20 million, the Local File must contain the group overview, benchmarking study, and arm's-length justification for intra-group services, royalties and loans; a Master File is required for groups with global consolidated turnover above CHF 50 million. Failure to submit contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof and enables the tax authority to apply an income adjustment and a penalty surcharge.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime as in the UAE, but imposes substantive requirements through (i) anti-abuse clauses in domestic law (Art. 21 DBG denial of tax advantages for purely artificial arrangements), (ii) the OECD/MLI Principal Purpose Test incorporated into treaty relief, and (iii) the ESTV's practical substance test for holding, domiciliary, pure finance and mixed companies in cantons (including Zug) that grandfather transitional privileges. A Swiss AG holding the e-commerce brand and collecting worldwide Shopify revenue must have, in Switzerland: an operative board with actual meeting minutes, a real business address, an independent economic decision-making process, bank accounts, payroll for personnel performing substantive functions, and no flow-through of capital to investors without commercial justification. Absent such substance, the tax authorities may deny residence benefits, reallocate income to the beneficial owner's jurisdiction, disallow treaty protection for the 35% withholding tax, and impose the full 35% withholding on deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – deliberate under-declaration of e-commerce revenue

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to three times the amount of the evaded federal and cantonal tax (Art. 175 DBG), plus default interest of 4.5%–5% p.a. on the unpaid principal from the statutory due date, plus cantonal administrative penalties of the same magnitude. The unpaid tax remains collectible with compound interest.

Director personal liability: The board of directors and the mandatory Swiss accounting representative personally bear penalty liability under Art. 181 DBG as the persons who caused the infringement. Directors may be individually fined, their travel abroad restricted where criminal tax proceedings are opened, and their private assets garnished to cover the company's fiscal debt if the company is insolvent.
Tax Fraud – falsified books and forged evidence

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to three years and/or a monetary penalty (Art. 176 DBG), with no statutory minimum; in aggravated cases the prison sentence can be combined with a fine of up to three times the evaded tax; additional cantonal criminal sanctions apply simultaneously. The company itself faces liability under Art. 102 Swiss Criminal Code for organisational defects.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of falsified returns are prosecuted personally and may be sentenced to imprisonment; they receive a criminal record, are barred from holding Swiss board positions, and face extradition where the home state requests them. Criminal asset seizure, travel bans and entry into the Schengen Information System are routinely ordered by Swiss investigating judges.
Constructive Dividend / Hidden Profit Distribution (Transfer-Pricing Override)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-adds the excess payment to taxable profit and simultaneously deems a constructive dividend; the Swiss AG must deposit 35% withholding tax on the constructive distribution within 30 days of the final assessment. The re-assessment can reach back 10 years and triggers TP re-documentation with a 5–10% surcharge on the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or benefited from the non-arm's-length transaction are personally and severally liable for the entire avoided withholding tax under Art. 12 VStG; where the company cannot pay, the director's personal assets are directly seizable. In cases of fraudulent intent, criminal sanctions for tax fraud apply.
Shell-Company Pact / Absence of Economic Substance in Switzerland

Statutory sanction: Denial of the domicile status may result in: (i) disqualification from treaty protection so that the 35% Swiss withholding tax becomes final and non-refundable; (ii) full taxation of the deemed liquidation of the Swiss entity upon transfer of assets abroad; (iii) retrospective profits reallocation to the state of effective management with double taxation; (iv) administrative fine under the anti-abuse provisions.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners are treated as responsible persons: they may be ordered to appear before the Zug tax authority, be subject to asset freezes in Switzerland, and face personal liability for all unpaid taxes and penalties. Repeated abuse triggers criminal prosecution for qualified tax evasion with imprisonment, and the Federal Act on Combating Money Laundering is applied to confiscate the funds routed through the Swiss accounts.
Money Laundering / AML Breach in E-commerce Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code punishes money laundering with imprisonment of up to three years or a monetary penalty, and up to five years in aggravated cases. For gross negligence, the penalty is a fine of up to CHF 500,000. Under Art. 102 SCC, the company itself is liable to a fine of up to CHF 5 million where the offence was not prevented despite organisational measures, and all serviced assets may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors found to have knowingly or negligently permitted laundering through the company's payment rails face criminal conviction, immediate passport confiscation and a global Interpol red notice, prohibition from practicing as directors, and personal confiscation of any ill-gotten gains. Civil liability to the damaged parties follows under the Swiss Code of Obligations with joinder of the estate.
Non-Deposit of Withholding Tax on Distributions / Veruntreuung

Statutory sanction: Statutory interest arrears of 4.5%–5% from the due date, penalty interest, a fine for gross negligence equal to 100% of the unpaid withholding tax, and criminal prosecution for 'Veruntreuung von Verrechnungssteuern' (Art. 186 DBG) punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and an additional fine. ESTV may issue a personal tax certificate against the responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Art. 12 VStG expressly makes the members of the board of directors jointly, severally and unconditionally liable for the full amount of the unpaid withholding tax as a personal debt of the directors. Even after resignation, directors remain liable for tax amounts deposited or accrued during their term; bankruptcy of the AG does not extinguish the directors' personal liability; enforcement includes attachment of personal Swiss bank accounts and real estate.
