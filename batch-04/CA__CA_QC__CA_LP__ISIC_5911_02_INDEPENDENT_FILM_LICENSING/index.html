Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Quebec limited partnership. Limited partners may contribute cash, property or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow account is required. At least one general partner (commandité) with unlimited liability must be identified in the declaration.

Funding & unlock path: No dedicated civil-defense permit is required for this activity. Any occupied office or warehouse must satisfy the Québec Building Act occupancy permit requirements and the Québec Fire Safety Code, including fire alarms, exits, extinguishers, and structural load limits for shelving. If employees are present, the employer must register with CNESST and maintain a workplace health and safety program.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Quebec limited partnership. Limited partners may contribute cash, property or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow account is required. At least one general partner (commandité) with unlimited liability must be identified in the declaration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax authority for GST/HST and federal income tax; Revenu Québec — provincial tax authority for Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec income tax; Registraire des entreprises du Québec — provincial business registry. E-government filing portal

For the Quebec Limited Partnership used for independent film licensing: (1) register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec under the Civil Code of Quebec, obtaining the Québec enterprise number (NEQ); (2) apply to the CRA for a partnership Business Number and GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or CRA My Business Account, providing the partnership agreement, foreign addresses of non-resident partners, and evidence of the partnership's Canadian establishment or commercial activity; (3) register for QST with Revenu Québec through the applicable Revenu Québec registration application, obtaining a QST/TVQ account; (4) if employees will be engaged, register for payroll and source-deduction accounts; and (5) file Form T5013 Partnership Information Return annually if the LP carries on a business in Canada or has a Canadian-resident partner or Canadian-source income. Registration is available to non-resident partnerships that make taxable supplies in Canada; registration becomes mandatory once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act: it is not an entity-level income taxpayer, and each partner is taxable on its agreed share of Canadian-source income. The partnership itself is a registrant for GST/HST and QST. Territorial scope is source-based: non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on Canadian-source business and property income, subject to tax treaty relief. No tax-exempt zone treatment applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; the LP is mandatorily classified as a partnership by provincial law and the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections and administrative mechanisms include: voluntary GST/HST registration before the CAD 30,000 threshold; elections regarding the effective date of GST/HST registration; annual GST/HST filing elections under section 256 of the Excise Tax Act; analogous QST elections available under Revenu Québec rules; and, for non-resident partners, treaty-based or section 216 net-income elections where Canadian-source income would otherwise be subject to gross withholding. Hybrid-mismatch rules apply automatically if a partner's jurisdiction classifies the LP differently; there is no opt-out from these rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person whose taxable supplies in Canada, including zero-rated supplies, exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters must register for GST/HST. Quebec applies the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST registration, based on the value of worldwide taxable supplies attributable to Quebec or made through a Quebec establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST and QST zero-rating for independent film licensing applies where the supply is made by the Canadian LP to a non-resident licensee that is not registered under GST/HST or QST, and the copyright or distribution rights are not for exploitation or resupply in Canada. Physical film prints or masters are zero-rated if exported from Canada for delivery outside Canada. If Canadian theatrical, television, streaming, or other exploitation rights are granted, the Canadian-portion consideration is taxable at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST. The LP may claim input tax credits for GST/QST paid on inputs attributable to zero-rated export sales, resulting in net refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A) for physical film prints, masters, and promotional goods where required
  • Commercial invoice and detailed statement describing the intangible copyright license or service supplied
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or courier proof of delivery showing final destination outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/bank credit advice proving payment received into a Canadian bank account from the non-resident licensee
  • Executed film licensing agreement, chain-of-rights documentation, and territory schedule demonstrating that Canadian exploitation rights were not granted
  • Certificate of origin or other regulatory certificate required by the importing jurisdiction

Canada and Quebec do not impose a real-time transaction-level fiscal clearance, API invoice clearance, or digital stamping mandate for GST/HST or QST. Invoices must contain prescribed information, including supplier and recipient identification, the supplier's registration numbers, description, date, consideration, GST/QST charged, and zero-rating indicator where applicable. Electronic invoices are permitted if source records are preserved. GST/HST and QST returns are filed electronically using CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec electronic services; no pre-approval of individual invoices is required.

Export zero-rating is a high audit priority for the CRA and Revenu Québec. If documentary substantiation is inadequate, the supply is reassessed as taxable, with GST/QST, compounded daily prescribed-rate interest from the filing deadline, and civil penalties. Under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, a false statement or omission carries a penalty of at least CAD 100 and up to 5% of the understated net tax; section 282 imposes a 25% penalty for wilful evasion. The Quebec Tax Administration Act contains equivalent QST penalties. Misrepresentation by a third party, gross negligence, or wilful evasion removes the normal limitation period, permitting retrospective clawback beyond the standard four-year assessment window. General partners may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/QST, and deliberately false export invoices or sham back-to-back licensing structures intended to convert taxable Canadian-use royalties into zero-rated foreign supplies may lead to criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF. There are no exchange controls restricting the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, or royalties. However, for a Quebec limited partnership (LP), non-resident partners are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on allocated or distributed Canadian-source income and to Quebec provincial withholding, and the LP must file T5013/RL-15 slips. Such withholding is a tax hurdle, not a currency hurdle. Motion picture licensing transactions are classified as royalty/rental and may be subject to a 25% withholding unless treaty reduced; the Canada-US treaty may reduce it to 5%-10%.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any export retention, surrender, or compulsory conversion rules. Exporters (including film licensing entities) may retain full foreign exchange receipts in CAD or foreign currency accounts without time limits. Receipts must be reported to FINTRAC as part of Large Cash and International Electronic Fund Transfer Transaction reporting only above CAD 10,000 thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); administered by the Department of Finance and the Canada Border Services Agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Canadian-regulated fintech EMIs (e-money institutions) are not a recognized category under the Bank Act; non-bank payment providers require registration as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs generally lack Canadian regulatory licenses and cannot offer fiduciary deposit accounts; they may be limited to ancillary services like payment processing under strict restrictions. Legal constraints include the prohibition on accepting deposits to a non-regulated EMI, the requirement for MSB registration in Quebec, and OSFI rules limiting corporate bank accounts to banks or regulated credit unions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The sending bank must remit from the partner's bank to the Canadian LP's account via SWIFT. Provide supporting documentation: limited partnership agreement, certificate of limited partnership from the Registraire des entreprises, source of funds statement, beneficial ownership declaration, and bank's AML onboarding package. For amounts above CAD 100,000, banks may request the partnership TIN (business number) and tax residency of all partners. Turnover: A Québec limited partnership has no statutory threshold requiring an independent audit under the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting legal publicity. An audit becomes compulsory only when (i) the partnership agreement so provides, (ii) a lender or creditor validly demands audited financial statements, (iii) a court orders an audit, or (iv) a limited partner with a serious motive requests an audit at the partnership's expense under the C.C.Q. general partnership rules.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Quebec provincial 11.5% = 26.5% combined general corporate rate; LP is fiscally transparent, so partners bear tax at their own status.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding under s.212(2) for dividends paid to non-residents; reduced by tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US treaty: 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder, 15% otherwise; treaty rates may be lower for a qualified partner).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory CAD threshold requiring a formal Local/Master File in Canada; CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for every cross-border non-arm's-length transaction over CAD 1,000,000? Actually no. Country-by-country reporting applies where group annual consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000. Transfer-pricing penalties apply where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations; CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) to disregard transactions lacking a genuine business purpose, and treaty benefits require beneficial ownership and, under some DTAs, a limitation-on-benefits clause; an LP must maintain real substance (office, negotiations, risk, personnel).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine to a maximum of 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil penalty under s.163(2) is the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be assessed personally for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1 and are jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations; on a conviction, they may face imprisonment, seizure of personal assets, publication of tax convictions, and border/travel restrictions enforced by the Canada Border Services Agency.
