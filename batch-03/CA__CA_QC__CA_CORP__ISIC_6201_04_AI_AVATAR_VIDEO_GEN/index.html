Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal GST/HST and Income Tax (portal: CRA My Business Account at canada.ca); Revenu Québec - Quebec QST and Provincial Tax (portal: clicSÉUR Affaires at revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA online, by mail, or telephone. For GST/HST, the non-resident entity files Form RC1-Type Registration and is issued a GST/HST account number. For Quebec provincial QST, the entity registers simultaneously or separately with Revenu Québec; Revenu Québec issues the Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and a QST account number. Required documentation includes certified constitutional documents, proof of directors, registered or extra-provincial registration, a notable Canadian representative/address, and foreign jurisdiction corporate proof. TIN/BN and QST numbers are used for invoicing, filings, and refund claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification system. Tax status follows the Income Tax Act and imposts entity-level liability on corporations. Available elections include: voluntary GST/HST and QST registration (including simplified registration for non-residents), subsection 216(1) rental-income net-basis election, section 219 non-resident corporation election, comparable treaty elections subject to hybrid mismatch adjustments, and the associated corporations election for Part I taxable-income allocations. No IRC 7701-style check-the-box election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in world-wide taxable supplies during any four consecutive calendar quarters (federal GST/HST threshold; identical CAD 30,000 threshold applies for Quebec QST registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export of goods if the vendor exports supplies as warranted under section 5 of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and retains proof. Digital services, software, and AI video generation SaaS are zero-rated as a supply of services to a non-resident recipient where the supply is not performed wholly in Canada and is acquired for use exclusively outside Canada, provided the recipient is non-resident and no reciprocal Canadian nexus is created. The relevant package must include a non-resident recipient declaration and auditable documentation showing the digital product was supplied from Canadian servers or by a Canadian establishment to the foreign customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) issued by CBSA for goods; for digital SaaS, a detailed export service declaration and foreign-recipient contract
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill for tangible goods; for digital exports, a signed electronic service delivery receipt and IP licensing record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-exchange proof showing payment received from abroad
  • Non-resident status certificate / business license evidence of the foreign customer

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; Canada and Quebec do not mandate continuous transaction tax clearance. Electronic invoices are accepted in standard XML, JSON, or EDI. Quebec requires B2G electronic invoicing for public-sector contracts above CAD 25,000 in value through the SEAO/GETS platform, using PEPPOL BIS or equivalent formats. GST/HST returns are filed via GST/HST NETFILE; QST returns through Revenu Québec clicSÉUR. APIs are available for direct filing and remittance. Tax authorities require interoperable audit-proof records and e-invoice archives for six years.

False or fraudulent zero-rating claims are reassessed retroactively with penalty exposure of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax under sections 280 and 285 of the Excise Tax Act, plus accrued daily interest. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is clawed back together with denied input tax credits. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to CAD 100,000 and up to two-year imprisonment for evasion, and repeat offenders face CDS seizure and prohibition of export permits. CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit cross-border digital merchants and share data under their taxpayer-administrative cooperation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange rate regime. Current and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange controls; the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act impose no pre-approval or surrender requirements. Profit remittance by way of dividends to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% unless reduced by tax treaty, e.g., 5% under Canada-US treaty). No capital controls, no repatriation restrictions, and no requirement to convert export proceeds into CAD. Certain sanctions and AML obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) may affect transfers to/from designated jurisdictions, but these are not FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion timeline applies to export receipts. Canadian law does not impose mandatory conversion ratios. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a resident bank account or repatriated as business needs dictate, subject only to record-keeping requirements under the Income Tax Act and customs/statistical reporting thresholds. For AIGC software exports (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210), license fees are treatable as royalties or services revenue; export documentation and transfer pricing compliance are the primary regulatory matters.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian regulation does not recognize the EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license; cross-border digital EMIs must either establish a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC or partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank to access local clearing. Non-resident EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) can provide ancillary multi-currency accounts and payment facilitation, but they are generally prohibited from acting as principal settlement banks for Canadian-domiciled entities without an authorized MSB/PA registration. The most viable structure is to use a domestic Schedule I bank as the primary settlement and liquidity hub, with an EMI as a downstream FX/e-wallet overlay. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with the proper cross-border wire instruction. The remitting entity must identify the Canadian operating company's account at a Schedule I bank in Quebec, with bank identifier (institution number 001-006, branch transit number, account number and Bank Identifier Code / SWIFT BIC). The purpose code must reflect 'Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Capital Injection' (e.g., under the ISO 20022 / CFI purpose code convention, use 'EQUI' for equity capital, or 'CTRR' for capital transfer in some banks). Banks may require a supporting corporate resolution and subscription agreement. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'LOAN' or 'INTR' (intercompany) with a loan agreement. Verification steps: (1) complete bank's corporate wire form with source of funds declaration; (2) provide beneficial ownership certificate and current register of directors; (3) confirm the funds are from a non-sanctioned bank account; (4) provide copy of the Quebec corporate registry attesting active status; (5) for any amount above CAD 10,000, the bank's FINTRAC reporting threshold triggers ECDD. The safest execution is a direct wire from the foreign parent's corporate account, not from a pooled EMI or crypto exchange, to minimize AML friction.Outbound trade settlement for AIGC video generation software/export must be executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank transfers, or through payment orchestration using commercial card rails and payout APIs. Recommended clearing modes: (i) SWIFT MT103 for license fees, royalties, intercompany distribution, and vendor payments above threshold; (ii) Commercial letter of credit (documentary collection) where a large enterprise customer requires a bank guarantee, but for SaaS sign-ups this is rare; (iii) SaaS payment gateways (Stripe, Paddle) which settle via domestic clearing in Canada (ACSS / Lynx / Interac) and cross-border via Mastercard/Visa or SWIFT. For recurring B2B SaaS charges, Stripe/Paddle are classified as Payment Facilitator or Merchant of Record depending on configuration; the Quebec entity must maintain its own bank account at National Bank or RBC to receive payout settlements. For all outbound wires, export transaction codes under the Canadian Balance of Payments reporting system (e.g., 'Computer software - custom or other' code 9826) may apply for statistical surveys. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined: federal Part I statutory rate 15% plus Quebec general corporate income tax rate 11.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Canadian corporation to non-residents; no separate Quebec dividend withholding tax is levied

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than 6 months after the taxation year-end; federal balance of tax due within 2 months after year-end (3 months for a CCPC that qualified for the small business deduction in the preceding year)

Deadline: · Frequency:

CO-17 due within 6 months of the fiscal year-end; Quebec balance due within 2 months of fiscal year-end (3 months for Quebec-controlled small businesses meeting the small business deduction criteria)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 aggregate consideration with each non-resident related party exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (or CAD 100,000 in respect of loans, loan guarantees, or other obligations) triggers mandatory annual disclosure; enhanced Local File / Master File regime under the CRA's revised Section 247 documentation rules applies where the taxpayer's gross revenue is at least CAD 50,000,000 and related-party cross-border transactions reach CAD 5,000,000 (Local File), and Master File / Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore zero-tax jurisdictions; substance is instead tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, Section 245 ITA), the sham doctrine, and the Country-by-Country Reporting coherence mechanism. A Quebec AI video generation corporation is expected to demonstrate real employees, offices, contract negotiation, algorithmic R&D, and income-generating activity in Quebec; artificial profit routing to low-tax affiliates without substantive functions, assets, and risks is subject to full recharacterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understated; criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) with fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or condone false T2 filings face personal civil liability for corporate withholding and GST/HST debts under subsection 227.1; the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation is found to be a sham, permitting CRA civil collection against personal assets, refusal of Section 159 tax clearance certificates preventing M&A exits, and criminal responsibility of authorizing directors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the transfer-pricing adjustment under subsection 247(2) reallocating income to the arm's length amount, plus a specific transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) of 10% of the net adjustment where contemporaneous documentation has not been prepared and reasonable efforts cannot be demonstrated.

Director personal liability: Directors approving sham transfer-pricing terms can be assessed personally under the joint and several liability rules for the corporation's unpaid Part XIII withholding and GST/HST arising from the restructured payments; the penalty stream also exposes them to professional disqualification and criminal facilitation charges if the documentation is fabricated.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the shell transactions under the sham doctrine or apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reversing claimed SR&ED credits, disallowing loss carryforwards, and imposing the 50% gross-negligence penalty on the resulting reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who knowingly sign false corporate returns become personally liable for penalties and restitution, may be prohibited from serving as directors under corporate law, and face proceedings to impose travel restrictions and asset freezes where significant unpaid assessments are at risk.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance by Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Criminal Code laundering charges under Section 462.31 carry imprisonment of up to 10 years; the corporation also faces forfeiture of proceeds under Section 462.38 and Know-Your-Customer license revocation by payment processors.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers and directors who direct or knowingly benefit from laundering through the AI SaaS invoicing flow face personal criminal liability, direct fines, disqualification from financial institution mandates, and personal asset forfeiture; banks and payment service providers will enforce director guarantees forcing personal repayment of the laundered proceeds.
