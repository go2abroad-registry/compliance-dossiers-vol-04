Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA; Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC) for federal direct tax, withholding tax and VAT; cantonal tax administrations (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung) for cantonal and communal taxes. Official portals: www.estv.admin.ch, www.uid.admin.ch (UID register) and the FTA VAT e-service portal ('Mein Konto').

E-government filing portal

The AG is entered in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and automatically obtains the unified business identifier (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which serves as the statutory tax number, VAT ID and invoicing identifier for all federal and cantonal taxes. VAT registration is filed with the FTA through the VAT e-portal once taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 or for voluntary registration; the FTA confirms the taxpayer registration and the 'MWST' suffix is appended to the UID for VAT purposes. Foreign entities with Swiss VAT liability register directly with the FTA; non-resident licensors may alternatively rely on the reverse-charge procedure when the Swiss recipient is liable. Formation of the AG requires a licensed revision office (Prüfstelle) and notarized statutes; no physical Swiss office is required, but a Swiss correspondence address and authorized representative are expected for registration and tax correspondence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer liable on worldwide income (Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax, DBG), with foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate exempt with progression under relevant treaty and domestic rules; no pass-through treatment is available for a stock company. — The AG is entered in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and automatically obtains the unified business identifier (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which serves as the statutory tax number, VAT ID and invoicing identifier for all federal and cantonal taxes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Swiss AG is a resident corporate taxpayer liable on worldwide income (Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax, DBG), with foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate exempt with progression under relevant treaty and domestic rules; no pass-through treatment is available for a stock company.

Available election forms

Swiss law has no US-style check-the-box election and no general hybrid mismatch election, but provides specific statutory elections: (1) patent/IP-box election granting up to 90% reduction of net qualifying IP royalty income at federal and cantonal level (STAF 2020 regime, Art. 24b DBG); (2) notional interest deduction (NID) election in cantons that enacted it, reducing excessive equity taxation on intra-group financing; (3) VAT group election for Swiss related parties; (4) opt-in to tax real estate supplies to preserve input VAT recovery; (5) tax-neutral restructuring and merger election for licensed carry-over of assets and tax attributes; (6) participation relief on intercompany royalty/dividend income is automatic, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual gross turnover from taxable supplies (Art. 10 para. 2 let. a MWSTG). Foreign businesses without a Swiss seat must register if their Swiss taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted. Outbound B2B IP licensing to non-resident licensees falls outside Swiss VAT scope, so such royalty income alone does not trigger Swiss VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the 0% rate applies when ownership in movable goods is transferred to an acquirer abroad and the goods are physically exported, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration and transport documents. Services: cross-border patent/trademark licensing and sub-licensing is governed by the B2B place-of-receipt rule (Art. 8 MWSTG); where the licensee has its seat or permanent establishment abroad and is a taxable person, the income is not subject to Swiss VAT, may be invoiced at 0%, and the licensor retains full input VAT recovery. Services performed directly in connection with exported goods are zero-rated as ancillary export services. The AG must maintain the full licensing contract identifying the registered patents/trademarks, the royalty calculation, the foreign licensee seat and the licensee's valid foreign VAT/UID number.

  • Certified customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzolldeklaration) containing the official export number and release stamp of the Federal Office of Customs and Border Security (FOCBS/BAZG)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing the physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice proving receipt of the royalty from abroad within the repatriation window
  • License/sub-license agreement with complete IP identification, royalty formula and foreign licensee seat
  • Commercial invoice and Swiss QR-invoice (QR-Rechnung) issued with an explicit 0% VAT statement
  • Certificate of seat/residence and valid foreign VAT identification number of the licensee

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing. The Swiss QR-Invoice (QR-Rechnung) is mandatory on payment slips issued to Swiss payers since 30 June 2020; structured e-invoicing (eBill, XML, EDI) is accepted with fully auditable electronic archiving. All VAT-relevant invoices must be retained for 10 years (Art. 58 MWSTG) and produced to the FTA on request; no government pre-clearance or fiscal stamping exists.

The FTA rigorously audits export substantiation: if the export is not proven within the statutory deadlines, the supply is reclassified as domestic turnover subject to VAT at 8.1% (or the applicable reduced/special rate), input VAT deductions are clawed back, and default interest at 5% p.a. accrues. Administrative fines reach up to three times the tax evaded for intentional violation (Art. 64 MWSTG) and up to ten times for fraudulent evasion; criminal prosecution under Swiss criminal and customs law may lead to imprisonment. The assessment limitation period is 10 years for tax evasion and 5 years in ordinary cases, with retrospective adjustment of zero-rated credits and refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) for both current and capital transactions and has accepted the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no foreign exchange controls, no mandatory Central Bank approvals for cross-border payments, and no restriction on conversion of licensee or shareholder payments. Profit remittance, royalty distribution, capital inflows and outflows are freely transferable subject only to ordinary bank compliance screening, anti-money-laundering law, sanctions filters, and the Swiss federal withholding tax framework applicable to dividend distributions only; royalty payments are not subject to Swiss withholding tax. Tax and transfer-pricing substance must still be demonstrable to convert economically genuine licensing income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no compelled conversion ratio for export receipts or foreign-currency proceeds. A Swiss IP holding company may lawfully retain foreign currency balances, convert or deploy them at will through licensed banks, provided its accounting system documents the underlying licensing agreements and arm's-length consideration.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera, within the framework of the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-low for primary treasury reliance. Swiss FINMA-licensed neobanks or FinTech companies acting under the FinTech Banking Licence (deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million but no interest business) may hold payment accounts, but they do not provide the full international treasury, FX derivative, custody and correspondent banking depth of a Tier-1 commercial bank. Offshore digital EMI accounts, particularly EU/UK E-Money Institution wallets, are not recognized as local bank equivalents for intercompany royalty inflows in a Swiss AG IP HoldCo and may trigger bank responsibility concerns, tax attribution obstacles, and inadequate AML-governance standards; acceptance depends on the specific counterparty's FINMA authorisation and the bank relationship of the Swiss entity. Accordingly, they should be used only as ancillary disbursement channels, not as the principal inbound royalty clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

CHF capital contributions from shareholders or intermediate holding companies enter Switzerland freely without SNB or SIF approval. Equity capital contributions must be labelled as such with the instruction narrative 'Equity contribution' and a stabilised purpose code, normally 'INTC' (Intercompany Payment) or 'ROYL' if routed as a license-funding royalty, as defined by the corresponding bank's SWIFT purpose-code matrix. Shareholder loans should be received under a written shareholder-loan agreement, with interest rate and repayment schedule aligned with Swiss transfer-pricing rules and OECD standards, and the remitting bank must complete standard bank-form declarations showing commercial origin. Authorised Swiss bank accounts require no exchange-control permit, but documentation inside the bank account opening file must support the source of funds, ownership chain, and the development-stage capital requirement of the AG.Outbound disbursements from the Swiss IP treasury rely on SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) as the primary rail for royalty payments to foreign licensees or upstream patent holders, with MT202 COV used only for bank-to-bank correspondent clearing. Domestic CHF settlements route through Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC/SIC4) or SIX Telekurs channels; EUR-denominated workflow runs through the TARGET2 / T2 system and SEPA Credit Transfers as permitted. Where a patent licensee requires payment against acceptance or instalment milestones, the confirming bank may issue a SWIFT MT700 documentary / commercial letter of credit or MT760 standby letter of credit as performance security; all outbound wires must include full transaction purpose and payment narrative in English, and any royalty transfer must cite the underlying licence agreement and, where applicable, the invoice or royalty statement.Bilateral OTC FX forwards with UBS, ZKB, BCV or another bank counterparty, transacted under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and a standard Swiss CSA for collateralisationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with exceptional capital controls or limited SWIFT-clearing availability, to hedge projected royalty receipts from restricted jurisdictionsCross-currency swaps (FX swap and long-term cross-currency interest-rate swaps) to manage duration and principal exposure of foreign-currency licence receivablesVanilla FX options, zero-collared tunnels, and participating forwards to maintain budget-rate certainty while preserving SME treasury flexibility under banks' existing FX-derivative credit linesCollateralised rolling spot-forward structures limited to genuine trade and royalty-transaction exposure, consistent with the AG's hedging policy and FINMA/AIFM conduct expectations
