Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax portal: www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; in Quebec, Revenu Québec / Agence du revenu du Québec administers the GST/HST, QST (TVQ), and Quebec corporate income tax — portal: www.revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or newly incorporated entity must first obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (extra-provincial registration, or federal/provincial incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act / Quebec Business Corporations Act). The federal statutory tax identifier is the 9-digit Business Number (BN). Apply through CRA online 'Register a business number' (BizReg) or Form RC1, selecting the GST/HST account and, where required, the Import/Export account. For Quebec sales tax and provincial corporate tax, register with Revenu Québec to obtain the QST file number linked to the BN/NEQ. Supporting records: certificate of incorporation or extra-provincial registration, registered office address, directors, beneficial owners, and business activity. Revenu Québec will issue a GST/HST and QST registration certificate when the registrant is not a small supplier or is otherwise required to register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A business corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Taxation Act (Quebec); it is not fiscally transparent and has no U.S.-style pass-through or check-the-box classification. Resident corporations pay federal and Quebec corporate tax on worldwide income, while non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada pay tax only on Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election for corporations; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available statutory elections relevant to a Quebec business corporation include the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) associated-company joint election for allocating the small business deduction, the eligible dividend designation election, the functional-currency reporting election (ITA s. 261), and non-calendar taxation-year elections in Quebec. The hybrid mismatch rules (disallowing deductions in hybrid mismatch arrangements) apply mandatorily and contain no elective safe harbour.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. The small-supplier threshold applies where the total value of taxable supplies made in Canada/Quebec in the preceding four calendar quarters does not exceed CAD 30,000. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded unless the only supplies are zero-rated; non-resident vendors making taxable supplies through Canadian fulfillment or permanent establishments may be required to register regardless. (No separate Quebec threshold: QST follows the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated where the sale is made to a recipient outside Canada and the goods are exported by the vendor in compliance with the Zero-Rated Supplies Regulations (ETA Sch. VI, Part V), generally before possession is transferred to the recipient or within 60 days thereafter. For oversized furniture sold online to foreign customers, the vendor must ensure the goods physically leave Canada, via common carrier or direct export, and retain contemporaneous transport evidence. Services supplied to non-residents and performed wholly outside Canada are also zero-rated; QST zero-rating applies similarly under the Taxation Act and the QST regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or CBSA-approved EDI; G13 / cargo control document
  • Bill of Lading and proof of delivery showing delivery outside Canada (carrier tracking, delivery confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment gateway records proving payment received from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Sales contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Carrier waybill and customs status records linking the shipment to the zero-rated sale

Canada and Quebec do not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing mandate. GST/HST/QST invoices must be issued for every taxable supply and must show the supplier's BN/QST number, invoice date, vendor and purchaser names, description, tax rates, and tax charged. Electronic invoicing is permitted if records are legible and retained for six years; filing of GST/HST and QST returns is electronic through Revenu Québec's CLIC or CRA My Business Account. No government pre-approval API is required before issuing invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully freely convertible; Canada has maintained Article VIII status under the IMF and has operated without exchange controls since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1958. Capital account transactions (inbound equity, shareholder loans, outbound intercompany advances) and current account transactions (trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties) require no Bank of Canada or Minister of Finance pre-authorization. Practical constraints are limited to: FINTRAC EFT reporting for each cross-border transfer equal to or above CAD 10,000; Part XIII federal withholding tax on dividend remittances (25% statutory rate, reduced to 5%-15% under a comprehensive tax treaty depending on the shareholder's jurisdiction); Quebec-specific requirements under the Taxation Act and the Quebec register of enterprises; and Investment Canada Act notification by a non-Canadian shareholder establishing a new Canadian business. Dividend and profit remittance is otherwise unrestricted; the corporation may maintain both CAD and foreign-currency ledgers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. An online retail corporation exporting oversized furniture may retain export receipt balances in USD, EUR, or any transferable currency in a Canadian foreign-currency account, or in an authorized offshore correspondent account, provided each cross-border receipt is tracked for FINTRAC EFT thresholds and reconciled to underlying sale contracts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Department of Finance Canada holds policy responsibility for the currency; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises banks on prudential matters; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CTF rules for CDD, EFT and currency reporting; Payments Canada operates the Lynx large-value clearing system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM — ancillary use only. Canada does not issue or recognise the European-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Cross-border fintechs operate either as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), or as payment service providers (PSPs) registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), whose final compliance phase came into force during 2025. A non-resident EMI cannot lawfully maintain a deposit-taking notional account for the Quebec corporation; it may only provide payment facilitation and disbursement aggregation outside the core banking relationship. Wise Payments Canada, Payoneer, and Airwallex may support low-value supplier disbursement and employee refund flows, but cannot serve as the statutory capital escrow, tax remittance, or fiduciary settlement layer. The offshore EMI must be avoided for holding foreign parent funds because Canadian banks will require the source bank to be a supervised entity in a jurisdiction acceptable to OSFI and FINTRAC.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM — ancillary use only. Canada does not issue or recognise the European-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Cross-border fintechs operate either as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), or as payment service providers (PSPs) registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), whose final compliance phase came into force during 2025. A non-resident EMI cannot lawfully maintain a deposit-taking notional account for the Quebec corporation; it may only provide payment facilitation and disbursement aggregation outside the core banking relationship. Wise Payments Canada, Payoneer, and Airwallex may support low-value supplier disbursement and employee refund flows, but cannot serve as the statutory capital escrow, tax remittance, or fiduciary settlement layer. The offshore EMI must be avoided for holding foreign parent funds because Canadian banks will require the source bank to be a supervised entity in a jurisdiction acceptable to OSFI and FINTRAC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a formal Local File or Master File filing threshold for most domestic corporations. Country-by-Country reporting is required only where consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750 million. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared whenever the corporation transacts with non-arm's-length non-residents, regardless of size, to avoid documentation failure penalties.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations, as applied in zero-tax jurisdictions, are not separately enacted in Canada. Instead, substance is tested through the general anti-avoidance rule, permanent establishment jurisprudence, beneficial-ownership tests in treaty claims, and gross-negligence penalties. A Quebec corporation that serves solely as a booking entity without real inventory, staff, or decision-making is exposed to treaty denial and Section 247 adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under Section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act: 50% to 200% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution under Section 239 carries fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years on indictment. Quebec additional penalties apply under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize false filings may be personally assessed for unremitted withholding and GST/QST; fraudulent behavior pierces the corporate veil, enabling civil seizure of personal assets, travel bans through court enforcement, and imprisonment for knowing participation.
Shell Company or Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of full Canadian taxable income in the hands of the Quebec entity; denial of treaty benefits; application of the GAAR penalty under Section 163(2) where avoidance outweighs real economic substance; potential Part XIII withholding on recharacterized payments.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company risk personal planning-penalty assessments, joint-and-several liability for taxes arising from sham structures, and criminal exposure under offshore compliance and foreign reporting penalties.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Section 247 transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length amount, plus penalty of 10% of the adjusted amount in cases where documentation is deficient; compounded with interest and late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length arrangements with affiliated overseas companies can be held accountable for planning penalties; if documentation is fabricated, criminal liability may arise for aiding false tax returns.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: Part XIII taxes are held in trust for the Crown; failure to remit triggers full liability, interest, penalties, and fines under Sections 227 and 231 of the Income Tax Act; Quebec may assert parallel collection.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes regardless of the corporation's dissolution; tax authorities may issue civil freezes, directors' liability assessments, travel bans under enforcement orders, and refer egregious cases for criminal prosecution.
AML Obligations through High-Risk Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Quebec securities or financial regulation penalties where applicable; CRA can also impose unreported-gain penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can incur personal administrative fines for willful AML non-compliance; serious money-laundering findings can lead to asset seizure, travel bans, and non-tax criminal charges.
