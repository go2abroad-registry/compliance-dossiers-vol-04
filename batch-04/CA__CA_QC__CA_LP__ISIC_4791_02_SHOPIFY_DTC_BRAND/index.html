E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada; tax classification is statutory. A Canadian LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (ITA s. 96) and is a separate registrant person for GST/HST and QST. Relevant elections: (i) ITA s. 97(2) election to transfer property to the partnership on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (ii) ETA s. 273 GST/HST joint venture election, mirrored by the QST regime, allowing a designated operator to report and remit for a qualifying joint venture; (iii) election to align the partnership's reporting period and GST/HST-QST filing frequency with CRA/RQ approvals; (iv) Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 12.7, 12.8, 18.5, 18.6) apply automatically, with only limited regulatory elections for securitization entities and no elective check-the-box for non-resident investors.

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when supplied by sale from Canada to a destination outside Canada, exported in a commercially reasonable time (CRA guidance normally expects export as soon as circumstances permit and ordinarily within 60 days of delivery), and not used or consumed in Canada before export; DTC parcel shipments by mail or courier to foreign customer addresses qualify. Services: ancillary digital and platform services supplied to non-residents (e.g., app subscriptions, digital marketing, order-processing services) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the recipient is a non-resident and the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property located in Canada; QST zero-rating rules are substantively identical. Sales to Canadian-resident consumers remain taxable at 14.975%; the zero-rate is claimed on the GST/HST-QST return and is supported by the export-evidence bundle retained in the Shopify order record.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA B13A Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form) for shipments exceeding C$2,000; for low-value DTC parcels, the Canada Post/courier customs manifest, CBSA-accepted parcel export report, or carrier-generated export declaration capturing export date, destination country, shipment value, and customs exit scan; Shopify fulfillment export records cross-referenced.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill: through bill of lading, courier air waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing shipment from a Canadian point of export to the foreign delivery address, including carrier tracking history with foreign customs entry scan and delivery confirmation.
  • Inward Remittance / Payment Evidence: bank statements and settlement reports from Shopify Payments, Stripe, or PayPal evidencing receipt of foreign-currency funds and CAD conversion, cross-referenced to order ID, payment transaction ID, and customs declaration (practical Canadian adaptation of the Inward Remittance Certificate standard).

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority digital stamping, or per-invoice pre-approval exists in Canada or Quebec for B2C e-commerce. Quebec imposes a mandatory B2B electronic invoicing duty on large supply chains (enterprises with operating revenue of C$50M or more must issue and receive e-invoices under Revenu Québec rules), which is not binding on this Shopify DTC seller. Voluntary PEPPOL interoperability is available. Mandatory electronic reporting applies through CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's Clic Revenu portal; Shopify transaction data (order, payment, fulfillment, tax export logs) must be retained for 6 years as the evidentiary base for nexus and rate application, but no government clearance API or stamping is triggered.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, with no expiry where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful default, or fraud exists. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reversed on assessment: the full 14.975% GST/HST-QST becomes payable with prescribed interest compounded daily. Additional penalties include: (i) up to 25% of net tax for gross negligence under ETA s. 280 and the corresponding Quebec penalty provisions; (ii) failure-to-register penalties and false-statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; (iii) criminal sanctions for willful evasion under ETA ss. 327–331; (iv) retrospective ITC clawback where exported goods are diverted to domestic use or where zero-rating documentation is fabricated; DTC exporters must retain the full export-evidence bundle for at least six years and respond to CRA/RQ 'export evidence' audit requests with order-level traceability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions; there are no exchange controls, no prior authorization requirements for foreign investment in a Quebec Limited Partnership (LP), and no restrictions on profit remittances or repatriation of capital. For a Canadian-domiciled LP with non-resident partners, distributions of Canadian-source income are generally not subject to Canadian withholding tax when paid to non-residents of Canada, provided the income is characterized as partnership business income and not as Part XIII withholding-tax-eligible items (e.g., management fees, interest, or royalties separately invoiced); however, non-resident partners earning Canadian-source business income may become subject to Canadian income tax filings under Section 150 of the Income Tax Act. DTC e-commerce sales via Shopify are treated as ordinary active business income, and conversion of foreign-currency settlement balances (USD/EUR) into CAD is freely permitted at any authorized bank or fintech. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) bank compliance with FATCA/CRS reporting for non-resident beneficial owners; (ii) Quebec's commercial registry transparency for ultimate beneficial owners; and (iii) potential Canadian GST/QST registration obligations for online sales, none of which restrict currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement in Canada; exporters and online sellers are not obligated to repatriate or convert export proceeds within a prescribed timeline, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. Under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act, the Canadian dollar shall be used as legal tender, but settlement in foreign currency is freely negotiable. For accounting and tax purposes, an accrual-basis Quebec LP must report foreign-currency gains and losses under subsection 39(1.1) of the Income Tax Act and must translate foreign-currency balances using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for income tax filing; however, no remittance deadline applies to the underlying funds. For robust treasury practice, the LP should proactively sweep USD settlement balances into CAD within 1-3 business days using a bank FX forward or spot order to mitigate currency depreciation risk; this is a policy choice, not a statutory mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial system authority; day-to-day FX administration is not centralized in a dedicated exchange-control ministry. Regulatory oversight of payments, clearing, and settlement systems is shared between the Bank of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for settlement/fintech payment operations; Low as a replacement for regulated banking infrastructure. Canada does not issue a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license; cross-border fintechs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, for Quebec, must also hold a money-services business license from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Québec, chapter M-30.1). For a Shopify DTC brand, such platforms are viable for (i) receiving payouts from marketplace clearing, (ii) converting USD/EUR retail proceeds into CAD, and (iii) disbursing supplier payments at lower cost. Primary legal constraints: (a) MSBs are prohibited from providing true deposit-taking or checking-account services, so all settlement balances must ultimately be swept to a Schedule I bank account; (b) Quebec licensing adds approval lead time; (c) MSBs cannot issue documentary letters of credit or provide credit facilities; and (d) significant counterparty concentration and platform risk limits their use for capital escrow or treasury reserves. Recommended arrangement: operate a Schedule I bank account as the treasury anchor and use a single FINTRAC/AMF-licensed MSB only as a payment platform pass-through.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions from a non-resident limited partner into the Quebec LP's bank account shall be routed via SWIFT MT103 through a Canadian Schedule I bank's correspondent network, with the following protocols: (i) the remitter must clearly state the purpose code and reference 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_LP - ISIC 4791 DTC E-COMMERCE' to ensure the depository bank does not classify the inflow as a loan; (ii) the receiving bank's compliance intake must receive a copy of the executed subscription/contribution agreement, the LP's REQ registration extract, and the partner's FATCA/CRS self-certification before or at the time of the wire; (iii) the principal currency should be CAD but USD inward wires are acceptable, to be immediately converted to CAD via a documented spot contract to eliminate open exposure; (iv) for transactions above CAD 100,000, the general partner must provide an executed board resolution and a source-of-wealth summary; (v) inter-company repayments or advances are discouraged unless textually labelled as inter-company loans with a clear term sheet and are subject to section 17(1) of the Income Tax Act for deemed interest; (vi) the LP must also evidence the capital contribution in its inter-entity capital account and reflect it in the annual financial statements for audit trail cleanliness.For the Shopify DTC brand operational model, outbound trade disbursements shall be executed through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-US international suppliers (factories in China, Vietnam, or Europe), using standard trade settlement instructions with a bank draft; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) as an optional instrument when the supplier demands payment security for large purchase orders above USD 250,000, but for the initial low-risk profile, TT (telegraphic transfer) prepayment is preferred to avoid L/C costs; (iii) Automated Clearing House (ACH) and Fedwire for US-based suppliers and 3PL partners, executed from the USD sweep account at the Schedule I bank; (iv) Shopify's own payout rails (direct deposit to the LP's CAD account) for settlement from Shopify Payments, with a controlled pool structure ensuring that Shopify, PayPal, and Stripe each settle directly into the LP's single treasury account rather than a third-party aggregation account; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for settlement/fintech payment operations; Low as a replacement for regulated banking infrastructure. Canada does not issue a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license; cross-border fintechs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, for Quebec, must also hold a money-services business license from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under the Money-Services Businesses Act (Québec, chapter M-30.1). For a Shopify DTC brand, such platforms are viable for (i) receiving payouts from marketplace clearing, (ii) converting USD/EUR retail proceeds into CAD, and (iii) disbursing supplier payments at lower cost. Primary legal constraints: (a) MSBs are prohibited from providing true deposit-taking or checking-account services, so all settlement balances must ultimately be swept to a Schedule I bank account; (b) Quebec licensing adds approval lead time; (c) MSBs cannot issue documentary letters of credit or provide credit facilities; and (d) significant counterparty concentration and platform risk limits their use for capital escrow or treasury reserves. Recommended arrangement: operate a Schedule I bank account as the treasury anchor and use a single FINTRAC/AMF-licensed MSB only as a payment platform pass-through.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined top general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and not a taxable entity, so this rate applies only to corporate partners on Canadian-source income allocated from the LP.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian taxable corporation; not applicable to Limited Partnership profit distributions because the LP is fiscally transparent. Remittances of a non-resident partner's share of LP income are generally treated as business income taxable under Part I, not as dividend withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions during the fiscal period generally triggers CRA contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (IC 87-2R); a CAD 5,000,000 threshold applies where the partnership's total gross revenue is below CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate ESR regime, but the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA) and Quebec's GAAR invalidate transactions lacking commercial substance. For a Limited Partnership, the limited partner's at-risk rules may disallow losses where the LP lacks economic substance; the CRA and Revenu Québec can recharacterize a sham partnership and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 2 years; Quebec Taxation Act and Tax Administration Act impose parallel provincial fines and imprisonment; repeated anti-money-laundering breaches may trigger FINTRAC penalty up to C$500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly participate in or authorize the false reporting are jointly and severally liable for penalties; courts may pierce the LP veil under Quebec Civil Code and Canadian jurisprudence in fraud cases, ordering civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, and adverse findings preventing travel abroad if an arrest warrant is issued.
Failure to File / Shell Company Abuses

Statutory sanction: CRA late-filing penalty up to C$2,500 per T5013, gross negligence penalty of 50% of additional tax payable, and interest; Revenu Québec penalty of 5% on the amount required plus 1% monthly; GST/QST failure to register carries penalties of 5% plus 10% of unremitted QST in some cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly and severally liable for GST/QST and source deductions under Excise Tax Act s.323 and Quebec QST Act; a unilateral tax debt can become personal through solvency director's liability assessments, and directors may face travel bans while an enforcement warrant is outstanding.
Transfer Pricing and Withholding Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(2) transfer-pricing reassessment and s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum C$5,000); interest compounded daily; Quebec Taxation Act applies a similar penalty. Failure to withhold Part XIII tax results in liability for the full 25% plus penalties under s.227(8) and interest.

Director personal liability: Corporate general partner directors who cause the LP to pay such fees may be de facto jointly liable for Part XIII and GST/QST remittances; in egregious sham cases, personal liability extends to the full amount of unreported income, and criminal prosecution can lead to extradition requests and travel restrictions.
