Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Kantonales Handelsregisteramt / Office du registre du commerce), acting under the supervisory authority of the Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt / Office fédéral du registre du commerce, EHRA/OFAC).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations, OR/CO), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft / stock corporation); Federal Act on the Amendment of the Swiss Civil Code, Part Five, Arts. 716a-731b (corporate governance and board duties); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung / Ordonnance sur le registre du commerce, ORC) Arts. 23-35.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 (or 20% of the nominal capital if higher) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount must be deposited into a blocked Swiss bank escrow account (Sperrkonto / compte de consignation) in the company's name and released only upon proof of registration. The remaining capital must be subscribed and payable on demand.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and building regulations: the registered office must comply with cantonal building standards, including fire safety evacuation plans and electrical installations certificates. No industrial workplace or civil defense clearance is required because the activity is office-based administrative. The employer must comply with Swiss Labour Law and the Safety at Work Ordinance (Verordnung über die Sicherheit von Arbeitnehmerinnen und Arbeitnehmern, VSA/ArGV 3), including workplace ergonomics and accident prevention; for any staff, mandatory accident insurance (SUVA) and workplace hazard assessments are required.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The Board of Directors resolves: (1) that the company's incorporation be executed by accepting the notarized Articles of Association and the required share subscriptions; (2) that the share capital of CHF 100,000 is subscribed as stated and the statutory minimum paid-up amount shall be deposited in the company's blocked bank account; (3) that the company adopts the registered office at the address indicated in the Articles and appoints the registered address for service; (4) that [Name] is appointed Managing Director (Delegate) with individual signing authority and [Name] is appointed Assistant with collective signing authority; (5) that all incorporation filings and banking formalities are authorized.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV) / Administration fédérale des contributions (AFC) / Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA), Bern — for federal direct tax and Swiss VAT; Cantonal and municipal tax authorities of the registered office for cantonal/communal corporate income tax; Register of Commerce authorities in the relevant Canton for commercial registration. Official portals: www.estv.admin.ch and www.zefix.ch.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Swiss Aktiengesellschaft: (1) establish the company by notarial deed and register it in the Commercial Register of the Canton (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce); minimum paid-in capital is CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be actually paid-up at registration; (2) upon commercial entry, the business is automatically assigned a Swiss Business Identifier (UID / Unternehmensidentifikationsnummer) in the format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx; (3) if the actual or expected worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per annum, or if voluntary or reverse-charge registration is appropriate, submit the VAT registration application to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA) through its eService platform; the UID is then used as the VAT identification number (MWST-Nr.); (4) Swiss corporate income tax registration is effected automatically by the cantonal tax authority once the legal entity is entered in the Commercial Register; a separate tax ID for direct tax purposes is issued by the canton of domicile and used on tax returns and payment orders. No shareholder residency restriction applies to a Swiss AG; but foreign founders must appoint at least one resident director and furnish bank verification, certified articles, passport copies and sworn statements as requested by the commercial registrar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Swiss AG is statutorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax at federal, cantonal and municipal levels, plus net wealth tax; no pass-through taxation is available. Within that fixed classification, the AG may invoke the following elections and reliefs: (a) participation relief/deduction (Beteiligungsabzug) for dividends and capital gains from qualifying participations of at least 10% of the investee's share capital to 10% per analog; typically requiring quality of interest and at least 12 months holding, effectively exempting such income; (b) notional interest deduction (NID) where the canton of residence has implemented it for equity financing over the business need; (c) cantonal patent/IP box regimes and R&D bonus bonuses under the Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF); (d) VAT flat-rate input tax credit (Saldosteuersätze) election for smaller businesses; (e) VAT method election between the effective net tax method and the rates/quota-based method; (f) tax-neutral reorganisation rules under Art. 61 et seq. of the Federal Direct Tax Act for mergers, demergers, and share-for-share reorganisations; (g) qualified holding company tax status at cantonal level in certain cantons (subject to transitional and TRAF limitations). Hybrid mismatch treatment is not elective; Swiss anti-avoidance and transfer pricing rules apply by law and reporting is through the OECD country-by-country framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the libre circulation of payments principle embedded in the Federal Constitution and the Federal Foreign Exchange and Payment Transactions Act. There are no surrender requirements, prior authorization hurdles, or profit remittance restrictions. Swiss francs (CHF) are freely convertible; legal tender is not capital-controlled, and dividends, royalties, and service fees may be repatriated without withholding tax on profits except for the 35% Swiss federal withholding tax on dividends, which is reduced under tax treaties. No stamp duties or exchange control approvals are required for routine operational flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Swiss law permits exporters and SaaS service providers to retain proceeds in original currency, hold offshore accounts, or convert freely through regulated banks. Only statistical reporting obligations apply under the SNB Banking Statistics Ordinance for international trans-shipment and billing positions above a materiality threshold, with no administrative conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF); foreign exchange monitoring is decentralized with mandatory reporting to SNB for positions exceeding CHF 20 million for non-bank financial counterparties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for non-licensed offshore Electronic Money Institutions. Switzerland is not part of the EEA/EMI Directive framework; foreign EMI licenses under UK FCA or EU PSD2 do not grant passporting rights into Switzerland. A foreign fintech/EMI may only provide payment services to Swiss clients through a Swiss-regulated bank as a white-label corridor or after obtaining a FINMA fintech licence (art. 1b Swiss Banking Act) and establishing a Swiss presence. Strict FINMA outsourcing and data protection requirements also apply, and non-bank issuers cannot provide full credit-scope treasury services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be routed via a pure SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's bank using ISO 20022 purpose code CAPT (Capital Payment). The receiving Swiss bank requires a board resolution of the shareholder and the Swiss corporation recording the equity subscription, an updated shareholder register extract, a notarized statement of contribution, and the tax value declaration statement to the cantonal tax office. Funds may be held in a Capital Escrow Account segregated from operational treasuries until the commercial registry increase is completed. No SNB conversion approval is needed; the payment is credited in CHF or any freely convertible currency at the receiving bank's discretion.Trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV messages cleared via Swiss SIX SIC/euroSIC rails, SEPA for EUR/CHF instantaneous where applicable, and the payment-versus-payment mechanism of CLS Bank for any simultaneous foreign exchange leg. Commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) are issued through the relationship corporate bank, with documentary presentation routed over SWIFT compatible 760/700 MT messages. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax imposed on after-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal and communal effective rate ranges from approximately 11.9% to 21.0% depending on the canton of residence of the AG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends paid by the Swiss AG from distributable profits; refundable to Swiss-resident beneficial owners and reduced or refundable under applicable double taxation agreements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is generally six months after fiscal year-end; cantonal authorities routinely grant an extension of up to twelve months upon reasoned request. For calendar-year companies the initial deadline is typically set around 31 March of the following year. Failure to file after reminders triggers an estimated assessment by the tax authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Mandatory Local File documentation is required where related-party transactions exceed CHF 5 million in a tax period or the Swiss entity reports annual gross revenue of CHF 80 million or more; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900 million; Country-by-Country reporting applies above the same CHF 900 million group revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or BVI; however, Swiss GAAR, the MLI Principal Purpose Test effective since 1 January 2022, and DTA beneficial ownership rules impose an equivalent factual substance requirement. The Swiss AG must maintain genuine premises, qualified personnel, active board decisions and substantive management in Switzerland; a letterbox holding company for logistics-tech IP or fleet-dispatch SaaS royalties is exposed to recharacterization of income, denial of treaty relief, revocation of cantonal tax rulings and criminal tax exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Simple evasion: administrative penalty of up to the amount of the evaded tax (up to three times the evaded tax in aggravated cases) plus recovery of tax and interest; qualified tax fraud: fine of up to CHF 30,000 per offense and full restitution of tax and penalties; prosecution under the Federal Criminal Administrative Law (VStrR) may result in custodial sentences of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns bear individual criminal liability; the corporate veil may be pierced for civil restitution; serious cases trigger seizure of personal assets, travel bans in mutual legal assistance procedures, and extradition exposure under international arrest notices.
Unpaid Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Default

Statutory sanction: The AG is liable for the unremitted 35% withholding tax plus late-payment interest; additional penal surcharges are imposed for negligent non-collection; proceedings under the Swiss Withholding Tax Act can yield fines equivalent to the tax withheld and punitive interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under Article 13 of the Withholding Tax Act for the duties of collection and remittance; criminal sentences of up to three years and personal asset seizure are available in aggravated cases.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment reallocating profit to Switzerland; secondary adjustment treating the excess as a constructive dividend subject to 35% withholding tax; penalty interest; fines of up to three times the evaded tax; denial of deductions; denial of treaty benefits under the MLI principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued under Article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations for corporate damage caused by tax restitution and penalties; criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion may lead to imprisonment, travel bans and confiscation of personal assets.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; recharacterization of license fees as constructive dividends; retroactive reassessment with default interest; revocation of cantonal tax privileges; administrative fines up to CHF 30,000 per offense.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for restitution of unpaid taxes and lost treaty benefits; criminal exposure for intentional abuse; in cross-border structures directors may face international asset freezes and travel restrictions.
Beneficial Ownership and Transparency Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Swiss Code of Obligations of up to CHF 10,000 per violation; forced deregistration of the entity; banking partners may freeze payment rails and terminate merchant agreements; persistent non-compliance blocks issuance of Swiss tax clearance certificates and notarial certificates of good standing.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and officers are personally liable for the fines and for resulting damages to creditors and authorities; serious breaches can lead to disqualification from board office, criminal registration and, in cross-border cases, travel restrictions.
