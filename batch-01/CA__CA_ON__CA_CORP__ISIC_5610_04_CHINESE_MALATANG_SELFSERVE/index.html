Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in law and practice. Current-account and capital-account flows — equity subscription, shareholder advances, dividend and royalty payments, loan repayments, and currency conversion — require no Bank of Canada or ISED authorization. The two practical gates are (i) Part XIII withholding tax at 25% on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to the Chinese shareholder/licensor, reduced by the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement to 10% for royalties and to 10%/15% for dividends (beneficial corporate owner with at least 10% of the voting shares / other beneficial owner); and (ii) FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. CRA compliance adds Form T106 for the related-party transactions, NR4 withholding information returns, GST/HST remittance on franchising revenue, and the Ontario employer health tax and payroll account if staff are engaged. No convertibility licence, repatriation quota, or waiting period applies to CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory CAD conversion ratio, no maximum retention period, and no equivalent to China's SAFE foreign-exchange settlement. Foreign-currency receipts (including sub-franchise royalties and overseas ingredient-association income) may be held indefinitely in CAD, USD, or CNH accounts at any Schedule I bank or Ontario credit union. An internal treasury standard of converting USD/CNH balances to CAD within two business days of receipt is nonetheless recommended to align payroll, GST/HST, and supplier cycles, and to avoid unhedged spot exposure. Every international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more is reported by the bank to FINTRAC; the corporate treasury should maintain a monthly reporting reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary institution and settlement engine for Lynx and ACSS; it exercises no FX-approval role. Responsibility for banking prudential regulation sits with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); foreign-investment vetting sits with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act; and sanctions are administered by Global Affairs Canada. Canada has no single SAFE-equivalent FX-control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium; acceptable only as a secondary rail. Canada does not create an EU-style electronic-money-institution license class; offshore fintech PSPs (subject to UK, EU, Hong Kong, or Singapore EMI licensing) are treated as foreign payment service providers and, if they hold, store, or transmit CAD, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and segregate client funds via safeguarding. Unregistered operation is an offence. An offshore EMI cannot deliver an Ontario transit-sort account for ACSS payroll debits, depository credits, or commercial L/C issuance, so it is not a lawful substitute for the primary Schedule I treasury account. Recommended use: a white-label EMI wallet collecting low-value franchise fees from international sub-franchisees, swept monthly to the Canadian operating account with balances kept below CAD 10,000 per instruction to simplify FINTRAC reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Three-phase compliant route. Phase 1 (PRC outbound gate): the Chinese shareholder completes NDRC overseas-investment filing, MOFCOM ODI approval, SAFE ODI FX registration, and a Chinese-bank capital remittance review with purpose code classified as overseas direct investment in a restaurant operating business; proceeds are converted from CNY to USD/CNH and remitted out. Phase 2 (correspondent wire): a SWIFT MT103 instruction is sent from Bank of China (Beijing SWIFT BKCHCABJ), often via a New York or Toronto correspondent, to the Ontario Schedule I bank (e.g., RBC ROYCCAT2 or TD TDOMCATTT) for credit to the Ontario corporation's CAD account; the bank records the transfer as an international EFT report to FINTRAC at the CAD 10,000 threshold; the reference line must read 'FDI CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [Ontario Company Inc.] — [Subscription No.]'. Phase 3 (Canadian post-receipt): file the ISED Investment Canada Act notification for a new Canadian business within 30 days of commencement; record the contribution as subscribed share capital or a shareholder loan in the minute book; file Form T106 where related-party transactions exceed CAD 1 million; where loan instruments are used, keep the debt-to-equity ratio within 1.5:1 to preserve the interest deduction for tax, and confirm withholding obligations on any interest remittance. Funds are released from the capital escrow subledger only upon board-approved project milestones (lease execution, fit-out, POS installation, licence grant).Two primary outbound rails. (2) Trade-payment channel: commercial letters of credit (MT700, at-sight or 60-day usance) issued by the Canadian bank for imported titanium bowls, induction cooktops, extraction hoods, seasoning blocks, packaging, and supply-chain equipment, with transport documents under Incoterms 2020 (FOB, CIF, or CPT) against a clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and inspection certificate where letters of credit are opened; below-CAD 100,000 supplier invoices may be paid by direct MT103 or documentary collection. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 aggregate fair-market-value threshold triggering contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and mandatory Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents); CRA also expects Local File documentation for each controlled transaction exceeding this threshold and Master File preparation when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CA$50,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate an explicit ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) regime such as BVI/UAE; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the judicial sham doctrine, and CRA treaty-beneficial-owner analysis collectively form a de facto economic-substance test; a Chinese-master-franchisor-controlled Ontario store with no permanent establishment, no local director, no Ontario bank account, and no substance will have its taxation recharacterized, its deductible payments disallowed, and treaty benefits denied on the ground of absence of beneficial ownership

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act ss. 162(1), 163(2), and 239 impose a civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest, and criminal penalties on summary conviction to a fine of 50%-100% of evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment up to 5 years imprisonment and a fine of 200% of the tax sought to be evaded

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under Income Tax Act s. 227.1 for corporate failure to withhold and remit source deductions once they knew or reasonably should have known of the failure and failed to act; officers who sign knowingly false T2 declarations or authorize double-bookkeeping risk personal criminal prosecution as parties under s. 21 Criminal Code, civil restitution orders, tax liens on personal property, and court-ordered passport travel restrictions where CRA initiates collection measures
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) disallows the tax benefit and recharacterizes the transaction; denied treaty benefit triggers Canadian Part XIII withholding of 25% on the full amount, plus s. 163(2) 50% gross-negligence penalty; a further interest and penalty-adjustment regime applies under the CRA's aggressive-planning framework

Director personal liability: Directors who promoted or approved the shell structure face personal liability for the assessed tax under s. 239 and OBCA director-fiduciary remedies; they can be added by CRA as statutory directors for unremitted withholding deductions, and may be subject to travel bans under a Federal Court order where unpaid assessments exceed CA$10,000 and a flight risk exists
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassesses under s. 247(2) to substitute an arm's-length consideration, denies the deduction in whole or in part, reclassifies excessive amounts as a disguised dividend or royalty subject to Part XIII withholding, and applies a s. 247(3) transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the amount of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5 million and 10% of gross revenue

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1 and for civil assessed penalties if they knowingly caused the corporation to file a false transfer-pricing disclosure; serious cases attract joint personal liability with the corporation for the reassessed tax plus interest
Cash Economy / Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Structural Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s. 462.31 (laundering proceeds of crime) is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to report a suspicious transaction to FINTRAC leads to administrative monetary penalties up to CA$1,000,000; CRA separately applies a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) for resulting income omissions

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or tacitly condone structured cash deposits can face personal criminal liability as accomplices or separate principal offences; CRA can also issue personal director liability assessments under s. 227.1 for any under-reported corporate income even if the corporation no longer has assets; travel bans and passport revocation may be ordered where the director leaves Canada
Payroll Source Deduction and Ontario Tax Remittance Failures

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 227(9) imposes a 10% late-remittance penalty plus 2% per month of unreported source deductions, with daily compound interest; Ontario Employer Health Tax Act provides a separate 10% penalty plus 2% monthly default interest, and failure to file Schedule 501 with the T2 triggers additional CA$100–CA$1,000 penalties

Director personal liability: Income Tax Act s. 227.1 makes directors liable to the full unremitted source withholding amount irrespective of whether the corporation became bankrupt; if a director received or should have received the withheld funds as a management fee, personal civil enforcement includes bank account freezes and, where a judge finds wilful non-remittance, a travel ban pending payment or an imprisonment term for contempt
