Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Federal Tax Administration FTA), Main Division Value Added Tax; associated registry authorities include the Federal Statistical Office (UID register) and the Cantonal Commercial Registers. Official portals: www.estv.admin.ch, www.uid.admin.ch, www.zefix.ch.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss-incorporated Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) Notarize the Articles and register the AG in the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister); the company automatically obtains a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer; format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) from the Federal Statistical Office. (2) Upon commencing taxable activities in Switzerland, submit a VAT registration application to the ESTV/FTA Main Division VAT within 30 days using the ESTV e-Service portal; attach the Commercial Register excerpt, Articles of Association, business activity description and beneficial-owner declaration. (3) The statutory VAT ID is the UID with the suffix MWST (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx MWST); the plain UID serves as the corporate income tax file reference at federal and cantonal levels. (4) Foreign entities without a Swiss registered office or Swiss branch performing taxable supplies in Switzerland must register directly with the ESTV under the non-established-taxpayer route; the CHF 100,000 turnover exemption does not apply to foreign non-established suppliers; the foreign registrant must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative (domiciliary agent) authorized to receive ESTV decisions and is jointly and severally liable for Swiss VAT debts. Because the CH_AG is a resident entity, domestic registration applies and no separate PE/branch registration is required where its Sitz and place of effective management are in Switzerland.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The Swiss AG is a separate taxable person subject to federal corporate income tax (statutory 8.5% of after-tax profit; effective approximately 7.8% of pre-tax profit) plus cantonal/communal profit tax (aggregate typical burden approximately 12%-21% depending on canton; Zug approx. 11.9%) and cantonal capital tax; resident AGs have unlimited worldwide tax liability subject to exemption with progression for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate; no pass-through eligibility; no tax-exempt zone status; for VAT purposes the AG is a taxable person by law unless the small-business exemption applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; Swiss tax classification follows Swiss corporate legal form (Handelsregister). Statutory elections available to this structure include: (a) VAT group taxation (Art. 21 MWSTG) for Swiss enterprises with financial, economic and organizational integration; binding for at least one five-year fiscal surveillance period and requires ESTV approval; (b) voluntary VAT registration by a business below the CHF 100,000 threshold; (c) cash-basis VAT accounting election (Art. 36 MWSTG) where prior-year turnover does not exceed approximately CHF 5.0 million; (d) tracking of capital contribution reserves to preserve privileged Swiss withholding-tax treatment on future distributions; (e) no elective hybrid-mismatch regime; Art. 58a DBG contains a mandatory unilateral anti-hybrid rule disallowing interest deductions for hybrid financing producing double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 net taxable turnover sourced in Switzerland per calendar year (Art. 10 MWSTG; CHF 150,000 for non-profit, sporting or cultural institutions). Non-established foreign suppliers have a nil threshold: any taxable supply with a Swiss place of supply mandates Swiss VAT registration. Import VAT on goods entering the Swiss customs territory arises irrespective of taxpayer turnover and is administered by the Federal Customs Administration (Art. 50 MWSTG).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported out of the Swiss customs territory qualify for VAT-exempt treatment with input VAT deduction, i.e. the economic equivalent of zero-rating (Art. 23 MWSTG), where: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or carried to a foreign destination; (ii) the supply is made to the foreign purchaser in a commercial transaction; (iii) export is proved by a Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration via e-dec/NCTS) accepted by the Federal Customs Administration; and (iv) transport documents (ocean/multimodal bill of lading, CMR, air waybill) show dispatch from Switzerland. Drummed and bagged penetration-grade bitumen sold by the CH_AG on EXW/FCA/FOB terms for containerized overseas shipment is zero-rated when the goods leave Switzerland in the same fiscal period. Services directly related to exported goods (international transport, freight forwarding, insurance, loading and inspection) are zero-rated under Arts. 24-25 MWSTG. Services supplied to recipients whose seat is abroad are outside Swiss VAT scope under Art. 7 MWSTG, so wholesale trading commissions and management margins do not attract Swiss VAT. Imported bitumen may be held in customs-warehouse suspension (Art. 30 MWSTG) so that import VAT is discharged by the subsequent zero-rated export; alternatively import VAT at 8.1% is recoverable as input VAT by the fully taxable AG.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss e-dec / Ausfuhrdeklaration with customs office acceptance reference; NCTS/T1 transit evidence for movements via EU/EFTA territory; exit confirmation from the Swiss customs territory for containerized ocean shipments).
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean or multimodal B/L showing Swiss container terminal as port of loading and a foreign port of discharge, consigned or endorsed to the overseas buyer; container and seal numbers must match the customs declaration).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment confirmation, SWIFT MT103 or TT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer; required under the compliance pipeline treasury rule for FX repatriation within 180 days from date of export).

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval API or digital stamping regime exists in Switzerland. Invoicing may be in paper or electronic form; the ESTV accepts PDF, XML and the Swiss eBill standard. Structured electronic invoicing (PDF/XML, EDIFACT/INVOIC) is mandatory only for invoices to the Swiss federal administration (B2G, since 1 January 2020). Private-sector e-invoicing is optional but common. Fiscal books, vouchers, customs declarations and export evidence must be retained for 10 years (Art. 70 MWSTG; Art. 958f CO).

The ESTV/FTA verifies export evidence arithmetically against VAT returns. Where export proof is missing, incomplete or contradictory, zero-rating is denied and the supply becomes taxable at 8.1% with default interest at 4.5% per annum; assessments may be reopened within a 10-year statutory limitation period (Art. 42 MWSTG). Tax evasion (Art. 96 MWSTG) attracts a penalty surcharge of up to three times the VAT evaded; procedural-duty violations (Art. 100 MWSTG) may draw fines up to CHF 8,000; fraudulent documentation may additionally trigger penalties under the Swiss Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law (VStrR). Non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days does not mechanically invalidate zero-rating under Swiss statutory VAT law, but it constitutes a high-scrutiny audit trigger that leads to validation of underlying export documents, beneficial-ownership facts and any intercompany financing, and may indirectly expose the transaction to reassessment and penalties if the commercial substance of the export is not fully substantiated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no approval requirement for profit or dividend remittances, and no restrictions on opening or funding CHF or foreign-currency accounts by non-residents. The sole structural hurdle is Swiss federal withholding tax of 35% on ordinary dividends or non-reportable interest paid to non-banking beneficiaries, which can be reduced or refunded under an applicable double-tax treaty; the accounting profit itself remains freely transferable after the withholding tax certificate is cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may hold receipts in any currency, use them to settle trade payables, or convert them freely. Institutional reporting obligations involve only the SNB's quarterly international payments statistics and monthly bank balance sheet statistics, collected by the intermediary bank, not the exporter.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); in respect of sanctions and export controls, the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) administers financial sanctions and export-related restrictions, while the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) licenses and supervises banks and securities firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary bank account. A non-Swiss electronic money institution (EMI) or digital payment platform cannot execute payment services in Switzerland without a FINMA licence, a passport under EU-UK equivalence (generally unavailable), or a formal agency-banking arrangement with a licensed Swiss bank. For a bitumen wholesale trader, a foreign EMI may be viable only for low-value collections from known counterparties; it should not be used for capital escrow, LC settlement, or significant trade disbursements due to sanctions exposure and limited recourse.

(ii) Funds are credited within one business day under the Swiss SIC interbank system for the CHF leg, or via Target2 for EUR/other currencies. (iii) No SNB regulatory approval is required for a capital injection, but the bank will conduct a KYC/AML threshold check on the provenance of funds, and a CHF and foreign-currency cash position report may be submitted to the SNB as part of the bank's periodic statistical reporting when cumulative flows exceed materiality thresholds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal direct profit tax (applied to net profit after tax, equivalent to 7.83% of pre-tax profit); cantonal and municipal corporate income taxes are additional and vary by canton. In the canton of Zug, the combined federal/cantonal/municipal effective rate for a stock company is approximately 14.6% of pre-tax profit.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of the financial year. For an AG with a 31 December balance-sheet date, the return is generally due by 30 June of the following year in most cantons; extensions are available upon reasoned application to the cantonal tax authority. For a non-calendar business year, the six-month period runs from the date of the annual balance-sheet closing.
