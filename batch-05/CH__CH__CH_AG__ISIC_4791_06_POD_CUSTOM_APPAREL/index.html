Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up at incorporation. The cash contribution must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened in the future company's name with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank; the bank issues an irrevocable capital confirmation under Article 652g OR. The amount is released to the board only after the company is entered in the Commercial Register. Non-cash contributions require a statutory audit report and full disclosure in the incorporation deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance from the cantonal fire authority covering escape routes, fire extinguishers, emergency lighting and electrical hazard inspection; structural load inspection under SIA 261 for printing and storage equipment; occupational health and safety clearance from Suva or the cantonal labour inspectorate; mandatory employer accident insurance under the Swiss Accident Insurance Act (UVG/LAA); and building-fire insurance issued by the cantonal building insurer (Gebäudeversicherung) for any operated premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFD), portal: www.estv.admin.ch; for VAT registration and indirect taxes, the ESTV Central VAT Office; for direct taxes, the competent cantonal tax administration of the company's corporate seat.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not domiciled in Switzerland must appoint a fiscal representative resident in Switzerland and submit the official Swiss VAT registration application (Form 201 / 202) to the ESTV, attaching certified articles of association, commercial register extract from the home jurisdiction, proof of appointment of the Swiss representative, and an estimate of Swiss turnover. Upon approval, the ESTV issues a Swiss VAT number (UID/Tax ID). For direct tax purposes, a foreign entity with a Swiss permanent establishment or taxable presence must separately register with the cantonal tax authority of the relevant canton to obtain a cantonal tax ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not domiciled in Switzerland must appoint a fiscal representative resident in Switzerland and submit the official Swiss VAT registration application (Form 201 / 202) to the ESTV, attaching certified articles of association, commercial register extract from the home jurisdiction, proof of appointment of the Swiss representative, and an estimate of Swiss turnover. Upon approval, the ESTV issues a Swiss VAT number (UID/Tax ID). For direct tax purposes, a foreign entity with a Swiss permanent establishment or taxable presence must separately register with the cantonal tax authority of the relevant canton to obtain a cantonal tax ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss tax law does not provide check-the-box classifications or hybrid entity elections for Aktiengesellschaften. Election mechanisms available under Swiss VAT law include: (i) voluntary VAT registration for persons below the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold, (ii) VAT group taxation for legally independent but financially, economically, and organizationally integrated entities, and (iii) simplified tax assessment for businesses with annual taxable turnover not exceeding CHF 5,020,000. No hybrid mismatch elections apply to foreign parent entities under domestic Swiss law; Swiss anti-hybrid rules apply in accordance with the OECD BEPS standards.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Businesses domiciled in Switzerland: annual worldwide taxable turnover exceeding CHF 100,000. Non-resident foreign entities: mandatory VAT registration where supplies performed in Switzerland exceed CHF 100,000 Swiss turnover (no threshold applies for foreign businesses supplying goods stored in Switzerland in certain cases); a Swiss fiscal representative is obligatory for all foreign VAT registrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated where tangible merchandise is physically dispatched or transported out of Switzerland and the supplier retains reliable documentary proof of export. Supplies of services to a foreign recipient are zero-rated under Swiss place-of-supply rules if the recipient's seat or business establishment is outside Switzerland, provided the conditions of Articles 8 and 9 of the Swiss VAT Act are met. For Print-on-Demand (POD) custom apparel sales, zero-rating applies to international shipments where the goods are exported directly to the end customer and the operator maintains shipping records, electronic tracking, and customs evidence. Input VAT attributable to export sales is creditable or refundable under ordinary Swiss VAT rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrerklärung via e-dec/TARES, or Swiss SAD/export manifest)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to a foreign destination
  • Export accompanying document with Swiss customs release stamp (Ausfuhrschein)
  • Commercial invoice containing exporter, importer, Incoterms, goods description, and value
  • Packing list and sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank payment confirmation or SWIFT credit record evidencing settlement by the foreign buyer (secondary proof)

Switzerland currently mandates no real-time electronic invoicing, no API fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping. VAT invoices must satisfy Swiss formal requirements under Art. 26 VAT Ordinance and must use the Swiss QR-bill standard for domestic payment slips. The Federal Council has approved a roadmap introducing mandatory e-invoicing (Peppol-based) for domestic B2B transactions from January 2026; until then, exchange of invoices remains paper-based or via voluntary EDI/e-billing channels.

Zero-rating claims are subject to enhanced documentary scrutiny by the ESTV and cantonal authorities. If the required export proof is missing or defective, the VAT initially zero-rated is retroactively assessed at the standard rate (8.1%) together with late-payment interest. Intentional or systematic supply of false export documents, fictitious POD transactions, or sham refund claims constitutes tax evasion under Article 70 of the Swiss VAT Act, leading to penalties of up to three times the evaded VAT, criminal prosecution, seizure of goods, and cancellation of the VAT registration. The federal limitation period for reassessment is ten years in cases of tax evasion; for ordinary omissions the period is five years. Export files must be retained for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible and Switzerland maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account liberalization, consistent with IMF Article VIII status and OECD Capital Movements Code obligations. No exchange-control license, central-bank permit, or profit-remittance authorization applies to dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan repayments, or capital liquidation proceeds of a Swiss AG. The Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at the statutory 35% rate applies only at distribution of dividends or Swiss bond interest and is reduced or refunded under applicable double-taxation treaties (e.g., 0% for qualifying parent-company participations under the Swiss parent-subsidiary regime; 15% for many treaty-resident private shareholders); this is a fiscal obligation, not a currency-convertibility hurdle. SNB statistical reporting obligations are ex-post, purely informational, and never a precondition to remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio of foreign currency into CHF. The POD retail company may hold 100% of USD, EUR, or GBP settlement funds indefinitely in Swiss or foreign bank accounts, and may denominate its treasury assets in any currency. Only ordinary Swiss profit taxation applies to accrued net income irrespective of currency situs: federal corporate income tax of 8.5% (effective) plus cantonal/communal flat taxes (e.g., Zug combined effective corporate income tax rate of approximately 11.85%); no tax is levied on unremitted export receivables beyond the accounting accrual basis.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank, with prudential banking and AML supervision by FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) and trade/export policy oversight by SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs). The SNB administers monetary policy, issues CHF, and operates the SIC clearing system; the Foreign Exchange Act (SR 941.13, in force 1 January 2023) imposes a retrospective balance-of-payments statistical reporting regime for cross-border transactions above CHF 25,000, executed via the reporting bank or directly by the reporting entity, but grants no prior-approval authority over market convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for FINMA-licensed domestic Swiss fintechs; Low-Medium for offshore non-Swiss EMIs. A FINMA fintech license (Art. 1b Banking Act) permits public deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million without interest; eligible domestic providers (e.g., Radicant by ZKB, Yapeal, neon) can host low-risk e-commerce operating accounts and are fully interoperable with SIC/SEPA. Crypto-capable FINMA-licensed banks (e.g., Sygnum Bank, SEBA Bank) are viable where the Zug 'Crypto Valley' Web3 nexus triggers digital-asset settlement, though enhanced due diligence applies. Offshore EMIs (UK, Lithuania, France, Germany e-money licensed) are not members of SIX Interbank Clearing and cannot hold direct Swiss IBANs; they operate through a Swiss correspondent omnibus account in the EMI's name, exposing the merchant to sub-account segregation risk and non-bank counterparty credit risk. Legal constraints: taking deposits from Swiss resident customers requires a Swiss banking license (Art. 1 Banking Act), and a non-Swiss EMI cannot act as the primary treasury bank of the AG; it may be used only as a supplementary payout-collection rail for marketplaces, with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the principal Swiss Tier-1 account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory share capital of the CH AG is CHF 100,000 nominal, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up (Art. 621, 632-633 OR). The foreign subscriber pays the capital contribution in CHF (or foreign currency converted at the SNB daily reference rate) by SWIFT MT103 directly into a blocked capital-contribution account (Kapitaleinzahlungskonto) opened in the name of the company in formation at a Swiss SIC-member bank (UBS or ZKB recommended). The bank then issues the capital-payment attestation (Kapitaleinzahlungsbestätigung) required by the notary and the Commercial Register Office; funds must be irrevocably at the free disposal of the board upon registration. For subsequent capital increases, the incoming cross-border transfer uses SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity funds) or SEPA pain.001 for EUR, referencing the company name, the ISIN/security number, and 'Kapitalerhöhung' in field 70; funds are held in a blocked sub-account until the increase is entered in the Commercial Register. No SNB or FINMA prior authorization is required; cross-border inflows above CHF 25,000 are included in the SNB balance-of-payments statistical reporting performed by the bank. The beneficiary must match the registered company name exactly and the remitter must be the subscriber to avoid an attestation delay.For POD supplier payments (typically print-on-demand fulfilment vendors in USD, EUR, GBP, or CNY), the standard clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 open-account payment from the Swiss operational account through a correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, T2 for EUR, and CHAPS for GBP). EUR payments to EU suppliers settle via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) or SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) using ISO 20022 pain.001/pacs.008 messages with near-real-time finality; domestic CHF supplier invoices settle via SIC instant payment (SIP) at 24/7/365 finality. For high-value procurement or inventory pre-financing exceeding approximately USD 250,000, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (UCP 600, SWIFT MT700) or standby LC (ISP98) may be opened, but is uneconomic for ordinary POD open-account purchases. Marketplace and payment-processor settlements (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments, Etsy) are received as daily batch payouts to the operational account; treasury policy should mandate a daily FX sweep converting surplus USD/EUR/GBP to CHF only when needed, using the bank's algorithmic FX conversion desk at mid-market rates. All disbursements require a delegated-authority matrix, dual-approval e-banking release, and positive-pay file-based reconciliation controls.Deliverable foreign exchange forwards (FX outrights) — permitted for hedging EUR/USD/GBP revenue streams from POD customer settlements against CHF operating expenses, under an ISDA 2002 or Swiss Master Agreement with any Tier-1 Swiss bank; maturities and notional must correspond to underlying commercial receivables.FX swaps (swap point transactions) — permitted for treasury liquidity management, converting excess foreign-currency settlement balances into CHF for a fixed period with an agreed reverse leg, without creating open market risk.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) — permitted for balance-sheet hedging of intercompany loans, equity funding, or future capital expenditures, with full ISDA/CSA or Swiss Master Agreement documentation; commonly available from UBS, ZKB, and BCV dealing rooms.European-style over-the-counter FX options — permitted for capping adverse conversion rates on non-CHF sales quotes (e.g., fixed-price USD collection sweeps) while preserving upside, subject to option-premium accounting under OR 959 and the bank's product-appropriateness assessment.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — legally permissible under Swiss law and not governed by exchange controls, but not market-standard and generally unavailable at Swiss Tier-1 banks for CHF because the CHF is fully convertible and always deliverable; NDFs should be considered only for illiquid emerging-market customer currencies, and only with a FINMA-regulated counterparty booking through a Swiss dealer.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for FINMA-licensed domestic Swiss fintechs; Low-Medium for offshore non-Swiss EMIs. A FINMA fintech license (Art. 1b Banking Act) permits public deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million without interest; eligible domestic providers (e.g., Radicant by ZKB, Yapeal, neon) can host low-risk e-commerce operating accounts and are fully interoperable with SIC/SEPA. Crypto-capable FINMA-licensed banks (e.g., Sygnum Bank, SEBA Bank) are viable where the Zug 'Crypto Valley' Web3 nexus triggers digital-asset settlement, though enhanced due diligence applies. Offshore EMIs (UK, Lithuania, France, Germany e-money licensed) are not members of SIX Interbank Clearing and cannot hold direct Swiss IBANs; they operate through a Swiss correspondent omnibus account in the EMI's name, exposing the merchant to sub-account segregation risk and non-bank counterparty credit risk. Legal constraints: taking deposits from Swiss resident customers requires a Swiss banking license (Art. 1 Banking Act), and a non-Swiss EMI cannot act as the primary treasury bank of the AG; it may be used only as a supplementary payout-collection rail for marketplaces, with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the principal Swiss Tier-1 account.
