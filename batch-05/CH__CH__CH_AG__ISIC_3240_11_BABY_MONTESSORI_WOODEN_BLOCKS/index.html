Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Relevant statutory elections for a Swiss AG: (a) tax-neutral reorganization election under the Swiss Merger Act and Art. 61/64 DBG allowing carry-over of hidden reserves in conversions, mergers, demergers and transfers of assets; (b) real-estate step-up election (disclosure of hidden reserves) upon transfer of 90%+ of share capital of a real-estate-rich AG under Art. 8(4) StHG; (c) election to repay distributable capital contribution reserves (Einlagenrückzahlung) as tax-free shareholder distributions under Art. 20(3) DBG, subject to the 'reserve of the reserves' floor; (d) VAT option under Art. 22 MWSTG to waive the rental/exemption status of real property and instead tax the supply at the standard rate in order to claim input-VAT deduction; (e) corporate-restructuring rulings enabling retroactive participation-relief optimisation. Hybrid-mismatch exposure is managed through the Swiss main-purpose anti-abuse doctrine and double-tax-treaty organic interpretation because Swiss law contains no check-the-box or statutory hybrid-double-dip election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 per calendar year of cumulative taxable turnover from supplies of goods and services (excluding exempt real-estate leasing and certain exempt supplies) under Art. 10(2)(a) MWSTG; foreign suppliers of services and digital supplies taxed at the place of the Swiss recipient must register if the annual turnover from Swiss-source supplies exceeds CHF 100,000 under Art. 10(2)(c) MWSTG; a Swiss commercial-register entry does not of itself trigger VAT registration but rebuts the small-business exemption if the statutory threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Swiss law zero-rates the export of physical goods when the supplier proves that the goods were transported from Switzerland into a foreign customs territory or into Liechtenstein by virtue of a valid Swiss customs export declaration and the power of disposal over the goods has passed to the foreign purchaser or its agent (Art. 23(2) MWSTG, Art. 37 and 39 MWSTV). Export of services is zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules where the recipient is established or habitually resident outside Switzerland and the service is economically used or exploited outside Switzerland (Art. 23(1) MWSTG, Arts. 7-8 MWSTG). The sale of eco-friendly beechwood Montessori sensory blocks qualifies for 0% provided the goods physically exit Switzerland, the export is customs-cleared, and the documentary bundle is retained. Swiss VAT law does not impose a statutorily fixed FX-repatriation period for 0% validity; the 180-day benchmark below is adopted by the exporter as internal documentary-discipline and external-audit tolerance standard.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhrschein / Sortie de marchandises) generated via the NCTS/Asycuda-world e-customs system, evidencing physical exit of the beechwood sensory blocks from the Swiss customs territory into the EU/EFTA or overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list named to the foreign buyer/consignee, stating the consideration, quantity, HS/ISIC coding and INCOTERMS delivery basis (EXW/FOB/CIF)
  • International transport document: ocean Bill of Lading (HBL/MBL), Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note with Swiss customs electronic release reference
  • Bank proof of export proceeds received from abroad (SWIFT MT103/MT202 advice or inward remittance bank certificate evidencing the foreign source of consideration)
  • VAT ledger posting sheet showing the zero-rated export turnover and corresponding input-VAT recovery reconciliation for the batch
  • Order and shipping records including ISIC 3240 batch identifiers, and where applicable the customs warehouse or temporary-export endorsements
  • For offshore services: written contract, foreign-recipient identity and business-establishment confirmation, and end-use certificate evidencing economic utilisation outside Switzerland

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or pre-clearance API requirement exists under Swiss VAT law. Mandatory electronic filing of VAT returns and corrective declarations through the ESTV 'e-tax MWST' portal. The QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the compulsory payment slip standard for invoice payments. Electronic invoicing is voluntary in the private sector (supported standards: Peppol BIS, Swiss Sword/EPRO-4.0, EANCOM); the Federal Administration requires structured electronic invoices (e-Bill/EPD) for B2G transactions. A general mandatory 'E-Rechnung' regime is under legislative review but is not yet in force.

The ESTV requires all export evidence to be retained for the statutory ten-year books-and-records period. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical exit or the receipt of foreign consideration in a VAT audit, the 0% rate is retrospectively revoked and the supply is reassessed at the standard 8.1% rate from the original supply date, with default interest at the FTA rate schedule and a late-payment surcharge under Art. 86 MWSTG. Intentional or grossly negligent evasion triggers administrative penalty of up to three times the tax evaded under Art. 69(1) MWSTG and, for deliberate export-VAT fraud, criminal proceedings under Art. 84(2) MWSTG with imprisonment up to three years or fine; in addition, the input-VAT deduction on the fraudulent export batch is disallowed and ancillary customs penalties apply under the Customs Act for false export declarations. Fabricated or backdated export documents to obtain refunds constitute criminal forgery and tax fraud under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 146) and can lead to debarment from simplified export procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF). There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on capital imports or exports, no limitations on dividend, interest, royalty, or principal repatriation, and no prior approval regime for cross-border payments. The Swiss franc is freely convertible in both spot and forward markets. Legal tender and monetary policy affairs, including the management of official reserves and the publication of the Swiss franc reference rates, are governed by the SNB. For a Swiss AG, the only practical obstacles are prudential bank due diligence and Swiss withholding tax and stamp duty considerations, not statutory convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export-proceeds surrender duty and no mandatory repatriation timeline. A Swiss exporter is not required to convert foreign-currency export receipts into CHF, nor to remit them to Switzerland within a prescribed statutory period. The statutory accounting currency for financial statements is CHF under the Swiss Code of Obligations and Swiss GAAP FER, but treasury policy may retain foreign-currency Balances. The SNB does not prescribe any mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export-settlement and FX conversion decisions are made at treasury discretion, subject only to bank internal documentation and Swiss governing law.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - the central bank of the Swiss Confederation, operating under the National Bank Act (NBG/NBA) with oversight from the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF); the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervises the banking and payment institutions that execute treasury transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium as a standalone primary banking solution; Medium as an ancillary payment collection rail. Offshore fintech EMIs (e-money institutions, payments institutions, or digital banks, whether UK, Lithuanian, Maltese, or Singapore-incorporated) do not hold a Swiss banking licence unless they operate through an authorised FINMA branch or hold a FINMA fintech licence under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act. Such an EMI cannot legally accept public deposits nor characterise itself as a Swiss bank. For a Swiss AG that must place its registered capital and receive commercial bank references, an offshore EMI is insufficient for the statutory capital escrow requirement. An EMI may nevertheless be used as a supplementary e-wallet rail to collect Amazon Pay or Stripe payouts, provided legal terms do not misrepresent it as a Swiss bank and the AG maintains its principal CHF treasury account with a Swiss licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Swiss AG should be remitted by the non-resident shareholder via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA for EUR) into a segregated Swiss CHF capital-transition account, with the payment reference set to 'Bareinlage' (cash contribution) or 'Kapitalerhöhung' (capital increase). Because Switzerland imposes no foreign-exchange control, no prior SNB certificate is required, but the receiving bank will ask the German-language purpose/comments field to reflect 'Direktinvestition / Aktienkapitalerhöhung' for internal BOP classification. The contributing shareholder must also provide a bank or wealth source-of-funds statement that demonstrates the lawful origin of the CHF 100,000 minimum capital. The bank will reconcile the incoming transfer against the board resolution and storage account details; after the capital is verified and reflected on the company's balance sheet, it can be released to the operational account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal statutory corporate income tax rate (effective federal rate 7.83% after deduction of the tax itself); combined federal + cantonal/communal effective rate ranges from approx. 11.9% (Zug) to 24.0% (Geneva); base-case assumed Zug AG effective rate approx. 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) levied on gross dividend distributions to all shareholders; for Swiss resident recipients the tax is fully refunded; for non-resident shareholders the 35% rate is mitigated only to the extent of an applicable double tax treaty (ordinary portfolio rate 15%; 5% for shareholdings ≥10%; 0% under the Swiss-EU dividend tax agreement for qualifying parent-subsidiary structures).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no statutory monetary threshold for mandatory Local File/Master File preparation; ESTV administrative practice applies a reduced documentation obligation where intercompany transactions total less than CHF 1 million (services/intangibles) or CHF 3 million (goods and financing) per year. The Swiss CbC reporting law (implementing OECD BEPS Action 13) requires a Master File and Country-by-Country Report for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least CHF 900 million; all other AGs must be prepared to submit full TP documentation within 60 days of an ESTV request irrespective of size.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as such, but a functional substance doctrine is binding under Swiss tax abuse case law and the Federal Act on Tax Reform and AHV Financing (STAF). The Swiss AG manufacturing Montessori wooden blocks must evidence real business substance: own leased or owned office/premises and warehousing, sufficient qualified payroll in Switzerland, board meetings and strategic decisions taken in Switzerland, independent management of commercial risk, and locally managed bank accounts. Entities engaged in pure intercompany trade, IP-holding, financing, or regional headquarter functions require enhanced substance: economic activity of at least 1/3 of gross income from personnel and physical premises, active exercise of control over subcontractors, and no automatic subordination to foreign instruction. Mailbox companies without such substance are denied treaty benefits, the participation deduction, and any cantonal tax holiday, and the 35% WHT is fully applied at source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung (DBG Art. 176; SHIG Art. 39)

Statutory sanction: Federal penalty equal to the evaded tax (minimum CHF 1,000) and up to 100% for first offence; qualified fraud using falsified records or forged agreements triggers a fine of 1.5 to 3 times the evaded tax (DBG Art. 186); cantonal surcharges of up to 100% of the evaded cantonal tax; default interest at 3.5% per annum on reassessed amounts; criminal prosecution for qualified tax fraud with imprisonment up to 3 years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto managing shareholder are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax plus penalties under Art. 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR). In cases of criminal tax fraud, directors face imprisonment under Swiss Penal Code Art. 146, seizure of private Swiss bank accounts, and a preventive travel ban (StPO Art. 220) when the prosecutor establishes flight risk; foreign directors are the subject of a Swiss extradition request via INTERPOL.
Failure to Deduct or Remit Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuergesetz Art. 26, 49, 50)

Statutory sanction: ESTV retroactively assesses the full 35% WHT on the gross distribution, plus default interest of 3.5% per annum; administrative penalty up to 100% of the unremitted amount (Art. 196/197 DBG in conjunction with VStG); reclaim of WHT is lost where the statutory deadline has lapsed; repeated violations lead to publication of the taxpayer's name and a tax security order covering future distributions.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the unremitted WHT as statutory representative of the AG; OR Art. 754 gives the tax authority a direct claim against the personal estate of each board member who approved the distribution. Negligent non-remittance of the withheld tax constitutes criminal misappropriation under Swiss Penal Code Art. 138; the Swiss courts regularly order the passport seizure and a travel prohibition for directors who seek to leave Switzerland pending prosecution.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Substance Fiction

Statutory sanction: Denial of all treaty benefits: the 35% Swiss WHT is fully retained with no refund; recharacterisation of the company's income as profit distributed to the actual owner; revocation of the participation deduction and patent box benefits; fines up to CHF 50,000 for procedural non-compliance and mandatory dissolution proceedings under revised Swiss corporate law if no economic activity can be established; the entity is reported under OECD automatic exchange of information as a 'paper company'.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the shell structure are liable for restitution of all taxes avoided, jointly and severally, and are personally exposed for damages under OR Art. 754; deliberate misuse of the Swiss-EU agreement constitutes fraud against the contracting state and is prosecutable with imprisonment; Swiss prosecutors may issue an asset freeze on the director's personal accounts and a travel ban (StPO Art. 220) pending criminal investigation; director disqualification of up to 5 years may be pronounced under the Swiss Criminal Code.
AML / Money Laundering (Bundesgesetz gegen Geldwäscherei und Terrorismusfinanzierung; Swiss Penal Code Art. 305bis and 305quater)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years with aggravating circumstances); criminal fine up to CHF 500,000; confiscation of the AG's bank assets and the personal assets of the involved directors under Penal Code Art. 70; administrative sanctions by FINMA or the Swiss FIU including licence withdrawal (where the AG acts as financial intermediary) and a ban on professional activity for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and de facto controllers face personal criminal liability for intentional laundering (imprisonment) or negligence (fine up to CHF 150,000 under Art. 305quater). The Swiss Federal Prosecutor routinely imposes travel bans and INTERPOL Red Notices for foreign directors; the company's banking mandate is terminated by the financial institution under Swiss banking due diligence rules, and directors are held civilly liable for all compliance-induced losses of the AG and third-party creditors.
