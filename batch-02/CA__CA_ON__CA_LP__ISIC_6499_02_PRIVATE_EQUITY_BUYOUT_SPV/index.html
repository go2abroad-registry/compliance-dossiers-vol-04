Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario provincial corporate tax and employer health tax; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html and https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1. A non-resident must register for GST/HST if it is carrying on business in Canada and its taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters, or if it is subject to mandatory registration for specified digital services. The Ontario Limited Partnership is registered through the Ontario government registry, and Ontario tax accounts are administered under the same CRA BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1. A non-resident must register for GST/HST if it is carrying on business in Canada and its taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters, or if it is subject to mandatory registration for specified digital services. The Ontario Limited Partnership is registered through the Ontario government registry, and Ontario tax accounts are administered under the same CRA BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian law; a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent by default. Available elections include income/loss allocation among partners under Income Tax Act s. 96(1), the joint non-arm's length supply election under Excise Tax Act s. 156, and optional returns for non-resident partners under Income Tax Act s. 216. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 18.4) may recharacterize certain payments but do not depend on an entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured over four consecutive calendar quarters of taxable supplies; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded, with separate mandatory registration rules for specified digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated if exported by the supplier for delivery outside Canada within the prescribed time limits and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and the supply is not in respect of real or immovable property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service performed in Canada, and the supply does not relate to prescribed activities. The supplier must obtain and maintain evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore nature of the service.

  • Customs export declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export reporting, including B13A or electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods or services and consideration
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Proof of payment or consideration received, such as bank wire transfer or SWIFT confirmation
  • CRA Business Number and GST/HST registration number
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the service recipient, such as foreign registration certificate or incorporation document, for offshore services zero-rating

No federal statutory real-time fiscal clearance mandate is currently in force. Electronic invoices are recognized if they satisfy CRA record-keeping requirements under Excise Tax Act s. 286 and Income Tax Act s. 230. Canada has launched a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service Provider Network, but no national API-based clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance requirement is mandated for GST/HST purposes.

The CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies where documentary requirements are not met, resulting in immediate GST/HST liability, interest, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act (ss. 280, 285, 286). False or fraudulent claims may attract civil penalties of up to 25% of the amount claimed, gross negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act. Retrospective clawbacks can reach back four years from the date of reassessment, or longer where there has been misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no statutory controls or licensing requirements for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or liquidation proceeds. Non-resident investors generally face no convertibility barriers; however, remittances may attract withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (Canada) subject to applicable tax treaty relief, and banks must be provided appropriate ownership and source documentation for economic substance validation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation. Exporters may retain and dispose of foreign currency at their discretion, and internal treasury policy should align with corporate cash management objectives and hedging programs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign exchange administration exists in Canada, and monetary policy is conducted through a freely floating currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium. Canada does not have a distinct Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category. Offshore digital EMIs or fintech payment service providers may operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, but they are not chartered banks and cannot hold deposit balances under Canadian banking law. For a private-equity SPV requiring large capital commitment escrows, syndicated wires, and operational liquidity, reliance on an offshore EMI is not a substitute for a tier-1 Canadian Schedule I bank relationship. They may serve as ancillary payment conduits if registered and FCA-/OSFI-compatible, but enhanced due diligence and ring-fencing mandates will reduce their viability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for investor capital commitments is SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank funded transfers). Remitter must be the actual investor with a clear fund beneficiary. Payment reference should state 'Capital Contribution under LP Agreement' and the underlying investment mandate. Purpose code under Statistics Canada classification for direct investment should be '6010 - Direct investment in Canadian enterprise' or the bank-mandated equivalent. The bank will require pre-notification via SWIFT field 70 and may request a copy of the capital call notice and executed LP commitment schedule. Ontario banks also require confirmation that the inbound funds do not derive from FATF-listed non-cooperative jurisdictions. Verification involves matching the remitter name and UBO information against the pre-onboarded investor registry; all incoming funds must clear the bank’s internal sanctions and AML filters before final crediting to the operational account.Primary clearing rails for settlement are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border operational expenses, management fees, or transaction disbursements; for syndicated loan closings, the bank may use SWIFT MT202 COV for large institutional wires. For underlying trade finance, Commercial Letters of Credit are issued via SWIFT MT700 or collateralised by cash letters of credit. Domestic CAD disbursements may clear through Payments Canada, using the Real-Time Rail (RTR) or large-value transfer system (LVTS) for high-value same-day settlement.
