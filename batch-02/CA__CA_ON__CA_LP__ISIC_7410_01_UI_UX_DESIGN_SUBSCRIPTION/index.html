Ontario imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership and does not require an escrow account. Capital contributions, allocation of partnership interests, profit-sharing, withdrawal rights, and additional funding obligations should be documented in the limited partnership agreement. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized and convertible currency regime with no exchange controls on either current or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible, and the Bank of Canada does not require approval for foreign investment inflows, capital subscriptions, or profit repatriation. Non-resident partners of an Ontario limited partnership may contribute capital and receive distributions without foreign exchange authorization. Remittance of Canadian-source income to non-residents is subject to Part I tax filing obligations for the partnership income allocable to non-resident partners, with treaty-based reduction available; interest paid to arm's-length non-residents is generally exempt under the Interest Exception. There are no statutory hurdles to converting CAD into foreign currency for legitimate commercial purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. Design subscription revenue received in USD or other foreign currency may be retained in Canadian domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, converted at negotiated commercial rates, or hedged freely. Financial institutions must report electronic funds transfers (EFTs) equal to or greater than CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC within 5 business days; the limited partnership has no direct surrender or licensing obligation, but must record all cross-border receipts on its books and report partner allocations annually on the T5013 Statement of Partnership Income.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange policy administered by the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act; anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); prudential banking regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Highly viable as a collection and clearing layer. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.), Stripe Billing, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate in Canada as registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, not as Canadian Schedule I banks. Business accounts are feasible for an Ontario LP receiving USD and CAD design-subscription payments, converting currencies at low spread, and disbursing to contractors. Legal constraints: (i) funds are not CDIC insured because the EMI is not a deposit-taking institution, though client funds are safeguarded in trust at Canadian chartered banks; (ii) the LP must verify that its target payment rails, Stripe Billing and Wise, are configured with the LP's registered business name and Ontario LP registration number; (iii) Ontario's provincial MSB licensing regime applies to the service provider, not to the LP subscriber, but the LP should obtain the EMI's licensing evidence; (iv) for substantive liquidity, tax payments, and large partner distributions, a Schedule I bank account remains mandatory. Feasibility rating: High for receivables and small payments; Medium as sole treasury host due to the absence of credit facilities, CDIC coverage, and full banking relationship services.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident individual partners holding greater than 25% of the partnership without a Canadian tax identification number or verifiable foreign identification

Root cause: Non-resident individual partners holding greater than 25% of the partnership without a Canadian tax identification number or verifiable foreign identification

Defense evidence: Notarized certified copy of government-issued photo ID, documentary proof of residence, foreign tax identification number, bank reference letter from the partner's home institution, signed source-of-wealth declaration, and certified true copies of the Limited Partnership Agreement and Register of Limited Partners.
Layered ownership structure involving a corporate general partner and multiple limited partners, creating uncertainty on ultimate beneficial ownership

Root cause: Layered ownership structure involving a corporate general partner and multiple limited partners, creating uncertainty on ultimate beneficial ownership

Defense evidence: Complete organizational chart down to each natural person, certified extract from the Ontario Business Registry, executed LLP/LP annual return, statutory declarations from each partner confirming no nominees or bearer interests, and a clear rationale for the LP structure in the subscription and treasury mandate.
Cross-border design-subscription revenue received in USD from clients located in jurisdictions with elevated money-laundering or sanctions risk, or from ambiguous card-not-present web payments

Root cause: Cross-border design-subscription revenue received in USD from clients located in jurisdictions with elevated money-laundering or sanctions risk, or from ambiguous card-not-present web payments

Defense evidence: Monthly transaction ledger exported from Stripe Billing and Wise, executed client subscription agreements with legal entity names and business addresses, copy of the public website privacy policy and terms of service, consolidated invoice history, and a third-party sanctions-screening report confirming no OFAC/EU/UN vetting alerts.
Large upfront capital injection exceeding CAD 100,000 from a non-resident limited partner in a single wire transfer

Root cause: Large upfront capital injection exceeding CAD 100,000 from a non-resident limited partner in a single wire transfer

Defense evidence: SWIFT MT103 confirmation with remitter name matching the partnership agreement, 12 months of bank statements of the funding partner, executed capital subscription and contribution agreement, source-of-funds narrative supported by tax returns or asset transaction evidence, and a notarized letter from the sending bank confirming the funding chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident partner to the Ontario LP's Schedule I bank account naming the LP as beneficiary with its registered legal name and LP number. The purpose of payment must clearly state 'Paid-in capital contribution to limited partnership' under Balance of Payments classification for capital contributions. Banks report any EFT of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC; no advance regulatory approval is required, but the LP must record the contribution in the partner's capital account, issue a capital account statement, and reflect the amount on the T5013 Schedule 50 partner ownership form. For follow-on distributions, the LP may repatriate capital freely; if the partnership interest is taxable Canadian property, a non-resident partner disposing of the interest should obtain a section 116 compliance certificate to protect the buyer or issuer from withholding exposure.Primary clearing rail for cross-border vendor settlements is SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer; for recurring small-value subscription-related payments to overseas designers and agencies, the LP should use Wise Business batch payments or commercial ACH/SEPA for EU suppliers. Domestic CAD clearing uses Payments Canada's Lynx for high-value wires, the Automated Clearing Settlement System for batch retail credit, and the Real-Time Rail for instant low-value payments (commercial rollout ongoing). Commercial documentary credits (UCP600 letters of credit) are permissible and may be issued by the Canadian bank for material vendor contracts, but are operationally unnecessary for intangible design services; standby letters of credit can be used for milestone guarantees. All outbound transfers must carry ISO 20022 structured remittance information, a unique end-to-end transaction reference, and must not route through sanctioned financial intermediaries.Deliverable outright FX forward contracts (USD/CAD vanilla forwards) with a Canadian Schedule I bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, enabling the LP to lock the CAD value of future USD design-fee receivables for tenors up to 24 months.Deliverable FX swaps (USD/CAD spot-forward rollovers) for treasury liquidity bridging between subscription revenue collection dates and partner distribution dates.Cross-currency swaps (USD/CAD) converting USD-denominated cash flows into CAD liabilities to achieve synthetic CAD funding while preserving USD operational liquidity.European-style FX options (plain vanilla calls, puts, zero-premium collars, and risk reversals) documented under the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions, with settlement through a licensed Canadian derivatives dealer.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible in Canada for currencies subject to offshore settlement restrictions, documented under ISDA terms and reported under provincial derivatives trade reporting rules; for USD/CAD, NDFs are redundant because CAD is fully deliverable in a liquid onshore market.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the limited partnership level (fiscally transparent; each partner is taxed on allocated income). For a Canadian-resident corporate partner, the applicable combined federal/Ontario headline rate is 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents. Because a limited partnership does not legally pay dividends, profit distributions to a non-resident partner are not subject to Part XIII withholding on dividends; the non-resident partner's allocable share of Canadian-source business income is taxed directly under Part I on a net basis, with treaty overriding where applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a Canadian partnership with only resident partners: within 5 months after the fiscal year-end. For a partnership with a non-resident partner, or a 'large partnership' (gross revenue over $5M or assets over $10M): on or before the 15th day of the 6th month after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and filed where the Canadian partnership (or partner) has reportable transactions with non-resident non-arm's length persons and the aggregate gross revenue from all such reportable transactions in the fiscal year exceeds 1,000,000 CAD.

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in Canada, but CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 ITA, judicial substance-over-form principles, and specific anti-hybrid rules under ss.12.8 and 18.4. A limited partnership must have genuine commercial activity; a bare shell engaged solely in tax-driven arrangements risks re-characterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (wilful omission of income)

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross-negligence penalty on understated income plus interest; criminal: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years under s.239 ITA.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be jointly and severally liable for CRA assessments under s.224(1.2) for deductions withheld but not remitted; directors' personal liability is limited to remittance type taxes, but a general partner is unconditionally liable for partnership debts. Travel bans under the Income Tax Act can be imposed for serious non-compliance.
Shell Company / Sham Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership arrangement as a sham or void transaction under GAAR (s.245), re-assess the foreign partner as carrying on business in Canada, apply 5% penalty pursuant to s.245(5) (plus interest), and disallow all deductions.

Director personal liability: In a sham finding, general partners who participated in the tax gimmick may be jointly liable for the reassessed tax and penalties; a non-resident limited partner can be personally assessed if the LP is deemed to be a corporation or if he or she participated in management (loss of limited-partner shield).
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: As an MSB (if applicable), administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for businesses and CAD 500,000 for individuals, plus up to CAD 1,000,000 per day for infringement of record-keeping rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners of a partnership that operates an unregistered MSB can be held personally liable for fines, and senior officers face imprisonment up to 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
Illegal Distribution / Return of Capital

Statutory sanction: The Ontario LP Act gives creditors a claim; a limited partner who receives an illegal distribution may be required to repay it, and the general partner is personally liable to the credit union or third-party creditor for the full debt outstanding.

Director personal liability: A general partner is personally and jointly liable for partnership obligations; a limited partner who participates in control of the business loses limited-liability protection and becomes personally liable for the partnership's debts. Criminal consequences may follow if the distribution was made with intent to defraud creditors.
