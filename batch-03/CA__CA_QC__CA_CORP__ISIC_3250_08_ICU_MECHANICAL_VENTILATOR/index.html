Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal income tax, GST/HST and Business Number (BN) issuance; Revenu Québec (Quebec Revenue Agency) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial tax administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account through canada.ca; Revenu Québec Mon dossier and CLIDE.

E-government filing portal

Once the Canadian corporation is incorporated federally or in Quebec, obtain the federal Business Number (BN) by registering via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Add a GST/HST program account suffix (e.g., RT0001). For Quebec QST, obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, then register for QST with Revenu Québec using Form LM-1 or the corresponding online application. The statutory TIN is the BN; the QST registration number is a secondary provincial identifier. Foreign shareholders and directors are permitted; no minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; a Business Corporation is per se a separate taxable entity. No hybrid mismatch election exists for a resident Canadian corporation; hybrid mismatch adjustments under Income Tax Act section 18.6 apply mandatorily. Corporate rollovers such as subsection 85(1) and capital dividend elections under subsection 83(2) do not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 of taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter, or in a series of four consecutive calendar quarters. A small supplier may elect to register voluntarily; an exporter of zero-rated medical ventilators should register to recover input tax credits and claim export refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of goods, such as ICU mechanical ventilators, where the supplier exports or causes the goods to be exported and delivers them to a place outside Canada, with proper export evidence retained. Offshore services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person who is not present in Canada at the time of performance, unless the service relates to Canadian real or immovable property or falls within an ETA/QSTA exception. The supplier must be a GST/HST and QST registrant and retain proof of export or foreign delivery for six years.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic export report) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with complete product description, quantity, HS code, and foreign destination
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical delivery to a place outside Canada
  • Sales contract or purchase order from the non-resident hospital, distributor, or governmental buyer
  • Proof of export, including carrier certificate or customs export movement records
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent SWIFT wire/letter-of-credit settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government e-invoice pre-approval exists at federal or Quebec level. Invoices must show the supplier's GST/HST registration number, QST number, rates, and tax amounts. Electronic records and returns are accepted through CRA and Revenu Québec electronic filing/API channels, but no clearance token is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year limitation period, and without limitation in cases of fraud, willful misrepresentation, or gross negligence. Deficient export documentation can cause retroactive denial of zero-rating, clawback of input tax credits, and assessment of GST/QST, interest, and penalties. Civil false-statement and gross-negligence penalties generally equal the greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the tax or credit amount at issue; criminal tax evasion convictions may result in fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible under both current and capital account. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control law; there is no restriction on conversion or repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, branch profits, or liquidation proceeds. Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and does not require central bank approval for current payments or capital transfers. Cross-border equity injections and profit repatriation are accommodated by all Schedule I banks without exchange control authorization. Tax-driven hurdles may exist: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and outbound interest/royalty payments under Income Tax Act section 212, treaty-based rate reductions under the Canada-US tax treaty if the recipient is treaty-qualified in a tax information exchange jurisdiction, thin capitalization / excess interest and financing rules under ITA sections 18(5) through 18(5.1) and 212.3, and Quebec-specific withholding obligations administered by Revenu Québec. For a Quebec business corporation, capital dividends and paid-up capital reductions need prescribed corporate resolutions to avoid deemed dividends. No exchange-administered 'current account imbalance' surcharge or deposit requirement exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion deadline for export proceeds. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency balances offshore or convert them to CAD at any time. The only legally binding constraints are record-keeping obligations under the Income Tax Act (six-year retention), correct accrual recognition of revenue in the year earned, and arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany export arrangements. For invoicing involving EDC-supported receivables, exporters must follow EDC's disbursement and claim documentation, but this does not create a central bank conversion obligation. There is no requirement to liquidate export receipts within a fixed number of days and no percentage of proceeds must be sold to the sovereign authority.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central bank; there is no standalone Canadian foreign exchange administration agency. Foreign exchange regulatory matters are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada (policy), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (prudential oversight of financial institutions), and the Canada Border Services Agency (trade transaction documentation). The Bank of Canada operates the high-value clearing system and has statutory authority over the national currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Legal constraints: under the Bank Act of Canada, only entities licensed as banks (Schedule I/Schedule II) or authorized deposit-taking institutions can receive deposits in Canada. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and unlicensed neobanks cannot open domestic deposit accounts or provide Canadian clearing-eligible CAD accounts without a partnership with a regulated Schedule I bank. A foreign EMI may lawfully operate as a money service business (MSB) if it registers with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, but this does not confer banking powers, does not grant CDIC deposit insurance, and does not provide direct access to the Lynx high-value payment system. An offshore EMI can be used for cross-border payout tagging, card-issuing rails and fintech 'bridge accounts' as a secondary liquidity optimization layer, but the primary treasury must remain with a Canadian bank.
