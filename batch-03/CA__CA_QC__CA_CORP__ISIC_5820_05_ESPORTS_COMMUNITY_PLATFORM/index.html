A foreign corporation must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online service or Form RC148. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act or Quebec Taxation Act for a business corporation; all corporations are entity-level taxpayers. Elections available are limited to specific corporate reorganizations (e.g., section 85 rollover, section 88 wind-up), deemed year-end elections under section 249.1, and QST voluntary/immediate registration elections under the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for zero-rating under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier by common carrier, or delivered to a common carrier for export, and proper export documentation is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident that are not performed in Canada are zero-rated under section 25; QST zero-rating rules under the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax mirror these criteria for supplies exported from Quebec.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System record), including AES/CIES export report
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and proof of export contract
  • Bank confirmation or inward remittance certificate showing receipt of foreign currency payment
  • Cargo control or freight forwarder documentation where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API requirement currently applies. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices and XML/EDI records, but do not require pre-clearance of individual invoices.

CRA may reassess within four years of the date of assessment, or indefinitely in the case of negligence, misrepresentation, or fraud. False export claims expose the taxpayer to gross-negligence penalties under section 163.2 (maximum 50% of the understated tax), interest, foreign-exchange gain reassessments, and, for serious tax evasion, criminal prosecution under sections 327 and 327.1 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible fiat currency without exchange-control licensing. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalized; Canada does not maintain a foreign exchange approval regime or repatriation permit framework for trade, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital injections. Profit remittance from a Quebec business corporation is unrestricted at the FX level, though outbound dividends and management fees are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; Quebec counterpart under the Tax Administration Act) and must be supported by auditable board resolutions and financial statements to satisfy the payer's withholding obligation. Sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, as well as FINTRAC cross-border reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000), remain the only practical gates on convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation timeline for export or service proceeds. Receipts in CAD or foreign currency may be held in any eligible Canadian or foreign bank account indefinitely. Reporting obligations are limited to (i) FINTRAC large cash transaction reports (CAD 10,000 or more), (ii) electronic funds transfer reports (EFTR) for international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) cross-border currency and monetary instruments reports for physical movements exceeding CAD 10,000, all under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision; foreign exchange surveillance is indirect through market operations and the Currency Act; no designated foreign-exchange control administration analogous to a ministry of finance exchange-control division exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-conditioned viability. Offshore digital EMIs and payment facilitators (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are permissible as ancillary settlement and payout conduits for foreign micro-disbursements, but a Quebec-registered esports platform must maintain its primary CAD treasury and tournament prize-clearing account at a CDIC-member Schedule I bank. Legal constraints: (i) under the Bank Act, non-bank EMIs cannot accept Canadian deposits for reinvestment purposes without a federal banking license; (ii) payment service providers operating in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and establish a compliant risk-management and incident-response framework; (iii) USD settlements must traverse a US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) to be compatible with Stripe/PayPal treasury cycles; and (iv) prize-pool fiduciary duties favor a segregated escrow designation linked to the tournament platform rather than an aggregated EMI float.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT with a precise remittance narrative, e.g., 'Share capital contribution - Esports Community Platform' or 'Intercompany loan - Esports Platform CA', including the legal entity name, Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), and the beneficiary bank's 3-digit institution number and 5-digit transit number. CAD wires settle through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the managed Real-Time Rail (RTR); USD wires clear through the bank's US correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS). Canada does not impose a formal FX purpose-code schema, but FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting (EFTR) applies to international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and the receiving bank will require the remittance purpose to be captured for internal and CRA reporting. Intercompany capital surplus and shareholder loans must be contemporaneously documented with a written funding agreement, prescribed interest-rate benchmark, and transfer-pricing file in order to avoid Part XIII withholding characterization or CRA recharacterization upon later repayment. Verification of inbound routings is achieved via SWIFT gpi end-to-end tracking (UETR), the account statement, and a reconciliation against the corporation's cap table and share-purchase agreements.Outbound disbursements employ (i) SWIFT MT103 for single high-value cross-border wires (e.g., sponsor fees, LC reimbursements, non-resident prize payouts above CAD 10,000); (ii) CAD domestic wire via LVTS or ACSS batch EFT for resident participants and operational vendors; (iii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for hardware, equipment, or licensed-content procurement requiring documentary compliance (proforma invoice, bill of lading, insurance certificate); and (iv) low-value mass tournament payouts via card-network payout rails or open-banking batch APIs integrated with the core payment gateway (Stripe/PayPal) to avoid MT103 fragmentation. A clearing-rail governance matrix must define acceptance rules, FX conversion point (account currency-level vs transaction-level), and settlement reconciliation timestamps. Legal constraints: (i) under the Bank Act, non-bank EMIs cannot accept Canadian deposits for reinvestment purposes without a federal banking license; (ii) payment service providers operating in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and establish a compliant risk-management and incident-response framework; (iii) USD settlements must traverse a US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) to be compatible with Stripe/PayPal treasury cycles; and (iv) prize-pool fiduciary duties favor a segregated escrow designation linked to the tournament platform rather than an aggregated EMI float.
