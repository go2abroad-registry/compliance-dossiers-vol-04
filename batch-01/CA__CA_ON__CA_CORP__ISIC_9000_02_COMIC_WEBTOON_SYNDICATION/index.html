Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), operating the My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; the Ontario HST is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial harmonized tax agreement on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

For a Canada-incorporated Business Corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act through the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch; (2) apply for the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service or Form RC1, electing the GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and corporate income tax (RC) accounts in the same application; (3) file the corporate initial return (Form RC160) with the CRA within the prescribed period; (4) for a non-resident-controlled corporation, complete the CRA non-resident ownership schedules and consider treaty-based thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing documentation, noting that the BN is the statutory TIN equivalent for corporate income tax and the GST/HST registration number is an RT-program account under the same BN. For a foreign entity with no Canadian incorporation that carries on business in Canada, it must apply to the CRA for a BN and applicable program accounts using the online Business Registration service (Form RC1) once it ceases to be a small supplier; non-resident digital suppliers of specified digital services must register when their Canadian-source digital sales exceed CAD 30,000 on a trailing four-quarter basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian Business Corporation (Inc. / Corp.) is a resident Canadian taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act, separate from its shareholders, with no pass-through eligibility; it may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) for the small-business deduction and as a taxable Canadian corporation for Part IV dividend taxes and refundable dividend treatment, but its income is always assessed at the entity level.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. Available elections are: (i) functional currency election under section 261 of the Income Tax Act (ITA); (ii) tax-deferred section 85 rollover elections on transfers of property to the corporation; (iii) section 216(1) election for non-residents to compute tax on Canadian rental income on a net basis; (iv) GST/HST group election for closely related corporations under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act; (v) GST/HST quick method and streamlined accounting elections; and (vi) in hybrid mismatch circumstances, CRA will apply the Canada-US Income Tax Convention Article IV(7) resident/entity tie-breakers and the hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules in ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5, with the corporation remaining opaque and fully subject to Part I tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding financial services and capital dispositions) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or the higher small-supplier threshold of CAD 150,000 for public service bodies; non-resident suppliers of specified digital services or digital platform operators connecting Canadian customers must register once Canadian-source digital supply revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or over the trailing four quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) direct exports of goods under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, where goods are exported within 60 days of the supply and documentary proof of export is retained; (ii) supplies of intangible personal property, including digital webtoon content and streaming-platform licences, to non-resident recipients who are not registered under the GST/HST and who are outside Canada, provided the supply does not relate to real property or tangible goods situated in Canada at the time of supply (section 10 of Part V, Schedule VI); (iii) exported services supplied to non-residents that are performed wholly outside Canada or that meet the section 7 conditions in Part V of Schedule VI; and (iv) remote digital sales made through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to end consumers whose billing address, credit-card country, and IP geolocation evidence demonstrate non-Canadian residence and consumption. The corporation must obtain and retain contemporaneous written evidence of non-residency to sustain the zero rate.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CADEX electronic filing for commercial goods; G7 postal label for low-value mail shipments)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / carrier certificate evidencing physical export of goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and purchase orders for the exported goods or digital supplies
  • Digital delivery and consumption logs, IP-geolocation and billing-address records, and Apple/Google platform sales reports substantiating non-resident end-consumption for zero-rated intangible property

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control architecture, or digital stamping requirement. Taxable registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or via registered accounting software, and the CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing pilot using Peppol interoperability. Invoices must contain the prescribed information under Schedule I to the Excise Tax Act, and records must be retained in readable or machine-readable format; digital platform operators and payment intermediaries are subject to the CRA non-resident digital economy registration and information-reporting framework effective 1 July 2021.

CRA export verifications scrutinize zero-rating claims and require the exporter to demonstrate goods actually left Canada or digital supplies were consumed by non-residents; deficient documentation results in reassessment restoring HST at 13% with arrears interest and a seven-year reassessment window, extended to ten years for neglect or misrepresentation and without limitation where fraud was committed. False or negligent statements attract a penalty under subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to 50% of the net tax, plus gross-negligence penalties under subsections 163(1)/(2) of the Income Tax Act where income tax adjustments stem from fabricated export evidence; intentional false statements, evasion, or record suppression may be prosecuted under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no foreign exchange controls on inward or outward remittances. Profit remittance barriers are limited to tax compliance: dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced to 5% for dividends under the Canada-US tax treaty for qualifying shareholders), but there is no FX approval requirement for such distributions. For digital webtoon publishing, revenue sourced from foreign platforms (Apple, Google) can be repatriated into Canadian chartered bank accounts without a requirement of prior Bank of Canada authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligations apply to export receipts. Canada does not mandate any timeline or ratio for selling foreign currency export earnings. Exporters may retain funds in CAD or permitted foreign-currency accounts at their discretion, subject to ordinary record-keeping for Income Tax Act, FATCA, and CRS reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign exchange administration. Financial institution supervision is delegated to OSFI and AML/ATF oversight to FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium as a stand-in for a full Canadian banking relationship. Offshore digital EMI/neobank platforms generally act as money-service businesses (MSBs) and, when operating in Canada, must be registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. They cannot accept deposits in the strict chartered-bank sense, do not qualify as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act, and cannot provide irrevocable clearing capabilities such as SWIFT E&F or direct CDSX membership. They may be used supplementary for FX and small volume payments, but capital injections, treasury concentration, and LC issuance require a federally regulated Canadian Schedule I Bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions in a taxation year triggers the documentation standard; a CAD 5,000,000 aggregate threshold triggers enhanced documentation and may be subjected to Transfer Pricing Memorandum review.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have sector-specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the manner of offshore centres, but the CRA vigorously applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the interpretation of 'beneficial ownership' in tax treaties, and Ontario's permanent establishment nexus rules. A webtoon syndication business claiming treaty or holding-company benefits must demonstrate actual decision-making, employees, office facilities, and substantive operational activity in Canada—or Ontario specifically—commensurate with the revenue it records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Concealed Webtoon Platform Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) not exceeding 50% of the understated tax; statutory interest and accounting of all unpaid balances.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participated in or silently benefited from the evasion risk personal prosecution, restitution orders, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, and in cases of fraudulent diversion, piercing of the corporate veil resulting in joint and several liability. Civil fraud and breach of fiduciary claims can be pursued by receivers or creditors.
Non-Remittance of Part XIII Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s.227(1) ITA equal to 10% of the unremitted amount (6% if paid by the lottery-covered director?), together with daily compound interest; continued failure is a separate offence and can be prosecuted as evasion.

Director personal liability: Under s.227.1 ITA, every director of the corporation is jointly and severally liable to pay the unwithheld amounts, interest, and penalties if the corporation has not paid them. Director assessment does not require a finding of intent; bankruptcy does not automatically shield directors, and provincial wills or CRA civil enforcement may include asset freezes and travel restrictions in egregious cases.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Content Licenses and Service Fees

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to adjust the profit allocation under s.247(2) and impose a penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment. The taxpayer is penalized unless reasonable efforts and contemporaneous documentation can be proven.

Director personal liability: Directors are not personally liable for s.247 transfer-pricing penalties unless treasury fraud or unpaid withholding tax results; however, if the adjusted tax cannot be collected from the corporation, directors may face s.227.1 trust liability for any Part XIII withholding component. Inwilful mispricing cases, directors could be charged as accomplices to tax evasion.
Sham / Shell Company or Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterise the transaction under GAAR (s.245 ITA); the benefit is denied and a 25% GAAR penalty applies if the dominant purpose was avoidance. Reassessments include full tax, compound interest, and potential Part VI.1 tax on excessive dividends.

Director personal liability: Since the first mask is a shell, the corporate veil is readily pierced: directors who signed as nominees and administered the shell can be assessed for the denied tax and penalties, and if travel interdicts or contempt orders are made where shell-dissolution/asset transfers occur, personal liability follows.
