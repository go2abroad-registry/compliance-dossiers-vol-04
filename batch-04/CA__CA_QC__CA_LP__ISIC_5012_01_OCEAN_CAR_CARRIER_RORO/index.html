Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None — Quebec limited partnerships have no statutory minimum paid-up capital. Limited partners may contribute cash, property or services actually rendered, and the liability shield is confined to the contributed amount credited to the capital account. No statutory escrow account is required; partnership funds are held in an ordinary commercial bank account of the partnership.

Funding & unlock path: Safety Management Certificate (ISM Class A) issued by an accredited classification society; fire safety and means-of-egress certification for any Quebec terminal interface; structural load and mooring inspections of ramps, decks and terminal berths; CNESST workplace health and safety registration and inspection clearance for all Quebec-based personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: None — Quebec limited partnerships have no statutory minimum paid-up capital. No statutory escrow account is required; partnership funds are held in an ordinary commercial bank account of the partnership.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — GST/HST program, Business Number portal at canada.ca/business-number, GST/HST NETFILE. Quebec: Revenu Québec (RQ) — Quebec Sales Tax (TVQ/QST), QPIP, CNESST; entity registry: Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) via Services Québec.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership operating Ro-Ro vessels into Canadian waters must: (1) determine Canadian nexus and whether taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold; (2) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account (RT0001) by filing Form RC1 (onshore) or RC167 (non-resident registration); (3) register with Revenu Québec for QST using Form LM-72-V and obtain the NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec for the declaration immatriculation of the société en commandite; (4) file the annual partnership information return T5013 (and Quebec schedule) to allocate income to partners. No entity-level income tax is payable because the LP is transparent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election regime; the Quebec LP is statutorily transparent under ITA s.96 and the Civil Code of Québec. Available elections and rules affecting fiscal identity: (i) ITA s.96(3) election for a non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period; (ii) ETA s.179 elections are restricted to closely related corporations and are not available to an LP; (iii) a non-resident carrier may elect simplified GST/HST non-resident registration to recover GST on Canadian inputs; (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangements are not elective — ITA s.18.4 disallows deductions for hybrid loan/royalty structures where classification differs between Canada and the counterparty jurisdiction; (v) GAAR (ITA s.245 / ETA s.274) applies to artificial conduit or credit-stripping structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the federal GST/HST and Quebec QST small-supplier threshold, measured on taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four calendar quarters or the current quarter. A non-resident Ro-Ro ocean carrier must register once Canadian taxable supplies (e.g., marine freight, cabotage, port services) exceed C$30,000, but may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits and QST refunds on Canadian port, fuel, repair and charter costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V (supplies of tangible personal property genuinely exported) and Part VII (transportation of goods to or from Canada and between non-Canadian points). For a deep-sea Ro-Ro vehicle carrier, the international ocean freight leg is zero-rated at 0% GST and 0% QST when vehicles are carried from a Canadian port to a foreign port or between foreign ports; stevedoring, harbor dues, towage and domestic cabotage legs, if any, remain taxable at 5% GST + 9.5% QST. The exporter/carrier must prove actual export through bills of lading, customs export records and freight settlement evidence; invoicing must reference the zero-rated supply under the ETA. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day figure is a commercial banking/SWIFT LC norm and is not a legal condition precedent to the claim.

  • CBSA export reporting — Form B13A Canada Export Declaration or eManifest marine export transmission filed before vessel departure for vehicle cargo loaded in Canada, with applicable thresholds and CUSMA variations for non-controlled goods
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill issued by the Ro-Ro ocean carrier evidencing the contract of carriage, voyage number, and foreign discharge port
  • Commercial invoice, stowage/cargo manifest and packing list identifying the vehicles, IMO vessel, origin/destination and freight payable
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT700 bank advice evidencing foreign-currency settlement of freight within 180 days

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or proprietary invoice-validation API exists federally or in Quebec. Mandatory requirements are limited to electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns by all registrants (GST/HST NETFILE / Revenu Québec E-TRANS). E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Interoperability Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0); paper and PDF invoices remain legally effective. CBSA marine eManifest filings are customs security transmissions, not indirect-tax clearance stamps.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit zero-rating claims within four years of the filing date, extended to seven years for neglect, carelessness or wilful misrepresentation. Unsupportable zero-rated exports are reassessed at 5% GST + 9.5% QST with compounded daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (about 8%–9% for corporate arrears in 2024–2025) plus penalties: failure-to-register penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 1% of taxable supplies (ETA s.280); false-statement or omission penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s.285); analogous QST penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes are subject to criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 (fines and imprisonment up to five years), loss of refund/rebate capacity, extended reassessment, and GAAR recharacterization (ETA s.274 / ITA s.245).

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no exchange-control restrictions on foreign investment, capital injections, profit repatriation, or FX transactions. Practical remittance hurdles are tax-oriented rather than FX-oriented: payments to non-resident partners from Canadian-source income of a fiscally transparent limited partnership are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, mitigated by an applicable income tax treaty; treaty relief requires appropriate Canadian beneficial-ownership forms and documentary evidence from the foreign partner. Bank cash-management due diligence may impose source-of-funds evidence for large inbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender foreign currency, to convert export proceeds into Canadian dollars, or to repatriate receipts within any statutory period. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad. Reporting obligations are AML-based, principally FINTRAC large-transaction and terrorist-financing reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, plus normal corporate record-keeping and tax reporting of foreign-source income and foreign affiliates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no dedicated foreign exchange administration is established because Canada maintains no exchange-control regime. Financial-market conduct, prudential supervision, and AML/FX-related administration are exercised by OSFI and FINTRAC respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium only as a supplementary treasury overlay, not as a primary clearing or escrow bank. Canadian non-bank fintech payment entities must be registered as money service businesses with FINTRAC, and are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Payment Canada rule sets; they generally cannot accept deposits or provide bank-grade liquidity services. An offshore digital EMI without a licensed Canadian agent and FINTRAC registration has low viability: it cannot directly access Canadian clearing, LVTS, or domestic correspondent banking. A regulated Canadian EMI may support cross-border payments, foreign-exchange execution, and multicurrency virtual accounts, but all onboarding, sanction screening, and KYC remain bank-mediated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners to the Canadian limited partnership must be routed by SWIFT MT103 for USD and cross-border CAD, using the Canadian bank's SWIFT BIC, beneficiary Canadian clearing code (institution-transit-account), and an explicit payment narrative stating 'Equity contribution / capital injection to Canadian limited partnership engaged in ISIC 5012 Ro-Ro vessel operations'. The payment must be free of unrelated goods-and-services language to avoid misclassification. Verification protocol includes bank KYC evidence of the source of funds, capitalization table, partnership resolution authorizing the account, and a statement that the contribution is not a loan and carries no repayment obligation. Canada has no exchange-control approval requirement for equity injections.Operational trade disbursements, including vessel charters, port fees, bunker suppliers, crew payments, repairs, and logistics vendors, should be settled through SWIFT MT103 using a Canadian ring-fenced operational account. For larger or secured trade obligations, commercial letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707, with compliance documents presented through the confirming bank. CAD-denominated domestic payments may clear through Payments Canada and LVTS; USD payments clear through the bank's United States correspondent via CHIPS or Fedwire; EUR and other currencies clear via SWIFT European correspondent networks.
