Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec – clicSÉQUR Affaires portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada/Quebec must first obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or using the Business Registration Online service. The BN must include the applicable program accounts, notably the GST/HST account (RT). For Quebec, the partnership must separately obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and register for QST through Revenu Québec’s clicSÉQUR Affaires portal. The registration must identify the general partner and, where a non-resident general partner acts for the LP, designate a Canadian representative and maintain books and records at an accessible Canadian address. No separate TIN/VAT ID is issued to the partnership at the federal level for income tax because the LP is tax-transparent; each partner reports its allocated share using its own BN or social insurance number.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent for Canadian and Quebec income tax; each limited and general partner is taxed on its allocated share of income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent for Canadian and Quebec income tax; each limited and general partner is taxed on its allocated share of income. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is itself a separate registrant and is treated as an entity-level taxpayer for indirect tax purposes).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; an LP’s pass-through status is mandatory. Relevant statutory elections for a cross-border catering LP include: (i) the GST/HST joint venture election under subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act, and the corresponding QST joint venture election, to have a designated operator of an in-flight catering joint venture account for net tax as a single registrant; (ii) elections relating to nil consideration among closely related entities where applicable through corporate partners; and (iii) the hybrid mismatch rules under Canada’s BEPS Action 2 implementing legislation are mandatory, non-elective anti-avoidance provisions that deny double-deduction or deduction/non-inclusion benefits when a Quebec LP is treated as transparent in Canada but opaque in a foreign partner’s jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for the small-supplier threshold (both GST/HST and QST). Non-resident partnerships carrying on business in Canada may be required to register even below the threshold when they make taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a business carried on in Canada or solicit orders for tangible personal property delivered outside Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating may be claimed for GST/HST and QST when the LP supplies in-flight meal trays, beverages, and cabin stores to a non-resident airline and the supplies qualify as exported supplies under subsection 23(1) of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI. This requires delivery at a Canadian airport onto an aircraft destined for a destination outside Canada, with the goods uplifted and removed from Canada in the course of an international flight. Related services, such as tray-up assembly, palletization, food preparation, and ramp delivery performed for the non-resident airline, are also zero-rated where the tangible personal property is exported or the service is supplied to a non-resident and the benefit is realized outside Canada. Domestic-flight catering, ground meals, crew meals consumed in Canada, and supplies to Canadian-resident entities are taxable at the combined 14.975% rate unless a separate zero-rating provision applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed electronically through the Canadian Goods Exported Reporting System (CERS) or an equivalent electronic export declaration where the CBSA requires reporting of uplifted aviation stores)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / Flight Manifest (master airway bill, cargo manifest, or airline uplift manifest identifying the flight number, destination airport, departure date, and catering load quantity)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX statement showing settlement received from the non-resident airline and the repatriation of exchange proceeds to Canada
  • Catering uplift/delivery receipt signed by the aircraft captain or airline handling agent, including the flight number, route, meal count, and CBSA confirmation where applicable

Canada and Quebec do not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls for GST/HST or QST. Electronic tax invoices are accepted if they contain the prescribed information and are delivered in an unalterable electronic format. Revenu Québec requires QST invoices to be presented in French or in French and another language and to display the supplier’s QST registration number. The in-flight catering operation may generate invoices, warehouse uplift manifests, or EDI/API-based settlement documents, but the system must preserve an audit-ready electronic trail and permit extraction by CRA and Revenu Québec auditors. No government API clearance is required for export zero-rating.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rating claim within the normal four-year reassessment period, and without limitation where a misrepresentation is attributable to fraud, wilful default, or gross negligence. Civil penalties for knowingly making a false or misleading tax statement include a penalty of up to 25% of the tax or net tax sought to be evaded, plus arrears interest and refusal of input tax credits. Revenu Québec applies parallel QST penalties under Quebec sales tax legislation. Tax evasion prosecution can lead to fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment. A successful audit clawback is retroactive to the original supply date, zero-rating refunds and rebates are repaid with interest, and the partnership’s general and limited partners may be jointly and severally liable for the partnership’s GST/HST and QST liabilities while partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with no general government exchange-control regime; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and Canada observes Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Current-account transactions (trade settlement, raw food imports, packaging, equipment leases) and capital-account movements (non-resident LP capital contributions, partner remittance, loan repayments, acquisition of foreign assets) are unrestricted. Profit remittance obstacles are tax-based, not FX-based: a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must allocate business income to partners via the T5013 Partnership Information Return and the Quebec Relevé 15; non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their share of Canadian-source business income through the LP, and distributions are not subject to Part XIII withholding unless they represent interest, royalties, rent, guarantee, or management fees, in which case Part XIII withholding of up to 25% applies unless reduced by an applicable Canada tax treaty. Quebec-specific taxation under the Taxation Act (Quebec) and Revenu Québec filing obligations apply in parallel.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender rules, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Inflight catering receipts invoiced to foreign airlines may be retained in USD or other foreign currencies indefinitely for working capital purposes. The applicable obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting by the bank of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (or equivalent); (ii) complete recordkeeping of export transactions under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations; (iii) if foreign receipts are held offshore, the LP must reflect the amounts in Canadian financial statements and in the T5013/Relevé 15 capital account reconciliation, and the partners must consider the foreign accrual property income rules and attribution rules under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority of Canada; Payments Canada operates the national clearing and settlement infrastructure (Large Value Transfer System transitioning to the ISO 20022 Lynx service, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System); the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) conducts prudential supervision of Canadian chartered banks and federal financial institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and monitors cross-border electronic fund transfers; in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises provincial financial services, insurance, and securities distribution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and capital escrow facilities; Medium for ancillary cross-border disbursement rails. The Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) restricts deposit-taking in Canada to authorized banks; offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar platforms) generally do not hold a Canadian banking licence and cannot lawfully accept the Quebec LP's deposits as their own liabilities. A legally viable structure restricts fintech use to a non-depository payments facility where the EMI is a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) and the LP is identified as the ultimate beneficial owner; such rails may handle low-value, high-frequency supplier disbursements and cross-border crew or contractor payments, but all inbound capital contributions and the end-of-day cash sweep must settle to a Schedule I bank account or an authorized Canadian foreign bank branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are routed by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian bank account; the domestic CAD leg clears through Payments Canada's high-value system (LVTS, transitioning to Lynx with ISO 20022). The MT103 must state the full legal name of the LP, the account number, and an explicit purpose description such as 'LP capital contribution under limited partnership agreement dated [date]' so the beneficiary bank can match the wire to the KYC profile. Canada imposes no foreign-exchange control purpose codes, but the bank will require the subscription agreement and source-of-funds evidence for each contribution of CAD 10,000 or more. USD-denominated contributions may be converted to CAD at the bank's spot rate or held in a USD sub-account at the LP's election; no forced conversion applies.
