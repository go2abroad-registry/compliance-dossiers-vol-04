Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - official national tax administration; primary portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario provincial corporate income tax is administered by the Ontario Ministry of Finance under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act, with the CRA acting as a federal-provincial collection agent.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a CRA Business Number (9-digit BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online through the CRA portal or by mail, with applicable programme accounts appended as BN suffix identifiers (e.g., RP for GST/HST, RT for corporation income tax, RC for payroll). Non-resident corporations carrying on taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST, and those earning Canadian-source income must register for Part I of the Income Tax Act. Ontario incorporation is effected through the Ontario Business Registry (ServiceOntario); the federal BN is thereafter used for Ontario corporate income tax filings and allocations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election regime; a Business Corporation is taxed at the entity level. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) the section 156 Excise Tax Act election among closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate GST/HST supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) Ontario corporate income tax allocation elections and the federal-provincial deemed allocation rules; (iii) hybrid mismatch designation and deduction/non-inclusion tracking positions under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3; and (iv) the section 211.1 Excise Tax Act deemed-supply election for registrants establishing a Canadian permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a supplier must register once worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated exports and financial services, exceed CAD 30,000 in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Non-residents exceeding this threshold, or electing to register, must register before making taxable supplies in Canada; large registrants face accelerated monthly instalment and filing requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act: (a) for goods exported from Canada where the goods are delivered outside Canada within 60 days; and (b) for services supplied to a non-resident person, subject to carve-outs for services in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or a non-resident recipient registered for GST/HST in respect of a Canadian business. Professional anti-dumping and trade-remedy defense consulting (ISIC 7490) supplied to non-resident exporters qualifies as a zero-rated exported service where the client is a non-resident, the benefit is consumed principally outside Canada, and the subject goods are situated outside Canada at the time of performance; zero-rated supplies must be reported on the GST/HST return to sustain input tax credit and refund entitlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13, Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/SWIFT evidence of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants issuing taxable supplies above CAD 150 must provide invoices containing the prescribed information under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act, including supplier BN, invoice date, description, and tax payable. The CRA imposes electronic record retention standards, accepts structured XML/EDI invoice formats without prior approval, and administers filing and payment through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account channels.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period and may assess at any time in the case of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud. Undocumented or unsubstantiated zero-rated exports are reclassified as taxable at the 13% HST rate (Ontario) plus interest under sections 296-298 of the Excise Tax Act. False statements or omissions attract a minimum penalty of CAD 250 plus 25% of the additional net tax under ETA sections 280 and 282; knowing evasion is subject to criminal prosecution under ETA section 327, and participating tax professionals may incur gross-negligence penalties under Income Tax Act section 163.2.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control laws under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act restricting inward or outward fund transfers. Dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, and repatriated capital may be remitted freely by an Ontario business corporation to non-residents, subject to Part XIII withholding taxes, the Ontario Taxation Act, and Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) reporting obligations. No prior approval from the Bank of Canada is required for capital injections, trade-disbursement payments, or profit repatriation. Canada is one of the most open G20 treasury regimes; the only frictions arise from bank KYC onboarding, sanctions compliance, and CRA transaction documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. An Ontario exporter of professional services (anti-dumping trade-defense legal consulting) is free to hold export receipts in CAD, USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. There is no prescribed repatriation timeline for export earnings, and no requirement to convert FX into CAD at any specific rate or through any designated bank. FINTRAC record-keeping and large-transaction reporting thresholds apply, and OSFI-regulated banks must satisfy their own monitoring standards, but no central-bank-directed surrender or conversion mandate exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority), Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federal banking prudential regulation, and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration or exchange-control permit regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderately feasible with legal constraints. Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences, but non-resident fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may provide cross-border payment and multicurrency settlement to an Ontario company provided they are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and with FINTRAC, or operate under a Canadian retail-payment-service-provider registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Such fintechs are generally treated as payment facilitators rather than chartered banks and may not offer deposit-taking, government-insured CDT accounts, or commercial credit. They are viable for fee disbursements, small payments to foreign counsel, and expedited cross-currency facilitation; however, a Canadian Schedule I bank account remains legally superior as the primary liquidity anchor for a professional corporation with significant cross-border trade-defense litigation funding. Feasibility rating: High for supplementary payment rails; Low as a full replacement for the primary banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder’s or investor’s bank to the Ontario company’s operating account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank, using the company’s full legal name, bank account number, and branch/transit ABA/CC code. As Canada does not mandate FX purpose codes, the remitter should nevertheless clearly reference "CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION" or "EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION" in the SWIFT narrative field, together with the corporate identifier and the underlying subscription/resolution, to pre-empt FINTRAC inappropriate-use flags. For amounts exceeding CAD 100,000 or the bank’s internal reporting threshold, provide contemporaneous settlement proof: subscription agreement, board resolution, the shareholder’s bank debit advice, and a notarised declaration of source of funds. A Canadian bank may require the inbound wire to reference the account in ISO 20022 format, and funds will settle over Lynx (Canada’s high-value payment system) for CAD-denominated wires or SWIFT for USD/EUR, typically within one banking day. No exchange-control approval is required. If the shareholder is a related non-resident, CRA may apply the shareholder benefit, thin capitalisation, or Part XIII distribution rules; therefore the treasury record must include the legal basis of the contribution (equity vs. loan) to avoid tax-recharacterisation risk.The primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate payment initiated via the Tier 1 bank’s cash-management platform, settled in CAD or foreign currency through the bank’s correspondent network (e.g., RBC Global Services, TD Global Payments, CIBC Global Wire Room) and using SWIFT GPI tracking. For disbursements to foreign anti-dumping counsel, expert witnesses, government agencies, and trade tribunals, the intended use is the payment of professional service fees, filing fees, and regulatory deposits, not commercial merchandise trade. Therefore, commercial letters of credit (LCs) are not typically required, although LCs can be used when a trade-defense cost security bond or a customs-bond guarantee must be posted in a foreign jurisdiction. A conventional documentary collection or LC is an acceptable overlay for securing disbursement to a foreign authority, but the ordinary and efficient settlement vehicle remains an escrowed SWIFT wire with clear beneficiary references. Banks will apply thresholds under the Ontario legal professional privilege template, and the Treasury must maintain an invoice-and-engagement-file matching the SWIFT narrative with the CBSA or foreign proceeding identifier. Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences, but non-resident fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may provide cross-border payment and multicurrency settlement to an Ontario company provided they are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and with FINTRAC, or operate under a Canadian retail-payment-service-provider registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Such fintechs are generally treated as payment facilitators rather than chartered banks and may not offer deposit-taking, government-insured CDT accounts, or commercial credit. They are viable for fee disbursements, small payments to foreign counsel, and expedited cross-currency facilitation; however, a Canadian Schedule I bank account remains legally superior as the primary liquidity anchor for a professional corporation with significant cross-border trade-defense litigation funding. Feasibility rating: High for supplementary payment rails; Low as a full replacement for the primary banking relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value of non-arm's length transactions with any single non-resident entity triggers CRA's requirement to prepare and retain a Local File and a Country-by-Country (CbC) Report notification. A Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenues above CAD 50 million or for intercompany transactions exceeding the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the business purpose doctrine apply. The CRA may disregard transactions that are a sham or that lack a bona fide commercial business purpose, including a corporate vehicle used solely to access treaty benefits. Ontario courts impose a functional management and substance test: the corporation must maintain a Canadian registered office, independent professional staff, client engagement records, board meetings, and risk-control functions. For anti-dumping defense consulting, substance will be evidenced by the actual conduct of trade law research, the delivery of advisory memoranda, and the management of legal proceedings for Canadian clients.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil penalties for gross negligence under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated tax. CRA may impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty for gross valuation misstatement and revoke the corporation's tax compliance status.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who counsel, participate, or acquiesce in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid portion of the corporate tax, interest, and penalties. They may also be subject to criminal prosecution and restrictions on international travel, and CRA may obtain court orders attaching personal assets in addition to corporate recovery.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization (s. 245 ITA) of the entire transaction, resulting in a disallowance of deductions, additional assessable income, a 25% GAAR penalty, and arrears interest. The corporation may be denied treaty benefits and disqualified from the Ontario small business deduction, and the CRA may publicly name the entity in its reported non-compliance list.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the corporation’s tax liability if they knowingly signed false attestations or permitted the use of the shell to defeat Canadian tax collection. Courts may order restitution, issue travel bans, and impose prison terms for aiding tax avoidance under the criminal fraud provisions of the Criminal Code.
AML / Professional Services Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation against the corporation for AML non-compliance; criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 10 years and unlimited fines for money-laundering conviction. Failure to reply to a FINTRAC request carries a penalty of up to CAD 2,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be subject to personal fines and imprisonment where they knowingly directed or permitted the violation. The court may also issue a prohibition order restricting corporate directors from acting in any Canadian business capacity, and may impose restitution orders for funds laundered through the corporation’s bank accounts.
