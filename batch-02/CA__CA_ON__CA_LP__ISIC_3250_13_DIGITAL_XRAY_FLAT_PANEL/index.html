Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), the federal tax administration, via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance retains provincial legislative oversight but CRA administers GST/HST and Ontario corporate income tax under a federal-provincial agreement.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA, either online through BRO or by mail. The BN application must include attestation of non-residency and Canadian partnership designation; the partnership should open a GST/HST account (RT) before making taxable supplies. Non-resident partnerships must also register under Form NR3013 for income tax reporting, enabling issuance of T5013 partnership information slips to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; the partnership itself is not assessed for income tax, and each partner is taxable on their share of Canadian-source income in their own hands (subject to Part XIII tax and treaty rules for non-resident partners).

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. Partnerships are mandatorily pass-through. Available statutory elections include: (i) a partnership fiscal-period election under s. 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) an s. 97(2) rollover electing a deemed disposition at any agreed amount for property contributed to the partnership; and (iii) section 216 election for non-residents with rental income from Canadian real estate. Hybrid mismatch defensive rules (excessive interest and financing expenses, s. 18.4 et seq.) apply to cross-border structures involving hybrid entities and may disallow deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); registration becomes mandatory upon exceedance, and non-resident partnership registrants must file GST/HST returns and collect/remit HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods exported by the supplier qualifies for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act if the goods are delivered outside Canada as part of the supply and the supplier retains evidence of export within 2 years. Services supplied to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and that are performed outside Canada (or are substantially in respect of property situated outside Canada) also qualify. For manufacturing of digital X-ray flat panel detectors, exported goods are zero-rated only if title and risk of loss pass outside Canada and shipment occurs within the documentary time frames.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or ACI eManifest)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading / waybill (ocean, air, or overland carrier)
  • Carrier's certificate or customs-stamped proof of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT wire payment evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada or Ontario. GST/HST filings are made electronically using CRA's GST/HST NETFILE; businesses are obliged to keep invoices and supporting records in an electronically retrievable format. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing standard is voluntary, though Ontario public-sector procurement increasingly expects PEPPOL-compliant B2G invoices.

CRA requires complete and timely documentary proof; failure to substantiate an export within a reasonable audit period may recharacterize the supply as taxable in Canada, triggering reassessment at 13% HST. Interest and penalties accrue from the original filing date. Penalties include a 25% false-statement penalty (ETA s. 285.01), gross negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 for wilful evasion. Reassessment windows extend up to 4 years generally, or 7+ years where misrepresentation or fraud exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Canada maintains no exchange controls, no central-bank convertibility approval, and no restriction on the repatriation of capital contributions, loan repayments, trade settlements, dividends, or partnership distributions. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: an Ontario limited partnership distributing Canadian-source income to a non-resident limited partner may trigger Part XIII withholding tax at 25% under the Income Tax Act unless reduced by a tax treaty; the LP must also file T5013 partnership information returns and comply with CRA source-withholding requirements. In addition, international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC by the Canadian financial institution; this is a monitoring requirement rather than an exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no required remittance timeline. Exporters and limited partnerships may retain export earnings in foreign currency, convert them at commercial rates, or use them for offshore payments. The only applicable obligations are ordinary recordkeeping, accurate customs declarations through the Canada Border Services Agency, and FINTRAC's reporting obligation for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. There is no regulatory duty to convert foreign currency into Canadian dollars.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is statutorily delegated to the Department of Finance Canada, with prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury bank account for the Ontario LP; Medium as a supplementary cross-border receivables or disbursement rail. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital payment platforms are not authorised to take deposits in Canada unless they are licensed as banks under the Bank Act; they generally operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act supervised by the Bank of Canada. Such providers do not offer CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue Canadian commercial letters of credit, and may have limited access to Canadian Lynx/ACSS settlement systems. A foreign EMI can be used only for ancillary payments where the ultimate settlement is routed through a Schedule I Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed by the investor or general partner as a SWIFT MT103 wire to the Ontario LP's Canadian Schedule I bank account. The remittance information in Field 70 or the bank's designated purpose narrative must clearly state 'Capital contribution to Ontario Limited Partnership dated [date], reference [capital call number]' and must not use ambiguous language such as 'personal transaction' or 'general payment'. Canadian law imposes no mandatory SWIFT purpose code for capital account transfers; where the receiving bank's system requires an ISO 20022 purpose phrase, use the bank-approved code for intercompany capital payments or capital transfers. CAD-denominated large-value payments are settled through the Lynx system; USD payments may be received through Fedwire or CHIPS at the Canadian bank's US correspondent and then credited to the LP's USD or CAD account. The receiving bank performs a corporate name match against the Ontario registry, verifies the account beneficiary's registration number, screens all parties against sanctions and PEP lists, and, for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, files a FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer report. Verification evidence should include the executed MT103 acquittance from the sending bank, the receiving bank's credit advice, the bank statement, an entry in the LP's capital account, and a written confirmation from the Ontario LP's accountant or tax preparer.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic-transfer payments to overseas suppliers, distributors, and service providers. Commercial letters of credit are appropriately issued through the Canadian bank using an MT700 in accordance with ICC UCP 600; amendments are effected by MT707, and reimbursements are made through MT400/MT798 as applicable. CAD payments to domestic payees may be cleared through Lynx for high-value payments or ACSS/EFT for ordinary operating payments; USD payments may be routed through Fedwire or CHIPS via the Canadian bank's US branch, and payments in third currencies use the bank's correspondent network. The bank will require underlying trade documents, including purchase orders, pro forma invoices, final commercial invoices, bills of lading or air waybills, certificates of origin, inspection certificates, and the LP's export declarations, especially where the equipment is subject to Health Canada or export-control screening. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory financial-audit size threshold applies to Ontario limited partnerships. An independent audit becomes compulsory where required by the partnership agreement, by an investor or lender covenant, by securities law (reporting issuer or NI 31-103 registrant/fund), or by a regulated financial-services activity. The Health Canada MDEL separately requires ongoing ISO 13485/MDSAP quality-system audits, which are regulatory rather than financial in nature.
