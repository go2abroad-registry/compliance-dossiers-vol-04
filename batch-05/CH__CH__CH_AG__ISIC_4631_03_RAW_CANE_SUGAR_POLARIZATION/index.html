Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) – official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration administered by the ESTV Main VAT Office and UID issuance by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss AG, the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, e.g. CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX) is automatically issued upon Commercial Register (Handelsregister) entry and serves as the tax and VAT identifier. If taxable turnover exceeds the applicable VAT threshold, the entity must submit VAT registration to the ESTV electronically or by paper, either directly or through a Swiss fiscal representative if non-resident. A foreign company operating a permanent establishment in Switzerland must register the branch in the Commercial Register after six months, obtain a Swiss UID, and meet the VAT registration obligation. Non-resident traders with Swiss taxable supplies exceeding CHF 100,000 must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative for VAT compliance. The UID confirmation and VAT certificate are delivered by the Federal Statistical Office and ESTV respectively.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. The Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily taxable as a separate legal entity. The principal statutory election is VAT group treatment under Art. 21 of the VAT Act for closely linked taxable persons; participation relief on qualifying dividends applies automatically; no other entity classification elections exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover (worldwide for Swiss-domiciled businesses; Swiss-source turnover only for non-resident businesses) excluding VAT

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported outside Switzerland to a foreign customs territory if the supplier proves dispatch or transport abroad and presents a Swiss export customs declaration. Services are zero-rated when the recipient is abroad and the service is used abroad. For raw cane sugar physically exported from Switzerland, 0% VAT is claimable; if the sugar is sold merchanting-style between foreign ports and never enters Swiss customs territory, the transaction is outside Swiss VAT and no zero-rating claim is made.

  • Swiss export customs declaration (e-dec/Passar release) or customs transit document
  • Commercial invoice including supplier UID and zero-rating citation
  • Packing list
  • Original transport document (bill of lading / sea waybill / CMR / air waybill)
  • Evidence of foreign customs entry or destination customs release where available
  • Sales contract and documentary credit / SWIFT MT700 or MT103 payment confirmation

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping and no API-authorization requirement for invoicing. E-invoicing is legally valid if the data is complete, secure and reproducible, e.g., Swiss 'Kernel' XML e-invoice or QR-bill. Invoices must contain the mandatory particulars in Art. 64 VAT Act / Art. 39 VAT Ordinance (supplier and recipient UID when applicable, invoice date, nature and extent of supply, consideration, tax rate and tax amount). Tax-relevant documents must be retained in Switzerland for 10 years.

The Swiss VAT administration may review export substantiation during the statutory limitation period (normally five years, extended to ten years for fraud). Failure to furnish sufficient export proof results in recharacterization of the supply as domestic, with VAT assessed at the standard rate 8.1% (or 2.6% reduced rate for foodstuffs if applicable), plus late-payment interest. Administrative fines under Art. 86-90 VAT Act range up to CHF 1,000 for minor omissions and up to ten times the tax evaded for intentional evasion; criminal sanctions under the Swiss Criminal Code may also apply. Swiss law imposes no foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the pipeline's 180-day benchmark is not a Swiss legal requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible on both current and capital account, and Swiss law draws no operative distinction between the two for foreign-exchange purposes; the legal openness rests on OECD Codes of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and Current Invisible Operations, IMF Article VIII obligations, EFTA/WTO commitments, and the absence in Swiss statute of any capital-licensing or FX-authorisation regime (Article 94 BV asserts the market-economy principle; National Bank Act emergency powers are extraterritorially inapplicable and have never been invoked). Profit remittance is unrestricted by monetary law; the only material gate is fiscal: 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividend distributions, mitigated by Switzerland's dense double-tax-treaty network (typically 0-5% for qualifying treaty shareholders), plus ordinary corporate income tax at effective cantonal/federal rates of roughly 11.9% to 21%. Dividend or liquidation proceeds may be remitted in any freely convertible currency. For a USD-denominated VHP raw can sugar arbitrage book, conversion of USD into CHF or EUR is executed in the wholesale interbank market at floating SNB rates without declaration, priority, or Ministerial consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in Switzerland. There is no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, no surrender quota, and no requirement to route receipts through domestic banks; a Swiss AG trading raw cane sugar (ISIC 4631) may lawfully retain USD, EUR, or BRL proceeds indefinitely in foreign nostro accounts or in Swiss banks. The SNB has operated a free-float policy since abandonment of the EUR/CHF minimum exchange rate on 15 January 2015. The only binding external constraint is sanctions law: dealings with embargoed jurisdictions (e.g., Russian/Belarusian sugar under Swiss sanctions) are prohibited, but this is a trade-control measure rather than an FX-conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera), established under the National Bank Act (SR 951.11); Switzerland maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration, and the SNB does not license, approve, or register cross-border payments; banking conduct supervision rests with FINMA, while trade sanctions and export-control enforcement rest with SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for core treasury and documentary settlement; Medium only as an auxiliary expense rail. Under the Swiss Banking Act (SR 952.0), the FinTech licence (Art. 1b) caps public deposits at CHF 100 million and does not authorise documentary credits, lending, or USD correspondent clearing; Swiss e-money/neobanks (YAPEAL, SWISS4, Swissquote) therefore cannot structure revolving LCs for VHP raw cane sugar cargoes. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in the UK or EU are not FINMA-regulated banks, fail the counterparty-acceptance criteria of the Swiss Bankers Association Due Diligence Agreement (CDB 20), and would trigger enhanced due diligence or refusal if used for trade-settlement remittances. At most, such EMIs may serve as a thin interface for incidental operating expenses (travel, insurance, professional fees) below sub-threshold amounts and fully reconcilable against the approved expense ledger; physical sugar settlement must remain on licensed Swiss bank rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the shareholder remits the fully paid-up share capital by SWIFT MT103 from its own bank into the AG's dedicated capital-receipt account (Liberierungskonto) at UBS or ZKB, with field 70 free-text narrative 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital' plus the AG's UID/identification number and reference to Articles of Association and Articles 621-633 of the Swiss Code of Obligations. Switzerland has no statutory purpose-code system analogous to China's SAFE codes; the bank relies on the remittance text, the subscription documents (Zeichnungsscheine), the board protocol, and the statutes. Verification before release of funds: founding audit confirmation (Bestätigung über die Liberierung) proving that at least CHF 50,000 of the legal minimum CHF 100,000 is genuinely funded and unconditionally at the company's disposal; payment of Swiss issue stamp duty (Emissionabgabe, 1% of capital issued above CHF 1 million) where applicable; compliance sign-off on the source of funds; and express booking of the paid-in amount as withholding-tax-free reserves from capital contributions (Reserven aus Kapitaleinlagen). No SNB licence or notification is required for inward capital. Turnover: Ordinary statutory audit (ordentliche Revision) is mandatory if the AG exceeds two of three thresholds for two consecutive financial years: total assets above CHF 20 million; net turnover above CHF 40 million; average of more than 250 full-time employees (Art. 727 CO). For this sugar wholesale AG, a limited audit with annual auditor confirmation of the share register is the presumptive regime unless threshold facts change.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal flat corporate income tax (Art. 68 DBG); combined federal + cantonal + communal headline effective rate on pre-tax profit ranges from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to 21.0% (Geneva) depending on the canton of seat and profit allocation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) is deducted at source on all dividend distributions resolved by the AG to shareholders (Art. 1 VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be lodged within 6 months after the close of the statutory financial year; for calendar-year companies the regular due date is 30 June of the following year, with a first extension routinely granted to 30 September and further extension to 31 December upon justified request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law does not codify a fixed CHF threshold for the Local File; the FTA/cantonal authorities request OECD-aligned TP documentation where controlled transactions are of material value (practice: related-party goods flows of approximately CHF 1-8 million per transaction category, or recurring intra-group services above CHF 500,000). Master File and Local File follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least CHF 900 million; the ESTV publishes quarterly safe-haven interest rates benchmarked for intra-group loans.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not enact UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, genuine economic substance is a mandatory condition under Art. 50 DBG: the AG must hold its actual place of management, premises, staff, books, and core business decision-making in Switzerland. A letterbox trading AG without substance risks reclassification as a domiciliary company, refusal of tax ruling protection, denial of treaty benefits under the OECD MLI principal-purpose test, and loss of cantonal incentive status. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million are additionally subject to the Swiss Minimum Taxation Act (GloBE / Pillar Two top-up tax), effective 1 January 2024.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Qualified Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the evaded tax; penalty surcharge of up to 3 times the tax evaded for simple evasion (Art. 175 DBG); qualified tax fraud (Steuerbetrug, Art. 186 DBG) involving forged or false documents is prosecuted as a criminal offence and punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine; the Federal Tax Administration additionally initiates penal proceedings under the VStrR.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable under Art. 754 OR for losses caused to the company and the tax authorities by grossly negligent or intentional breaches of duty; personal criminal fines and imprisonment for qualified tax fraud; pending criminal proceedings may lead to asset seizure and, where an arrest warrant issues, effective restriction of international travel.
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the seat and place of actual management (Art. 50 DBG); retroactive taxation of the full profit at standard cantonal rates; denial of DTA benefits and of the participation relief under the MLI anti-abuse rule; cancellation of advance tax rulings; forced liquidation procedure under Art. 731b OR if the company lacks valid administration; fines for misleading the commercial register.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Swiss Supreme Court practice where the company is an abused instrument: directors face restitution claims, personal liability for the full unpaid corporate tax and creditor losses, criminal prosecution for false statements to the tax authority, and travel bans or imprisonment through criminal enforcement measures in aggravated cases.
Anti-Money Laundering (GwG) and Financial Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to CHF 500,000 for violation of the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Art. 37 GwG); money laundering under Art. 305bis StGB is punishable with imprisonment of up to 3 years; negligent money laundering with a fine; corporate liability of up to CHF 5 million under Art. 102 StGB for failures in organization; seizure and forfeiture of cargo, freight receivables, LC proceeds, and bank balances.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated anti-money-laundering officer incur personal criminal liability, disqualification from management functions for up to 5 years, unrestricted enforcement against personal assets, and, in aggravated cases, a prison sentence; compliance failures also trigger immediate debarment from correspondent banking and irrevocable letter-of-credit (LC) facilities used in sugar commodity trade.
Withholding Tax Fraud (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: Administrative recovery of the unpaid withholding tax; penal surcharge of up to 3 times the amount evaded under Art. 60/60bis VStrR; obstruction fines of up to CHF 10,000; immediate interest on arrears at 3.5% p.a.; realization of bank accounts, cargo proceeds, and LC receivables by the FTA.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as withholding agents: individual criminal prosecution for qualified withholding tax evasion leads to imprisonment of up to 3 years under the VStrR, personal asset seizure, professional disqualification, and filing of criminal charges that can result in international travel restrictions where an arrest warrant is issued.
