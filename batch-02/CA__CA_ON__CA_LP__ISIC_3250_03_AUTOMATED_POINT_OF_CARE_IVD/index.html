Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Ontario imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership and does not require a statutory escrow account. The declaration should state the agreed capital contributions and partnership interests. Contributions may be cash, property, or services if permitted by the partnership agreement. Limited partners should not participate in management in a manner that jeopardizes statutory limited liability.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building permits, occupancy approval, fire inspection, and a compliant fire safety plan under the Ontario Fire Code. Obtain structural and equipment-load review for laboratories, clean areas, compressed gases, and production machinery. Complete Electrical Safety Authority approvals for regulated electrical work. Implement the Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act program, including WHMIS, chemical exposure controls, ventilation, biosafety controls, machine guarding, lockout procedures, emergency response, worker training, and workplace inspection records.

Paid-in capital rule: Ontario imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership and does not require a statutory escrow account. The declaration should state the agreed capital contributions and partnership interests. Contributions may be cash, property, or services if permitted by the partnership agreement. Limited partners should not participate in management in a manner that jeopardizes statutory limited liability.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - File Form 1 (Declaration of Partnership) with the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch and remit the prescribed registration fee to create the Ontario public LP record; obtain Ontario LP registration number. Step 2 - Register the LP for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account (RT prefix) through CRA's Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Step 3 - Appoint a general partner resident in Ontario to act as the statutory agent for service of process and CRA correspondence; non-resident partners must provide their foreign tax identification numbers and, where applicable, appoint a Canadian resident representative under section 128 ITA and ETA section 298 for service. Step 4 - Open a dedicated Canadian banking account in the LP's name; bank attestation is not a CRA registration condition but is an evidentiary requirement for export invoicing and FX reconciliation. Step 5 - Register for Ontario Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) and CRA payroll deduction accounts (PD36) if the LP employs personnel. Step 6 - Obtain and maintain the Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under the Food and Drugs Act for placing automated point-of-care IVD analyzers and microfluidic cartridges on the Canadian market; the MDEL must be quoted on invoices but is not itself a tax account. Step 7 - Maintain the partnership agreement, capital contribution schedules, and partner allocation provisions contemporaneously with CRA account records. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; partnership fiscal transparency under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA) is mandatory and non-waivable. Available elections relevant to an Ontario LP: (i) deemed disposition and rollover on transfer of the LP's business to a corporation under ITA subsections 98(3) and 98(5); (ii) election under ITA section 22 for transfers of accounts receivable to a corporation; (iii) GST/HST election under section 156 ETA for closely related corporations (available only if the LP incorporates an operating subsidiary and the common control test is met); (iv) partnership permanent establishment allocation election among Canadian provinces for Ontario tax purposes; (v) treaty-based relief applications and mutual agreement procedure under the Canada-US tax treaty where non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income; (vi) application of Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA section 18.4) requiring partners to document that no deduction/non-inclusion mismatch arises from the LP structure; (vii) voluntary GST/HST registration election, which is advisable for the LP to recover input tax credits on manufacturing inputs even if the CAD 30,000 threshold is not reached.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar year (small supplier threshold) based on the LP's worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, over any four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the day the threshold is exceeded. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST regardless of the threshold. Voluntary registration is available and commercially recommended for the IVD manufacturing LP to claim input tax credits on components, cleanroom materials, equipment, and professional services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA). Section 1 (goods supplied to a non-resident) requires: (a) the Ontario LP sells tangible personal property - namely immunoassay analyzers, microfluidic cartridges, reagents, and associated disposables - to a non-resident purchaser; (b) the goods are delivered to a place outside Canada by the LP or on the LP's behalf; and (c) the LP retains documentary evidence of export. Goods must generally leave Canada within 60 days after transfer of possession to the buyer or carrier; longer periods require documented commercial justification. Services performed on or in respect of exported goods, including installation, calibration, software loading, and field validation of point-of-care IVD instruments, may be zero-rated under Part V section 7 where the service is acquired by a non-resident person and is not performed in Canada for Canadian use. Export supplies are deductible in the computation of the LP's GST/HST net tax, and input tax credits attributable to those exports are fully recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / Canadian Export Declaration filed via CARM where prescribed; for controlled goods or exports valued above CAD 2,000)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT TT wire transfer confirmation from the overseas buyer evidencing settlement in the LP's Canadian bank account)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the HS classification (e.g., HS 3822.00 for diagnostic reagents; HS 9018.19/9027.80 for analyzers) and the export declaration number
  • Continuous Transportation Certificate or route documentation where goods transit through the United States under the Canada-US outbound transit regime
  • Non-resident purchaser identity and address evidence (purchase order, incoterms contract, and foreign corporate registration) substantiating the direct export test

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. The Pan-Canadian B2B/B2G e-invoicing framework is voluntary and uses the PEPPOL network with the Canadian CIUS-EN 16931 / CIUS-UBL specifications, administered by the Digital Governance Council; CRA does not operate a transaction-level approval API. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with monthly filing frequency or annual taxable supplies above CAD 1 million; all registrants must retain invoicing and source records in an accessible electronic or paper format for six years. Border-related electronic transmission is mandatory through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system for import declarations and ACE/eManifest for highway cargo; export reporting is mandatory for controlled goods or goods valued above CAD 2,000 destined outside Canada, filed via CARM or authorized third-party portal.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard CRA commercial-reality review of export trade receivables and intercompany settlement expectations. If the export zero-rating is disallowed for insufficient documentation, the CRA will: (a) reassess the supplies as taxable at 13% HST, requiring the LP to remit the 13% tax on the gross selling price plus daily compound interest retroactively to the reporting period; (b) deny input tax credits previously claimed on inputs used in the disallowed supply; (c) apply the false-statement penalty under ETA section 285 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax; (d) extend the normal four-year reassessment window in cases of misrepresentation or negligence (six years or indefinitely for fraud); (e) impose the gross negligence penalty under ITA section 163(2) on the LP's partners equal to 50% of any understated partnership income; (f) hold general partners personally liable for unpaid HST under ETA section 323; and (g) pursue criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA section 327 and ITA section 239, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. CRA export verification programs also validate the freight-forwarder's delivery receipts, customs broker records, and bank settlement documentation before any refund or net-tax claim is accepted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement for both current and capital account transactions. Non-resident direct investment into an Ontario LP requires no exchange license or conversion mandate. Profit remittance / partnership distributions from a Canadian LP to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 15% unless reduced by treaty; there is no foreign-exchange barrier, but the remittance transfer must be made through a Canadian authorized bank/financial intermediary and reported to the CRA for treaty based withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters and manufacturers (including IVD manufacturers) may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts; however, all cross-border wire receipts must be supported by underlying trade documentation, destined to the registered Canadian bank account, and must be reported to Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations and to the CRA for income reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation via OSFI; AML/FX transaction supervision coordinated by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium—Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are legally usable only for non-escrow operational liquidity and cross-border FX conversion, not for statutory capital escrow or LC issuance. An EMI operating into Canada must be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC (unless exempt), and if providing certain payment services to Canadian residents, may require a retail payment activity license under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). The Ontario LP's 3250 IVD manufacturing operation should maintain its primary escrow and borrowing relationships at a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank; EMIs cannot offer regulated deposit products or letters of credit.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-Ontario general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent, with taxation imposed only at the partner level

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% general statutory Canadian withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner; for the Limited Partnership, operating distributions are not dividends and generally are not subject to withholding, except the partner's share of Canadian-source income may be taxable under Part I or Part XIII on an annual allocation basis

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation must be prepared under CRA guidelines for all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions; the T106 Information Return is required when the aggregate fair market value of such transactions exceeds CAD $1 million; Country-by-Country Reporting is compulsory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD $750 million or more

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty anti-abuse provisions requiring real economic substance; the Ontario Limited Partnership must demonstrate substantive employment, premises, operational risk control, and actual decision-making in Ontario to avoid recharacterization as a shell or conduit; Ontario has no separate Economic Substance Regulations but CRA may deny treaty benefits and impose the 25% GAAR penalty on a tax benefit if a transaction lacks genuine commercial purpose

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Income Tax Act section 239(1) of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; additionally, Ontario enforcement may seek a lifetime ban on managing a business in the province

Director personal liability: Corporate general partners and their directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid source deductions and related penalties; directors may also face criminal fines and personal bankruptcy exposure, and serious evasion can trigger a travel ban pending investigation or extradition proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA will invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) to deny the tax benefit, reassess partners, and impose a 25% penalty on the tax benefit; treaty relief may be denied under the Limitation-on-Benefits clause of the applicable income tax convention

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its directors can be held personally liable for the reassessed partnership taxes and penalties if they were the directing mind behind the artificial transaction; in extreme cases, the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the principals liable for restitution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A 10% transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3), equal to the lesser of 10% of the net adjustment and CAD $5 million, together with interest; repeat or wilful non-compliance may attract a 50% gross-negligence penalty

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be held secondarily liable for the LP's unpaid assessment where they authorized or acquiesced in the transfer-pricing misstatement, and may be subject to civil restitution orders under provincial securities or CBCA procedures.
AML / FINTRAC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD $1 million per violation for a partnership or corporate partner under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; willful blind spots may result in criminal liability under the Criminal Code with up to ten years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner can be personally prosecuted for failing to implement an AML compliance program, and are subject to personal fines, civil forfeiture of proceeds, and travel restrictions pending criminal money-laundering proceedings.
