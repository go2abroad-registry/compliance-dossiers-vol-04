Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada); business portal: CRA My Business Account via canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), followed by separate registration for the GST/HST program and, if applicable, corporate tax account. For Ontario, entities must additionally file an Ontario Corporations Information Act annual return and register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for employer health tax only if payroll thresholds are met. Non-resident corporations with Canadian permanent establishments must file information returns and, where taxable, elect under the Income Tax Act Part I via ordinary registration through the CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian 'check-the-box' election exists; Canadian corporations are automatically taxable at the entity level. Available elections include the non-resident corporation tax election under Income Tax Act Section 216 (for rental income), the Section 217 election (for employment income), and various hybrid-mismatch declarations under S.18.4/94/94.1 where cross-border arrangements are involved. Ontario provincial tax registration follows federal classifications for the most part.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services and zero-rated exports) either in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident with no permanent establishment in Canada may voluntarily register for GST/HST even if below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated if the supplier maintains adequate evidence of export, including physical movement to a destination outside Canada, and the sale is made to a non-resident purchaser (with certain exceptions for exempt recipients). For services, zero-rating applies when the primary place of performance or beneficial enjoyment is outside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident, subject to the specific wording of Excise Tax Act Section 23.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or authorized electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading with Canadian port of export and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice reflecting the foreign purchaser and zero-rated supply
  • Forwarder/carrier certificate or proof of conveyance
  • Bank wire or TT remittance evidence (foreign exchange documentation)

No statutory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance requirement exists in Canada. GST/HST is self-assessed and remitted via NETFILE or third-party software; electronic records, including EDI or XML invoices, are acceptable substantiation. Ontario does not impose digital stamping or pre-clearance approval for commercial invoices.

CRA may reassess GST/HST where export evidence is insufficient, converting the zero-rating to taxable supply and requiring tax collection plus interest. Penalties apply for knowingly making a false or misleading statement: the greater of CDN $100 and 25% of the tax amount, in addition to interest and potential criminal prosecution. Retrospective clawbacks cover up to the statutory limitation period (4 years, or longer in fraud cases).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions, no central bank approval requirements for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation, and no limitations on the purchase or sale of foreign currency. Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in designated accounts. All transactions, however, must comply with FINTRAC reporting obligations, including large cash transaction reports and electronic funds transfer reports where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) (monetary authority); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/ATF oversight); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulation).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately viable for ancillary payment processing. Offshore digital EMI/licensed e-money institutions are not directly regulated by Canadian banking authorities; they may engage in payment facilitation only if registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) or by partnering with a regulated Canadian financial institution. Direct treasury concentration with offshore EMIs is not recommended for primary liquidity management. A Canadian-licensed MSB or a banking-as-a-service provider with a Schedule I bank sponsor is preferred.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general rate (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%; Ontario rate scheduled to decline to 10.5% effective Jan. 1, 2026, yielding 25.5% combined)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding under ITA Part XIII on dividends paid to non-residents; reduced by treaty, e.g., 15% portfolio and 5% direct (10%+ voting interest) under the Canada-U.S. Treaty; other Canadian DTAs generally prescribe 10%-15%

Schedule 4 - Non-Capital Loss Continuity Claim non-capital losses carried forward from prior taxation years (maximum 20-year carryforward) or carried back 3 years; complete the loss continuity schedule with year-by-year balances. Non-resident corporations may deduct only losses attributable to the Canadian PE; loss restrictions apply upon change of control. Late filing: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delinquency (maximum 12 months) under ITA s. 162(1); repeated failure within 3 years: 10% plus 2% per month (maximum 24 months); unpaid balances accrue daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed rate plus 4 percentage points; gross negligence penalty: 50% of understated tax; failure to e-file in prescribed form can also attract separate administrative penalties.
T2SCH511 / T2SCH521 - Ontario Tax Calculation and Allocation Compute Ontario corporate income tax at 11.5% (3.2% for income eligible for the Ontario small business deduction); use Schedule 521 to allocate taxable income to Ontario when a permanent establishment exists outside the province. A non-resident with a PE only in Ontario allocates 100% of Canadian taxable income to Ontario; salaries paid to non-resident employees are deducted only if reasonable and attributable to the PE. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taxation years beginning on or after Jan. 1, 2023: contemporaneous TP documentation mandatory where gross revenue is at least CAD 50,000,000 OR total value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions is at least CAD 5,000,000; prior threshold was CAD 1,000,000 in intercompany transactions; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above approximately CAD 953,000,000 (inflation-adjusted)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the CRA substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), and beneficial-ownership analysis for DTA relief impose an effective substance standard: the Ontario trading entity must possess real decision-making authority, inventory risk, employees (purchasing, quality control, logistics), and cash-management functions commensurate with its contractual risk profile; a letterbox Canadian entity used to access treaty benefits will be recharacterized and denied relief.
