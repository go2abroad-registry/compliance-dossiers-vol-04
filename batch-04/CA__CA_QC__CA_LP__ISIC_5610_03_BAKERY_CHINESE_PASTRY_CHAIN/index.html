Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal income tax and GST/HST; Revenu Québec (RQ) for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), which since 2013 also administers federal GST/HST for Quebec-based businesses; primary portals: canada.ca/revenue-agency and revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with GST/HST and payroll program accounts by filing CRA Form RC391 (Registration of a Non-Resident Business) or online through the CRA's My Business Account portal; it must then register with Revenu Québec for QST and Quebec employer accounts using Form LM-101-V, filed electronically through the RQ business registration service. For income tax, the Quebec LP is transparent and has no separate corporation income tax account, but the LP's BN/QST account, the partnership agreement, and the TP-600/T5013 information return together identify the partners; each non-resident partner must disclose its home-country tax identification number. The LP declaration must also be filed with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec before tax account activation.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax (ITA s.96 flow-through of income, loss, and credits to general and limited partners); for GST/HST and QST the partnership is separately registered as a 'person' and is the operator of the bakery chain, collecting, remitting, and claiming input tax refunds in its own capacity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax (ITA s.96 flow-through of income, loss, and credits to general and limited partners); for GST/HST and QST the partnership is separately registered as a 'person' and is the operator of the bakery chain, collecting, remitting, and claiming input tax refunds in its own capacity. Non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I on their share of Canadian-source business income derived through the LP, while passive investment-type amounts allocated to them may attract Part XIII withholding, subject to applicable treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (1) ITA s.216(1) election for a non-resident to file a Canadian income tax return on Canadian rental/business income in lieu of Part XIII withholding; (2) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1(4); (3) GST/HST closely related entity election under ETA s.156; (4) Quick Method accounting election under ETA ss.257-260 with optional QST harmonization in Quebec; (5) rebate and deemed-remittance elections under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Sales Tax Act; (6) hybrid mismatch protective filings under ITA ss.18.2/18.3 to preserve transparent treatment for payments routed through US check-the-box LLC or LP investors; and (7) treaty-based transparent entity assertions under Canada-US Treaty Article IV(6)(c)(i).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148 and QSTA s.29: a non-resident LP carrying on business in Quebec must register for GST/HST and QST once its worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four calendar quarters; once registered, the LP must charge tax on all taxable supplies unless zero-rated or exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Sales of bakery products by the Quebec LP to a non-resident buyer are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA and the corresponding QST provisions where goods are exported by the LP or its carrier as soon as reasonably possible after delivery, title passes outside Canada, and contemporaneous export documents are retained. Services: Supplies of master-franchise services, central-kitchen consulting, quality assurance, and similar services to a non-resident franchisee are zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident person that is not registered under Part IX of the ETA and the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance and not ultimately exported; franchise royalties for intellectual-property licensing are generally zero-rated as services when these conditions are satisfied. The LP must obtain and preserve full corroboration of export and receipt; CRA and Revenu Québec apply an objective ordinary-course-of-trade test and will not honour zero-rating where goods are diverted into the Canadian market.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (customs automated export reporting; CUSMA low-value consignment rules do not replace the exporter's zero-rating evidence)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods as exported and naming the non-resident buyer
  • Bill of Lading, through-bill of lading, air waybill, or cargo control document evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 wire record, or bank confirmation evidencing payment of the export invoice by the non-resident buyer
  • Signed delivery receipt or carrier waybill showing actual delivery outside Canada
  • Copy of the GST/HST return and QST return schedules claiming zero-rated export treatment
  • Original master-franchise or service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient for zero-rated service supplies

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or pre-invoice authorization regime applies in Canada. Electronic invoicing is permitted and e-invoices must be readily intelligible and retained for six years; the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing standard is voluntary as of 2025. Mandatory digital obligations for the registrant LP: GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; all prescribed books and records must be kept at the partnership's Canadian establishment or be electronically accessible to CRA and RQ on request; invoices must display the supplier's BN, QST registration number, and tax-exclusive prices; Quebec-specific point-of-sale and inventory record rules apply to the bakery franchise trade.

There is no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline in Canadian law; the 180-day benchmark reflects CRA/RQ administrative practice and standard transfer-pricing comparables for collection of export receivables, with balances outstanding beyond six to twelve months being scrutinized under ITA s.247 as unpaid-priced or deemed-financing adjustments. CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rating claims within four years, or indefinitely where false statements, negligence, or fraud are shown. If export documentation is defective, the supply is taxable at the combined 14.975% rate, the input tax credit or refund is clawed back, and penalties accrue: a 25% gross-negligence penalty on the mis-stated net tax (ETA s.285.1), late-filing and failure-to-collect penalties of 6% plus 2% per month (ETA s.280(1)), director and partner personal liability, and criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act. Interest is compounded daily from the date of the original refund; for hybrid structures, Canada may also deny deductions or impose Part I or Part XIII adjustments to the foreign parent if the export arrangement is characterized as a hybrid mismatch under ITA ss.18.2/18.3.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized, free-floating currency regime with no exchange-control statute in force; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, and no central-bank license or prior approval is required for profit repatriation, dividend or royalty remittances, capital injections, or liquidation proceeds. Practical constraints are limited to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) — statutory 25% on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, reduced to treaty rates under the Canada–China Income Tax Treaty (generally 15% on non-portfolio dividends, 10% on royalties, and 10% on interest) — plus FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and Investment Canada Act notification for the establishment of any new Canadian business by a non-Canadian investor. For a Quebec limited partnership in the bakery-franchise sector, the formation itself is a non-reviewable new-business establishment requiring only a Form C56 Notice to ISED; however, any subsequent acquisition of control of an existing Canadian bakery business may trigger net-benefit review if applicable WTO-investor thresholds are exceeded.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency earnings, and no central-bank custody requirement for receivables. A bakery master franchise and central kitchen exporting goods or services (including intercompany royalties, franchise fees, and supply-chain receipts) may retain foreign currency in CAD, USD, or CNH accounts indefinitely. The only obligations are statistical reporting of international merchandise trade to Statistics Canada, customs entry documentation through the Canada Border Services Agency, and ordinary books-and-records retention under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy oversight by the Department of Finance Canada; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); investment review administration by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (limited to secondary payment pass-through; cannot serve as primary treasury or escrow bank). Legal constraints: Canada does not recognize the EU electronic-money-institution licence; a foreign EMI operating in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Global digital payment providers such as Wise (Wise Payments Canada) or Payoneer may offer correspondent settlement, mid-market FX conversion, and cross-border receivables collection, but they are not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, cannot take deposits, cannot issue CDIC-insured instruments, and cannot hold capital escrow accounts or operate commercial letters of credit. For a Quebec LP collecting Master Royalty Wires and POS settlement, the regulated Schedule I bank account must remain the primary liquidity-clearing node; the EMI can only be engaged as a supplementary collection conduit for small-value inbound retail transfers, with the added compliance burden of FINTRAC EFT reporting and a restricted ability to fund third-party disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital to the Quebec LP must use a direct SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire from the Chinese investor's own bank account (e.g., Bank of China, Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, or China Construction Bank) to the LP's operational or capital-subscription account held at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remittance instruction must include an ISO 20022 purpose code of 'CSUB' (capital subscription) or 'ACQS' (acquisition) and a clear narrative such as 'Capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership — bakery franchise expansion'; the receiving bank will require the certified LP agreement, capital call notice, source-of-funds attestation, and ISED Notice evidence. If the aggregate establishing investment triggers the Investment Canada Act threshold — unlikely for a start-up bakery but mandatory regardless — the non-Canadian investor must file Form C56 with ISED within 30 days of establishment. Where any single international electronic funds transfer is CAD 10,000 or more, the bank files the FINTRAC report; structured multiple sub-10,000 transfers are prohibited and will trigger enhanced surveillance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (limited to secondary payment pass-through; cannot serve as primary treasury or escrow bank). Legal constraints: Canada does not recognize the EU electronic-money-institution licence; a foreign EMI operating in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Global digital payment providers such as Wise (Wise Payments Canada) or Payoneer may offer correspondent settlement, mid-market FX conversion, and cross-border receivables collection, but they are not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, cannot take deposits, cannot issue CDIC-insured instruments, and cannot hold capital escrow accounts or operate commercial letters of credit. For a Quebec LP collecting Master Royalty Wires and POS settlement, the regulated Schedule I bank account must remain the primary liquidity-clearing node; the EMI can only be engaged as a supplementary collection conduit for small-value inbound retail transfers, with the added compliance burden of FINTRAC EFT reporting and a restricted ability to fund third-party disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Canadian limited partnership entity level (flow-through); a taxable corporate partner is exposed to the combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate of 26.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act. For the limited partnership structure, profit allocations themselves are not dividends, but Canadian-source passive distributions and deemed payments to non-resident partners are subject to the 25% benchmark rate unless treaty-reduced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The CRA expects a Local File where aggregate controlled transactions involving goods, services, intangibles or financing exceed CAD 1 million in a taxation year. A Master File is expected where the consolidated group revenue reaches CAD 500 million. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not currently apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, and treaty-shopping principles. For the Quebec pastry chain, the LP must demonstrate real operational substance in Quebec: employees in the central kitchen, physical inventory, procurement and franchise management staff, board decisions taken in Quebec, and verifiable local regulatory compliance. A limited partnership that only receives passive royalties or allocations without meaningful premises, staff and logistic infrastructure is vulnerable to GAAR, Part XIII assessment, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax; fines on conviction for tax evasion of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax under section 239 of the Income Tax Act; imprisonment for up to five years. Revenu Quebec imposes equivalent penal sanctions under the Quebec Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings and payroll amounts under subsection 227.1(1). The corporate veil may be pierced in the presence of fraud. Civil restitution, travel bans and surrender orders can be sought under tax warrants.
Artificial Loss Allocation / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule can deny the tax benefit and impose a penalty of 25% of the disallowed benefit. Reassessments may also reclassify allocations as taxable dividends or Part XIII payments, with interest compounding. Quebec's Civil Code permits disregard of legal personality when the entity conceals fraud, abuse of right or a violation of public order.

Director personal liability: General partners face joint liability for misallocations and false filings. Directors who authorize artificial structures may be assessed as persons who participated in the avoidance arrangement and may be barred from acting as directors in Quebec upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Gross Value Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to the arm's length price. A penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment applies if the adjustment exceeds the greater of 10% of the taxpayer's gross adjustment and CAD 5,000. Secondary adjustments may create deemed interest or dividends to the non-resident related party.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued for filing false or misleading statements. General partners of the LP are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax liabilities to the extent of their partnership authority, and personal prosecution under subsection 239(1) may follow where the mispricing is wilful.
AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 1 million per violation; criminal money-laundering offenses carry imprisonment of up to 10 years and unlimited fines. Revenu Quebec and FINTRAC may coordinate asset freezes and seizure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in unregistered transfers or conceal beneficial ownership may be prosecuted personally, subjected to asset forfeiture, issued travel-control orders, and disqualified from acting as directors. Reliance on nominee partners will not shield them from compliance obligations.
