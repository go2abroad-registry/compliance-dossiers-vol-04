Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online portal; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial aspects (HST is administered federally by CRA)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) and, if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a separate GST/HST account. Apply through CRA Form RC1, online Business Registration, or by completing the non-resident registration form RC447. Ontario-specific province registration may be required only for provincial levies such as Employer Health Tax if the partnership has Ontario payroll; no separate Ontario corporate or partnership number is required for GST/HST.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) and, if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a separate GST/HST account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; each partner is taxed on their allocated share. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is a separate 'person' and a mandatory registrant once taxable supplies exceed the threshold. No hybrid mismatch election is available domestically; anti-hybrid rules apply under the Income Tax Act (Part VI.1) to conflicting entity classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made by the partnership; for non-residents, only supplies treated as made in Ontario/Canada are counted toward this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, when proof of export is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident person qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, Section 23 provided the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is performed in whole or in part in Canada but is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. Localization, translation, and media dubbing services rendered to overseas clients will typically qualify as zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration or B13A)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent commercial shipping document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Wise/Payoneer payment confirmation evidencing foreign-currency receipt

No mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime exists for GST/HST in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and documents in standard formats; no digital stamping or API clearance is required. The pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework is optional and still voluntary as of the effective date.

CRA may reassess the partnership to apply HST on the full invoice amount where export documentation is missing or insufficient. A knowingly false zero-rating claim is subject to gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided, daily interest on the outstanding amount, and, in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Retroactive cancellation of zero-rating triggers recapture of associated input tax credits previously claimed on expenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirement for foreign currency conversions, and no restriction on profit or capital distributions from a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident limited partners. Remittances to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25% without treaty relief, reduced under Canada-U.S./Canada-U.K. tax treaties), but this is a tax matter, not a foreign exchange barrier. Since the LP is export-oriented and invoice-based, CAD/USD and CAD/EUR liquidity is fully fungible through bank swap desks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements. No timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export receipts to Canada within a certain period. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency accounts (USD/EUR) at a Canadian chartered bank or held at a foreign bank subsidiary, subject to normal factual reporting under Canada's foreign income verification rules (Form T1135 if aggregate foreign property exceeds C$100,000).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/KYC; no dedicated foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for Wise B2B (Wise Edge Technologies Canada Inc.) and Payoneer as supplementary settlement rails, but LOW as primary banking substitutes. Canadian chartered banks remain mandatory for CAD clearing, CDIC-insured deposits, and irrevocable LC issuance. Under the Bank Act, Wise and Payoneer are money service businesses (MSBs) requiring FINTRAC registration; they do not hold banking licenses, cannot take demand deposits in the legal sense, and cannot provide credit. They are legally viable for cross-border vendor payments and small receipts, but must be linked to a Tier 1 corporate bank account for capital escrow and high-value trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: send via SWIFT MT103 from foreign parent/holding entity to the Ontario LP's CAD/USD pooled account at a Tier 1 chartered bank. Use purpose code FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) for paid-in capital, or 'GIN'/'BOND' if the injection is structured as a partnership contribution; in practice Canadian banks require a plain-English description such as 'LP capital contribution' to avoid FINTRAP misclassification. All cross-border electronic fund transfers (ETRs) of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC within 24 hours per the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Banks verify the authenticity through the bank's SWIFT Reference, the sender's KYC profile at the instructing bank, and the LP's own source-of-funds statement. Confirm GFIP (Global Foreign Interbank Payment) details with correspondent bank to ensure the final beneficiary is the Ontario LP with its legal entity identifier.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) settled by the issuing Canadian bank through SWIFT GPI, delivering same-day/1-day settlement to counterparties in EU, US, and Asia. Use separate 'TSU' or 'TRA' purpose codes for royalty/license fees; use 'TRD' for translation subcontractor payments. For high-value procurement of dubbing studios, subtitling platforms, or master license deals, issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) denominated in USD/EUR, payable at sight, with negotiation of compliant documents.
