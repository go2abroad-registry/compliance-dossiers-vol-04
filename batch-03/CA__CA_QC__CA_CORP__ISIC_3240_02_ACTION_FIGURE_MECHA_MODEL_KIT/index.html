Provincial (Quebec): Revenu Québec – portal: Cliquez Affaires (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/cpa/). CRA administers GST/HST and Part I federal corporate income tax; Revenu Québec administers QST and Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Quebec).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act and Part I of the Taxation Act (Quebec). No pass-through treatment is available at the entity level for an Inc./Corp.; profits are taxed at the corporation, with dividends subject to the dividend gross-up and notional tax credit mechanism for Canadian-resident shareholders. A non-CCPC (e.g., a corporation controlled by a foreign parent) is ineligible for the small business deduction and faces a higher federal rate and full Quebec corporate rate. Territorial scope: Canada taxes worldwide income of resident corporations; a non-resident corporation is taxed only on income from carrying on business in Canada and capital gains on taxable Canadian property, subject to any applicable Canada–foreign tax treaty. A branch tax under subsection 219(1) applies to unincorporated non-resident corporations at a 25% rate (or lower treaty rate) on after-tax Canadian-source earnings remitted to the foreign home jurisdiction.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under Canadian law; all business corporations are mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Available elections include: (i) subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election for a non-resident taxpayer with rental or royalty income to file a net-basis return and be taxed on net Canadian-source income; (ii) subsection 216.1 election for non-residents disposing of Canadian resource property; (iii) section 85 rollover election to transfer capital property to a corporation at agreed elected values; (iv) election under subsection 256(9) concerning associated-corporation status for the small business deduction; (v) GST/HST section 156 election between closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (vi) hybrid mismatch arrangements referenced in Part XVI.3 of the Income Tax Act (BEPS Action 2), which disallow deductions for certain payments giving rise to deduction/no-inclusion outcomes; and (vii) corresponding elections under section 297 of the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax for closely related persons to treat inter-entity supplies as nil-value supplies for QST purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act and the parallel QST threshold under section 5 of the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax). A non-resident manufacturing corporation carrying on business in Quebec must register for GST/HST and QST once quarterly taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available for manufacturers to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on molds, steel, resin, and tooling inputs used in export production. An existing registrant entity must monitor the 30-day cumulative threshold once any quarter pushes cumulative turnover over CAD 30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the Excise Tax Act applies to a supply of goods made by a registrant where the goods are exported by the supplier (or at the supplier's direction) as part of the supply and title transfers to a non-resident buyer before export; the supply must be made to a recipient who is a non-resident or not registered, and the goods must not be acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada prior to export. For services, section 7 of Schedule VI zero-rates services performed wholly outside Canada, while section 9 zero-rates advisory, consulting, or professional services rendered to a non-resident person provided the recipient is not registered under the Excise Tax Act and the supply is not for use in Canada in a commercial activity. For QST, the parallel zero-rating provisions are found in Schedule III of the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax, applying the same export tests. An action-figure mecha model kit manufacturer in Quebec that exports finished kits via international courier, freight forwarder, or ocean/air carrier may claim zero-rating on export sales and simultaneously recover input tax credits (federal ITCs) and input tax refunds (Quebec ITRs) on raw materials (engineering-grade ABS/PS resin, steel alloy mold inserts, packaging, and production utilities) used to manufacture the exported goods; refunds are generally paid on a periodic net-filing basis or by cash refund application with CRA Form GST189 for non-registrants and Revenu Québec Form VD-32 for ITR recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration) or carrier's ACI eManifest export reporting submitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) evidencing the outbound movement of goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean bill, air waybill, or rail/truck waybill) naming the Canadian manufacturer as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the HS code (e.g., 9503.00 figures/models) and confirmed FOB or CIF terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT MT103 wire-transfer evidence, PayPal transaction record, or foreign exchange conversion receipt demonstrating FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Non-resident purchase order or sales contract, plus any applicable import certificate or customs clearance proof from the destination country
  • ISO 9001/MDSAP manufacturing records, mold registration documentation, and batch records correlating the shipped goods to the zero-rated supplies (to substantiate the physical movement of the specific mecha model kits)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-injection, or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE, EDI, or CRA My Business Account); Revenu Québec requires electronic QST filing for registrants above the same threshold. E-invoicing remains voluntary; Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian Peppol PINT technical standard, and the federal government is promoting Peppol interoperability, but there is no statutory requirement for prior government clearance of invoices. Registrants must retain invoices and all source documents in a legible format for six years from the end of the fiscal year.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating and claw back previously claimed ITCs/ITRs if the exporter fails to maintain adequate evidence of export within a reasonable period; no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline exists in Canada, but the 180-day benchmark is applied as a documentation best practice and treaty-disputes reference. Reassessment window is generally four years from the filing due date, but is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (subsection 298(3) of the Excise Tax Act). Penalties include a 50% gross negligence penalty (subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act), interest on unremitted GST/HST/QST, 25% failure-to-file penalties, and criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act for wilful evasion; directors and officers may be personally liable for unremitted GST/HST/QST under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act and the equivalent QST provision. In an export zero-rating audit, the Crown may also reassess to impose full hotel/retail tax (5% GST + 9.975% QST) on the supply value plus penalty and interest, and any refund paid in error may be recovered under section 296 of the Excise Tax Act within two years of payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility. Canada has been an IMF Article VIII member since 1945 and imposes no exchange controls, no convertibility restrictions, and no FX licensing on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may be freely wired abroad. The only hurdles are tax-based, not FX-based: Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5-15% on dividends and 0-10% on royalties under applicable treaties), and dispositions of Canadian property by non-resident shareholders may require Section 116 clearance before funds are released. Inbound non-Canadian controlling investments may trigger Investment Canada Act review only above statutory financial thresholds (commonly enterprise value around CAD 1.3 billion for WTO investors, with a lower CAD 493 million threshold for non-WTO state-owned investors as of 2024). Banking practice imposes only AML/source-of-funds verification, not exchange-control gates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts. Exporters may freely retain, invest, or convert foreign currency balances in Canada or abroad at market rates. The compliance obligations that do apply are: (i) CBSA export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) for shipments with FOB value above CAD 2,000; (ii) FINTRAC reporting for any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) transfer pricing documentation where receivables are due from related foreign parties under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act. Prudent treasury practice recommends daily or weekly repatriation of USD receipts into a dedicated USD clearing account with a prescheduled rolling FX forward aligned to the accounts-receivable cycle.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), administered in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada is not an exchange-control jurisdiction; no central approval authority exists for FX allocations, import payment licensing, or profit remittance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary FX and low-value disbursement layer; Low as a primary treasury clearing rail. Foreign digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not hold Canadian bank licences and cannot lawfully accept demand deposits or settle directly through Payments Canada rails (Lynx or ACSS). They must register with FINTRAC as Money Service Businesses under the PCMLTFA and, where applicable, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration and risk-management regime. Funds held with an EMI are unsecured e-money, not CDIC-insured deposits. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is injected exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from the ordering party's named account directly to CA_CORP's segregated Capital Subscription Account at the designated Schedule I bank (National Bank or RBC). Canada maintains no central-bank FX approval or formal purpose-code list; the SWIFT field 70 remittance text should state 'Share capital subscription - CA_CORP' and field 50F must identify the actual beneficial owner/funder to avoid FINTRAC travel-rule suspicion. CAD wires settle with T+0 finality over Payments Canada Lynx; USD wires arrive via the bank's US correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) and are either credited to the USD sub-account or converted under an agreed standing instruction. Compliance gates: (i) OFAC and consolidated sanctions screening at both correspondent and beneficiary banks; (ii) FINTRAC large transaction reporting where the value is CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) underlying subscription agreement retained; (iv) Investment Canada Act filing where the non-Canadian investor exceeds applicable thresholds; (v) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting if direct investment crosses CAD 3 million in total assets.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for supplier settlements, licensing royalties, and trade payables, with a confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 for high-value mold/tooling capital expenditure. CAD disbursements above CAD 100,000 settle over Lynx with immediate finality; smaller CAD payments clear through ACSS; USD disbursements are settled through the bank's US correspondent with a prefunded nostro balance. Payment discipline: dual-approver release for all wires above CAD 25,000, SWIFT BIC validation, positive-pay reconciliation, prohibition on beneficiary-account changes absent amended bank documentation, and FX conversion booked at least two business days before value date. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a supplementary FX and low-value disbursement layer; Low as a primary treasury clearing rail. Foreign digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not hold Canadian bank licences and cannot lawfully accept demand deposits or settle directly through Payments Canada rails (Lynx or ACSS). They must register with FINTRAC as Money Service Businesses under the PCMLTFA and, where applicable, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration and risk-management regime. Funds held with an EMI are unsecured e-money, not CDIC-insured deposits. The acceptable blueprint is to use EMI virtual IBANs solely for inbound e-commerce card receipts and micro-supplier dispersion, with mandatory daily sweeps to the domestic Schedule I bank account.
