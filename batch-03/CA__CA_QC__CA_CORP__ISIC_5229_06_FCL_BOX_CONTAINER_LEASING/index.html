Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A post-office-box-style virtual office is impermissible as the registered office of a QBCA company. The registered office must be a physical location in Quebec at which the Registraire and legal process servers can actually leave notices and serve process (QBCA ss. 19-22; ARLPE s. 6). For a pure container-leasing entity without client-facing premises, a serviced office or a law firm's physical office with a real reception desk and an accepted-service-of-process protocol is permissible under prevailing Registraire practice; a bare mail-forwarding or virtual mailbox address is not compliant.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency and fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility, with no statutory approval, quota, or differential FX rate for profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital returns. Repatriation may be executed in CAD or any freely convertible foreign currency, subject only to tax treaty withholding and standard banking due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign currency receipts in designated foreign-currency accounts, convert at market rates at their discretion, or hedge those positions freely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the federal Minister of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) oversee prudential banking matters. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises anti-money-laundering and cross-border funds reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for direct deposit-taking and local custody, Medium as a cross-border payment facilitation layer. Under the Bank Act, only Canadian chartered banks and authorised credit unions may accept deposits in Canada. An offshore digital EMI cannot open a domestic bank account or hold client funds directly; it may register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and use a partner Canadian bank or licensed payment processor to clear and settle transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with remittance information referencing 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDERS LOAN', the corporate registration number, and the intended Canadian bank account. Foreign-currency wires are converted by the receiving bank at prevailing market rates. Any wire of CAD 10,000 or more is automatically reported to FINTRAC as a cross-border report. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment and penalties under GAAR; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for fraudulent transactions and may face penalties and imprisonment.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties including imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they fail to implement compliance measures or knowingly participate.
