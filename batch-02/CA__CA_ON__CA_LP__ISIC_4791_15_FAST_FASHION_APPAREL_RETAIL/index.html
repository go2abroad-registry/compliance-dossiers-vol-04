Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Registration and Business Number Portal; provincial tax authorities for Ontario (Ontario Ministry of Finance) for provincial portions of HST and other provincial levies

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a non-resident limited partnership) must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account via the CRA's non-resident registration process. The application can be filed online through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), or via authorized representative. The partnership must provide its constituent documents, the identity and addresses of all partners (general and limited), and a designated representative for service of process in Canada. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN plus a program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST). Voluntary registration is available before the mandatory threshold is reached; mandatory registration applies once the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity. Non-residents may be required to post security for GST/HST in certain circumstances under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not offer US-style check-the-box elections. A limited partnership is generally a fiscally transparent flow-through entity for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is generally treated as a separate person from its partners, and an election may be available to have a partner report and remit GST/HST on behalf of a qualifying partnership (section 273 of the Excise Tax Act). Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (section 18.4 and related provisions) address partnership interest mismatches; no generic election is available to alter the pass-through default. Ontario does not provide an entity classification election for income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000.00 worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or in any single calendar quarter), provided the supplies are made in the course of a commercial activity carried on in Canada. Non-resident persons making taxable supplies in Canada are subject to the same threshold, subject to specific agency rules for non-resident registrants. Voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (including fast-fashion apparel and footwear) are zero-rated under section 6 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are exported by the supplier as part of a supply made in Canada, ownership transfers outside Canada, and the goods are delivered outside Canada by common carrier or mail, or by the supplier's own transport, within prescribed timeframes. Offshore services that are performed entirely outside Canada or relate to goods exported from Canada may also be zero-rated under sections 7 and 10 of Part V of Schedule VI. The vendor must retain evidence that the goods were exported from Canada, including a customs export declaration, waybill, bill of lading, or other commercial shipping document, and must obtain and preserve such evidence within two years of the filing of the return in which the zero-rating was claimed.

  • Canada Customs Export Document (or CRA-approved equivalent electronic export report via the Canadian International Trade Single Window)
  • Commercial invoice showing the foreign purchaser, description of goods, Incoterms, and consideration
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance certificate from a financial institution showing foreign-sourced funds
  • Any contract or purchase order confirming the sale to a non-resident and passage of ownership outside Canada
  • Carrier tracking reports or customs transit documents if applicable

No federal or provincial real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists in Ontario or federally for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is permitted but optional. Taxpayers must issue prescribed information in a readable form, and invoices must be retained electronically. The CRA does not require digital stamping or API-based pre-clearance of individual invoices. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with prescribed turnover thresholds, using CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST TELEFILE; GST/HST web services (API) are available for registered intermediaries and tax software. Ontario's Ministry of Finance does not impose a separate invoice clearance system for HST.

The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period (and later in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default) to deny zero-rating where export documentation is insufficient or false. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: (i) a penalty of up to 50% of the net unpaid tax under section 285 for knowingly making a false statement or omission; (ii) a penalty of CAD 500 under section 280 for failure to provide prescribed information; (iii) interest at prescribed rates on any assessed bulk amount; and (iv) criminal prosecution for fraud under the Income Tax Act and Criminal Code for fraudulent evasion of GST/HST. Retroactive clawbacks can include disallowance of input tax credits claimed in association with the exported goods, recovery of unpaid tax plus interest and penalties, and suspension of the registrant's BN. Exporters must maintain full contemporaneous documentation and ensure repatriation of foreign currency within the contractual terms; the CRA typically expects export proceeds to be received within a reasonable period, and a 180-day benchmark is commonly used as an internal control threshold, though no express statutory FX repatriation deadline exists for GST/HST zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions; Canada imposes no exchange control law, capital-account licensing, or surrender regime under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) or the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2). The CAD is freely floating. Profit remittances from the Ontario limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are not blocked; such profit allocations are taxed in Canada under Part I of the Income Tax Act through the partnership (T5013) but generally are not subject to Part XIII withholding when the allocation constitutes a true partnership profit distribution rather than a deemed dividend, interest, or management fee. The principal gatekeeping obligations are FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD $10,000 or more and OSFI-regulated bank AML verification, not exchange-control permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender period or mandatory conversion ratio applies to Canadian export receipts. All proceeds from cross-border e-commerce sales may be retained in any currency and held offshore or onshore. Operational obligations: (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report (EFTR) for every cross-border EFT of CAD $10,000 or more, submitted within 24 hours of receipt; (ii) CBSA export declarations and customs accounting for apparel/footwear shipments; (iii) CRA foreign-currency translation under s. 261 of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) maintenance of an anti-money-laundering compliance regime under the PCMLTFA, including a designated compliance officer, training, and risk assessment, because the LP's treasury volume will exceed FINTRAC monetary thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; foreign exchange and cross-border administration delegated through the Department of Finance Canada, Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs and export controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not license Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) as a distinct category; only Schedule I and Schedule II banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) may accept deposit liabilities. Shopify Payments (processing via Stripe Canada) and Klarna's Canadian Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) rails are permissible and commercially viable settlement integrations, provided each payment service provider is FINTRAC-registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) and complies with Ontario consumer-protection law. An offshore UK/EU EMI is not viable as the LP's primary CAD settlement account because it cannot participate directly in Payments Canada clearing; foreign fintech settlement must route through a Schedule I Canadian clearing bank. If the LP itself engages in currency exchange as a business (rather than hedging its own commercial exposure), it must register as an MSB with FINTRAC.

An offshore UK/EU EMI is not viable as the LP's primary CAD settlement account because it cannot participate directly in Payments Canada clearing; foreign fintech settlement must route through a Schedule I Canadian clearing bank. If the LP itself engages in currency exchange as a business (rather than hedging its own commercial exposure), it must register as an MSB with FINTRAC.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Ontario 11.5% general corporate rate; note: LP is a pass-through entity, no entity-level tax, partners taxed individually)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents. However, an LP does not pay dividends; distributions to partners are generally not subject to withholding tax unless they are considered interest, rent, royalties, or certain other amounts. For non-resident partners, income allocations may be subject to Part XIII tax or Part I tax depending on the nature.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian TP documentation thresholds: A Local File is required if the total value of transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services, interest, royalties). A Master File is required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50 million. For an LP, these thresholds apply to the partnership's transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. For an LP, the partnership must have genuine economic substance (e.g., real business operations, employees, premises) to avoid challenges under GAAR or treaty abuse rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act. For an LP, the general partner (if a corporation) can be held liable; directors may face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR to recharacterize transactions, and impose penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities, plus criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly fail to comply with AML obligations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million), plus interest and back taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the TP mispricing.
