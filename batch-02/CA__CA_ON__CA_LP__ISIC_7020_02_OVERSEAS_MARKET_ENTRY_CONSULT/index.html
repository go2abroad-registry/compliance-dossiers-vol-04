Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Ontario limited partnership must be validly constituted under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, RSO 1990, c. L.8, with a registered office in Ontario. Step 2: The General Partner applies on behalf of the partnership for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by filing RC1 (Request for a Business Number), opening the GST/HST and other applicable program accounts. Step 3: Ontario does not issue a separate TIN; the federal BN serves as the single statutory identifier for GST/HST, payroll, and income tax, while provincial registration is effectuated through the Ontario Business Registry solely for extra-provincial or administrative record purposes. Step 4: Each non-resident partner must separately obtain a CRA non-resident account and, where treaty relief is sought, provide a Canada Revenue Agency Form NR301 or equivalent certificate of residence to avoid Canadian withholding on partnership income allocations. Step 5: Where the LP supplies only offshore consultancy services to non-residents, registration is not compulsory if the CAD 30,000 threshold is not exceeded, but voluntary registration under Excise Tax Act s. 240 may be elected to recover input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification election of the US IRS Form 8832 type; an Ontario limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96 and is not a taxpayer for income tax purposes, though a registrant partnership is deemed a person for GST/HST purposes. Available elections include: (i) election under ITA s. 249.1 to adopt a non-calendar-year partnership fiscal period; (ii) GST/HST closely-related nil-consideration election and joint venture election under ETA s. 273; (iii) functional-currency reporting election under ITA s. 261; (iv) net-basis treaty election for non-resident partners receiving Canadian business income; and (v) where hybrid-mismatch recharacterization applies (ITA Chapter 6 rules, ss. 17.2-17.6, 20.1, 96.1, 112.3), no elective override is available—classification is determined by legal form, common law partnership analysis, and treaty tie-breaker rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 307(1), computed on total worldwide taxable supplies (net of zero-rated supplies and financial services); for non-resident suppliers of digital services or platform operators, the threshold is applied to Canadian-source specified supplies under the July 2021 digital economy measures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act applies to: (i) goods exported from Canada, when exported by the vendor within 60 days of transfer of ownership (ss. 1-2); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident client (s. 20), where the service is for consumption wholly outside Canada, the client is not a GST/HST registrant, and the service does not relate to real/immovable property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; and (iii) intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident (s. 21). For the present market-entry consultancy, an advisory engagement was delivered to a non-resident corporate client covering foreign-market feasibility, regulatory licensing, and route-to-market assessment is generally zero-rated under s. 20 if consumed offshore. Supplies that relate to Canadian real estate, are made to Canadian-resident clients, or are physically consumed or made available in Canada are taxable at the 13% Ontario HST rate. Zero-rated supplies count toward the CAD 30,000 registration threshold.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Form B13A filed with the Canada Border Services Agency where goods valued above CAD 2,000 or prescribed controlled goods are exported; for pure consultancy service exports, the equivalent export evidence is an executed cross-border service agreement stipulating offshore consumption and the non-resident status of the recipient
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - shipping document evidencing physical export of the goods; for service exports, electronic delivery records, email engagement trails, and platform delivery logs serve as substitutable evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, interbank transfer receipt, or account statement evidencing receipt of the non-resident client's consideration into a Canadian or treaty-recognized foreign currency account within the repatriation benchmark period

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or government pre-printed numbering mandate. Registrants must retain source books and records for six years, issue valid GST/HST invoices whenever tax is charged, and file returns electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE when annual taxable revenues exceed CAD 1.5 million. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoice standard is voluntary for B2B/B2G transactions but is increasingly contractually required by public-sector and large corporate buyers; CRA accepts electronic invoices as valid source records provided they are legible, complete, and retained in an accessible format. Use of Wise B2B or bank wire payment rails does not modify the statutory invoicing or record-keeping obligations.

CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax within the normal four-year reassessment period (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)); there is no limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established. A false zero-rating or refund claim attracts a civil penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the tax sought under ETA s. 285 (gross negligence); failure to register, collect, or remit may attract ETA s. 280 penalties of up to 20% of the uncollected net tax, plus prescribed interest. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 can yield fines up to CAD 200,000 per count and imprisonment of up to five years. Refund claims are retroactively clawed back with prescribed interest (currently approximately 8% on overdue amounts and 5% on refunds), and every general partner of the limited partnership is jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST debts under ETA s. 272.1. CRA may also invoke the general anti-avoidance rule under ITA s. 245 where the export arrangement artificially defers Canadian taxation, and will ordinarily deny zero-rating where the documentary bundle is incomplete.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII country and maintains a free-floating, fully convertible currency. Current and capital account transactions are not subject to exchange control restrictions. Profit remittance by non-resident limited partners is legally unrestricted, requiring only standard banking channels and applicable tax compliance. Practical hurdles include anti-money-laundering due diligence, FATCA/CRS self-certification, and proof of source-of-funds; no ex-ante government approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export proceeds. Cross-border receipts exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Exporters must retain underlying commercial documentation (contracts, invoices, customs declarations) but are free to hold or convert CAD proceeds at commercial banks, or retain foreign currency under normal banking arrangements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sovereign central bank issuing and regulating the Canadian dollar (CAD). Banking supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). There is no dedicated foreign exchange administration authority; exchange control functions are historically repealed and no active approvals are required for capital or trade flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability: High as complementary cross-border payment rails, but low-to-medium as primary treasury banking. Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, not as chartered banks. They can legally aggregate cross-border wires, offer foreign-exchange conversion, and provide multi-currency virtual accounts, but they cannot accept CAD demand deposits, issue credit, or hold funds with deposit insurance. They are an acceptable secondary settlement layer for sub-consultant disbursements and low-value consultancy fees, but principal investor capital escrow and statutory borrowing should reside at a Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: overseas limited partners or ultimate beneficial owners execute a SWIFT MT103 wire to the LP's Canadian operational account at a Tier-1 bank. Beneficiary naming on the wire must identify the General Partner in its capacity as agent for the LP, followed by the LP's legal name and bank account number (Canada uses institution number + transit number rather than IBAN). The remitting bank must reference the wire purpose as 'LP Capital Contribution' or 'Direct Investment - Non-Resident'; Canada does not prescribe a federal purpose code, but the bank's KYC team will map to an internal maintenance type. Supporting documentation must include the LP certificate, written capital call, subscription form, investor declaration of source of funds, and a notarized resolution of the general partner. If the inbound contribution exceeds CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting obligations arise and the bank will require a reason code; wires routed through a Money Service Business (e.g., Wise) are generally not accepted for capital-injection purposes unless followed by an explanatory corporate-to-corporate MT103 from the EMI's pooled bank account.For overseas market-entry consulting, primary outbound clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers for vendor payments, sub-consultant fees, licensing/royalty payments, and reimbursements; standard bank forms require the beneficiary's full SWIFT BIC and account/IBAN. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or term) issued on a Canadian bank's trade desk when procuring physical or digital infrastructure from foreign suppliers requiring documentary protection; documents include invoice, packing list, and evidence of advisory milestone acceptance. (3) For low-value intercompany or intra-group flows, Wise Business or Airwallex virtual account transfers through their Canadian MSB licences can achieve cost-effective settlement, but MT103 remains the gold-standard for constructive payment evidence. Each outbound disbursement must incorporate the correct ISO 20022 remittance purpose code (e.g., 'Market Entry Consulting Fees' or 'INTL Business Services') and retain trial evidence of the underlying consultancy service to counter AML structuring triggers.Deliverable FX Forwards (standard bilateral OTC contracts between the LP and its Canadian, bank-regulated dealer, typically up to 12-24 months, CAD/USD and CAD/EUR pricing against spot with two-way margin)OTC FX Options (calls/puts, European or American style, enabling the LP to cap conversion costs while retaining upside on CAD/EUR or CAD/USD trade settlement)Cross-Currency Swaps (XCCY) for matched principal and interest flows on dual-currency capital contributions or term debt, executed under ISDA 2002 agreements with collateral in CAD T-bills or cash)FX Swaps (roll or swap of forward settlement dates for working capital circularity, treated as separate spot and forward legs)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) exclusively for hedging non-convertible emerging-market counterparty currencies within the consultancy client base, under dealer confirmation templates referencing an ISDA 1998 NDF AnnexCME/ICE-listed FX futures and options on CAD, USD, EUR for exchange-traded margin-efficient risk transfer and hedge accounting under IAS 39 / IFRS 9

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability: High as complementary cross-border payment rails, but low-to-medium as primary treasury banking. Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, not as chartered banks. They can legally aggregate cross-border wires, offer foreign-exchange conversion, and provide multi-currency virtual accounts, but they cannot accept CAD demand deposits, issue credit, or hold funds with deposit insurance. They are an acceptable secondary settlement layer for sub-consultant disbursements and low-value consultancy fees, but principal investor capital escrow and statutory borrowing should reside at a Tier-1 bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada federal general corporate income tax 15% plus Ontario general corporate income tax 11.5% = combined 26.5%. The Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not itself taxed at entity level; tax is imposed on each partner's allocated share of income at the partner's applicable federal and Ontario rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner, subject to treaty reduction. Profit allocations from an Ontario limited partnership are not dividends; non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source partnership income, and distributions of that already-taxed income are generally not subject to additional Canadian withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year fiscal period, the T5013 return must be filed by May 31 of the following year (five months after fiscal period end); T5013 slips must be provided to partners by March 31. Fiscal periods cannot exceed 12 months without CRA approval.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the deadline is June 30 of the following year. Tax balance-due is due two months after year-end (three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations); instalments are required quarterly for larger amounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions of the taxpayer in a taxation year exceeds C$1,000,000. Mandatory Country-by-Country Report and Master File thresholds apply to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of C$750,000,000 or more. T106 (Information Return on Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed for reportable transactions exceeding C$1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but requires genuine economic substance through general anti-avoidance rules (Income Tax Act s. 245), the sham doctrine, Canadian common-law business purpose, and permanent establishment tests under tax treaties. An Ontario LP providing overseas market-entry consulting must maintain real contracts, employees, offices, business records, bank accounts, and substantive decision-making in Canada or in the jurisdiction where the PE is located. A shell LP inserted solely to access treaty benefits or defer Canadian tax is vulnerable to GAAR reassessment, treaty abuse denial, penalties, and the possible recharacterization of the entire structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), s. 239)

Statutory sanction: On conviction by indictment: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded. On summary conviction: imprisonment up to 2 years less a day and a similar fine. CRA also assesses 50% gross negligence penalties and arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and corporate general partners who authorize or participate in evasion may be prosecuted personally and held jointly and severally liable for corporate unpaid tax and penalties. Conviction can trigger travel bans, asset seizure, civil restitution orders, and imprisonment.
GAAR Abusive Avoidance / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny the tax benefit under s. 245(2), impose a penalty equal to 25% of the denied tax benefit under s. 245(4), and assess interest. A 'shell' finding can result in treating the LP as a sham, voiding allocations, and taxing the Canadian source income on a look-through basis.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the LP is a sham or where a limited partner participates in control. Directors of the corporate general partner may face civil restitution, penalty assessments, asset freezes, travel bans in criminal proceedings, and, in flagrant cases, imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust income to the arm's-length amount under s. 247(2). Where documentation is inadequate or not prepared contemporaneously, the s. 247(3) penalty equals 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, with no offset for reasonable efforts. Interest applies to reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors do not automatically face personal liability for transfer-pricing adjustments, but directors who sign false returns or cause the corporation to evade tax can be assessed as tax evaders under s. 239, prosecuted personally, and subjected to restitution orders and travel bans.
Withholding Tax / Source Deduction Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess the full unwithheld amount, interest, and penalties under ss. 227(8)-(9). Directors are personally and jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts. Criminal prosecution under s. 238 can result in fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The directors and officers of the corporate general partner can be held personally liable without proof of intent for unremitted withholdings. The Crown has priority in bankruptcy, and personal travel bans and imprisonment can follow wilful non-compliance.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach C$1,000,000 for corporations and C$500,000 for individuals per violation. Criminal offences may result in imprisonment up to 5 years, asset forfeiture, and prohibition orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly participate in AML violations are personally exposed to fines, imprisonment, civil restitution, and travel bans. Courts may also order disqualification from serving as directors or officers.
