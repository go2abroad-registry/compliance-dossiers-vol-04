Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For income tax the CA_LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96 and the Quebec Taxation Act; partners, not the partnership, report allocable Canadian-source business income, and non-resident partners are exposed to Canadian income tax only to the extent the LP carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada. For GST/QST purposes, however, the partnership is a distinct person and registrant in its own name: the CA_LP invoices marine survey fees, charges GST and QST, and claims input tax credits, while the general partner, as counterparty of record, remains responsible for the partnership's tax obligations in liquidation and assessment proceedings.

Available election forms

Canadian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Quebec LP is transparent by force of law. Relevant elections include: (i) a fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1 to align the partnership taxation year with the partner inclusion rules; (ii) the GST/HST Quick Method election and annual-basis remittance election for eligible small registrants; (iii) an election to align the QST reporting period with the GST/HST reporting period for submission to Revenu Quebec; and (iv) an election by the LP to waive the zero-rating of export supplies where the documentary burden is disproportionate to the tax saving. The 2021 Canadian hybrid-mismatch regime (ITA ss.18.6-18.7 and the parallel QSTA provisions) operates automatically as a mandatory denial/inclusion rule without elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 Canadian dollars of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, in any single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; the identical small-supplier threshold applies to both GST/HST and QST. A foreign marine insurance surveyor physically performing inspections at Quebec ports or on vessels in Quebec territorial waters will normally exceed the threshold in its first season and must register no later than the end of the following calendar month/quarter; if services are performed by a non-resident without a permanent establishment and total revenues remain below C$30,000, registration is optional but is recommended to claim input tax credit refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A marine insurance surveyor may claim zero-rating on two alternative statutory bases. First, a supply of an inspection, testing, or evaluating service in respect of tangible personal property that is exported by the recipient is zero-rated where the goods are exported from Canada no later than the 60th day after the service is fully performed, the exporter is the recipient or related person, and the supplier obtains the prescribed export evidence within the document-retention window. Second, a supply of a surveying or claims-advisory service to a non-resident underwriter may be zero-rated where the non-resident is not registered and does not carry on business in Canada and the service does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; an on-site hull or cargo survey at a Quebec port therefore cannot qualify under the second rule, and the surveyor must instead rely on the export-of-goods rule if the damaged cargo or vessel is subsequently exported. Desk-based claims review, loss-ratio analysis, and survey report preparation performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated as non-resident services. QST zero-rating mirrors the federal test; where zero-rating applies, the LP retains full input tax credit entitlement and may claim a refund of Quebec input tax refunds in respect of the exported supply.

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or invoice-by-invoice API mandate exists under the Excise Tax Act; CRA accepts GST/HST returns through NETFILE and B2B/EDI gateways but does not validate individual invoices in real time. Quebec, however, has enacted a phased mandatory electronic-invoicing and e-reporting obligation under the Tax Administration Act (CQLR c. A-6.002): large enterprises must issue PEPPOL-compliant structured e-invoices (Factur-X or UBL) from January 1, 2026, with medium and small enterprises brought in progressively by 2029. Until the applicable phase-in date, paper or generic PDF invoices bearing the GST/HST and QST registration numbers, supplier name, client name, description of the marine survey services, and tax amounts remain legally compliant for deduction and zero-rating documentation.

CRA and Revenu Quebec perform targeted export-verification audits of zero-rated professional fees because marine surveys are frequently performed at the border and the export evidence is often late or internally inconsistent. The standard assessment window is four years; it extends to seven years for careless or misleading statements and is unlimited for fraudulent misrepresentation. Civil penalties include a 25% gross-negligence penalty on the net tax understated, a default penalty of 5% plus 1%-2% monthly for unreported GST/QST, and Quebec administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Act of up to 25% of the amount misreported. Retrospective clawbacks are common: if the prescribed export documentation is not retained for six years, the authorities reverse the zero-rating, assess GST/QST at 14.975% on the full survey fee, deny the corresponding input tax credits, and charge interest compounded daily. Criminal liability arises under the Excise Tax Act where export invoices are fabricated or grossly inflated, with fines on conviction, imprisonment, and publication of the taxpayer's name in the federal and Quebec tax registries.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full legal convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) for both current account and capital account transactions under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52), the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. B-2) and Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittance, partnership distributions, dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, capital repatriation and liquidation proceeds may be paid to non-residents without prior regulatory consent. The practical hurdles are limited to: (i) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for non-controlling or above-threshold controlling acquisitions (this marine surveying business, classified under ISIC 5222 / NAICS 488390, falls below the WTO-increased threshold and is not a Cultural Business); (ii) Part XIII Canadian withholding tax, treaty-reduced where applicable, on passive distributions to foreign partners; (iii) Quebec registration obligations under the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and the French-language business rules of the Charte de la langue française; and (iv) statutory reporting under the Income Tax Act, including Form T1135 when the cost amount of foreign property held by a Canadian tax resident partner exceeds CAD 100,000. Service fees and claim-settlement monies are freely receivable and convertible in either CAD or foreign currency at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or service fee revenue, including marine survey fees earned from non-resident underwriters. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in Canadian Foreign Currency Accounts or offshore bank accounts. The only compliance obligations are informational: (i) Canada Border Services Agency export declarations where goods accompany or are embedded in the surveying engagement; (ii) FINTRAC's International Electronic Funds Transfer Report obligation imposed on the financial institution for every cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more, with no requirement that funds be brought onshore; and (iii) the T1135 foreign-property reporting threshold applicable to resident partners, which is an income-tax reporting duty, not a currency-restoration duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa, acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as banking regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML supervisory authority. Canada operates a market-determined freely floating exchange rate regime; there is no foreign exchange control act, no central-bank approval desk for commercial transactions, and no exchange control quota or licensing system applicable to current or capital account operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for operational disbursement rails and Low for escrow or fiduciary functions. A FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business operating in Canada, including Wise acting through its Canadian MSB registration, may lawfully execute low-value cross-border vendor wires, expense reimbursements and foreign-currency payouts for the LP. However, an offshore EMI that is not registered as an MSB with FINTRAC cannot hold a CAD settlement account, cannot clear through Payments Canada rails, and will not satisfy the compliance standard for escrowed third-party funds. Where the marine surveying contract requires the LP to receive underwriter claim monies and disburse them to repairers or adjusters, those funds must be held in a Canadian Schedule I bank or regulated trust company and must never be commingled in an EMI omnibus wallet. Recommended protocol: deploy Wise only for operational disbursements below the governing partnership agreement's materiality threshold and route all capital injections and escrowed claim settlements through a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital contributions must be transmitted as SWIFT MT103 messages (or ISO 20022 pacs.008 equivalents) carrying the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' or the narrative 'Capital contribution — Quebec Limited Partnership', and must reference the LP's NEQ registration number and the corresponding capital-call letter. Settlement occurs through the receiving Canadian bank's LVTS or ACSS clearing infrastructure into a dedicated CAD, USD, or foreign-currency operational/escrow account depending on the LP Agreement's currency clauses. The remitting bank will generate FINTRAC reporting automatically where the wire equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, and the receiving bank must reconcile source-of-funds documentation against the capital-call schedule. For USD injections, best practice is to route via the Canadian bank's US clearing node (Fedwire or CHIPS) and then perform an internal ledger transfer into the LP's USD sub-account, thereby avoiding unnecessary FX spreads; the bank will apply its published corporate FX rate only when the partner instructs conversion into CAD.Trade settlements and claim disbursements flow through two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 international payments for vendor, adjuster and repairer wires, and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued, advised or confirmed by a Canadian Schedule I bank under UCP600 for large documentary claim settlements or recurring supply arrangements. The CAD legs clear through Payments Canada's ACSS or LVTS, while USD legs clear via Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent or wholly owned US banking entity. Every disbursement instruction must identify the beneficiary's legal name, full address, BIC and account number, and must carry a purpose narrative referencing the claim-file number and underlying survey report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational disbursement rails and Low for escrow or fiduciary functions. A FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business operating in Canada, including Wise acting through its Canadian MSB registration, may lawfully execute low-value cross-border vendor wires, expense reimbursements and foreign-currency payouts for the LP. However, an offshore EMI that is not registered as an MSB with FINTRAC cannot hold a CAD settlement account, cannot clear through Payments Canada rails, and will not satisfy the compliance standard for escrowed third-party funds. Where the marine surveying contract requires the LP to receive underwriter claim monies and disburse them to repairers or adjusters, those funds must be held in a Canadian Schedule I bank or regulated trust company and must never be commingled in an EMI omnibus wallet. Recommended protocol: deploy Wise only for operational disbursements below the governing partnership agreement's materiality threshold and route all capital injections and escrowed claim settlements through a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined 26.5% (Canada federal 15% + Quebec provincial 11.5%) for corporate partners; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada's statutory Part XIII withholding tax rate is 25% for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. A Limited Partnership, being fiscally transparent, does not pay dividends to partners; distributions are not subject to dividend withholding tax. Allocated taxable income is instead reported on each partner's return (T1/T2) and taxed according to the partner's status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD $1 million in aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border transactions with a single non-resident related party triggers mandatory T106 and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File). A Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or CAD equivalent.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code. The federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and Quebec GAAR (Tax Administration Act s.1079.4) deny tax benefits arising from arrangements lacking true economic substance, commercial purpose, or that rely on artificial steps. In an LP context, the general partner must demonstrate actual managerial capacity, premises, and operational activity in Quebec; a pure shell partnership with non-resident partners may be challenged and recharacterized.
