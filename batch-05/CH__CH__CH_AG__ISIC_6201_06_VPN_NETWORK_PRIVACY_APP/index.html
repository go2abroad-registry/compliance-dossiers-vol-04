Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV/FTA), Eigerstrasse 65, 3003 Bern, Switzerland; official portal and electronic services at https://www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG), the foreign shareholder first completes notarial formation and registration with the competent Cantonal Commercial Register Office. The commercial register entry automatically generates the Swiss business identification number (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which serves as the statutory TIN for federal and cantonal direct taxes. For value added tax purposes, the AG must submit a VAT registration application to the FTA through the official e-service portal after UID issuance; the FTA then allocates a distinct Swiss VAT number (MWST-UID). A non-resident controlling shareholder may act through a Swiss officer or director; if the AG has no Swiss registered office, a domestic VAT representative may be required to accept service and ensure compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide a US-style check-the-box election, and an AG is mandatorily taxed at entity level for federal, cantonal, and municipal direct taxes. Available statutory elections include, without limitation: (i) the Swiss VAT group election under Art. 20 of the Swiss VAT Act, allowing closely related enterprises to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) the election between the agreed-consideration method and the received-consideration method for VAT; (iii) the election to apply the net-net tax deduction method versus the saldo (flat-rate input tax deduction) method where permitted by the FTA; and (iv) for internationally active groups, the Swiss participation relief and equity method elections for qualifying substantial holdings. Hybrid mismatch elections as understood in GILTI/check-the-box systems are generally unavailable; immigration/anti-hybrid characterization follows Swiss legal-form principles and OECD BEPS Action 2 adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000.00 of worldwide annual taxable turnover from supplies to Swiss taxable recipients and from services physically performed in Switzerland; a foreign-owned Swiss AG is required to register for VAT if the threshold is exceeded, and a non-resident business with Swiss-source turnover has the same registration duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to supplies of goods transported by the supplier, by the buyer, or on behalf of either party directly from Switzerland to an external customs territory, and to qualifying cross-border services under Art. 23 of the Swiss VAT Act. A VPN/network-privacy SaaS provider may claim 0% on services supplied to a business customer that is incorporated or has its commercial establishment abroad, or to services that are demonstrably used and economically exploited wholly outside Switzerland. The eligibility condition requires contemporaneous documentary proof that the recipient is an overseas entrepreneur or, in the case of a private recipient, that the actual place of use/benefit is outside Switzerland; digital download, login location, and foreign ISP data may support the claim. Supplies to Swiss private consumers, including through VPN applications sold to Swiss users, remain taxable at the standard rate of 8.1%.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec or ATLAS-equivalent export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, combined transport document, CMR consignment note, Air Waybill, or other statutory transport evidence
  • Commercial invoice with Swiss UID/VAT number of supplier and buyer identification
  • Contract, purchase order, or licence/SaaS agreement establishing export or offshore service terms
  • Bank SWIFT credit confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Optional but recommended: foreign registration/residency certificate of the recipient and technical evidence of offshore use (server logs, IP geo-location)

Switzerland does not operate a general real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory government-authorised digital stamping, or continuous transaction control API regime. E-invoices are legally valid if they reliably capture all VAT invoice content elements. For payment processing, the Swiss QR-bill standard is obligatory for paper and electronic payment slips in domestic CHF payments; federal procurement e-invoicing is Peppol/eBill based, but no blanket private B2B e-invoicing or online VAT clearance system is imposed. VAT returns must be filed electronically to FTA proprietary software; third-party software taxpayers may submit returns through FTA-developed e-service APIs only in authorised XML/JSON-like formats.

The FTA and cantonal tax authorities conduct risk-based export zero-rating audits, particularly for digital and SaaS supplies. If documentation is deficient or fraudulent, the zero-rated treatment is retrospectively reversed, additional VAT at 8.1% is assessed with default interest at 4% annually, and administrative fines of up to three times the tax evaded or up to CHF 250,000 may be imposed. Wilful tax evasion or forgery of customs proofs triggers criminal referral, possible debarment from e-clearing privileges, and joint liability of directors/officers. The limitation period for reassessment is generally ten years, and the burden of proving export status and service recipient resides in the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully convertible, exchange-control-free regime. Current-account flows (SaaS and VPN licence revenue) and capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation) are freely transferable without prior governmental approval. CHF is an independently floating reserve currency; the SNB imposes no quantitative convertibility limits, compulsory conversion rates, or import/export FX licensing. Profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal, not monetary: dividends paid by a Swiss AG are subject to the 35% statutory withholding tax (reclaimable or reduced under an applicable double-taxation agreement), and related-party payments require transfer-pricing documentation, but neither triggers any FX authorisation requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio applies to Swiss export proceeds. A Swiss AG may retain foreign-currency earnings indefinitely in any domestic or foreign account. Swiss Code of Obligations Art. 958-963b requires only periodic translation into the CHF functional currency for financial statements; there is no statutory 180-day rule and no minimum conversion percentage. The only reporting obligation is the monthly SNB statistical return filed by the paying bank for transactions above the statistical threshold. A VPN/network-privacy app collecting USD, EUR, or stablecoin subscription revenue may settle entirely through a Swiss AML-regulated bank without any SNB filing of its own.

Central bank supervision: The Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank) as monetary authority and FX-surveillance institution; the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) as licensing and AML/CTF supervisor for banks and financial intermediaries; the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) as sanctions and international financial-policy enforcement bodies.

They are acceptable strictly as pass-through payment-collection rails with full travel-rule identity pass-through and transparent disclosure under FINMA Circular 2016/07.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct tax rate on net corporate profit, plus cantonal and communal profit taxes; the aggregate headline for a commercial CH_AG generally ranges from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to approximately 21.0% (Geneva) depending on registered seat and applicable cantonal tariff.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% of the gross dividend distribution paid by the CH_AG. Distributions from properly evidenced reserves from capital contributions may be returned to shareholders outside the withholding tax net if the statutory reporting conditions and Federal Tax Administration declaration procedures are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

There is no single national deadline; cantonal procedural law generally requires filing within six months after fiscal year-end. A calendar-year AG is normally required to file in the first half of the following year, with extensions to 30 September or 31 October available on application. Default interest at 4.0% per annum runs after the original deadline, and late filing may trigger procedural penalties.
