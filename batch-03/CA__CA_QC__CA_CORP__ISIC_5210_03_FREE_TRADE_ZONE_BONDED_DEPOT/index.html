Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the CBCA or QBCA; shares may be issued for nominal consideration (e.g., C$1 per common share) payable in cash, property, or past services, with cash consideration deposited to the corporate bank account. No escrow account is mandated at incorporation, but Canada Border Services Agency customs bonded warehouse licensing requires a security bond (typically C$250,000-C$500,000 or an amount equal to one to two months of applicable duties and taxes) to be posted and maintained.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire safety service in accordance with the Quebec Fire Safety Code (CQLR c B-1.1, r. 5) and the Building Act; structural load inspection certificate from a licensed engineer to verify racking and floor capacity for bonded goods; occupational health and safety clearance issued by the Commission des normes, de l'equite, de la sante et de la securite du travail (CNESST) under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (CQLR c S-2.1), including first aid, emergency response planning, and dangerous goods storage measures under the Civil Protection Act (CQLR c S-2.3).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the CBCA or QBCA; shares may be issued for nominal consideration (e.g., C$1 per common share) payable in cash, property, or past services, with cash consideration deposited to the corporate bank account. No escrow account is mandated at incorporation, but Canada Border Services Agency customs bonded warehouse licensing requires a security bond (typically C$250,000-C$500,000 or an amount equal to one to two months of applicable duties and taxes) to be posted and maintained.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal income tax, GST/HST, and customs/import program accounts; Revenu Québec – Quebec provincial income tax and Quebec Sales Tax (QST). Portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-portal-business-account.html) and Revenu Québec 'clicSÉQUR Affaires' (https://www.revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 with supporting constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, articles, or equivalent foreign registration, proof of registered office, director/officer details, and permanent establishment information). The BN serves as the Canadian tax identification number and can include separate program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RM (customs/import-export), and RC (payroll). For Quebec-specific QST and provincial corporate tax registration, the same BN is cross-referenced and the entity must complete Revenu Québec Form LM-101-V, or register online through clicSÉQUR Affaires, to obtain a QST registration number. If the corporation is a non-resident, it must also evaluate whether it is carrying on business in Canada under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act; registration is required where taxable supplies are made in Canada above the small-supplier threshold, where goods are imported for commercial use, or where the corporation has a permanent establishment in Quebec. For a customs bonded warehouse in a free trade zone, the operator must additionally obtain a Customs Bonded Warehouse licence from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), post applicable security, and obtain a BN with an RM customs account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian-resident business corporations are taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, with Quebec adding its own entity-level corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Quebec). There is no shareholder flow-through for a Business Corporation. Territorial scope for non-resident corporations: Non-residents are subject to Canadian income tax only on certain Canadian-source income, including profits from carrying on business in Canada and gains from dispositions of taxable Canadian property. The free trade zone / bonded depot designation is a customs duty deferral and indirect tax measure; it does not create an income tax exemption zone. A Quebec Business Corporation may be eligible for Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status, which affects small business deduction, refundable dividend tax, and tax instalment rules, but CCPC status is not a tax classification election.

Available election forms

Canada and Quebec do not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Entity classification follows legal form: an incorporated company is a taxpayer in its own right, while a partnership or trust is treated separately under the Income Tax Act. For non-resident corporations in particular, relevant statutory elections include the s. 216 Income Tax Act election to compute Canadian tax on net rental and timber-royalty income, the s. 216.1 election for resource royalty income, the s. 219 non-resident corporation branch tax filing, and elections regarding treaty-reduced withholding rates. The hybrid mismatch rules (including deduction-denial rules and treaty-relief provisions applicable to hybrid entities and hybrid transfers) do not offer an entity classification election; instead, they operate to deny deductions in cross-border structures unless the relevant jurisdiction applies equivalent treatment. A foreign entity that is transparent in its home jurisdiction may nonetheless be treated as an association, partnership, or corporation under Canadian domestic law, so classification must be determined based on legal characteristics and can be confirmed by a CRA advance income tax ruling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters (the small-supplier threshold for both CRA GST/HST and Revenu Québec QST). A non-resident corporation that imports goods into Canada or carries on business in Canada may be required to register for GST/HST and QST regardless of the small-supplier threshold, and a customs bonded warehouse operator must be registered for GST/HST and QST on all taxable supplies made in Canada, including removals, storage fees, and handling charges.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property from Canada are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act and, for Quebec, under the QST zero-rating rules, provided the supplier proves that the goods are delivered or shipped to a recipient outside Canada, ownership passes to a non-resident person, and the goods are actually exported as part of the same commercial transaction. For a customs bonded warehouse / free trade zone depot, goods held under customs control and subsequently exported outside Canada can be zero-rated if all documentary proof of export is retained; domestic supplies of storage, handling, and logistics services inside Canada are generally taxed at 5% GST and 9.975% QST unless the service is specifically supplied for the non-resident and not consumed in Canada. QST zero-rating follows the same export substantiation standard, requiring the supplier to show that the goods are physically removed from Canada and that the consideration is received from a non-resident in the course of the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canada Customs Invoice or authorized electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill showing export from Canada and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX receipt evidencing payment received from foreign buyer

Canada and Quebec have no real-time fiscal clearance mandate equivalent to continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping for B2B invoices. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices and require that electronic records be complete, legible, and available on audit. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically once annual taxable supplies exceed C$1 million, using GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec's online business portal. For government procurement and B2G invoicing, Canada has adopted PEPPOL technical standards, but there is no statutorily mandated tax-authority pre-approval API or invoice stamping regime for ordinary bonded warehouse transactions.

CRA and Revenu Québec can disallow zero-rating claims and reassess within four years of the filing date in normal circumstances; the time limit is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default. Where export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, the tax authority will assess the unpaid GST/HST and QST together with statutory daily interest and late penalties. The Excise Tax Act imposes monetary penalties for false statements, omissions, and deliberate or negligent underpayment, and the Quebec Taxation Act contains equivalent QST penalties. In more serious cases, CRA or Revenu Québec may pursue prosecution for tax evasion, and directors can be held liable for the corporation's unremitted GST/HST and QST. Bonded warehouse operators should therefore maintain a complete and time-stamped export compliance file spanning customs declarations, transport documents, payment reconciliation, and FX repatriation records, and should comply strictly with the 180-day repatriation deadline where treaty or regulatory conditions apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control approval or surrender requirement applies to capital injections, dividend remittances, interest, or bond-warehouse trade payments. The only practical constraints are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and certain payments (statutory 25%, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty and comparable treaties), (ii) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for high-value inbound non-Canadian acquisitions, and (iii) FINTRAC reporting for each transfer exceeding CAD 10,000. Profit remittances are never blocked on FX grounds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export receipts. A bonded warehouse operator may retain foreign currency indefinitely in USD/EUR-denominated accounts at Canadian chartered banks. The only mandatory obligations are: (i) FINTRAC filing of International Electronic Funds Transfers (EFTs) over CAD 10,000 in either direction, and (ii) Customs Act export declarations under the CBSA when goods physically leave the bonded depot for foreign destinations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and final settlement authority; exchange-control oversight is split between the Department of Finance Canada (monetary/FX policy), the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA, customs bonded warehouse licensing and duty collection), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC, cross-border wire and cash reporting). No standalone Foreign Exchange Administration exists; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM as the sole treasury rail, SUPPLEMENTARY only in practice. Legal constraints: (i) any non-bank payment provider must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, effective September 2025, as a Retail Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); (ii) CBSA duty and bonded-warehouse tax payments settle exclusively in CAD through the Canadian clearing system, which requires a direct or correspondent clearing relationship with a Tier 1 chartered bank; (iii) offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may support named accounts but cannot provide a Canadian bank-owned Lynx settlement account; and (iv) cross-border EMIs cannot execute CBSA account-debit instructions. Legal constraints: (i) any non-bank payment provider must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, effective September 2025, as a Retail Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); (ii) CBSA duty and bonded-warehouse tax payments settle exclusively in CAD through the Canadian clearing system, which requires a direct or correspondent clearing relationship with a Tier 1 chartered bank; (iii) offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may support named accounts but cannot provide a Canadian bank-owned Lynx settlement account; and (iv) cross-border EMIs cannot execute CBSA account-debit instructions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties for mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation; Master File and Country-by-Country Report required where multinational group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not an Economic Substances Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction, but applies OECD/G20 BEPS substance principles, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), and transfer pricing recharacterization under section 247 of the Income Tax Act; transactions lacking demonstratable commercial substance in bonded warehouse or free trade zone arrangements may be disregarded, recharacterized, or reassessed to reflect arm's-length conditions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act section 239: imprisonment up to 5 years plus fine of 50% to 200% of taxes evaded; civil gross negligence penalty 50% of understatement; Quebec penalties under Tax Administration Act: 25% to 50% of the amount evaded and seizure of property.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who participate in, direct, or acquiesce to fraudulent filings may be jointly and severally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties; travel bans, departure orders, and extradition may be issued under tax collection procedures; personal civil restitution orders can pierce the corporate veil for fraud or intentional misconduct.
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR denying the tax benefit, imposition of 50% gross negligence penalty, denial of treaty benefits, disgorgement of tax credits, revocation of GST/HST registrations and Quebec bonded warehouse license revocation by the Ministère du Revenu.

Director personal liability: Courts may lift the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade; directors face personal tax liability for amounts they directed from the corporation, civil penalties for negligent representation, and injunctions; inability to leave Canada while a tax fraud investigation is pending may result in travel restrictions.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to CAD 2,000,000 for administrative violations; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31: imprisonment up to 14 years; customs contraventions may trigger seizure of goods and cancellation of bonded status.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or fail to prevent trade-based money laundering are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, forfeiture of assets, and Civil Remedies for Laundered Property; travel bans may be imposed under anti-money laundering enforcement, and directors may be barred from serving as officers of any Canadian corporation.
