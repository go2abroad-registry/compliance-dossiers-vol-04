Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the registered seat; federal supervision by the Swiss Commercial Register Authority under the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (OR), Articles 626-670 (formation, organization, and shareholder rights of the stock corporation), Article 628 (minimum capital), Article 631 (statutes), Article 635 (paid-up capital), Article 643 (incorporation declaration), Article 644 (commercial register application).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation incorporating the Articles of Association (Statuten) together with the incorporation report and proof of capital contribution; thereafter file for Commercial Register entry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum aggregate nominal share capital CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up prior to registration. Payment is made into a blocked escrow account in the company's name and released only upon production of Commercial Register registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and building clearance per the cantonal building insurance and fire prevention authority; an occupancy permit from the municipal building inspectorate is required for employee-occupied offices; occupational workplace safety inspection per SUVA accident prevention guidelines; worktime and welfare compliance under ArG.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum aggregate nominal share capital CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 must be fully paid up prior to registration. Payment is made into a blocked escrow account in the company's name and released only upon production of Commercial Register registration certificate.

Category: Not applicable – general commercial activity subject only to registration and tax permit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV/FTA), VAT Division; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; e-services via SuisseTax and www.uid.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (CH_AG) must file VAT registration with ESTV before performing taxable Swiss supplies. If annual turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000, registration is compulsory. For foreign suppliers with no Swiss establishment, a Swiss fiscal representative is required, except for the simplified e-commerce procedure (quick registration). Required documents: certificate of incorporation, statutory seat, business description, expected Swiss turnover, and authorization letter. ESTV assigns the Swiss UID (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) and VAT registration number; processing time 10–25 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity (CH_AG) must file VAT registration with ESTV before performing taxable Swiss supplies. If annual turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000, registration is compulsory. For foreign suppliers with no Swiss establishment, a Swiss fiscal representative is required, except for the simplified e-commerce procedure (quick registration). Required documents: certificate of incorporation, statutory seat, business description, expected Swiss turnover, and authorization letter. ESTV assigns the Swiss UID (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) and VAT registration number; processing time 10–25 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid branch classification elections. A Swiss AG is mandatorily taxed as a separate legal entity; no pass-through status is possible. Available optional regimes include election for simplified VAT net-taxation method (for groups), voluntary VAT registration below the threshold, participation relief election, and group capital tax relief. For hybrid mismatches, Switzerland applies mandatory BEPS/ATAD-compliant rules without elective departure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover sourced in Switzerland (Art. 10/11 MWSTG); for foreign digital service suppliers, the same threshold applies; below it registration is optional but can be elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To apply 0% VAT on exports of goods or offshore services, the AG must prove (1) for goods: physical removal from Switzerland to a foreign destination evidenced by customs declarations and transport documents; (2) for services: the recipient is a foreign business with no Swiss seat, and the service is supplied to that business under B2B place-of-supply rules; specifically, digital/intangible services are Swiss-exempt if the business customer is abroad. Documentation must be in place at the time of goods/service supply.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (LAD/e-dec/ERIKA) with a customs officer stamp or electronic release
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (proving physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer confirmation (SWIFT MT103) showing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and contract with the foreign customer

No mandatory real-time electronic clearing or fiscal stamp. Switzerland uses the Swiss QR-Bill for paper and electronic invoices; invoices for taxable supplies must include VAT details. B2G e-invoicing adopts Peppol/B2G via eBill (since 2020) for federal procurement. All VAT-relevant records must be retained in an audit-proof electronic format for 10 years; no fiscal API clearance is currently imposed.

If the export documentation is incomplete or false, the ESTV retrospectively assesses VAT at the standard rate for the year, plus default interest of 5% p.a. (tax claim period: 10 years). Administrative fines under Art. 69/70 MWSTG amount up to 100% of the evaded VAT; intentional forgery of export evidence triggers criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to CHF 500,000. The AG may also be barred from the simplified export invoice procedure for up to 3 years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland imposes no exchange-control regime; the CHF is freely convertible on both capital and current account with full cross-border capital mobility guaranteed under the FMCA, and no prior authorisation, repatriation, or compulsory conversion requirements apply. Profit remittance is governed solely by corporate solvency rules under the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO Art. 660) and by a 35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends, which is fully refundable to Swiss residents and generally reduced to 0% under the Swiss-EU or relevant tax treaties for qualified foreign recipients; no SNB approval or central-bank-level remittance consent is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law and SNB regulations neither prescribe a statutory surrender timeline nor a mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; an exported SaaS subscription revenue may be retained indefinitely in any currency (CHF, USD, EUR) in domestic or foreign bank accounts, subject only to AMLA documentation, FINMA circular 2023/1 ‘Statutory auditor AML duties’ at the bank level and SNB statistical reporting on cross-border payments for balance-of-payments compilation.

Central bank supervision: Schweizerische Nationalbank (SNB / Swiss National Bank), acting under the National Bank Act of 2003 (NBA), the Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital (FMCA), and the Foreign Exchange and Payment Transactions Ordinance; the SNB is the sole monetary authority, while bank licensing and prudential supervision are conducted by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for ancillary treasury rails, Low for primary banking. FINMA-licensed fintech companies under Art. 1b Banking Act may hold public deposits up to CHF 100 million and offer digital regulated accounts with Swiss IBANs; however, full e-money issuance requires a FINMA banking or fintech licence, and foreign EMIs (e.g., UK or Lithuanian EMI licence holders) are not passportable into Switzerland unless authorisation transfer are impossible, so offshore digital EMIs can only be used for FX conversion or distribution-side collection, not as the main corporate settlement account. The target rails (Stripe Billing, Paddle) should settle directly or via a cascade into a tier-1 Swiss bank account; fintech EMIs may act as merchant-of-record payment sub-accounts subject to FINMA outsourcing rules and bank-substitute risk limits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial CHF 100,000 statutory minimum capital (Art. 621 CO, CHF 50,000 must be paid in immediately) must be wired via SIC (CHF) or SEPA/SWIFT MT103 (EUR/USD) from the shareholders' bank account to the AG’s capital escrow account using SWIFT field 70 purpose code 'Kapitaleinlage / Contribution to share capital' plus reference to the deed of incorporation (Errichtungsurkunde). The bank will require the notarial deed, the subscribed capital statement, and the confirmation by the licensed auditor (for mandatory audits) or the board in the audit-exemption declaration; the funds must be credited to a blocked or earmarked account referenced to the commercial register application, and the capital contribution must be posted separately in the capital contribution reserve (Reserven aus Kapitaleinlagen / KER) ledger to preserve qualification for tax-free repayment under Swiss withholding-tax law. For foreign currency injection converted to CHF, the conversion must be executed at an onshore bank-quoted exchange rate, and the paying bank must complete the customer-due-diligence documentation; no SNB reporting or licence is required for capital import.Operational disbursements for the SaaS (cloud infrastructure, marketing services, affiliate commissions, software licensing, personnel) shall be executed through SIC/SIC Instant for CHF, SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR, or SWIFT MT103 (with mandatory UETR) for USD and other currencies. The recommended clearing rails: (i) corporate online-banking SWIFT MT103 with field 70 business-purpose codes for all non-SEPA payments, (ii) commercial letters of credit (UCP600, usually not required for software services but available for future hardware resale), (iii) escrow-backed milestone settlements for large platform-integration engagements, and (iv) direct-debit/QR-bill for Swiss domestic counterparties. To avoid dual FX conversion, the account must be configured as a multi-currency treasury account allowing native settlement of USD (via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's New York correspondent), EUR (via Target2), and CHF (SIC); SNB regulations do not require export proceeds to be brought onshore, but adequate cash-pooling and 13-26 week cash-flow forecasting must be documented for the bank’s periodic reviews.Deliverable FX Forwards (custom USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, GBP/CHF contracts up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or Swiss Master Agreement; economically covering SaaS subscription revenues and cloud-infrastructure costs)FX Swaps (swap of principal and interest in diff currencies for intercompany liquidity management)Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for long-term hedging of capital injections or acquisition financingFX Options (vanilla European-style options, including collars and zero-cost structures, for asymmetric protection on USD/CHF and EUR/CHF exposure)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to secondary emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) where local convertibility restrictions prevent deliverable hedging; CHF/ EUR / USD NDFs are generally unavailable because CHF is a fully deliverable currency with a deep onshore forward market, and any synthetic products must comply with FINMA market-rules and hold-adequacy requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct profit tax on net income after tax (equivalent to 7.83% taxable-base pre-tax); plus cantonal/communal profit tax, typically 11.5%-21% aggregate ordinary rate depending on corporate seat (e.g., Zug aggregate 11.9%, Geneva aggregate 21%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on cash dividends, bonus shares, hidden profit distributions, and profits remitted by the Aktiengesellschaft to shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return is due within 6 months after fiscal year-end; this period is automatically extended in most cantons to no later than 9 months after year-end for tax-authorised advisors, and at the discretion of the cantonal authority to 12 months. If no return is submitted, the cantonal and federal tax authorities issue an ex officio assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Swiss Local File is expected where aggregate cross-border transactions between the AG and related parties exceed CHF 1,000,000 per fiscal year; a Master File should accompany groups with consolidated revenue above CHF 1 billion and related-party cross-border transactions exceeding CHF 1,000,000. Documentation must be contemporaneous, prepared in German, French, Italian, or English, and filed with the cantonal tax administration upon request.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no offshore-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but the economic-substance doctrine is applied through Swiss case law on actual management, tax treaty anti-abuse rules, and OECD BEPS. A Zug-registered AG that merely holds a no-code website builder brand, earns licence fees, and has all operational functions performed from a foreign affiliate will be treated as an artificial arrangement and denied treaty benefits, participation exemption, and withholding tax relief. Core business functions, strategic decision-making, personnel, premises, and CRM/payment-support activity must be documented in Switzerland to substantiate the commercial seat.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax evasion: fine of up to twice the amount of federal tax evaded, never less than CHF 1,000; legal interest of 3.5% per annum on the back tax. Qualified tax fraud with falsified books carries a custodial sentence of up to 3 years or a monetary penalty under the Direct Federal Tax Act and cantonal direct-tax laws.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for the AG's tax debt, but a director who signs, prepares, or orders a false return may be personally fined and exposed to civil restitution under Art. 754 Code of Obligations for fiscal damage. Criminal prosecution can trigger asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Swiss Criminal Code for fraud or related offences.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as an abusive arrangement: disallowance of intercompany deductions, upward transfer-pricing adjustment, Swiss corporate tax on diverted profits, and withholding tax at 35% on deemed hidden profit distributions under Art. 20 Swiss Withholding Tax Act. Treaty relief is denied, and cantonal procedural penalties apply.

Director personal liability: For an insolvent shell AG, directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they failed to preserve statutory accounts, file insolvency notification, or maintain true share capital. A director who organises an artificial arrangement may face criminal conviction for fraud in tax collection, asset seizure, travel bans, and extradition under mutual legal assistance.
AML and Payment Intermediation Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative enforcement by FINMA: prohibition of business activity, disgorgement of profits, and penalties up to CHF 500,000 for negligent violation of FinIA/FinSA. Knowingly laundering proceeds under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code carries custodial sentence up to 3 years, or up to 5 years for qualified laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to FINMA enforcement as natural persons in serious cases, and criminal liability under Art. 305bis/306 SCG can trigger pre-trial detention, asset freezing, Interpol notices, travel restrictions, and civil liability to merchants and customers.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by tax authorities under the OECD TNMM, CUP, or Profit Split benchmarks; upward profit adjustment; 35% withholding tax on recharacterised hidden distributions; late-payment interest at 3.5% per annum; and procedural penalties for lack of documentation up to CHF 10,000 at the cantonal level.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly omits required documentation or instructs transfer-pricing analysts to fabricate comparables can be prosecuted under Swiss administrative tax penalty law. In insolvency, civil restitution claims survive, and managing directors may face travel bans while administrative proceedings are pending.
