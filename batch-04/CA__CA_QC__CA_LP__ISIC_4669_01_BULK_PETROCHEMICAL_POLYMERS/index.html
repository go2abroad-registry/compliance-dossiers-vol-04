Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; central portal “My Business Account” (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial counterpart for QST: Revenu Québec via clicSÉQUR portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnership obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, through the online non-resident registration service, or by submitting the relevant provincial form to Revenu Québec for QST registration. The LP must apply separately for a GST/HST program account (RT) and a QST account (RM/RQ). No entity-level income tax TIN is required because the LP is generally not a taxpayer for Canadian or Quebec income tax purposes; each partner reports its share separately.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign limited partnership obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, through the online non-resident registration service, or by submitting the relevant provincial form to Revenu Québec for QST registration. The LP must apply separately for a GST/HST program account (RT) and a QST account (RM/RQ). No entity-level income tax TIN is required because the LP is generally not a taxpayer for Canadian or Quebec income tax purposes; each partner reports its share separately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box classification regime. The limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 20.2 apply mandatorily without elective opt-in and there is no analogous election for QST. Available elections include fiscal-period election under section 249.1, the GST/HST Quick Method under section 146.1, and the QST equivalent for qualifying small suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible personal property (virgin polyethylene/polypropylene granules) exported by the supplier, by the recipient, or by an authorized agent as part of a continuous journey to a destination outside Canada, with possession effectively transferred outside Canada. The supplier must retain documentary proof adequate under section 169 and s. 146 of the Excise Tax Act. Export-related services supplied to non-residents, such as transport and logistics performed in Canada in respect of exported goods, may also be zero-rated under sections 144 and 145 ETA.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or approved EDI/CARM cargo control documentation
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Multimodal Transport Bill / Air Waybill
  • Packing List
  • Sales Contract or Purchase Order referencing export terms
  • Proof of Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance (e.g., SWIFT MT103, letter of credit payment confirmation)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is permitted but not mandatory; registrants must issue invoices containing the supplier's BN, recipient, date, description, and applicable tax amounts. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically for most registrants; Revenu Québec mandates electronic QST return filing for large businesses and accepts structured data exchange.

CRA and Revenu Québec conduct high-risk audits of zero-rating claims, especially for export bulk commodities. Failure to substantiate export movement may result in reassessment of the full taxable supply at the combined 14.975% rate, recapture of claimed input tax credits, and interest charges. Gross negligence or false documentation attracts civil penalties under ETA s. 281.1 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax misstated, plus compound interest. Revenu Québec may similarly impose penalties and reassess within the normal 3-year limitation period; fraud is not time-limited. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; the CAD floats freely and Canada is in compliance with IMF Article VIII. A Quebec limited partnership may receive foreign capital, fund imports of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules, and repatriate profit to non-resident partners without exchange-control approval. The operational constraints are fiscal and regulatory only: non-resident partners pay Part I tax on their allocated share of Canadian-source business income (via T5013 filings), distributions of already-taxed income carry no further Part XIII withholding, and all wire transfers above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting and Canadian sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; wholesale exporters may retain, invest, or disburse foreign-currency receipts at their discretion. For ISIC_4669 bulk polymer trading, the exporter should nonetheless maintain a per-shipment documentary file (sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, LC/MT103, and customs entry) to substantiate pricing and destination and to withstand trade-based AML review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and payment-system supervisor; no centralized FX-rationing authority exists because Canada maintains full capital mobility. Regulatory oversight of treasury operations is distributed across OSFI (federally regulated banks), FINTRAC (cross-border transaction reporting and AML), the Bank of Canada (Retail Payment Activities Act supervision), and the Autorité des marchés financiers du Québec (provincial money-services licensing).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — viable only as a supplementary payments channel, not as the primary treasury or trade-finance anchor. Foreign digital EMIs and non-resident fintech providers must register with FINTRAC as money services businesses and, when providing payment functions to Canadian users, obtain retail payment activity registration from the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. EMIs are structurally unable to issue or settle UCP600 irrevocable documentary credits for B2B polymer trade, so the operational account and LC execution capacity must reside with a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) to the bank's Montreal branch and settled in CAD or USD through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system. Field 70 remittance information must state the purpose: 'Capital contribution — Quebec LP CA_LP — ISIC_4669 petrochemical polymer wholesale'. The receiving bank matches the credit against the LP contribution schedule and the escrow-opening resolution before releasing funds to the operational account; every transfer at or above CAD 10,000 triggers FINTRAC reportable-transaction and sanctions screening. Supporting verification comprises the partnership agreement contribution schedule, the bank statement from the remitting institution, and a source-of-funds declaration from each limited partner.Supplier disbursements are executed across three rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cash-against-documents and spot-FX settled imports; (2) UCP600 irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank (MT700) and advised or confirmed by the supplier's bank, with payment released against compliant presentation of invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and inspection certificate; and (3) Payments Canada Lynx/ACH for any CAD domestic settlement legs. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions and CAD 500,000 for services transactions with non-arm's length non-resident parties (per CRA guidance). Master File required if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty shopping provisions require genuine economic substance. Partnerships must demonstrate real business activity, office, and personnel to claim treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes (s. 227.1) and for negligence causing corporate tax debt. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under the Criminal Code.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) may deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the benefit. Treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in Canada's treaties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they authorized the arrangement (s. 160). Civil restitution and injunctions may be issued.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, imprisonment for individuals, and suspension of business registration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful non-compliance. Travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if gross adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million), and charge interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the mispricing. Civil liability for restitution of taxes and interest.
