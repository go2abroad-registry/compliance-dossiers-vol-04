Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Québec limited partnership; partners freely determine contributions in the written partnership agreement, provided each limited partner's contribution is in money or property, not services (art. 2255 C.c.Q.). No escrow account is mandated by law; however, funds for the trade-finance rails (revolving letters of credit, SWIFT TT) should be held in a Schedule I Canadian bank account in the partnership's name, and a Canadian custodian bank account is standard practice to evidence the capital subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification under the Quebec Construction Code and the National Fire Code of Canada, issued by the local Service de sécurité incendie, including automatic sprinkler acceptance, cold-room emergency egress, and hazardous refrigerant detection; Structural load inspection by a licensed engineer for the pallet racking system and freezer mezzanine; Occupational health and safety clearance from CNESST under the Act respecting occupational health and safety, including cold-environment work protection plans; municipal certificate of occupancy (certificat d'occupation) for the warehouse unit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec. In Quebec, Revenu Québec administers both the federal GST (under a single administration agreement) and the provincial QST. Corporate income tax filings for the LP are made to both CRA and Revenu Québec. Portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html and https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service, including a GST/HST program account (RT) and possibly an import/export program account (RM). In parallel, register with Revenu Québec to obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and a QST account (account identifier derived from the NEQ). Non-residents must appoint a Canadian representative and supply a domestic address for service. After registration, the LP receives separate BN/NEQ credentials and must file mandatory registrations with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec if carrying on commercial activity in Québec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not permit a full US-style check-the-box election. The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; no election is available to treat it as a corporation. Relevant statutory elections include: (a) subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election for non-resident partners to compute Canadian-source income tax on a net basis; (b) subsection 97(2) rollover election on transfers of capital property to the partnership; (c) GST/HST elections under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act (closely related persons) to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration, where applicable. Hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.4 and 18.5 ITA) may apply to deductible payments involving partnerships with differing tax characterizations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST and QST registration; non-residents importing goods into Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold to obtain input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including frozen beef and seafood) are zero-rated for GST/HST and QST under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and corresponding Quebec legislation. The exporter must hold the goods continuously in Canada and export them by way of sale to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable period (normally 60 days). Commercial invoices, customs declarations, and written proof of exportation must be retained. Services supplied to a non-resident (including storage, logistics, and brokerage relating to export sales) qualify for zero-rating only where they are performed wholly or mainly outside Canada or are directly connected to the non-resident's export business.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently implemented in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoicing is permitted and legally equivalent to paper invoices if it contains required GST/HST/QST invoice information. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic records and filings via their portals; however, a comprehensive pan-Canadian e-invoicing mandate with API-based clearance is not yet in force.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit export invoices and customs records. If zero-rating is applied fraudulently or without proper documentation, penalties apply under the Excise Tax Act (sections 280.1, 285) and Quebec taxation laws. Gross negligence penalties may reach 25% of the tax claimed, and clawbacks of input tax credits (ITCs) can be made for four years (or longer for misrepresentation). Criminal prosecution can lead to fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 18 months. Participation in the Voluntary Disclosure Program may reduce penalties. Export documentation must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely; there are no exchange controls, counterparty restrictions, or central bank approvals for capital injections, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Repatriation of profits is not FX-restricted. The only material statutory hurdles are federal and provincial income tax filing obligations, Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on specified payments to non-residents (e.g. interest, rent, royalties, management fees), and the Investment Canada Act notification or review regime for foreign investments that acquire control of a Canadian business. For a Quebec limited partnership, current-account trade flows and capital-account injections can be settled freely in CAD, USD, or other foreign currency through a Schedule I bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or waiting-period rule for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. Administrative reporting obligations apply only for anti-money-laundering purposes: financial institutions must report electronic funds transfers and large-cash/transaction records of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and any person importing or exporting monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must report to the Canada Border Services Agency. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no separate foreign exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary receivables/payables and foreign-currency disbursement, Low as the primary treasury or escrow rail. Canada has no EU-style EMI licence; offshore digital EMIs operating in Canada must register as money services businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC for foreign-exchange, remittance, and money-transfer activities, and may be subject to the future Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime. They may not accept deposits in Canada, as deposit-taking is reserved for chartered banks under the Bank Act. Offshore EMIs can only provide virtual currency accounts or pass-through settlement if they maintain a correspondent or virtual-IBAN arrangement with a Canadian Schedule I bank. Major Canadian banks often treat wires originating from an unregistered EMI as higher risk, and may reject or freeze funds if travel-rule information is incomplete.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from parent or foreign partners must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the Canadian beneficiary bank. Canada does not use IBAN; the beneficiary must be identified using the Canadian financial institution number, branch transit number, and account number, together with the limited partnership legal name and account currency. Remittance information must state the purpose clearly, e.g. 'LP capital contribution'. The receiving Canadian bank will verify the sending bank's SWIFT/BIC, the originator and beneficiary legal entity names, and compliance with FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting above CAD 10,000. The funds may be converted to CAD or retained in USD under standing instructions. No Bank of Canada approval is required for the inflow. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for gross related-party transactions in goods; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for other transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires substantial business purpose for treaty benefits. Partnerships must demonstrate real commercial activity, adequate staff, and premises in Canada to avoid treaty shopping challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties under Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset seizure possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reassessment of income; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; court orders for restitution; travel bans if non-compliance persists
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply; civil liability for damages; travel bans and asset freezes
