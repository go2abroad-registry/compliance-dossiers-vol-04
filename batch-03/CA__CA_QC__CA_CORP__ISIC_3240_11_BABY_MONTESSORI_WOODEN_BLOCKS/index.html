Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal GST/HST and income tax, via Business Registration Online and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html); Revenu Quebec (RQ) for QST and Quebec corporate income tax, via CliquezAffaires / Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for import/export accounts and customs reporting.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident business corporation must: (1) incorporate federally or provincially and obtain articles of incorporation and a certificate of status; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 submitted to CRA Business Registration Online, appointing a Canadian agent or representative for service of process; (3) activate the GST/HST account (BN suffix RT0001) and the Import/Export account (suffix RM0001) with CBSA; (4) register with Revenu Quebec for the QST number (same BN with suffix TQ0001) and obtain the Quebec Corporation Registration Certificate under the Quebec Business Corporations Act; (5) if establishing a physical presence, register for Quebec payroll deductions (suffixes RP/QP), CNESST, and QPIP. The BN is issued immediately online for eligible applicants; foreign incorporation documents must be legalized or apostilled and translated into English or French, and a Canadian registered address is mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada subject to federal Part I tax (15% general rate after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction) plus Quebec corporate income tax (11.5% general rate; 5% small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income if CCPC status and associated-corporation rules are met). No pass-through classification is available; profits are taxed at the entity level and dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under applicable income tax treaties.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (a) ITA s. 216 election permitting non-resident landlords of Canadian real property to file net-basis Part I returns instead of the 25% gross Part XIII withholding; (b) ITA s. 219 branch tax election allowing a non-resident corporation to pay 15% branch tax (treaty-reduced) on Canadian business profits; (c) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property at elected values in corporate reorganizations; (d) ITA s. 93(1) election to deem a dividend from a foreign affiliate so as to reduce foreign accrual property income exposure; (e) hybrid mismatch arrangements are governed by mandatory anti-hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.4, 20.2, and 212.3 enacted under Budget 2022) with no elective opt-out; affected taxpayers must file prescribed disclosure forms such as Form T1134.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1) and QSTA s. 41). Zero-rated export supplies count as commercial activity for threshold purposes, but the first CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies is exempt from GST/QST unless a voluntary registration election (Form GST22 or Revenu Quebec VD-335) is filed. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers must register for the simplified GST and QST regime regardless of the threshold under ETA s. 246.1.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 5-6 when the supplier exports the goods, transfers ownership to a recipient outside Canada, and retains documentary proof of export within 60 days of shipment (delivery to a common carrier, customs export declaration, foreign destination evidence). Services: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 when supplied to a non-resident and the service is not performed in respect of real property located in Canada or tangible personal property that remains in Canada after the service is performed; professional advisory and digital services qualify if consumed abroad. QST zero-rating mirrors the GST rules under the QSTA Schedule to s. 1. A Montessori wooden toy manufacturer exporting finished goods may claim full input tax credits (ITCs) for all input GST/QST, yielding a refundable export credit typically paid by CRA or Revenu Quebec within 30-45 days of filing. Failure to meet export substantiation recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 14.975% with no credit.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping exists in Canada. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (PEPPOL) is voluntary; CRA and Revenu Quebec accept PEPPOL BIS 3.0 and UBL 2.3 formats. Mandatory electronic reporting nonetheless applies: GST/HST returns must be filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; Quebec returns via Revenu Quebec Mon dossier (EDT service). For exports, CBSA mandates electronic filing of the B13A export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the CARM portal when the FOB value exceeds CAD 2,000, with a 15-day post-shipment filing deadline. Licensed customs brokers must integrate with the CARM API (JSON/XML); direct exporters use the CBSA web portal. All fiscal data must be retained for six years in a CRA-accepted electronic format.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within four years of the supply (seven years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; unlimited in cases of fraud) and claw back claimed ITCs and zero-rating benefits. Penalties include: (a) a fine of 25% of the net tax for false statements or omissions (ETA s. 285.1; QSTA s. 991); (b) failure-to-file penalties of CAD 100 per month per return plus daily compounded interest; (c) a gross negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be avoided; (d) retrospective clawback of the full 14.975% GST plus QST from the date of the original supply if the export, payment, and FX repatriation cannot be traced to the same transactional trail; (e) criminal prosecution for evasion under ETA s. 327, QSTA s. 1014, or ITA s. 239, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and unlimited fines; (f) joint and several liability of marketplace intermediaries under ETA s. 296; and (g) application of the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) to disregard export-structuring arrangements lacking genuine economic substance, with an additional 25% GAAR penalty.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a free-floating and fully convertible currency for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, royalties, interest, or capital gains to non-residents, and no requirement to obtain exchange-control approval for cross-border transfers. Profit remittances by a Quebec business corporation are executable at the prevailing interbank rate but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (normally 25%, reducible by tax treaty) and must be reported on CRA Form NR4. In Quebec specifically, dividend payments to non-resident shareholders must also comply with the Quebec Revenue Agency's reporting rules, but there is no currency conversion hurdle or mandatory holding period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold foreign-currency receipts in Canadian-chartered bank accounts or convert them freely. The only reporting obligation is the FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer report (required when an EFT of CAD/foreign-currency equivalent to CAD $10,000 or more enters or leaves Canada) and the standard CRA record-keeping requirement to prove the underlying export transaction with invoices, customs entries, bills of lading, and proof of settlement. There is no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, whether expressed in US dollars or other foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking prudential supervision; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control administration and no centralised capital-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium viability. Canadian law does not recognise an 'Electronic Money Institution' as a separately licensed banking category. Offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, and Wise can operate as money services businesses (MSBs) or foreign money service providers, but they are not deposit-taking institutions in Canada and must be registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For this low-risk e-commerce exporter, a licensed EMI is acceptable as a supplemental collection and FX-conversion rail, especially for USD marketplaces like Amazon and Stripe, but core treasury liquidity, trade finance, and bank-grade custody should remain at a Schedule I domestic bank to preserve uninsured-deposit protection, avoid settlement delays, and satisfy trade-creditor due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections and shareholder loans must be executed via SWIFT MT103 with full originator and beneficiary identification, including legal entity name, registration number (Quebec enterprise number / federal incorporation number), and account number. The wire narrative must include the IMF BPM6 purpose concession (Direct Investment in a Reporting Enterprise – Equity or Direct Investment – Intercompany Lending) or the bank's standard capital purpose code; 'capital injection' alone is insufficient. Before release, the receiving bank generally requires the executed share subscription or loan agreement, a board resolution, the updated REQ/corporate registry extract confirming share capital, beneficial ownership certification, and, where the amount exceeds $250,000, a pre-clearance request to the bank's financial intelligence unit. FINTRAC is automatically notified of every cross-border EFT equivalent to $10,000 CAD or more; no federal treasury or Bank of Canada prior approval exists. For Quebec business corporations, the solvency test and consideration requirements under the Quebec Business Corporations Act should be satisfied before booking the share capital increase. Funds are typically cleared through the bank's own ledger using Lynx (for CAD) or Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent lines (for USD), and a formal deposit or reserve account is not required by law.Outbound payments for beechwood logs, non-toxic paints, and manufacturing equipment will be executed through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct high-value supplier payments with a full invoice narrative, Incoterm, purchase order number, and customs entry reference; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (sight or usance) issued through the bank's trade finance desk for large recurring shipments, with the LC routed via SWIFT MT700 and settled against presentation of bill of lading, packing list, weight certificate, phytosanitary certificate for wood, and commercial invoice; (iii) domestic CAD supplier disbursements through the Lynx high-value system or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for recurring small-to-medium vendor payments; and (iv) Canadian-dollar fast transfers via Interac RTP where available for urgent sub-CAD $250,000 payments. For USD-denominated trade payables, the bank will issue an MT103 from a USD operational sweep or use a spot FX trade to fund the USD clearing account. All outbound trade disbursements must be recorded against CBSA import entry numbers and reconciled to the underlying inventory ledger; the Incoterm must be explicitly stated to avoid customs valuation disputes.Plain-vanilla OTC FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) for committed future export sales and known import payables, executed under an ISDA or Canadian FX master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted-clearing currencies such as USD/CNY, used solely when onshore deliverable forwards are unavailable and closed as part of a documented physical trade flow.Vanilla FX swaps (short-dated buy/sell and sell/buy structures) to manage temporary working capital mismatches between Amazon settlement dates and supplier payment due dates.Cross-currency swaps (floating-for-fixed and fixed-for-fixed) to hedge long-term USD revenue surplus or USD-denominated debt into CAD, provided the swap is supported by an identifiable export receivable stream.Vanilla FX options and zero-premium costless collars on CAD/USD to cap currency downside without upfront premium, subject to the clearing bank's derivatives credit approval and the corporate board's approved hedging mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low-to-Medium viability. Canadian law does not recognise an 'Electronic Money Institution' as a separately licensed banking category. Offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, and Wise can operate as money services businesses (MSBs) or foreign money service providers, but they are not deposit-taking institutions in Canada and must be registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. For this low-risk e-commerce exporter, a licensed EMI is acceptable as a supplemental collection and FX-conversion rail, especially for USD marketplaces like Amazon and Stripe, but core treasury liquidity, trade finance, and bank-grade custody should remain at a Schedule I domestic bank to preserve uninsured-deposit protection, avoid settlement delays, and satisfy trade-creditor due diligence.
