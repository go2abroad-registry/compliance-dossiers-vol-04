Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is not recognized as the statutory registered office of a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. The company must maintain a real office address in Switzerland, normally in the canton of incorporation, where board records, accounting files, commercial correspondence and statutory registers are accessible. A professional domiciliation service provider may provide the registered address, provided it possesses material infrastructure, does not merely receive mail, and reports physical persons subject to register to the competent authorities. Use of 'virtual-only' post boxes materially increases the risk of refusing registration in Zug and may trigger a formal commercial substance assessment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Schweizerische Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions / Swiss Federal Tax Administration FTA-ESTV); VAT and withholding tax portal: www.estv.admin.ch; online business e-Services for VAT via Swiss Federal Tax Administration portal and Swiss Commercial Register (Zefix).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarially incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) and register it in the Cantonal Commercial Register of the registered office, obtaining the Swiss company identification number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID). Step 2: The UID, allocated by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office and shown on the commercial register extract, serves as the statutory business tax identification number for all federal tax purposes. Step 3: Register through the FTA online e-Service for VAT if annual worldwide taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; foreign non-resident entities must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative and file a VAT registration application to obtain the VAT-specific number (CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST). Step 4: Maintain bank account, corporate registrations and records in Swiss francs or with audited forex basis, and file through the secured FTA portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not maintain a check-the-box pass-through election for corporate entities; the AG is mandatorily subject to entity-level federal and cantonal/communal corporate income tax, with no fiscal unity or group consolidation for federal income tax purposes. Available elections affecting tax profile include: (i) voluntary Swiss VAT registration for businesses below the obligatory threshold; (ii) election to waive VAT exemption for small enterprises; (iii) option to tax real property under Article 22 of the Swiss VAT Act; and (iv) scope rules under Swiss hybrid mismatch legislation implementing BEPS Action 2, which disallow certain mismatched deductions without any elective check-the-box mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (excluding Swiss VAT) for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is available below this threshold and, for non-resident persons, Swiss VAT registration may be required for taxable transactions in Switzerland even without a Swiss establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 23(1) of the Swiss VAT Act, provided the physical export is duly evidenced by a Swiss customs export declaration. For the bulk marine trade of granular elemental sulfur classified under ISIC 4669/NAICS 423520, zero-rating requires actual removal of goods from Swiss customs territory, commercial delivery to a non-Swiss consignee under documentary transport evidence, and retained proof that title, possession and risk passed to the foreign buyer. Directly related freight, insurance and brokerage services supplied to a foreign principal may also be zero-rated as international services where the place-of-supply rules place them outside Switzerland. The taxpayer must obtain and archive full export substantiation prior to issuing zero-rated invoices.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (e-dec / Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with movement reference number, customs office release and electronic export confirmation
  • Commercial export invoice containing supplier and consignee identification, ISIC 4669 goods description, bulk quantity, weight, Incoterms, price and express zero-rating statement
  • Original through bill of lading or multimodal transport document evidencing loading of the granular sulfur and destination outside Switzerland
  • Bank inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice, or equivalent FX receipt evidence showing payment received within the mandatory 180-day repatriation period

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime for VAT invoices. There is no mandatory API-based pre-clearance of invoices with the FTA. Invoices must satisfy Swiss VAT invoice-content requirements (Article 26 of the Swiss VAT Act) and, in practice, must include supplier/recipient identification, Swiss UID/VAT number, invoice date, description of goods, quantity, consideration and VAT rate. The Swiss QR-bill is the standard machine-readable payment instrument for Swiss invoices; eBill/Peppol formats are accepted where the recipient agrees, but none constitutes a tax-authority clearance mechanism.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict audit scrutiny by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration. If the exporter cannot produce the mandatory customs, transport and FX repatriation evidence, the FTA will retrospectively clawback the zero rate and assess Swiss VAT at 8.1% on the transaction proceeds, together with default interest and input tax adjustments. Intentional tax evasion triggers a penalty under Article 97(2) of the Swiss VAT Act of up to three times the evaded tax; negligent non-compliance attracts a penalty of up to one-third of the evaded tax. Falsifying customs declarations is separately prosecuted under the Swiss Customs Act, carrying criminal fines of up to three times the evaded duties and potential imprisonment, with a ten-year limitation period for fraud-related reclaims.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax levied on after-tax income (effective 7.83% on pre-tax profit); combined federal, cantonal and communal burden in the Canton of Zug approximately 14.6%, rising to 19.0%–21.0% in other cantons.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends and hidden profit distributions made by the Swiss AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the fiscal year-end (Art. 125 DBG); for a calendar-year taxpayer the deadline is 30 June; the cantonal authority grants an automatic extension to 31 August and may grant further discretionary extensions, but default interest accrues after the expiry of the legal deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Ziffer 1 – Umsatzerloes (Gross trading turnover) Total gross revenue from the sale of sulfur mineral ore, freight income, brokerage commissions and incidental commercial income before deduction of expenses. Report only revenue attributable to the Swiss permanent establishment (PE) or the Swiss AG itself; foreign sales routed directly through the foreign head office must be excluded and documented with a transfer pricing file. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 2 – Geschaeftsaufwand (Operating expenses) Arm's-length cost of goods sold, ocean freight, charter hire, stevedoring, insurance, brokerage fees, administrative overhead and financing charges. Non-resident traders must reduce the claim to expenses exclusively incurred for the Swiss PE; intercompany management fees and cost contributions must be substantiated with a contemporary TP study. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 3 – Reingewinn (Net profit per commercial accounts) Net profit before tax as per the audited statutory financial statements of the AG, including depreciation, provisions and revaluations. The commercial net profit must be reconciled with the Swiss statutory accounts; non-resident agreements must be adjusted for any hidden profit distributions. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 4 – Aufrechnungen (Non-deductible expenses and hidden profit distributions) Add-back of tax-deductible free licence fees, excessive royalties, artificial rebates to foreign shareholders, and any expenditure lacking economic substance. Any payment to a non-resident affiliate that exceeds arm's length is recharacterized as a hidden dividend distribution and subjects the Swiss company to 35% withholding tax liability. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 5 – Verlustvortrag (Loss carryforward utilization) Offset of earlier-year assessed losses against the current taxable profit; the loss pool may be carried forward for 7 years and must be applied in chronological order. Non-resident shareholders converting a Swiss branch into an AG cannot transfer foreign losses to the Swiss entity; loss carryforward is only permitted for the Swiss taxpayer itself. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 6 – Beteiligungsabzug (Participation exemption) Deduction of 100% of qualifying dividend income and capital gains from equity participations of at least 10% or a market value exceeding CHF 1 million. The participation must be owned by the Swiss AG and not effectively attributed to a foreign permanent establishment; a schedule of all subsidiary holdings and bank statements must be annexed. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 7 – Patentbox-Abzug (IP income relief) Reduction of the taxable base of qualifying royalty/licence income from Swiss or foreign-registered patents by up to 90% (overall cap of 90% of IP-relevant income). Trading companies without R&D nexus cannot claim the box; license-in models must pass the nexus ratio calculation documented with patent ownership chains. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.
Ziffer 8 – Steuerbarer Reingewinn (Taxable net profit) Final taxable profit figure after commercial-book reconciliation, deductions, participation and patent box relief; the basis for federal and cantonal tax assessment. The final figure must be signed by the board; for non-resident controlled AGs, the tax office attaches a declaration of beneficial ownership and substance facts. Late filing triggers reminder fees (CHF 100–300 per reminder), default interest of 4.5% p.a. on the resulting assessment, and procedural fines (Ordnungsbusse) up to CHF 10,000; a missing return enables the tax authority to issue a discretionary assessment with a surcharge of up to 30%; fraudulent under-declaration is penalized with a fine equal to the evaded federal tax, doubled in serious cases, plus confiscatory interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold exists under Swiss law; the Federal Tax Administration applies OECD TP Guidelines and requires contemporaneous documentation on request. In practice, groups with consolidated Swiss turnover above CHF 50 million or individual intercompany transactions exceeding CHF 2 million must prepare a TP study; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not administer legacy ESR regimes, but the general anti-abuse doctrine applies: Art. 18 of the swiss Code of Obligations, the 'substance over form' principle, the OECD BEPS anti-treaty-shopping rules, and the 2020 corporate tax reform introduce mandatory substance requirements (office, staff, management, accounting, decision-making) for all Swiss AGs; a dormant or letter-box company will be denied treaty benefits, participation exemption and withholding tax refunds, and may be liquidated or re-allocated to the place of effective management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Art. 175–177 DBG; Art. 56 StHG)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 100% of the federal/cantonal tax shortfall, doubled to 200% in aggravated cases; default interest at 4.5% p.a.; discretionary assessment with a 30% surcharge; under Art. 186 of the Tax Penal Act (VStrR), tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to CHF 50,000 in addition to the tax recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories may be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes, social security contributions and VAT, and can face criminal prosecution personally; travel bans, passport confiscation and asset freezing are routinely imposed; strict liability is imposed for false statutory accounts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (Art. 18 OR; 2020 Tax Reform Anti-Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Denial of participation exemption, patent box and DTA treaty relief; recharacterization of profits as taxable in the foreign effective-management jurisdiction; retroactive tax assessments and a penalty surcharge of up to 20%; removal of the company from the Commercial Register and forced liquidation under the Federal Act on Commercial Register Fees.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid social security and tax debts under Art. 52 AHVG and Art. 12 VStrG; imprisonment up to 2 years for assisting in tax evasion; disqualification from board positions in Switzerland and potential extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Withholding Tax Default and Hidden Profit Distributions (Art. 17, 39 VStG)

Statutory sanction: The AG is strictly liable for the 35% withheld tax plus default interest; wilful failure to collect or remit constitutes a criminal offence under Art. 39 VStG, punishable by a fine of up to CHF 20,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; substantial unpaid WHT may trigger seizure of assets and a blocking order on bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are joint debtors for the WHT under Art. 17 VStG and may be personally prosecuted for non-remittance; the Swiss tax authorities issue personal penalty orders and, for large sums, pursue criminal complaints for mismanagement under Art. 165 StGB.
AML and International Sanctions Violations (GwG; Art. 305bis StGB; SECO/FINMA Regime)

Statutory sanction: Violation of due-diligence obligations under Art. 9–13 GwG triggers administrative fines up to CHF 500,000 per case; engaging in predicate money laundering under Art. 305bis/305ter StGB is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and a criminal fine; SECO may freeze funds and delist the company from export licences; tax deductibility of the entire transaction is forfeited.

Director personal liability: Individual criminal liability for directors, money-laundering reporting officers and beneficial owners, with imprisonment up to 5 years in professionally aggravated cases (Art. 305bis StGB); personal travel bans, Schengen alert issuance, book seizure and confiscation of profits are imposed; direct accessorial liability for failing to implement a valid AML compliance programme.
