Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), GST/HST and Income Tax Directorate; taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account and Represent a Client (Canada.ca). Ontario Ministry of Finance handles provincial OST matters, but HST is collected by CRA under the Federal GST/HST umbrella. Federal registration is via the CRA Business Registration Online service.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership (LP), the initial step is to file or obtain an Ontario Business Information Number under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario) if arm’s-length comfort is required, then register for the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a Program Account suffix (e.g., RT0001 for GST/HST) through CRA Business Registration Online or by completing Form RC348. The registration must be performed by the general partner or a designated resident representative authorized under the partnership agreement. CRA will accept a non-resident general partner as registrant, but non-resident partners may be required to provide a Canadian representative under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for service of documents and compliance. The tax identifier for GST/HST purposes is the BN/RT program account. Ontario LP statutory filings (Form LP-1) are made with ServiceOntario/ the Ministry of Government and Consumer Services, but CRA does not condition GST/HST registration on provincial LP registration for non-residents; in practice, the LP should complete both steps to obtain a valid Canadian legal persona.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian federal income tax, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership files an information return (T5013 Summary) but is not liable for income tax; each partner, whether resident or non-resident, is taxed on its allocated share of income, gain, loss, and recapture. For GST/HST, however, the Excise Tax Act deems the partnership to be a separate person for purposes of registration and net tax remittance, while the general partner normally acts as the representative for GST/HST liabilities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian federal income tax, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership files an information return (T5013 Summary) but is not liable for income tax; each partner, whether resident or non-resident, is taxed on its allocated share of income, gain, loss, and recapture. For GST/HST, however, the Excise Tax Act deems the partnership to be a separate person for purposes of registration and net tax remittance, while the general partner normally acts as the representative for GST/HST liabilities. A foreign LP may therefore be eligible for pass-through income treatment while being a distinct GST/HST registrant, subject to provincial partnership registration and Ontario HST rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election for entity classification. A Canadian limited partnership cannot elect to be treated as a corporation for Income Tax Act purposes. Relevant elections and statutory mechanisms available include: (i) designation under Excise Tax Act section 273 of a representative member or agent responsible for GST/HST registration and compliance; (ii) an election by the partnership to have a fiscal period other than a calendar year where the parties meet the conditions under section 249.1 of the Income Tax Act; (iii) for non-resident partners receiving Canadian-source partnership income, an ITA section 216 election to file a Part I tax return on net taxable rental or property income instead of Part XIII non-resident withholding; and (iv) statutory hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5 that operate mandatorily and cannot be overridden by a unilateral election. No Canadian election permits a hybrid partnership to be recharacterized as an opaque entity for federal tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies? No, zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies and count toward the small supplier threshold). A foreign LP exporting scrap copper wire must register once its worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, unless the LP is already required to register because it is carrying on a business in Canada. Because the LP will regularly export, mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered at or before the first commercial shipment if the volume implies annual worldwide taxable supplies above CAD 30,000. Registration is required to claim input tax credits on Canadian deductible operating costs and to issue zero-rated export invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of tangible personal property, including shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire, by an Ontario LP to a non-resident purchaser is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the property as part of the supply. Specifically, the scrap must be delivered to and accepted by the carrier for immediate export from Canada, and the supplier must have evidence satisfactory to the Canada Revenue Agency that the goods were actually exported. The zero-rating applies only to goods supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada and who is not acquiring the goods for consumption, use, or supply in Canada. The LP must retain documentation showing that the benefits of ownership are transferred to the non-resident buyer outside Canada and that the goods did not remain in Canada for Canadian use. Merely issuing an invoice marked 'zero-rated export' is insufficient; the exporter must prove exportation through transport documents and customs reports. If the goods are sold to a Canadian intermediary and later exported by that intermediary, the supply to the intermediary is generally subject to HST and may be relieved only under the delayed export rules if the exporter obtains a signed attestation from the intermediary and evidence of export within a reasonable time. For the LP acting as principal, the invoice must reference the applicable Schedule VI zero-rating provision and the export must occur before CRA considers the supply to have been made in Canada.

  • Canada Export Declaration (B13A) or CAED electronic export declaration filed with CBSA, including HS classification for copper scrap (HS 7404), weight, value, exporter BN, and country of destination
  • Commercial Invoice prepared by the Ontario LP identifying the non-resident purchaser, terms of sale (Incoterms), description of shredded copper wire, quantity, unit weight, and value in CAD and foreign currency
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck) or multimodal transport document evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination and signed by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation (SWIFT MT103) showing the export proceeds received in Canadian or permitted foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Packing List and weight list with lot numbers for the shredded copper wire
  • Independent assay certificate or composition statement confirming copper content, impurity levels, and recovery rate for customs valuation and ISIC 4669 scrap classification
  • Copy of the foreign customs import declaration or entry document if available to substantiate the physical arrival of the goods outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital tax stamping for GST/HST invoices. CRA requires registered entities to issue invoices that contain prescribed information: supplier name and BN, purchaser name, date, description of goods, GST/HST amount or an indication that the supply is zero-rated, and invoice serial number. Electronic invoicing is permitted and accepted without prior CRA approval. For export transactions, customs clearance is mandatory through the Canadian Single Window / CBSA Portal, using the Canada Export Reporting System (CAED) for shipments above CAD 2,000, and all exports must be reported electronically or via approved service provider before conveyance. Although no CRA stamping API applies, a GS1 or EDI interface may be used with Canadian customs brokers for the export of scrap copper wire; failure to submit accurate electronic export data may invalidate zero-rating documentation.

If the Canada Revenue Agency determines that a supply was not exported within the statutory time frame, the zero-rating is denied and the full 13% HST (Ontario) becomes payable, together with interest compounded daily under the Excise Tax Act and penalties under section 280 for making false statements or omissions. A knowing or willful misrepresentation may attract a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided, and criminal prosecution is possible under section 327.1 of the Excise Tax Act and section 380 of the Criminal Code. CRA auditors will require the complete shred-trail: purchase invoices from Canadian scrap suppliers, GST/HST treatment on upstream supplies, CBAM and anti-fraud flags, Canadian Customs export report, bill of lading, and proof of remittance from abroad. The absence of an Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 within 180 days of shipment will be treated as a red flag that the sale was fictitious, could cause the zero-rated export claim to be clawed back with arrears interest, and may expose the LP, its general partner, and non-resident partners to joint and several liability for unpaid HST and penalties. Under CRA’s normal reassessment period, the export claim may be reassessed for four years (or longer in case of misrepresentation), and no refund of input tax credits will be available if the corresponding export zero-rating is disallowed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible currency under the Currency Act. No prior exchange-control approvals apply to current-account or capital-account flows. An Ontario Limited Partnership may receive capital injections from non-resident partners without Bank of Canada or Investment Canada approval except the general reviewable thresholds for acquisitions of control. Profit and distribution remittances to non-resident partners are unrestricted, but distributions are subject to 25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on the taxable portion unless reduced by treaty. CRS and FATCA self-certifications are mandatory for account opening. Accounts may be maintained in CAD and major foreign currencies; no surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no mandated repatriation window. Export receipts for recycled copper wire may be retained in any freely convertible currency. The documentary regime requires: CBSA export declarations (B13A) for shipments valued above the prescribed threshold, Statistics Canada reporting of goods exports, accurate HS classification of copper scrap (likely 7404.00), ISRI grade specification on invoices, and full traceability from the underlying contract through wire transfer settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), responsible for monetary policy and the national Lynx high-value payment system; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions; Global Affairs Canada and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administer export controls and cross-border trade documentation; the Minister of Finance holds the Exchange Fund Account for official reserves; Export Development Canada (EDC) is the crown export credit agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability as a supplement only. Foreign digital-only EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury) are not authorized banks under the Bank Act and may not take Canadian-dollar deposits or issue CDIC-insured current accounts. They may operate as registered payment service providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC MSB registration, but they cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot act as issuing/advising bank for trade LCs, and lack bulletproof correspondent clearing for high-value scrap copper settlements. They may operate as registered payment service providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC MSB registration, but they cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot act as issuing/advising bank for trade LCs, and lack bulletproof correspondent clearing for high-value scrap copper settlements. Recommended role: collateral zero-balance collection account for small receivables; never the primary treasury rail.
