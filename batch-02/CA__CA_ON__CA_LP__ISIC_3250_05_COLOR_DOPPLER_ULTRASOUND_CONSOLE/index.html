Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) Portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by completing Form RC1. A limited partnership is generally treated as a pass-through entity and must obtain a partnership GST/HST account; the partnership itself is not a taxable entity for income tax purposes, but it must file an information return (T5013). Non-resident partners may require a Canadian tax identification number for withholding and reporting purposes. Ontario imposes a provincial portion of HST administered federally by the CRA.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by completing Form RC1. A limited partnership is generally treated as a pass-through entity and must obtain a partnership GST/HST account; the partnership itself is not a taxable entity for income tax purposes, but it must file an information return (T5013). Non-resident partners may require a Canadian tax identification number for withholding and reporting purposes. Ontario imposes a provincial portion of HST administered federally by the CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian tax law provides no check-the-box election for limited partnerships; fiscal transparency is mandatory. Limited partnerships may elect to combine supplies for GST/HST registration purposes under Section 156 of the Excise Tax Act, and non-resident partners may rely on treaty-based withholding reductions. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act apply to cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold); a non-resident engaged in commercial activity in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Section 42 of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods in accordance with prescribed documentary requirements and the goods are delivered outside Canada. Color Doppler ultrasound consoles exported to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating as exported goods. Services provided to non-residents may also be zero-rated if the supply does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (G13) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other evidence of foreign currency receipt
  • CBSA export confirmation or carrier certificate of export
  • Proof of delivery outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping regime in Canada; CRA requires specified invoice fields and supporting records for GST/HST purposes. Electronic invoicing and records are accepted if complete and legible. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing adoption is optional and not legally mandated.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST, interest, and penalties if export substantiation is insufficient. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include fines up to 50% of the net tax for gross negligence, and criminal penalties for knowingly evading tax. The normal reassessment period is four years, extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control licence, repatriation approval, or central-bank notification is required for inbound capital contributions, outbound trade payments, or profit remittances. A non-resident limited partner's Canadian-source income is taxed directly to the partner through the Ontario LP's T5013 allocation; the LP withholds Part XIII tax at 25% (treaty-reduced) only on prescribed payments such as interest, royalties, rents, management fees, or other amounts characterized as taxable distributions, while capital distributions and loan repayments move freely. Profit-remittance planning therefore centres on withholding-tax compliance, transfer-pricing substance, and Form NR4/T5013 reporting rather than FX convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in the early 1950s; no timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in CAD or foreign currency. The exporter may retain proceeds in domestic or foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I bank. Remaining legal obligations are FINTRAC reporting (CAD 10,000 threshold for large cash transactions and electronic funds transfers), CBSA export declarations via the Canadian Export Reporting System, T1135 specified foreign property reporting, economic-sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, and standard corporate records retention rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) – central monetary authority operating Canada's flexible/floating exchange-rate regime without exchange-control powers; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) – financial intelligence unit for cross-border electronic funds transfer and large-transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) – prudential regulator of Canadian Schedule I and Schedule II banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability, narrowly bounded. Bank of Canada-registered payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), plus FINTRAC-registered money services businesses, may support low-value operational disbursements, FX conversion, and vendor settlements. They cannot operate the capital-escrow account, issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, clear through the Lynx high-value payment system, or hold CDIC-insured deposits. EMIs are therefore restricted to operational vendor payments and ancillary FX; the primary liquidity and clearing corridor must remain a Schedule I chartered bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Complete Partnership Register and executed LP Agreement; declaration of Canadian and non-resident partner status with CRA NR4/T5013 profiles; KYC dossiers for every partner holding 10% or greater interest; source-of-wealth consolidation covering 6 to 12 months of bank statements; corporate charters and certificates of good standing from each partner's home jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital calls are funded exclusively by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers from each limited partner's own bank account to the LP's designated capital-escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Wire narratives must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [ENTITY NAME] – [CAPITAL CALL NO.]' with a clear receipt timeline. The bank applies FINTRAC large-transaction and EFT reporting at CAD 10,000, screens the sending bank, beneficiary, and all intermediaries against sanctions lists, and requests FATCA/CRS status declarations per partner corridor. Funds tendered by unnamed third-party payers are rejected absent a signed assignment of the capital-account rights.Trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers (with MT202 COV cover where an intermediary is involved) for supplier payments, and UCP-600 or eUCP-governed documentary letters of credit for buyer-financed export transactions. Large-value CAD legs clear through Lynx, Canada's ISO 20022 high-value payment system; USD legs settle via Fedwire or CHIPS through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; other currencies use the SWIFT correspondent banking network.
