Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online and My Business Account portals, for the federal Business Number and GST/HST administration; Revenu Québec, including the clicSÉQUR-Entreprises portal, for Québec Sales Tax (QST), Québec employer and source-deduction accounts, and related Québec tax registrations. Commercial importers and exporters also interact with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM system.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated federal or Québec corporation generally obtains a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, incorporation-related registration, telephone registration, or an authorized representative. The corporation then opens the applicable CRA program accounts, including RT for GST/HST, RM for import-export, RP for payroll, and RC for corporate income tax where required by CRA procedures. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, importing commercial goods, or otherwise required to register must apply for a BN and the relevant program accounts through CRA procedures; it should maintain a Canadian service address or authorized representative where required and provide incorporation documents, legal name, jurisdiction, directors, ownership information, business activities, effective dates, and authorized-contact details. A corporation carrying on business in Québec must register for a Québec identification number and applicable QST account with Revenu Québec. A GST/HST registrant making taxable supplies in Québec generally administers GST federally and QST separately with Revenu Québec. Commercial importers must obtain or activate an RM import-export account in CARM and satisfy CBSA importer-account requirements. Registration does not itself establish corporate income-tax residence; residence and permanent-establishment analysis remain separate questions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian corporation is ordinarily a separate taxpayer and has no United States-style check-the-box election. It may elect specific statutory regimes where legally eligible, including Canadian-controlled private corporation treatment based on ownership and control facts, associated-corporation rules, the small-business deduction regime, and certain status elections under the Income Tax Act. A corporation may also use permitted amalgamation, wind-up, rollover, or reorganization provisions, subject to statutory conditions. Partnerships and certain trusts may be fiscally transparent, but a corporation cannot generally elect to be treated as a partnership or pass-through entity for Canadian income-tax purposes. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for specified mismatch arrangements; they do not create a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, calculated under the applicable small-supplier rules, is the principal GST/HST registration threshold for most businesses. The threshold generally considers worldwide taxable supplies of the supplier and associated persons, subject to statutory exclusions and special rules. A person may register voluntarily below the threshold. Québec generally applies a CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST purposes, with separate rules for suppliers, specified Québec suppliers, non-resident suppliers, and taxable supplies made in Québec. Registration can also be required without relying solely on the threshold where the business is carrying on business in Canada or Québec, importing taxable commercial goods, or falls within a special registration regime. A non-resident e-commerce supplier should separately test ordinary GST/QST registration, the simplified GST/HST regime, and Québec specified-supplier rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST purposes, qualifying exports of tangible goods are generally zero-rated where the goods are supplied for export and the statutory delivery, possession, shipping, or export conditions are satisfied. Evidence should demonstrate that the goods left Canada or were delivered to a non-resident in circumstances prescribed by the Excise Tax Act and its regulations. Goods shipped directly from Canada to a foreign customer normally require commercial shipping and customs evidence linking the sale, goods, purchaser, carrier, and export. Goods supplied to a non-resident but delivered in Canada do not automatically qualify. Services may be zero-rated where they are supplied to a non-resident and satisfy the specific exported-service rules; many services connected with real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services performed in Canada are excluded. QST treatment must be tested separately; qualifying exports of movable property from Québec and qualifying services supplied outside Québec may be zero-rated or outside the QST base under Québec legislation, but the precise statutory conditions and documentary evidence differ. Input tax credits and input-tax refunds require valid tax documentation, proof of commercial use, and reconciliation to returns.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the Canadian supplier, foreign purchaser, goods description, quantity, value, currency, terms of sale, and destination
  • CBSA export declaration or applicable electronic export-reporting record, including the applicable reporting exemption analysis where no declaration is required
  • Canada Customs Invoice or equivalent commercial documentation where required by CBSA or the carrier
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, postal record, or other carrier document showing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Packing list and shipment records linking the exported goods to the customer invoice and production or inventory records
  • Proof of delivery or foreign import/customs record where available
  • Purchase order, sales contract, payment record, and customer correspondence establishing the foreign transaction
  • Foreign purchaser and destination evidence, including non-resident status where relevant
  • For zero-rated services, contract, statement of work, deliverables, location-of-performance evidence, customer residency evidence, and records proving satisfaction of the applicable exported-service rule
  • GST/QST return workpapers reconciling zero-rated revenue, export documentation, collected tax, input tax credits, and input tax refunds

Canada and Québec do not generally impose a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction-control mandate for ordinary private-sector sales. Invoices and electronic records must contain information sufficient to support GST/HST and QST collection, input-tax-credit or input-tax-refund claims, tax calculation, and audit verification; required records must be retained for the statutory period, generally six years from the end of the relevant year unless a longer period or authorization applies. Electronic invoicing is generally permitted if records remain reliable, readable, accessible, and reproducible. CRA electronic filing and payment requirements apply to specified registrants and circumstances, and Revenu Québec has separate electronic-filing obligations for designated taxpayers. Commercial import declarations and accounting are filed through CBSA CARM; customs data may be transmitted electronically, but ordinary keyboard-keycap sales are not subject to a general fiscal e-invoice pre-clearance requirement.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated to Canada within 180 days or another fixed period. Export proceeds may remain abroad, subject to ordinary income-tax, transfer-pricing, foreign-exchange reporting, anti-money-laundering, customs, and corporate-record requirements. During an audit, CRA and Revenu Québec may deny zero-rating where export, non-resident, destination, delivery, or service-location evidence is insufficient and assess GST or QST, applicable interest, and consequential reassessments of net tax, input tax credits, or refunds. False statements or omissions may attract statutory penalties, including gross-negligence penalties where the legal test is met; deliberate fraud or tax evasion may result in prosecution and fines or imprisonment. Improperly obtained refunds can be reassessed and recovered with interest. Customs non-compliance may trigger CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System sanctions, reassessment of duties and taxes, seizure or detention in appropriate cases, and other enforcement measures. Documentary retention, transaction-level reconciliation, and prompt correction of unsupported zero-rated claims are essential.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Capital controls are not imposed; non-residents may freely invest, operate, repatriate capital, and remit dividends, interest, royalties, and loan repayments without prior regulatory approval, subject only to corporate solvency tests, tax withholding rules, and FINTRAC reporting obligations. There are no currency licensing, approval, or quota requirements applicable to a Quebec business corporation in the keycap manufacturing sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign-currency earnings in domestic or offshore bank accounts and may convert to Canadian dollars at their commercial discretion. For statistical balance-of-payments purposes, exporters must report annual export values to Statistics Canada through the Canadian International Merchandise Trade returns; however, this is purely informational and does not impose surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); regulatory coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a dedicated exchange-control administration or foreign-exchange licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary collection rail, Low as the primary treasury hub. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Mercury, or Payoneer can provide multi-currency account numbers and low-cost card operations, but they are not chartered Canadian banks, do not offer CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot provide standard Canadian clearing codes for direct deposits or pre-authorized debits. Legally, an EMI directing services into Canada is required to register as a money service business with FINTRAC; a Quebec corporate client must still independently comply with FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds and cannot rely on the offshore EMI to perform its Canadian AML obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose code for equity capital formation or investment. Beneficiary name and account number must exactly match the Canadian bank records; reference fields should include the corporate name, share certificate number, and a clear statement of 'equity injection for working capital'. For wires at or above CAD 10,000, the receiving Canadian bank will file an Incoming International Electronic Funds Transfer report with FINTRAC. To clear the funds without delay, provide the bank with a board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares, a complete shareholder register, source-of-wealth evidence, and a use-of-funds breakdown showing mould tooling, resin purchases, and operational expenditures.Supplier payments should use SWIFT MT103 for disbursements below USD 50,000, with the appropriate goods-trade purpose code, beneficiary BIC, account number, and invoice references. For larger or recurring resin and tooling purchases, commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C at sight) are the preferred clearing rail through the Canadian issuing bank to the supplier's confirming bank. For North American and European suppliers, USD ACH and SEPA rails can be used when a correspondent account allows it; all other corridors must clear via SWIFT correspondent banking. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary collection rail, Low as the primary treasury hub. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Mercury, or Payoneer can provide multi-currency account numbers and low-cost card operations, but they are not chartered Canadian banks, do not offer CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot provide standard Canadian clearing codes for direct deposits or pre-authorized debits. Legally, an EMI directing services into Canada is required to register as a money service business with FINTRAC; a Quebec corporate client must still independently comply with FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds and cannot rely on the offshore EMI to perform its Canadian AML obligations.
