E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada follows legal-form classification; no US-style check-the-box election exists. The LP is fiscally transparent for income tax (Income Tax Act s. 96): the partnership files T5013 information returns and partners are taxed directly on allocated income. For GST/HST, the partnership is a 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and may make a joint election with all members under ETA s. 177 for the partnership to be the sole registrant in respect of partnership activities, with members jointly and severally liable. Additional elections include closely related corporation elections (ETA s. 156) and annual ITC allocation elections. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.5, effective July 1, 2022) operate automatically and non-electively to disallow deductions arising from mismatches between Canadian pass-through treatment and a foreign partner jurisdiction's separate-entity treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports and offshore services) of the partnership measured over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration once exceeded; voluntary registration available below the threshold to recover input tax credits on export-related costs

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (supply of goods for export): the partnership must supply goods by way of sale, export the goods or cause them to be exported as part of the supply contract, and delivery must occur outside Canada within a reasonable period (CRA administrative practice: typically within 60 days of sale). Documentary proof of export must be retained for six years. International freight, chartering, and brokerage services supplied to non-residents and directly related to the exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI s. 7 where supplied to a non-resident in respect of tangible personal property situated outside Canada at the time of performance. Input tax credits on export-related inputs are fully recoverable through periodic returns; net credit balances are refunded by CRA. Canada does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects the letter-of-credit settlement cycle and CRA's reasonableness review of export proceeds.

  • Commercial invoice showing HS code 2503 (granular elemental sulfur), INCOTERM, quantity, unit price, and non-resident buyer details

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API validation, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime in Canada. CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, and XML invoices for substantiation. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) is voluntary for B2B and adopted for B2G procurement; certain Ontario public-sector contracts may require Peppol-compliant e-invoicing, but no statutory mandate applies to private dry bulk commodity exports.

CRA may reassess the partnership within the normal four-year period (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or neglect) and reverse zero-rating where export documentation is insufficient, assessing 13% HST on gross proceeds plus arrears interest and daily compound interest. ETA s. 280 penalties apply for false statements or omissions: the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the tax sought to be evaded (10% for a repeat infraction). Wilful evasion under ETA s. 281 is an indictable offence punishable by fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties (AMPS) of up to CAD 25,000 per erroneous or false export declaration may be imposed. Input tax credits improperly claimed on export-related inputs are clawed back with interest, and failure to preserve export evidence for six years bars any refund claim.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime with no exchange control legislation, no capital or current account restrictions under the Currency Act or Bank of Canada regulations, and no central-bank approval requirement for profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest payments, or capital repatriation. A Canadian limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada), and non-resident limited partners are generally not subject to Canadian withholding tax on distributions if the distribution does not constitute a section 212.2 amount; however, Part XIII withholding applies to deemed Canadian-source payments such as interest, royalties, and management fees. Profit remittance may be executed freely in CAD or any convertible foreign currency through Canadian Schedule I banks without Form FX-1 or similar approval; only standard FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more. In practice the LP must maintain clean tax-residency documentation for each limited partner to disengage any partner-level withholding-origin concern under Ontario partnership law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation of export receipts. Elemental sulfur and sulfur mineral ore trade proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, converted into Canadian dollars at any time, or held in offshore accounts. Exporters are not required to deposit export proceeds with a designated Canadian bank. However, FINTRAC reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) require that Canadian banks report international and domestic electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the LP must retain all export sale records, bills of lading, and settlement documentation for the statutory six-year tax record-keeping period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); statutory foreign-exchange policy administration is coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act; AML/ATF enforcement for financial intermediaries is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); prudential bank supervision is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to material regulatory constraints. Foreign digital EMI and payment-service-provider platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, Revolut Business) are not Schedule I banks and cannot issue commercial letters of credit or participate directly in Lynx or CHIPS settlement; they operate as money services businesses (MSBs) using Canadian sponsor bank rails. Any such provider handling Canadian payments must be registered with FINTRAC as an MSB and must have Canadian-resident AML compliance representation. The LP may use an offshore EMI for forex conversion and corridor payments, but the core treasury architecture must retain at least one Canadian Schedule I bank account to access trade finance issuance, LC confirmation, and Lynx settlement finality. For a sulphur-mineral-ore trading LP, a pure-EMI structure is treated as low-to-medium in bank counterparty credit, and cannot fully substitute for a regulated bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners may inject capital using SWIFT MT103 wires to the Canadian bank's correspondent clearing account, with field 70 remittance information referencing 'LP CONTRIBUTION' and the Ontario LP registration number. Canada does not assign mandatory statutory purpose codes, but the receiving bank will require a purpose narrative in field 72 or in ISO 20022 pain.001 remittance data for FINTRAC record-keeping; for CAD settlements, funds clear through Lynx (Payments Canada high-value payment system) on a final, irrevocable basis; for USD settlements, clearing occurs through CHIPS or the bank's internal New York clearing account. For amounts exceeding CAD 100,000, privileged source-of-funds documents such as the limited partnership agreement, capital call notices, and bank statements of the remitting limited partner must be supplied; the bank will apply its FX rate or a pre-agreed forward contract to convert the contribution into operating currency. Inbound wires should avoid cryptocurrency intermediaries, which would trigger FINTRAC MSB licensing and materially delay settlement.Trade settlements for granular elemental sulfur and sulfur mineral ore cargoes should be executed via: (1) commercial letters of credit under SWIFT MT700 issued by the Canadian bank at the LP's instruction, with payment against presentation of conforming documents under UCP600, and confirmed by a first-class bank at the discharge port; (2) SWIFT MT103 straight transfers for freight, demurrage, port charges, and minor supplier disbursements, with the remittance field prefixed 'SULFUR CARGO PAYMENT' and the relevant bill-of-lading or contract reference; (3) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) as a lower-cost fallback for established counterparties. Canadian-dollar disbursements should be concentrated over Lynx for intraday finality; USD disbursements over CHIPS using the Canadian bank's USD clearing account.
