Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Articles 620–763 (Stock Corporation Law), as revised by the Federal Act on Company Law Revision (effective 1 January 2023); Federal Act on the Commercial Register (HRA) and Commercial Register Ordinance (HRV), Articles 15 et seqq.; OR Article 633 (minimum paid-up capital) and OR Article 627 (registered office and purpose in Articles of Association).

Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000. The escrowed funds are released only after production of the definitive Commercial Register entry certificate; non-cash (contribution in kind) rules under OR Article 634 apply if assets are contributed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC); online portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch and ePortal tax filing platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not domiciled in Switzerland must apply to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) for a Swiss VAT number and UID using the online ePortal or prescribed registration form. The applicant must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative resident in Switzerland, and submit certified corporate constitutive documents, a power of attorney, proof of foreign tax residence, evidence of projected Swiss taxable activity, and a statement of anticipated global turnover. The FTA then issues the UID and separate VAT registration number (CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST). For an Aktiengesellschaft establishing a permanent establishment, additional cantonal tax registration is required with the cantonal tax administration where the PE is situated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not domiciled in Switzerland must apply to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) for a Swiss VAT number and UID using the online ePortal or prescribed registration form. The applicant must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative resident in Switzerland, and submit certified corporate constitutive documents, a power of attorney, proof of foreign tax residence, evidence of projected Swiss taxable activity, and a statement of anticipated global turnover. The FTA then issues the UID and separate VAT registration number (CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST). For an Aktiengesellschaft establishing a permanent establishment, additional cantonal tax registration is required with the cantonal tax administration where the PE is situated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election exists in Switzerland. A Swiss AG is mandatorily an entity-level corporate taxpayer. The only relevant elective mechanism is Swiss VAT group taxation under Art. 11 of the Swiss VAT Act, which allows related Swiss entities to elect consolidated VAT filing. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are counteracted by mandatory Swiss alignment rules and do not permit elective entity reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover in Switzerland and abroad; foreign suppliers whose full taxable supply exceeds this threshold must register and appoint a Swiss fiscal representative. Turnover below CHF 100,000 remains exempt from VAT unless an election to waive exemption is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Switzerland by the project cargo supplier or through a commissioned logistics operator, provided Swiss Customs receives a valid export declaration and disposition, and the goods leave Swiss territory. Offshore services and freight forwarding/logistics services directly related to exported goods are also zero-rated where the recipient's place of business is abroad. The exporter must hold adequate documentary proof of export, transport, and foreign payment for the underlying contract.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec Export / NCTS transaction code)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent CMR / AWB / multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation of foreign invoice settlement
  • Certificate of Origin if contractual or statutory condition for foreign clearance
  • Proof of Export Attestation (e.g., Ausfuhrnachweis) where required by customs status

Switzerland does not impose a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance API. E-invoicing is based on private standards such as the Swiss QR-bill and eBill, with Peppol-style interchange permitted, and B2G e-invoicing is required for certain federal procurement. VAT returns are filed electronically through the FTA ePortal; no digital stamping or government pre-clearance of individual invoices is currently mandated.

The FTA and Swiss Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If substantiation is incomplete or false, the 0% rate is stripped and 8.1% VAT is reassessed retroactively, plus default interest at 4.0% per annum. Violations may attract fines of up to CHF 30,000 under Art. 97 of the Swiss VAT Act for tax shortfall, procedural penalties up to CHF 10,000, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent customs declarations. The Swiss fiscal representative is jointly and severally liable for the VAT and penalties. The general statutory limitation for reassessment is 10 years in cases of intentional concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible. Switzerland maintains no exchange controls on current or capital account; Article 27 of the Federal Constitution (economic freedom) and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) guarantee free cross-border settlement without prior SNB authorization. Profit remittance: dividends paid by CH_AG are not subject to FX approval or repatriation limits, but a 35% Swiss anticipatory (withholding) tax applies and may be reduced or refunded under an applicable double-taxation treaty. De facto friction arises solely from bank-level AML/UBO verification, never from convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory repatriation timeline. CH_AG may freely retain invoiced receivables from its OOG heavy-haul project cargo services (ISIC 5229) in CHF, USD, EUR, GBP or any agreed currency, in domestic or foreign banks, without reporting or surrender obligations beyond general statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB - Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse), operating under the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank of 3 October 2003 (SNB Act), with coordinated supervision by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for anti-money-laundering, banking conduct and financial-market-infrastructure matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary payment rail, LOW as the sole treasury provider. Legal constraints: EU/UK EMI licences do not passport into Switzerland, so a Swiss-domiciled AG must maintain its primary treasury at an entity licensed under the Swiss Banking Act (Federal Act on Banks and Savings Banks). FINMA-licensed Swiss FinTech companies authorized under Article 1b of the Banking Act (public-deposit cap of CHF 100 million, no interest-bearing accounts) may execute payment and FX services but cannot provide the credit, documentary-letter-of-credit and cash-pooling depth required for project-cargo milestones. Recommended architecture: a Tier-1 Swiss bank for clearing, custody and LC issuance, supplemented by a multi-currency EMI (e.g., Wise Business or equivalent) for low-value subcontractor disbursements in USD/EUR.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders remit the CHF 100,000 minimum capital from their own individually verified account via SWIFT MT103 (non-SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) or 'COMA' (Commercial) and a payment reference stating 'Fonds propres / Participationskapital - CH_AG'. The receiving bank requires the founding report (Gründungsbericht), the notarised deed of incorporation, a Form A AML declaration, and the statutory auditor confirmation of full payment (Vollzugsbestätigung). The remitting account name must match the shareholder and UBO name on the escrow; third-party funding requires a notarised declaration and will be treated as debt unless re-characterised. Deadline set by cantonal law, commonly within six months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year companies this generally falls between 30 June and 31 August of the following assessment year.

For a Swiss AG: mandatory full entry. For a non-resident corporate taxpayer with a Swiss permanent establishment (PE): only the profit attributable to the Swiss PE is entered; non-Swiss source income is excluded. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no strict statutory Local/Master File turnover threshold; however, group entities with consolidated revenue above CHF 900 million are subject to Country-by-Country Reporting notification and filing duties. Swiss tax authorities may request transfer pricing documentation on a case-by-case basis; administrative practice expects adequate Local/Master File documentation for intercompany transactions exceeding roughly CHF 5 million per category (raw materials, services, intangibles, financing).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply formal offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the Swiss tax administration enforces substance through general anti-abuse rules, Art. 44a DBG, beneficial-ownership requirements for DTA/WHT relief, and OECD BEPS minimum standards. For an AG to obtain treaty benefits and WHT reimbursement, it must maintain actual management in Switzerland, adequate personnel, business premises, and real economic activity; letterbox companies are denied treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Filing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine under Art. 186 DBG of up to three times the evaded tax; additional cantonal/communal tax surcharges; default interest. In severe cases, the tax authority may use criminal information in enforcement proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers can incur personal fines and imprisonment up to three years under Swiss tax criminal law and Criminal Code Art. 14/15; civil piercing of the corporate veil is possible for unpaid corporate taxes, and travel bans or exit restrictions may be imposed in asset-enforcement proceedings.
Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: FTC Act (VStG) Art. 61 makes the payer company liable for the un-withheld tax; penalty fines up to CHF 10,000 and default interest are applied.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable under Art. 178 VStG and CO Art. 756 for corporate withholding-tax debt if the company's assets have been distributed or insufficient funds remain; personal civil restitution and criminal liability for unjust enrichment follow.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment adjustment to arm's-length income, additional assessment interest, and potentially penalty under Art. 174 DBG for inaccurate records/documentation; refusal of treaty credit for adjusted income.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve distorted intercompany pricing may face civil claims for corporate tax losses, criminal liability for tax evasion if the pricing is used to conceal income, and personal liability for the part of group taxes due; in egregious cases they may be banned from managing companies in Switzerland.
Shell Company / AML Abuse

Statutory sanction: AMLA Art. 37 imposes fines up to CHF 500,000 for wilful violation; licence revocation and registration exclusion; confiscation of assets under Criminal Code Art. 70. Violation of US/OFAC or EU sanctions linked to the cargo route may also create parallel penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face money-laundering liability under Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis (imprisonment up to five years) and Art. 305ter (financial negligence); civil restitution, freezing orders, travel bans, and statutory disqualification from corporate directorships are common measures.
