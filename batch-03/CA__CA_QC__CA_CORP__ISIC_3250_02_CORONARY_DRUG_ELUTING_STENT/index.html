Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; primary portals: CRA 'My Business Account' and Revenu Québec 'Cliquez Affaires'

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, electing to register for GST/HST; simultaneously register with Revenu Québec for QST (Form LM-1 or Cliquez Affaires), payroll deduction accounts, and any Quebec-specific taxes. Non-resident corporations may need to appoint a deemed resident agent or attach Form RC1645 to evidence Canadian place of management; all registrations can be completed electronically.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-controlled private corporation / Quebec business corporation subject to both federal and provincial income tax; territorial scope limited to Canadian-source income and dividends from taxable Canadian corporations; not a pass-through) — Foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, electing to register for GST/HST; simultaneously register with Revenu Québec for QST (Form LM-1 or Cliquez Affaires), payroll deduction accounts, and any Quebec-specific taxes. Non-resident corporations may need to appoint a deemed resident agent or attach Form RC1645 to evidence Canadian place of management; all registrations can be completed electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-controlled private corporation / Quebec business corporation subject to both federal and provincial income tax; territorial scope limited to Canadian-source income and dividends from taxable Canadian corporations; not a pass-through)

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Relevant elections: section 216(1) income tax election for non-residents receiving rental/royalty income; section 219(1) election for non-resident corporations making certain investments; anti-hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5 with parallel Quebec Taxation Act provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies; QST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Quebec (small supplier thresholds). Non-resident vendors making supplies of digital services or services rendered in Quebec may be required to register regardless of physical presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including Class III medical devices such as coronary drug-eluting stents) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier sells or ships goods to a non-resident for direct export, or when a registered customer exports the goods. Off-shore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated if performed outside Canada or incidental to the export; medical devices may also be zero-rated on domestic sale if a qualifying listing under Health Canada, but export zero-rating requires actual removal from Canada and standard documentary proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration / CAED) or CBSA Export Report
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (ocean waybill or master airway bill) with CBSA stamp or transport record
  • Freight forwarder certificate or forwarder's warehouse/delivery records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corporate bank statement proving export proceeds received in Canada

CRA may reassess GST/HST and QST within 3-4 years of the return, extended to 12 years for misrepresentation/negligence. Gross negligence penalties up to 25% of the tax amount, failure-to-collect penalties, interest, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Criminal Code for fraudulently claiming export zero-rating; Revenu Québec has a 4-year normal reassessment period (extended for fraud) with parallel penalties, including fines under the Taxation Act and Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Capital and current account transactions are generally free from exchange-control restrictions. Profit remittances may be made via dividends, management fees, royalties, or equity repatriation without central-bank approval, subject to corporate solvency requirements, Quebec/Canada tax compliance, Part XIII withholding tax, and transfer-pricing rules under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Exporters are not required to convert foreign-currency proceeds or meet any repatriation deadline. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency; however, transactions above C$10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting and financial institutions must maintain records for AML/CTF purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no standalone foreign-exchange control administration exists; prudential regulation of banks is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs). Canada does not employ the EU EMI licensing model; cross-border fintech entrants require either federal bank licensing under the Bank Act, an authorized foreign bank branch, or registration as a money services business with FINTRAC plus compliance with Quebec retail-payment regulations. Offshore EMIs cannot independently clear CAD or access Lynx/Payments Canada without a Canadian clearing partner, though some FINTRAC-registered fintechs may offer virtual IBAN-like services through a tier-1 sponsor bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is legally required for controlled transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1 million per transaction or in aggregate; mandatory Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated gross revenue above CAD 1 billion

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation exists in Canada; however, economic substance and genuine commercial purpose are subject to Canada’s General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), place of central management and control determinations, and Quebec permanent establishment rules. Transactions require real operational activity, employees, and decision-making in Quebec to avoid treaty-shopping or de facto permanent establishment exposure

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Federal and Quebec Income Tax

Statutory sanction: Federal: fine from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to two years per count (Income Tax Act s. 239); Quebec: parallel penal fines under Taxation Act s. 1050 and civil gross negligence penalties at 50% of omitted tax

Director personal liability: Directors who participate or acquiesce may become personally liable for the corporation’s tax withholdings under ITA s. 227.1; criminal conviction triggers derivative civil liability, director disqualification, and potential travel prohibition while warrants are outstanding
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment of taxable income plus interest; penalty under ITA s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net TP adjustment exceeding the de minimis threshold (typically the greater of CAD 100,000 or 10% of gross adjustments)

Director personal liability: Although TP penalties attach primarily to the corporation, directors and CFOs who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation may face civil fraud claims, contribution liability in shareholder suits, and CRA gross negligence allegations; foreign authorities may pursue evidence extraction and asset tracing against directors personally
Anti-Money Laundering and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act up to CAD 500,000 per violation; criminal charges for laundering proceeds under Criminal Code s. 462.31 with fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be criminally prosecuted for knowingly assisting or failing to prevent laundering; personal travel bans, asset forfeiture proceedings, and court-ordered restitution are available; Quebec’s securities and financial-market regulator can suspend permits and order director removal
Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization disallowing the treaty benefit, punitive interest, and tax reassessment; under the Quebec Act to fight money laundering, false statements corporate filings can draw fines and forced dissolution; CBCA criminal remedies for false returns may reach CAD 25,000 and manager disqualification for federally incorporated entities

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil when a company is an instrument of fraud; directors exposing false ownership information face criminal proceedings under Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud) and s. 340 (public accounts); personal restitution, director bans, travel restrictions, and imprisonment are applicable in serious cases
