Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity engaging in commercial activity in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) by submitting CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically, by fax, or by mail. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier and is used for GST/HST registration by adding an RT program identifier. Non-resident corporations must register for GST/HST if they make taxable supplies in Canada and are not small suppliers; voluntary registration is permitted before taxable supplies commence. The CRA assigns the BN and confirms the registration through written notice; no in-person appearance or local registered office is required for tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. A Canadian business corporation is generally subject to entity-level federal income tax under the Income Tax Act, with no pass-through election available. GST/HST-specific elections include voluntary registration under ETA subsection 240(1.1), the election for closely related corporations under ETA subsection 273.1(3), and the simplified method election for computing input tax credits. Hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.4 and section 20.1 of the Income Tax Act apply to disallow certain elective outcomes for hybrid entities and transparent instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies when a person's taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, determined on a trailing four-calendar-quarter basis. Non-residents that carry on business in Canada are subject to the same small supplier threshold, provided their Canadian taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 30,000; however, a non-resident that makes taxable supplies in Canada but does not carry on a business in Canada is not a small supplier and must register. For Ontario, the Harmonized Sales Tax is collected at 13% (5% federal GST plus 8% Ontario provincial component).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) if the supplier ships the property directly out of Canada, or delivers it to a common carrier for export, within the timelines prescribed by ETA Section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI. Certain services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated, including information, consulting, and software-related services if the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, corporeal moveable property located in Canada, or services performed directly on such property. The supplier must retain documentary evidence proving exportation and establishing the goods were exported within 12 months of the supply, or the supply will be subject to HST at the rate of 13% in Ontario.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) transaction report, mandatory for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or regulated/controlled exports
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill showing consignee outside Canada and origin in Canada
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order describing the goods, quantity, and sale terms
  • Packing list and shipping manifest
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank wire confirmation showing receipt of foreign currency or settlement from the overseas buyer
  • Proof of payment and clearing records from the payment service provider (e.g., Stripe, Shopify Payments)
  • Freight forwarder or carrier certificate confirming international carriage and delivery outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping of invoices. Electronic invoicing is permissible if records are retained in an accessible and readable format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE portal if the entity has a taxable turnover exceeding applicable thresholds; paper filing is allowed for some small suppliers. No CRA API-based clearance mechanism applies at the time of invoice issuance. The HST collected is reported on the GST/HST return, and electronic remittances use CRA's online banking or payment gateway services.

CRA and CBSA may jointly audit export transactions to verify zero-rating eligibility. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims can trigger reassessment of the supply at the full Ontario HST rate of 13%, together with interest and penalties. A gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated may apply under ETA subsection 285(2). Criminal prosecution is possible for making a false statement or omission; upon conviction, an individual may be liable to a fine of up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years, while a corporation may face fines up to CAD 100,000. Retrospective clawbacks may cover the standard four-year reassessment period, extended to ten years where there is misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. Documentary proof must be retained for at least six years from the end of the relevant reporting period to substantiate export zero-rating and GST/HST refunds or rebates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full external convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange control laws restricting the conversion of the Canadian dollar for either current account transactions (trade, services, royalties) or capital account transactions (equity injection, dividend remittance, loan repayment). Profit repatriation to any jurisdiction is freely permitted, subject to applicable Canadian withholding taxes on dividends (generally 25% statutory, reduced by treaty), arm's-length transfer-pricing rules, and applicable anti-money-laundering reporting obligations. No pre-approval or central bank license is required for remittances of dividend or capital amounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not require surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Any export receipt may be retained in freely convertible foreign currency or converted at will. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio (0% mandatory conversion). Companies must nevertheless maintain accurate books and records, report large cross-border electronic transfers at or above CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, and file annual foreign-reporting returns where applicable (T1135 for specified foreign property).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, and similar treasury-tech facilitators) may be used for payment facilitation and settlement in Canada, but they must operate as or partner with a money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC. They are not recognized as banks for deposit-taking and cannot provide CDIC-insured accounts. Cross-border fund routing through an unregistered offshore EMI may trigger compliance friction with Canadian banks and FINTRAC reporting. A registered and licensed arrangement is viable for low-risk operational flows, but the primary clearing and treasury relationship should remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a non-resident shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 using the correspondent banking network to the Canadian corporate operational account held at a Schedule I chartered bank. Documentation must include the board resolution authorizing share issuance under the OBCA, updated share register, CRS/FATCA self-certification (typically W-8BEN-E for a U.S. shareholder), and a remittance instruction referencing 'equity subscription contribution.' The transfer must be reported to FINTRAC if it is an electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. If the investment is into a non-Canadian controlled corporation and the asset value meets thresholds, the Investment Canada Act notification or review requirement must be assessed before inbound routing. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% combined general corporate rate (Federal Part I general rate 15% + Ontario general corporate income tax rate 10%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and deemed Part XIII distributions paid to non-resident shareholders. Under the Canada-US DTA: 15% for portfolio dividends, 5% for a corporate shareholder owning 10% or more of voting shares, and 0% for a qualifying parent owning 80% or more (subject to limitation-on-benefits).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ITA s. 247(4) requires mandatory contemporaneous documentation where the aggregate fair market value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CA$1,000,000 in a taxation year. A Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CA$100 million. The transfer-pricing penalty applies when a reassessment adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but substance-over-form controls are embedded in ITA s. 247(2) recharacterization, the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245), OECD BEPS attribution of profits, and the 25% GAAR penalty on denied tax benefits for transactions entered after April 27, 2024. The Ontario retail corporation must demonstrate physical inventory, active sales employees, risk-bearing purchase contracts, and decision-making in Ontario; a letter-box entity with margins stripped out to a no-tax foreign affiliate will face TP adjustment, treaty-obstacle scrutiny, and possible Part XIII recategorization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer-Pricing Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) upward adjustment plus 10% transfer-pricing penalty where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; reassessment interest from the original due date; failure to file T106 incurs CA$100-per-day information-return penalties (maximum CA$25,000) and, where gross negligence is proven, a 50% penalty under s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII and payroll withholdings under ITA s. 227.1; willful participation in TP fraud triggers criminal prosecution under s. 239, travel bans, director disqualification, and civil restitution claims in Ontario courts.
Part XIII Withholding Failure on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(8) and (9): 10% penalty on the amount that should have been withheld plus prescribed-rate daily compounded interest; the full unrecovered Part XIII amount remains payable by the Canadian payer; late NR4 filing triggers form-return penalties of CA$25 per day (minimum CA$100, maximum CA$2,500).

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the full unremitted withholding amount, penalties, and interest regardless of corporate insolvency; CRA may issue a director's liability certificate and enforce against personal bank accounts and property; repeated misconduct supports s. 239(1) evasion charges and imprisonment.
Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years. The gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) adds 50% of the understated tax, and Criminal Code s. 380(1) fraud charges may be stacked.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a mere façade; directors face civil restitution, asset-freezing orders, departure (travel) bans, disqualification from office, personal liability for fraudulent conveyances under ITA s. 160, and criminal custodial sentences for deliberate omission.
Shell Company and GAAR Artificial Structures

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s. 245(2) denies the full tax benefit, and the new s. 245(9) penalty adds 25% of the denied amount; associated-corporation redetermination triggers SBD clawback and repayment of the differential between 25.0% and 12.2% plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Schedule 8 or Schedule 91 filings are liable under s. 163(2) for 50% penalties as persons who participated in the misrepresentation; Ontario courts apply veil-piercing in sham situations; senior officers face travel restrictions and criminal prosecution for fraud as principals in the evasion.
