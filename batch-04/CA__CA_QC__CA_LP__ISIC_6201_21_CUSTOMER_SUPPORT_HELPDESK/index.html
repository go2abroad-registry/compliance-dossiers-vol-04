Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) via the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial (Quebec): Revenu Québec via the CLIC portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/). The Quebec Limited Partnership (CA_LP) is a distinct 'person' for GST/HST and QST purposes and requires a federal Business Number (BN) and a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) registered with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec.

E-government filing portal

1) File the Limited Partnership declaration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ; the LP is constituted under Book IV of the Civil Code of Quebec. 2) Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number using Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number') or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service, selecting GST/HST, payroll, and deemed import/export accounts as applicable; the LP is registered in its own name, not in the partners' names. 3) Once registered federally, complete the single-window enterprise registration with Revenu Québec to activate the QST account, which is mandatory when taxable supplies in Quebec exceed CAD 30,000 or when the business is in a no-threshold sector (none applicable to helpdesk SaaS). 4) For a non-resident limited partner, file CRA Form RC3 (Application for Authorization of a Tax Representative) and confirm compliance with Part XIII non-resident withholding on Canadian-source allocated income; for QST, non-resident partners are deemed to carry on the partnership's Quebec business. 5) Activate the CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CLIC credentials, appoint an authorized representative/filer, and register for electronic GST/HST and QST return filing (mandatory for registrants reporting electronically by default).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s.96, a Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: no entity-level federal or Quebec income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income/loss, and non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source partnership income. For GST/HST and QST, however, the Excise Tax Act and Quebec Sales Tax Act deem the partnership to be a person, so the LP holds its own GST/HST and QST registrations and must collect/remit tax on taxable supplies. The commercial model (helpdesk SaaS, low AML risk, no statutory license) does not alter this default classification.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election. Relevant elections and hybrid-mismatch mechanisms for a Quebec LP: (i) ITA s.96 designation of each partner's income/loss allocation in the partnership agreement, binding unless CRA considers it artificial; (ii) ETA s.209(6) election to voluntarily register as a GST/HST registrant while below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (reverse is not permitted once over the threshold); (iii) ETA s.191 election for supplies among closely related corporations (largely unavailable to an LP unless 90% corporate control exists within the group); (iv) ETA s.211 election for exempt financial services (not applicable to helpdesk SaaS); (v) ITA s.215(2) and s.216(4) elections enabling a non-resident partner to have withholding tax remitted on a net basis or deemed-paid basis; (vi) hybrid mismatch alignment rules (ITA ss.18.2, 18.21, the EIFEL regime effective for taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2023) mandate an interest-deduction cap for large groups and are mandatory, not elective. No specific Quebec pass-through or transparency election exists beyond the default statutory treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.98%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. The GST/HST small-supplier threshold (ETA s.148(3)) is based on worldwide taxable supplies (including supplies by associated persons) over the preceding four calendar quarters and any current quarter; the QST small-supplier threshold (Quebec Sales Tax Act s.85) is likewise CAD 30,000. Once the CA_LP's taxable helpdesk SaaS revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in any qualifying four-quarter window, GST/HST and QST registration becomes mandatory; voluntary registration is available below the threshold and is often elected to claim input tax credits on startup costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec LP supplying multi-channel helpdesk, customer-support, and conversational-bot cloud services (ISIC 6201) may zero-rate its supplies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 (services supplied to non-residents) if all of the following apply: (a) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered under Part IX of the ETA at the time the supply is made; (b) the service is not performed wholly or substantially in Canada (remote helpdesk delivery via cloud infrastructure and offshore support agents satisfies this); (c) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situate in Canada; and (d) the supply is not for consumption, use, or enjoyment in Canada by a GST/HST registrant. Equivalent zero-rating applies for QST under the Quebec Sales Tax Act s.14 and s.9, with the recipient required to be a 'non-designated consumer' or non-resident who is not a QST registrant. If the helpdesk services support Canadian-based clients or Canadian go-to-market SaaS subscriptions, the supply is taxable at the combined 14.98% rate; reverse-charge self-assessment obligations may apply to non-resident recipients in limited circumstances.

No real-time fiscal clearance, no pre-stamped digital tax invoice, and no per-transaction API authorization exists in Canada or Quebec for B2B/B2C invoices. Mandates applicable to the CA_LP: (i) electronic filing of GST/HST returns through CRA GST/HST NETFILE (GST34 form) and electronic remittance for registrants above CRA thresholds; (ii) electronic filing of QST returns through Revenu Québec's CLIC/EBusiness services for registrants required to report electronically; (iii) adoption of the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is mandatory for suppliers to the federal government (B2G) from fiscal years 2026-2027 onward for large taxpayers, but remains voluntary for commercial invoices issued to Stripe Billing / PayPal customers. The CA_LP must nevertheless maintain compliant transaction records and invoice fields required by CRA and Revenu Québec (seller identity, BN/QST numbers, tax rates, and amounts).

Canada and Quebec do not codify a statutory 180-day exchange-repatriation deadline; however, the CRA and Revenu Québec require exporters to substantiate zero-rating on an ongoing basis, and a 180-day benchmark is the practical audit-evidence horizon for matching export declarations to inward remittances. Penalties for fraudulent zero-rating claims: (i) federal Excise Tax Act s.280(1) imposes a penalty of CAD 5,000 or 25% of the net tax sought to be avoided, whichever is greater, for false statements or omissions; (ii) ETA s.239 creates criminal liability (fine up to 25% of the deficiency plus imprisonment for up to 5 years) for willful evasion with intent to deceive; (iii) CRA can reassess retroactively for up to four years plus a 12-year window where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default (ETR s.298); (iv) Revenu Québec under the Tax Administration Act (CQLR c. A-6.002) s.1033.1 imposes penalties of CAD 5,000 to CAD 25,000 plus 50% of the amount evaded for deliberate false statements, and s.1032(1) penalizes failure to register or file QST; (v) QST clawbacks require repayment of the full QST not collected, plus interest from the original supply date and compounding penalties; (vi) joint and several liability may extend to general partners who knowingly participate in a false zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; CAD is freely floating with no exchange-control permission requirements. Profit remittance for the Quebec LP is constrained only by taxation: distributions of partnership income to non-resident limited partners attract Part XIII withholding under subsection 212(1) of the Income Tax Act (default 25%, reduced by double-tax treaties, e.g., Canada-US treaty rates of 5-15%), and the LP must file T5013 and NR4 returns and maintain CRS/FATCA partner self-certification. The remaining commercial hurdle is bank KYC documentation proving source of funds rather than any FX quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory timeline, and no requirement to sell export or SaaS receipts into CAD; receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at domestic banks. The only applicable reporting requirements are FINTRAC's mandatory reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, CRS/FATCA foreign-account reporting, and Quebec and Canada tax disclosure obligations. No conversion is compelled at any threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and payments-system authority; OSFI as prudential banking supervisor; FINTRAC as AML/ATF supervisory agency; Department of Finance Canada for sanctions and financial policy; and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) of Quebec as the provincial money-services-business regulator. Canada has no dedicated SAFE-style foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a receivable collection gateway; Low as a primary liquidity-clearing rail. Wise (FINTRAC-registered MSB) and PayPal Business (authorized MSB) can lawfully collect Stripe/B2B helpdesk fees and sweep into the Tier-1 operational account; Airwallex may be used for supplier payout automation if registered with FINTRAC. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI or payment service provider that is not FINTRAC-registered cannot lawfully provide money-services-business services in or from Canada; (ii) in Quebec, money-services-business activity is further subject to the Money-Services Businesses Act and AMF registration or exemption; (iii) using unregistered offshore EMIs as the main treasury account may trigger aggravated FINTRAC EDD, destabilize the partnership capital-account mapping, and create non-Canadian functional-currency exposure under subsection 261(1) of the Income Tax Act.
