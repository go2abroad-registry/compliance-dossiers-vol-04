Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Business Number registration via My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and Quebec enterprise registration via CléNumérique/Registraire des entreprises (https://www.revenuquebec.ca)

E-government filing portal

1) Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); 2) Submit CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register online through Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the nine-digit Business Number (BN) preceded by 'RP' for a partnership; 3) Apply separately to Revenu Québec for the QST and GST/QST combined registration file using the NEQ as the key identifier; 4) Appoint an authorized attorney or resident representative in Quebec to receive service of process and tax notices, required for non-resident general partners; 5) Non-resident partners must obtain an Individual Tax Number (ITN) from the CRA for proportional reporting; 6) The general partner acts as the 'representative' or 'operator' of the LP for all registration, filing, and remittance acts; 7) Maintain the LP as a registrant at the partnership level with the BN ending in 'RT0001' to segregate GST/HST account from partner-level accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96 and the Quebec Taxation Act; income, losses, credits, and tax attributes flow through to limited and general partners on a proportional basis; the partnership itself files an information return (T5013 and Quebec equivalent) but pays no federal or Quebec income tax. For GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate person capable of registration only if it is a 'Canadian partnership' as defined in ETA s. 123(1) and the analogous Quebec QST definition.

Available election forms

Canada has no federal 'check-the-box' entity classification election; eligibility is determined by statute and common law. Available elections relevant to a pass-through LP: (i) election to compute partnership income on a business-year basis (ITA s. 96(4)); (ii) election to allocate eligible capital property gains at the partnership level (ITA s. 1100(2) and s. 13(7)); (iii) ETA s. 156 election to treat closely related registrants as a single GST/HST entity, with mirror election under the Quebec QST Act s. 130.1; (iv) election to register voluntarily as a small supplier for GST/HST and QST to recover input tax credits and refunds earlier; (v) for non-resident partners holding rental or timber interests, ITA s. 216 election to file on a net-basis rather than gross withholding; (vi) hybrid mismatch elections and self-correction must respect Canada's OECD hybrid rules in ITA ss. 18.4–18.7, which apply to partnership interests and deny deduction/income-inclusion mismatches without a domestic elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies (worldwide, excluding zero-rated exports and financial services) over one calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters for GST/HST small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148); identical C$30,000 threshold applies to QST (Quebec fiscal regime). Mandatory registration applies without threshold where the non-resident LP 'carries on business' in Canada/quebec via a permanent establishment, supplies real property, or is a non-resident contractor undertaking prescribed activities. Export-oriented surgical consumables manufacturing likely qualifies for voluntary small-supplier registration to recover 5% GST and 9.975% QST on inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to a GST/HST and QST registrant that supplies: (i) tangible personal property exported by the supplier as part of a supply made to a non-resident person who is not registered, per Schedule VI s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act and the mirroring Quebec provision; (ii) a supply of goods that is entrusted to a common carrier for direct export before the goods are otherwise used in Canada; (iii) a supply of a service to a non-resident customer when the service is performed wholly or principally outside Canada (Schedule VI s. 7) or where the service relates to exported goods and the non-resident is not in Canada; (iv) for manufacturing of absorbable polyglycolic acid braided sutures, zero-rating applies to selling the finished surgical goods to foreign hospitals/distributors if the goods leave Canada in documented physical possession of the supplier, the common carrier, or the purchaser 'as soon after the supply as is reasonably possible' up to the 30-day export window; (v) the exporter must hold evidence that the goods were actually exported, including a CBSA export declaration, bill of lading, freight waybill, customs coded tariff schedule, and proof of clearance into the foreign destination; (vi) jurisdiction-specific requirement: services rendered to a non-resident in 'ordinary animal' course? must be rendered directly to the non-resident and not benefit a person in Canada; QST zero-rating requires equivalent proof filed with Revenu Québec, including statement of reason for export and a signed certificate of the person exporting.

  • Commercial invoice (sales contract) identifying the foreign buyer, shipment terms, HS code 9018.32/3006.10 (or ISIC 3250 equivalent), and zero-rated status
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / outbound transportation record with carrier signature and destination port of import
  • Customs entry at destination and/or proof of foreign customs release clearing the sutures into the destination market

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance API, or digital stamping regime exists federally or in Quebec; GST/HST and QST invoices are subject only to content rules (ETA s. 223; Quebec QST Act s. 251.61) and may be paper or electronic. Canada participates in the PEPPOL e-invoicing network for business-to-government transactions on a voluntary basis; CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic returns via NETFILE, EDI, and Revenu Québec's online business portal (CléNumérique), requiring XML/SOAP for high-volume excise and QST filers. There are no blockchain stamping or government pre-clearance APIs for surgical suture invoices; fiscal documentation must be retained in French or English for audit purposes.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated export claims retroactively within four years (or later in cases of misrepresentation), denying the zero-rating and clawing back all ITCs/QST ITRs claimed plus statutory interest compounded daily. Penalties are severe: (i) under ETA s. 285(1), a false or reckless statement on an input tax refund includes a penalty equal to 50% of the overstated refund or credit; (ii) under ETA s. 280, failure to keep proper export substantiation documents carries fines up to C$25,000; (iii) criminal evasion under ETA s. 281 exposes individuals to fines up to 200% of the evaded net tax and imprisonment of up to two years; (iv) Revenu Québec mirrors these penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act, imposing civil penalties up to 50% of the QST credit improperly claimed plus quarterly compound interest and potential suspension of the QST registration number; (v) for intra-group export schemes, CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) to void invoice structures and imposes gross negligence penalties of 50% of total tax avoided; (vi) under ITA s. 152(4), misrepresentations attributable to neglect or fraud extend the reassessment window beyond normal limitation periods, creating unlimited exposure. Therefore, the exporter must retain the full documentary bundle for seven years and operationalize a 180-day FX repatriation review trigger to preserve zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and floating. Canada maintains a fully open capital account with no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions, including no prior approval for foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, or management fees. Quebec limited partnerships may remit profits to non-resident partners subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax and Quebec withholding tax on amounts deemed to be dividends, interest, royalties, or rents; the Canada–Quebec relief system and applicable tax treaties may reduce or exempt such withholding. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to bank compliance due-diligence, FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting thresholds, sanctions screening, and the requirement that non-resident partners obtain a Canadian tax identification number where treaty relief is claimed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no minimum conversion ratio, and no designated foreign-exchange account regime for export receipts. Exporters may freely hold CAD, USD, or other foreign-currency proceeds in bank accounts in Canada or abroad. However, all cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC, physical currency or monetary instruments crossing the border at or above CAD 10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency, and corresponding payments must be supported by commercial documentation sufficient to satisfy financial-institution sanctions and anti-money-laundering obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), supplemented by FINTRAC for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing transaction surveillance, and by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for Quebec financial-sector regulation; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI or electronic-money institutions are not registrable as banks or deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act or the Quebec Act respecting financial services cooperatives. They may act only as money-services businesses if registered with FINTRAC and, where operating in Quebec, subject to the AMF regime. No offshore EMI may hold Canadian operating deposits under the legal designation of 'bank account'; a relationship must instead be structured as a custodial payment-institution arrangement. Major Canadian Schedule I banks remain the viable primary clearing and liquidity providers for a Quebec limited partnership, while FINTRAC-registered payment fintechs are acceptable only for ancillary disbursement and automated treasury workstreams.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Quebec limited partnership must be routed through a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank account in the legal name of the partnership. Preferred protocol is SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary account details matching the partnership's Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), a full legal name, and the bank's institution number and transit number. Payment rationale should explicitly state 'capital contribution to Quebec limited partnership' and reference the limited partnership agreement and approved budget. Banks will require prior validation of the remitter, source-of-funds evidence, and sanctions screening before crediting the account. Inward electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; for USD inflows from foreign investors, banks may route payments through a US correspondent and may request an intermediate-bank fee recovery. There is no Canadian regulatory purpose-code submission requirement, but the bank's commercial payment form may require a narrative code such as 'capital in kind' or 'equity investment'; the remitting foreign bank's purpose code should be reconciled with underlying equity documents.Outbound trade disbursements for surgical suture needle assembly operations should be settled through SWIFT MT103 for outright interbank wire payments, and through SWIFT MT700-series documentary letters of credit where counterparties require title and payment security. For commercial payments, the partnership must provide the overseas beneficiary's full bank details, invoice and packing-list references, and the underlying contract. Canadian banks screen every outbound wire against sanctions lists; FINTRAC reportability applies to outbound electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Payments related to imports of raw materials or equipment must include customs documents, and any pre-export or post-export documentary compliance must be maintained. For recurring supplier payments, the partnership may propose an open-account settlement schedule with a bank-provided FX conversion service. Clearing rails should include: SWIFT MT103 for low-touch confirmed wires, commercial documentary credits for high-value or first-time counterparties, and CAD TARGET-style large-value clearing through Payments Canada's Lynx system for CAD-denominated domestic settlement legs.

Offshore digital EMI or electronic-money institutions are not registrable as banks or deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act or the Quebec Act respecting financial services cooperatives. They may act only as money-services businesses if registered with FINTRAC and, where operating in Quebec, subject to the AMF regime. No offshore EMI may hold Canadian operating deposits under the legal designation of 'bank account'; a relationship must instead be structured as a custodial payment-institution arrangement. Major Canadian Schedule I banks remain the viable primary clearing and liquidity providers for a Quebec limited partnership, while FINTRAC-registered payment fintechs are acceptable only for ancillary disbursement and automated treasury workstreams.
