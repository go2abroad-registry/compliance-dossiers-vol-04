Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Ontario law. The Declaration must state each limited partner's contribution amount and whether it is in cash or property. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Canadian federal sales tax and income tax administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax measures. Primary online portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (cra-arc.gc.ca/mybusinessaccount).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership (LP) conducting taxable activity in Ontario must first register extra-provincially under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (LP Act) via the Ontario Business Registry. Thereafter, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account, either online through My Business Account, by telephoning the CRA's international tax services line, or by submitting Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'. Non-resident entities without a Canadian address must appoint a Canadian representative or agent for service and provide a certified copy of the partnership agreement and extra-provincial registration. The CRA may issue the GST/HST account immediately upon verification of the LP's legal existence and jurisdictional contacts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general 'check-the-box' entity classification election; the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and is not a taxpayer in its own right. Income/loss/credits flow through to partners, including non-resident partners, who may be liable under Part XIV Canadian non-resident tax. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership may apply to be treated as a separate registrant and may, if eligible, elect to have a non-resident partner's offshore supplies excluded from the partnership's registration. Certain targeted anti-hybrid rules under ITA sections 12.6 to 12.72 and 18.9 disallow hybrid mismatch deductions without an elective opt-in; the LP may nonetheless choose how to allocate taxable income among partners in the partnership agreement, subject to reasonableness and anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — applies to a sole proprietor, partnership, or corporation whose worldwide taxable supplies (other than capital gains from personal property) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident registrants are subject to the same small-supplier threshold but must register when making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of carrying on a business in Canada or on a 'supplies made in Canada' basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the transcontinental block train expedited freight forwarding service, the supply of transportation of goods to a destination outside Canada (including international cross-border rail freight to the United States or beyond) is zero-rated as a 'supply of a transportation service of goods to or from a place outside Canada' under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA). The supplier must retain documentary evidence demonstrating that the transportation originates or terminates outside Canada and that the goods actually left Canada. Where the intermodal service includes domestic rail segments before the goods cross the border, the entire continuous freight transportation supply is zero-rated if arranged as a single shipment contract under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, item 11.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or CARM Single Window Interface electronic export report) — required for any U.S.-bound shipment exceeding CAD 2,000 in value
  • Through Bill of Lading or intermodal waybill evidencing the contract of international carriage from Canadian origin to named foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/wire transfer confirmation showing full export freight charges repatriated to the Canadian or appointed payee within the statutorily established foreign exchange receipt deadline
  • Proof of foreign destination delivery (consignee acknowledgment, rail terminal receipt, U.S. Customs entry or border crossing document)

No mandatory real-time tax stamping or fiscal clearance is imposed by the CRA. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information, retain electronic books and records, and file periodic GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE or electronic filing where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million or where the registrant is a non-resident. For export freight, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires electronic pre-arrival reporting using the Single Window Interface and CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management). Digital e-invoicing is accepted but optional; no third-party clearance or digital stamping is required.

The CRA may reassess and recover any input tax credit or tax refund erroneously claimed in respect of zero-rated export supplies, with interest compounded daily. Penalties include the ETA section 280 'false statement or omission' penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, plus fines up to CAD 10,000 on a summary conviction or CAD 25,000 on an indictment; gross negligence penalties under ITA section 163.2 can be imposed at 50% of the understated tax. Where export evidence is missing, the CRA will treat the supply as taxable in Canada at 13%, claw back all related input tax credits, and impose documentation penalties of CAD 1,000 for non-resident registrants failing to maintain proper export records. Repeated or fraudulent zero-rating claims may result in civil fraud prosecution, monetary civil penalties of up to CAD 25,000, and a requirement to post security for future GST/HST obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-control approvals, no capital-account licensing, no limits on foreign-currency bank accounts, and no impediment to non-resident capital contributions into an Ontario limited partnership. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the FX layer; the only material friction is tax-driven: non-resident limited partners are taxable on allocated Canadian-source partnership income through the LP's T5013 Partnership Information Return, and cash distributions of income attract Part XIII withholding at the applicable treaty-reduced rate (commonly 15%), whereas documented returns of capital and capital gains are exempt from Part XIII. Rail freight forwarding service receivables under ISIC 5229 are current-account items with unimpeded conversion. All international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported by the bank to FINTRAC, so each transfer must carry a coherent commercial purpose and supporting transport/invoice documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule. There is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to exchange USD, EUR, or CNH freight-forwarding earnings into CAD. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained indefinitely in designated or sweep sub-accounts at the LP's Canadian bank. The only binding duty is AML-monetary: Schedule I banks must report international electronic fund transfers at or above CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, and the LP must retain waybills, bills of lading, and commercial invoices substantiating each receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); residual exchange-control policy rests with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act (Exchange Fund Account) and the Bank of Canada Act. No dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada because the Foreign Exchange Control Board was abolished in 1951. OSFI prudentially supervises the Schedule I banks, and FINTRAC administers cross-border transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium. Foreign-registered digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be onboarded for low-value corridor payments, but they are not Payments Canada members and cannot provide final CAD settlement through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). An overseas EMI carrying on business in Canada must be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC, and an unregistered offshore EMI cannot lawfully maintain a Canadian-currency funding relationship. Consequently, EMIs are limited to supplementary float disbursements for small drayage and per-container charges; the anchor treasury account and all documentary letter-of-credit issuance must sit with a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited-partner contributions should be routed as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers from the contributor's own bank account, never from a third-party or pooled account, denominated in CAD, USD, EUR, or CNH. The remitter must state an ISO 20022 purpose code such as INVC or PCAP and identify the LP by exact legal name, Ontario registered address, and the beneficiary bank account pre-approved in the KYC file. The receiving Schedule I bank will final-settle CAD obligations through LVTS or clear USD/EUR through its correspondent network under Payments Canada rules, subject to intraday verification that the payment matches the pre-loaded capital subscription schedule. Each capital receipt must be posted to the LP's capital accounts, reflected in the annual T5013 allocation, and reported in the bank's FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer report if the amount is CAD 10,000 or above. Properly documented paid-in capital from a non-resident limited partner is not subject to Part XIII withholding; recharacterization as a service fee, management charge, or shareholder loan must be avoided.Disbursements for rail freight and intermodal operations settle over three rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for operational payables to rail operators, terminal handlers, drayage carriers, and customs brokers, each bearing a transparent purpose code (FRET, SUPP, or INTP) and supported by a freight invoice or waybill retained for CRA inspection; (ii) commercial letters of credit for larger or recurring counterparty settlements, issued by the LP's Schedule I bank under UCP 600, with presentation of commercial invoices, transport documents, and packing lists, and, where requested, confirmation by a correspondent bank along the Eurasia corridor; and (iii) domestic electronic funds transfer and LVTS same-day wires for high-value Canadian-dollar obligations. RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, and CIBC maintain documentary-credit and trade desks able to issue or confirm letters of credit within three to five business days.
