Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a Québec limited partnership (Civil Code of Québec, arts. 2236–2248). Limited partners must contribute money or property, not services; general partners may contribute capital, property, or services. No escrow, bank attestation, or capital deposit is mandated. The Declaration of Limited Partnership must state the contribution of each limited partner and, for general partners, describe their contribution or obligation to contribute; where required, the partnership agreement fixes capital accounts and distribution entitlements.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification per the Règlement sur la sécurité incendie under the Act respecting fire safety (CQLR c. S-3.4) and municipal fire code inspections for storage occupancy; structural load and building occupancy classification conformity under the Québec Construction Code, Chapter I — Building (CQLR c. B-1.1) for any leasehold improvements, including municipal building permits where required; CNESST employer registration and workplace compliance under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. S-2.1) for storage personnel; emergency measures, fire extinguisher servicing, and general liability insurance maintained as part of the operating permit.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required for a Québec limited partnership (Civil Code of Québec, arts. 2236–2248). Limited partners must contribute money or property, not services; general partners may contribute capital, property, or services. No escrow, bank attestation, or capital deposit is mandated. The Declaration of Limited Partnership must state the contribution of each limited partner and, for general partners, describe their contribution or obligation to contribute; where required, the partnership agreement fixes capital accounts and distribution entitlements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — tax-transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax purposes; income is allocated to, and taxed at, the partner level. The LP is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax, but it is treated as a registrant/person for GST/HST and QST purposes. Non-resident partners are taxable only on their Canadian-source partnership allocations, subject to limitation-of-benefits and treaty provisions. No tax-exempt zone status applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box entity classification election exists in Canadian law; partnership status as a flow-through vehicle is mandatory. Available statutory elections are limited to: voluntary GST/HST/QST registration before the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; the election under subsection 156(1) of the Excise Tax Act to treat closely related registrants as a single GST/HST registrant where the common ownership test is met; and corresponding provincial elections where Revenu Québec recognizes the federal grouping treatment. Hybrid-mismatch provisions in the Income Tax Act and Quebec taxation statutes apply automatically and cannot be overridden by entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: registration is mandatory when a supplier's worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the previous four consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold includes zero-rated export supplies, including wholesale ophthalmic frames. QST: the same C$30,000 small-supplier threshold generally applies to taxable supplies made in Quebec; registrants below the threshold may register voluntarily. A foreign LP making taxable supplies from a Canadian business platform in Quebec may be required to register even in the absence of a domestic physical establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of tangible personal property, including acetate optical frames, that are exported from Canada as part of a continuous journey to a destination outside Canada, or mailed/shipped by common carrier to a foreign destination. The supplier must be registered and hold contemporaneous evidence of export. Ancillary services performed in respect of the exported goods and supplied to a non-resident person may also be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met. Merely selling goods to a non-resident without physical export from Canada does not qualify. The LP may claim input tax credits and GST/HST/QST refunds attributable to export zero-rated supplies in the same return in which the 0% rate is applied.

  • Completed commercial invoice describing the ophthalmic frames, HS code and sales value
  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or approved electronic export report) where required, generally for consignments valued above C$2,000 or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing the Canadian LP as consignor, a non-Canadian consignee and a destination outside Canada
  • Proof of export from Canada Border Services Agency or destination-country customs entry record/importation stamp
  • Freight forwarder delivery order and warehouse movement records evidencing the continuous international shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank payment confirmation proving FX repatriation within the 180-day invoice cycle
  • Intercompany or sales contract, purchase order and insurance documents corroborating the export transaction

Canada and Quebec do not currently mandate real-time transaction clearance, fiscal stamping or government pre-approval of invoices. Registrants must issue tax-compliant electronic or paper invoices containing the supplier's name, Business Number and QST registration number, recipient details, date, description of goods, and either the tax charged or an explicit zero-rating statement. Electronic invoices in EDI, PDF or XML are accepted. GST/HST and QST returns above prescribed revenue thresholds must be filed electronically. Export evidence and underlying business records must be retained for at least six years for audit purposes.

Export zero-rating claims are an audit priority for CRA and Revenu Québec. If goods have not actually left Canada, or if the required export documentation is incomplete, the 0% rate is retrospectively reversed and the LP must remit 14.5% combined GST/QST on the supply value, plus statutory interest compounded daily. Associated input tax credits and refunds are clawed back. Administrative penalties may be imposed for false statements, omissions, gross negligence and wilful evasion under the Excise Tax Act, Quebec Tax Administration Act and related customs legislation. The normal four-year reassessment window may be extended indefinitely where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness or fraud. Criminal prosecution for wilful tax evasion may result in fines and imprisonment. The general partner and limited partners may also face joint and several liability for unremitted indirect taxes to the extent of LP assets and under applicable partnership and civil-liability rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully freely convertible on both capital and current accounts. There are no statutory exchange controls, no differential treatment for capital versus current transactions, and no requirement for central-bank approval of profit remittances or dividend distributions. Profits of a Quebec Limited Partnership may be distributed to non-resident partners without currency licensing, provided the general partner has complied with partnership taxation filings and any applicable withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Large wire transfers and monetary remittances are subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds and bank-level source-of-funds due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules in Canada. Exporters are not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender foreign-currency export proceeds, and they may lawfully retain such proceeds in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. The only compliance obligations are reporting-related: financial institutions must report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and physical cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more require corresponding declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority; the foreign-exchange framework is governed by the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility as auxiliary clearing vehicles, medium as primary treasury bank accounts. Canadian-regulated fintech and EMI equivalents (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, dLocal) can operate only as money service businesses, not as banks; they must be registered with FINTRAC and comply with provincial money-services licensing. They are suitable for Platform_Pay rail collection, supplier disbursements, and low-value cross-border settlements, but cannot provide credit, commercial letters of credit, or full FX hedging. All platform-pay balances serving as treasury assets should be swept to a Tier 1 Canadian chartered bank account to reduce custodial and liquidity risk.

Root cause: Non-resident limited partners with an opaque ownership or control chain.

Defense evidence: Provide the notarized limited partnership agreement, certificate of general partner status, register of partners, and a completed beneficial-ownership schedule identifying every individual owning 25% or more of the partnership. Supplement with passport copies, proof of residential address, corporate registry extracts from the jurisdiction of formation, and a narrative commercial rationale letter explaining the distribution of acetate optical frames and the Canadian wholesale business model.
Potential exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions or politically exposed persons.

Root cause: Potential exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions or politically exposed persons.

Defense evidence: Deliver board- or partner-level attestations confirming no sanctioned counterparties, combined with OFAC/OSFI sanctions-screening results, entity and individual watchlist screening, and a supplier/customer ledger for the first six months. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners should be routed through SWIFT MT103 into the LP's Tier 1 Canadian CAD account using the general partner's name and partnership registration number. There is no CAD conversion obligation and no central-bank approval; the bank will require a completed FINTRAC-compliant client remittance form for wires equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. Field 70 should specify 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / LP SUBSCRIPTION' and, where possible, include the partnership's agreement reference. For USD inflows, funds may be credited to a USD clearing account and hedged or converted when needed. Canadian banks will conduct source-of-funds checks; the LP should attach its capital call notices and bank statements of each remitting partner to accelerate acceptance.Supplier settlements for wholesale optical frames can be executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for high-value purchases, or documentary commercial letters of credit under UCP600 where bank-confirmed trade security is required. The clearing rails include the Canadian Payments Association (now Payments Canada) for domestic CAD clearing, SWIFT for international wires, and CLS for FX settlements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as auxiliary clearing vehicles, medium as primary treasury bank accounts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with a single non-resident in one taxation year triggers the Form T106 filing requirement; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (OECD-compliant local/master elements) is expected where the LP is part of a MNE group with gross revenues exceed CAD 5,000,000 or has cross-border related party distribution/wholesale transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule and Quebec's analogous GAAR require real commercial substance and for the partnership to have permanent establishment capacity, active risk management, and demonstrable business purpose. A limited partnership with no employees, no premises, and all decisions made outside Quebec is vulnerable to re-characterization of its Canadian-source income as passive royalties and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Federal ITA penalties of 50% to 200% of evaded tax, criminal fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; Revenu Québec may levy civil penalties of 90% to 200% and proceed with criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for remittance failures and civil restitution; serious cases trigger court orders freezing personal assets, seizure of passports and travel bans, and up to 5 years imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA can reprice the transactions and charge 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment; late T106 filing penalty of CAD 24 per day up to CAD 2,400; interest accrues on additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: In a limited partnership corporate general partner, directors can be liable for the 10% penalty if they knowingly directed, participated in, or acquiesced in the non-compliance; cross-border advisory fee sharing taints directors' bonus and indemnity claims.
Shell Company and lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (ITA s.245) and Quebec GAAR permit revenue authorities to ignore the entity or allocation; treaty benefits may be denied and the entire distribution recharacterized as royalties subject to 25% Part XIII withholding, plus 50% misrepresentation penalty under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who structure the scheme may be jointly liable for the corporation's tax plus promoter penalties; CRA can issue a 'tax avoidance' compliance order, prohibit the director from leaving Canada, and seize corporate and personal records.
Failure to Withhold on Non-Resident Partner Allocations

Statutory sanction: Part XIII withholding failure triggers a penalty equal to 10% (first occurrence) or 20% (subsequent occurrence) of the unremitted amount under ITA s.227(9), plus interest; Quebec civil demand for the unpaid withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for the full amount of unremitted Part XIII taxes, plus Revenu Québec allows recovery against personal assets; bank support and travel permits can be refused until payment in full.
AML and Unregulated Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations (and implicitly of officers) and CAD 100,000 for individuals; Quebec's anti-money-laundering statutes also impose disciplinary licence consequences; criminal confiscation could follow the commercial transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use unregulated rails to evade Canadian tax or sanctions are subject to personal fines up to CAD 500,000, disgorgement of profits, imprisonment up to 5 years, and travel prohibition under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
