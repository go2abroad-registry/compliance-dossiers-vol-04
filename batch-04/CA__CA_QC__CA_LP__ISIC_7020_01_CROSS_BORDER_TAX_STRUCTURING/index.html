Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Quebec law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow-account mandate for a societe en commandite; capital is fixed contractually in the contrat de societe and may be contributed in cash, property, or services, and the accurately declared capital schedule is registered in the enterprise declaration.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy permit (permis d'occupation) issued by the local municipality; fire safety certificate (attestation de securite incendie) under the Quebec Building Code and Fire Safety Code; CNESST workplace health and safety clearance and workers' compensation registration for employees; RBQ structural conformity inspection of office floor loading (design live load minimum 2.4 kPa); accessibility compliance under the Quebec Building Code; mandatory professional liability insurance for OCPQ and Barreau permit holders.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) (sole federal/national tax administration; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) together with Revenu Québec (provincial tax authority responsible for Quebec business numbers, QST, and certain provincial tax compliance; portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec limited partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by using the online 'Register a business' service in My Business Account or by filing Form RC1; a non-resident taxpayer may file Form RC71 'Request for a Non-Resident Account ID' with supporting evidence including the partnership agreement, foreign registration certificate, name and address of the general partner, and a contact person/agent in Canada. The 9-digit BN will be activated for GST/HST (account type RT) if registration is required or elected. The partnership must also obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and register for QST with Revenu Québec through the Registraire d'entreprise du Québec or Revenu Québec's business online services; in Quebec this is a separate QST account type. For a limited partnership, the registration under both CRA and Revenu Québec is made in the partnership's legal name and the partnership is a distinct registrant for indirect tax, although for income tax the partners are taxed on their respective shares. Foreign partners do not obtain a Canadian TIN; the partnership's BN is the applicable statutory identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box entity classification system. A Quebec limited partnership is classified by provincial and federal law as a partnership and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation for domestic income tax. The relevant statutory elections are limited to matters such as partnership fiscal-period alignment under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act, joint elections around eligible deferred income, and elections governing non-resident partner WHT administration. For hybrid entity mismatches, Canada applies domestic hybrid-mismatch provisions and treaty tie-breaker rules without elective entity classification; a foreign limited liability company or protected cell company is therefore classified according to its legal characteristics and the relevant treaty, not by an election. For GST/QST, associated registrants may use section 156 of the Excise Tax Act and parallel Quebec rules to elect to have a supply by one associated registrant deemed to be a supply by another in order to avoid tax fragmentation, but this does not alter the partnership's pass-through income tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). Registration is mandatory for GST/HST and QST when the registrant's taxable supplies made in Canada (and in Quebec for QST) exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident suppliers of certain Quebec-directed intangible or digital services and non-residents carrying on business in Canada or Quebec may be required to register with CRA and Revenu Québec even below this threshold; there is no registration exemption threshold for a limited partnership carrying on an active commercial or consulting business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec LP registered for GST/HST and QST may zero-rate an export supply if the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding Quebec legislation. Goods qualify for zero-rating if they are exported continuously from Canada by the registrant or the registrant's agent and proof of export is retained. Cross-border tax structuring and transfer-pricing advisory services qualify for zero-rating when the recipient is a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST or QST, the service is not primarily for consumption/use in Canada, and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada. If the non-resident recipient is a GST/HST or QST registrant and acquires the service for use in Canada or to make taxable supplies in Canada, the supply becomes taxable at the regular rate. The LP must maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the non-resident status of the client, the offshore nature of the service, and the absence of Canadian consumption or use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Border Services Agency export report) where applicable for goods exported from Canada; B13A is required for most controlled, regulated, or high-value exports and simplified reporting applies to low-value courier shipments.
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck waybill) or equivalent carrier document showing the consignee outside Canada and the Canadian place of export.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank receipt, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement) evidencing receipt of the export sales proceeds from the non-resident buyer or client; this is typically required by Canadian banks and is routinely requested by CRA and Revenu Québec as third-party evidence of the export payment.
  • Commercial invoice or engagement letter identifying the non-resident client, the supply description, the delivery terms, and the place of supply; for advisory services include a written statement that the service is supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/QST and is not acquiring the service for use in Canada.
  • Proving documentation for services, including workpapers, timesheets, or project completion reports, demonstrating that the service is performed offshore or is not directed at Canadian real property or tangible personal property.
  • Customer contract, purchase order, and any export permits or certificates of origin required by customs programs applicable to the goods or jurisdiction of destination.

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing tax-control-towers, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control mandate applies in Canada or Quebec. Electronic invoicing is permitted on a voluntary basis and, if used, the invoice must contain the prescribed GST/QST information, including the issuing registrant's BN/NEQ, recipient name, supplier name, description of supply, tax rates, and tax amounts. CRA and Revenu Québec require invoice records to be preserved in an auditable format and produced on request. From an administrative perspective, CRA and Revenu Québec use digitized audit platforms and taxpayer portals, but there is no statutory obligation to submit invoices for real-time fiscal clearance.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess export zero-rating claims within four years from the general assessment period; no limitation period applies if there is misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud attributable to the registrant or its agent. Where the export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Quebec at 14.975% combined GST/QST, and the resulting net tax amount becomes collectible with prescribed daily compounding interest. Civil penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act may be the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated net tax or over-claimed refund where amounts were misstated knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; Revenu Québec imposes materially parallel penalties under the Tax Administration Act. Repetition or failure to remit can trigger additional percentage penalties, and failure to maintain export documentation can also cause the loss of the zero-rating claim without access to ordinary refund input-tax-credit entitlements. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is not a statutory fixed deadline for zero-rating, but failure to receive inward remittance within that benchmark will create a presumption of a non-genuine export and trigger heightened audit scrutiny; documentary credit terms or contractual payment delays may be refuted only with contemporaneous contracts, correspondence, bank statements, and evidence of the non-resident client's creditworthiness. In egregious cases, a deliberately false zero-rating claim may be treated as tax evasion and prosecuted under the Excise Tax Act or the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. No statutory distinction is made between current-account and capital-account transactions; repatriation of dividends, partnership profits, management fees, and contributed capital is unrestricted, subject only to tax withholding, record-keeping, and standard anti-money-laundering controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers may lawfully retain foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts and have no obligation to chain proceeds into Canadian dollars. Cross-border electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy oversight is exercised by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, with banking conduct and anti-money-laundering supervision by OSFI and FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Offshore digital EMI accounts are operationally usable for settlement and disbursements but only if the EMI is duly registered as a money services business with FINTRAC, holds the required provincial license (e.g., Quebec or Ontario money-transmitter licensing), and maintains a fully compliant AML/KYC program. Unlicensed foreign EMIs cannot obtain domestic clearing routes, cannot be funded through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System, and are prohibited from soliciting Canadian corporate clients; therefore, the LP should treat offshore EMIs as secondary disbursement vehicles, never as the primary capital-receiving rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The receiving bank will require the partnership name, capital-account reference, legal-entity identifier, and underlying source-of-funds evidence for any transfer that is part of a recurring pattern or above CAD 10,000. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec provincial combined corporate headline rate; applicable to corporate partners receiving allocable income from a flow-through Limited Partnership)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident recipient; a Limited Partnership distribution is not a dividend and is not subject to Part XIII withholding. Instead, non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source partnership income, and withholding may apply under Part XIII on certain income components (e.g., interest, rents, royalties) flowing through the partnership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed by the last day of the fifth month after the partnership's fiscal period end. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the statutory deadline is May 31 (or June 15 for certain non-calendar partnerships with approval).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The RL-15 must be filed with Revenu Québec by the last day of the fifth month after the partnership's fiscal year-end (same as federal T5013). Copies to partners, if personal income tax returns are impacted, must be provided on or before February 28/29 of the following calendar year (or the legal filing deadline for partner returns).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$150,000 for services, intangible property, and interest transactions; C$1,000,000 for tangible goods transactions. For a cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory practice, the C$150,000 threshold is immediately triggered by any intercompany service fee, consulting revenue, or sub-licensed intellectual property charge.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) and the doctrine of substance over form to deny tax benefits where the primary purpose is unauthorized tax avoidance. The Quebec Limited Partnership must maintain real economic activity — including payroll, office premises, client-facing decision making, and genuine commercial contracts — to avoid recharacterization as a conduit. While no standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime exists in Canada, CRA and Revenu Québec require adherence to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Canadian treaty law; artificial arrangements lacking mind and management in Quebec are vulnerable to permanent establishment reassessments and GAAR nullification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberately Unreported Canadian-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction for tax evasion under ITA s.239(1) yields a fine up to C$200,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years (summary) or 5 years (indictment); CRA may also assess interest on unpaid balances; filing a false T5013 triggers additional Part I penalties and revocation of administrative relief.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner or agent who authorized, acquiesced, or provided the false filing are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unpaid payroll and Part XIII amounts; civil fraud actions allow veil-piercing, personal restitution and freezing of assets; an arrest warrant or travel ban may be issued during a criminal tax investigation; conviction may result in imprisonment.
Off-Market Transfer Pricing / Artificial Migration of Profit

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer pricing reassessment under s.247(2); 10% penalty under s.247(3) if the net transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million or $50,000? (practically: 10% of the aggregate adjustment); interest and compounding penalties; Revenu Québec may mirror Quebec Income Tax Act penalties for the same adjustment; documents that are deficient or absent are treated as a lack of reasonable efforts for the penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable under ITA s.160 for amounts transferred to non-arm's length persons without fair consideration; under provincial fraud statutes and commercial law, directors who knowingly set pricing to strip Canadian profit may be required to reimburse the partnership; Canadian courts may lift the corporate veil if the structure is deployed as a sham; cross-border travel and immigration remedies (including denial of admission) may be invoked against non-resident principals who exercise management.

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 denies all tax benefits attributable to the transaction; CRA may also assess penalties for misrepresentation of facts under s.163.2 (third-party civil penalties up to C$1,000,000 for advisors) if the arrangement is marketed as a shelter; Quebec Taxation Act provisions similarly void the provincial fiscal consequences; retroactive cancellation of provincial credits and complex interest,

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the veil if the LP is a pure sham, rendering the general partner's directors personally liable for all unpaid federal and provincial taxes; personal civil liability extends to tax arrears, penalties, and promotional legal costs; non-resident directors instructing the sham can face a charge of counselling evasion and are exposed to an extradition risk treaty reciprocity; travel bans and seizure of passports are enforceable in criminal and fraud proceedings.
