Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); main registration and filing portal: CRA Business Registration Online at canada.ca/business-number; for Quebec QST: Revenu Québec at revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership making taxable supplies in or from Canada obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) as its national tax ID/TIN for GST/HST, by filing Form RC1 (or RC1V for non-residents) or through Business Registration Online. The Quebec limited partnership must also register with Revenu Québec for QST using the partnership registration service, submitting the certificate of limited partnership or foreign registration extract, general partner identification, foreign or agent address, and existing foreign tax ID. The BN serves as the TIN for GST/HST remittance, customs/export reporting, and partner-level compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification. A Quebec Limited Partnership is automatically fiscally transparent for federal and provincial income tax purposes. Available elections include the GST/HST partnership nil-consideration election for inter-partner supplies and the corresponding QST election; hybrid mismatch recharacterisation may apply under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act where non-resident partners create mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (GST/HST and QST) made over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter (small-supplier threshold); a non-resident partnership that exceeds the threshold and is carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act (and Part V, Schedule V of the QST Regulation), supplies of subtitle transcription and AI voice-dubbing services to non-residents are zero-rated where the recipient is not resident in Canada, the service is not supplied to a person resident in Canada, and the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Digital delivery through online platforms to foreign customers qualifies if supported by written contracts, non-resident address/evidence, geolocation or server logs, and payment origination outside Canada.

  • Service agreement and statement of work with non-resident recipient and territorial licensing terms
  • Tax invoice expressly denominated as zero-rated for GST/HST and QST with 0% applied
  • Delivery and usage logs (server metadata, IP addresses, time-stamped data exports)
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/FX settlement report
  • Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading only if physical deliverables are exported; not applicable to pure digital subtitle delivery

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-authorisation, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Canada/Quebec. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices and records provided the format is legible, complete, and preserved under the statutory record requirements.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess the limited partnership and its partners to reverse zero-rating where evidence is insufficient, requiring repayment of uncollected GST/HST/QST plus compound interest. Civil penalties include gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understatement, escalation up to 100% for fraudulent refund claims, and fines for tax evasion; clawback periods generally extend four years (ten years for misrepresentation or gross negligence), with criminal liability possible for intentional fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts; there is no exchange-control approval regime in Canada. Non-resident capital contributions to a Quebec limited partnership, operating cash flows, and profit remittances may proceed freely in CAD or foreign currency. The practical frictions are tax-driven and AML-driven rather than currency-control-driven, namely Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, Quebec and federal tax registrations, transfer-pricing documentation, and FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The limited partnership may retain, hold, or convert foreign currency export earnings at its discretion, subject to accounting presentation, functional-currency consistency, and the realization of foreign-exchange gains or losses for Canadian income-tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange and money-services business oversight is shared with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Quebec deposit-taking institutions are additionally supervised by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for collections and small-value disbursements; Low as the primary treasury or capital-holding vehicle. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (s.96) and the Quebec Taxation Act; the partnership itself pays no income tax. Where a comparable taxable Canadian corporation operates the same AI subtitle/post-production business, the combined federal-Quebec headline CIT rate is 26.5% (federal general rate 15% after abatement and general reduction, plus Quebec general corporate rate 11.5%). The LP’s income is allocated to its partners and taxed at their respective marginal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Because the entity is a Limited Partnership, distributions to partners are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding. If the operating assets are owned by a taxable Canadian corporation, the statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 25% under s.212(2) ITA, reduced by applicable treaties: generally 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of the voting shares under the Canada-United States tax treaty. The LP structure avoids Quebec dividend WHT entirely at the partnership level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under s.247 ITA is legally expected where the aggregate value of transactions between the LP and each non-resident related party exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal period. Annual Master File and Local File documentation, including a benchmarking study using the approved methods, must be prepared before the partnership’s filing due date and provided to CRA within three months of a written request. A country-by-country report (Form RC4649) is mandatory where the ultimate parent’s consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000; below this threshold, a simplified documentation regime applies but the CAD 1 million transaction threshold still triggers TP penalties for deliberate mispricing.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no ESR certificate regime analogous to the UAE’s Economic Substance Regulations; however, the Income Tax Act s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the Quebec Taxation Act’s general anti-abuse doctrine, and judicial commercial-substance principles require the LP to carry on a real business with employees, contracts, office infrastructure, and risk. A Quebec limited partnership whose only activity is the passive receipt of AI royalties or interposed invoicing between a foreign parent and Canadian clients will be re-characterized by the CRA as a conduit; the GAAR can disallow the tax benefits, reattribute the income to the beneficial owner, and expose the general partner to liability as the person carrying on the business. The Civil Code of Quebec also requires a limited partnership to be registered in the Quebec Enterprise Registry and to maintain its registered office in Quebec.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2) ITA; a joint penalty with the partnership of 50% on the income misallocated; criminal conviction under s.239(1) ITA attracts a fine not exceeding 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; Revenu Quebec imposes an equivalent 50% penalty plus costs and criminal fines up to double the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the LP is fully, personally, jointly, and severally liable for the LP’s tax obligations under Quebec law; directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted payroll withholdings, GST/QST, and source deductions under s.227.1 ITA; criminal conviction can trigger prohibition orders restricting international travel and, in cross-border cases, extradition under the Canada-U.S. Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty.
Shell Company and Sham Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Quebec apply the GAAR or the sham transaction doctrine to disregard the LP, reattribute revenue to the foreign legal entity with a Canadian permanent establishment, disallow expense deductions, and assess 50% civil penalties under s.245; if fraudulent documents are used, a fine of up to CAD 50,000 or 200% of tax avoided applies under Quebec consumer and tax statutes.

Director personal liability: The veil is pierced where the corporate general partner is undercapitalised or merely acting as an agent; the general partner’s directors face joint and several liability for tax debts of the LP, civil restitution of misappropriated partnership funds, and criminal liability for fraudulent concealment, with court-ordered travel bans and imprisonment of up to 10 years under the Criminal Code for fraud over CAD 5,000.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment equal to the full difference between the arm’s-length price and the declared price under s.247(2); a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment under s.247(3); if gross negligence, an additional 50% penalty; unpaid Part XIII withholding on the recharacterised royalty and daily interest charges (currently 5% for federal, plus Quebec daily interest at a prescribed rate) are assessable.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner who approved the intercompany pricing contract are jointly liable for the resulting tax and penalties if the LP is insolvent; immigration and tax courts can prohibit directors from leaving Canada while assessments are outstanding, and continued non-compliance can lead to contempt proceedings and imprisonment for failing to produce documentation.
Money Laundering and Unregistered Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 250,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for entities for non-compliance; knowingly laundering proceeds of crime violates Criminal Code s.462.31 and carries imprisonment of up to 10 years; FINTRAC can recommend prosecution, and all profits are subject to forfeiture.

Director personal liability: A director or general partner who launders partnership funds is personally liable for civil restitution, forfeiture of assets under the Criminal Code, travel bans pending trial, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where funds are transferred to personal accounts or to the non-resident limited partner, thereby collapsing limited liability protection under the Civil Code of Quebec.
