Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A corporation must maintain a registered office address in the applicable jurisdiction. An Ontario corporation requires a registered office in Ontario; a federal corporation requires a registered office in Canada and must comply with federal registered-office and records-office requirements. A commercial virtual office may be used if it is a genuine physical address, accepts service of process, maintains required corporate records or provides compliant records access, and satisfies registry and financial-institution verification requirements. A post-office box alone is insufficient. For deep-sea Ro-Ro shipping, a virtual office is not adequate as the sole evidence of commercial substance because the business requires demonstrable management, chartering, vessel-compliance, safety, customs, sanctions, insurance, port, and operational arrangements. The company should maintain Canadian management records, contracts, accounting records, banking controls, responsible personnel, and documented operating arrangements proportionate to its activities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — administered via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Step 1: Determine whether the corporation has a permanent establishment in Canada, employs personnel, or derives Canadian-source revenue triggering a Canadian tax presence. Step 2: Register through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) accompanied by the corporation’s certificate of incorporation, foreign registration documentation, and a certified copy of a power of attorney if using a representative. Step 3: Apply to add a GST/HST account to the BN through My Business Account if taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 per calendar year or if voluntary registration is electable. Step 4: For exporters, separately apply for a CBSA importer/exporter account (RM number) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal to file export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. A business corporation is taxed as an entity separate from its shareholders, and its classification follows domestic statutory rules under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. Foreign entity classification is determined by CRA administrative policy, not elective. Relevant elections are limited to partnership designation under ITA subsection 96(1), joint venture elections, and GST/HST elections such as section 242 for non-resident rebate treatment; none of these alter the entity-level taxation default for a Canadian or provincial business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies derived from Canadian sources (the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA subsection 240(1); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is effective on a prospective basis).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of a service of transporting goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada is zero-rated. This covers ocean Ro-Ro carriage of motor vehicles exported from Canada to foreign destinations. Directly connected services supplied by the carrier (e.g., port handling, terminal access, security and marine documentation) are generally zero-rated as part of the principal export freight service. To claim the 0% rate, the registrant must maintain evidence that (i) the supply is rendered in the course of a commercial activity, (ii) the transportation is from Canada to a non-Canadian destination, and (iii) the goods are actually exported and not subsequently returned to Canada without further importation.

  • Canada Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or electronic ERS submission) showing the Ro-Ro cargo value and destination
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (port-to-port or through bill) evidencing vehicle quantities, vessel name, load port in Canada, and discharge port outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and cargo packing/vehicle list
  • ACI eManifest Cargo Control Number (CCN) confirmation with proof of cargo release by CBSA
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire/foreign exchange records evidencing payment by a non-resident counterparty

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are permitted and recognized. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5 million, and GST/HST registrants without a permanent establishment in Canada are generally required to use CARM or designated reporting channels. For exports, CBSA mandates electronic submission of the Canada Export Declaration (Form B13A) or its ERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) equivalent for all non-regulated exports valued above CAD 2,000, and marine cargo requires an ACI eManifest with a Cargo Control Number (CCN) prior to vessel arrival or departure.

CRA and CBSA maintain joint audit programs targeting exported goods and zero-rated ocean transport. If the exporter or carrier fails to produce the mandated customs documentation, custody/control evidence, or foreign payment proof within days of a request, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 13% HST and claw back any input tax credits claimed, with compound interest. Penalties include: a 6% per annum penalty on net tax arrears; a disregard-of-liability penalty under ETA subsection 280(1) equal to 5% of the tax amount, increased to 10% for repeated failures; gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided; and criminal prosecution for willful misrepresentation under the Excise Tax Act and the Income Tax Act (fines and imprisonment). The 180-day FX repatriation expectation is a CRA administrative best practice; absence of repatriation within that period triggers heightened documentation review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar is fully freely convertible for both current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, freight, port disbursements, brokerage, crew wages) and capital-account transactions (equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, shareholder advances, profit repatriation). There is no prior central-bank or exchange-control permit for inward capital injection or outward profit remittance. A Canadian corporation may pay dividends to a foreign shareholder only after Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at a statutory rate of 25%, which is reduced by applicable tax treaties. Council prior approval is not required. However, cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the Canadian financial institution, and the remittance narrative must be accurate so the payment is not recharacterized as a dividend or taxable benefit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, forced conversion, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio requirements. The Ontario RoRo corporation may lawfully retain USD/EUR revenues in a foreign-currency operating account or a foreign bank account. For Canadian income tax purposes, revenue and foreign-exchange gains/losses are translated at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or another reasonable rate. Trade documentation such as charter party, bills of lading, commercial invoices, bank statements, and SWIFT messages must be retained for at least six years. Deep-sea RoRo transportation services are zero-rated for GST/HST under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. No customs export declaration is required for the transportation service itself, but cargo manifests and port-clearance documents must be kept for bank and sanctions compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) is the central monetary authority; the Department of Finance Canada, through the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act, administers official foreign-exchange reserves. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administrative monopoly. OSFI supervises Canadian banks and FINTRAC is the financial intelligence unit for cross-border fund movement and AML/ATF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI/e-money institutions are not permitted to serve as a primary corporate clearing account in Canada unless registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and supervised by FINTRAC. A foreign EMI without Canadian registration cannot legally clear CAD or receive CAD settlement from a Canadian Schedule I bank. Canadian banks will reject or heightened-review credits originating from unregistered offshore EMI providers. For the RoRo treasury, a fintech EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment aggregator for non-Canadian operating disbursements, after verifying that the EMI has valid FINTRAC registration and appropriate sanctions controls; it must never hold vessel charter proceeds or escrowed capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a full SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign parent or shareholder directly into the Canadian corporate operational or escrow account. The beneficiary bank must be a Canadian Schedule I bank; the receiving branch should be the designated Ontario treasury branch. In SWIFT field 70, clearly state 'share subscription proceeds' or 'intercompany shareholder loan — CA_CORP-2025'; attach the corporate resolution and reference number. The Canadian bank will file a FINTRAC EFT report for any wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; no pre-approval from the Bank of Canada or Ontario government is required. If the capital injection is an intercompany loan, execute a written shareholder loan agreement, charge at least the Canadian prescribed-rate interest, and maintain evidence that the loan proceeds are used for working capital, vessel charter, or capital expenditures, so that Canada Revenue Agency does not recharacterize the transfer as a deemed dividend or apply the thin-capitalization restriction.Use SWIFT MT103/MT202 through the Canadian bank's global transaction services for operating disbursements such as bunkers, port fees, stevedoring, towing, canal tolls, crew agency, and freight commissions. High-value USD wires are cleared via the bank's U.S. correspondent through Fedwire or CHIPS; Canadian-dollar high-value payments are cleared through Payments Canada's Large-Value Transfer System (LVTS). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (per transaction type). Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse rules. Economic substance is required to claim treaty benefits; shell companies without real business activity may be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1) if they failed to withhold/remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of tax, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided under GAAR (s.245).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential criminal charges for conspiracy.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply.
