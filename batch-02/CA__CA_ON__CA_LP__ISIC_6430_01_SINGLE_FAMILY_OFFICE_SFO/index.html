Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

An Ontario limited partnership must maintain the registered or principal address information required by its declaration and Ontario registration filings. A commercial virtual office may generally be used for registration and receipt of legal notices if it is a real Ontario address, the provider is authorized to receive service, records and notices are actually maintained or promptly forwarded, and the filing is not misleading. A mailbox-only address, accommodation address, or undisclosed nominee address is unsuitable. For a single-family office investing only family capital, a virtual office may be acceptable during the holding phase, subject to the bank, custodian, auditor, tax adviser, and insurance provider accepting it. The structure must not hold itself out as a portfolio manager, investment fund manager, dealer, trust company, bank, or adviser unless the applicable securities and financial-services registration requirements have been analyzed and satisfied.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-registration.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario provincial tax administration (e.g., Ontario Employer Health Tax, provincial tax credits and enforcement).

E-government filing portal

A foreign general partner or non-resident limited partner establishing an Ontario LP obtains a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online in a single one-stop filing with Ontario. The non-resident may instead file Form RC2A (Request for Preliminary Business Number) before formal registration, and the partnership (as a person for GST/HST purposes under Excise Tax Act subsection 240(2)) files Form RC1 (Request for GST/HST Registration) to open program accounts. CRA issues a 9-digit BN suffixed by two-letter program account codes (RC for income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll). The LP must annually file the T5013 Partnership Information Return to obtain the partnership return identifier. No provincial TIN is separately issued for the LP; the Ontario tax account is integrated with the federal BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign general partner or non-resident limited partner establishing an Ontario LP obtains a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online in a single one-stop filing with Ontario. The non-resident may instead file Form RC2A (Request for Preliminary Business Number) before formal registration, and the partnership (as a person for GST/HST purposes under Excise Tax Act subsection 240(2)) files Form RC1 (Request for GST/HST Registration) to open program accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity election; an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96, with income and loss allocated to partners annually. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST elections under Excise Tax Act sections 156 and 157 for supplies between closely related persons/partnerships and deemed supplies; (ii) partnership fiscal period elections under subsections 249.1(4)/(5) of the Income Tax Act to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (iii) section 85 rollover elections (Form T2057) and sections 97/98 partnership transfer elections (Form T2064) for tax-deferred contributions of property to the LP; (iv) no elective hybrid mismatch neutralization is permitted — hybrid mismatch deduction disallowances under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 to 18.6 and 20.2 apply automatically to arrangements involving the LP and foreign investors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act section 232. Mandatory registration is triggered when worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters. For a family office LP earning predominantly exempt financial-service income (dividends, interest, securities custody), taxable turnover is normally nil, so registration is generally voluntary; however, the LP must monitor fee-based management or advisory services that fall outside the exempt financial-services definition.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods and services exported from Canada is available under Parts V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Goods qualify under section 1 where the supplier exports them by commercial channel of transport and retains documentary evidence of export. Services qualify under section 5 where supplied to a non-resident person and (a) neither the service nor a part thereof is performed in Canada, or (b) the service is in connection with real property situated outside Canada or tangible personal property ordinarily situated outside Canada. Financial services to a non-resident (e.g., investment management or payment services) may be zero-rated under section 7/8 where the service relates to a transfer of funds or financial instruments and is supplied to a non-resident without a significant in-Canada performance nexus. A single-family office supplying portfolio-management services to a foreign family trust or offshore holdco must document the place of performance and non-residence of the recipient to substantiate the 0% rate; exempt supply characterization (dividend and interest flows) must be separately excluded from zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. Canada maintains no exchange-control act and imposes no currency-license, conversion-mandate, or capital-repatriation restrictions. Inbound equity contributions to an Ontario Limited Partnership are unrestricted at the FX layer. Profit remittance is likewise unrestricted, although non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on allocated Canadian-source business income reported on T5013 and, in narrow cases involving interest, rent, royalties, or distributions characterized as dividends from a 'Canadian investment fund', Part XIII withholding at 15% (treaty-reduced) or 25% may apply. Returns of contributed capital are not subject to withholding. The Investment Canada Act net-benefit review applies only to acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, not to passive family-office capital formation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian statute imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation or surrender obligation on export proceeds or any foreign-currency receipts. An Ontario LP may freely hold CAD or USD clearing balances at any Schedule I bank. The sole enforceable obligations are FINTRAC cross-border currency/monetary-instrument reporting (ECI form) and international electronic funds transfer reporting (IETR) for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, plus standard OSFI-mandated sanctions screening at the receiving and correspondent banks.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential and foreign-exchange supervision delegated to OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); AML/financial-intelligence enforcement via FINTRAC; payment-system infrastructure governed by Payments Canada (LVTS and Real-Time Rail).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU-style EMI licence. Cross-border fintechs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) operate only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses under the Retail Payment Activities Act on a payment-facilitation basis and are prohibited from deposit-taking; they cannot serve as a primary treasury, custody, or CDIC-insured banking venue for a single-family-office LP. The Bank Act reserves deposit business for authorized Schedule I/II banks, so an EMI purporting to offer deposit accounts to an Ontario LP would breach federal banking law.

Cross-border fintechs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) operate only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses under the Retail Payment Activities Act on a payment-facilitation basis and are prohibited from deposit-taking; they cannot serve as a primary treasury, custody, or CDIC-insured banking venue for a single-family-office LP. The Bank Act reserves deposit business for authorized Schedule I/II banks, so an EMI purporting to offer deposit accounts to an Ontario LP would breach federal banking law.
