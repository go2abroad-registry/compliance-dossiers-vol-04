Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners may contribute capital, property, or services pursuant to the partnership agreement; no escrow or deposit account is required by Quebec law.

Funding & unlock path: Office lease must evidence building compliance with the Quebec Building Act (CQLR c B-1.1) and the National Building Code; a fire inspection certificate must be obtained from the municipal Service de sécurité incendie; the partnership must register with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST), post a workplace first-aid notice, and adopt a health and safety policy where more than 20 workers are employed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners may contribute capital, property, or services pursuant to the partnership agreement; no escrow or deposit account is required by Quebec law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and income tax administration; Revenu Québec (RQ) - Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial income tax administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec Clavier SOLIDES.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal business number and GST/HST program account by registering with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC348. For Quebec, the partnership must separately register for a QST account with Revenu Québec using Form LM-101-V or online via Clavier SOLIDES. The registration must be done in the partnership's legal name and requires designation of an agent or representative in Canada for service and tax administration purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal business number and GST/HST program account by registering with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC348. For Quebec, the partnership must separately register for a QST account with Revenu Québec using Form LM-101-V or online via Clavier SOLIDES. The registration must be done in the partnership's legal name and requires designation of an agent or representative in Canada for service and tax administration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no federal check-the-box election; an LP is pass-through for tax purposes unless it elects to be treated as a corporation under specific circumstances not generally available. Relevant elections include: deemed fiscal year-end election under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act for foreign partnerships; GST/HST elections under section 177 of the Excise Tax Act respecting closely related entities; QST equivalents under the Taxation Act; hybrid mismatch arrangement deductions are denied under section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act, with corresponding rules in Quebec.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies arising from carrying on business in Canada for GST/HST; CAD 30,000 in annual taxable turnover in Quebec for QST, determined on a quarterly or trailing-four-quarter basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act when exported in the ordinary course of the supplier's business under section 22 of Schedule VI. Medical device regulatory consulting services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI where the services are not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of supply, or services specifically excluded by the Act. The supplier must maintain documentary evidence proving the recipient is a non-resident and, if applicable, that the service was performed outside Canada or supplied for consumption outside Canada. Equivalent zero-rating principles apply under Quebec's QST system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CUS-50 or eManifest/ACI export report)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation evidencing foreign currency settlement

Canada does not mandate continuous real-time e-invoice clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or API pre-clearance. GST/HST and QST returns must be filed electronically via CRA and Revenu Québec portals. All invoices and supporting records must be retained in Canada for audit purposes, and prescribed electronic record-keeping requirements apply under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, with no time limit in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, fraud, or wilful default. Denial of zero-rating results in reassessment of GST/HST plus interest and penalties. Penalties include: failure to provide required evidence under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, false statements or omissions under section 285.1, and gross negligence penalties. Fraudulent export zero-rating can attract penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal prosecution, and equivalent QST reassessments, interest, and penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII status. There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital, and no requirement for prior approval on cross-border transfers. Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible through authorized financial institutions. Payment of management fees, royalties, or interest to non-residents may attract non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief; profit allocations to non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership are generally not subject to withholding unless characterized as a distribution of Canadian-source income subject to tax. No significant profit remittance hurdles beyond standard anti-money laundering verification and accurate tax reporting forms (i.e., NR4, T106, Part XIII remittances).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any permitted currency, held in domestic or offshore accounts, and converted at market rates at the discretion of the exporter. The Bank of Canada and the Canada Revenue Agency do not administer export surrender requirements. Reporting obligations only arise under FINTRAC for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 or as otherwise required under cross-border currency reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with legal constraints. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding no Canadian license cannot offer payment services to Canadian residents without either registering as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC or obtaining authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) where applicable. Offshore EMIs may be used solely as non-regulated technical rails for foreign currency collections, but Canadian corporates handling cross-border payments for internal treasury purposes require either a domestic Schedule I bank account, a Canadian MSB license, or reliance on a licensed payment service provider. Strict OSTP compliance, KYC data residency expectations, and banking de-risking policies increase friction. Recommended approach: combine Tier 1 Canadian bank accounts for primary clearing and a regulated Canadian MSB/EMI subsidiary for card-accepting flows. Viability rating: Conditional.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate headline rate (federal Part I 15.0% + Quebec Part IV 11.5%). A Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: the entity does not pay income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocable share of Canadian and Quebec-source income at the partner's own rate (corporate or individual). Top combined Quebec marginal individual rate is 53.31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (ITA s.212) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; for a limited partnership, a distribution or allocation of Canadian-source partnership income to a non-resident partner is subject to the same 25% gross-basis Part XIII withholding, unless waived.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than the last day of the fifth month after the partnership fiscal year-end (ITA s.229); for a December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing deadline is May 31 of the next calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as federal: the CO-17 is due no later than the last day of the fifth month after the fiscal year-end (Quebec Taxation Act, s.1011 et seq.); for a December 31 year-end, the deadline is May 31.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 (Information Return on Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 25,000 in the fiscal year; CRA contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is administratively expected when aggregate controlled transactions reach CAD 10,000,000, and a Master File aligns with OECD Action 13 for groups with consolidated gross revenues of at least CAD 50,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting threshold is CAD 750,000,000; s.247(4) penalty protection requires reasonable efforts and supporting documentation regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); substance is nevertheless mandatory through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), transfer-pricing arm's-length recharacterization (ITA s.247), treaty anti-abuse principles, and the permanent-establishment concept (Art. V Canada–US DTA). A Quebec LP used for FDA/CE consulting must maintain an active head office in Quebec, employ or contract substantive personnel, conduct actual client-facing regulatory work in Canada, and avoid circular payments merely to route income to non-resident partners; otherwise CRA may disregard the partnership or reallocate income to the general partner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Blindness

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years upon indictment (2 years upon summary conviction); civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s.163.2 of up to 50% of the misstatement amount; Revenu Québec mirrors the same evasion offense under the Quebec Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be personally assessed for unremitted corporate taxes and withholdings (ITA s.227.15), directors' liability certificates are registrable in federal and Quebec courts, and CRA may issue departure clearances or travel bans under the Income Tax Act and Quebec Tax Administration Act where substantial tax debt remains outstanding.
