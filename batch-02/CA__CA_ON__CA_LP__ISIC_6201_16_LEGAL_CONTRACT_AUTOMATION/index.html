Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Federal Government of Canada; registration and filing portal: CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online (BRO), and GST/HST NETFILE; Ontario Ministry of Finance applies only for non-HST provincial levies, with HST administered federally by CRA.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 through Business Registration Online. The partnership must register for a GST/HST account under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) if it is not a small supplier (total taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters). If the non-resident partnership has no substantial presence in Canada, it must apply to CRA for authorization of a deemed resident representative under ETA section 484 to receive notices and discharge obligations. Each partner separately includes partnership income in their own Canadian tax filing; the LP itself does not obtain a separate income TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada. A Canadian limited partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; no election can convert it to separate entity taxation. Limited partnership fiscal-year election may be made under Income Tax Act subsection 220(4.1) to establish a non-calendar taxation year. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership may elect to file as part of a closely related group under ETA subsection 273, and may also make section 167 (sale of business) elections. Hybrid mismatch rules are addressed under the Act's general anti-avoidance rule and OECD BEPS recommendations; no elective hybrid mismatch regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; foreign suppliers must monitor Canadian-source supply volumes to determine mandatory registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST when the supplier transfers possession or ownership to a non-resident and exports them directly. Services, including LegalTech SaaS and e-signature authentication, qualify for 0% where the service is supplied to a non-resident person and the service is performed wholly or partly outside Canada, provided any Canadian performance does not relate to real property located in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services delivered to a person in Canada. Intangible property such as software licenses is zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and the end-use of the property occurs outside Canada. Evidence must demonstrate that the customer is a non-resident and that the place of consumption is outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CBSA Export Declaration in EDAS/CCI).
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing foreign currency receipt.
  • Customer contract and commercial invoice referencing zero-rated supply.
  • SaaS service delivery logs, IP geolocation records, and server access evidence.
  • Written acknowledgement from the foreign customer confirming non-residency and offshore end-use.

No national continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice clearance API, or digital stamping mandate applies in Ontario. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed information on invoices and maintain complete books and records in accordance with ETA section 286. Electronic invoices are accepted; CRA uses risk-based audits and data-matching rather than real-time clearance. Mandatory electronic filing applies for GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE, E-file, or third-party software APIs for registrants, but no pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

CRA may audit zero-rating claims and reassess within the normal four-year statutory period, or longer in the case of misrepresentation. If a claim is disallowed, the partnership is liable for unremitted GST/HST at the applicable rate, plus interest and arrears penalties. Civil penalties under ETA section 280 can apply for false statements or omissions, potentially up to 25% of gross negligence tax. Knowingly participating in fraudulent export zero-rating exposes the partnership to fines up to CAD 100,000 for a first conviction and imprisonment for up to two years under ETA section 327. Retrospectively denied refunds are clawed back in full, and future zero-rating eligibility may be suspended or subjected to higher audit burden.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is a jurisdiction without foreign-exchange control legislation. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both capital and current account, and Canada fully observes the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There are no exchange-control approvals required for remitting profits, dividends, partnership distributions, or repatriating capital to non-residents, provided the underlying transaction is not a money-laundering or sanctions violation. Banking transactions in USD or other convertible currencies are permitted within Canadian chartered banks. Practical hurdles are limited to FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting (reportable at CAD 10,000 within 24 hours per transaction or series) and OSFI/FINTRAC sanctions screening. Profit distributions from a Canadian limited partnership to a non-resident partner are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (typically 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty for a US corporate partner, unless a reduced rate applies); capital account repatriation as a non-taxable return of capital is generally not subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Exporters and service suppliers (including LegalTech SaaS providers) are unconditionally entitled to retain export receivables and SaaS subscription revenue in foreign currency, provided such funds are held in a foreign-currency account at a regulated Canadian financial institution and any cross-border wire exceeding CAD 10,000 is reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation; FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting; the Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account and foreign-currency reserve policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium. A Canadian limited partnership may lawfully use licensed fintech payment processors and payment service providers (Stripe, Wise, PayPal, Payoneer) as embedded treasury rails for SaaS subscription collection and cross-border disbursements, but they are not substitutes for a regulated banking account and cannot hold the entity's core treasury liquidity. Foreign EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated or EU/EMD-regulated electronic money institutions) have no passporting rights in Canada and are not authorized to maintain deposit accounts or issue electronic money to Canadian-resident entities without entering the Canadian retail payments framework. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), effective November 2024, payment service providers operating in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada; a foreign EMI processing Canadian payments on behalf of the LP must be RPAA-registered or operate through a registered processor. Stripe (via Stripe Canada) and Wise (as an authorized Canadian payment service provider) are the recommended fintech rails for the stated target rails (Stripe, Credit Card), while the daily operating and escrow treasury remains at a Schedule I chartered bank. The LP's contractual flow should ensure that customer credit-card payments via Stripe settle into the operating account within standard settlement cycles (typically 2 business days for CAD/USD settlement).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For partner capital contributions inbound from non-resident limited partners to the Ontario LP's CAD/USD account at a Schedule I bank: use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code matching Canada's Balance of Payments and Statistics Canada IGD classification — code 7001 (direct investment - equity in enterprise) or 'partnership capital contribution' as accepted by the receiving bank; include in field 70 'REMITTANCE INFO' the capital contribution agreement reference, LP name, and capital account allocation. Contribution wires from foreign fiat into CAD require the bank's dealing room to execute a spot trade; the corresponding CAD credit is placed into the Capital Escrow Account. All inbound wires exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC under large-transaction reporting rules; banks also screen the OFAC and Canadian sanctions lists. Where the contribution originates in USD from a US limited partner, the bank may route over its US clearing network (RBC US, TD Bank US, BMO Harris) and book the USD without a forced CAD conversion; the capital account records the contribution at the agreed exchange rate or USD book value per the LP agreement. For large injections exceeding CAD 50,000 equivalent, direct SWIFT with a documentary capital contribution agreement is mandatory to avoid an AML inquiry; 24-48 hours clearing time is standard, with enhanced KYC where the sending bank is in a high-risk jurisdiction.Domestic vendor and payroll disbursements: use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) EFT/batch payments and Interac e-Transfer for expenses under CAD 3,000; payroll via the bank's payroll service or ACSS pre-authorized debit. High-value CAD disbursements exceeding CAD 100,000 equivalent: route via Lynx (Canada's high-value RTGS system, operated by Payments Canada) directly or by instructing the bank in a CAD wire. For outbound cross-border trade settlements to SaaS infrastructure vendors, licensors, and subcontractors: use SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) for single disbursements; commercial letters of credit (LC) and documentary collections are used only where the counterparty requires banking-grade trade settlement, which is atypical for LegalTech licensing but permissible under Ontario law. Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR), when fully operational, provides 24/7/365 instant CAD settlement; Stripe payouts and credit-card receivables settle via the Visa/Mastercard networks and Stripe's Canadian acquiring bank — settlement to the LP's operating account occurs in CAD (net of processing fees) typically T+2. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium. A Canadian limited partnership may lawfully use licensed fintech payment processors and payment service providers (Stripe, Wise, PayPal, Payoneer) as embedded treasury rails for SaaS subscription collection and cross-border disbursements, but they are not substitutes for a regulated banking account and cannot hold the entity's core treasury liquidity. Foreign EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated or EU/EMD-regulated electronic money institutions) have no passporting rights in Canada and are not authorized to maintain deposit accounts or issue electronic money to Canadian-resident entities without entering the Canadian retail payments framework. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), effective November 2024, payment service providers operating in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada; a foreign EMI processing Canadian payments on behalf of the LP must be RPAA-registered or operate through a registered processor. Stripe (via Stripe Canada) and Wise (as an authorized Canadian payment service provider) are the recommended fintech rails for the stated target rails (Stripe, Credit Card), while the daily operating and escrow treasury remains at a Schedule I chartered bank. The LP's contractual flow should ensure that customer credit-card payments via Stripe settle into the operating account within standard settlement cycles (typically 2 business days for CAD/USD settlement).
