Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is in full compliance with IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar is freely floating, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without exchange control approval or profit-remittance restrictions. An Ontario Limited Partnership may freely receive capital contributions from non-resident partners, service cross-border payables and remit profits abroad. The sole regulatory impediment is tax-related: distributions of income to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., 15% under the Canada-US Treaty), with NR4 reporting and timely remittance by the partnership. Capital distributions and returns of contributed capital are generally not subject to withholding. No domestic licence or exchange-control permit is required to operate the matcha procurement and distribution business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished formal exchange controls in the early 1950s and never reintroduced statutorily mandated surrender or conversion rules. There is no requirement to convert export receipts or retained foreign currency within a fixed timeline; the Ontario LP may freely hold, deposit, convert or remit Japanese Yen, US Dollars or any other fully convertible currency generated from its cross-border distribution trade.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is otherwise exercised by the Minister of Finance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering, cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting, and sanctions-related compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services businesses (e.g., Wise Canada, Payoneer) can support low-value trade remittances and offer faster cross-border settlement, but they require the same full KYC onboarding and are subject to the CAD 10,000 international EFT reporting threshold. A foreign EMI without a Canadian MSB registration or a Canadian place of business may not lawfully solicit Canadian clients, and using an unregulated offshore EMI is inadvisable for the matcha trade because it creates an adverse audit trail and sanctions-screening gaps. For transactions above CAD 10,000, or where a commercial letter of credit is required, a Schedule I bank channel with SWIFT MT103 and LVTS/gpi routing is legally more certain. Recommended architecture: domestic bank as the cash-management host and sole capital injection account; a FINTRAC-registered EMI used only for sub-CAD 10,000 ancillary payments if necessary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident partner capital injections is via SWIFT MT103 worldwide or Fedwire for US-dollar contributions cleared through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent. Canadian SWIFT/BICs for major accounts include ROYCCAT2 (RBC), TDOMCATT (TD), NOSCCATT (Scotiabank) and BOFMCAM2 (BMO). Inbound wire instructions must identify the LP name, full account number, and in field 72/77B the clear purpose: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [LP NAME] - LIMITED PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]'. Because Canada maintains no exchange controls, no pre-approval or capital-import licence is required; the receiving bank will automatically report the inward international EFT to FINTRAC for any transfer equal to or greater than CAD 10,000. On receipt, the treasury function must verify the wire against the partner's subscribed capital schedule under the LP agreement, issue the corresponding capital account statement, and maintain an escrow ledger that separates contributed capital from operating cash to demonstrate the fungible flow of funds to the bank's compliance team.Primary outbound rail is SWIFT MT103 for high-value settle-up payments to Japanese matcha manufacturers and wholesale exporters, executed through the bank's correspondent network (typically JPY clearing via MUFG Bank, Mizuho or Sumitomo Mitsui; US-dollar settlement via the bank's New York branch or Fedwire). For recurring procurement exceeding USD 25,000 per shipment, the recommended rail is a Commercial Letter of Credit (sight payment) issued by the Canadian bank and advised/confirmed by a Japanese correspondent bank, with documents comprising the commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, and laboratory certificate of analysis negotiated against a clean transport document. Documentary collections (CAD/DP terms) are an acceptable fallback for smaller shipments under an established supplier relationship. All outbound disbursements must be paired to a purchase order and supplier invoice in the bank's file, and each international wire is subject to FINTRAC reporting if equal to or above CAD 10,000. For urgent cross-border low-value settlement below CAD 10,000, the bank's SWIFT gpi or a FINTRAC-registered EMI can be used; however, the primary instruction for the matcha trade is to run all payments above the threshold exclusively through the Schedule I bank and to maintain a rolling outward wire register with matching pre-advice (cover payment) to the beneficiary's bank.Outright FX forwards (CAD/JPY and USD/JPY) executed with a Schedule I bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex, with tenors of up to 12 months matching the inventory procurement cycleFX options (plain-vanilla European call/put structures) on JPY allowing the LP to cap the worst-case purchase rate without locking a firm rate, subject to general-partner board approvalCross-currency swaps (USD/CAD and USD/JPY) to convert partner capital injected in USD or JPY into CAD operational funding, and later to re-denominate trade payables when commodity prices shift materiallyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs), while technically permissible for Canadian counterparties under negotiation of an ISDA agreement, are operationally unnecessary for CAD/JPY because both currencies are fully deliverable and freely convertible; they are reserved solely for emerging-market currency exposure if a supplier chain expands beyond JapanMulti-currency operating accounts (natural hedge) allowing the LP to hold core JPY and USD working balances to avoid forced conversion at each settlement, thereby reducing transaction costs and aligning with the 60-90 day payable cycle of the matcha trade

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services businesses (e.g., Wise Canada, Payoneer) can support low-value trade remittances and offer faster cross-border settlement, but they require the same full KYC onboarding and are subject to the CAD 10,000 international EFT reporting threshold. A foreign EMI without a Canadian MSB registration or a Canadian place of business may not lawfully solicit Canadian clients, and using an unregulated offshore EMI is inadvisable for the matcha trade because it creates an adverse audit trail and sanctions-screening gaps. For transactions above CAD 10,000, or where a commercial letter of credit is required, a Schedule I bank channel with SWIFT MT103 and LVTS/gpi routing is legally more certain. Recommended architecture: domestic bank as the cash-management host and sole capital injection account; a FINTRAC-registered EMI used only for sub-CAD 10,000 ancillary payments if necessary.
