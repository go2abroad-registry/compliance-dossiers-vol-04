Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) at the registered office seat; federal supervision by the Bureau of the Federal Commercial Register (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt, EHRA) within the Federal Department of Justice and Police.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Arts. 629-632 (incorporation, share capital, paid-up minimum), Art. 627 (mandatory contents of articles), Arts. 716 ff. Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified public incorporation deed (öffentliche Urkunde), followed by the electronic application for entry in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister) filed by the notary through the official portal (EasyGov.swiss / the competent cantonal commercial register portal).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The share capital (Aktienkapital) must be at least CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash before registration; paid-up funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) in the name of the future company, and the bank's written confirmation of free disposal must be annexed to the deed; non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) require a licensed contribution audit report.

Funding & unlock path: Cantonal fire police (Feuerpolizei) certification covering fire-extinguishing equipment and storage of flammable polymerization agents; structural load adequacy certificate for casting benches and resin storage racks; workplace occupational safety clearance by SUVA and cantonal labor inspectorate under the Federal Labor Act (ArG) and ArGV 3, including ventilation, personal protective equipment for sensitizing epoxy systems, and first-aid readiness; civil defense shelter obligations apply only above statutory gross-floor-area thresholds and are inapplicable to a small casting workshop.

Paid-in capital rule: The share capital (Aktienkapital) must be at least CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash before registration; paid-up funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) in the name of the future company, and the bank's written confirmation of free disposal must be annexed to the deed; non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) require a licensed contribution audit report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC); central e-portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and UID issuance via the central business register (Zefix) and the FTA VAT unit.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft in the cantonal commercial register (Handelsregister) with minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000 (at least CHF 50,000 paid up); non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2: The commercial register office notifies the Swiss Federal Statistical Office, which issues the permanent UID business identification number in the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX. Step 3: Apply to the FTA VAT unit through the ESTV online VAT registration service to obtain the Swiss VAT number (the UID followed by MWST, TVA, or IVA). Step 4: A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office must apply directly to the ESTV VAT unit when Swiss-source taxable turnover arises, and must appoint a Swiss VAT representative under Art. 67 MWSTG where no Swiss place of business exists. Step 5: Cantonal and communal tax registration occurs automatically through the UID data flow; the AG must file annual Swiss federal and cantonal corporate tax returns (e.g., in the canton of Zug).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid entity election; the AG is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer for Swiss federal, cantonal, and communal corporate income tax, Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer at 35%), and Swiss VAT. Available statutory elections include: (1) VAT group election under Art. 12 MWSTG consolidating Swiss-resident group entities as a single taxable person; (2) election between treaty-based exemption and foreign tax credit for foreign permanent-establishment income; (3) optional cantonal STAF incentive elections (patent box, R&D super-deduction, notional interest deduction) where the relevant canton has enacted them; (4) participation relief for qualifying dividends and capital gains applies automatically and is not elective. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Swiss Anti-Hybrid Law (BEPS Action 2 implementation, effective 1 January 2020) operate non-electronically, denying deductions for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid transfers, and disregarded permanent establishments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 150,000 annual taxable turnover (effective 1 January 2025; previously CHF 100,000). Resident entrepreneurs measure worldwide turnover from taxable activities; non-resident entrepreneurs measure Swiss-source supplies. Distance-selling and e-commerce platform deemed-supplier rules may attribute VAT liability to electronic interfaces in cross-border e-commerce, and import VAT continues to apply at the Swiss border on low-value consignments.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold by the Swiss AG and physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination qualify for the 0% VAT rate, provided the goods leave Swiss customs territory on or before delivery and are not consumed or used in Switzerland. For online tabletop-game dice sales via Kickstarter/Stripe and PayPal, the AG must evidence export through the Swiss customs exit declaration plus independent transport documentation (post/courier tracking, bill of lading, CMR, or air waybill). Custom casting, design, and related services supplied to foreign recipients are zero-rated or outside Swiss VAT scope according to place-of-supply rules. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports remains fully recoverable. If export substantiation is deficient, the supply is recharacterised as domestic taxable at the standard rate and input VAT is adjusted.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (e-dec exit declaration; additional NCTS/ECS electronic transit declaration for EU-bound movements)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, Air Waybill, or registered post/courier shipment record with tracking evidence (e.g., DHL/PostPilot pickup confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export confirmation / proof of physical exit (customs-stamped or electronically verified exit confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit evidence (required where trade finance or Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal settlement documentation demands proof of receipt; no Swiss exchange-control certificate is statutorily mandated)

Switzerland does not, as of 2025, operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and must satisfy Swiss VAT content requirements under Art. 26 MWSTG; the standardised QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is used for payment slips but is not a fiscal clearance device. VAT-relevant records, including invoices, export declarations, and proof-of-dispatch evidence, must be retained for 10 years. For export transactions, electronic customs processing via e-dec and, for EU-transit movements, NCTS/ECS is mandatory, with physical exit confirmation.

The FTA audits export substantiation in routine and risk-based VAT reviews. If zero-rating is claimed without complete customs and transport evidence, the FTA retrospectively reassesses VAT at 8.1% on the export consideration, charges statutory default interest from the original due date, and may impose civil fines under Arts. 84, 86, and 87 MWSTG: up to four times the evaded tax for intentional evasion, up to twice the evaded tax for negligence, up to CHF 30,000 for serious procedural contraventions, and up to CHF 8,000 for minor infractions. Aggravated export-documentation fraud may trigger criminal prosecution under Art. 85 MWSTG with custodial penalties or fines up to CHF 800,000. The general assessment limitation period is five years from the end of the relevant tax period, extended to ten years in cases of evasion; directors, VAT representatives, and responsible officers may be held jointly and severally liable for the clawback and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has a full free-convertibility regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The CHF is a free-floating reserve currency; the SNB may intervene in FX markets for monetary-policy purposes, but such intervention imposes no compliance burden on SMEs. Capital inflows (equity, shareholder loans, founder contributions) and capital outflows (dividends, loan repayments, royalties, trade settlements) may be executed freely and without quota, maturity, or counterparty restrictions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends, subject to reduction or exemption under double-taxation treaties (most relevant jurisdictions preserve a 0-5% treaty rate upon proper refund application), and (ii) AEOI/CRS information reporting for owners residing outside Switzerland; there is no capital-export prior license, no disposal notification, and no repatriation timeline. For a Swiss AG with crowdfunding and e-commerce receipts, all foreign-currency funds may be retained in USD, EUR, or any other freely convertible currency at any FINMA-regulated or foreign bank without monthly conversion obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement on export proceeds. The AG may keep 100% of all foreign-currency receivables (Stripe USD settlements, PayPal balances, Kickstarter payout amounts) in multi-currency bank accounts, convert them to CHF at free-market rates, or use them for direct supplier disbursements in the same currency. The only related duties are ordinary: accurate bookkeeping and VAT (MWST) records, bank-clients' source-of-funds documentation under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), and the general corporate duty to keep funds at the free disposal of the company.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank), Bern and Zurich, operating under the National Bank Act of 2003 (NBG), with domestic prudential enforcement delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). There is no separate foreign-exchange control administration; cross-border payments and FX operations are not subject to licensing, notification, or approval by any Swiss authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-High for settlement aggregation, but the AG must retain a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank as the primary treasury anchor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Multitude and similar e-money institutions) are permissible as auxiliary liquidity rails: they may receive Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal USD or EUR payouts, convert currency, and sweep proceeds to the Swiss operational account, provided the EMI applies AMLA-equivalent KYC and the funds are ultimately booked in Switzerland. Legal constraints: (i) a foreign EMI is not a FINMA-authorised bank and therefore cannot hold the statutory paid-in capital escrow, cannot issue the bank confirmation required for the commercial-register incorporation filing, and cannot provide CHF IBANs in the SIC clearing system; (ii) the use of an offshore EMI must be disclosed in the annual financial statements and presented to the Swiss bank during onboarding to avoid undisclosed-account flags; (iii) the Zug 'crypto valley' domiciliation address is not predatory by itself, but a offshore-EMI-heavy structure combined with a Zug registered office will trigger enhanced due diligence; the recommended pattern is a Swiss multi-currency account at UBS/ZKB/Swissquote as master account plus one Wise/Payoneer account used strictly for PSP payout concentration.

Compliant protocol: (1) Founders remit the subscribed share capital (minimum nominal CHF 100,000, at least CHF 50,000 paid up on incorporation) via SWIFT MT103 from the founder's OECD-bank account to the AG's capital-escrow account at UBS/ZKB/BCV; (2) the remittance-information field must state 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital / Ch: Capital Contribution' together with the notary file number; no SNB approval or purpose-code licence exists, but the source of funds must be visible from the supporting bank statement; (3) the notary issues the 'Auszahlungsbeleg' and the bank confirms funds are at free disposal, both filed with the Commercial Register; (4) subsequent shareholder loans are routed by MT103 as 'Darlehen Aktionär / Shareholder Loan', documented by a written loan agreement at arm's-length interest (0% acceptable in the 0% reference-rate corridor for small loans), and recorded to avoid reclassification as hidden equity or constructive dividend.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 for SEPA-zone suppliers) for all raw-material, mould-tooling, packaging and freight payments to China and EU counterparties, cleared in CHF via SIX SIC Interbank Clearing, in EUR via TARGET2/SEPA, and in USD via corresponding US clearing; (ii) Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700, UCP 600) for large-volume epoxy-resin or mould-sourcing contracts above CHF 50,000, with the Swiss bank as issuing bank and a second advising/confirming bank in the supplier country; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for medium-value repeat purchases; (iv) ancillary fintech rails (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) reserved for micro-supplier disbursements under CHF 5,000 per transaction to preserve KYC proportionality; (v) domestic SIC instant payments for Swiss logistics. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate-to-High for settlement aggregation, but the AG must retain a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank as the primary treasury anchor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Multitude and similar e-money institutions) are permissible as auxiliary liquidity rails: they may receive Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal USD or EUR payouts, convert currency, and sweep proceeds to the Swiss operational account, provided the EMI applies AMLA-equivalent KYC and the funds are ultimately booked in Switzerland. Legal constraints: (i) a foreign EMI is not a FINMA-authorised bank and therefore cannot hold the statutory paid-in capital escrow, cannot issue the bank confirmation required for the commercial-register incorporation filing, and cannot provide CHF IBANs in the SIC clearing system; (ii) the use of an offshore EMI must be disclosed in the annual financial statements and presented to the Swiss bank during onboarding to avoid undisclosed-account flags; (iii) the Zug 'crypto valley' domiciliation address is not predatory by itself, but a offshore-EMI-heavy structure combined with a Zug registered office will trigger enhanced due diligence; the recommended pattern is a Swiss multi-currency account at UBS/ZKB/Swissquote as master account plus one Wise/Payoneer account used strictly for PSP payout concentration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 900,000,000 group consolidated turnover (CbCR and mandatory Master File under LBBV); Local File expected for Swiss AG entities with material cross-border related-party transactions, in practice above CHF 10,000,000 per annum, per ESTV Circular 3/2007 and OECD TPG

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies no onshore Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore hubs, but treaty benefits and the participation exemption require substantive local activity; the OECD MLI principal purpose test (PPT) is incorporated into DTAs and may deny relief to shell holding or marketing AGs without local staff, office, and decision-making in Switzerland; pure domicile companies (Verwaltungsgesellschaften) must satisfy the ESTV circular on substance to retain DTA access and avoid 35% WHT exposure

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to three times the evaded federal and cantonal tax, default interest, denial of deductions, discretionary assessment with uplift; criminal proceedings under Art. 175 and 186 DBG

Director personal liability: Board members may be prosecuted personally; willful evasion can lead to custodial sentences of up to 3 years, fines, and confiscation of ill-gotten profits
Qualified Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to two times the evaded tax, compulsory criminal indictment before the cantonal criminal court, publication of conviction, and withdrawal of tax privileges

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment up to 3 years, travel restriction (Ausweissperre) for foreign nationals, and joint civil liability under Art. 754 Code of Obligations for all corporate tax damages
Withholding Tax Violation (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: The AG personally owes the full unwithheld 35% amount plus interest; late remittance surcharge of 5%, disciplinary fine up to CHF 10,000, and administrative penalty up to three times the unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable where the AG lacks assets and poor bookkeeping caused the shortfall; criminal penalty for directors who knowingly refrain from withholding under Art. 62 VStrR
Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI principal purpose test, retroactive application of 35% withholding tax on all distributions, reclassification as abusively transparent, and refusal of the participation exemption for the relevant fiscal years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for restitution of unpaid withholding tax and civil damages; asset blocking, travel bans for foreign directors, and derivative criminal liability for complicity in abusive tax arrangements
AML and Social Security Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Reimbursement of omitted social security contributions with compound interest and fines up to three times the contribution arrears; per the Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), fines up to CHF 500,000 and license restrictions, reinforced by Swiss banking secrecy waivers in due diligence breaches

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal sanctions including imprisonment up to 5 years for qualified money laundering under Art. 305bis Swiss Criminal Code, personal liability for unpaid AHV amounts, and professional disqualification orders
