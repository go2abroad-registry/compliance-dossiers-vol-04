Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ontario Business Registry (ServiceOntario), Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery — Companies and Personal Property Security Branch acting by the Director appointed under OBCA.

Governing statute

Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA), R.S.O. 1990, c. B.16, as amended — ss. 4 (incorporation), 9 (corporate name), 16-18 (Articles of Incorporation), 20 (registered office), 115-116 (notice of directors and registered office), 118 (resident Canadian directors), 124 (first meeting of directors). Option to incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44 is not elected; the Ontario-registered Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is used for Ontario-domiciled PropTech operations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 — Articles of Incorporation (OBCA), filed electronically via the Ontario Business Registry (onbiz.ca); issuance of Certificate of Incorporation under s. 6 OBCA.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under OBCA. Articles must state authorized share capital (e.g., 1 million Class A common shares valued at nominal or no par value) and the first board fixes issue price for subscribing shares, payable in money/property/services subject to solvency tests (ss. 24-35 OBCA). Non-resident ownership is permitted; at least 25% of directors (minimum one director) must be resident Canadians and the registered office must be in Ontario.

Funding & unlock path: The head-office lease must carry a valid occupancy permit under the Ontario Building Code (O. Reg. 332/12) evidencing structural load and egress compliance; annual fire safety certification is required under the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07); a workplace safety program under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1 applies, and a WSIB clearance certificate is retained once employees are hired. If a serviced/co-working office is used, the landlord's existing permits and fire evacuation plan must be copied into the minute book.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under OBCA. Articles must state authorized share capital (e.g., 1 million Class A common shares valued at nominal or no par value) and the first board fixes issue price for subscribing shares, payable in money/property/services subject to solvency tests (ss. 24-35 OBCA). Non-resident ownership is permitted; at least 25% of directors (minimum one director) must be resident Canadians and the registered office must be in Ontario.

Not applicable - no industrial licensing board is triggered for a cloud-native PropTech SaaS operator (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 531390). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); digital portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident corporations apply for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1; the CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with program accounts: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), etc. Foreign entities must provide certified proof of incorporation, constitutional documents, and director/residence information. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters (or in a single calendar quarter); voluntary registration is permitted for entities that seek input tax credit recovery. Application may be filed online or by mail, and the BN is issued once registration is validated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident corporations apply for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1; the CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with program accounts: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), etc. Foreign entities must provide certified proof of incorporation, constitutional documents, and director/residence information. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters (or in a single calendar quarter); voluntary registration is permitted for entities that seek input tax credit recovery. Application may be filed online or by mail, and the BN is issued once registration is validated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. The Entity-Level Taxation model is mandatory for incorporated entities. Available statutory elections include: ETA s.156 election to treat supplies among closely-related corporations as non-taxable; ITA s.216 or s.219(2) elections to adjust the tax base for non-resident corporations; and the GST/HST annual filing election. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through mandatory provisions (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.2) implementing BEPS Action 2, not elective arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 of taxable supplies (excluding supplies of zero-rated services outside the small supplier calculation) measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or a single calendar quarter; this threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for any person, including non-resident vendors making taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V when exported as part of a sale, including where delivered to a common carrier or placed into a bonded export warehouse, with proof of physical removal from Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if provided in respect of real property situated outside Canada, performed wholly outside Canada, or the recipient receives the benefit outside Canada and is not registered for GST/HST. The place-of-supply rules in ETA s.5 and ss. 17-18 determine territorial use; the documentation bundle must support the place-of-supply and export conclusions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export report, ACI/export manifest, or CUS/RN B3)
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or other contract of carriage showing destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment

No real-time government fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists at the federal level. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants whose designated gross revenue exceeds CAD $1.5 million; certain larger taxpayers must use CRA-approved web services/EDI. All remittances exceeding CAD $10,000 must be made electronically. CRA systems support JSON/XML API through the business portal; e-invoicing is not otherwise mandated.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply and recover the tax, interest and penalties if the exporter fails to substantiate actual export within the required period. Civil penalties under ETA s.280 range from CAD $250 to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; s.285 provides penalties for false refund claims. The normal statutory assessment limitation of four years is extended to ten years for negligent misrepresentation and is indefinite where culpable conduct or fraud exists. Criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code (s.380) carries fines and/or imprisonment for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no exchange control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject to statutory withholding tax obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and, where applicable, Investment Canada Act review or notification for non-resident direct investment in Canadian entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency. However, all incoming and outgoing international electronic fund transfers (EFT) of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for use as a primary treasury account; Medium as a supplementary payment rail. Offshore digital EMI and fintech providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are legally permitted in Canada only as payment service providers or agents, not as deposit-taking institutions. Any entity providing retail payment services in Canada must be licensed or provisionally recognized under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which came into force in 2024 under the Bank of Canada's supervision. A Canadian corporation should maintain its primary operating account at a Schedule I bank and use offshore EMIs solely for settlement acceleration or niche cross-border payout acceptance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (15% federal general rate + 10% Ontario general rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; treaty rates are commonly 15%, with 5% applicable for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting power under the Canada-US tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The balance-due date is two months after year-end generally, extended to three months for eligible CCPCs with Canadian active business income.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 560 - Total tax payable and self-assessment Aggregate federal, provincial, and provincial capital tax, minus credits and instalments to show net balance owing or refund. Remit any Part XIII withholding tax due on dividends or cross-border payments separately from the T2 balance; the T2 records the income tax self-assessment only. Late filing penalty under sections 162 and 162.5 of the Income Tax Act is 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per full month up to 12 months, with a minimum CA$100 penalty. A repeat failure within a prescribed period attracts an additional 10% penalty. Daily compound interest is charged on unpaid balances, and CRA may assess gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of tax owing where the omission is knowingly false.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is mandatory when reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident exceed CA$1,000,000 in the taxation year. A contemporaneous Local File is expected for taxpayers above this threshold. A Master File is administratively expected for taxpayers whose worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds CA$50 million, consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines adopted by the CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, genuine economic substance is enforced through the Canadian corporate residency concept, treaty anti-abuse Limitation on Benefits/Principal Purpose Test, transfer-pricing rules under section 247, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245. Transactions lacking commercial substance and undertaken primarily to obtain a tax benefit may be recharacterized by the CRA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239 liability for tax evasion: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may also impose civil penalties of 50% under section 163(2) for false statements or omissions, plus interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in the evasion may be assessed jointly for unremitted withholding tax under section 227.1, ordered to pay civil restitution, and prosecuted under the Criminal Code for fraud and tax evasion, with possible passport surrender, travel bans, and jail terms.
Shell Company Structure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny the treaty benefit under General Anti-Avoidance Rule section 245 and treaty Limitation on Benefits/Principal Purpose Test protections, reassess the Canadian company, impose section 163(2) penalties, and refer the matter to FINTRAC for money-laundering reporting violations if false beneficial ownership declarations are made.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil on the grounds of fraud, sham, or wrongdoing, making directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and distributions received; criminal exposure arises under the Criminal Code if proceeds of crime are laundered through the shell.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income under section 247(2), apply a severe penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where no reasonable efforts were made, and disallow the deduction for intercompany services lacking documentation and benefit to the payer.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation or knowingly permit unsubstantiated intercompany charges can be personally assessed for unremitted tax under section 227.1, face CRA asset-freeze orders, and be prosecuted for tax evasion with available penalties including imprisonment up to 5 years.
Unremitted Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer corporation is assessed for the unremitted withholding tax plus interest and penalties up to 15% of the amount that should have been remitted. Director liability automatically attaches under subsection 227.1(1) for the unpaid withholding tax, allowing CRA to seize assets directly from directors.

Director personal liability: Directors become jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings, including interest and penalties; failure to comply can trigger civil seizure, director travel restrictions under provincial enforcement procedures, and criminal fraud charges under Criminal Code section 380 where deliberate non-remittance is proven.
