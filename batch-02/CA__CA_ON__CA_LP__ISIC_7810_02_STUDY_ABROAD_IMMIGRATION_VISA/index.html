Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Ontario limited partnerships. Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - national tax authority for federal income tax and GST/HST. Portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA), accessible via Business Registration Online (BRO). Ontario provincial counterpart: Ontario Ministry of Finance, responsible only for Employer Health Tax and land transfer tax; all income tax and HST are administered federally through the CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. L.2) through the Ontario Business Registry; a foreign extra-provincial LP must complete equivalent extra-provincial registration. Step 2: Obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) for the LP using Business Registration Online or paper Form RC1; the LP must create an RT program account for GST/HST, an RP account for employer payroll remittances, and an RM account for import/export transactions where applicable. Step 3: Register voluntarily for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act if input tax credits are desired prior to exceeding the small supplier threshold. Step 4: Open the CRA MyBA account, link the BN, and authorize a representative via the Represent a Client service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; an Ontario-registered Limited Partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96. Available elections: (1) joint election under ITA s. 97(2) to transfer eligible capital property into the LP on a tax-deferred basis; (2) elections under ITA s. 98(3)/(4) to defer tax on distributions on partnership dissolution; (3) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(1) for a small supplier; (4) GST Quick Method election under ETA s. 225.1 for businesses with annual worldwide GST-included taxable supplies of C$400,000 or less; (5) annual GST/HST filing election under ETA s. 238(1) instead of quarterly filing; (6) partnership agreement allocation of profits and losses, provided the split is commercially reasonable and not arbitrary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold is measured on worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies and financial services) in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For services: Schedule VI, Part V, s. 9 ETA zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident person where the service is not performed while the individual is physically present in Canada and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Study-abroad placement, Golden Visa advisory, and immigration-visa consultancy delivered to a non-resident student or client located outside Canada qualify as zero-rated offshore services. If the identical service is rendered to a person while physically present in Canada or is intended to benefit a Canadian resident, it is taxable at 13% HST. For goods: tangible personal property exported as part of the service package (for example, study materials or information kits) may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 if the goods are exported within 60 days of the supply and the exemption documentation is retained.

  • CBSA Electronic Export Declaration (EED/B13A) - mandatory for exported goods only, not required for a pure service export
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for goods) or a signed International Service Agreement / detailed engagement letter (for services)
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank MT103 SWIFT credit advice evidencing FX receipt from the non-resident client within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Copy of the foreign client's passport or non-resident certificate evidencing status
  • Detailed service delivery log documenting that the advisory work was performed outside Canada and for the exclusive benefit of the non-resident recipient

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, QR digital stamping, or government e-invoice authorisation regime exists in Canada as of the 2025 tax year. The CRA accepts paper, PDF, and E-invoice records and requires GST/HST returns to be transmitted via NETFILE, EFILE, or the CRA My Business Account API. Every invoice must satisfy ETA s. 223 by stating the supplier name, the Business Number (RT account), the invoice date, the 13% Ontario HST rate where the supply is taxable, the amount of tax, and, for exports, a clear zero-rated indicator. A voluntary Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework based on Peppol PINT is available but not yet legally obligatory. For exported goods, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires an electronic export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CADERS); pure service exports require no customs clearance filing.

CRA audit practice tests export zero-rating against the ETA s. 223 record-keeping standard; missing documentation or a failed place-of-supply analysis will cause the supply to be reassessed as taxable at 13% HST, with arrears interest charged on the unremitted amount. A false or careless export claim triggers the ETA s. 285(2) civil penalty of 35% of the amount by which the claimed refund or rebate exceeded the true entitlement; a second or continued misstatement attracts a penalty of 50%. Willful evasion is subject to criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327(1), with fines of up to C$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 12 months. The CRA may further disallow input tax credits, apply the ITA s. 162(7) penalty for late or incomplete T5013 partnership returns, and reopen the export transactions within the six-year ordinary reassessment window for negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account items. Profit remittance by an Ontario limited partnership is unrestricted, but distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief. Cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC. Capital account flows, including equity injections and repatriation, are free from administrative approval; however, banks must screen transactions against Canadian sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Freezing Assets of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act. CRS/AEoI and FATCA classification obligations also apply to all financial accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds or service receipts. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency freely in Canadian or foreign bank accounts. The only relevant obligations are statistical reporting under the Statistics Act for cross-border transactions above prescribed thresholds (typically CAD 200,000 quarterly) and accurate computation of foreign exchange gains/losses under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); foreign exchange administration and compliance oversight shared with the Department of Finance Canada, FINTRAC, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada does not operate a separate exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a statutory escrow provider because capital escrow for immigration and international student services requires a regulated Canadian financial institution or trust company. Medium for non-escrow operational collection and payment flows through registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs). Ontario does not license standalone electronic money institutions; a fintech cross-border payment provider must be registered with FINTRAC and operate settlement accounts through a Schedule I Canadian bank. Offshore digital bank accounts may receive and disburse non-client operational funds but cannot act as trust/escrow accounts under Canadian regulatory practice without express financial institution licensing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injection must use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary account name matching the Ontario limited partnership registration. The purpose field should state 'capital contribution to limited partnership' and include the LP registration number and capital call notice reference. The remitting partner must send from its own bank account; third-party wires are not permitted for equity injection without a formal assignment and certified KYC. Canadian banks must report all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. The recipient LP must provide a copy of the partnership agreement, a capital call schedule, and FATCA/CRS classification for each partner. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Ontario corporate income tax). An Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a taxpayer; corporate general and limited partners are taxable on allocated partnership income at this composite headline rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. This dividend withholding rate does not apply to distributions from an Ontario Limited Partnership because an LP is fiscally transparent: distributions to partners are a return of capital, loan repayment, or income allocation, not a dividend. Non-resident limited partners are instead taxed under Part I on their allocated share of Canadian-source partnership business income; if the non-resident partner is a corporation and realizes unremitted net Canadian business profits through the LP, a 25% Part XIV branch tax may apply, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than the earliest filing-due date of any partner's return that includes the partnership allocation. For an Ontario LP with a December 31 fiscal year-end and a corporate general partner, the T5013 must be filed by June 30 of the following calendar year (six months after the LP's fiscal year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. For a corporate partner with a December 31 year-end, the T2 is due by June 30 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 60 days after the anniversary of the limited partnership's registration date under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for related-party transactions triggering T106 (Related Party Transactions) reporting and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. Multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 are subject to Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting, and Canadian entities in such groups must maintain a Master File and Local File. Under ITA s.247, all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions, irrespective of amount, must be priced on an arm's-length basis.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to certain offshore centres. Instead, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), section 247 transfer pricing rules, and treaty 'beneficial ownership' / principal purpose tests impose a substantive economic-meaning requirement. An Ontario LP must have actual commercial purpose, employees, premises, bank accounts, and decision-making in Canada. A passive limited partner with no real investment risk or control may be recharacterized as a financing arrangement or sham; likewise, a non-resident corporate partner claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate residence-based substance and that its Canadian LP profits are not artificially routed. CRA will disregard transactions lacking economic substance and may apply GAAR plus the 25% GAAR penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2), plus daily compound interest on unpaid amounts from the original deadline.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unpaid taxes that the partnership failed to remit, and under s.160 for any non-arm's-length transfer of assets to avoid payment. Ontario courts may grant travel bans, asset seizure, equitable tracing, and fraud injunctions against directors and partners.
Failure to File Information Returns / T5013 Omission

Statutory sanction: CRA penalty under ITA s.162(7): CAD 25 per day, maximum CAD 2,500, for late or omitted information returns. A false statement in the T5013 may lead to a summary conviction offence, a fine of up to CAD 25,000, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty on any partner-level tax payable. Ontario may also administratively cancel the LP registration for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The general partner, and any limited partner who participates in management or control, becomes personally liable for the partnership's penalty and tax obligations. Directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly fail to cause the partnership to comply can be assessed under s.227.1 and may be prohibited from serving as directors under Ontario corporate law.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the limited partnership as a sham or apply GAAR to recharacterize the arrangement; tax benefit is denied, GAAR penalty is 25% of the tax benefit, and transfer-pricing adjustments under s.247(3) attract a 10% penalty (minimum CAD 5,000). The Ontario Registrar may strike the LP from the registry for cessation of business.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who are involved in the control or management of the LP lose limited liability and become jointly and severally liable for all LP debts. Directors of the corporate general partner owe fiduciary duties and may be personally liable under s.160 and s.227.1 for taxes and penalties, and can be barred from managing another Ontario entity.
AML/CFT Regulatory Offence

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can be up to CAD 100,000 for an individual and CAD 500,000 for a corporation per violation; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of crime carries a maximum term of up to 10 years imprisonment and unlimited fines. FINTRAC may publish non-compliance notices and the Federal Court may issue compliance orders.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and general partners who authorize, permit, or acquiesce in a contravention are individually guilty of the offence. They may be subject to criminal prosecution, director disqualification, civil asset forfeiture, and travel restrictions; the corporate veil is penetrated for AML offences.
Unauthorized Practice of Immigration Law

Statutory sanction: Under the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants Act (Canada) and the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations, s.91, unauthorized representation or advice for consideration is an offence; on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years. The College can seek cease-and-desist orders, injunctions and administrative monetary penalties.

Director personal liability: A corporate general partner cannot itself hold an RCIC licence; the licensed individual must be a principal of the licensed entity. Unlicensed partners who exercise control over immigration-consulting employees are personally exposed to prosecution, and the Ontario court may pierce the LP to hold each general partner and controlling limited partner liable for fines, restitution, and litigation costs.
