Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) as national tax administrator and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal operator; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax policy; Ontario Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) is harmonized and administered exclusively by the CRA; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declarations under the Customs Act, including the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident exporter intending to zero-rate exports of baijiu must obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account (suffix 'RT') before the first supply. Registration is executed online via the CRA BRO portal selecting 'GST/HST account', or by paper filing of Form RC1 (or RC1-NR for non-residents) faxed to the CRA International Tax Services Office in Ottawa. A limited partnership is entitled to register in its own name for GST/HST because a partnership is a 'person' under Excise Tax Act s. 272.1. The procurement SOP requires: (i) proof of non-resident legal existence (foreign registry extract, partnership agreement, and certificates of good standing, with certified English/French translations as required); (ii) appointment of a Canadian resident agent or holder of power of attorney for service and records; (iii) election to file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or certified electronic filing service); (iv) voluntary registration election where revenues are below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, so that input tax credits (ITCs) on export-cost purchases are recoverable; and (v) separate extra-provincial limited partnership registration with the Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, which is a corporate registry filing and not a tax ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime; legal-form classification governs. Under Income Tax Act s. 96, the limited partnership is not a taxable entity and each general or limited partner is taxed on its allocable share of partnership income or loss. Under Excise Tax Act s. 272.1, the partnership is nonetheless a separate GST/HST registrant. Relevant statutory elections available to this structure include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration prior to exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold (ETA s. 240(2.1)); (ii) annual GST/HST reporting-period election for small registrants (ETA ss. 237-238); (iii) Quick Method election (ETA s. 225.1), where appropriate; (iv) Income Tax Act s. 216 election to file a treaty-based return on Canadian-source passive rental income, if applicable; (v) Income Tax Act s. 93(1) elections for future foreign-affiliate reorganizations; and (vi) hybrid mismatch limitation rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.6 et seq. (BEPS Action 2 / MLI Article 3), which restrict interest deductions on hybrid instruments and prevent double non-taxation; no hybrid interest deduction is assumed in the base export structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - aggregate value of taxable supplies made in Canada over any four consecutive calendar quarters. For a non-resident carrying on business in Canada, GST/HST registration is mandatory once this threshold is exceeded, including zero-rated export supplies; below the threshold, voluntary registration is available and is recommended to recover input tax credits on export-related Canadian costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V, section 15 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of baijiu (personal property) is zero-rated at 0% GST/HST where the registrant supplies the goods to a non-resident purchaser and delivers or makes the goods available at a place outside Canada, followed by expeditious export. The registrant must demonstrate: execution of a genuine foreign sales contract, physical movement of the consignment outside Canada, and clear linkage between the invoice, the customs declaration, and the bill of lading. Federal excise duty on spirits under the Excise Act, 2001 is likewise relieved for goods actually exported under CBSA supervision. Zero-rating fails where goods are diverted into the Canadian market, resold domestically, delivered to a bonded facility without actual export, or exported without the required CBSA reporting.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA Canada Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED)) lodged for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods, identifying the product, value, HS 2208.90, destination country, and exporter BN.
  • Bill of Lading or Ocean/Air Waybill: dated on-board receipt proving physical export, naming a foreign port or place of discharge outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank-confirmed SWIFT MT103 or wire transfer statement demonstrating receipt of export consideration in foreign currency within the normative repatriation window.
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Packing List: cross-referencing the B13A/CAED declaration, HS classification, product description, case volume, and stated zero-rated export treatment.
  • Foreign destination delivery evidence: port-of-discharge receipt, destination-country customs entry, or final buyer's proof of receipt retained to withstand CRA export-verification audit.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal or Ontario level. GST/HST returns above prescribed filing thresholds must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or a CRA-certified electronic filing service. Business-to-business invoices must disclose the supplier's BN/RT registration number, transaction date, description, tax treatment, and applicable rate; zero-rated exports must be clearly marked as 'zero-rated' or '0% GST/HST'. The CRA prohibits electronic suppression of sales software (ESSS) and requires fiscally sound electronic records. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing pilot is voluntary only; export declarations are lodged through the CBSA CARM/CAED portal rather than through any fiscal stamping system.

CRA export-verification audits scrutinize the B13A/CAED declaration, bill-of-lading dates, and remittance evidence. Missing or inconsistent proof permits retrospective reassessment of the supply at 13% HST within the normal four-year limitation period, which extends to ten years for negligence or misrepresentation and is unlimited for fraud. Civil penalties include Exchange Tax Act s. 285 for false statements and omissions and Income Tax Act s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50%; criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 may attract fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Where exported goods are returned to Canada or diverted to the domestic market, Customs duties and HST become payable, input tax credits are clawed back with compound statutory interest, and future zero-rating may be denied pending implementation of strengthened documentary controls.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no general exchange control regime; capital movements, profit remittances, dividend distributions, and intercompany payments are unrestricted. Hurdles are limited to standard AML/ATF scrutiny, FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000, and documentary requirements for reasonable source-of-funds verification. Profit repatriation from an Ontario limited partnership may also be subject to withholding tax under applicable tax treaties unless a reduction or exemption is available.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any authorized dealer, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, held in a Canadian bank or authorized foreign exchange account, converted at commercial rates, or used directly for commercial obligations. Any inbound or outbound wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC in accordance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the underlying transaction must still satisfy audit, tax, and AML documentation standards.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); foreign exchange administration is handled under the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction reporting and AML/ATF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms and non-bank cross-border payment service providers may be used for trade-related payment processing and FX conversion, but they cannot lawfully open deposit-taking bank accounts in Canada unless licensed as banks or authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act, 2021. Regulatory constraints include mandatory registration for payment service providers, reliance on a licensed Canadian bank for settlement accounts, and enhanced due diligence requirements for high-proof alcohol wholesalers. Direct account opening with an offshore EMI without a Canadian nexus is not treated as a substitute for a Canadian bank account and may raise concerns for the CRA, FINTRAC, and LC issuing banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the investor to send funds by SWIFT MT103 from its home jurisdiction to the Ontario LP’s designated CAD account. The payment narrative should clearly state the purpose as 'capital contribution to Ontario limited partnership for liquor export operations'. The bank may require the completion of its ‘purpose of remittance’ form, the underlying limited partnership agreement, and a source-of-funds declaration. For any single remittance at or above CAD 10,000, the Canadian receiving bank must file an electronic funds transfer report with FINTRAC. International wires originating from China must comply with Chinese SAFE regulations; where a Chinese entity makes the injection, the ‘code for cross-border transaction’ should reflect 'personal direct investment' or 'foreign direct investment' as applicable, and the capital item must match the registered business scope and be supported by a capital verification report, if required. The Ontario LP should also provide the bank with a written capital contribution resolution and a list of authorised signatories.
