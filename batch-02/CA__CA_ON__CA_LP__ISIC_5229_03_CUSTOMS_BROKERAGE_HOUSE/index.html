Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca and CRA My Business Account; supplemental customs licensing by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations.

E-government filing portal

Default tax classification

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies in Canada (small supplier threshold, section 148 ETA); registration is mandatory once exceeded, and non-residents carrying on business in Canada are deemed registrants under subsection 240(1) ETA; customs brokerage services are generally taxable at 13% HST when supplied in Ontario unless zero-rated as an export service under section 7 of Schedule VI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported in accordance with section 6 of Schedule VI to the ETA and to services supplied to non-residents under section 7 of Schedule VI; a customs brokerage house may zero-rate its services when the supply is made to a non-resident client and the service is in respect of exported goods, provided the broker retains evidence of export and payment; alternatively, brokerage services supplied in Ontario to a Canadian importer are subject to 13% HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or CBSA eManifest export report) with confirmation of export
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or truck) evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance statement evidencing receipt of payment in the broker's designated Canadian account

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or EDI for registrants with annual taxable supplies above the electronic filing threshold; licensed customs brokers are required to transmit customs declarations through CBSA's CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) and eManifest portal using API/broker interfaces (Automated Broker Interface), with mandatory electronic record keeping and eight-year retention of supporting export documentation.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period and, in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, without time limit; zero-rating is denied and input tax credits are clawed back where export documentation is incomplete; penalties under section 285.1 ETA for false statements or omissions, plus gross negligence penalties up to 25% of the net tax, statutory interest under section 280 ETA, and CBSA administrative monetary penalties for incorrect export declarations; for serial non-compliance CRA may issue a requirement to provide information, revoke registrations, and impose third-party civil penalties under section 284.01 ETA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised exchange control regime with no general restrictions on capital or current account convertibility under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Investment Canada Act. Non-resident limited partners may contribute capital in CAD or freely convertible foreign currency without exchange control approval; profit distributions and capital withdrawals are freely remittable through Canadian authorised banks. Domestic encumbrances are limited to statutory withholding or reporting obligations, not convertibility. Key frictions include: (i) Part XIII or subsection 194 withholding on partnership income allocations to non-resident partners (generally 15% unless treaty reduced, applied to Canadian-source partnership income of the Ontario limited partnership); (ii) FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfer of $10,000 or more per transaction and currency reporting of $10,000+; and (iii) the availability of same-day real-time settlement only for CAD via Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and Real-Time Rail (RTR) for retail payments, while USD wires require cross-border correspondent clearing through the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or Fedwire. There is no offshore conversion restriction, no surrender requirement, and no central bank approval gate for remittance of customs brokerage operating revenues to foreign exchange vendors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose statutory surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or export proceeds accounts for export receipts. A licensed customs brokerage house is not an exporter of goods; its proceeds are service fees and disbursement reimbursements, and may be retained in any currency. Nevertheless, entities engaged in goods movement must maintain full books and records under the Customs Act (section 40) and the Income Tax Act (section 230) for seven years, and any receipt of funds over $10,000 in cash or via cross-border electronic transfer is reported to FINTRAC. Inward and outward remittances exceeding CAD currency thresholds require prescribed regulatory reporting only; they are not subject to exchange control authorisation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); enforcement of the Currency Act and the Canadian Payments Act by the Department of Finance Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI and BaaS platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Neo Financial) are feasible only as secondary operating or disbursement rails, rated Medium for ancillary flows and Low for core capital escrow. Each provider must be licensed or authorised in its home jurisdiction (e.g., IKT, FinCEN MSB, AFM/SFM) and must not be a substitute for a Schedule I regulated Canadian bank. Legal constraints: (i) only a bank authorised under the Bank Act may accept deposits in Canada on a day-to-day basis; (ii) EMI remittance products may not be treated as deposit accounts, disclaiming eligible deposit insurance and CDIC coverage; (iii) FINTRAC EFT reporting obligations still apply to the partnership; and (iv) customs duty disbursements to CBSA must settle through the designated government financial institution or an approved Canadian clearings participant, which most offshore EMIs cannot access directly. The blueprint is to maintain a primary Tier 1 bank account and use a validated EMI only for small-value third-country payment efficiency where the correspondent fee would exceed the platform spread; capital injections, non-resident partner contributions, and payments above CAD 50,000 must route exclusively through SWIFT at a Tier 1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital subscriptions from Canadian and non-resident limited partners are routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 (for high value) to the RBC or BMO locked-box capital escrow account in CAD or USD. Each injection must be prefixed with a payment narrative referencing the partnership name, LP interest certificate number, and purpose code using the ISO 20022/SWIFT extended code list; the recommended purpose code is '6010 – Capital contribution to a non-financial enterprise' where permitted, or, for service-business investment, 'GFAC – Investment income/capital contribution' under bank-specific variants. Inbound USD wires arriving to a CAD account are converted by the receiving bank under a standing FX order, and any USD deposit retained in a USD-denominated Nostro account must be registered in the foreign currency accounts module of the receiving bank. For amounts equal to or above CAD 10,000, the partnership must ensure the bank reports the electronic funds transfer to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA regulations; banks require the partnership to complete a source-of-funds declaration on the beneficial owner to allow settlement release. The partnership must not accept cash capital contributions above CAD 10,000 without full CTR filing, and no capital subscription will be accepted from third-party payers other than the named limited partners, preventing nominee triangulation. All inbound injections are reflected in the partnership's Internal Revenue Service/commercial books under the capital account of each limited partner; any converting of capital must be at the spot rate of the banking day to avoid hidden FX charges and to substantiate the Canadian-dollar tax basis.Outbound disbursements from the operational account are cleared through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD payments to domestic customs administrative parties, and through SWIFT MT103 for cross-border remittances. The dominant rail for customs clearance payments is the Automated Broker Interface (ACI) of the CBSA, under which the licensed broker transmits the B3-3 form and disburses duties using either a settlor arrangement with an approved financial institution (ACH) or a wire transfer from the operational account; the settlement code must include the CBSA broker identifier aligned to ISIC 5229 duties-related flow. For import/export trade settlements, the partnership uses: (i) full SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with clear cash-flow narrative and an account-holder reference 'CBSA BROKER + broker code'; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600) confirmed by international banks when its importer clients require proof of payment—these are presented through the Tier 1 bank's trade finance desk and settled via MT202 cover; and (iii) SWIFT MT101/MT102 batch transfers for initiation of a series of small-value supplier payments to foreign customs and bonded-warehouse operators. Where the partnership holds USD client funds, a USD concentration account is maintained at RBC and swept to BMO's USD correspondent network in New York or to TD Bank US through the Canadian bank's US branch to avoid double intermediary fees. All outbound wires are subject to the CAD 10,000 FINTRAC monitoring and sanctions screening; for commercial LC operations the partnership must present the transport document (airway bill/Bill of lading), commercial invoice, packing list, and inspection certificate to trigger the bank's settlement instruction. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border digital EMI and BaaS platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Neo Financial) are feasible only as secondary operating or disbursement rails, rated Medium for ancillary flows and Low for core capital escrow. Each provider must be licensed or authorised in its home jurisdiction (e.g., IKT, FinCEN MSB, AFM/SFM) and must not be a substitute for a Schedule I regulated Canadian bank. Legal constraints: (i) only a bank authorised under the Bank Act may accept deposits in Canada on a day-to-day basis; (ii) EMI remittance products may not be treated as deposit accounts, disclaiming eligible deposit insurance and CDIC coverage; (iii) FINTRAC EFT reporting obligations still apply to the partnership; and (iv) customs duty disbursements to CBSA must settle through the designated government financial institution or an approved Canadian clearings participant, which most offshore EMIs cannot access directly. The blueprint is to maintain a primary Tier 1 bank account and use a validated EMI only for small-value third-country payment efficiency where the correspondent fee would exceed the platform spread; capital injections, non-resident partner contributions, and payments above CAD 50,000 must route exclusively through SWIFT at a Tier 1 bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable at entity level (pass-through). Reference combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate: 15% federal + 11.5% provincial = 26.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable for LP distributions (not dividends). However, if the LP earns Canadian-source income and distributes to non-resident partners, the LP may be required to withhold under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act at a rate of 25% unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian TP documentation is required for transactions with non-arm's length parties if the aggregate value exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services). Master File required for groups with revenue over CAD 50 million; Local File required for transactions above thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. The LP must demonstrate real business activity, especially for customs brokerage, to avoid GAAR challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they fail to withhold or remit. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties under s. 163.2 for false statements. CRA may also impose a 10% penalty on the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax liability if they participated in the scheme, and may face criminal charges for conspiracy.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1 million for entities per violation. Criminal penalties for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to comply. They may also face imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustments, penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(3)), and interest on unpaid taxes. Documentation penalties up to CAD 24,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the penalty if they directed or acquiesced in the non-compliance, and may face personal assessment under s. 160 for transfers to non-arm's length persons.
