Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax registration; Ontario Ministry of Finance – Ontario Corporation Tax and provincial HST component. Online portals: CRA My Business Account (Business Registration Online) and Ontario ONT-TAX.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated business corporation must: (1) apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts') or electronically through CRA Business Registration Online; (2) activate a GST/HST account effective from the first taxable supply in Canada; (3) register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario Corporation Tax where a permanent establishment is present in Ontario; (4) provide the incorporation certificate, bylaws, and a Canadian service address or authorized representative mandate; and (5) complete know-your-client verification through a bank or tax intermediary to meet CRA's non-resident taxpayer identification requirements. Federal and provincial accounts are synchronized; separate Ontario provincial tax registration uses the same BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: Canadian-resident corporations taxed on worldwide net income at Part I rates, plus provincial corporate tax in Ontario where permanent establishment exists; non-resident corporations taxed only on Canada-source business income and certain capital gains / Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable (no Canadian free-zone regime for income tax). Fiscal classification is a per-se corporate taxpayer: no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under the Income Tax Act; a business corporation cannot elect pass-through treatment. Relevant available elections for cross-border metal trading include: (1) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election for closely related corporations to zero-rate otherwise taxable inter-corporate supplies; (2) ETA s. 157 election to apply the net tax calculation as a margin and non-profit subject to conditions; and (3) Income Tax Act hybrid mismatch adjustment procedures under ss. 18.6–18.18 (not reclassification elections but required filings where a hybrid entity or instrument is involved). Where the corporate structure involves a non-resident parent, s. 256 association rules and treaty tie-breaker provisions must be addressed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies of the supplier and associated entities); a non-resident exporter selling tangible goods from Ontario into Canada or engaging in commercial activity through a permanent establishment is generally required to register if the threshold is met, and voluntary registration is needed to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI Part V and s. 20 of the GST/HST Regulations, a supply of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire (HS 7404.00) is zero-rated when: (i) the vendor is a GST/HST registrant or required to register; (ii) the goods are physically exported before use or consumption in Canada; (iii) the vendor delivers the goods to a carrier or sends them by common carrier to a destination outside Canada; and (iv) the vendor retains documentation sufficient to satisfy the 'export in accordance with the regulations' test. Title must pass to a non-resident buyer outside Canada; a bona fide sale backed by an inward remittance within 180 days is typical commercial evidence, although the ETA does not impose a fixed foreign-exchange repatriation deadline. Input tax credits on procurement and logistics costs are recoverable to the extent the zero-rated export is properly documented.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CED) submitted via the CBSA Export Reporting System, including HS classification (e.g., 7404.00), weight and declared value
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic) or multimodal transport document showing consignor in Canada, consignee outside Canada and discharge port outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payment receipt confirming receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and purchase contract evidencing the scrap-metal sale, terms of trade (Incoterms) transferring risk and title outside Canada
  • Carrier certificate or Cargo Control Document (CCD) issued by the exporting carrier
  • Customs broker summary report or port/customs log validating physical exit from Canada

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government-issued digital stamping, or continuous transaction control system. CRA does require every registrant to issue invoices/credit notes containing prescribed information (ETA ss. 221–223 and the Information Regulations), and almost all GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or authorized commercial software. The 'My Business Account' portal supports electronic remittances, assessments and notices. There is no statutory API obligation imposed on exporters; CRA offers optional web-service integration to intermediaries but no state-level clearance of invoices is required.

CRA applies strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating for scrap metal because of historically high fraud exposure. If the exporter cannot produce the documentation bundle above, or if documents are false, reconstructed after an audit challenge, or internally inconsistent, CRA will re-assess the supply as taxable at 13% Ontario HST, add per-subject interest, and recapture any input tax credits previously claimed on related purchases. Civil penalties under ETA s. 285.1 may be imposed for false or omitted information: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax deficiency; gross negligence or wilful blindness can result in a penalty of up to 25% of the deficiency, with criminal exposure under Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud) for deliberate export-fraud schemes. Ontario's provincial HST component is equally reassaluable, and CBSA can suspend or deny future export privileges. Records must be retained for at least six years and made electronically available on demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with no exchange-control legislation. Current and capital account transactions are free from approval requirements; there is no general reserve or repatriation permit regime. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders are legally unrestricted, subject to corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act and the Ontario Business Corporations Act, and to Part XIII withholding taxes of the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, reduced under applicable tax treaties). Cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting, and transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or persons must be blocked under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. No conversion charge, surrender quota, or performance threshold is imposed on CAD-to-foreign-currency transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; proceeds may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency and repatriated freely. The exporter must preserve books and records in Canada for six years under the Income Tax Act, report taxable income on the annual T2 corporation return, disclose controlled or affiliated foreign entities via Form T1134 and specified foreign property via Form T1135 where thresholds apply, and provide Canadian International Merchandise Trade data to Statistics Canada. All inbound and outbound transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 within a 24-hour period must be reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada, in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury operations; MEDIUM for ancillary cross-border collections and contingency vendor payments. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or Trezoro are not licensed as banks by OSFI. They may lawfully provide payment services into Canada only if registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business, or if they operate through a Canadian Schedule I bank under a custodial or licensed-relationship model; Ontario also requires registration under the provincial Money Services Business Act for in-scope providers. EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit or provide structured trade finance, and they generally cannot serve as settlement accounts for international LC disburse because counterparty confirming banks will not accept an EMI BIC/SWIFT address as a reimbursing institution. The principal hard constraints are enhanced beneficial-ownership KYC, inability to obtain a Canadian CRA business number tied to the rush account, limitations on receiving proceeds from trade documents, and enhanced due-diligence friction when funds flow to high-risk satellite jurisdictions. Accordingly, for a shredded copper wire trading entity, EMIs should be restricted to low-value expense disbursements and temporary liquidity buffers, never used as the principal clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent shareholder's bank to the Canadian entity's operational or Capital Escrow Account, quoting the ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code 'EQUI' or the bank-specific equivalent 'Equity Injection / Direct Investment' in field 70, and referencing the board resolution that authorized the share allotment. For transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving bank files the required FINTRAC EFT report automatically. To ensure the credit is not erroneously classified as operating revenue, the entity must provide: (a) a board resolution stating the nature of the payment as contributed capital; (b) share subscription agreement and issued share certificate evidencing the capital subscription; (c) beneficial-ownership breakdown of the transferring entity; (d) a funds-completion memo confirming no third-party subrogation. No Bank of Canada or OSFI authorization is required; however, if the transferor is in a sanctioned jurisdiction, the transaction is prohibited absent a permit from the Department of Global Affairs Canada. Foreign-currency injections may be converted to CAD at the receiving bank's spot rate without restriction.Trade settlements for recycled scrap copper wire purchases should primarily use Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank in favor of the foreign supplier, advised and confirmed via SWIFT MT700/MT710, payable against presentation of a clean-on-board marine bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, weight certificate, and contamination inspection certificate. For open-account settlements, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'TRD' for merchandise trade, using pre-vetted beneficiary bank details and sanitized payment narratives. Large CAD payments should be settled through the LVTS or the modern Real-Time Rail; USD payments through Fedwire or CHIPS; EUR payments through TARGET2. Accordingly, for a shredded copper wire trading entity, EMIs should be restricted to low-value expense disbursements and temporary liquidity buffers, never used as the principal clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in a single taxation year triggers mandatory T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; consolidated MNE groups with revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000 must also file Country-by-Country Reporting and maintain a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a distinct Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore financial centres. However, the CRA assesses economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), treaty anti-abuse rules, and the required true operational presence in Canada. Arrangements lacking business purpose or real personnel, premises, and decision-making in Canada can be recharacterized for tax purposes and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the understated tax for gross negligence under subsection 163(2); criminal liability for tax evasion under section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or consent to fraudulent reporting are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax remittance errors, may be subject to reinstatement demands, civil restitution for CRA collection, and criminal convictions leading to personal fines and imprisonment. A travel ban may be imposed during an investigation or once a warrant is issued.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income to arm's length price; a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the adjusted amount (where net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue) applies under section 247(3).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for failing to exercise due diligence in ensuring adequate TP documentation was maintained; gross negligence penalties can be assessed against the corporation, and directors can be subject to jurisdictions of penalty collection via director liability certificates if the corporation fails to pay.
Shell Company and Sham Arrangement / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR to deny the deductible pass-through and the treaty benefits; in cases of sham, the arrangement is voided for tax purposes; penalties include 25% of the tax avoided under subsection 245(3) and recapture of previously granted treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors of both Canadian and offshore shell entities may be subject to joint liability for taxes avoided, civil penalties for facilitating a sham, and potentially criminal charges if intent to deceive is shown. A travel ban or exit control can be imposed under a CRA criminal investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and FINTRAC Reporting Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation per violation, and up to CAD 250,000 for individuals; the CRA is also empowered to levy additional withholding tax penalties for unreported remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for corporate AML failures if they knowingly engaged in or directed the breach; they may also face imprisonment under Part II of the PCMLTFA for money laundering offences and sanctions of up to ten years, plus seizure of personal and corporate assets.
