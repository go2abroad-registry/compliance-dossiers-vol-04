Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), acting under the supervisory framework of the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), SR 220, Articles 620 to 763 (law of the Aktiengesellschaft / société anonyme), in particular Articles 626 to 632 (formation, share capital and registration) and Article 627 (mandatory articles of association); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, SR 221.411); Federal Act on the Commercial Register (SR 221.411).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister / demande d'inscription au registre du commerce) submitted through the eRegister electronic portal, accompanied by the notarially certified Articles of Association (Statuten / statuts) and the bank deposit confirmation required for the paid-up capital.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Aktiengesellschaft requires a minimum share capital of CHF 100,000, fully subscribed in shares with a minimum par value of CHF 0.01 per share. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in no event less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, must be paid up prior to registration. The paid-up capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the company in formation with a bank or FINMA-licensed financial intermediary in Switzerland. The bank deposit confirmation must accompany the Commercial Register application, and the escrowed funds are released only upon production of the final Commercial Register extract evidencing the registration.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises require a basic fire safety confirmation under the guidelines of the Swiss Fire Protection Association (VKF), including compliance with escape route, fire extinguisher and smoke alarm requirements, verified by Gebräudeversicherung Zug or the municipal fire prevention authority. Structural load inspections apply only to the standard office occupancy limits and do not require a special engineering clearance. Occupational health and safety measures must comply with the Swiss Labor Act (Arbeitsgesetz) and the Swiss Accident Prevention Ordinance (VUV), including ergonomic workplace design, accident prevention training, and mandatory accident insurance through SUVA or a private insurer under the Swiss Federal Act on Accident Insurance (UVG).

Paid-in capital rule: The Aktiengesellschaft requires a minimum share capital of CHF 100,000, fully subscribed in shares with a minimum par value of CHF 0.01 per share. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in no event less than CHF 50,000 in aggregate, must be paid up prior to registration. The paid-up capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the company in formation with a bank or FINMA-licensed financial intermediary in Switzerland. The bank deposit confirmation must accompany the Commercial Register application, and the escrowed funds are released only upon production of the final Commercial Register extract evidencing the registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni – AFC); official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and returns are processed via the ESTV 'Sage' e-services platform and the Swiss Federal Tax Administration VAT portal at https://www.estv.admin.ch/estv/en/home/value-added-tax.html.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG): (1) Establish the entity in the Swiss Commercial Register (Handelsregister), which generates the permanent eleven-digit Swiss Business Identification Number (UID – Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer). (2) Register for VAT with the ESTV using Form 130 (Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuerpflicht) within 30 days after exceeding the statutory threshold or upon commencement of taxable activities in Switzerland; the ESTV issues a separate MWST-registration number (VAT ID) in the format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST. (3) For a foreign non-established supplier, registration is mandatory where Swiss taxable turnover (excluding VAT) exceeds CHF 100,000, or immediately in the case of supplies of electronically supplied digital services (SaaS) to non-business consumers (B2C) within Switzerland (Art. 10, para. 3 MTWSTG). (4) Files periodic VAT returns (quarterly, semi-annual or annual based on turnover) through the ESTV 'Sage' portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. Material statutory elections available to a Swiss AG with international scope: (a) VAT group taxation election per Art. 12 MWSTG for closely aligned domestic entities; (b) cantonal patent/IP box election for qualifying IP profits at reduced cantonal rates; (c) participation relief election (statutory) for capital gains and dividends from qualifying participations of ≥ 10% or CHF 1 million market value; (d) notional interest deduction (NID) election for Swiss finance branches or financing companies in designated cantons; (e) restructuring step-up and deemed-liquidation election. Switzerland implements OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch rules via the Swiss Hybrid Mismatch Ordinance (HSR) effective 1 January 2020, denying deduction for hybrid mismatch arrangements even absent a domestic 'check-the-box' mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual global taxable turnover excluding VAT and exempt supplies (Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act – MWSTG). For non-established suppliers of electronic services (B2C), registration is required when the annual turnover from Swiss private consumers exceeds CHF 100,000; B2B supplies to Swiss VAT-registered businesses transfer the VAT liability to the recipient under the reverse-charge rule, which does not trigger the supplier's registration obligation except where physical nexus or e-services thresholds apply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate is currently imposed by the ESTV. Swiss invoices must satisfy minimum statutory content under Art. 26 MWSTG (supplier name and address, MWST-ID/UID, date, description, rate, and tax amount). Since 30 June 2020, QR-billing (Swiss QR Code / QR-Rechnung) is mandatory as the standard payable channel for all Swiss business invoices referencing a Swiss IBAN. E-invoicing (Peppol BIS / eBill) is available on a voluntary basis; there is no mandatory API integrated clearance. VAT returns are filed electronically via the ESTV 'Sage' e-services portal at regulated intervals, generally quarterly for taxable turnover exceeding CHF 5,005,000 (2024), otherwise semi-annual or annual.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely traded on global FX markets, and there are no Swiss exchange control laws or regulations restricting the conversion of CHF into foreign currency, nor any restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees to non-residents. Cross-border capital movements are subject to standard financial market regulations, anti-money laundering laws, and prudential banking requirements, but not to licensing or prior approval by the SNB. Profit remittances from a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) are generally unrestricted; however, dividend distributions are subject to Swiss withholding tax at 35%, a portion of which may be reclaimed under an applicable double taxation treaty, and the company must ensure corporate law solvency and documentation requirements are satisfied. There is no differentiation between capital and current account convertibility as a matter of Swiss federal law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation. There are no mandatory timelines for the repatriation of export-related foreign currency receivables, no conversion ratios, and no requirement to channel export proceeds through a particular Swiss bank. Swiss-resident exporters may hold foreign currency accounts offshore or domestically at their discretion, and may use such proceeds for operational liquidity, reinvestment, or FX hedging in accordance with normal treasury policies. The only relevant obligations arise under anti-money laundering due-diligence rules and, if applicable, the Swiss Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital, which itself contains no surrender or conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — strictly limited to non-deposit-taking payment service arrangements. Swiss law does not grant a passport or automatic mutual recognition to fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) licensed in the EU/EEA or in other jurisdictions. An offshore EMI that performs deposit-taking or public-refinancing activities in Switzerland requires a FINMA banking or Fintech license under the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA). However, pure payment transaction processing, without deposit taking in Switzerland, may be provided by a non-Swiss PSP on a cross-border basis, subject to compliance with Swiss AML/CFT legislation, data protection rules, and client disclosure duties. For a PropTech SaaS platform handling rental payments, custody of client funds in an EMI account may be constructed as a contractual sub-accounting arrangement rather than a Swiss bank account, but this raises legal recharacterization risk if the platform becomes a de facto deposit taker. Therefore, the viability of offshore EMIs is best used for payment initiation and settlement facilitation, not for holding client funds earmarked as rental deposits, which should be entrusted to a Swiss-licensed bank or a FINMA-authorized fintech.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into a Swiss AG from non-resident shareholders are freely permitted and require no exchange-control approval or special licence. The compliant routing protocol is to effect the injection by means of a SWIFT wire transfer in CHF, EUR, or USD from the shareholder's account to the Swiss operating account, with clear payment narrative quoting the company's purpose, e.g., 'CHF Share Capital Increase under Art. 652 of the Swiss Code of Obligations' or 'Paid-in capital — par value and premium'. The Swiss bank debiting the account will require the uploading of a certified board resolution approving the capital increase, minutes of the extraordinary general meeting, and, if the subscription is in currency other than CHF, a currency conversion statement from the company's auditor. The bank will obtain a statement of compliance with Swiss anti-money laundering rules; the payer may require a beneficiary reference / IRB (International Bank Reference Number) for internal reporting. In practice, the wire should be directed through the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) system or euroSIC for EUR, and, if the amount exceeds CHF 15,000, the bank's AML transaction monitoring system will automatically record the reason for payment; the company should be ready to furnish the share register and a notarised declaration from the beneficial owner confirming the source of funds from legitimate business or personal wealth.For cross-border trade and SaaS service disbursements from Switzerland, the principal clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct straight-through payments, TARGET2/euroSIC for EUR disbursements, and SIC for CHF. For larger or more structured property-remediation and construction settlements under which a PropTech platform manages supplier payments, a Documentary Letter of Credit (LC) may be issued by a Swiss bank on behalf of the AG, with the supplier's bank as advising bank; however, given the recurring, low-margin nature of SaaS subscription fees, MT103 transfers are the default, with a robust audit trail of invoice numbers and purpose codes. For outbound payments to partners in non-SWIFT jurisdictions, priority should be given to SWIFT-compliant correspondent banking chains with a Swiss clearing bank, ensuring that the remitting bank executes the payment in compliance with Swiss Federal Office of Police economic sanctions and maintains the full remitter mandate under the FATF Recommendation 16. Payout settlements to international landlords can also be batched via SWIFT netting, but each beneficiary identity and tax-reference field must be maintained in the treasury workbench to avoid suspicious-transaction filings.Book-entry spot FX contracts for immediate conversions between CHF, EUR, and USD, executed through a Swiss-licensed bank or a regulated FX prime broker.FX forward contracts on physically deliverable CHF/EUR and CHF/USD cross rates, with a maximum maturity up to five years for a corporate non-financial counterparty under standard ISDA documentation.FX swaps combining an FX spot transaction with a forward transaction for periodic treasury rebalancing of the platform's EUR-technology-procurement and CHF-revenue streams.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for hedging any long-term CHF or Euro debt exposure, permitted with a Swiss bank or international swap dealer, provided that the AG qualifies as a financial counterparty or takes the small non-financial counterparty threshold only if transaction notional is modest.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging residual exposure to illiquid or developing-market currencies such as RMB, INR, BRL, or TRY, permitted as off-book derivatives via a FINMA-regulated dealer, allowing net settlement in CHF while circumventing local currency convertibility restrictions in the counterparty's jurisdiction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory corporate income tax on net profit (the federal effective rate is approximately 7.83% because the federal tax itself is deductible; in the unitary model used in this dossier cantonal/communal taxes are not distinguished).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions of a Swiss AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the business year (by 30 June for a 31 December year-end); extension of up to an additional 6 months is available on written request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 900,000,000 group consolidated annual revenue (mandatory Master File and Local File for direct federal tax purposes; CbC report required for ultimate parent groups meeting the threshold). Below this threshold, the AG is expected to keep contemporaneous arm's-length documentation for any material related-party transaction when requested by the Swiss tax authority, particularly for SaaS platform licensing, software development, and management fees.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a substance-over-form doctrine under Art. 57 of the Federal Direct Tax Act and Art. 10 of the Withholding Tax Act. There is no separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) framework as in the UAE; however, treaty benefits, participation exemption and IP tax incentives are denied if the AG does not have sufficient economic substance in Switzerland (adequate office space, local employees, actual management decisions, and core business activities). A PropTech SaaS AG must ensure that its cloud platform development, customer contracts, and product management decisions are demonstrably executed in Switzerland.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 100% of the evaded federal tax plus the tax due and interest; cantonal penal surcharges up to CHF 20,000; the claim for tax also survives a liquidation of the AG. In severe cases, criminal prosecution leads to imprisonment of the responsible person.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency of the AG if they personally participated in or negligently failed to stop the evasion. This can lead to civil restitution of the tax stolen, a travel ban (12 months or more), and imprisonment up to 1 year for wilful evasion (Art. 175 DBG).
Non-filing / Late Filing of Annual Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Procedural fine up to CHF 10,000; automatic assessment for 3 years at the tax administration's discretion (Ermessenveranlagung); a default surcharge equal to 0.5% per month of the assessed federal tax, capped at 20%.

Director personal liability: A director who is personally responsible for the filing and who fails after warning is subject to a personal fine of up to CHF 2,000; for persistent refusal, imprisonment for up to 6 months (Art. 196 DBG).
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and statutory exemptions (participation, IP box); recharacterisation of income as taxable profit; recomputation of withholding tax on deemed distributions; fines up to 100% of the tax benefit (Art. 57 DBG / Art. 10 VStG).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain the shell status are jointly and severally liable for all underpaid taxes, and may face criminal sanction for false certification; if they sign annual substance declarations they also incur civil liability under Art. 754 CO.
AML / Payment Intermediary Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CHF 500,000; cancellation of a required polygraph licence if the activity requires one; in extreme cases dissolution of the company and confiscation of assets (Art. 41 GwG).

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorised money-laundering compliance officer face personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 3 years and fines (Art. 305bis Criminal Code); the corporate veil is pierced for recovery of assets from unlawful activity.
