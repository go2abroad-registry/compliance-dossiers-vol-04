Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); portal: 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online (BRO)' — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Ontario's Ministry of Finance administers only provincial levies (e.g., Employer Health Tax); federal GST/HST, corporate/partnership income reporting, and Ontario tax collection are administered by CRA under the Ontario Tax Collection Reform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Perfect the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.5 (ServiceOntario Form 3 declaration) and obtain the Ontario file number. Step 2: Complete CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically via Business Registration Online; the general partner must identify all partners, and the LP receives a nine-digit Business Number (BN) plus a separate GST/HST program account ending in 'RT0001'. Step 3: The GST/HST registration is effective on the date the LP first makes a taxable supply (or the date the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is crossed, whichever is earlier); CRA issues the RC2 confirmation and the BN must be printed on invoices as 'GST/HST No. [BN RT0001]'. Step 4: File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return within five months of the fiscal year-end — mandatory where the LP has a non-resident partner, a corporate partner, more than five partners, or gross revenues over CAD 2,000,000. Step 5: Remit Part XIII non-resident partner withholding on Canadian-source income distributions, subject to a possible Form NR6 / s. 216 election; the LP must also file the GST/HST final return and cancel the account upon dissolution.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for income tax: income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level; there is no federal or Ontario income tax at the partnership level. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and is itself the registrant, collector, and remitter of HST (entity-level indirect taxation). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no general foreign exchange control law requiring central bank approval, license, or repatriation quota for profit distributions, consortium equity injections, or trade settlements. Profit remittances and limited partnership distributions to non-resident partners are freely permitted, subject to: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act; (ii) Ontario Limited Partnership reporting of non-resident partners and the LP's registered office; (iii) FINTRAC record keeping and suspicious transaction reporting; and (iv) applicable tax treaty withholding reduction under the Canada-U.S. or other applicable treaty. E-commerce proceeds received through Stripe, PayPal, and other PSPs in CAD or foreign currency may be held in domestic or offshore accounts without conversion mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines in Canada. Exporters and e-commerce operators may retain foreign currency freely in Canadian or overseas bank accounts. Applicable compliance obligations are limited to: (i) reporting of cross-border currency or monetary instruments physically transported in or out of Canada in excess of CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency; (ii) record keeping of wire transfers and trade documentation under the PCMLTFA Regulations; and (iii) CRA reporting of specified foreign property (T1135) when the aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000, which may include offshore PSP settlement balances or foreign bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT and cross-border transaction oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); monetary policy and currency issuance by the Bank of Canada under the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium (conditional). A Canadian limited partnership may lawfully use a fintech EMI or international payment service provider for cross-border collections and multi-currency working capital, provided the EMI is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business (MSB) or holds an equivalent foreign payment institution license, and maintains segregated client funds. Legal constraints include: (i) mandatory FINTRAC MSB registration for Canadian-provided payment services; (ii) Canadian bank 'de-risking' policies that may refuse settlement accounts for PSP-only e-commerce flows; (iii) the inability of an offshore EMI to provide a Canadian domestic clearing number for LVTS/ACSS participation; and (iv) enhanced due diligence triggered by non-resident limited partners. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the non-resident limited partner instructs its foreign bank to initiate a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the LP's Canadian Tier 1 bank account (LVTS participant), using the LP's legal name and account number, and embedding in field 70 the remittance information 'Capital contribution to Ontario limited partnership per LP Agreement dated [date]' together with an ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'CFDI' (capital transfer to partnership) or 'CCRD' where required. The Canadian receiving bank will apply source-of-funds verification, FINTRAC-compliant record keeping, and potentially enhanced due diligence for non-resident remitters. Intraday CAD clearing is effected through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day value, while smaller contributions clear through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). For physically transported currency or bearer negotiable instruments exceeding CAD 10,000, a FINTRAC Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report (E667) and CBSA declaration is mandatory; electronic wire injections do not require prior regulatory approval but must be documented against the LP capital account ledger.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (i) high-value cross-border supplier payments via SWIFT MT103, using correspondent banking networks of the Canadian Schedule I bank; (ii) commercial letters of credit (advised, confirmed, or standby) issued by the Canadian bank for the purchase of consumer electronics and smart gadgets from Asia and the United States, with documents drawn under UCP600; (iii) domestic CAD disbursements via ACSS (ACH-equivalent electronic funds transfer) or LVTS for same-day obligations; (iv) USD-dominated settlements via the Canadian bank's United States clearing arrangement or an in-Canada USD clearing account; and (v) PSP payout structures (Stripe, PayPal) in which the merchant settlement is routed to the LP's primary CAD operating account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Ontario (11.5%) general corporate income tax rate; the Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent — the LP itself pays no income tax, and each partner reports their allocated share of Canadian-source business income at their own marginal rate (individual top marginal rates up to 53.53% federal+Ontario combined; corporate partners at 26.5%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal period-end; for a standard December 31 year-end the return is due by May 31 of the following year. T5013 slips must be furnished to every partner by the same date.
