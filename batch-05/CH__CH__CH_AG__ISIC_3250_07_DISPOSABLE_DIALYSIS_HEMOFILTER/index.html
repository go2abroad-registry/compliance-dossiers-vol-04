Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration Fédérale des Contributions AFC), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern, Switzerland; official portals: www.esty.admin.ch, www.vat.admin.ch (VAT), www.uid.admin.ch (business identification number); cantonal and communal tax administrations (e.g., Zug, Zurich) administer corporate income tax and capital tax at cantonal/communal level; commercial register data published via www.zefix.ch.

E-government filing portal

The Swiss AG is entered in the commercial register (Handelsregister) of its registered canton; the Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the Swiss Business Identification Number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer UID, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX), which is the sole statutory tax identifier for VAT, direct taxes, and statistics. The AG files Form 'Angaben für die Mehrwertsteuer' with the ESTV VAT Main Division to enter the Swiss VAT register; the ESTV confirms registration in writing ('Bestätigung der UID'). The canton of the registered office then opens a corporate income tax/capital tax file; no separate federal corporate income tax number exists. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office that makes taxable supplies of more than CHF 100,000 in Switzerland must obtain a UID either through registration of a Swiss branch in the commercial register or by direct application to the Federal Statistical Office/ESTV under the simplified foreign-besucher procedure; a foreign parent company holding shares of the Swiss AG does not itself require a Swiss tax ID unless it has a Swiss permanent establishment or VAT nexus. VAT registration must occur within 30 days after the taxable turnover threshold is exceeded; registration is effective on the date the UID is communicated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the AG is an autonomous Swiss legal person subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability on worldwide profits at federal (effective approx. 4.4 percent on pre-tax profit after proportional deduction of federal tax), cantonal and communal levels; no pass-through of profits or losses to shareholders; distributions are subject to 35 percent Swiss withholding tax. Territorial scope: resident AGs are taxable on worldwide income, with exemption/credit for foreign permanent establishments and real estate, and with the participation exemption (Beteiligungsabzug) for qualifying dividends and capital gains on participations of at least 10 percent or a market value above CHF 1 million at federal level. Tax-Exempt Zone: not applicable; however, the AG can use the Swiss patent box (up to 90 percent cantonal relief on qualifying IP income) and the notional interest deduction on equity under the 2020 Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (STAF). The AG is not eligible for any check-the-box pass-through regime in Switzerland.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box classification election. Available statutory/administrative elections relevant to a manufacturing AG: (1) option for voluntary VAT registration where taxable turnover is below the CHF 100,000 threshold (Art. 11 para 1 MWSTG) - limited to Swiss-resident taxable persons; (2) VAT group taxation (Art. 13 MWSTG) allowing a controlling Swiss parent and its Swiss subsidiaries to be treated as a single VAT person; (3) waiver of exemption from VAT for turnover below threshold (Art. 11 para 2 MWSTG); (4) direct tax group taxation (Steuergruppe) under Art. 61 StHG allowing the Swiss parent and Swiss subsidiaries to file consolidated Swiss taxable results; (5) cantonal privileged status elections under STAF/StHG: holding company status, domiciliary/mixed company status (legacy phase-out), finance branch notional interest deduction, and patent box election; (6) hybrid mismatch neutrality under Art. 8a StHG (effective 1 January 2020) - no elective hybrid regime, but double deduction and deduction-without-inclusion mismatches are automatically denied at the Swiss level; (7) for export-oriented AGs, election to use the 'Ostschweizer' deemed commercial substance procedure or advance tax ruling applications to the ESTV and cantonal authorities to fix transfer pricing and zero-rating treatment of cross-border sales.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover on supplies rendered in or from Switzerland (Art. 10 para 2 lit. a MWSTG). The threshold is calculated on the worldwide taxable turnover in Switzerland (excluding exempt supplies) and includes zero-rated export deliveries; a Swiss manufacturing AG with export turnover therefore remains registrable and must file VAT returns to recover input VAT. Foreign suppliers not domiciled in Switzerland are not eligible for the Art. 11 waiver; if their Swiss-taxable turnover does not exceed CHF 100,000, they cannot register for VAT and may instead claim input VAT refunds under the Swiss VAT refund procedure for foreign businesses (reciprocal refund, minimum CHF 500 per application, deadlines and documentation prescribed by ESTV). Registration is mandatory within 30 days after the calendar year in which the CHF 100,000 threshold is reached; new companies may register voluntarily from the start of business to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including disposable dialysis hemofilters manufactured under ISIC 3250, are taxable at the zero rate (Art. 23 para 1 lit. a MWSTG) when the goods are physically transported by the supplier, by a third party on the supplier's behalf, or by the foreign customer, from Swiss customs territory to a foreign destination and are delivered to a foreign recipient. The zero rate is mandatory and not optional; the supplier retains full input VAT deduction. The supplier must hold documentary proof of export (Nachweis der Ausfuhr) before applying the 0% rate; domestic delivery within Switzerland to a Swiss freight forwarder is taxable at 8.1% unless the goods leave Switzerland under the supplier's export customs declaration. Services directly connected to such exported goods (repair, cleaning, assembly, technical testing of hemofilters performed in Switzerland) are zero-rated under Art. 23 para 2 MWSTG where the goods are destined for export. B2B services with a foreign recipient's place of business abroad (e.g., engineering, R&D, licensing) are deemed supplied at the recipient's seat under Art. 8 MWSTG and are outside Swiss VAT scope with full input VAT recovery, subject to reverse-charge treatment abroad. The export invoice must be issued in net amounts and state '0% Exportsteuerbefreiung gemäss Art. 23 MWSTG'.

  • Foreign purchaser evidence: commercial register extract, foreign VAT ID, W-8BEN-E or CRS self-certification proving non-Swiss residence of the buyer
  • Swiss VAT invoice (Art. 26 MWSTG) containing the AG's UID and the export declaration reference number
  • For intra-group export sales: OECD transfer pricing documentation substantiating arm's length pricing and avoiding Swiss tax recharacterization

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government API, digital stamping, or pre-authorization system for B2B/B2C invoicing. The ESTV does not clear invoices before issuance. Mandatory invoice content under Art. 26 MWSTG for a registrant: supplier name and address, supplier UID, recipient name and address, date of supply, description and quantity of goods, consideration, VAT rate, and VAT amount; simplified invoices are permitted where the total amount is at most CHF 3,300. Electronic invoicing is legally accepted if the electronic document satisfies Art. 26 content requirements and is archived authentic and legible for 10 years. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is the standard e-payment invoice format. E-invoicing to the Swiss federal government is mandatory from 1 January 2025 for new contracts using Peppol BIS 3.0 and from 1 January 2026 for existing contracts; no such mandate applies to private-sector B2B. The absence of the supplier UID on an invoice causes loss of the input VAT deduction (Art. 28 para 4 MWSTG).

Swiss law applies the mercantile-trust principle (Vertrauensprinzip); the ESTV may audit export documentation and, if the customs declaration or transport evidence is incomplete or contradictory, will reverse the zero rate, assess VAT at 8.1% on the export proceeds, and impose default interest from the original tax period obligation. The ordinary assessment limitation is five years (Art. 69 para 1 MWSTG), extended to ten years in cases of tax evasion (Art. 69 para 2 MWSTG). Administrative fines: up to CHF 10,000 for procedural violations (Art. 60 MWSTG); negligent tax evasion is fined up to the evaded tax amount, and wilful under-declaration up to twice the evaded amount (Arts. 61-62 MWSTG). Serious repeated export fraud involving forged documents is prosecuted as Abgabebetrug under Art. 63 MWSTG with imprisonment of up to ten years and/or a fine of up to three times the evaded tax, in addition to the recomputed tax and interest. Directors, CFOs, and compliance officers may be held jointly and severally liable. No statutory FX repatriation deadline exists in Switzerland because the country maintains no exchange controls; the value 0 in the dossier reflects the absence of a mandatory deadline. However, the ESTV may apply economic substance scrutiny where export proceeds remain permanently unrealized, potentially recharacterizing the transaction as a financing arrangement for Swiss direct tax purposes; for VAT purposes, the critical compliance risk is incomplete documentary proof. For the medical-device sector, an export-fraud finding also triggers Swissmedic licensing review and EU MDR vigilance obligations and may expose the AG to debarment from Swiss federal procurement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility for the Swiss franc (CHF). The Foreign Exchange Ordinance and the free movement of capital pursuant to the Swiss Federal Constitution and international agreements (IMF Article VIII status) impose no general restriction on cross-border transfers, dividend repatriation, capital withdrawals, or shareholder loans. Profit remittance is subject only to Swiss withholding tax (35% on dividends, subject to treaty relief or domicile exemption) and corporate income tax at cantonal/federal levels; no currency conversion approval or central bank authorization is required. De facto convertibility is absolute; the CHF is a freely usable hard currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Switzerland. Swiss exporters are not obligated to repatriate receivables within a specific timeline, nor to convert export earnings into CHF. Companies may retain export proceeds in any currency, hold offshore bank accounts, or pass them through treasury centers, subject to standard transfer pricing and tax documentation rules. Regulatory reporting obligations are limited to SNB statistical surveys (direct investment and international trade in services) where turnover thresholds are met, but no permission or compulsory conversion is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary banking, Medium for supplementary payment routing. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK- or Lithuania-based electronic money institutions) generally do not possess a Swiss banking licence and cannot accept public deposits from Swiss customers without FINMA authorisation under the Swiss Banking Act. They may provide payment services only on a cross-border basis into Switzerland under limited issuance, but most compliance-conscious treasuries reject them as sole providers. A FINMA-licensed 'fintech company' (Innovation Permit under Art. 1b Banking Act) or a Swiss payment institution under the Financial Institutions Act (FinIA) is viable for high-volume low-value payments, but capital escrow, trade finance (LC), and cross-border settlement require a regulated tier-1 Swiss bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: (1) Inbound equity injection from parent shareholder via SWIFT MT103 (primary) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) using the company's name and one-time purpose code. Standardised SEPA/ISO 20022 pain.001 is accepted for Euro funding via SEPA Credit Transfer from the EU. Verify the IBAN of the Swiss account (CH…), BIC of the receiving bank, and include a payment purpose statement: 'Capital increase / shareholder contribution / equity injection' together with reference to the Swiss Commercial Register excerpt (Handelsregisterauszug). Under Swiss AML rules, the receiving bank must record the ultimate originator; if the remittance exceeds CHF 30,000 and is not SEPA, the originator must be identified. For equity injections, the bank may require a notarial deed of capital increase and the annual general meeting resolution, but no central bank permit is necessary. Use 'Investment/Equity/Capital' SWIFT purpose codes (e.g., P0025 or P0101 under Swiss custom usage) where supported; for franc transfers, settle via SIX SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) in near-real time.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 single payment transfers for supplier downpayments, freight, insurance, customs, and operational disbursements; recommended under SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) for transaction tracking. (2) Documentary letter of credit (commercial LC) for bulk raw material import (e.g., polyethersulfone pellets, filter potting materials, precision nozzles) via SWIFT MT700/MT701 issued by the Swiss bank, advising through a correspondent bank in the exporter's jurisdiction, with settlement via SWIFT MT754/MT756 or MT202. (3) SEPA Direct Debit and SEPA Credit Transfer for EU counterparties where the beneficiary has an EU IBAN. (4) Correspondent banking through US banks for USD settlements, using Fedwire/CHIPS with an intermediate correspondent; incorporate ODFI/BIC details and intermediate account numbers. Each outbound disbursement must include a customs invoice, transport documents (Bill of Lading / AWB), certificate of origin (for EUR.1 or form A if eligible under Switzerland's trade agreements), and a purpose code aligned with the underlying commercial contract (e.g., P0126 for goods purchase).
