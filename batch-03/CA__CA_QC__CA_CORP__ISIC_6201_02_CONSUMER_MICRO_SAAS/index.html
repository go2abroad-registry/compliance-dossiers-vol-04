Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST, federal corporate income tax, and Business Number (BN) administration via My Business Account portal; Revenu Quebec - Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec corporate income tax, and enterprise registration through the Registraire des entreprises using the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act; no minimum capital is required and non-resident share ownership is permitted. 2) Obtain the Quebec NEQ automatically from the Registraire des entreprises upon provincial registration. 3) Register with CRA through Business Registration Online to obtain the 9-digit BN and open the GST/HST program account (BN-RR). 4) Register for QST with Revenu Quebec; for a Quebec-incorporated entity, the QST registration is coordinated with the provincial corporate file and the federal GST/HST registration. 5) Open payroll program accounts (CRA RP account and Revenu Quebec employer accounts) only if employees are present; a micro-SaaS operated through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy normally has no Canadian payroll. 6) Retain the BN and NEQ for payment-platform onboarding, banking, and export zero-rating documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Business Corporation is always a separate entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (ss. 89 and 248). Available elections include: (i) GST/HST group registration election under Excise Tax Act s. 273 and the parallel QST group election under the Quebec Sales Tax Act; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 93 election on the disposition of a foreign affiliate's taxable surplus; (iii) Income Tax Act s. 216 election for non-resident corporations earning specified Canadian rental income - not applicable to a resident Canadian operating company; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch deduction elections under recently enacted ITA ss. 18.2-18.4 implementing BEPS Action 2 for cross-border payments involving hybrid entities such as a US LLC. None of these mechanisms converts CA_CORP into a pass-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148 and the Quebec Sales Tax Act: cumulative worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration becomes mandatory once this threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available to a Quebec-incorporated corporation from day one. Under a genuine Merchant-of-Record contract, Paddle or Lemon Squeezy will collect and remit GST/HST and QST on Canadian consumer sales, but the developer's supply to the non-resident platform may still be counted for threshold purposes - the MoR agreement must clearly establish principal versus agent status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to SaaS supplied to a non-resident for use entirely outside Canada under ETA Schedule VI Part IV s. 6 (exported intangible personal property) and Part V s. 7 (services to non-residents), mirrored by Quebec QST zero-rating rules. Core criteria: the recipient is a non-resident with no Canadian establishment; the SaaS is not used in connection with real or tangible property located in Canada; billing address, payment instrument, and IP geolocation all point outside Canada; and, in Merchant-of-Record sales, Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acquires title and resells to end customers. Zero-rated export revenue counts as a taxable supply for the CAD 30,000 registration threshold.

  • Goods-only export evidence (not applicable to pure digital SaaS): CBSA Customs Export Declaration (CADEX/ACI eManifest) and Bill of Lading/Air Waybill; no customs filing is required for SaaS delivered electronically.
  • Digital substantiation bundle: executed SaaS subscription or master licence agreement with the non-resident customer or Merchant of Record; invoices issued to the non-resident party; customer attestation of non-Canadian residence and place of use.
  • Payment and FX evidence: Paddle/Lemon Squeezy payout and settlement statements, inward remittance records, foreign-currency bank receipts, and FX conversion confirmations; plus IP-geolocation and access logs demonstrating non-Canadian download and use.

None. Canada and Quebec impose no national real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no digital stamping, and no mandatory API-based e-invoice validation. CRA and Revenu Quebec operate an audit-based documentary regime; PDF invoices and Paddle/Lemon Squeezy billing records are accepted. Voluntary e-invoicing follows the PEPPOL standard for federal procurement, and Quebec's certified sales recording module (SRM) applies only to designated point-of-sale sectors, not to ISIC 6201 SaaS operations.

CRA Export Review and Revenu Quebec audits closely scrutinize zero-rating claims. If denied, the supplier is reassessed for uncollected GST (5%) plus QST (9.975% on the GST-inclusive base) with retroactive effect within the four-year reassessment period (ETA s. 298 and Quebec Tax Administration Act), which is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation or fraud. Civil penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the tax or refund amount under ETA s. 285.1 and the corresponding Quebec provision. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and the Quebec Tax Administration Act carries fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment. Input tax credits and QST input tax refunds attributable to the claimed export sale are clawed back; if the Merchant-of-Record relationship is recharacterized as agency, CRA and Revenu Quebec may additionally assess unremitted GST/HST/QST on all Canadian consumer SaaS sales processed by Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, with compounded daily arrears interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial, investment, and treasury transactions. Canada generally has no capital-account approval requirement, exchange-control registration, mandatory use of domestic currency, or general restriction on repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, loan principal, or liquidation proceeds. Current-account payments are not subject to routine foreign-exchange licensing. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law authorization, solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening, lender or escrow restrictions, and bank documentation requirements. Payments involving restricted jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, unusual payment chains, or high-risk virtual-asset activity may be delayed or rejected. Quebec corporations must also consider applicable Quebec corporate-law approvals and French-language documentation requirements where relevant to local operations and contracting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or convert a prescribed percentage within a fixed period. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. Exporters must comply with applicable customs, export-control, sanctions, GST/HST, income-tax, accounting, and contractual requirements. Financial institutions must report certain international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, or equivalent, to FINTRAC when the applicable reporting conditions are met; this is a reporting obligation and not a conversion or repatriation requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange-control authority imposing currency conversion, capital-repatriation, or export-proceeds-surrender controls. Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance is administered principally by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), while cross-border currency reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for merchant collection and payout functions, subject to provider eligibility and underwriting; Low as a complete substitute for a Canadian domestic operating bank. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy generally operate as merchant-of-record or payment-platform providers rather than ordinary Canadian deposit-taking banks. They may collect subscription revenue, calculate or facilitate indirect taxes, and remit net proceeds to the corporation, but account availability, supported countries, currencies, payout destinations, reserves, chargeback treatment, prohibited-business rules, and customer-verification standards are contractual. They do not normally provide unrestricted deposit-taking, Canadian cheque services, domestic payroll functionality, or a guaranteed corporate banking relationship. The corporation must accurately identify the merchant of record, beneficial owner, operating location, software product, customer geography, refund policy, data practices, and tax registrations. Use of an offshore EMI or payment account does not eliminate Canadian corporate income-tax, GST/HST, Quebec sales-tax, transfer-pricing, sanctions, or AML obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route funds directly from the identified shareholder, parent, or lender bank account to the Canadian corporation's account at the receiving institution. Use a payment narrative such as 'equity subscription,' 'shareholder loan,' or 'intercompany loan' only when supported by executed documents. Canada does not use a universal central-bank purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate capital inflows; the bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification and supporting documents. Provide the corporation's exact legal name, account number, institution and transit details, SWIFT/BIC where applicable, remitter identity, ultimate beneficial owner, agreement date, currency, amount, and expected use of proceeds. Avoid third-party funding and split transfers designed to evade monitoring. Reconcile the incoming wire to the general ledger, issue shares or record debt consistently with the documents, assess thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing implications, and preserve records for tax, audit, and AML purposes. International electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, or equivalent, may trigger financial-institution reporting to FINTRAC; the corporation should not attempt to structure transfers to avoid reporting.For ordinary SaaS expenses, use bank wires, international electronic funds transfers, EFT, bill-payment rails, or corporate cards to verified suppliers. For substantial or documentary trade purchases, a commercial letter of credit may be issued or advised through a Canadian bank, with documentary conditions matching the underlying contract. A letter of credit is generally unnecessary for routine software subscriptions, cloud hosting, contractors, and digital advertising. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporation tax rate (Canada federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%); 12.2% combined rate applies to qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) active business income up to CA$500,000 per year

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada, before application of any tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. Quebec CO-17 filing deadline is also the last day of the sixth month following fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 of aggregate consideration for non-arm's length transactions with non-residents triggers the formal contemporaneous documentation requirement and the CRA Form T106 reporting obligation; a master/local file package is expected under CRA Transfer Pricing Memorandum when controlled transaction exposure exceeds CA$1,000,000, and Country-by-Country Report thresholds apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CA$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary operating corporations. However, CRA and Revenu Québec apply the substance-over-form doctrine, the business-purpose test, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act section 245 to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking commercial or economic substance. A Canadian micro-SaaS corporation must maintain genuine operational oversight in Canada: real board decisions, substantively located personnel, Canadian bank accounts, written contracts, and auditable books; otherwise it may be recharacterized as a conduit or deemed to have a permanent establishment or foreign residence for its beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act section 239(1): fine of at least 50% and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to two years on summary conviction. Civil gross-negligence penalty under Income Tax Act section 163(2) equals 50% of the understated tax. Revenu Québec imposes equivalent penalties under the Taxation Act, together with daily compound interest and the loss of all loss carryovers or credit claims.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, assent to, or acquiesce in false filings are personally liable as parties to the offence and may be convicted and imprisoned. Under the CBCA and QBCA, directors have an overarching fiduciary duty to ensure the corporation maintains accurate records; deliberate falsification permits piercing the corporate veil and personal civil restitution to the Crown. A director defending an evasion prosecution cannot shelter behind the corporate entity.
Unremitted Source Deductions, QST and Payroll Liabilities

Statutory sanction: Directors' liability assessment under Income Tax Act section 227.1 and Excise Tax Act section 323, with parallel liability under Quebec Taxation Act and QST Act for the full unremitted amount, plus interest and penalties. CRA and Revenu Québec may garnish personal bank accounts, impose liens on personal property, and deny discharge from tax debts in insolvency where trust amounts were misappropriated.

Director personal liability: Statutory director liability is automatic where the corporation fails to remit; no fraud is required. Each director and former director can be assessed personally and jointly and severally. Directors may be barred from leaving Canada on outstanding trust-fund assessments and may face criminal prosecution for intentional failure to remit.
Transfer Pricing, Profit Stripping and Management Fee Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA and Revenu Québec may recharacterize consideration to the arm's length amount under Income Tax Act section 247(2); a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment applies where the adjustment exceeds the prescribed threshold and no adequate contemporaneous documentation exists. Part XIII withholding can also be assessed at 25% on deemed dividends, royalties, interest, or management fees paid to non-residents, with interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing documentation or orchestrate sham intercompany transactions may be prosecuted for tax evasion. The courts may disregard the corporate veil where a wholly-owned IP-holding vehicle is a mere façade, leaving the director and controlling shareholder personally liable for the tax adjustment and penalties.
Shell Company, Sham Substance and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act section 245 disallows the tax benefit; Revenu Québec applies a parallel GAAR. Treaty benefits may be denied under the applicable DTA's limitation-on-benefits or beneficial ownership clause, and the corporation can be reassessed as a non-resident or as a permanent establishment of a foreign principal. Penalties include 50% of the amount of the misreporting plus interest; criminal tax evasion applies if deceit is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow a shell corporation to be used for tax avoidance are exposed to personal assessments for unremitted withholding or payroll trust amounts, and where the corporation is a sham, creditors and the tax authorities can pierce the veil. Directors can face prohibition from managing corporations, asset freezing, civil restitution, and imprisonment for knowingly participating in a sham or fraudulent arrangement.
AML, Payment Processor Misuse and Off-Book Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Criminal Code of Canada, laundering proceeds of crime is an indictable offence carrying up to ten years' imprisonment. Income Tax Act section 239 penalties apply to false books and omission of revenue: 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment. The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act authorizes large administrative monetary penalties and criminal sanctions for evasion of reporting requirements. Civil reassessment, gross negligence penalties, and compound interest apply regardless of criminal outcome.

Director personal liability: Directors participate criminally if they knowingly direct or tolerate payment-processing misconduct; personal convictions carry imprisonment. Separate from criminal liability, AML/OFAC sanctions violations and statutory director liability for unpaid corporate tax can be assessed directly against directors. Where the corporate shell is merely a conduit for diverted funds, courts will order disgorgement and civil restitution against the controlling director and permit attachment of personal assets.
