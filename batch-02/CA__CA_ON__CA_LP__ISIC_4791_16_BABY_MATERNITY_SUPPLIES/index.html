Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST account administration and federal tax filings; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario Retail Sales Tax (RST) and Ontario provincial tax accounts; Ontario Business Registry for extra-provincial limited partnership registration. CRA portal: My Business Account; GST/HST NETFILE returns through CRA-approved service providers.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) carrying on business in Ontario or otherwise making taxable supplies in Canada, the LP must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and register for the GST/HST account by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the applicable online registration through CRA My Business Account. A foreign LP that is not resident in Canada but is required to register for GST/HST because it makes taxable supplies in Canada may be required to file Form RC151 (GST/HST Registration for Non-Resident Businesses) or use the CRA online non-resident registration portal. Before or concurrently, the LP must be registered extra-provincially in Ontario under the Limited Partnerships Act by filing a Declaration of Limited Partnership with the Ontario Business Registry, and obtain an Ontario limited partnership registration number. For relevant employer activities in Ontario, an Employer Heath Tax (EHT) account may be required with the Ontario Ministry of Finance if remuneration paid in Ontario exceeds the statutory exemption threshold. An Ontario RST account is separately required only if the LP makes taxable retail sales of tangible personal property, which is generally not the case for an online e-commerce exporter; where required, registration is made through the Ontario Retail Sales Tax (RST) account system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian 'check-the-box' election is available. A limited partnership is, by default, fiscally transparent and not a taxpayer; each partner computes income from the partnership as if carrying on the business directly. No statutory election permits a partnership to be taxed as a corporation at the federal or Ontario level. Canada's anti-hybrid rules under section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act and the OECD hybrid mismatch rules will re-characterize or deny deductions to the extent a hybrid instrument or hybrid entity gives rise to double non-taxation; no elective mechanism exists to alter this outcome. For GST/HST, certain closely related corporations may elect to zero-rate internal supplies using the Related Entity Election (Form GST25 or GST26), but this does not apply to partnership equity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Cad $30,000 per calendar quarter or over any four consecutive calendar quarters in taxable supplies made in Canada; non-residents supplying services or digital products in Canada must register if the threshold is exceeded. A non-resident that exceeds the threshold is a registrant mandatorily. Exports of goods are zero-rated and do not require registration solely for export turnover unless domestic taxable supplies exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier has delivered the goods to a place outside Canada, has shipped them by common carrier to a non-resident consignee, or has exported them under the prescribed conditions. A zero-rated supply does not bear GST/HST, and the registrant may claim input tax credits on inputs used to make that supply. For services, a supply to a non-resident is generally zero-rated under section 23 of Part V of Schedule VI if the service is not performed in Canada even in part, or if the service is a tangible personal property service performed while the property is temporarily in Canada and is exported after the service. For an e-commerce seller of baby and maternity supplies, the core zero-rating criterion is documented proof of physical export, including the supplier's invoice, freight terms, customs entry, and payment evidence showing that the goods left Canada.

  • A written sale invoice containing full supplier and customer details, description, quantity, and price
  • Customs Export Declaration (via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or Form B13A Export Declaration, where required by export reporting regulations)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing destination outside Canada and the consignee outside Canada
  • Container or shipment tracking evidence from the carrier confirming arrival outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer records showing receipt of payment in a foreign currency from the foreign buyer or from the non-resident e-commerce marketplace
  • Any third-party logistics (3PL) export certificates, packing lists, and transit documents evidencing departure from Canada

Canada does not currently impose a real-time continuous transaction control or digital stamping mandate. There is no mandatory national electronic invoicing clearance gateway for GST/HST. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices if the format contains all prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (i.e., supplier name and registration number, date of invoice, a description sufficient to identify the supply, consideration, and applicable taxes). CRA requires that books and records be kept in Canada or be made available to the CRA in an accessible format upon request, including electronic records. For high-volume e-commerce, CRA may require exemption-certificate registrations, e-payment logs, and bank records as supporting evidence. Procurement digital record burdens are limited to the information that must be included on invoices and the obligation to retain transactional data for at least six years.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rating relief where export documentation is inadequate or where the goods did not actually leave Canada. Under section 281.1 of the Excise Tax Act, a registrant may be assessed a penalty equal to the greater of $100 and 25% of any net tax deficiency attributable to false statements, misinformation, or omissions made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. CRA may apply the general penalty under section 280 for failure to file returns, and the criminal provisions of section 327 of the Excise Tax Act apply where a person willfully makes a false or deceptive statement; upon conviction, penalties include fines of up to $50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years. In addition, under section 298, interest compounds daily on unpaid amounts, and reassessments of a zero-rated export can invalidate claimed input tax credits retrospectively, leaving the taxpayer liable for uncollected GST/HST plus interest and penalties. The CRA's audit practice for export zero-rating transactions requires production of the complete shipment-payment nexus within 30 days of initial audit notice, and unexplained discrepancies will trigger a full forensic review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. There are no exchange-control permits, central-bank pre-approvals, or deposit surrender requirements for inbound equity contributions, intercompany loans, trade settlements, or profit/partnership distributions to non-resident partners. The material operational hurdles are (i) FINTRAC reporting of large electronic funds transfers (threshold CAD 10,000 per transaction) and (ii) Canadian withholding tax on non-resident partners' share of Canadian-source income (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally 25% unless reduced by an income tax treaty), which is an administrative tax matter, not an exchange-control restriction. The Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisition of direct control of Canadian businesses at statutory financial thresholds, which an Ontario limited partnership in e-commerce does not ordinarily trigger.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Online-sale receipts from maternity and infant commodities (ISIC 4791) may be retained in foreign currency or converted into CAD at the entity's discretion. All export-related foreign-currency receipts are freely disposable, subject only to standard recordkeeping and FINTRAC wire-reporting obligations when a single transaction reaches or exceeds CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). The Bank of Canada administers no foreign-exchange permit system; all FX regulation is indirect and financial-integrity driven.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-to-High, but only through FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or payment service providers (PSPs) that are licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Offshore digital EMIs (non-Canadian, European-style e-money institutions) without a Canadian MSB/PSP registration cannot lawfully hold deposits, operate payment rails, or offer true account services in Canada. Stripe and PayPal are viable for receivables (the enterprise's stated target rails) because their Canadian entities are FINTRAC-registered MSBs, but they are not substitutes for a Tier-1 bank account and do not provide CDIC deposit insurance. For treasury-grade capital injection, supplier settlement, and FX hedging, a Tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory. Fintechs may be used as settlement collection nodes, with automatic sweeps into the LP's bank account to minimize counterparty risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) The non-resident sponsor wires funds via SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the Ontario LP's account at a Canadian Tier-1 bank, with beneficiary field citing the LP's full legal name, Ontario LP registration number, and the annotation 'Capital contribution — subscription for LP units'; (2) the ISO 20022 / SWIFT purpose description should use an equity-investment purpose code (e.g., 'EQUI') to avoid misclassification as trade proceeds or a loan; (3) both the originating and receiving banks will file FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for any transaction ≥ CAD 10,000, so the LP must maintain a reconciled capital-contribution schedule matching all wires to subscription agreements; (4) if the subscription constitutes a security, the issuance must be exempt under National Instrument 45-106 (e.g., accredited investor or family/friend exemption) and documented with a form NI 45-106F6 report filed with the Ontario Securities Commission; (5) no Bank of Canada approval is required, but the CRA may apply income-tax debt-equity characterization (thin-capitalization rules) if the contribution is structured partly as partner debt; (6) funds arriving in CAD are immediately available through the CPA ACSS domestic clearing; USD contributions may be cleared via correspondent banks in New York and settled in USD, with optional same-day FX conversion to CAD through the bank's dealing desk.For supplier settlements: (1) Routine and recurring supplier invoices are paid via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in CAD or USD, using a trade-in-goods payment purpose description such as 'Payment for inventory — baby and maternity products (HS 343? use appropriate HS chapter)' to ensure correct trade classification and FINTRAC reporting; (2) for new or high-ticket suppliers, a sight Commercial Letter of Credit is opened via SWIFT MT700, payable against presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, certificate of origin, and packing list; (3) for open-account credit terms, documentary collections (D/P or D/A) through the Canadian bank's correspondent network provide a low-cost alternative with title retention; (4) domestic Canadian supplier payments clear through CPA ACSS using EFT/direct deposit, while USD-denominated domestic payments settle via the Canadian USD clearing system (US ACH via correspondent); (5) receivables from Stripe/PayPal in CAD/USD are swept daily into the LP's Tier-1 operating account to minimize PSP float and concentration risk; the LP should hold a separate merchant settlement reserve in a CDIC-insured deposit or Government of Canada treasury bill to cover refunds and chargebacks.Outright deliverable CAD/USD foreign-exchange forward contracts (rolling up to 12 months), executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a counterparty Tier-1 bank; no OSFI or Bank of Canada approval is required for genuine commercial hedging of forecast inventory purchases and collection inflows.Cross-currency swaps (CAD vs. USD) to hedge long-term notional exposure arising from non-resident partner capital accounts, intercompany partner loans, and periodic distribution flows; requires the Ontario LP to enter into a Credit Support Annex (CSA) for collateral posting and to obtain general-partner board authorization.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore Asian currencies (e.g., CNY) for hedging supplier payables in currencies with limited offshore convertibility; CAD/USD NDFs are unnecessary because CAD is fully deliverable, but NDFs for third-country supplier currency exposures are permissible through a Canadian bank's offshore booking desk.
