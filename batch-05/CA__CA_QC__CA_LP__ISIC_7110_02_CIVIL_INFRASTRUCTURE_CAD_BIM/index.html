Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST, Income Tax Act administration, Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; Revenu Québec (www.revenuquebec.ca) — Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial income tax, QST registration and filing for all registrants with a Quebec establishment; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca) — mandatory Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) for any partnership carrying on an enterprise in Quebec.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint an agent for service of process in Canada if the foreign LP has no place of business in Canada. Step 2: Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account via CRA Business Registration Online (form RC1 or RC160 for non-resident registrants). Step 3: Register the limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ (mandatory before carrying on an enterprise in Quebec). Step 4: File the joint GST/HST-QST registration (form LM-1-V) with Revenu Québec to activate the QST number; Quebec administers the federal GST/HST collection for Quebec-based suppliers under the CRA-Revenu Québec collection agreement. Step 5: Registration becomes compulsory in the calendar quarter in which worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold to recover input tax refunds. Step 6: Non-resident LPs must file a partnership information return (TP-600 for Quebec; T5013/Partnership Return if any member is Canadian-resident) and may be required to withhold on distributions to non-resident partners under Part XIII.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes under ITA s. 96: each partner computes its share of income as if the business were carried on directly, and no entity-level income tax is imposed on the LP itself. For GST/HST/QST, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' (ETA s. 248; QSTA) and must register once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. Territorial scope: Canada taxes each partner on worldwide income if resident, but non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income allocated from the partnership, subject to tax treaty relief. No income-tax-exempt zones or special tax-free jurisdictions exist within Quebec; the federal income tax and Quebec provincial tax apply concurrently.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; available statutory and administrative elections relevant to a CA_LP include: (i) ETA s. 150 voluntary registration election permitting a small-supplier LP to register early and claim input tax credits; (ii) ETA s. 211 joint venture election permitting a qualifying civil-infrastructure joint venture to be treated as a single person for GST/HST; (iii) ETA s. 227 quick-method election (generally irrelevant for zero-rated exports); (iv) ITA s. 96(9) election to deem a partnership to be a corporation for limited statutory purposes where conditions are met; (v) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners to file a Canadian Part I return on Canadian-source income instead of Part XIII withholding; (vi) ITA s. 18.4 excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) calculations for cross-border hybrid partnership debt; (vii) QSTA provisions permitting a non-resident registrant to elect mirror treatment of zero-rated supplies for QST; and (viii) hybrid mismatch recharacterization rules under ITA s. 18.2/18.21 for interest or royalty payments with disproportionate tax treatment in a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1) and mirror QST threshold; measured on worldwide taxable supplies over the current calendar quarter plus the four preceding calendar quarters; exemption applies to non-residents only if they do not carry on business in Canada, in which case no GST/HST or QST registration is required for zero-rated offshore BIM/CAD services; registration must occur once the threshold is exceeded or when the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada other than zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (and the mirror QSTA rules for Quebec sales tax), a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated at 0% GST and 0% QST where the supply is not performed in whole or in part in Canada in direct relation to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or an individual physically present in Canada at the time the service is performed. Offshore BIM/CAD drafting and structural design (ISIC 7110 / NAICS 541330) delivered electronically to a non-resident civil-infrastructure client will qualify for zero-rating provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person not registered under ETA s. 241 nor carrying on business in Canada; (ii) the contractual deliverables (digital models, drawings, BIM data) are transmitted electronically outside Canada; and (iii) no Canadian real property, Canadian-situated goods, or services to individuals physically present in Canada are involved. If the CAD/BIM output is used for a Canadian project or the recipient is registered for GST/HST, the supply becomes taxable at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST.

  • Signed ISIC 7110 services agreement and statement of work identifying the non-resident client, delivery jurisdiction, and clause confirming electronic delivery of BIM/CAD deliverables outside Canada
  • Commercial invoices marked 'Zero-rated export — ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 / QSTA Schedule VII' showing the foreign recipient's name, address, and tax identification number
  • Electronic-delivery log or cloud/FTP upload certificate evidencing transmission of the drawing files and BIM models to the foreign client outside Canada
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103, Wire/Wise transaction record, or bank statement showing foreign-currency or CAD settlement originating from the non-resident client
  • Non-resident status certificate or statutory declaration from the client (foreign TIN/VAT registration certificate) and an internal zero-rating substantiation memo filed with the GST/HST and QST returns
  • Note: A Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) and bill of lading are not required for pure services exports; such customs documentation would apply only if tangible goods are also shipped, in which case the B13A must be filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) before departure

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance regime exists in Canada or Quebec as of the current tax law state. Electronic invoicing in PDF/EDI or PEPPOL-compliant formats is accepted where the registrant maintains a complete and verifiable audit trail. Canada has announced a voluntary federal e-invoicing roadmap based on the PEPPOL framework, but no legislation mandates its use. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns and certain prescribed electronic remittances for large accounts through its Mon Dossier Entreprise portal; however, zero-rated export invoices for offshore BIM/CAD services do not require pre-clearance, fiscal stamping, or CRA/Revenu Québec approval.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period, extended to 10 years where a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default is established. If zero-rating substantiation is deficient, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST plus 9.975% QST, with retroactive assessment, prescribed-rate arrears interest, and penalties: (i) late-filing/remittance penalty of 5% of the unremitted net tax plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months (maximum 17%) under ETA s. 280; (ii) false statements or omissions penalty of CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 per occurrence under ETA s. 285; (iii) tax-evasion penalties of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment on indictment under ETA s. 239, with mirror provisions in the QSTA; (iv) GAAR recharacterization under ETA s. 274; and (v) for the partners, ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the federal income tax avoided. Revenu Québec applies reciprocal penalties and interest on QST amounts, and the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is administratively monitored; balances outstanding beyond 180 days may trigger imputed interest on intercompany or partner advances under ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing rules and Part XIII withholding exposure on deemed benefits.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, and Canada has no general approval requirement for an LP's ordinary inbound investment, foreign borrowing, foreign-currency conversion, or outbound payment. Transactions remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, bank underwriting, tax rules, securities and investment rules where applicable, and reporting obligations. There is no general statutory profit-remittance approval for a Quebec LP. Distributions and allocations must follow the partnership agreement and applicable partnership, solvency, tax, and withholding rules. A non-resident partner's Canadian-source income may be subject to Canadian tax, withholding, information reporting, and treaty procedures; the tax treatment depends on the income, partner, and structure. Obtain Canadian tax advice before distributions, particularly where the LP carries on business in Canada or has non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio for receipts from BIM, drafting, or engineering services. Exporters may generally retain receipts in foreign currency or convert them through a financial institution. Canadian export, tax, customs, sanctions, and statistical-reporting obligations may apply depending on the services, customer, destination, and transaction; banks may request contracts, invoices, proof of service, and payment-purpose details.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration or routine exchange-control authority imposing licensing or surrender requirements on ordinary corporate cross-border payments. The Canada Border Services Agency, FINTRAC, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Department of Finance Canada, and financial institutions have separate customs, anti-money-laundering, prudential, sanctions, and reporting roles.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for payment-service providers that accept Canadian business customers; provider eligibility, available currencies, and product functionality vary. Wise Business may be considered where the LP and its beneficial owners meet current eligibility and verification requirements, but Wise is not a Canadian deposit-taking bank and its services are not a substitute for a Canadian bank account. Confirm the provider's Canadian regulatory status, safeguarding and deposit-protection treatment, permitted use, transaction limits, and ability to support the LP's ownership and payment corridors. An offshore electronic-money institution does not avoid Canadian AML, sanctions, tax, or bank reporting obligations and may be unable to provide local CAD account details or required Canadian payment services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route contributions and loans through an account held in the LP's legal name at a Canadian financial institution or an appropriately regulated provider that accepts the entity and corridor. Use an authenticated SWIFT customer credit transfer or another bank-supported payment rail, with the LP's exact legal name, account details, remitter identity, and a concise, accurate purpose such as partner capital contribution or documented partner loan. No universal Canadian purpose code or foreign-exchange approval code applies to ordinary private-sector funding; follow any fields requested by the remitting or receiving institution.
