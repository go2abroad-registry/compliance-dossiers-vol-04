Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including its online My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO); for Quebec-administered provincial tax accounts, Revenu Québec, including its My Account for businesses portal.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada generally obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts, including a GST/HST account, through BRO, the CRA, or an authorized representative. A non-resident corporation may apply for a BN and GST/HST account through the CRA’s non-resident registration procedures; it may need to provide incorporation, identity, business-activity, and Canadian-supply information and may be required to appoint a Canadian representative or provide security, depending on its circumstances. A corporation required to register for Quebec sales tax (QST) must register with Revenu Québec; Quebec-based businesses generally register through Revenu Québec, while certain suppliers outside Quebec register under the specified QST system. A corporation incorporated federally or in Quebec remains subject to applicable federal and Quebec corporate income-tax filing and registration requirements. Registration obligations depend on the entity’s actual activities, place of business, and supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate taxable entities; Canada has no general check-the-box election to treat a corporation as a flow-through entity. Available elections are transaction- or regime-specific and do not generally change the corporation’s underlying tax classification. Cross-border hybrid arrangements may be subject to Canada’s hybrid mismatch rules, including rules addressing deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes; eligibility and consequences require transaction-specific analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters for the GST/HST small-supplier test, subject to statutory conditions and exceptions. Quebec generally has a CAD 30,000 threshold for specified non-resident suppliers’ taxable supplies to specified Quebec consumers under the specified QST system; ordinary QST registration obligations and exceptions must be assessed separately. The GST rate is 5%; the Quebec QST rate is 9.975%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated if the statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains adequate evidence of export. Exported services may be zero-rated where the applicable place-of-supply and statutory conditions are satisfied; exclusions apply, including certain services relating to Canadian real property, tangible personal property, or persons in Canada. A sale to a non-resident is not, by itself, sufficient to establish zero-rating. A registrant may generally claim input tax credits or input tax refunds for eligible tax paid on inputs used in commercial activities, subject to documentation, allocation, and filing rules.

  • Canada Customs export declaration or applicable export-reporting evidence, where required
  • Commercial invoice, customer order, and shipping records identifying the goods and destination
  • Carrier-issued bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or equivalent proof of shipment and delivery outside Canada
  • Proof of payment and customer correspondence, where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • Foreign-exchange or inward-remittance evidence may support the audit trail but is not a general statutory prerequisite for GST/QST export zero-rating

Canada has no general real-time tax-invoice clearance, digital-stamping, or mandatory API pre-clearance regime for ordinary GST/QST invoices. Registrants must issue and retain invoices and prescribed supporting records meeting applicable CRA and Revenu Québec requirements. Quebec’s mandatory-invoicing rules apply to specified sectors and circumstances, not generally to online retail sales of 3D printers or consumables. Electronic records must remain accessible and auditable.

Canada imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must substantiate the statutory zero-rating conditions and retain records for the applicable retention period. Unsupported or incorrectly zero-rated supplies may be reassessed at the applicable GST/HST or QST rate, with interest and penalties. False statements or knowing or grossly negligent misrepresentations may attract additional statutory penalties; fraud may result in prosecution. Input tax credits or refunds claimed without adequate support may be denied or recovered, with applicable interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, bank policies, and any transaction-specific regulatory restrictions. There is no general requirement to obtain prior approval to exchange currency, remit dividends, repay bona fide shareholder debt, or transfer capital across the border. Distributions remain subject to corporate-law solvency tests, applicable withholding tax, treaty eligibility and documentation, and Canadian tax filing and payment obligations. A Canadian-resident corporation with non-resident ownership may have Canadian reporting obligations, including foreign-affiliate or non-arm's-length transaction reporting where applicable. Banks may require evidence of source and purpose of funds, beneficial ownership, underlying contracts, invoices, and tax or corporate approvals before processing material transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Canada and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, sanctions, tax, accounting, and contractual requirements. The corporation should retain export contracts, customs and shipping records, invoices, and receipt evidence to support tax, audit, and bank reviews.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange control authority or routine exchange-control licensing regime. The Bank of Canada conducts monetary policy and oversees designated financial market infrastructures; it does not approve ordinary corporate foreign-currency conversions. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers applicable anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting obligations. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and ancillary cross-border payments; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account or as a capital-escrow solution. An offshore electronic-money institution does not, solely by virtue of its foreign authorization, provide Canadian deposit insurance or establish a Canadian deposit-taking relationship. Confirm the provider's Canadian regulatory status, permitted activities, safeguarding arrangements, settlement bank, CAD availability, chargeback and reserve terms, and ability to support the corporation's legal name and Canadian tax profile. Payment service providers may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC requirements depending on their activities. Stripe and Shopify Payments availability and underwriting are conditional on eligibility, verification, product terms, and ongoing risk review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Form T106 are required when aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions involving goods, services, royalties, or interest exceed CAD 1 million in a fiscal year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are applicable only for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 750 million. Quebec follows the federal transfer-pricing documentation requirements without imposing a separate threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the business purpose doctrine, and substance-over-form jurisprudence to disregard shell companies or conduit arrangements whose sole purpose is a tax benefit. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules and treaty-shopping protections under the OECD MLI also deny benefits where the recipient lacks economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax under Income Tax Act s. 163(2); criminal liability on conviction for tax evasion under s. 239 carries a fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years for individuals; Quebec counterpart provisions expose the corporation to analogous fines and possible suspension of licences or registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in, authorize, or seal false filings are jointly and severally liable for civil penalties; the corporate veil is pierced under common law fraud, and personal bankruptcy prevention, travel bans, seizure orders, and imprisonment under the Criminal Code are available sanctions. A director may also face a third-party penalty under s. 163.2.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Leakage

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessments increase income and apply penalties under s. 247(3) of the Income Tax Act equal to 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable efforts were not made to document the arm's-length price. Revenu Quebec may impose a parallel provincial transfer-pricing penalty of 10% plus interest on the re-assessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily liable for transfer-pricing penalties if acting within corporate governance, but directors who sign false information returns or cause the corporation to omit required disclosures may incur wilful-evasion penalties, civil recovery for resulting corporate losses, and liability under third-party penalty provisions in insolvency scenarios.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard transactions under GAAR, treat the arrangement as a sham, deny Part XIII treaty benefits, and reassess the Canadian entity on the full income generated. Penalties include 50% gross-negligence penalties plus interest, and foreign tax authorities may trigger cross-border anti-avoidance reviews.

Director personal liability: Where the substance-less Canadian corporation is a vehicle of tax avoidance, directors and principals are exposed under GAAR's consequential adjustment power. Courts may order civil restitution or unjust enrichment, and Criminal Code fraud sanctions can apply when the shell is used to conceal revenue from creditors or tax authorities.
Non-Resident Withholding Compliance

Statutory sanction: The CRA assesses the corporation as agent or trustee for the unwithheld amount and adds a 10% failure-to-deduct penalty plus interest. Revenu Quebec independently assesses provincial withholding tax under the Taxation Act for dividends or royalties paid to non-residents.

Director personal liability: Directors are deemed personally liable for unremitted withheld taxes under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and for GST/HST trust amounts under the Excise Tax Act. These claims are secured and may be collected against personal assets without piercing the corporate veil, including through travel bans and bankruptcy proceedings.
