Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a current account convertibility regime fully aligned with IMF Article VIII obligations. Capital account transactions are subject to post-transaction notification requirements to the Bank of Canada rather than prior approval for most corporate entities. Profit remittance is permitted without restriction once tax compliance, beneficial ownership verification, and FINTRAC AML obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Canada. No mandatory surrender ratio applies; conversion occurs at prevailing market rates. Exporters are required to deliver export documentation to authorized foreign exchange dealers within statutory timelines, with mandatory statistical reporting to the Bank of Canada via Form 1135 or equivalent. Non-repatriation may trigger Income Tax Act penalties and potential license suspension under the Export and Import Permits Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institution cross-border viability remains severely constrained in Canada. Offshore platforms must obtain FINTRAC registration and may be prohibited from operating in high-risk sectors such as energy commodity trading without specific exemptions. Domestic Schedule I banks remain the only compliant channel for full cross-border payment and treasury services.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined for general business corporation in Ontario (federal Part I general rate: 15% after 10% provincial abatement and 13% General Rate Reduction; Ontario General Corporate Rate: 11.5%). Manufacturing and processing profits: 25.0% combined (federal 15% + Ontario M&P 10%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s. 212(2) on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: aggregate fair market value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions triggering mandatory Form T106 filing. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (functional, risk, comparability analysis) is required to avoid the 10% penalty under ITA s. 247(3). Country-by-Country Report (CRA Form RC4649) required for Canadian-headquartered multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE/Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) and treaty Limitation on Benefits / Principal Purpose Test under the OECD Multilateral Instrument require genuine commercial substance. A 'brass-plate' Ontario broker with no office, employees, or risk management functions will have its intercompany marine-fuel brokerage recharacterized by the CRA, treaty benefits denied, and income reallocated to the jurisdiction of actual decision-making.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated amount; compounded daily arrears interest; Ontario prosecutions under the Corporations Information Act also apply for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding and GST/HST; courts may lift the corporate veil, issue asset-freeze orders under s. 225.2, impose travel restrictions as bail conditions, and sentence directors to imprisonment for wilful evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; reassessment of income; daily arrears interest; secondary adjustment for deemed dividend or shareholder loan triggering Part XIII WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false T106 returns or knowingly arrange non-arm's length pricing may be assessed under s. 163.2, face professional disciplinary action, and be personally implicated in fraudulent conveyance claims where assets were stripped from the Canadian entity.
Shell Company / Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial under s. 245; forfeiture of treaty benefits under Limitation on Benefits / Principal Purpose Test; reallocation of income with 50% gross-negligence penalty; CRA may void the purported reimbursement of commissions and deem them dividends subject to Part XIII WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors may be treated as trustees of deemed trust amounts, lose Ontario fuel-trading licences under the Technical Standards and Safety Act, and face Criminal Code fraud charges for sham documentation supporting conduit transactions.
AML/KYC Breach (PCMLTFA and FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 for a corporation per serious violation; criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 10 years; property restraint and forfeiture orders; revocation of the Ontario fuel licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly fail to implement AML controls may be charged as parties to the offence; courts can impose travel bans as bail terms, freeze personal assets under restraint orders, and the regulator can permanently bar directors from commodity-trading activities.
Withholding Tax Default (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(9) assessment of the full unremitted amount plus interest and penalties; s. 227(8) penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount where the return was not filed and amounts not remitted; CRA may garnish the Canadian payer's bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII withholding without proof of fault; CRA pursues personal bank accounts, real estate, and intercompany receivables through civil enforcement and can obtain a director travel ban pending criminal fraud proceedings.
