Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada; taxpayer portal: 'My Business Account' (MyBA) for GST/HST and corporate income tax; business registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online; provincial counterpart: Ontario Ministry of Finance (for Ontario income tax administered by CRA under a single administration agreement)

E-government filing portal

A Canadian business corporation (federal or provincial) obtains its statutory tax identity through CRA Business Number (BN) registration. The foreign parent or non-resident establishing a Canadian corporation must complete CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' online or by mail, providing the incorporation certificate/Articles, directors' information, registered office address, and details of all program accounts needed (income tax account '0', GST/HST account 'RT', payroll 'RP'). For Ontario provincial incorporation, the Ontario registered corporation information is transmitted to CRA via Ontario's corporate registry; no separate Ontario TIN exists. The CRA issues a 9-digit BN, and the GST/HST registration adds an 'RT' program identifier; for Ontario corporate income tax, the same BN is used. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada without Canadian incorporation must separately register for a non-resident income tax account and obtain a BN/GST/HST registration, typically through RC1 and Form RC1415 (if applicable to the activity) before commencing taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election is available under the Income Tax Act (Canada); a business corporation is automatically a separate taxpayer resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income. Relevant election mechanisms: (i) functional currency election under ITA s. 261 for tax reporting in a permitted foreign currency; (ii) CCPC status application where eligible (statutory characterization, not elective filing); (iii) election under ITA s. 216 by non-residents to file on a net basis for Canadian rental/royalty income; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 apply mandatory disallowances rather than elective treatment. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch trading election is available for CA_CORP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for mandatory GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240 and s. 241; a non-resident vendor carrying on business in Canada or a supplier engaged in a commercial activity in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded, subject to deemed supply rules for e-commerce marketplaces; voluntary registration is available below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI applies to: (i) supplies of goods exported by the supplier from Canada, with the goods having been exported as part of the sale and not further consumed in Canada; (ii) services provided to a non-resident person when the services are not consumed, used, or performed substantially in Canada; (iii) certain incorporated goods, intellectual property, and commissioned exports. The supplier must be registered for GST/HST and must retain evidence sufficient to demonstrate export and, for services, the non-resident status and place of benefit. For a consumer panel survey SaaS platform, zero-rating is available if the market research services are provided to non-resident clients and are not substantially performed in or attributable to Canadian domestic consumption, provided the invoicing and delivery records substantiate the offshore service supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A, B13A CAED, or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) EDI report) where goods are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (ocean, air, truck, or rail) showing the consignor, consignee, port of exit, and description of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank transfer/SWIFT confirmation from the non-resident payor) evidencing settlement of the export proceeds

Canada does not operate a national real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing clearinghouse for GST/HST. Registrants are required to issue prescribed tax invoices in paper or secure electronic format, retain all records and books in electronic or paper form, and file GST/HST returns electronically if annual gross taxable sales exceed CAD 1,500,000 or if the registrant is a corporation required to file electronically. There is no digital stamping or mandatory CRA API clearance system; however, CRA may request e-audit access through MyBA and may require production of export documentation in XML/EDI (Customs Self-Assessment, Canadian Export Reporting System) for customs-export verification.

CRA rigorously audits zero-rated export claims under the GST/HST regime and can reassess to collect uncollected 13% HST, plus arrears interest compounded daily and penalties, if documentation is deficient or transactions are recharacterized as domestic supplies. False statements or omissions in export documentation attract penalties under ETA ss. 280 and 281, including the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax amount; gross negligence may extend the normal four-year reassessment period and expose the party to 50% gross-negligence penalties on the unreported tax, prohibition orders for repeat offenses, and criminal prosecution for willful evasion. Clawback includes denial of input tax credits previously claimed against zero-rated exports, and CRA may restrict refunds or require financial security pending audit conclusions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current-account and capital-account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control licensing regime, no prior approval for CAD purchases, and no restriction on dividends, royalties, intercompany loans, capital repatriation, or profit remittance. The only compliance gates are corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act / Ontario Business Corporations Act, Part XIII withholding tax on outbound non-resident payments (25% statutory, reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties, remitted via CRA), GST/HST registration obligations, and FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting thresholds. No conversion quotas, no surrender obligations, and no exchange-rate verification requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender percentage on export proceeds, service fees, or cross-border invoicing receipts. Receivables denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or other currencies may be held freely in domestic or foreign bank accounts, converted at negotiated corporate FX rates, or retained for onward disbursement without OSFI, FINTRAC, or Bank of Canada authorization.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Bank of Canada Act as the monetary and financial stability authority. Foreign-exchange policy coordination rests with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision of federally regulated deposit-taking institutions is exercised by OSFI; and AML/CFT supervision and EFT reporting are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility: High as an auxiliary disbursement rail, Medium as a primary operating account. Wise (TransferWise) and PayPal Business are legally permissible in Canada for collecting B2B client receipts and executing mass payouts to consumer panelists, and payment-integration agreements are standard for SaaS market-research platforms. However, neither is a Schedule I chartered bank: balances are not CDIC-insured, client escrow funds may not be held with them, and they cannot provide credit facilities or overdraft protection. The compliant structure is a Schedule I bank as the master treasury account, with Wise/PayPal linked as sub-rails for batch mass payouts and local-currency settlement; all settlement accounts must be configured so the originating bank account remains the sole funding and reconciliation source.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 reporting threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border non-arm's length transactions per year; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be prepared for any material related-party transactions, with penalties applied when the net transfer pricing adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone ESR regime; the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, s.245) and judicial substance-over-form doctrines to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. For market-research platforms, a functional analysis must demonstrate that strategic decisions, risk assumption, and key personnel are located in Canada to sustain the arm's length allocation of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty up to 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; civil fraud may sever the corporate veil, and officers may face travel bans, extradition, restitution orders, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under s.247(2); penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (suspended if TP documentation is prepared and provided in writing within 90 days of the CRA's request).

Director personal liability: Directors generally not liable for s.247 penalties unless complicit in preparing false records; however, negligence in governance may expose to a duty-of-care claim from shareholders and CRA's authority to pursue the controlling shareholder under asset-combining rules.
Abusive Tax Avoidance / Shell Company Substance Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) denies the tax benefit and can impose interest; a 15% consequential penalty may apply when the avoidance transaction is carried out as part of a series. CRA may also revoke the small business deduction and recharacterize the income as dividend.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or unjust enrichment; directors face personal civil restitution, a Canada-wide travel ban, and criminally for money laundering under the Criminal Code if the structure is used to conceal proceeds.
AML / Money Laundering Filings Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for administrative violations; criminal money laundering offences carry up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly fail to implement AML compliance programs may face criminal liability, fines, and up to 10 years' imprisonment; travel prohibitions and extradition are possible for cross-border platforms.
