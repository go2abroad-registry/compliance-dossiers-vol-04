Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the canton of the registered office, operating under the supervisory authority of the Federal Office of Justice; electronic filing via the e-GR / PrivaSphere portal and the Zefix national business name index.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Part Five: Commercial Register and Commercial Companies, Articles 620-763 governing the stock corporation (Aktiengesellschaft); in particular Article 626 (mandatory content of the Articles of Association), Article 630 (commercial register application), Article 630a (director eligibility declarations), Article 631 (paid-up capital requirement), Article 640 (audit opt-out), and Article 950 (commercial register information system); supplemented by the Swiss Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HregV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised constitutive incorporation instrument (Gruendungsurkunde / acte constitutif) together with the Articles of Association (Statuten), filed as the statutory registration deed with the Commercial Register and subsequently published in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 41 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard AG profile requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (Article 621 OR); at least 20 per cent of the nominal amount, with a hard floor of CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration; the paid-up portion must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) at a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank and confirmed by a written bank capital confirmation; contributions in kind require a licensed auditor confirmation; the unpaid balance is callable by the Board of Directors at any time.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must maintain a valid building fire insurance confirmation (Gebaeudeversicherung); if racking or storage systems are introduced, a structural load certificate and fire safety inspection by the cantonal fire protection authority (Feuerpolizei) under VKF / NVK rules are required. For occupational safety, SUVA and SECO labour-law clearance apply: the company must complete a risk assessment under the Swiss Accident Prevention Ordinance, provide first-aid equipment, conclude mandatory accident insurance for employees, and keep documented workplace instructions in the corporate file.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard AG profile requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (Article 621 OR); at least 20 per cent of the nominal amount, with a hard floor of CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration; the paid-up portion must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) at a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank and confirmed by a written bank capital confirmation; contributions in kind require a licensed auditor confirmation; the unpaid balance is callable by the Board of Directors at any time.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for a pure automated dropshipping operation under ISIC 4791. No industrial licence or manufacturing authority is engaged. If the company later introduces Swiss-based warehousing or fulfilment activities, the Cantonal Office of Economy and Labour (Amt fuer Wirtschaft und Arbeit) and the municipal trade authority may impose warehouse and logistics operating conditions.

Category: None required. Swiss law does not issue a specific operating licence for distance selling or automated dropshipping; the commercial register entry constitutes the only statutory authorisation to operate as a retailer. No manufacturing or operational permit is applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV; French: Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC), official portal https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and filing via SuisseTax e-portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities entering Swiss VAT nexus must obtain a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr./UID CHE) from ESTV via the SuisseTax registration portal or Form 9.2 before commencing taxable supplies. Non-Swiss-established businesses apply directly; Swiss branches and Aktiengesellschaften obtain the business identification number through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) after commercial register entry, which imports automatically to ESTV.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities entering Swiss VAT nexus must obtain a Swiss VAT number (MWST-Nr./UID CHE) from ESTV via the SuisseTax registration portal or Form 9.2 before commencing taxable supplies. Non-Swiss-established businesses apply directly; Swiss branches and Aktiengesellschaften obtain the business identification number through the Swiss Central Business Name Index (Zefix) after commercial register entry, which imports automatically to ESTV.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss VAT group election per Art. 53 Swiss VAT Act for related enterprises; no check-the-box classification for Swiss AGs; hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Swiss implementation of BEPS Action 2 but there is no unilateral entity classification election; limited cantonal tax consolidation options exist for income tax without changing the federal entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies for Swiss-established businesses; non-established foreign suppliers have no CHF threshold and must register when performing taxable supplies in Switzerland, subject to distance-selling provisions for imported goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to outright exports of goods where ownership is transferred to a foreign buyer and goods are physically delivered or transported out of Switzerland, evidenced by a customs declaration. Certain services named in Art. 23 para. 2 Swiss VAT Act and ancillary services directly related to exported goods qualify for zero-rating. Dropshipping transactions qualify only if title passes to the foreign or end customer and the goods are demonstrably exported from Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Narwa or Tradenet declaration in Switzerland or official border customs office release),
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / consignment transport documents proving exit from Switzerland,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX receipt proving consideration received from abroad within the documentary evidence record,
  • Commercial invoice and export purchase orders,
  • Proof of foreign destination and recipient, including end-customer delivery addresses.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or mandatory digital fiscal invoice regime. Tax invoices may be issued electronically in any agreed format, but must satisfy statutory VAT invoice content requirements; Swiss standardized e-invoicing formats (eBill/QR-bill) are accepted. ESTV retains full audit rights over electronic records.

ESTV audits zero-rated exports strictly; if the export proof is missing or incomplete, VAT is retroactively assessed at the standard rate of 8.1%, plus default interest. Proven fraudulent zero-rating triggers criminal tax evasion penalties of up to three times the tax evaded, in addition to reassessed tax and interest, under Art. 106 ff. Swiss VAT Act, with no limitation shield for intentional misdeclaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full, unrestricted convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) on both current and capital account transactions, in full compliance with IMF Article VIII. There is no foreign-exchange control statute, no central bank approval requirement for cross-border payments, and no restriction on profit distribution, dividend remittance, or repatriation of invested capital. The only binding obligations are (i) FINMA anti-money-laundering identification and reporting duties under the AMLA and (ii) statistical reporting thresholds applicable to financial intermediaries. Profit remittance by an AG requires a formal shareholders' resolution, confirmation of distributable profit per Art. 660 and Art. 671 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO), and a 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at source unless distributions constitute repayment of paid-in capital; no exchange-control approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or repatriation timeline exists under Swiss law. Mandatory conversion ratios are zero: export receipts in foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in domestic or foreign bank accounts without any SNB or FINMA mandate. The only relevant duties are commercial bookkeeping and tax reporting obligations (Art. 957 ff. CO; Art. 58 VAT Act), including mandatory balance-sheet recognition of foreign-currency receivables and valuation of open positions per Swiss GAAP (FER) or IFRS; VAT treatment follows Art. 23 VAT Act and does not require FX conversion at customs.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank (Banque nationale suisse)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, subject to material legal constraints. A foreign e-money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut may be used only as an auxiliary settlement channel and cannot substitute for a FINMA-supervised bank account. The Banking Act (Art. 1) and FinIA (Art. 5) reserve the taking of deposits from the public to licensed institutions; an offshore EMI soliciting Swiss corporate clients without FINMA authorization is legally fragile. Practical blockers include absence of a Swiss CHF IBAN, no access to SIC instant clearing, non-recognition for Swiss withholding-tax collection accounts, and supplier refusal to accept EMI payouts. Viable model: maintain a Swiss tier-1 bank account as the treasury anchor and statutory settlement account, add a Swiss-regulated fintech (e.g., Swissquote Bank Ltd, Yuh, or Neon) for low-cost payment routing, and deploy the foreign EMI only for niche cross-border payouts where the foreign entity documentation is accepted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing options: (i) Share capital increase via notarial deed and commercial register filing, with funds wired by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own account using the purpose specification 'Einlage Kapitalerhoehung / contribution to share capital', plus a bank confirmation (Geldeinzahlungsbestaetigung) per Art. 632/633 CO; (ii) Shareholder loan documented by an interest-bearing loan agreement, wired via MT103 with purpose 'shareholder loan facility', subject to the arm's-length and thin-capitalization safe harbor in Art. 65 VSB 19 (1:1 debt-to-equity as a general rule for non-banks); (iii) Current-account credit (contocorrente) facility with the parent or principal shareholder. Switzerland imposes no purpose-code approval regime; however, the receiving Swiss bank records the SWIFT purpose tag and will require source-of-funds evidence for flows of CHF 100,000 or more. Tax consideration: a 1% Swiss issuance stamp duty (Emissionsabgabe) applies to paid-in capital injections above CHF 1 million, but a shareholder loan structure avoids issuance duty entirely. For inbound transfers from a non-EU shareholder, no notarial reporting to the SNB is required; only the standard client-identification file must be maintained under AMLO-FINMA.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for USD, EUR, and CNY supplier settlements via UBS/ZKB correspondent banking connectivity (direct CHIPS, Fedwire, and TARGET2 access); (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU suppliers, executed same-day with purpose code 'Goods purchase / Warenkauf'; (iii) domestic SIC and instant-payment rails for CHF disbursements to Swiss suppliers; (iv) commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700) and documentary collections (DP/DA) for large-value or repetitive supplier orders, available but rarely used in card-driven dropshipping; (v) bank-issued virtual corporate cards routed over Visa/Mastercard networks for marketplace payments; and (vi) PSP mass-payout API rails (Stripe Connect, PayPal Payouts) for automated vendor disbursements in EUR, GBP, and USD.
