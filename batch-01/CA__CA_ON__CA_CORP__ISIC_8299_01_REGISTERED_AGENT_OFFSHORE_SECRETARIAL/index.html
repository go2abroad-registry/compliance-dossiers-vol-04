Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) — federal tax administration for income tax and GST/HST, including the Ontario provincial HST component; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/business-registration-online.html; Ontario corporate registry via ServiceOntario (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry); customs/export enforcement by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or Ontario Business Corporations Act via the Ontario Business Registry; obtain the Ontario corporation file number (not a tax ID). Step 2 — Register with CRA through BRO or the Ontario one-stop business registry to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and tax program accounts: RT (corporate income tax, T2), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RM (import/export). Step 3 — For non-resident owners/investors, complete Form RC437 (classification of corporate status / CCPC determination) and, if applicable, register for GST/HST using Form RC1 or online BRO. Step 4 — File an initial corporate return (T2) within 6 months of fiscal year-end; the BN is the single tax identification number used for all CRA programs. A foreign entity without Canadian incorporation may register directly for a BN using Form RC1, and for GST/HST with a simplified non-resident account where required. Step 5 — Maintain Ontario Employer Health Tax and Workers' Safety & Insurance Board (WSIB) registrations only if Canadian employees are present.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; corporations are always separate taxpayers. Available statutory elections: (a) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 150(2) election for a non-resident corporation to file a full Canadian return; (b) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents on Canadian rental/royalty income to be taxed on a net basis instead of Part XIII withholding; (c) ITA s. 219 election for the branch tax regime of non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada; (d) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 150 joint election for closely related Canadian corporations/financial institutions to treat certain supplies as excluded from GST/HST; (e) s. 85 rollover elections for tax-deferred transfers between related corporations. None of these alter the entity-level classification of the Ontario business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold (ETA s. 148). Registration and GST/HST collection become mandatory when total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports, financial services to non-residents, and certain goods for consumption outside Canada) exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A corporate secretarial/registered agent service provider making only zero-rated exported services to non-residents is not required to register, but may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits on Canadian overhead.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of corporate secretarial, administration, registered-agent and offshore bookkeeping services to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 if: (1) the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST; (2) the service is not rendered to an individual physically in Canada; (3) the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada; (4) the service is not performed in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; and (5) the service is not of a type that relates to the recipient's Canadian establishment. Where the service involves day-to-day management or 'control and supervision' of a Canadian shelf company or physical corporate records located in Ontario, CRA may re-characterize the supply as a Canadian domestic supply subject to 13% HST. Substantiation requires a signed contract identifying the offshore principal, board resolutions, evidence of the non-resident's legal seat/address, and contemporaneous invoices denominated in foreign currency where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) — required only where tangible goods are bundled; for pure services, no B13A is required
  • Commercial invoice referencing 'Zero-rated export — ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7'
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for any incidental physical shipments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank wire/SWIFT proof of foreign currency payment from the non-resident client
  • Non-resident status evidence — foreign incorporation certificate, certificate of good standing, registered address and director register
  • Service agreement and supporting workpapers showing the Canadian place of performance and foreign place of benefit/use

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CFDI-style mandate exists in Canada. Taxpayers must issue and retain prescribed GST/HST invoices containing: seller's name/BN, date, description, amount, GST/HST payable or 'zero-rated' indication, and the buyer's name (retail sales may use simplified receipts). Electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) are permitted and accepted by CRA. CRA does not require API-based clearance of individual invoices; the Pan-Canadian digital reporting/e-invoicing framework (2025-2026) is voluntary and not yet statutory. For exports, the invoice should clearly cite 'ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 — zero-rated supply to non-resident'.

No statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline exists for Canadian service exports; CRA reviews the arm's length nature and actual receipt of consideration under the transfer-pricing rules (ITA s. 247). If no consideration is received, CRA may apply ITA s. 17 deemed interest on shareholder loans/advances, and the zero-rating/input tax credit claim may be denied. Retrospective clawback periods: CRA may reassess for 4 years (ordinary), 7 years (misrepresentation, neglect), and without limitation (fraudulent/culpable conduct). Penalties: ETA s. 280 — 25% of the net tax deficiency (minimum C$250) for false statements/ignorance; ETA s. 327 — fines up to C$50,000 (summary) or C$100,000 (indictable) and imprisonment up to 2 years for evading GST/HST; ITA s. 239 — similar criminal penalties plus CIT clawback; Criminal Code s. 380 — fraud over C$5,000 is punishable by up to 14 years' imprisonment. Directors and officers may be personally liable under ETA s. 323 for unremitted GST/HST. Failure to maintain the export documentation bundle will cause CRA to reverse the zero-rating, assess 13% HST retroactively, charge statutory interest (compounded daily), and apply penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange control legislation on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Inbound equity contributions, loans, and repatriation of capital are not subject to prior exchange-control consent. Profit remittance is restrained only by domestic tax and AML obligations: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties; the branch tax on unremitted surplus; Part XXVI.1 transfer-pricing rules under the Income Tax Act; the Investment Canada Act review on direct/indirect acquisitions of Canadian businesses by non-Canadians, including registered-agent scale enterprises; and sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act. Foreign exchange conversion is freely available at market-based rates through all Schedule I banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no Canadian statutory surrender requirements, no mandatory repatriation percentage, and no forced conversion timeline for export proceeds. Canadian exporters must, however, file export declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for controlled or reportable goods under the Customs Act and associated regulations. All proceeds of commercial transactions flowing into or out of an Ontario registered-agent treasury account remain in the exporter's/entity's control; the only compliance triggers are general AML obligations, sanctions screening, tax reporting, and FINTRAC large-transaction and suspicious-transaction reporting once aggregate receipts exceed CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for direct domestic Canadian clearing. An offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) is not a registered Canadian bank or a member of Payments Canada and therefore cannot directly hold a Canadian chequing account or clear through the Lynx/RTR clearing structure. An offshore EMI may still be used as a multi-currency virtual IBAN or pre-funded payment processor, but only in an auxiliary role to a Schedule I Canadian bank account. Legal constraints include the Bank Act prohibition on deposit-taking without federal authorization, the Payment Services Act (once in force) and provincial money-services licensing expectations, FINTRAC registration for foreign money-services providers dealing with Canadian clients, and enhanced correspondent due diligence imposed on the Canadian clearing bank. Any EMI arrangement must be structured as a payment instruction layer rather than the sovereign treasury bank of the Ontario registered-agent entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscriptions for the Ontario entity should use a SWIFT MT103 wire to the dedicated Canadian Capital Escrow Account. The remitter must identify itself as the shareholder and provide the account number, SWIFT/BIC, and contemporaneous evidence of the underlying share subscription agreement. In the MT103 narrative field, include 'PURPOSE CODE: CAPITAL-01; CA_CORP/ISIC_8299_01 SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION FOR REGISTERED AGENT AND SECRETARIAL OPERATIONS; REF: BOARD RESOLUTION [date] AND SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT [date]'. Canadian receiving banks generally require the ordering bank to conduct source-of-funds verification. For multi-currency settlements, the Canadian bank maintains nostro/vostro accounts and should be instructed via authenticated SWIFT MT202 cover instructions.
