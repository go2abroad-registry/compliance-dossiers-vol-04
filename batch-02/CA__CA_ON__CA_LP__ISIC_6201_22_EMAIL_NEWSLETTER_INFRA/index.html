Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or registered agent service (e.g., registered office services provided by law firms, corporate service providers, or licensed registered offices in Ontario) is permissible for an Ontario limited partnership, provided the registered office address is a physical street address in Ontario and the entity is able to receive legal process and government mail. A PO Box alone is not permitted as the registered office. The registered office may be a commercial virtual office facility as long as the office is staffed and capable of receiving and forwarding documents; however, the Ontario Ministry may not accept a virtual office that is merely a mail forwarding depot without physical presence. For ISIC 6201 email SaaS infrastructure business, a virtual office is assessed as permissible with moderate risk; the partnership must maintain a designated representative agent in Ontario. The Canada Revenue Agency may require actual commercial substance for GST/HST registration and transfer-pricing purposes, but a genuine email infrastructure business operated by the partnership with cloud-based servers can meet substantive commercial substance requirements without a dedicated physical office in Ontario, provided central management and control functions are demonstrably exercised from Ontario or delegated to an Ontario-based general partner.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal via canada.ca; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax registration (Ontario Business Registry).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must first register with the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery via the Ontario Business Registry to obtain an Ontario Business Identification Number (BIN) and register the LP. Subsequently, the partnership must apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using the BRO (Form RC1, Request for a Business Number). The partnership itself registers for a BN in its own name and, if required, for GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. Non-resident partners do not directly obtain Canadian TINs through the LP registration; each partner must separately obtain an Individual Tax Number (ITN) if filing Canadian returns, but the LP BN serves as the primary tax identifier for GST/HST and information returns. A non-resident LP with no Canadian presence may register directly through the CRA non-resident account application process; a deemed registered threshold applies only under special elective rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Ontario provincial income tax purposes. Income, gains, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in accordance with partnership agreement allocations (subject to Canadian tax allocation rules and subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act). The LP file is not a taxpayer for income tax; it files an information return (Form T5013) and is liable only for GST/HST and certain provincial levies (e.g., Ontario employer health tax) if applicable. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source business income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain payments; the LP is not subject to entity-level income tax. — Foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must first register with the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery via the Ontario Business Registry to obtain an Ontario Business Identification Number (BIN) and register the LP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Ontario provincial income tax purposes. Income, gains, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in accordance with partnership agreement allocations (subject to Canadian tax allocation rules and subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act). The LP file is not a taxpayer for income tax; it files an information return (Form T5013) and is liable only for GST/HST and certain provincial levies (e.g., Ontario employer health tax) if applicable. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source business income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain payments; the LP is not subject to entity-level income tax.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box election akin to the U.S. IRC 7701 regime. However, limited partnerships can elect under section 10 of the Income Tax Application Rules to have certain transition rules apply; for GST/HST, the partnership may elect under section 150 of the Excise Tax Act to treat closely related entities as a single registrant, or use the quick method of accounting (for Canadian-resident suppliers with eligible revenue). A non-resident LP may elect to have a deemed taxable Canadian presence for GST/HST under subsection 143(1) of the Excise Tax Act to simplify registration and input tax credit recovery. Hybrid mismatch rules under subsections 18.2 and 18.21 of the Income Tax Act may disallow deductions in respect of offshore hybrid arrangements, requiring affected taxpayers to file Form RC2322 or an equivalent disclosure; no specific election exists to avoid the hybrid mismatch application, but the partnership agreement can be structured to align with Canadian tax-transparent treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter, and under the Excise Tax Act section 240, any person who is not a small supplier must register. Non-resident persons that make taxable supplies in Canada (including via Canadian e-commerce platforms) are required to register if taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters combined. For digital economy cross-border suppliers, the simplified GST/HST registration threshold remains CAD 30,000 in annual global Canadian-source digital supplies; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility applies under sections 123-124 of the Excise Tax Act for exports of goods and services. For a Canadian-resident supplier (including an Ontario LP) providing digital services (e.g., email infrastructure API and newsletter hosting) to non-resident clients who are not registered under GST/HST and who use the service principally for consumption outside Canada, the supply is zero-rated under section 23 (export of services). The supplier must maintain documentary evidence that the recipient is non-resident and that the service is not for use in Canada. The zero-rating applies when the recipient is a non-resident individual or entity not registered for GST/HST at the time the supply is made, and the service is not performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada. For software and digital services, the place of supply rules under section 142.1 treat the supply as made in Canada if the recipient is in Canada, making zero-rating contingent on the recipient's ordinary residence and IP location. If the supply qualifies as an exempt export (section 23.2 for financial services), no GST/HST applies. For goods exported by the LP as part of a bundled physical product, zero-rating requires the goods be exported within a reasonable time (60 days) and documentary evidence retained.

  • Form B13A completed via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the simplified 'Non-Domestic Status/Export' declaration when value of goods exceeds CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice with the exporter's GST/HST registration number and a statement 'Supply of Service to Non-Resident – Zero-Rated under Section 23' as applicable
  • Bill of Lading or other transportation evidence if tangible goods are involved (e.g., a hardware component for newsletter infrastructure)
  • Proof of payment from the non-resident customer (bank transfer record, SWIFT confirmation, stripe settlement report)
  • Retained contract with the non-resident recipient showing the provision of digital services from Canada
  • IP location evidence or corporate registration certificate of the recipient to substantiate non-residence
  • For exempt financial services (if applicable), Form GST Schedule (RC4600) and the recipient's written certification that the service is not for consumption in Canada

Canada does not currently mandate a federal continuous real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance regime. GST/HST is collected and remitted on accrual basis, with returns filed monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on revenue and reporting period. Electronic data interchange (EDI) and PDF invoices are acceptable; no pre-clearance API tax stamping is required. For Ontario RST (Retail Sales Tax) — not applicable to digital services — no electronic fiscal device mandate exists. Starting 2024, Ontario participates in the federal GST/HST audit and reporting framework; digital platform operators are subject to information reporting requirements under sections 211.1-211.8 of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST rules for platform-based supplies of short-term accommodation or personal property). Bulk transactional email API (a digital service) is subject to GST/HST at the place of supply under subsection 142.1(2) rules; no special invoicing certification is required. The CRA requires invoices to contain seller's BN, date, description, amount paid, and applicable tax; electronic records must be retained in machine-readable format.

Incorrectly claimed zero-rating for export services is subject to reassessment by the CRA, disqualifying the supply from zero-rating and treating it as taxable at 13% HST, with arrears interest (using prescribed rates) and penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act (negligence penalty of up to 25% of the net tax understated) and section 285 for repeated failure. Fraudulent claims may attract criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 5 years imprisonment and/or fine up to CAD 500,000 for summary conviction; higher for indictable offense). Retrospective clawback period is generally four years under the Excise Tax Act (subsection 298(1)), extended to seven years if the taxpayer was careless or negligent, and unlimited in cases of fraud or misrepresentation. Additionally, failure to file non-resident export documentation on request may result in denial of the zero-rating and penalties under section 292. Ontario provincial HST portion (8%) is equally subject to clawback as part of the composite HST remittance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a fully convertible currency regime for both capital and current account transactions. No exchange control legislation, prior approval clearance, or central bank allocation is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation by a Canadian limited partnership. Fiscal obligations, such as Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident partner distributions (statutory rate 25%, treaty-mitigable), are the only practical constraints, and are administered by the Canada Revenue Agency rather than by exchange control authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts. Exporters and service providers may retain export proceeds in Canadian or foreign currency deposit accounts. Reporting obligations apply only where an instrument of transfer crosses a Canadian border or is processed through a Canadian financial institution, in which case an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report or Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report must be filed with FINTRAC for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with critical legal constraints. An offshore digital Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may be used as a non-holding technology overlay only, but the Canadian limited partnership may not hold treasury balances through an EMI that is not registered as a Foreign Money Services Business with FINTRAC. Any EMI providing money transfer or foreign exchange dealing services to Canadian residents must be registered and fully compliant with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Therefore, onshore account connectivity or use of a Canadian-regulated Payment Service Provider is required for principal treasury operations.
