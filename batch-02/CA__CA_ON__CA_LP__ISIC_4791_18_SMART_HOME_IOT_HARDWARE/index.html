Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); CRA 'My Business Account' portal is the primary federal tax registration and compliance portal. Ontario provincial registration is administered by the Ontario Business Registry. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs, import/export controls, and collection of tax at importation. Ontario provincial corporate income tax on partnership-source income is assessed and collected by CRA under the Ontario-Canada tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Legal formation: register the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through the Ontario Business Registry and obtain the LP registration confirmation. Step 2 - CRA Business Number: apply for a Business Number (BN) through CRA's 'Register for a Business Number' online service or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number - Certain Registrations', supplying the LP's legal name, Ontario registration details, registered office address, and, if non-resident, evidence of foreign registration. Step 3 - GST/HST program account: open the RT program account on the BN for the LP; a non-resident LP must complete the non-resident questionnaire and will be issued a non-resident GST/HST registrant account. The BN is the sole statutory tax identifier; no separate Canadian TIN exists. Step 4 - Import/export account: open an RM program account for CBSA/CRA import-export transactions so the LP may act as importer of record and pay HST at the border. Step 5 - Partnership reporting: file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue T5013 slips to partners; non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada must file Canadian Part I income tax returns. Step 6 - Withholding: discharge any Part XIII withholding obligations for interest, rent, royalties, or management fees payable to non-residents; ordinary partnership profit allocations are generally taxed under Part I at the non-resident partner level rather than by Part XIII withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For federal income tax purposes, the Ontario LP is a fiscally transparent partnership: it is not an income-tax taxpayer, and income, losses, credits, and capital gains flow through to partners. However, the partnership is a distinct 'person' under the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST purposes and must register, collect, and remit HST in its own name when carrying on commercial activity in Canada. Territorial scope: a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada is taxable on Canadian-source business income at the partner level; the LP itself files information returns. Tax-exempt-zone status does not apply to retail IoT hardware.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election and no elective hybrid-mismatch override; classification follows the legal form and treaty facts. Relevant elections for an LP include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration under Excise Tax Act subsection 240(3) where below the small-supplier threshold; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method of Accounting for simplified remittance; (iii) a non-calendar fiscal period where all partnership members are non-resident persons; (iv) designation of the LP as importer of record through the CBSA CARM portal; and (v) federal-provincial single business registration program account alignment. Hybrid anti-avoidance rules apply automatically and cannot be elected around.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of Canadian taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the 'small supplier' threshold). A non-resident LP importing goods or delivering goods through Canadian fulfilment warehouses, or making taxable supplies through a Canadian distribution platform, becomes a mandatory registrant when the threshold is met; voluntary registration is available below CAD 30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of smart-home IoT hardware is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act (Schedule VI, Part V) where the supplier ships, mails, or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, or transfers physical possession to a common carrier, courier, or customs broker for immediate export. Qualifying cross-border services embedded in IoT devices may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada and not connected to Canadian real property. The exporter must obtain satisfactory written evidence of export, including sales contract, commercial invoice, foreign-address delivery proof, and foreign-currency receipt confirmation, and retain that evidence for six years. Foreign-currency receipts should be corroborated by an Inward Remittance Confirmation (bank FX receipt) within 180 days; although Canadian exchange-control law does not impose a statutory 180-day repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is standard CRA audit and banking documentary practice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or CBSA EDI/CARM export report, where required)
  • Commercial invoice showing product description, quantity, value, and destination country
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Waybill for the export leg
  • Inward Remittance Confirmation (bank foreign-currency receipt / SWIFT or transfer evidence)
  • Courier shipment manifest and delivery proof showing an address outside Canada
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the foreign buyer
  • Customs control/list-control declaration and export permit (if hardware appears on the Export Control List)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API mandate applies to B2B or B2C invoicing in Canada. A GST/HST registrant must issue supporting invoices showing the Business Number and GST/HST registration number, retain electronic or paper source records for six years, and file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA's My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, or EDI. Imported goods are cleared through the CBSA Single Window Initiative and CARM, which requires electronic customs declarations and HST payment at importation before goods enter Ontario. The Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing framework is voluntary and does not impose fiscal clearance.

The exporter bears the burden of proving physical export with contemporaneous documentation. If CRA determines that export evidence is deficient or false, it will reassess the supply at 13% HST, deny or claw back any refund or input tax credit, and charge prescribed interest from the original due date. Civil penalties include late-filing penalties and gross-negligence/false-statement penalties up to 50% of the net tax attributable to the misstatement, and the general partner may be jointly and severally liable for LP HST debts. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act can result in substantial fines and imprisonment. CRA data-matching of export declarations, inbound freight bills, and foreign-currency receipts makes fabricated zero-rating files vulnerable to retrospective reassessment and clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange-control legislation and no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no statutory approval, quantitative limit, or repatriation restriction applicable to non-resident capital contributions, dividend/profit distributions, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds of an Ontario limited partnership. Effective constraints are limited to: (i) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business by a non-Canadian above the annually adjusted WTO-investor threshold (approximately CAD 1.2B for 2024-2025); (ii) Canadian income tax and Part XIII non-resident withholding on specific outbound payments; (iii) Statistics Canada direct-investment data collection obligations; and (iv) PCMLTFA source-of-funds verification. Profit remittance from the CA_LP to non-resident partners is unrestricted in substance: the non-resident partner is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its allocated share of Canadian-source income via the T5013 regime, with no separate remittance tax on the actual outward transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds to domestic banking channels. The CA_LP may lawfully retain smart-home IoT hardware export receipts in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies in non-resident foreign-currency accounts or in Canadian Schedule I bank foreign-currency accounts. Hedging of such proceeds may be executed freely. The only compliance obligations are accounting translation to the CAD functional currency under IFRS (IAS 21) for financial statements and accurate reporting on the annual T5013 partnership information return.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and financial-system authority; innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) as administrator of the Investment Canada Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator for all federally incorporated Canadian banks; and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisory agency for money services businesses and reporting entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorization. Foreign digital EMIs and global neo-banks may operate in Canada only through (a) partnership as an agent of a Schedule I bank or (b) registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, with additional oversight under the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework once fully applied. Such entities cannot provide the CA_LP with full treasury-grade services (direct Lynx membership, statutory deposit insurance, commercial lending, and trade finance). Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary cross-border payout/disbursement rail for low-value contractor payments; Low as the primary treasury custodian or for holding capital escrow balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a genuine bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor to the CA_LP's designated Schedule I operational account. Canada has no statutory wire purpose-code system equivalent to Asian FX-administration regimes; however, the bank's AML system requires the remitter to include the Ontario LP's exact legal name, the account number, and a descriptive payload identifying 'Increase of Partnership Capital' or 'Equity Contribution'. Beneficiary bank verification (name, SWIFT/BIC, IBAN-style routing for CAD) must match the executed subscription agreement to avoid holds. First-time inbound wires from non-client counterparties attract a 7-to-10 business-day funds-availability hold while the bank completes EDD. For cross-border equity balances exceeding CAD 1M, the LP must file Statistic Canada's Follow-up of Direct Investment in Canada questionnaire. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Ontario provincial); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays nil entity-level corporate tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII dividend withholding rate is 25% on corporate dividends paid to non-residents; LP profit allocations are not 'dividends' and are therefore outside Part XIII — they become Part I taxable business income to the non-resident partner subject to graduated rates and treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual transaction value with a single non-arm's length non-resident counterparty triggers the mandatory Form T106 and full contemporaneous Local File documentation; a Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with consolidated gross revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000 (Country-by-Country Reporting threshold) or where the Canadian entity is a large taxpayer with highly complex intercompany flows.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no codified Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to other jurisdictions, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) (Income Tax Act s.245) attacks artificial transactions lacking business purpose; the LP must maintain a genuine presence in Canada — real office, inventory, employees, and decision-making — to preserve the liability shield, deductibility of losses, and access to treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misreported Revenue

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax (s.163(2)); criminal tax evasion conviction under s.239(1) for an indictable offence is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: CRA may prosecute the general partner, officers, and directors; conviction can trigger direct personal liability for taxes plus penalties (s.227.1), immediate travel bans, and restrictions on leaving Canada during audit or enforcement.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may recharacterize the arrangement and disallow all tax benefits (s.245); a 25% penalty applies on the tax benefit (s.245(4)); failure to file T5013 or Part I returns additionally attracts late-filing fines and gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP are liable for unpaid GST/HST or payroll remittances; fraudulent preference rules may pierce the limited partnership shield, exposing limited partners to creditor claims and criminal restitution.
Transfer Pricing Penalty - Undocumented Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income to arm's length and impose a 10% penalty on any net transfer-pricing adjustment exceeding CAD 50,000 where the taxpayer fails the 'reasonable efforts' documentation safe harbour (s.247(3)); interest on understated Part I tax accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for corporate Part I tax if they knowingly condoned the misreporting (s.227.1); personal net-worth litigation, inability to exit Canada without CRA consent, and potential contempt of court for obstructing documentary production may follow.
