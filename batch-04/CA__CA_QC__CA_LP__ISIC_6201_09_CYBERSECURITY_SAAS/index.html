Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial income tax. Primary portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec CLIC.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Quebec limited partnership: (1) Verify whether the LP carries on business in Canada or derives Canadian-source income; (2) Apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or the CRA business portal; (3) Register for a GST/HST account with CRA if taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000; (4) Register separately with Revenu Québec for QST (and any Quebec employer accounts) using Form LM-10 or CLIC; (5) File partnership information return T5013 and allocate income/loss to partners, each partner reporting their share for federal and Quebec income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' regime exists under Canadian federal law. A limited partnership is pass-through by default and may make statutory elections under the Income Tax Act, including: joint election under ITA 96(3.1) for partnership property acquisitions, s.97(2) for property transfers to the partnership, and s.216 election for non-resident investment income. Quebec QST permits analogous partner elections for nil consideration transfers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable sales from Canada) for GST/HST; CAD 30,000 for QST registration; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register if taxable supplies exceed threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports is available under the Excise Tax Act for (a) goods that are delivered by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, or (b) a supply of a service, including cybersecurity SaaS, made to a non-resident person who is not registered under the GST/HST and is not carrying on a business in Canada; the service must be supplied for the recipient's commercial use and the consideration must be payable outside Canada. Quebec QST zero-rating similarly applies when the supply is made outside Quebec to a non-resident who is not using the service in Quebec.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13) or equivalent proof of export for tangible goods
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for physical shipments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/SWIFT MT103 evidence of payment
  • Executed service agreement with non-resident client
  • IP delivery logs and access records evidencing remote SaaS delivery
  • Due diligence file confirming recipient's non-resident status and commercial purpose

No mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance, API fiscal stamping, or digital certification regime for GST/HST/QST. Invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format but must contain prescribed particulars (supplier BN, recipient, date, description, amount). The Canadian federal government supports voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing (CyCana/Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Exchange). Quebec does not require validation or clearance through Revenu Québec. Tax records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess within four years (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default) and require payment of uncollected GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a 5% penalty on net tax plus a 15% negligence penalty, and fines up to 200% of taxes evaded (or imprisonment) for knowingly making false statements. Revenu Québec may impose reciprocal QST assessments and penalties. Aggressive export zero-rating claims lacking contemporaneous documentary proof are routinely challenged and clawed back.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains no exchange-control statute, and the Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely. Current-account flows (exported cybersecurity SaaS subscriptions, vulnerability-scanning and penetration-testing fees) and capital-account flows (limited-partner capital contributions, withdrawals, profit distributions) are fully convertible without central-bank, OSFI, or FINTRAC approval. No currency-rationing or forced-conversion rules exist. Profit repatriation to non-resident limited partners is legally unrestricted from an FX perspective; the operative constraints are Canadian tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (Canada): the Quebec LP must file prescribed partnership information returns, non-resident partners are taxable on Canadian-source business income allocable to them (treaty relief available), and passive Canadian-source payments to non-residents may attract Part XIII withholding. A separate and material hurdle for a cybersecurity SaaS business with non-resident owners is the Investment Canada Act: inbound capital that acquires control of a Canadian business may trigger the WTO-investor net-benefit review (approximately C$5.2 billion direct-acquisition threshold in 2025) and, more importantly, the discretionary national-security review regime, which has no monetary threshold; the 2023-2024 ICA amendments also introduced mandatory pre-implementation notification for investments in prescribed sensitive sectors and technologies. A national-security posture assessment should precede any foreign capital wiring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for SaaS or digital-services export receipts. A Quebec LP may retain subscription and trade receipts in CAD or in foreign currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts at its discretion. Regulatory reporting obligations sit with the bank and any licensed payment service provider: FINTRAC-classified reporting entities must report international electronic fund transfers (EFTs) of C$10,000 or more and cash transactions above C$10,000. The LP itself has no surrender, declaration, or licensing obligation for ordinary B2B SaaS invoicing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority, operator of the Lynx high-value settlement system, and supervisor of the Retail Payment Activities Act payment-service-provider registration regime. Prudential regulation of Schedule I and II banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing supervision is exercised by FINTRAC; Quebec financial-sector conduct matters are overseen by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF); Quebec tax and payroll collection is administered by Revenu Québec.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer-style providers) are not authorized banks in Canada and cannot take deposits as principal; they operate as money-services businesses regulated by FINTRAC and, for payment services, are required to register (or hold transitional status) with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. A Quebec LP may use an EMI only as a supplementary collections or disbursement accelerator for small-value SaaS subscriptions, with pooled funds held in trust accounts at Canadian settlement banks. EMIs are not viable for capital escrow, letter-of-credit issuance, investment-grade treasury services, or Lynx/ACSS clearing access, and they expose the LP to correspondent-bank de-risking, lack of CDIC deposit insurance, and heightened CRS/opaque-structure diligence. The primary liquidity rail must remain at a Schedule I domestic bank in Canada.

executed with the bank's capital-markets desk under an ISDA Master Agreement or a Canadian FX options master agreement.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) to convert long-term USD or EUR capital or subscription obligations into CAD, available to LPs with audited financial statements; subject to an ISDA Master Agreement, Credit Support Annex, and Canadian Securities Administrators OTC-derivatives trade-reporting obligations when applicable thresholds are crossed.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — generally unavailable and unnecessary for CAD because the Canadian dollar trades freely on deliverable onshore and offshore markets; however, NDFs are permissible as hedges of SaaS vendor or supplier costs in capital-controlled emerging-market currencies such as INR, BRL, KRW, and others, documented separately under the bank's emerging-market FX master agreement and supported by demonstrable underlying exposure to preserve ordinary Canadian tax treatment.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer-style providers) are not authorized banks in Canada and cannot take deposits as principal; they operate as money-services businesses regulated by FINTRAC and, for payment services, are required to register (or hold transitional status) with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. A Quebec LP may use an EMI only as a supplementary collections or disbursement accelerator for small-value SaaS subscriptions, with pooled funds held in trust accounts at Canadian settlement banks. EMIs are not viable for capital escrow, letter-of-credit issuance, investment-grade treasury services, or Lynx/ACSS clearing access, and they expose the LP to correspondent-bank de-risking, lack of CDIC deposit insurance, and heightened CRS/opaque-structure diligence. The primary liquidity rail must remain at a Schedule I domestic bank in Canada.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada federal general corporate rate 15%; Quebec general corporate rate 11.5%; combined reference rate 26.5%. The Quebec Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent; no entity-level income tax is levied. Canadian-resident partners are taxed on allocated income at graduated rates up to 53.31% combined federal-Quebec; non-resident partners may be taxable under Part I or Part XIII of the Income Tax Act.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under federal Part XIII; Quebec imposes a parallel 23.5% withholding on dividends paid to non-residents; combined federal-Quebec statutory exposure is 25% for treaty-rate purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 per transaction category in aggregate cross-border non-arm's length transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation under ITA s.247(4) and Quebec Tax Administration Act s.47; below the threshold, documentation is not required but pricing must still be arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) with a two-stage test: (1) the transaction lacks a genuine economic or business purpose and (2) results in a tax abuse. The Quebec LP must maintain real commercial substance in Quebec: a resident general partner or permanent establishment, substantive board and management presence, local employees and premises, and genuine business activity. A shell LP or conduit arrangement with no substantive activity in Quebec will be recharacterized by the CRA, and the Canada-US LOB provision in the treaty may deny benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Federal: ITA s.239(1) - fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2); interest on unpaid amounts. Quebec: Tax Administration Act s.53 and Criminal Code equivalent penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholdings if they failed to exercise due diligence; personal civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterization under GAAR; disallowance of treaty benefits; reassessment of all income to the substantive partner; 50% gross-negligence penalty; Part XIII withholding of 25% imposed on deemed distributions; interest from the date of original assessment.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors are personally assessed for unpaid Part XIII amounts; travel bans and asset freezes are available under the Tax Administration Act; criminal prosecution under s.239 for aiding the tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, minimum CAD 5,000, where total adjustments exceed the lesser of CAD 5M or 10% of gross revenue; plus interest and reassessment of the adjusted amount; Quebec equivalent penalty under s.1086.1 of the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are liable for unpaid penalties and interest where they knowingly consented to artificial pricing; criminal fines up to CAD 25,000; in aggravated cases, restitution orders and imprisonment up to 2 years.
AML and Financial Transparency

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act - administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2M per violation; criminal penalties for laundering the proceeds of crime, imprisonment up to 14 years; Quebec law requires accurate beneficial ownership disclosure to the Registraire des entreprises; failure to file the annual declaration triggers registration revocation and fines of CAD 314 for the LP and CAD 628 for the general partner.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false records face personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; a court may issue a departure order to prevent tax evasion; corporate veil is pierced where the LP is used as an instrument of fraud.
