Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration portal: www.ezv.admin.ch (Federal Office for Customs and Border Security) and www.vat.admin.ch; e-services via the ESTV eService platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its Swiss branch/AG must first register with the Swiss commercial register (Handelsregister) at the cantonal level to obtain a Swiss company identification number (UID, Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer). For VAT purposes, the entity must then register with the Federal Tax Administration (ESTV) by filing the VAT registration form (Registrierung für die Mehrwertsteuer) or through the electronic portal. Registration is mandatory if the entity conducts taxable activities in Switzerland and has annual worldwide turnover above CHF 100,000 for goods or services supplied in Switzerland, or if it supplies goods/services from abroad into Switzerland that are taxable in Switzerland and turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. The ESTV issues a separate VAT number (MWST-Nr / Numéro IDE / No IVA) distinct from the UID. Non-resident entities must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative domiciled in Switzerland to fulfill VAT filing and payment obligations if they are not established in Switzerland and have no Swiss residence or permanent establishment. The UID application must be submitted within 30 days of business commencement; the VAT registration application must be filed before the start of taxable activity or immediately upon exceeding the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide an elective pass-through 'check-the-box' regime comparable to the US. However, the Swiss VAT Act provides an optional voluntary VAT registration election for businesses with turnover below CHF 100,000 (subject to irrevocable commitment of at least two years). Swiss entities may choose between ordinary VAT taxation and VAT group taxation (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) under Art. 20 of the Swiss VAT Act, allowing closely linked taxable persons to be treated as a single taxable entity. For corporate income tax, Swiss law provides statutory elections such as: (a) application of the Swiss participation relief (Beteiligungsabzug) for qualifying dividends and capital gains; (b) taxation under cantonal holding company or mixed company status in cantons that still grant such statuses; (c) reorganisations under the Swiss Merger Act, which require approval rather than unilateral election. Hybrid mismatch rules under Swiss law (Art. 8a VStG and implementing ordinances) do not contain an affirmative election; they merely deny deductions or recharacterize payments in cases of hybrid mismatch, but taxpayers may elect the ordering of rules within narrow statutory confines. Federal law does not allow a one-time elective deemed liquidation or step-up except for specific reorganisations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide taxable turnover from goods supplied in Switzerland and services received or supplied in Switzerland, excluding exempt supplies and investment gold; a separate lower threshold of CHF 100,000 applies for non-established foreign suppliers. There is no separate threshold for e-commerce marketplace operators; platform suppliers are jointly liable under special rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of tangible movable goods to foreign destinations is zero-rated (tax rate 0%) under Art. 23(1) and Art. 53 of the Swiss VAT Act, provided the supplier can prove that the goods were transported abroad within a statutory deadline and that the foreign consignee is the acquirer. The supply qualifies if the goods are actually exported by the supplier, the recipient, or a third party instructed by the recipient, and if the destination is abroad (not a Swiss customs exclusion zone like Samnaun). Services related to exported goods (e.g., transport, handling) and certain cross-border services (including intellectual property licences, services to foreign recipients that are not used in Switzerland) are also zero-rated under Art. 23(2) and Art. 53 VAT Act if the recipient's registered seat or residence is abroad. For sales via marketplace/Stripe, the supplier must identify the place of supply in accordance with Art. 7-8 VAT Act: goods placed in Switzerland and shipped abroad qualify as export when the transport begins in Switzerland and ends in a foreign country. Dental aligners manufactured and sold by a Swiss AG to foreign orthodontists are zero-rated if shipped to a non-Swiss address and properly declared. The refund of input VAT is only possible for export business via the VAT return (negative VAT position), subject to the general right to deduct input tax under Art. 28-32 VAT Act; alternatively, non-established foreign entities may claim a VAT refund under the Swiss VAT refund procedure (Form 30) for taxes borne on goods/services purchased in Switzerland, subject to reciprocity.

Switzerland does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) model requiring pre-clearance approval by the tax authority before issuance. Electronic invoicing is permitted under the ordinary VAT invoice rules if the invoice contains all statutory data fields, authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are ensured. The recognised e-invoicing standard is the Swiss e-invoice format (Peppol BIS-based, XML/UBL 2.1, or Swiss-specific formats such as Swico or EBill for consumer billing). From 1 January 2024, Swiss payment invoices must display a QR code (QR-IBAN) for domestic B2B and B2C invoices in CHF; for non-CHF and foreign transactions, the old payment slip can still be used. There is no mandatory digital stamping or fiscal seal. Invoices must be retained for 10 years. The Swiss VAT Act requires issuance of a VAT invoice for B2B supplies above CHF 400 (invoice amount incl. VAT) with full data set; for B2C, a simplified invoice is sufficient below CHF 400. There is an obligation to file electronically (VAT return via eService or Swissdec-certified software) for businesses with turnover above CHF 100,000; no periodic real-time data submission to the tax authority is required before the filing deadline.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration scrutinises zero-rated export supplies with substantive documentary requirements. If the exporter fails to prove export within the statutory time limits, the supply is recharacterised as domestic taxable at the standard rate, and VAT is newly assessed with interest. Penalties: tax evasion (Hinterziehung) under Art. 69 Swiss VAT Act: penalty up to 3 times the tax evaded; simple violation under Art. 70: admonition or fine up to CHF 8,000; violation of bookkeeping and other obligations under Art. 66-68: fines up to CHF 5,000 (severe cases up to CHF 10,000). For fraudulent export zero-rating, criminal penalties under the Swiss Criminal Code and Art. 186-188 VAT Act apply, including imprisonment up to 5 years. A retrospective clawback can extend 10 years for tax evasion; the ordinary limitation is 5 years for simple VAT assessments. The exporter must retain export documents for 10 years; failure to produce documentary evidence leads to denial of zero-rating and reassessment at 8.1% or the applicable reduced rate. Additionally, customs penalties under the Administrative Penalty Act (2019) may impose fines up to CHF 20,000 and confiscation of goods for false declarations or misclassification. The Federal Supreme Court requires bona fide commercial substance and proof of physical movement; circular transactions or shell intermediaries will be challenged as abusive.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full capital and current account convertibility for the Swiss franc. There are no foreign exchange controls, no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no central bank approval for cross-border transfers, and no restrictions on the conversion of CHF into other currencies. Foreign investors may inject equity and debt capital freely, and a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft may remit dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees abroad without prior exchange administration clearance. The only substantive remittance hurdle is tax-related: dividends and similar profit distributions attract Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at 35%, recoverable or reduced if the beneficial owner qualifies for a double-taxation treaty relief, the Swiss domestic participation exemption, or a Swiss parent-subsidiary mechanism. Interest on arm's-length loans, commercial trade payments, and salaries may be paid cross-border without withholding tax provided the recipient is not subject to Swiss fiduciary obligations. The CHF itself is fully convertible, and pricing and invoicing may be denominated in CHF, EUR, USD, or any agreed currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Switzerland requiring exporters to surrender export proceeds to the SNB or to convert foreign currency receipts into CHF. Export proceeds may remain in freely convertible foreign currency accounts at Swiss banks or be credited to any foreign bank account at the discretion of the Swiss exporter. The Swiss franc is freely floating under a flexible exchange-rate policy driven by the SNB's monetary policy. No mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or FINMA approval applies to export receipts. Export transactions are nevertheless subject to ordinary anti-money-laundering and sanctions compliance, and the receiving bank must be able to identify the commercial underlying, the beneficial owner, and the source of funds in the event of unusual patterns. For Swiss value-added tax purposes, export proceeds are generally treated as zero-rated supplies, but the tax status has no effect on FX surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); supervision of banks and financial market infrastructure is exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority FINMA (Eidgenössische Finanzmarktaufsicht). The legal framework is the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments, the Banking Act of 1934 (as amended), and FINMA Anti-Money Laundering Ordinances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Switzerland is not a member of the EEA, so an offshore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensed in the UK, Lithuania, or Singapore cannot benefit from an EU payment-services passport in Switzerland. An offshore EMI wishing to offer account or payment services to a Swiss AG must rely on a Swiss-licensed partner bank for SIC and SEPA settlement or obtain a Swiss FinTech authorization under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, limited to deposits not exceeding CHF 100 million and subject to FINMA supervision. For a low-risk dental aligner manufacturer, a reputable Swiss Tier-1 bank remains more practical and cheaper than a FinTech EMI because of the company's need for documentary trade facilities, Swiss franc clearing through SIC, and credible banking counterparty status for international orthodontic distributors. Offshore digital EMIs are feasible only as a supplementary payment channel for marketplace collections and are not recommended as the primary treasury bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial share capital of CHF 100,000 for an AG must be paid into a blocked or escrow account in the name of the company before the commercial register registrar will issue the certificate of incorporation. The chosen Swiss bank will provide a capital-escrow deposit confirmation, and the subscription proceeds must come from a verifiable bank account in the name of the shareholder or, if the contribution is made by a third party, with a written designated-funds declaration stating the underlying capital relationship. All wires must be sent via SWIFT MT103 using the bank's own settlement identity, with the remitter field matching the shareholder's name and the further-credits field clearly identifying the Swiss AG. The Swiss bank will require the original or certified copy of the notary's capital-payment attestation and will perform an onward transfer of funds to the company's operational account only after the commercial register entry is effected. No foreign-exchange declaration or SNB approval is required, but the payment reference should be 'Sacheinlage / contribuciòn de capital' or 'Share capital contribution – AG incorporation', and the source-of-funds documentation must be retained for ten years under Swiss AML obligations.The Swiss AG will clear trade disbursements primarily through the SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) system, SEPA (if settling in EUR within the EEA), and SWIFT MT103 messages for cross-border settlement with USD, CNY, or other convertible currencies. Export shipments of dental clear aligner sheets and custom-formed trays are settled by irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) in favour of the Swiss company, by L/C for the Swiss company's own purchases of PETG copolymers and automated trimming equipment, or by telegraphic transfer with clean payment terms. For commercial L/C transactions, the issuing bank forwards SWIFT MT700 messages to the Swiss advising bank, and the Swiss beneficiary presents compliant documents (commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, airway bill/bill of lading, sanitary and MEDDEV attestations if required) for payment through the accepting bank. Reduced export risk is achieved by using confirmed irrevocable L/Cs subject to UCP600, particularly where distributors in emerging markets are involved. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. Switzerland is not a member of the EEA, so an offshore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensed in the UK, Lithuania, or Singapore cannot benefit from an EU payment-services passport in Switzerland. An offshore EMI wishing to offer account or payment services to a Swiss AG must rely on a Swiss-licensed partner bank for SIC and SEPA settlement or obtain a Swiss FinTech authorization under Article 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, limited to deposits not exceeding CHF 100 million and subject to FINMA supervision. For a low-risk dental aligner manufacturer, a reputable Swiss Tier-1 bank remains more practical and cheaper than a FinTech EMI because of the company's need for documentary trade facilities, Swiss franc clearing through SIC, and credible banking counterparty status for international orthodontic distributors. Offshore digital EMIs are feasible only as a supplementary payment channel for marketplace collections and are not recommended as the primary treasury bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% (federal corporate income tax; cantonal and communal taxes apply, effective combined rate typically 12–20% depending on canton)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% (statutory Swiss withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; reduced to 0% for qualifying Swiss parent companies under participation relief, and to 0–15% under applicable DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds CHF 50 million and the value of intercompany transactions exceeds CHF 5 million (per transaction type). For smaller entities, a simplified documentation (Contemporaneous TP Study) is recommended.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the Federal Act on the Tax Treatment of Holding Companies and the Participation Relief require that a company have genuine economic substance (e.g., employees, premises, and decision-making) to benefit from participation relief or treaty benefits. The OECD's Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) actions, particularly Action 5, require substantial activity for IP holding companies. For a dental aligner manufacturer, substance is evidenced by R&D, production, and management activities in Switzerland.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 3 times the evaded tax (federal and cantonal), plus interest. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally liable for tax evasion if they knowingly participated or failed to act. Civil restitution of evaded taxes, travel bans, and potential extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 35% withholding tax on deemed distributions. Fines up to CHF 100,000 for non-compliance with substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the shell structure. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to CHF 500,000 for the company, and imprisonment for individuals. Regulatory sanctions include revocation of licenses and exclusion from public procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors can face imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines. Civil liability for damages to third parties. Travel bans and asset seizures are common.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 20–50% of the additional tax assessed, and interest. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the additional tax if they approved the non-arm's length pricing. Travel bans and professional disqualification may apply.
