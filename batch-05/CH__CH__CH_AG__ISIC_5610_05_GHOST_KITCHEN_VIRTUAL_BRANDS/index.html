Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Swiss AG must maintain a registered office (Sitz) in the canton stated in its articles; the Commercial Register accepts a 'c/o' or care-of address at a licensed Swiss fiduciary, corporate-services, or attorney office if postal reachability and physical availability are contractually secured. A fully letterbox-only address without any Swiss operational nexus is not sufficient for an active delivery-only ghost-kitchen operator. The recommended structure combines a registered office at a virtual-office service provider with a leased or owned licensed kitchen facility in Switzerland, plus a Swiss bank account, Swiss bookkeeping, and administration actually managed from the seat. For treaty protection and corporate substance, the effective place of management (tatsächliche Verwaltung) must be in Switzerland; a purely foreign-managed letterbox entity risks re-domiciliation or denial of tax residence benefits.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV / AFC) of the Swiss Confederation, VAT Division; operational portal at www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not established in Switzerland but undertakes an economic activity in Switzerland must submit the official Swiss VAT registration application (Form 103) to the Federal Tax Administration, including the legal form, business purpose, projected Swiss turnover, and the contact details of a Swiss fiscal representative if required. Upon approval, the FTA issues a Swiss VAT identification number (MWST/TVA/IVA) and assigns a tax period and reporting obligation. The applicant must also register for foreign entity direct tax purposes with the cantonal tax administration of the place of business, if any, and, in the absence of an establishment, no direct tax registration is generally required unless treaty-qualified permanent establishment nexus arises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity that is not established in Switzerland but undertakes an economic activity in Switzerland must submit the official Swiss VAT registration application (Form 103) to the Federal Tax Administration, including the legal form, business purpose, projected Swiss turnover, and the contact details of a Swiss fiscal representative if required. Upon approval, the FTA issues a Swiss VAT identification number (MWST/TVA/IVA) and assigns a tax period and reporting obligation. The applicant must also register for foreign entity direct tax purposes with the cantonal tax administration of the place of business, if any, and, in the absence of an establishment, no direct tax registration is generally required unless treaty-qualified permanent establishment nexus arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. Swiss statutory law classifies an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) as a corporate taxpayer resident in Switzerland subject to federal, cantonal, and communal profit tax on its worldwide income, subject to treaty-based relief for foreign permanent establishments and real estate. The AG may elect, under Article 21 of the Swiss VAT Act, to form a VAT group with wholly controlled Swiss group entities, thereby treating the group as one taxable person for VAT purposes. Certain Swiss group financing, participation, and R&D super deductions require statutory elections or disclosure on the tax return. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by Swiss law implementing the OECD Hybrid Mismatch Rules, with no elective deviation from those rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies and services, excluding exempt supplies, for Swiss-resident businesses; CHF 250,000 annual turnover from Swiss-source services for non-resident businesses without an establishment in Switzerland; separate CHF 100,000 threshold applies to distance-selling and marketplace facilitators; reduced CHF 20,000 threshold for small non-profit, charitable, or public-interest entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Switzerland to a non-Swiss destination are zero-rated when the vendor proves that ownership has passed to the foreign buyer and that the goods have actually left the Swiss customs territory. Services are zero-rated for exported use, including consultancy, digital services, catering and food supply services used wholly outside Switzerland, and services physically rendered abroad. The ghost kitchen must maintain evidence of the foreign customer relationship, delivery to the foreign destination, and the actual receipt of payment from outside Switzerland. Proof of export must be preserved in the ordinary course of business and made available to the Federal Tax Administration upon request.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrschein / Exportschein) or Swiss electronic export summary with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or equivalent transport document showing departure from Switzerland and arrival abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice / SWIFT confirmation evidencing payment transfer from the foreign buyer to the Swiss seller account
  • Commercial invoice and delivery note referencing the exported goods and the underlying ghost kitchen order
  • Written contract or purchase order from the non-resident customer proving the offshore commercial relationship
  • Proof of foreign customs clearance, if applicable (such as import declaration in the destination country)

No mandatory government real-time tax clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime exists. Invoicing must comply with Swiss VAT invoice content rules, including supplier and recipient identification, VAT rate, amount of VAT, and transaction date. Domestic and cross-border invoices must adopt the Swiss QR-bill standard where payment is made by Swiss bank transfer, and export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Swiss customs system (e-dec / SWISS-PRODEC). There is no mandatory API clearance layer beyond the customs export declaration and periodic VAT returns.

Swiss VAT authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims through documentary audits and cross-border information exchange. If the exporter cannot furnish the prescribed export pack within the statutory retention period, the zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back, VAT becomes due at 8.1% with default interest, and administrative fines of up to three times the evaded tax may be imposed. Where false or forged export documentation is used to obtain an unjustified VAT advantage, criminal prosecution may follow under Article 80 of the Swiss Federal Act on Value Added Tax, with penalties up to 3 times the tax evaded, and, in severe cases, imprisonment; the limitations period for tax evasion and VAT fraud is 10 years, and for minor negligence claims 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without a general FX permit regime. Switzerland maintains a liberalised current and capital account under Federal Constitution Art. 27 (economic freedom) and the National Bank Act; there are no exchange control restrictions on capital inflows, equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, or current-account payments. Profit remittance is unrestricted by the SNB, but outbound dividends attract a statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer, SR 642.21) which is fully refundable under the domicile principle or partially reclaimable under an applicable double tax treaty. Actual convertibility of the CHF is unencumbered; the SNB intervenes in FX markets only for monetary-policy price stability, not for convertibility rationing. The sole constraints on cross-border flows derive from the international sanctions framework (Embargo Act, SR 946.231.1) under which payments to sanctioned jurisdictions or persons are frozen and reported to SECO.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Cross-border trading income from ghost-kitchen delivery services, brand licensing, and international consulting fees may be held indefinitely in any convertible currency. The only binding obligations are statistical and AML-related: (i) SNB balance-of-payments reporting (ZIVAP/SNO-Gen) once cumulative annual cross-border payments exceed CHF 500,000 requiring monthly electronic filing of transaction-level data with the SNB; and (ii) FINMA AMLA diligence for all cash transactions in excess of CHF 100,000 per counter-party, which is inapplicable to the digital payout rails of UberEats and DoorDash but relevant for potential cash reconciliation in commissary procurement.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank, SR 951.11) acting both as the central monetary authority and as the operator of the SIC clearing system and CHF settlement accounts; complemented by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and AML regulation under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (SR 955.0) and the Financial Services Act (FinSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low as a primary treasury rail; viable only as a peripheral disbursement sleeve. Legal constraints: (i) EEA-authorised e-money institutions (e.g., Lithuania-licensed or UK FCA EMIs) cannot passport money-remittance or e-money services into Switzerland without FINMA authorisation; their use is limited to payouts into foreign delivery partners and EU suppliers, not for CHF custody or capital escrow; (ii) any FinTech EMI holding CHF on behalf of the AG requires a Swiss banking licence or FinTech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act, with a hard CHF 1 million sight-deposit cap; (iii) FINMA-regulated Swiss digital banks (e.g., Yapeal, Alpian, Radicant) permit remote video-KYC onboarding under FINMA Circular 2020/05, but capital-escrow accumulation above CHF 1 million is prohibited, forcing the use of UBS/ZKB for the share-capital deposit. Recommended architecture: UBS/ZKB core settlement accounts plus a single GBP-denominated EMI sleeve for UK-side UberEats/DoorDash payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Legal constraints: (i) EEA-authorised e-money institutions (e.g., Lithuania-licensed or UK FCA EMIs) cannot passport money-remittance or e-money services into Switzerland without FINMA authorisation; their use is limited to payouts into foreign delivery partners and EU suppliers, not for CHF custody or capital escrow; (ii) any FinTech EMI holding CHF on behalf of the AG requires a Swiss banking licence or FinTech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act, with a hard CHF 1 million sight-deposit cap; (iii) FINMA-regulated Swiss digital banks (e.g., Yapeal, Alpian, Radicant) permit remote video-KYC onboarding under FINMA Circular 2020/05, but capital-escrow accumulation above CHF 1 million is prohibited, forcing the use of UBS/ZKB for the share-capital deposit. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold in Swiss law; Federal Tax Administration practice requires Local File documentation when related-party cross-border transactions involve material amounts (commonly interpreted above CHF 3,000,000 per annum) or when inter-group financing exceeds CHF 1,000,000; Master File/CbCR is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue over CHF 900,000,000; ordinary documentation must be available within 30 days upon tax authority request

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations as found in offshore jurisdictions do not apply to Switzerland; however, the Swiss anti-treaty-abuse doctrine and OECD BEPS rules require actual business substance (office, staff, active decision-making, physical kitchen operations); a letterbox AG being used for passive profit-shifting will have DTA benefits denied, profits recomputed, and possibly 35% WHT triggered on deemed distributions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (qualified and simple)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 50% of the tax evaded (simple), up to 100% or more in qualified cases, plus default interest and reassessment; Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax imposes fines up to ten times the evaded amount where extraordinary culpability is shown; illegal asset seizure and confiscation of criminal proceeds

Director personal liability: Board members and shadow directors who sign false tax returns or instruct concealment are criminally liable; imprisonment for up to 5 years is possible; personal net worth may be attached for tax restitution and procedural fines; travel bans can be imposed during criminal investigation
Shell Company / Letterbox AG

Statutory sanction: Reallocation of profits to the foreign actual entrepreneurial benefactor, denial of treaty benefits, added assessed income subject to CIT at combined rates, deemed-dividend treatment and assessment of 35% WHT if economic substance refutation is sustained

Director personal liability: Formal directors who knowingly participate in shell-company structures may be jointly and severally liable for deficient withholding taxes; criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion and failure to take mandated bankruptcy measures; in extreme cases they can be imprisoned and banned from acting as directors in Switzerland
Withholding Tax Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Federal withholding tax assessed as a direct claim against the company, default interest from due date, penalty of up to 100% of unpaid tax; if the foreign shareholder becomes unreachable, the AG remains liable for the gross amount

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for wilful non-deduction of withholding tax and social-security deductions; their salary and seizure orders and, in serious cases, criminal imprisonment up to three years
AML / Financial Compliance

Statutory sanction: Position not a licensed Swiss financial intermediary for ordinary restaurant collection; however, if the AG enters into lending, payment processing or arranged crypto-currency payouts, the Anti-Money Laundering Act applies; administrative fines up to CHF 500,000 and possible revocation of banking permission; supervisory sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to report suspicious transactions under the GwG/AMLA can be prosecuted for money laundering (StGB Art. 305bis); imprisonment up to three years, up to 5 when acting as criminal organisation member; personal bans from holding office and asset freezing orders
