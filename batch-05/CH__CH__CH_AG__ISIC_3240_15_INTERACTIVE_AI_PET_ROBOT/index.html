Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung (ESTV), Administration fédérale des contributions (AFC), Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni (AFC); digital portal: 'My ESTV' at estv.admin.ch; public commercial register: Zefix at zefix.ch.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG (Aktiengesellschaft) receives its permanent UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) automatically upon entry into the cantonal Commercial Register, which is the compulsory first registration step. The UID is the statutory business identifier used for VAT, direct tax, and statistical purposes. For a non-resident entity without a Swiss commercial-register seat, the ESTV assigns a UID and Swiss VAT account upon written/online application (Form 171 plus supporting statute, board resolution, and proof of foreign registration); the applicant not established in Switzerland must designate a Swiss service-of-process address and may be required to appoint a Swiss fiscal representative. All VAT accounts are administered centrally by the ESTV VAT division, while income-tax assessments are handled by canton Zurich or the relevant canton with federal corporate tax coordination via the Consensus/EWR system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation, Territorial_Scope (resident AG taxed on worldwide income; foreign permanent-establishment profits and foreign real estate exempt under Swiss territorial exemption); Tax_Exempt_Zone not applicable; no Pass_Through_Eligible classification for an AG.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Switzerland; the AG is a mandatory entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections: (1) Swiss VAT small-business election to opt out of VAT if turnover does not exceed CHF 250,000; (2) Swiss VAT group election (Steuergruppe) for related Swiss entities under a single UID; (3) participation-exemption election/waiver for qualifying dividends and capital gains; (4) patented IP-box election (90% income exemption) and R&D super-deduction (150%); (5) canton-level tax grouping (e.g., Zug, Schwyz) for cantonal direct taxes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover for Swiss-established enterprises; CHF 250,000 threshold for the voluntary small-business exemption election; non-resident suppliers generally reverse-charged on Swiss B2B services, but must register for Swiss VAT if supplying e-services or goods to Swiss consumers and exceeding CHF 100,000 in annual Swiss-derived turnover. Interactive AI robotic toys sold B2C in Switzerland are subject to the 8.1% standard rate; no reduced rate applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supply of goods qualifies for the Swiss 0% VAT rate under Art. 21-22 MWSTG only if (a) economic ownership of the goods is transferred to a non-Swiss buyer, (b) the goods are physically transported/consigned abroad, and (c) the seller obtains and retains sufficient documentary proof of export within the VAT return period applicable to the supply. Services directly related to the exported robotic goods or international transport/logistics services can also be zero-rated when the recipient is abroad and rights are effectively used outside Switzerland. Input VAT relating to the zero-rated export is fully creditable to the exporter.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration / e-dec export) with customs receipt stamp or electronic clearance certificate
  • Commercial transport evidence: Bill of Lading (Konossement), Air Waybill, CMR international consignment note, or postal/courier export certificate showing non-Swiss consignee and destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in CHF or convertible foreign currency (pipeline benchmark; Switzerland has no exchange-control requirement)
  • mandatory_fx_repatriation_deadline_days

No real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based fiscal stamping, and no continuous transaction control in Switzerland. Mandatory elements are the Swiss QR-Bill/QR-Rechnung payment standard for customer invoicing and statutory invoice content requirements under Art. 26 MWSTG. Electronic invoicing (EDI/Peppol/e-bill) is accepted, but fiscal returns must be filed electronically through the 'My ESTV' portal on a quarterly basis. For exporters, export declarations must pass through Swiss customs 'e-dec' or equivalent digital customs clearance systems.

Swiss Federal Tax Administration may retrospectively review export proof within the 10-year statutory books-and-records retention period. If 0% zero-rating is improperly claimed, the export VAT is reclaimed at 8.1% with default interest, and input VAT credits are clawed back. Administrative penalties under Arts. 96-98 MWSTG range up to three times the evaded tax, plus imprisonment in serious cases, and forged export documentation triggers Swiss Penal Code Art. 251 plus customs-evasion fines up to five times the evaded duties. For foreign entities, the ESTV may also deny refund entitlement and revoke the VAT registration for persistent false export zero-rating. The mandatory 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is a contractual/pipeline control, not a Swiss statutory requirement; failure to repatriate does not independently trigger Swiss VAT clawback but is treated as a red flag in audit scoring.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is a fully convertible currency. Switzerland operates an open liberalised legal order with no exchange-control statute, no regime differentiated between current account and capital account transactions, and no approval requirement for inward remittance, dividend payment, loan repayment, or liquidation proceeds. Capital can be moved freely in or out of the country in any internationally traded currency. The only material frictions for profit remittance are tax-oriented, not exchange-control-oriented: dividends paid by the CH_AG to foreign shareholders attract a 35% Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which is reclaimable in full or substantially reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or where the beneficiary can invoke the LOB-consistent refund procedure. The CH_AG may hold foreign currency accounts (USD, EUR, CNY, etc.) without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules, no repartriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Switzerland. The CH_AG is free to maintain export earnings in USD, EUR, or CNY, provided such amounts are properly reflected in the financial statements under the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR) and in VAT export evidence under the Swiss VAT Act. The SNB does not require or request any conversion of foreign export currencies into CHF.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera), co-supervised for banking and financial market conduct by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Swiss effective rate for the Zug jurisdiction (2024): 11.9% - Federal statutory CIT rate of 8.5% on after-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% on pre-tax profit) plus Zug cantonal/communal profit tax of approximately 4.19% of pre-tax profit; national combined rate range 11.9% to 21.0% depending on canton and commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends distributed by a Swiss AG to shareholders, including non-resident shareholders; the tax is due to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) before distribution and is normally withheld by the paying company from the gross dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Swiss Local File mandatory if annual group turnover equals or exceeds CHF 50 million; Master File mandatory if consolidated group turnover equals or exceeds CHF 1 billion; simplified contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required even below the Local File threshold where related-party transactions exceed: CHF 5 million for purchase/sale of goods, CHF 1 million for services (including management fees and R&D cost-sharing), and CHF 0.5 million for financial transactions or IP licensing; the Swiss tax administration may compel documentation for any taxpayer by formal assessment notice under Art. 21 of the Tax Administration Act of 2020.

Economic substance scope: OECD Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for zero-tax jurisdictions are not applicable to Switzerland, which levies statutory corporate tax at an effective rate above 11%; nevertheless, the Swiss AG must demonstrate genuine commercial substance for treaty and intra-group purposes: local board meetings in Zug, decision-making bodies present in Switzerland, local employees with technical competence for the AI pet robot product, and a real office premises; artificial arrangements diverting profit to a zero-tax foreign entity and leaving the Swiss AG as a bare shell will fail the Swiss anti-abuse doctrine (substance-over-form), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test for treaty benefits, and the beneficial owner requirement under the withholding tax refund procedure; the Federal Supreme Court and the FTA will reattribute the profit and deny treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Profit Understatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty assessment under DBG Art. 174 (federal), StHG Art. 55/56 (cantonal Zug), and Art. 186 (serious tax fraud) for use of forged or falsified documents; fine of up to three times the evaded tax plus default interest at 4.75% per year; in tax-fraud cases imprisonment up to two years or monetary penalty; statute of limitation for gross negligence is 10 years; all group tax benefits including patent box and foreign tax credits are revoked retroactively.

Director personal liability: The Chairman, CFO and any board member signing the tax return are personally subject to criminal fines and imprisonment for tax fraud; known officers may be named in the penalty order even after the company's solvency; the FTA may issue a travel ban (exit restriction) and international debt-collection assistance to enforce collection against personal assets where the director is deemed the 'wirtschaftlich Beteiligter'.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: The Swiss paying company becomes personally liable for payment of the entire 35% withholding tax together with interest (Art. 11 VStG) and a fine up to three times the endangered tax pursuant to Art. 66 VStG; treaty refund denied and taxable dividend reclassified, with potential criminal proceedings for tax fraud against the responsible directors and the Swiss agent of any foreign shell entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Swiss authorized signatory (manager) are jointly and severally liable for withholding tax debts of the company upon insolvency; under Art. 12 SchKG (Debt Enforcement Act), officers who knowingly caused tax shortfalls by failing to remit withheld sums face personal liability and debt-enforcement seizure of their Swiss bank accounts; criminal conviction may carry imprisonment or fines payable from the director's personal estate.
Money Laundering, UBO Transparency and FATCA/CRS Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA) by financial intermediaries involved, FINMA enforcement orders, penalty fines up to CHF 1 million; Swiss Code of Obligations disclosure violations attracting fines and delisting/withdrawal of business license; FATCA/CRS misreporting penalty of up to CHF 250,000 per infraction plus corrective administrative proceedings; the tax authority may reverse all legal non-recognition of the AG, treating it as transactionally opaque.

Director personal liability: Under the AMLA and the Swiss Criminal Code, directors who knowingly conduct suspect transactions or fail to report them face imprisonment up to five years and personal monetary fines; the corporate veil is pierced when shareholders and directors use the AG to defraud creditors or tax authorities, permitting civil restitution proceedings in the Swiss Federal Criminal Court; board members are prohibited from leaving Switzerland pending investigation and may be subjected to asset freezes and travel bans.
