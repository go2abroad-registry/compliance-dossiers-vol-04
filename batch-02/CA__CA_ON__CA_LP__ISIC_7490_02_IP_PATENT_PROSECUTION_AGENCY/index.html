Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); online portal: CRA My Business Account via canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; provincial employer tax authority: Ontario Ministry of Finance (ontario.ca/page/ontario-taxes).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Ontario Limited Partnership (LP) under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act (or as an extra-provincial LP if formed elsewhere). 2) File Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with CRA through the 'Register a business' portal or by mail; CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a parallel GST/HST program account (RT suffix). 3) Mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when taxable worldwide commercial revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters; registration is available voluntarily below the threshold to claim input tax credits. 4) If payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000, open an Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) account with the Ontario Ministry of Finance. 5) The LP must file a T5013 information return and issue T5013 slips to partners; each resident partner needs a SIN or personal BN, and each non-resident partner must disclose its foreign TIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax purposes; the LP itself is not a taxpayer, files only an information return (T5013), and all Canada-source income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Ontario income tax purposes; the LP itself is not a taxpayer, files only an information return (T5013), and all Canada-source income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act only where the LP carries on business through a Canadian permanent establishment, subject to treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or entity classification election; fiscal transparency follows statutorily from the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act. Relevant elections: (i) GST/HST closely-related election under Excise Tax Act s.156 for supplies between the LP and a qualifying related corporation; (ii) partnership rollover and deemed-disposition elections under ITA ss.98(2) and 98(3); (iii) hybrid mismatch deduction-denial and reporting rules (ITA ss.18.4-18.6) apply to cross-border arrangements exploiting entity classification differences; the LP cannot elect corporate taxation domestically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold, ETA s.148); once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 30 days after the calendar quarter in which the threshold is surpassed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Patent prosecution, PCT filing, and international trademark services qualify for 0% GST/HST where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; (iii) the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada; and (iv) the supplier neither knows nor ought to know that consideration will not be paid within two years of the supply (ETA Schedule VI Part V ss.7-10). A supply of patent or trademark rights to a non-resident is likewise zero-rated, and associated input tax credits remain recoverable by the LP.

  • Non-resident status certificate (IRS Form W-8BEN-E for US corporate clients, or equivalent foreign residence attestation)
  • Executed patent prosecution engagement letter / PCT services contract naming the non-resident client
  • Itemized commercial invoice addressed to the foreign client's billing location
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103, Wise payment credit advice, or bank transfer confirmation) proving receipt within the two-year rule
  • Canada Customs B13A export declaration and Bill of Lading / Air Waybill only where physical specimens, prototypes, or trademark evidence are shipped abroad; pure service exports require no customs clearance

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada; CRA does not pre-authorize invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL-based Pan-Canadian 'Bison Digital' network. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000, and all registrants must retain invoices and supporting records for six years.

CRA may reassess within four years (extended to ten years for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud) and retroactively claw back the 13% HST with daily compounded interest where export zero-rating substantiation is deficient. Penalties include a 25% net-tax misstatement penalty under ETA s.280, a 25% false-claim penalty under ETA s.285.1, and criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 carrying fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Zero-rating is also denied where consideration is not paid within the ETA two-year (730-day) evidentiary rule, notwithstanding the absence of any Canadian exchange-control repatriation deadline.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a clean floating exchange-rate regime for the Canadian dollar (CAD) with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no exchange-control statute, no general licensing requirement for foreign-currency transactions, and no restriction on capital injections, profit remittances, or non-resident distributions from an Ontario limited partnership. The practical hurdles are tax-driven rather than FX-regulatory: distributions of income to non-resident limited partners may attract Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, reducible or eliminable under an applicable tax treaty (notably the Canada-United States Tax Convention), and bank-side conversion margins apply. Repatriation of pure capital contributions is unrestricted and not subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds within any prescribed timeline. A patent prosecution agency may retain foreign-currency receivables in an OSFI-supervised foreign-currency account without mandatory conversion to CAD. The only compliance filters are the FINTRAC reporting obligations applicable to financial institutions for international electronic fund transfers and large cash transactions at the CAD 10,000 threshold, together with Income Tax Act reporting obligations (Form T5013, Form T1135/T1134) that arise for the partnership and its partners but are unrelated to foreign-exchange control.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central monetary authority, managing Canadian foreign exchange reserves and overseeing the domestic payments and clearing infrastructure (Lynx RTGS, CDSX and the Retail Payment Activities Act regime). Prudential supervision of authorised foreign-exchange dealers is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act, while cross-border wire monitoring and AML/ATF enforcement are performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, conditional. Wise (Wise Payments Limited) is viable as a secondary, low-value outward disbursement channel because its Canadian operation is a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB). However, an Ontario LP cannot hold its primary treasury, client escrow, or trust funds with Wise: sections 156 and 545 of the Bank Act reserve deposit-taking in Canada to banks chartered under the Bank Act, and the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires licensing or transitional registration of payment service providers. Unregistered offshore EMIs cannot lawfully clear client deposits or execute recurring patent-office disbursements. The legally safe structure is a primary Schedule I bank account for escrow and operating balances, with Wise permitted only for sub-CAD 15,000 associate and non-official disbursements, explicitly excluding client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners should be routed by SWIFT MT103 in the partner's home currency to the LP's foreign-currency account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with the payment reference clearly stating 'capital contribution under limited partnership agreement dated [DATE] - partner [NAME]'. The receiving bank converts at spot to CAD or credits the foreign-currency sub-account; domestic CAD clearing is settled through Lynx RTGS on a T+0 basis. Contributions are not taxable income and do not attract Part XIII withholding, provided the partnership records the contribution to the partner's capital account rather than as a fee or loan. Compliance artefacts include the signed partnership agreement, CRS/W-8 self-certifications, and the bank's funds-flow questionnaire; wires at or above CAD 10,000 are captured by the bank's international electronic funds transfer reporting automatically, with no separate client filing required. For small contributions under CAD 10,000, a Wise Business transfer is acceptable routing, but the source-of-funds narrative must accompany the bank's due diligence file.Two clearing rails are recommended. First, Official_Patent_Office_Wire: SWIFT MT103 disbursements from the LP's Schedule I foreign-currency account directly to the designated patent-office bank account, including the WIPO International Bureau account at UBS Zurich, the USPTO deposit accounts funded via Pay.gov or EFS-Web, the European Patent Office EUR deposit account with Deutsche Bundesbank, and CNIPA or JPO disbursements via their designated London or New York correspondents. Second, the Wise Business rail for small-value associate disbursements under CAD 15,000, using local clearing systems (ACH in the United States, SEPA in the European Union, CHAPS in the United Kingdom) but strictly excluding client funds, which must remain in the bank escrow ledger. Every outbound MT103 must carry the purpose narrative 'international patent prosecution services - official fees and associate disbursements', and each beneficiary account must be verified as a registered patent or trademark office or a regulated IP law firm. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% combined Canadian federal (15%) and Ontario provincial (10%) general corporate rate; the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a taxpayer. Income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level at the applicable corporate or individual marginal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on any distribution, allocation, or crediting of Canadian-source partnership income to a non-resident partner; this amount is withholdable at source under sections 194.2–194.3 of the Income Tax Act. The statutory rate may be reduced to the applicable treaty rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 must be filed no later than 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due June 30). Ontario's LP-1 declaration is due on the anniversary of registration or within 60 days of any change in registered information.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) must be filed when reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million in fair market value annually. No fixed statutory threshold applies to compel a Master File/Local File, but CRA transfer-pricing penalty exposure under section 247(3) is triggered when the total adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue and the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to establish arm's-length prices. Full contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation should be maintained where cross-border transactions are material.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime like the UAE. Instead, substantive activity is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), sham transaction jurisprudence, and the Multilateral Instrument's minimum-standard benefit denial rule. A non-resident partner's LP interest must carry genuine economic substance, actual business activity in Ontario, and a non-tax commercial purpose; otherwise the CRA may disregard, recharacterize, or deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163.2 of 50% of the understated tax, plus interest and the denial of any refund.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any limited partner who takes control or exercises management authority are jointly and severally liable. Directors of a corporate general partner face personal liability under section 227.1 for uncracked withholding and related penalties unless due-diligence is proven.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: The LP and general partner are liable for the full gross amount under withheld tax per section 215(2), plus daily compounding interest and late-remittance penalties (typically 5% plus 1% per month up to 12%).

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner and the general partner can be assessed personally by the CRA for the unremitted amounts without first exhausting remedies against the entity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustment adding the quantum, plus a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) of the Income Tax Act when the adjusted amount exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue; separate penalties up to CAD 100 per day apply for failure to file T106.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing penalties, but they become personally liable if the adjustment creates an underlying unremitted withholding liability for non-resident allocations.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / AML

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 disallows the tax benefit and imposes a 25% penalty on the full benefit, plus interest; the CRA may deny treaty withholding relief under the MLI benefit-denial rule. Where unlicensed money-transfer services are rendered for foreign clients, penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act reach CAD 500,000 for entities and CAD 250,000 for individuals per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the sham or authorize FINTRAC non-compliance face criminal liability and up to 5 years' imprisonment; officers can also be disqualified from acting as directors and be subject to administrative monetary penalties.
