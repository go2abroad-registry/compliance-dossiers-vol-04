Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal tax administration portal: 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online'. Ontario's HST component (8%) is administered federally by CRA; the Ontario Ministry of Finance remains competent for Ontario-only levies such as Employer Health Tax where the LP or its partners have Ontario payroll.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada, including through Canadian e-commerce fulfillment or inventory storage, must: (1) obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) using CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or via Business Registration Online (BRO); (2) open a GST/HST program account ('RT') using Form RC1 or the dedicated non-resident GST/HST application (Form RC171) and, where required, designate a Canadian agent authorized for service; (3) for physical imports/exports of industrial MRO goods, activate an 'RM' Import/Export account with CRA and arrange customs accounting documentation under the Customs Act; (4) if employees are engaged in Ontario, register an 'RP' payroll deduction account with CRA and an Employer Health Tax account with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; (5) file an annual partnership information return (T5013) even where all partners are non-resident. A foreign entity must supply a registered Canadian address or an authorized representative before the BN is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes under Income Tax Act section 96. The LP is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to each partner in accordance with partnership law. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada are taxable under Part I on their Canadian-source share and are generally deemed to have a Canadian permanent establishment through the partnership. Entity-level taxation arises only if the LP is tainted as a 'specified investment flow-through' (SIFT) partnership, which is not applicable to active B2B industrial MRO trading. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is an independent 'person' required to register and collect HST in its own BN.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election; the LP's flow-through status is mandatory. Relevant elections available to a CA LP: (a) joint GST/HST election under ETA subsection 167(2) to zero-rate a transfer of the business as a going concern; (b) related-party election under ETA section 156 to treat qualifying intra-group supplies as made for nil consideration, subject to strict conditions; (c) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA subsection 249.1(4); (d) election regarding alternative cost base allocations among partners (ITA section 96) and CRA administrative elections for non-resident partners' treaty allocation. Anti-hybrid and interest-limitation regimes (OECD BEPS Action 2 principles and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation, EIFEL) apply to constrain mismatch outcomes but do not confer elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter (aggregate worldwide taxable supplies threshold under ETA subsections 148(1) and 149(1)); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required effective the first day of the following calendar month. For non-resident suppliers of digital or intangible services, a separate mandatory simplified registration applies without the CAD 30,000 threshold; for the B2B catalog reseller of physical MRO goods, physical inventory or fulfillment activities in Ontario will also establish taxing nexus and registration duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may claim 0% GST/HST under ETA Schedule VI, Part I on supplies of tangible industrial MRO hardware exported from Canada if: (a) the goods are exported by the LP or on its behalf as part of the sale; (b) the purchaser is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply; (c) the goods are shipped directly out of Canada and leave within a reasonable time (CRA administrative practice generally expects export within 60 days, or as evidenced by the shipping documents); and (d) the supply is not consumed by a person in Canada. Zero-rating may also extend to offshore support services under Schedule VI, Part V where performed for a non-resident and not related to Canadian situs property. The LP must preserve objective documentary support for each zero-rated export; where documentation is incomplete, CRA will treat the supply as taxable at 13% HST.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or via a licensed customs broker for shipments above the CAD 2,000 reporting threshold
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, unit values, and HS codes for the industrial MRO hardware
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill naming the CA LP as exporter of record and the non-resident purchaser as consignee
  • Carrier proof of export (customs-stamped manifest, port/airport departure record, or border crossing evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/wire/credit-card settlement records evidencing cross-border payment from the non-resident buyer
  • USMCA/Canada-US documentation or low-value shipment records where goods transit through the United States
  • Customs cargo control number (e.g., ACI e-manifest reference) when clearing US customs

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance mandate exists in Canada. Invoices are freely issued under the ETA without preapproval. Nevertheless, electronic invoicing obligations are implicit: (a) every GST/HST registrant must issue a sufficiently detailed invoice containing the registrant's BN, the supplier's name/address, and the consideration; (b) electronic records and e-invoices (including XML/UBL/PEPPOL formats) must be retained in CRA-accepted digital format for six years under ITA section 230 and ETA section 286; (c) export invoices must show the zero-rating basis; (d) the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL framework is available for B2B/B2G e-invoicing on a voluntary basis and will not alter HST collection mechanics.

Where export zero-rating claims are not substantiated, CRA may reassess the LP to collect uncollected HST at 13% of the supply value plus compound statutory interest. The civil gross-negligence penalty equals 50% of the understated net tax under the Income Tax Act and comparable ETA provisions; knowingly making false statements or evading tax attracts summary conviction fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to two years (ETA section 327). The normal CRA reassessment period is four years from the filing date, but no limitation period applies where misrepresentation or fraud is shown. CRA export-verification programs and 'Trust and Verify' audits commonly test documentation within 6 to 18 months after filing, and any HST refunds or input tax credits claimed in connection with zero-rated exports are clawed back with interest upon audit failure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility exists. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating major currency under IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no exchange controls, no licensing gates for cross-border capital movements, no administered dual-rate regime and no approval requirement for profit remittance since the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. For the Ontario Limited Partnership distributing to non-resident partners, the only material friction is tax-driven, not FX-driven: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), imposes 25% withholding on passive remittances (interest, royalties, management fees) unless reduced by the Canada-United States Tax Convention (1980) or another applicable treaty; (ii) a non-resident partner's allocated share of Canadian-source business income is taxable on a net Part I basis via Section 115 with NR4/T5013 reporting rather than by withholding; (iii) CRS/FATCA classification of this operating B2B MRO business is 'Active Non-Financial Entity' (NFE), avoiding automatic-exchange reporting, but the bank will still require a partner structure schedule. Ontario adds no provincial exchange-control restriction; provincial consumption tax (HST 13%) and filing obligations do not impede cross-border treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The seller of industrial MRO supplies may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or any convertible currency and may retain foreign-currency balances in Canadian or offshore accounts indefinitely. Residual obligations are: (i) FINTRAC EFT reporting of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more received or sent by or on behalf of the LP within a 24-hour period (PCMLTFA s. 9(1.1); SOR/2002-184, ss. 36-39); (ii) GST/HST zero-rating documentary proof for exported tangible goods under Excise Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. E-15, Schedule VI, Part V, namely a bill of lading, airway waybill or customs entry evidencing export from Canada; (iii) five-year transactional record retention under PCMLTFA s. 6; and (iv) CRA Form T106 disclosure for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions by the partnership where applicable thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central monetary authority constituted under the Bank of Canada Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2, responsible for monetary policy, currency issuance and financial system stability; prudential regulation of deposit-taking banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act, S.C. 1991, c. 46; the exchange-rate framework and official Exchange Fund Account are administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), S.C. 2000, c. 17, as the national AML/ATF intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM for payment facilitation (Wise Business, Stripe, Adyen, Payoneer, Airwallex) but LOW as the sole repository for treasury or escrow capital. Legal constraints: (i) under the Bank Act, s. 410, only authorized Schedule I/Schedule II banks may accept deposit liabilities in Canada, so an offshore EMI (e.g., US-based Mercury or UK-based ClearBank) cannot lawfully hold CAD deposits for a Canadian-resident LP without a Canadian licensed banking sponsor; (ii) a Canada-facing fintech must be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA and is not a member of Payments Canada, requiring partner-bank clearing for wire transactions; (iii) for the target payment rails (Credit Card and SWIFT Wire), the optimal stack is a Tier-1 bank operational account as the primary treasury ledger, with Stripe/Adyen acquiring card volumes and Wise/Airwallex executing low-value cross-border disbursements as sub-ledger rails only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the non-resident partner wires USD or CAD by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian operational account (bank BICs, e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, TDOMCATTTOR for TD, BOFMCAM2 for BMO, CIBCCATT for CIBC, NOSCCATT for Scotiabank) with the beneficiary line reading 'Ontario Limited Partnership [legal name]' and the reference/memo field stating 'Capital Contribution — LP Agreement dated [date], Article [number]'. Canada does not mandate a government-approved purpose code for in-bound wires, but the bank's FINTRAC-compliant CDD file must contain the capital-call notice, the GP resolution and an aggregate contribution schedule. Any international wire of CAD 10,000 or more triggers an EFT report. To preserve tax character, the contribution should be documented as equity capital (not a loan or shareholder advance) to avoid deemed-interest withholding; a non-arm's-length non-resident contributor should be tested for Form T106 reporting under section 233.1 ITA. Currency conversion is executed at the bank's commercial spot rate or pre-booked via the permitted forward instruments; no Bank of Canada record is required.International supplier payments: SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the LP's CAD/USD account with field 70 citing the commercial invoice number and goods description; documentary trade finance is available through irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 (and eUCP 2.0 for digital presentation) with Canadian banks, plus standby LCs and documentary collections as fallback. Domestic Canadian clearing: high-value CAD interbank transfers settle through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system against the Bank of Canada, while batch EFT/ACH items clear through ACSS; credit-card receivables are settled by the acquiring processor (Moneris, Chase Paymentech, Stripe, or Global Payments) into the LP operational account. All outgoing international wires of CAD 10,000 or more are EFT-reported to FINTRAC, and beneficiary banks in sanctioned jurisdictions are automatically blocked by the sending bank's consolidated screening engine.Deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards (vanilla, custom-dated) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and CSA with a Schedule I Canadian bank; no governmental authorization required.FX swaps and rollovers on the same deliverable settlement infrastructure for working-capital and trade-receivable maturity matching.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD principal-exchange) under ISDA documentation for long-term supplier or financing exposure.OTC vanilla FX options (CAD puts and calls) written by an OSFI-regulated bank, permitted for hedging the B2B catalogue settlement cycle.USD/CNH non-deliverable forwards covering supplier invoices denominated in RMB/CNH, because CAD itself is fully deliverable and a CAD NDF is non-standard and economically unnecessary.Synthetic forwards combining CAD/USD spot and options to hedge the float between USD receivables, CAD operating costs and foreign-currency supplier payables.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM for payment facilitation (Wise Business, Stripe, Adyen, Payoneer, Airwallex) but LOW as the sole repository for treasury or escrow capital. Legal constraints: (i) under the Bank Act, s. 410, only authorized Schedule I/Schedule II banks may accept deposit liabilities in Canada, so an offshore EMI (e.g., US-based Mercury or UK-based ClearBank) cannot lawfully hold CAD deposits for a Canadian-resident LP without a Canadian licensed banking sponsor; (ii) a Canada-facing fintech must be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA and is not a member of Payments Canada, requiring partner-bank clearing for wire transactions; (iii) for the target payment rails (Credit Card and SWIFT Wire), the optimal stack is a Tier-1 bank operational account as the primary treasury ledger, with Stripe/Adyen acquiring card volumes and Wise/Airwallex executing low-value cross-border disbursements as sub-ledger rails only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (goods) / CAD 500,000 (services) per non-resident related party transaction

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial substance, especially in cross-border arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment; denial of treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate taxes if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential criminal charges.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering up to CAD 500,000 and 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete) plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
