Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

AG minimum nominal capital is CHF 100,000; at least CHF 50,000 (50%) must be actually paid up before registration. The paid-in amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) held by a Swiss bank and evidenced by a bank certificate to the notary; if the deed requires full payment, the entire CHF 100,000 remains blocked until the board obtains free control after registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); sole software portal: www.estv.admin.ch and the eService VAT filing portal (eservices.estv.admin.ch). Cantonal and municipal tax administrations administer income and capital taxes at sub-national level.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is incorporated by public notarial deed and entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); the Federal Statistical Office (BFS) then issues the enterprise identification number (UID/IDE/IID). VAT registration is filed separately with the ESTV through the eService portal within 30 days of commencing taxable activity; the ESTV assigns the VAT suffix MWST/IVA/TVA to the UID (format: CHE-123.456.789 MWST). Foreign entities not established in Switzerland may either obtain a direct UID/VAT ID from the ESTV or appoint a Swiss fiscal representative in order to file VAT returns, subject to the statutory thresholds and place-of-supply rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is incorporated by public notarial deed and entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); the Federal Statistical Office (BFS) then issues the enterprise identification number (UID/IDE/IID). VAT registration is filed separately with the ESTV through the eService portal within 30 days of commencing taxable activity; the ESTV assigns the VAT suffix MWST/IVA/TVA to the UID (format: CHE-123.456.789 MWST). Foreign entities not established in Switzerland may either obtain a direct UID/VAT ID from the ESTV or appoint a Swiss fiscal representative in order to file VAT returns, subject to the statutory thresholds and place-of-supply rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections relevant to the shipping-bunkering AG include: VAT group election (consolidated VAT registration of closely held entities under Art. 21 LTVA); participation deduction election for dividend and capital-gain income on qualifying participations (Art. 69 DBG); election to maintain capital contribution reserves as distributable Swiss-reserve equity (Art. 5s DBG); and the election to treat domestic branches or co-entrepreneurial joint venture activities as independent taxable units. Hybrid mismatch legislation implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 has been in force since 1 January 2020 and disallows passthrough or hybrid mismatch election outcomes that create double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover for Swiss-established businesses; for non-established foreign businesses, CHF 100,000 of annual turnover derived from taxable services performed in Switzerland, with deliveries of goods located in Switzerland triggering immediate registration without a monetary threshold; small businesses may elect voluntary registration under Art. 7 LTVA; the 8.1% standard rate applies from 1 January 2024 (increased from 7.7%; reduced rate 2.6%; accommodation rate 3.7%).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 53(1) LTVA, the 0% rate applies to supplies of goods physically exported from Switzerland into a foreign customs territory and to services directly related to export or import, including international transport. For marine bunkering, fuel and lubricants delivered into the bunker tanks of a vessel engaged in international merchant navigation qualify as zero-rated export-like supplies provided the operator demonstrates: (i) the vessel departs on a verifiable foreign itinerary, (ii) delivery takes place under Swiss customs custody or within the free-port/transit framework, and (iii) the product is intended exclusively for consumption in international navigation. Supplies to vessels operating solely on domestic Swiss waterways (e.g., Lake Geneva or the Rhine within Swiss territorial limits) remain taxable at the standard 8.1% rate; the burden of proving export eligibility rests on the supplier on pain of retrospective assessment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Swiss Customs Main/E-DEC declaration) evidencing exit from the Swiss customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or international waybill showing foreign destination port and vessel identity
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) signed by the vessel's chief engineer stating the quantity and grade of fuel delivered
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment receipt from the foreign counterparty

No real-time State-operated fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate exists in Switzerland. Invoices must contain mandatory statutory content under Art. 37 LTVA (supplier name/address, UID, invoice date, nature of supply, consideration exclusive of tax, VAT rate and tax amount); eBill and QR-bills are compulsory for electronic supplier payments to Swiss federal and cantonal authorities and are standard in B2B practice, but tax clearance is effected exclusively through periodic VAT returns submitted via the ESTV eService portal. Bunkering operators may issue electronic PDF/XML invoices without pre-authorisation, provided the mandatory content is complete and the VAT number of the customer is stated.

The ESTV and the Federal Customs Administration (Oberzolldirektion) routinely audit export zero-rating and require the supplier to retain the complete documentary bundle for at least 10 years. Missing, false, or late documentary evidence triggers retrospective clawback of the 0% rate with default interest at 4.5% per annum. Intentional tax evasion (Art. 76 LTVA) exposes the taxable person to a fine of up to three times the evaded tax (one time where the evaded tax is below CHF 50,000), confiscation of the goods, and criminal prosecution of responsible officers; negligent under-declaration (Art. 77 LTVA) attracts fines and administrative penalties, and knowingly issuing false export or BDN certificates may lead to a fine of up to CHF 10,000 per document plus loss of the right to deduct input VAT and suspension of the bunkering licence under ISIC 5222.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and OECD standards. No exchange controls, prior approvals, or quantitative restrictions apply to cross-border payments, profit remittances, dividend repatriation, or shareholder loans. The 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividends is imposed at the tax level, not the FX level, and may be reduced or eliminated by a double tax treaty. Bunkering proceeds in USD, EUR, or CHF can be held, transferred, and converted freely without SNB authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency earnings to CHF. Bunker fuel exporters and service providers may retain USD/EUR revenues in Swiss or offshore accounts and freely decide on conversion timing and hedging.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank/Banque nationale suisse/Banca Nazionale Svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Switzerland's FinTech Licence regime (Art. 1b Swiss Banking Act) permits a non-bank fintech to take public sight deposits up to CHF 100 million without a full banking licence. However, this licence is not an EMI passport, and Switzerland is not part of the EU/EEA passporting network. A Swiss-domiciled AG engaged in bunkering will generally require a traditional bank for SWIFT interoperability and for Standby LC issuance; a fintech EMI may be used only for ancillary pre-funded employee expenses or secondary clearing, not as a primary treasury rail.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory rate 8.5% of post-tax profit (approx. 7.83% pre-tax), combined with cantonal and communal profit taxes yielding a typical effective headline rate of 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton; Zug approx. 11.9%–15.1%, Zurich approx. 19.7%, Geneva approx. 21.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax on dividends paid to all shareholders, including non-resident shareholders; domestic Swiss shareholders may receive full refund.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due six months after the balance sheet date; for calendar-year companies the general cantonal deadline is 30 September or 31 October depending on the canton; written extension applications are available for justified cause.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax law does not prescribe a statutory mandatory Local File/Master File, but administrative practice requires contemporaneous TP documentation whenever the Swiss entity's related-party cross-border transaction volume exceeds CHF 5 million in any fiscal year or when a tax adjustment risk is material; a Master File is required where the global group's net turnover exceeds CHF 50 million. The documentation must substantiate arm's length results per OECD TP Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply Gulf-type Economic Substance Regulations, but it imposes a substantive economic activity requirement through Art. 58 LIFD and international treaty jurisprudence: a Swiss AG must have actual offices, qualified employees, real decision-making power, bank accounts, and genuine fuel procurement/refuelling logistics in Switzerland. For bunkering activities, legal title to fuel, cargo ownership, invoicing procedures, safety/labour operations must be performed by the Swiss entity. Failure of economic substance leads to denial of treaty benefits under the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) Principal Purpose Test and permits cantonal/FTA substance auditing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement (Federal and Cantonal Direct Tax)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 174–175 DBG/LIFD, intentional tax evasion triggers a fine equal to up to 100% of the evaded tax plus a procedural fine; in aggravated cases or where the evaded tax exceeds CHF 30,000, the court may impose a mandatory compliance penalty and publish the judgment; unpaid federal and cantonal tax interest accrues at 4.5% annual. In addition, a false or fraudulent electronic tax return subjects the AG to an administrative summary assessment and loss of all loss carry-forward claims for the manipulated year.

Director personal liability: Board members, the CEO and signatories who knowingly sign false tax returns expose themselves to joint and several personal criminal fines; the court may impose imprisonment up to 3 years under Swiss criminal law (Art. 146/251 StGB) for documentary forgery/fraud. Directors are personally liable for resulting unpaid taxes if they fail to file compulsory balance-sheet reports of losses (Art. 754, 725 OR); travel bans may be imposed under fiscal enforcement proceedings.
Offshore Shell Company / Sham Substance in Bunkering

Statutory sanction: The tax administration re-characterises the entity as a sham PE or de facto foreign controlled resident; treaty benefits are denied (MLI PPT), withholding tax at 35% is withheld or clawed back on all profit repatriations; cantonal authorities may order dissolution of the company under Art. 731b OR for non-existence of legal seat and organisation; the company is deemed to lack the necessary charter/settlement place, and fines up to CHF 100,000 may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors' personal criminal liability arises where they knowingly facilitate a sham structure (corporate veil piercing); under Art. 754 OR, the FTA and creditors can sue directors for compensation if the company is unable to pay its tax debts. Directors and shadow directors face travel bans, asset attachment and, in cross-border tax fraud cases, extradition risk; imprisonment from one to five years under Swiss criminal law for forgery, fraud and money laundering.
AML / Money Laundering in Bunker Payments and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG/AMLA) and Criminal Code Art. 305bis, acts of money laundering carry imprisonment of up to three years and/or a fine up to CHF 500,000; aggravated cases (such as participation in a criminal organisation) attract up to five years' imprisonment; commercial-scale violations of UN/Swiss economic embargos under Art. 6 of the Federal Embargo Act may lead to imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to CHF 2 million; the AG can be classified as a non-complying financial intermediary and lose its banking access.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who fail to perform adequate KYC and transaction monitoring may be held personally criminally liable as principals of the money-laundering offence; they can be subject to immediate travel bans, bank account freezes, and confiscation of personal assets; civil liability arises under Art. 41 CO with joint and several indemnification of the company's losses; imprisonment up to five years and permanent disqualification from serving as director of a Swiss AG apply in serious cases.
