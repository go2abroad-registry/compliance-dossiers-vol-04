Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - sole national tax administration for federal income tax, GST/HST, Part XIII withholding, and payroll. Ontario corporate income tax and HST are collected by CRA under the Canada-Ontario tax collection agreement. Digital portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). The Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery registers the limited partnership itself but is not a tax authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the LP under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act via the Ontario Business Registry to obtain the Ontario registration number. Step 2: Apply for a federal Business Number (BN) using CRA Form RC1, indicating the GST/HST account requirement; in the election section, designate the sole general partner or a resident agent as the partnership's GST/HST representative (ETA s. 273(1) election where all partners are non-residents). Step 3: CRA opens the GST/HST account in the LP's own name notwithstanding income-tax pass-through; the effective registration date is the earlier of the first taxable supply made in Canada or the date the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. Step 4: If Ontario employees are hired, also open the CRA payroll account and Ontario Employer Health Tax account. Step 5: File the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually (mandatory electronic filing for partnerships with 11 or more members); no separate Ontario income tax registration is needed because CRA administers Ontario's income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - an Ontario LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes (ITA s. 96): income, gains, and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who bear federal and Ontario income tax in their own hands. The LP itself pays no entity-level income tax. For GST/HST (ETA), however, the partnership is a distinct 'person' that must register and may claim input tax credits. Non-resident partners of an LP carrying on business in Canada are statutorily deemed to carry on business in Canada (ITA s. 253), triggering Canadian Part I filing obligations or Part XIII withholding where applicable.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists; pass-through status arises by operation of law. Available statutory elections and designations include: (1) ETA s. 273(1) - a non-resident registrant may elect, with a resident person's concurrence, to designate that person as its GST/HST representative; (2) ITA s. 249.1(4) - the partnership may elect a non-calendar-year fiscal period where all partners are not calendar-year taxpayers; (3) ITA s. 96(1)(f) - partnership agreement profit/loss allocations are respected if arm's-length and commercially reasonable; (4) Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) - no elective form exists; CRA automatically disallows deductions for dual-deduction or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, so compliance is achieved by evidencing commercial substance and avoiding hybrid mismatch structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold: aggregate worldwide revenues from taxable supplies (other than financial services) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Zero-rated export sales count as taxable supplies for the threshold, although taxed at 0%. Once the threshold is exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies from the first subsequent taxable supply. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold, except that supplies of real property in Canada trigger registration with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Dried goji berries and herbal botanicals qualify for 0% GST/HST as supplies of tangible personal property exported by the vendor under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6. Conditions: (i) the purchaser is a non-resident person; (ii) the vendor ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, or to a common carrier/consignee for export, as soon after the supply as is reasonable; (iii) the vendor retains prescribed documentary evidence under ETA Regulations s. 5, including the supply contract, commercial invoice, transport document, customs export declaration, and written export confirmation from the purchaser where applicable; (iv) the goods must not be reimported into Canada. Input tax credits on Canadian inputs (freight, warehousing, repacking, inspection) are fully recoverable, producing refundable GST/HST positions. Canada does not prescribe a statutory FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is the commercially accepted substantiation benchmark used by CRA.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration receipt (filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System, CES) confirming physical departure of goods from Canada
  • Commercial invoice naming the non-resident buyer, Incoterms, and HS classification for dried goji berries and herbal botanicals (e.g., HS 1211 / HS 1237)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) showing Canadian origin port and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement by the non-resident buyer
  • CFIA Phytosanitary Certificate and country-of-origin certificate where required by the importing jurisdiction
  • Foreign customs entry declaration or signed delivery receipt proving the goods arrived outside Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API authorization, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA promotes voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing but does not require pre-clearance of invoices. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns for registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (fiscal years beginning after 2021) and to T5013 returns for partnerships with 11 or more members. Books and records must be retained for six years to support CRA audit matching; no invoice clearance certificate is required.

CRA may deny zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete, the export was not effected 'as soon as reasonably possible', or the goods are traced back into Canada; the full 13% HST on gross sales is then reassessed with compounded daily interest at CRA's prescribed rate. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or negligence is established. Specific penalties: ETA s. 280(1) - the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax sought to be evaded or rebate overclaimed; ETA s. 323 - directors and general-partner managers are jointly and severally liable for unremitted amounts; ITA s. 163(2) - 50% gross-negligence penalty on partners' understated income from the LP; criminal prosecution for tax evasion is also available. Previously refunded input tax credits and GST/HST rebates are recoverable by CRA as Crown debts with interest, and any clawback is not statute-barred where false statements were made knowingly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no restrictions on conversion of Canadian dollars or on payments and transfers for international transactions. Capital contributions, profit distributions, royalties, interest, trade settlements and investment repatriations may be freely routed through Canadian banks. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: non-resident limited partners' Canadian-source partnership income is subject to Canadian reporting and withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act, but no exchange approval is required. Foreign direct investment in Canada may still require review under the Investment Canada Act; however this does not affect FX convertibility as a treasury matter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may hold receipts in CAD or any foreign currency. There is no surrender requirement to the Bank of Canada. FINTRAC requires reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and banks must conduct sanctions and anti-money-laundering screening on all related transactions. Commercial export documentation for dried goji berries remains subject to Canada Border Services Agency reporting and CFIA food-safety requirements, but this is a customs matter and does not limit retention or conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Department of Finance Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control authority; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed and not replaced.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. A foreign fintech EMI cannot take deposits or carry on banking business in Canada without an OSFI-issued authorization under the Bank Act. Such an entity may operate only as a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) for payment facilitation or currency exchange. It is viable as a supplementary cross-border payment rail, not as the Ontario LP's primary treasury or capital escrow account. The LP should maintain its core accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank and use regulated EMIs only where the EMI's FINTRAC registration, sanctions screening, data protection, and settlement audit trail are contractually documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections to the Ontario LP should be routed using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent telegraphic transfer to the LP's account at a Tier 1 Canadian Schedule I bank. The receiving bank will conduct sanctions, AML and source-of-funds verification; no Bank of Canada exchange approval is required. The payment instruction should include the LP's exact legal name, Ontario LP registration number, and a purpose code or reference in SWIFT Field 70 stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'LPCAP' together with the capital call notice reference. The bank will require the executed subscription agreement, general partner resolution, source-of-wealth documents and, where applicable, proof of investment review clearance under the Investment Canada Act. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% combined federal–Ontario general corporate income tax rate (federal 15.0% + Ontario 10.0%). A Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; tax is borne by partners: Canadian-resident corporate partners pay the 25.0% combined general rate (12.2% combined on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income where the partner is a Canadian-controlled private corporation and the LP is not a 'specified partnership'), and individual partners pay Ontario personal income tax up to the 53.53% top marginal rate (including surcharge). Non-resident corporate partners carrying on business through the LP are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source active income at 25.0%, subject to treaty relief.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% Part XIII withholding tax (ITA s.212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. For the LP structure, distributions of Canadian-source business income to non-resident partners are taxable under Part I (at 25.0% corporate rate or treaty-exempt rate) rather than Part XIII; however, distributions of portfolio income (interest, dividends, royalties) flowing through the LP to non-resident partners are subject to the Part XIII 25% withholding regime. If the non-resident partner fails to file the NR6 undertaking, the withholding agent must remit 25% of the cash distribution as a safeguard.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 annual aggregate of controlled cross-border transactions between the Ontario LP and non-resident affiliated parties (per CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and ITA s.247); contemporaneous documentation must exist before the tax return filing date to sustain the 'reasonable efforts' defence against the 10% section 247(3) transfer-pricing penalty, which is triggered when the net transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. Country-by-country reporting obligations commence at CAD 750,000,000 global consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada imposes no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to offshore financial centres; however, genuine economic substance in Ontario is mandatory under the CRA's business-purpose doctrine, ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test. The LP must demonstrate operational reality through Ontario warehousing, physical inventory ownership, import documentation, purchase orders, shipping records, quality-control personnel, and strategic decision-making. A pure flow-through booking arrangement for dried goji berry trading with no Ontario-based commercial activity is rebuttably presumed abusive and will be re-characterized or denied treaty benefits entirely. Thin-capitalization rules further restrict debt from specified non-resident shareholders to a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio, with excess interest disallowed.
