Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal income, GST/HST and excise duty administration: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) with the Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Quebec sales tax (QST) administration: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) in coordination with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec for the enterprise number (NEQ); customs and export reporting: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES); provincial liquor control for exporting spirits from Quebec: Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux (RACJ) and, where relevant, Société des alcools du Québec (SAQ) permit controls.

E-government filing portal

For the Quebec limited partnership: (1) register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (declaration of immatriculation) to obtain the NEQ; (2) register the federal Business Number (BN) with CRA Business Registration Online, selecting RP (GST/HST), RC (payroll if employees exist) and RD (Excise Duty framework under the Excise Act, 2001); (3) register for QST at Revenu Québec through the enterprise registration module; GST/QST registration is mandatory once the LP's taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four trailing consecutive calendar quarters, or earlier when the LP imports commercial goods into Quebec or otherwise carries on a Quebec enterprise; (4) obtain the RACJ exporter's liquor permit and, when duty deferral is desired, an excise warehouse licence or an excise-drawback exporter designation from CRA Excise Duty; (5) complete CBSA exporter registration linked to the BN for electronic export declarations. The non-resident Chinese partners need not be listed in the BN file, but the general partner acts as the LP's administrative representative and must identify itself and the LP to CRA and Revenu Québec.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96 and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 599, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not an income taxpayer; each partner pays tax on its distributive share of the LP's Quebec business income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96 and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 599, the limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not an income taxpayer; each partner pays tax on its distributive share of the LP's Quebec business income. Canadian-resident partners pay tax on worldwide income; non-resident (Chinese) partners are liable for Canadian Part I tax only on Canadian-source partnership income (Income Tax Act s. 2(3), s. 115 and s. 212(13.1) for amounts flowing out of the partnership), with parallel Quebec rules. For GST/QST the LP itself is a separate 'person' and the registrant, entitled to claim input tax credits on purchases of Baijiu inventory, warehousing, freight and marketing services. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable in Quebec for export distribution; export relief is achieved through statutory zero-rating and excise drawback rather than through any geographic zone exemption. Entity_Level_Taxation: not applicable at the LP level for income tax purposes, but the LP is the entity-level registrant for GST, QST and excise purposes.

Available election forms

Canada and Quebec do not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; partnership transparency is mandatory. Available elections and designations relevant to this LP include: (i) voluntary GST/QST registration election for a small supplier under Excise Tax Act s. 240(3) and QSTA art. 259, permitting early input tax credit recovery before crossing the C$30,000 threshold; (ii) rollover election under Income Tax Act s. 97(2) and Taxation Act (Quebec) s. 729 on the transfer of Baijiu inventory, warehouse leases and distribution contracts into the LP at tax cost; (iii) designation of the general partner as the tax-matters partner so that the federal BN and QST number are consolidated in a single filing profile; (iv) for excise duty purposes, election to operate as a licensed excise warehouse user or to claim monthly duty drawback on exported spirits instead of paying duty upon import; (v) with Chinese partners present, the LP must maintain contemporaneous documentation that no hybrid-mismatch double deduction arises (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4 and 94.2, and the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation rules) and may elect to compute Part XIII non-resident withholding on the LP's net allocable amounts payable to non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or across the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (GST: Excise Tax Act s. 148; QST: QSTA art. 259). Mandatory earlier registration applies to any non-resident importing commercial goods (including Baijiu) into Quebec or carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment, warehouse, or agent. The voluntary small-supplier registration threshold is likewise C$30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registrant's supply of Baijiu made in Quebec to a non-resident buyer is zero-rated for GST and QST under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V (supply of goods that are exported) and the parallel QST zero-rating, provided that: (i) physical export occurs as soon as possible after the supply and, in administrative practice, within 60 days of transfer of possession; (ii) the LP retains shipping, customs, and payment evidence establishing that the goods left Canada and entered the named foreign market; (iii) title and risk of loss pass to the foreign buyer at or before export; (iv) for Baijiu previously imported from China, the import entry is matched to the export entry and the goods are not diverted to Quebec consumption; (v) federal spirits excise duty is relieved by exporting from a licensed excise warehouse (duty-unpaid movement) or by a drawback claim under the Excise Act, 2001, and the RACJ export permit declares the withdrawal so that no Quebec beverage markup applies; (vi) incidental logistics and freight services supplied to the non-resident buyer in respect of the exported goods are separately zero-rated only if supplied directly to the non-resident and performed in respect of the exported Baijiu.

  • CBSA B13 Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES), reflecting the LP's Business Number, destination port, HS code 2208.90 (Chinese Baijiu), quantity, alcoholic strength, and invoice value
  • Original or certified electronic Bill of Lading (ocean/air/rail or combined transport bill) proving physical movement of the container from Canada to the named foreign destination with the Baijiu consignee identified in the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of settlement (SWIFT MT103 trace or letter-of-credit negotiation record) evidencing receipt of export proceeds by the LP within 180 days of the export date

Canada does not mandate real-time tax-authority clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing. Obligations are record-based: every registrant must issue a compliant invoice stating the supplier's BN, the LP's legal name, date, description and quantity of Baijiu, unit price, GST and QST indicated separately or an explicit zero-rated/export notation, and must retain all electronic records for six years (Excise Tax Act s. 286(2)). Revenu Québec requires electronic filing and payment for most QST registrants and accepts PEPPOL UBL 2.1 e-invoices. Mandatory digital transmission applies to customs and trade data: CBSA eManifest/PARS for imports and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) for the LP's export declarations, which constitute the legally authoritative outward clearance records. No tax-authority pre-approval of invoices is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec jointly audit export zero-rating and excise drawback files; the burden of proof is on the LP to produce the complete documentation bundle with matching inventory movement within the six-year record-retention window. A confirmed export substantiation failure triggers reversal of the zero-rating, immediate reassessment of 5% GST plus 9.975% QST on the export invoice value, reassessment of federal spirits excise duty with interest, and denial or recapture of related input tax credits and duty drawback. Civil penalties include a 25% gross-negligence penalty on federal tax (Excise Tax Act s. 285(1)), a 50% penalty for false statements or omissions made with intent (s. 280), QST penalties under QSTA arts. 1012, 1015 and 1016 (25%-50% tiers), and a 50% anti-avoidance penalty under the Excise Act, 2001 for fabricated export documentation. Criminal exposure includes fines up to C$50,000 and imprisonment for excise offences (Excise Act, 2001 s. 218), and liability under Criminal Code s. 380(1) fraud where export claims are materially false. The general partner of the Quebec LP bears unlimited joint and several liability for all such clawbacks; normal four-year reassessment limits do not apply where misrepresentation or fraud is established; SAQ/RACJ export permits are suspended immediately upon diversion of goods to the domestic Quebec market, and diverted goods are forfeited with duty and taxes payable as if domestically released.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible on both current and capital account under IMF Article VIII. There is no Canadian foreign-exchange control regime, no prior exchange-control approval for capital injection, and no administrative limit on profit remittance or capital repatriation. Remittance of distributable profits from a Quebec Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is governed by the limited partnership agreement, solvency requirements, Part XIII non-resident withholding tax rules, and FATCA/CRS documentation obligations. Banks will impose independent AML/EDD review on all high-value wires but no exchange-control permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. Exporters may retain baijiu export receipts in foreign currency, hold them in foreign-currency bank accounts, or convert them to Canadian dollars at commercial rates without a mandatory timeline or conversion ratio. All cross-border currency or monetary instruments of C$10,000 or more must be reported to CBSA/FINTRAC, and international wire instructions are subject to FINTRAC transaction-reporting obligations when filing thresholds are met; exporters must also retain trade documentation under customs and income-tax record-keeping rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no centralized exchange-control ministry; cross-border payment review is distributed among these federal authorities, with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administering physical cross-border currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium. Foreign digital EMIs may function as supplementary payment collection and low-value disbursement rails in Canada only if they are registered with FINTRAC as money service businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and comply with applicable Quebec money-services business requirements. They cannot lawfully take Canadian deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, or act as the primary treasury counterparty for a capital-escrow structure. EMIs may be acceptable for sub-C$100,000 open-account trade disbursements where full transaction transparency exists, but the Quebec LP must maintain a Schedule I bank account as the principal liquidity concentration point.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Canadian-dollar legs clear through Lynx, the high-value payment system, while U.S.-dollar legs may clear through the bank's New York correspondent. They cannot lawfully take Canadian deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, or act as the primary treasury counterparty for a capital-escrow structure. EMIs may be acceptable for sub-C$100,000 open-account trade disbursements where full transaction transparency exists, but the Quebec LP must maintain a Schedule I bank account as the principal liquidity concentration point.
