Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, accessible via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax rates and notices, though CRA administers the provincial component of the Ontario HST.

E-government filing portal

For the CA_CORP federal/provincial business corporation, the statutory tax identifier is the CRA Business Number (BN). Foreign-controlled Canadian corporations apply by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) together with certified articles of incorporation, corporate by-laws, registered office details, and pre-registration turnover declarations. CRA issues a 9-digit BN and, once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold or registration is elected, opens a dedicated GST/HST account. The same BN is used as the income tax account and Ontario provincial tax account. Foreign entity applicants must provide a Canadian representative address and identify a resident agent if CRA requires one for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (taxable Canadian corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act; resident in Canada by virtue of incorporation; taxable on worldwide income; not eligible for flow-through treatment; separate taxpayer from shareholders). — For the CA_CORP federal/provincial business corporation, the statutory tax identifier is the CRA Business Number (BN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (taxable Canadian corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act; resident in Canada by virtue of incorporation; taxable on worldwide income; not eligible for flow-through treatment; separate taxpayer from shareholders).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or equivalent recharacterization mechanism exists under federal or Ontario law. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (i) subsection 216 Income Tax Act election for non-residents earning Canadian real property income; (ii) GST/HST section 156 Excise Tax Act joint election for closely related corporations to simplify supplies; (iii) capital dividend account election under subsection 83(2) of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) associated corporation designations for Canadian-controlled private corporation allocation. Hybrid mismatch rules and procedural elections under the Income Tax Act must be assessed under the OECD MLI and the Canada Tax Court jurisprudence; no blanket election can override the entity-level taxation classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies made by the entity in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. For a port terminal stevedoring entity, registration is generally required from the first day of the calendar month following the month in which the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, supplies of goods exported from Canada are eligible for 0% GST/HST if the goods are sold for export and physically shipped to a destination outside Canada. Services, including offshore and port services, may be zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident person and not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Stevedoring and terminal services physically rendered on bulk cargo within a Canadian port are generally taxable at 13% HST unless the operator can establish the service is part of a qualifying zero-rated supply of international transportation, the recipient is a non-resident, and the bulk cargo is in transit to a foreign port with title, custody, and export documentation clearly aligned. A valid zero-rating claim requires a written supply contract, detailed descriptions of the service, cargo destination, non-resident legal identity, and contemporaneous export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / Automated Export Declaration) evidencing commodity, weight, value, port of loading, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic ocean/through bill of lading) covering the bulk cargo movement from the Canadian port to the overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT credit, or validated foreign exchange report confirming Canadian-dollar repatriation of export proceeds within the prescribed period

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, government clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are legally valid if they contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and GST/HST Regulations. Registrants must file GST/HST returns via CRA's electronic GST/HST NETFILE or EDI services, remit through CRA-approved electronic payment systems, and retain all source records, including customs documentation, for at least six years.

CRA may reassess and claw back improperly claimed zero-rating within the four-year normal assessment period, and without limitation where misrepresentation, concealment, or fraud is shown. If export zero-rating is denied, the taxpayer is liable for the unpaid 13% HST, substantial statutory interest, and, in the case of knowingly false statements or omissions, a gross negligence penalty under section 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act (commonly 50% of the tax evaded). Criminal prosecution for tax evasion is also available where the facts establish fraudulent export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has no general foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittance beyond standard corporate solvency and dividend declaration tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act and Ontario Business Corporations Act, and no requirement for central bank approval for cross-border payments. Non-resident investors may repatriate capital and dividends freely, subject to applicable tax withholding under the Income Tax Act and treaty relief. Currency convertibility risk is negligible for operational and trade-related flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in USD or other currencies, or converted at market rates through authorized financial intermediaries. All cross-border money remittances and wire transfers of CAD $10,000 or more are reportable to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, which imposes record-keeping and client identification duties but no surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada) with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no separate foreign exchange control ministry exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Foreign digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and non-bank cross-border payment providers do not possess banking powers in Canada and generally cannot accept deposits unless licensed as a bank or regulated under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Physical presence in Canada is required to establish a Canadian payment account relationship. Offshore EMIs may be used only as supplementary treasury overlay for multi-currency receivables/payables, provided they are not used for direct CAD deposit-taking or to evade Canadian anti-money laundering obligations. Primary operating and escrow liquidity must remain with Schedule I chartered banks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Offshore or multi-tier holding structure with ultimate beneficial owners resident in a non-FATF/EU-Quivalent jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Corporate organizational chart, certificates of corporate status for each entity, register of directors and shareholders, passport copies with notarization, source of wealth statements, audited financial statements or opening balance sheet, and tax clearance certificates from Canada Revenue Agency or origin country tax authority.
Port/stevedoring activity involving vessels, cargo, or counterparties that may attract sanctions screening flags

Root cause: Port/stevedoring activity involving vessels, cargo, or counterparties that may attract sanctions screening flags

Defense evidence: Valid stevedoring license or port authority terminal agreement, client contracts showing non-sanctioned cargo origin/destination, International Maritime Organization and vessel registration details, good standing certificates, sanctions screening policy, and a transaction-level compliance procedure aligned with Global Affairs Canada, OSFI, and FINTRAC guidance.
High-value or frequent cross-border wires to trade suppliers without established trade-cycle documentation

Root cause: High-value or frequent cross-border wires to trade suppliers without established trade-cycle documentation

Defense evidence: Signed supplier contracts, purchase orders, bills of lading, cargo manifests, port handling tickets, proforma invoices, and an annual expected revenue/trade-flow forecast identifying the recipient counterparties and the commercial rationale for each wire class.
Capital injection from a foreign parent ref-nominal or mismatch of entity legal name with operating trade name

Root cause: Capital injection from a foreign parent ref-nominal or mismatch of entity legal name with operating trade name

Defense evidence: Consolidated group financial statements, intra-group loan or equity subscription agreement, board resolutions authorizing the capital injection, the Ontario/Canada business registry excerpt confirming the exact legal name, and corporate seal / signing certificate evidencing binding corporate action.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders must be routed through Canadian Schedule I banks via SWIFT MT103 wire or local wire credit using Canadian clearing rails. The remitter must use the exact legal entity name of the Canadian corporation and the transaction description should clearly identify the purpose: 'Equity capital injection under shareholder resolution' or 'Shareholder loan – operational funding for stevedoring terminal'. CAD-denominated domestic funds settle through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the new Real-Time Rail (RTR). For CAD wires from foreign banks, a CAD correspondent account at a Canadian Schedule I bank is necessary. FINTRAC reporting applies for transfers exceeding CAD $10,000. The bank will require a source of funds declaration and supporting corporate documents; funds must be derived from legal banking or identifiable treasury sources. There is no specific CAD purpose code regime; the wire narrative must be a clear and defensible legal purpose statement.Outbound trade payments for terminal operations, subcontractor services, bunker fuel, and port equipment are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for international USD/foreign-currency payments, or domestic CAD wires via LVTS and RTR. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) and documentary collections are permissible for structured equipment purchases. Payments to port authorities and municipality-required charges are executed through Canadian wire rails or automated clearing settlement (payments.ca). Trade settlement documentation, including invoices, contracts, bills of lading, and customs entries, must be retained to substantiate each disbursement.
