Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); national portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account (MyBA); customs: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM); Ontario: Ministry of Finance (Ontario corporate tax administered by CRA on Ontario's behalf) and Ministry of Government and Consumer Services for extra-provincial registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated NVOCC must (1) apply for extra-provincial registration in Ontario under the Corporations Information Act or obtain a federal certificate under the Canada Business Corporations Act; (2) submit Form RC1FA (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts for Non-Resident) or use CRA Business Registration Online to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the RT (GST/HST) program account and RM (Importer/Exporter) customs program account; (3) provide certified corporate certificates, registered office address and directors, and a Canadian agent/representative; (4) if the entity is a Canadian-resident corporation, register for corporate income tax (RC account) and Ontario provincial corporate tax through the CRA T2 interface. The CRA issues the BN and program accounts within 2 to 15 business days; a non-resident services branch carrying on business in Canada will also need payroll (RP) and import/export (RM) accounts, and an advance income tax ruling is recommended where non-resident status is unclear.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian corporation is taxed at the entity level under ITA Part I, and a foreign disregarded entity is classified by the CRA according to its legal form and treaty context. Relevant elections and mitigations for an NVOCC group: (a) ETA s. 156 election for closely related Canadian corporations to treat intercompany taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (b) ETA s. 217(2) separate branch or division election permitting a non-resident registrant to file and deduct GST/HST on a branch-by-branch basis; (c) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident corporation electing to pay Canadian tax on Canadian-source property income in lieu of Part XIII withholding; (d) CRA administrative recognition of single-entity treatment for GST/HST between head office and Canadian branches; (e) hybrid-mismatch constraints: ITA ss. 12.7(3), 18.5, 26 and 94B and ETA s. 272.1 may disallow deductions, deny input tax credits or impose withholding on payments routed through foreign fiscally transparent entities or hybrid branches; (f) advance income tax rulings available from the CRA to confirm entity classification and permanent establishment exposure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: mandatory registration once worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export services) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident NVOCC carrying on business in Canada through an Ontario agent or place of business, soliciting orders in Canada, or importing goods under an RM account is required to register for GST/HST; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits and to support zero-rating of export ocean freight services. A business that makes only zero-rated supplies is not required to charge tax, but registration is commercially necessary to issue valid export invoices and recover Canadian input tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An NVOCC ocean freight forwarder may apply 0% GST/HST under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (i) Part V s. 7 zero-rates ocean freight transportation of goods from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada and vice versa; (ii) Part V s. 7.1 zero-rates the arranging-for of such international freight transport (agency and brokerage services); (iii) Part V s. 6 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and is outside Canada, provided the service does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (iv) Part V s. 1 zero-rates exported goods delivered to a common carrier for export. The zero-rating attaches to the international ocean transport element; separately invoiced domestic inland trucking, warehousing, terminal handling and documentation services must either qualify as part of a continuous export shipment contract or be treated as taxable at 13% HST. The NVOCC must maintain an unbroken documentary chain and may claim input tax credits on Canadian expenses, generating periodic refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Automated Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System filing for outbound ocean goods)
  • Bill of Lading (original, electronic or certified copy of the ocean carrier Master Bill and the NVOCC House Bill of Lading showing the foreign port of discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation, SWIFT MT103 or FX settlement voucher evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident principal)

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction clearance, or digital stamping requirement. The CRA accepts electronic invoices (EDI, PDF, PEPPOL format) if the invoice contains the supplier's Business Number and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, vendor and recipient identification, consideration, and applicable tax rate. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) for all corporate registrants, with limited exceptions, and to CBSA export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) and the ACI eManifest regime for ocean cargo; freight forwarders must transmit cargo control and export declaration data electronically before departure, and failure to do so prevents loading and triggers penalties.

CRA and CBSA treat NVOCC export zero-rating as a high-risk audit area. On audit failure the CRA may reassess the full export billings at 13% HST with compound daily interest (ETA s. 280.2), deny the corresponding input tax credits, and claw back refunds. Normal reassessment is limited to four years (ITA s. 152(4), ETA s. 298), but no limitation period applies for misrepresentation, wilful default, carelessness or fraud. Penalties include: (i) ETA s. 285 false statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax that would have been payable (or refund claimed); (ii) ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of any federal income tax understated; (iii) CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per shipment for false or late export declarations, in addition to Customs Act s. 109.1 penalties of up to CAD 2,500 per false statement; (iv) criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 and Customs Act ss. 153 to 155 for fraudulent export documentation; (v) registration revocation and prohibition on voluntary registration for repeat defaults. The audit standard requires the NVOCC to produce the entire export package described above for every shipment; missing bills of lading, export declarations, foreign port arrival evidence or remittance records will trigger retrospective clawback of zero-rating and ITCs for the full assessment window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There is no central-bank authorization, license, or preclearance requirement for acquiring, holding, or repatriating foreign currency. A Canadian NVOCC / ocean freight forwarding corporation may invoice in USD or other currencies, maintain foreign-currency operating accounts, and remit dividends, branch profits, interest, royalties, and management fees to non-residents, subject to Part XIII withholding tax and applicable income tax treaty reductions. There are no profit-remittance registration hurdles or forced conversion mechanisms; however, foreign-currency gains must be measured and reported in Canadian dollars under the Income Tax Act, and a functional-currency election requires CRA approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or designated-account rules requiring surrender of export proceeds in Canada. Export and transportation receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or any freely usable currency. Nevertheless, all accounting records and annual tax filings must be maintained in Canadian dollars unless a valid functional-currency election is in force. Cross-border transfers of $10,000 CAD or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting, with full audit trail and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the central monetary authority, but Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration. Exchange control and cross-border payments oversight are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Canada Border Services Agency, reflecting Canada's open capital account under IMF Article VIII.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional - Medium. Offshore digital EMI / neobank platforms generally do not hold Canadian banking licenses and are not members of Payments Canada; therefore they cannot provide domestic Canadian clearing accounts or full deposit insurance. Their lawful utility is limited to supplemental cross-border payment execution, treasury visibility, or pre-funded vendor disbursement tools, subject to FINTRAC MSB registration if they operate in Canada. A principal operating account and capital escrow relationship must remain at a Schedule I Canadian bank to access SWIFT, correspondent banking, and trade-finance rails with acceptable regulatory certainty.
