Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), administered by the Ministère de l’Emploi et de la Solidarité sociale

Governing statute

A Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is constituted under the Civil Code of Québec, including articles 2186 and 2236–2249. Registration and disclosure are governed by the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, including its registration requirements. This is partnership formation, not incorporation of a corporation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d’immatriculation (Declaration of Registration) filed in the Québec Enterprise Register

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 55 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital or paid-up-capital escrow is prescribed for a Quebec limited partnership. The partnership agreement should specify each partner’s contribution. The general partner manages the partnership; limited partners should not take part in its administration toward third parties, subject to the Civil Code rules governing their liability.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific fire, structural-load, or industrial workplace clearance ordinarily applies to an office-only operation. The premises must satisfy applicable municipal building and fire-safety requirements, and the employer must comply with Quebec occupational health and safety duties. Confirm any occupancy, fire, or accessibility requirements with the municipality and building owner.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO); Revenu Québec – Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) and Revenu Québec Online Services

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or investing in a Quebec limited partnership must register the partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). For federal tax purposes, the partnership obtains a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. The general partner (resident or non-resident) applies on behalf of the limited partnership. If the LP makes taxable supplies in excess of CAD 30,000, it must register for GST/HST. Quebec requires separate registration for QST through Revenu Québec. Non-resident general partners may need to provide evidence of Canadian representative status or power of attorney where required. The LP must file annual information returns (T5013), and Quebec requires an equivalent Relevé 15 or annual provincial filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing or investing in a Quebec limited partnership must register the partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). For federal tax purposes, the partnership obtains a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. The general partner (resident or non-resident) applies on behalf of the limited partnership. If the LP makes taxable supplies in excess of CAD 30,000, it must register for GST/HST. Quebec requires separate registration for QST through Revenu Québec. Non-resident general partners may need to provide evidence of Canadian representative status or power of attorney where required. The LP must file annual information returns (T5013), and Quebec requires an equivalent Relevé 15 or annual provincial filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian limited partnerships are statutorily classified as flow-through entities for income tax purposes; no check-the-box election exists. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (notably section 18.4 and related provisions) apply to prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches where a foreign jurisdiction treats the entity differently. Partnerships may file joint elections under subsection 97(2) of the Income Tax Act for tax-deferred transfers of property to the partnership and may adopt a non-calendar taxation year under subsection 96(3) with CRA approval. Quebec follows the federal classification for QST and provincial income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST; CAD 30,000 for QST in Quebec. Non-residents with no permanent establishment in Canada may have modified registration obligations, but a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must generally register once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI (Excise Tax Act). Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated if the service is performed wholly or mainly outside Canada, or relates directly to exported goods. Executive search and headhunting services provided to non-resident clients may qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not in connection with real property located in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply. The supplier must maintain sufficient documentary evidence of the non-resident status and of the place of performance or benefit.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CERS electronic filing)
  • Commercial invoice for goods or services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for goods)
  • Proof of export control data (PARS/ACI release documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • Retainer agreement / engagement letter identifying non-resident client and jurisdiction
  • GST/HST zero-rating support documentation (non-resident status evidence, place-of-supply substantiation)

Federal GST/HST does not currently mandate real-time electronic clearance or government-approved digital stamping. Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain prescribed information under section 3 of the Excise Tax Act. Quebec has implemented a mandatory electronic invoicing and e-reporting regime for QST registrants, phased in from 2023 to 2025, requiring B2B invoices to be transmitted through a software provider certified by Revenu Québec. No continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time clearance exists federally or provincially.

Under the Excise Tax Act, an exporter that zero-rates without proper substantiation may be assessed GST/HST, interest and penalties. Penalties include a 5% failure-to-remit penalty plus 1% per month (uncapped), gross negligence penalties of 50% of the amount, and a 100% penalty for false statements or omissions. Criminal prosecution is possible for wilful evasion. The CRA can reassess within four years of the date of assessment; longer reassessment periods apply in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Revenu Québec may apply similar QST clawbacks and penalties, including interest and fines. Retrospective clawbacks may also include denial of input tax credits and recovery of zero-rated refunds previously paid. Executive search and headhunting services are scrutinized for evidence that the service was actually rendered to a non-resident and enjoyed outside Canada; insufficient documentation can lead to domestic supply characterization and full tax recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without government approval or repatriation licensing. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible, floating currency. For a Quebec Limited Partnership engaged in executive search, inbound retainer payments from foreign clients and outbound partnership distributions are unrestricted. Repatriation of profits to non-resident limited partners is not subject to exchange control, but the Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax applies to specified Canadian-source payments (management fees, interest, royalties, and certain partnership allocations), and non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxed under section 115 via CRA and Revenu Québec filings. Practical hurdles are limited to tax compliance, CRS/FATCA reporting, and the bank's own anti-money-laundering (AML) due diligence rather than FX licensing. No prior central-bank or OSFI approval is required for foreign direct investment into the LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation of foreign-currency export proceeds. The CA_LP executive-search enterprise may retain USD, EUR, or GBP denominated retainers indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts with Canadian Schedule I banks, or convert to CAD at negotiated market spreads. The only compliance requirements are disclosure-based: FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution for single international electronic fund transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more, the LP's T5013 Partnership Information Return allocation of Canadian-source business income to partners, and FATCA/CRS beneficial-ownership reporting for non-resident partners.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential banking supervision exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and corporate registry administration by the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority analogous to SAFE; FX administration is market-based and governed by the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium, slated as a supplementary settlement rail, not a primary treasury repository. Wise Canada Inc. (Wise B2B) is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and is a viable corridor for low-value cross-border retainer disbursements and subcontractor payments (USD, EUR, GBP, SEK, and 40+ currencies). Legal constraints: (i) Wise is not a Schedule I/Schedule II chartered bank and does not provide CDIC deposit insurance; (ii) the LP must maintain its primary capital escrow account and legal corporate account at an OSFI-supervised bank because Quebec retainer-escrow and partnership capital obligations require a regulated financial institution; (iii) Wise's platform may cap high-value transfers and requires a whitelisted counterparty list to satisfy the LP's internal treasury controls; (iv) all Wise-originated or Wise-received transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting, so the LP must preserve underlying engagement documentation; and (v) Wise cannot provide commercial letters of credit, performance guarantees, or credit facilities. Therefore, use Wise B2B exclusively for operational expense disbursements and small sub-retainer payments, while all client retainers, capital injections, and profit distributions flow through the primary chartered-bank accounts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Partnership ownership opacity: LP structure with a corporate general partner plus non-resident limited partners, potentially obscuring ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO).

Root cause: Partnership ownership opacity: LP structure with a corporate general partner plus non-resident limited partners, potentially obscuring ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO).

Defense evidence: (i) REQ certificate of registration (attestation d'immatriculation) with NEQ number; (ii) notarized partnership agreement (acte de société) and, where applicable, a trust declaration; (iii) full UBO schedule listing every partner with 25% or greater interest and all directors of the corporate general partner; (iv) certified corporate registry extracts of the GP from its home jurisdiction; (v) organizational chart certified by the GP's corporate secretary; and (vi) current and historical bank reference letters from the partners' primary financial institutions.
Non-resident partner jurisdiction risk: inbounds from or partner tax residency in FATF-monitored or higher-risk jurisdictions, or transfers from a jurisdiction exhibiting sanctions exposure.

Root cause: Non-resident partner jurisdiction risk: inbounds from or partner tax residency in FATF-monitored or higher-risk jurisdictions, or transfers from a jurisdiction exhibiting sanctions exposure.

Retainer-based fee red flag: the executive-search model receives large placement fees without physical goods, raising suspicion of commission laundering or undisclosed third-party beneficiaries.

P-16.1) or a written exemption opinion from Quebec counsel for C-suite executive-search-only mandates; and (vi) a no-commingling attestation for any prepaid retainer balances.
Fintech rail exposure: use of Wise B2B or similar EMIs for cross-border disbursements may be perceived as unregulated third-party settlement and a control failure.

Root cause: Fintech rail exposure: use of Wise B2B or similar EMIs for cross-border disbursements may be perceived as unregulated third-party settlement and a control failure.

Defense evidence: (i) Corporate resolution of the general partner expressly authorizing Wise B2B as a designated disbursement rail and naming the authorized treasury signatories; (ii) treasury policy limiting Wise to per-transaction caps and counterparty whitelisting; (iii) end-to-end reconciliation reports matching each Wise transfer to an underlying client engagement or subcontractor agreement; (iv) FINTRAC MSB verification evidence for the Wise entity; and (v) quarterly A/B clearing-rail audit comparing Wise versus SWIFT MT103 charges to demonstrate prudent treasury management.
Escrow-like prepaid retainers: clients may prepay large search retainers, creating trust-account concerns and potential liability for misuse of client funds.

Root cause: Escrow-like prepaid retainers: clients may prepay large search retainers, creating trust-account concerns and potential liability for misuse of client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol for the initial and ongoing capital contributions from non-resident partners: the remitting partner originates an international wire via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's beneficiary account at a Schedule I bank in Quebec. Mandatory fields: (i) beneficiary name must exactly match the REQ-registered partnership name and LP number; (ii) field 70 (Remittance Information) must state CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_LP - [partnership NEQ or REQ registration number]; (iii) beneficiary bank account must be the dedicated Capital Escrow Account with the account title General Partner c/o [LP name]; and (iv) the remitter must provide to its bank the LP's registered address in Quebec and the GP's full identification. For banks requiring a purpose or reason code, use EQUITY CAPITAL / PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION rather than trade-related codes. Documentary packet to be provided to the receiving branch: executed capital-contribution resolution, updated partner ledger, source-of-funds statements for amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, and a signed indemnity confirming no Part XIII withholding applies to capital contributions. After credit, the LP must record the receipt in its capital schedule and reflect the allocation in the annual T5013 partnership return. Foreign currencies are accepted only in USD, EUR, or GBP; CAD conversion is executed at the bank's commercial rate with an FX conversion invoice for FATCA/CRS transparency.Given the ISIC 7830 executive-search scope, outbound clearing rails are optimized for services, retainer refunds, and subcontractor fees rather than merchandise trade. Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for international payments to non-resident subcontractor headhunters, partner agencies, and independent consultants, using SWIFT field 70 with the underlying invoice and placement-reference number to support TRC (travel rules compliance). Domestic CAD and USD payments between Canadian participants use the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the bank's EFT batch, with same-day settlement for wire-class disbursements above CAD 25,000. Secondary rail: Wise B2B for low-value (under CAD 15,000) disbursements in non-USD corridors such as EUR, GBP, SEK, and INR, with a monthly reconciliation feed feeding the partnership's treasury ledger. Commercial letters of credit are not applicable to headhunting services because no physical goods or title documents exist; however, a standby letter of credit (SBLC) may be requested by blue-chip client programs as a performance guarantee and can be issued against the LP's operating-balance collateral at National Bank or RBC. Retainer-refund liabilities and candidate fee rebates are remitted via the same MT103/LVTS rails with memo codes identifying the reversal and the original invoice.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts (plain-vanilla FX forwards) with a Schedule I bank under a standard ISDA Master Agreement or a bilateral bank forward confirm; maturities aligned to the LP's retainer-projection cycle (30, 60, or 90 days).Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) for hedging recurring multi-quarter retainer flows above CAD 500,000 notional, executed with a Schedule I bank under ISDA/CSA collateral arrangements.Over-the-counter CAD/USD plain-vanilla options (call and put) for selective hedging where the LP has a contingent placement pipeline but uncertain closing timing; premium paid in cash, no leverage.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options listed on the Montreal Exchange (TMX Group), subject to the LP's brokerage mandate and regulated derivatives documentation, providing transparent margin-based hedging without ISDA.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally unnecessary and permissible only where a counterparty bank demonstrably cannot settle CAD deliverable; in practice, because CAD is fully convertible, NDFs are disfavored and may trigger enhanced internal-hedging-policy review; if used, they must be executed through a Canadian-authorized derivatives dealer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5% = combined 26.5% (general corporate rate, non-resident-owned corporations may be subject only to federal rate under Part XIV).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) for dividends paid to a non-resident recipient; for a limited partnership, cash distributions of retained income retain their character as partnership income and are not dividends — withholding on the income allocation to a non-resident partner is typically assessed on the allocable amount under Part XIII at 25% or at a reduced treaty rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

If the partnership fiscal period ends on December 31, the return must be filed no later than March 31 of the immediately following year; if the fiscal period ends on any other date, the return is due within five months after the end of that fiscal period.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than March 31 following the partnership's fiscal year-end for calendar-year fiscal periods; for non-calendar year-ends, within five months after the fiscal period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: $1,000,000 CAD (threshold for filing Form T106 – Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents; consolidated group revenue of €750 million triggers Country-by-Country Reporting).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf style; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions are applied to disregard transactions lacking genuine economic substance. A limited partnership must demonstrate real office presence, management, business purpose, and sufficient personnel in Quebec to defend its corporate residency and treaty eligibility, especially where the executive search platform generates significant cross-border revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax evaded under s. 163(2); criminal conviction under s. 239(1) punishable by a fine of not less than 50% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment; CRA may reassess beyond the normal three-year period in cases of misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP can be held jointly and severally liable for the partnership's unremitted taxes, interest, and penalties under the Income Tax Act s. 227.1 if they directed or allowed the fraudulent filing; travel bans, asset seizures, and personal bankruptcy orders may follow a criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA application of GAAR (s. 245) to deny treaty benefits, recharacterize income as taxable in Quebec, impose interest and penalties, and recast transactions at fair market value; potentially a 25% avoidance penalty plus restitution of avoided Canadian tax.

Director personal liability: The general partners who designed or beneficially controlled the shell arrangement may be personally assessed for the tax shortfall if they exercised dominion or control over the partnership's assets; civil fraud remedies include restitution, injunctions, and detention orders on outbound remittances.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) of up to $100,000 per violation for individuals and $500,000 for entities; repeated or willful non-compliance can result in criminal prosecution with fines up to $1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally penalized if they knowingly failed to implement a compliance regime, authorized a transaction designed to circumvent reporting thresholds, or traded with sanctions-listed persons; a court can issue a travel ban and freeze personal assets pending forfeiture or restitution.
Failure to Remit Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: The CRA may issue a requirement to pay for the unremitted amounts, impose a 10% penalty on the net deficiency, interest at prescribed rates, and assess director liability; criminal sanctions under the Income Tax Act include fines and up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Every person who was a director or general partner of the LP at the time of the failure is jointly and severally liable for the unremitted amounts under s. 227.1, with no due-diligence defence where the non-remittance was intentional; civil judgments may extend to seizure of personal property and restrictions on international travel.
