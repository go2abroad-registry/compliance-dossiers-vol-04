Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and federal income tax program accounts; Revenu Québec for Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax remittance and provincial tax registration. Primary online portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Revenu Québec's CLIC enterprise portal (https://clic.revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity participating in a Quebec Limited Partnership must complete the following chain: (1) obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) by filing a declaration of registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec; (2) register the limited partnership with Revenu Québec for QST and other provincial accounts; (3) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account by filing the Canada Revenue Agency application (commonly Form RC1 or online through the CRA's Non-Resident GST/HST Registration process), identifying the partnership as the registrant and appointing a Canadian resident representative if required; (4) ensure all non-resident partners obtain or are referenced by the appropriate CRA tax identifiers for purposes of the partnership information return; (5) maintain a physical or deemed establishment in Quebec adequate to support registrant status and service of process; (6) keep all registration confirmations in the partnership's permanent tax file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Quebec Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the federal Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act; the partnership is not a separate income taxpayer. Income, losses, credits, and taxable capital gains are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners for inclusion in their own tax returns. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is itself treated as a registrant where it carries on a commercial activity, and is responsible for registering, collecting, and remitting indirect taxes.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; a limited partnership cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. The following partnership-relevant elections exist: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration election under section 242 of the Excise Tax Act for a person not required to register, which accelerates eligibility to claim input tax credits; (ii) election under subsection 238(1) of the Excise Tax Act to make annual GST/HST returns on a non-calendar fiscal period subject to CRA approval; (iii) analogous QST election under Revenu Québec's taxation scheme for registrants using a fiscal period other than the calendar year; (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangements are not elective — statutory provisions in sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act apply to deny deductions where appropriate; and (v) the partnership may, through its partnership agreement, validly allocate profits and losses among partners, but such allocations must be compliant with subsection 96(1) and the general anti-avoidance provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters for both GST/HST and QST. For QST, a person who is registered for GST/HST is generally not a small supplier and is required to register for QST if carrying on a commercial activity in Quebec. A non-resident supplying B2B enterprise SaaS to Quebec customers or carrying on commercial activity through a Quebec partnership exceeds this threshold, it must register with both CRA and Revenu Québec before the supply is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A B2B enterprise SaaS supply supplied to a non-resident is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act and QST regime where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant and has no establishment in Quebec in respect of the supply; (ii) the SaaS is delivered electronically via cloud access or download, not as tangible goods; (iii) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and is not performed wholly or primarily in Canada in a manner that benefits a person in Canada; (iv) the service is not supplied to, or for use by, a Canadian resident's permanent establishment; and (v) the invoice and underlying contract clearly evidence that the recipient is the party contracting for its own enterprise use. If those conditions are met, the supply is reported at 0% GST and 0% QST, and the partnership may claim input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated export.

  • Service Agreement / Master Subscription Agreement clearly identifying the non-resident B2B customer, the cloud services, and the consideration
  • Commercial Invoice with explicit zero-rated export designation and the partnership's GST/HST and QST registration numbers
  • Proof of non-resident status of the purchaser (e.g., foreign corporate registry extract, foreign VAT/GST registration certificate, or statutory declaration)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT payment confirmation, or bank statement evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Canada
  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) and Bill of Lading / Air Waybill only where the SaaS is delivered on physical media or bundled with hardware entering the export stream
  • Technical and usage records demonstrating that the software is hosted and consumed primarily outside Canada (e.g., log of user IP addresses and deployment region)

No real-time government fiscal clearance, pre-certification API stamping, or digital tax control module is mandated by CRA or Revenu Québec for B2B SaaS supplies. Electronic invoices are legally valid if they contain the prescribed particulars under section 3 of the GST/HST Regulations and equivalent QST rules: supplier name and registration number, invoice date, transaction date, description of supply, consideration, applicable taxes, and indication of zero-rated status. Registered recipients may require PEPPOL/EDI or other formats by contract, but there is no statutory digital fiscal clearance requirement for cross-border SaaS exports.

CRA and Revenu Québec may conduct post-assessment and export-verification audits of zero-rated SaaS supplies. If the registrant cannot substantiate an export with contemporaneous documentation, CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 14.975% GST/HST plus QST, impose arrears interest from the date the tax was originally reportable, and deny input tax credits. Under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act, a person who knowingly, or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, makes a false statement may be liable to a penalty of 50% of the misstated tax; parallel QST penalties apply under the Quebec Tax Administration Act. The four-year normal reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion may also be pursued, leading to fines, penalties, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations; both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible without general exchange-control licensing. Profit remittances, royalty payments, interest, SaaS subscription revenues, and intercompany capital movements may leave Canada freely, subject only to anti-money-laundering verification and economic sanctions screening. Dividends and partnership distributions to non-residents are not restricted but may trigger Canadian Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and applicable Quebec tax rules. No prior Central Bank approval is required for foreign direct investment, intercompany loans, or repatriation of proceeds, provided the transactions are accurately documented and not routed through sanctioned entities or jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Canadian exporters, including B2B SaaS providers invoicing in USD, EUR, or other currencies, may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts, accept conversions at commercial bank rates, or use hedging instruments without any surrender requirement. All export income must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency for income tax purposes; balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Statistics Canada may apply to large cross-border service exporters.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority; foreign exchange policy is implemented jointly with the Department of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate and does not operate a centralized FX control administration. The Currency Act and the United Nations Act govern legal tender and sanctions-related FX restrictions, while FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT obligations on money-transfer providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility, primarily as a payment orchestration-tier activity. Canadian law does not grant a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; foreign digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or as unregulated agents providing bank-transfer services. To open a full treasury account under a Quebec LP, the LP must provide its provincial business number (NEQ), partnership registration certificate, and AML documents. A foreign EMI that merely extends an account to the LP without a Canadian MSB registration cannot be the primary treasury conduit unless it partners with a tier-1 Canadian-regulated bank or aQuebec-based MSB registered with FINTRAC. For operational, low-value B2B software subscriptions, Wise and Stripe are acceptable as collection rails, but high-value intercompany funding and FX hedges must remain with a tier-1 bank to avoid sanction-induced freezing and to meet commercial-court diligence standards.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Quebec LP, inbound equity or debt capital from a parent, foreign investor, or limited partner must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to a tier-1 Canadian bank account. When received from a non-resident entity, the beneficial ruling under subsection 212(3) of the Income Tax Act (or an applicable treaty) requires the bank to request a 'Form NR301' declaration if the payment will be a loan. The wire transfer must include a transparent message field: 'Contribution to capital of Quebec Limited Partnership CA_LP', 'Intercompany loan to CA_LP', or 'Prepaid enterprise SaaS service fee'. Canadian banks do not mandate a numeric purpose code; the underlying commercial agreement and payment invoice must be retained. If the transfer is an equity contribution, bank policy will require a notarized copy of the subscription certificate or partnership agreement amendment. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank subjects the incoming remittance to sanctions screening and, for politically exposed persons, enhanced regulatory due diligence. The wire should be routed through a correspondent account in Canada (e.g., National Bank of Canada via CIBC or RBC) and never through a jurisdiction subject to OFAC sanctions, otherwise auto-freezing risks arise under Canada's sanctions enforcement regime.Outbound clearing for B2B Enterprise SaaS trade settlements should be executed via two rails: (1) recurring card payments through Stripe Billing using the customer's single-use token or charge API with settlement in CAD/CAD-USD and clearance across Visa/Mastercard networks; (2) corporate disbursements for vendor license fees, contractors, and overseas payroll using SWIFT MT103 messages from the tier-1 bank, with explicit purpose text such 'Royalty payment for software licenses under agreement ref [X]' or 'Independent contractor fee for enterprise implementation services'. For transactions in SEPP (Mexican) or other peso markets, an MT103 serial payment is preferred, and banks will typically quote a FX conversion in the domestic market. Do not use documentary Letters of Credit for typical SaaS operational expenses because LCs are reserved for product goods with inspection certificates; if required by a large enterprise customer, a standby LC issued on the client's behalf can be discounted but is rarely needed for software services. High-value SaaS disbursements above CAD 50,000 should be pre-advised to the bank 48 hours in advance to avoid risk-management holds during statutory holidays in Quebec (e.g., April 23, Quebec National Holiday, Canada Day).Outright FX forwards (up to 12 months) with a tier-1 Canadian bank, used to lock USD/CAD conversion for recurring SaaS subscription revenue.FX swaps (swap swaps) for rolling short-term liquidity and offsetting intercompany cash balances between USD and CAD.Cross-currency swap (CCS) when the LP originates debt from abroad and must service USD interest and principal while generating CAD revenue.Vanilla European call/put FX options (typically on USD/CAD) to cap downside without obligating delivery.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible or necessary for CAD due to the advanced onshore forward market; Canadian Regulators permit CAD NDFs only on limited dispensation, and banks will strongly discourage them.Covered currency collar structures involving simultaneous purchase of a call and sale of a put are permitted, provided they comply with OSFI hedge-accounting standards and the LP's partnership agreement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility, primarily as a payment orchestration-tier activity. Canadian law does not grant a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; foreign digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) or as unregulated agents providing bank-transfer services. To open a full treasury account under a Quebec LP, the LP must provide its provincial business number (NEQ), partnership registration certificate, and AML documents. A foreign EMI that merely extends an account to the LP without a Canadian MSB registration cannot be the primary treasury conduit unless it partners with a tier-1 Canadian-regulated bank or aQuebec-based MSB registered with FINTRAC. For operational, low-value B2B software subscriptions, Wise and Stripe are acceptable as collection rails, but high-value intercompany funding and FX hedges must remain with a tier-1 bank to avoid sanction-induced freezing and to meet commercial-court diligence standards.
