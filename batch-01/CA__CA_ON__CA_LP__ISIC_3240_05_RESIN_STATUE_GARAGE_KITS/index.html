Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods that are exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The polyresin statue garage kits must be delivered by the supplier or the purchaser to a destination outside Canada, or the supplier must export them as a result of a sale, and the goods must be exported in accordance with the regulations. Services performed on movable tangible property for non-residents, if the property is exported, may also be zero-rated under section 5 of Part V. The LP must retain documentary evidence showing export and the non-resident status of the purchaser.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 / G13) or proof of export through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing delivery outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods and confirming foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of funds from the non-resident buyer
  • Customs export transaction supporting documents such as packing list, shipper's export declaration, and freight carrier confirmation

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice clearance mandate applies in Canada. CRA requires prescribed invoice information under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or using certified third-party software; original source records must be retained in Canada or accessible electronically on request. No mandatory digital stamping regime exists.

CRA may deny zero-rating treatment and reassess the LP for uncollected GST/HST, interest, and penalties within the normal reassessment period. Export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive verification of documentary evidence. If a false statement or omission is made knowingly, the Excise Tax Act imposes a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the amount of tax that was or would have been remitted. Gross negligence penalties may reach 50% of the amount understated. Willful evasion is a criminal offence punishable by fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawbacks apply to improperly claimed refunds or input tax credits, including the reinstatement of GST/HST payable on the exported supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full IMF Article VIII current and capital account convertibility. There is no exchange control legislation, no central bank permission requirement for inbound capital contributions, and no restriction on converting CAD balances into USD, EUR, or CNY for legitimate trade or investment. Non-resident limited partners may therefore inject equity and receive profit distributions without foreign-exchange licensing. The sole tax friction on outward profit remittance is Canadian income taxation of the LP's Canadian-source business income allocated to partners, generally tax-treaty relievable, together with Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act (Canada) for any separate cross-border payments of interest, royalties, rents, or management fees. As a practical matter, banks conduct independent source-of-funds diligence and FINTRAC receives electronic funds transfer reports for wire transactions of CAD 10,000 or more; these compliance screens are not convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds received by a Canadian-resident partnership. The LP may hold all export receipts in USD or EUR indefinitely within a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The binding compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting for receipts of CAD 10,000 or more, which is report-based and not a surrender rule; (ii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting for physical exports exceeding CAD 2,000, which must reconcile with underlying banking inflows; and (iii) CRA income-tax and functional-currency translation — the LP must measure income in CAD unless it elects to compute in an eligible functional currency under Income Tax Regulations section 2611, using Bank of Canada daily exchange rates. Adopting a prudent internal treasury policy that converts net export proceeds within five business days is recommended but is a corporate decision, not a legal mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada exercising statutory responsibilities under the Currency Act; banking conduct supervision rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border financial-transaction surveillance rests with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility, strictly ancillary. Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and similar FINTRAC-registered money services businesses may provide useful multi-currency receivables and payables rails, but they cannot function as the LP's primary settlement bank because they lack payments-clearing membership in Payments Canada (LVTS and Real-Time Rail) and may trigger Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration obligations when collecting CAD-denominated payments from Canadians. The legally sound treasury architecture is a Schedule I Canadian bank operating account as the settlement account of record, with the EMI used only as a sub-ledger for inbound EUR/USD preorder settlements and low-value supplier disbursements. Engaging an unregistered offshore EMI to collect Canadian retail preorder deposits would constitute unlicensed retail payment activity and is therefore not viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners inject capital by SWIFT MT103 wire to the LP's dedicated Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, naming the LP's legal entity as beneficiary exactly as it appears on the bank's signature card. The remittance information field should read: 'Capital contribution to Ontario LP — ISIC 3240 polyresin statue production'. Canada does not apply the U.S. Treasury TPO purpose-code table; receiving banks parse ISO 20022 Purpose Codes, and the recommended codes for partner capital are CFDI (Contribution to Financial Institution) or GIFT where the wire is purely an equity injection. A mismatch between the stated purpose and the remittance text will normally cause a two to three day compliance hold. Best practice is to transmit a capital-call compliance package (executed capital call, LP agreement extract, source-of-funds dossier) to the bank's relationship manager before wiring, obtain written clearance, and then send the full contribution as one aggregated wire. Deliberately splitting a single capital call into multiple wires below the CAD 10,000 reporting threshold constitutes prohibited structuring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and will trigger automatic FINTRAC surveillance.Production and supplier disbursements clear through three rails. First, direct SWIFT MT103 wires via the Schedule I bank's correspondent network for USD, EUR, and CNY invoices, using the OUR cost option and embedding commercial invoice numbers, purchase order numbers, and HS classification codes in the remittance field for straight-through processing. Second, for production batches with a contract value exceeding CAD 500,000, an irrevocable at-sight documentary letter of credit conforming to UCP 600, opened by the LP's Canadian bank and confirmed by the supplier's US or North-East Asian receiving bank, with required documents of commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and inspection certificate. Third, domestic Canadian disbursements through Payments Canada LVTS for high-value CAD transfers and Real-Time Rail for immediate sub-threshold payments, with the standard EFT file rail for payroll and recurring local supplier runs. All outbound payment messages are screened against OFAC and UN sanctions lists by the originating bank. Use of unlicensed private settlement networks, unregistered crypto off-ramps, or nester correspondent chains that bypass a regulated Canadian bank is prohibited and must not be adopted.Deliverable USD/CAD and EUR/CAD vanilla forward contracts: physically settled with the LP's Schedule I bank dealer desk under a negotiated master agreement; the LP participates solely as a non-dealer end-user hedging preorder revenue against CAD-denominated production costs, and no securities registration is required.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) and rollover swaps for staggered capital-call timing and monthly conversion of USD and EUR preorder settlements into CAD; each trade is documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and CSA credit support annex and requires general-partner-approved trading limits.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies that are not freely deliverable in Canadian domestic markets, including CNY, KRW, and MXN, where the counterparty is the same Schedule I bank acting through its affiliate desk; NDFs are permissible mark-to-market swap contracts under Income Tax Act section 39.2, but for the CAD/USD pair an NDF is commercially unnecessary because Canadian dollars are fully deliverable and no liquid offshore CAD NDF market exists.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility, strictly ancillary. Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and similar FINTRAC-registered money services businesses may provide useful multi-currency receivables and payables rails, but they cannot function as the LP's primary settlement bank because they lack payments-clearing membership in Payments Canada (LVTS and Real-Time Rail) and may trigger Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration obligations when collecting CAD-denominated payments from Canadians. The legally sound treasury architecture is a Schedule I Canadian bank operating account as the settlement account of record, with the EMI used only as a sub-ledger for inbound EUR/USD preorder settlements and low-value supplier disbursements. Engaging an unregistered offshore EMI to collect Canadian retail preorder deposits would constitute unlicensed retail payment activity and is therefore not viable.
