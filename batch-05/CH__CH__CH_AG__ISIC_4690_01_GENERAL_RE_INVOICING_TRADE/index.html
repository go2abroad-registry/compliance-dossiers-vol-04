Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

CHF 100,000 minimum share capital; at least 20% of each share's nominal value must be paid up, and at least CHF 50,000 in total must be deposited before registration; the deposit must be evidenced by a blocked-account confirmation from a Swiss bank and remains escrowed until the company is registered

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to an office-only re-invoicing operation; if a physical office is leased, the employer must obtain fire safety certification from the competent cantonal fire authority, verify structural load limits, and secure a workplace safety clearance through SUVA and the cantonal labor inspectorate.

Paid-in capital rule: CHF 100,000 minimum share capital; at least 20% of each share's nominal value must be paid up, and at least CHF 50,000 in total must be deposited before registration; the deposit must be evidenced by a blocked-account confirmation from a Swiss bank and remains escrowed until the company is registered

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC) — central authority for VAT, federal withholding tax and stamp duties; portal: www.estv.admin.ch. Direct federal, cantonal and communal corporate income tax is administered by the Tax Office of the Canton of Zug (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung Zug / Amt für Steuern), portal: www.zg.ch/steuern.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Swiss AG must: (1) have its articles of incorporation notarized and be entered in the Commercial Register of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt Zug), which automatically generates the Swiss business identification number UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the Federal Statistical Office UID register; (2) file VAT registration with the ESTV Main Division VAT (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer) using form 1043 or the ESTV e-service when worldwide VAT-taxable turnover exceeds CHF 250,000, or voluntarily when below, obtaining a VAT ID of the form CHE-###.###.### MWST; (3) register for direct tax purposes with the Tax Office of the Canton of Zug using the UID, obtaining the tax account number for federal, cantonal and communal corporate income and capital tax; (4) register the company as a Swiss withholding tax debtor (Verrechnungssteuer) with ESTV for future dividend distributions. The UID is the mandatory statutory business identifier for invoicing and customs e-declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Swiss AG must: (1) have its articles of incorporation notarized and be entered in the Commercial Register of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt Zug), which automatically generates the Swiss business identification number UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) from the Federal Statistical Office UID register; (2) file VAT registration with the ESTV Main Division VAT (Hauptabteilung Mehrwertsteuer) using form 1043 or the ESTV e-service when worldwide VAT-taxable turnover exceeds CHF 250,000, or voluntarily when below, obtaining a VAT ID of the form CHE-###.###.### MWST; (3) register for direct tax purposes with the Tax Office of the Canton of Zug using the UID, obtaining the tax account number for federal, cantonal and communal corporate income and capital tax; (4) register the company as a Swiss withholding tax debtor (Verrechnungssteuer) with ESTV for future dividend distributions. The UID is the mandatory statutory business identifier for invoicing and customs e-declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box regime; the AG is a resident capital company taxable on worldwide profit with territorial carve-outs for foreign permanent establishments. Available elections include: (i) VAT group election (Art. 21 MWSTG) treating closely linked enterprises as one taxable person; (ii) participation relief election (Art. 7 LIFD / Art. 70 DBG) exempting qualifying dividends and capital gains where the AG holds at least 10% of the share capital or an acquisition cost of at least CHF 1 million; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) in cantons offering the measure, including Zug; (iv) repayment of paid-in capital reserves without Swiss withholding tax instead of dividend distribution; (v) the Swiss hybrid mismatch rules (Art. 58a-h DBG, enacted under TRAF 2020) apply mandatorily and do not provide elective check-the-box or opt-out mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 of worldwide annual turnover from taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies), aggregated at VAT-group level as of 1 January 2025 (previously CHF 100,000); voluntary VAT registration is permitted below the threshold; export supplies and certain exempt supplies with input tax deduction are disregarded for threshold computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 23 MWSTG, the 0% rate applies to: (a) goods transported or dispatched directly by the supplier from Switzerland to a foreign destination, to the supplier's own foreign warehouse, or on board aircraft or ships used in international traffic; (b) auxiliary services, including transport, loading, unloading and forwarding, directly connected to those exported goods; and (c) supplies whose place of supply is outside Switzerland under B2B recipient-based rules, which are outside Swiss VAT rather than zero-rated. A pure offshore re-invoicing hub whose goods never touch Swiss territory has no Swiss VAT nexus and needs no zero-rating claim. For goods dispatched from Swiss stock, zero-rating requires a Swiss Customs export declaration plus transport and delivery proof. International B2B services must be substantiated with the customer's foreign VAT ID and business-substance evidence; absent proof, ESTV deems the supply taxable in Switzerland at 8.1%.

  • Swiss Customs export declaration (Zollanmeldung / SAD) submitted via e-dec or the FOCBS customs processing system, showing goods value, CN code, destination country and exporter UID
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or CMR international consignment note for road transport) evidencing physical dispatch from Swiss territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice, evidencing receipt of the foreign sales consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the export declaration and harmonized commodity codes
  • Proof of delivery (POD) or signed foreign warehouse receipt confirming arrival of goods abroad
  • Re-invoicing chain continuity file (purchase contracts, sales contracts, inspection certificates, correspondence) substantiating commercial reality

Switzerland does not mandate real-time electronic fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls or digital stamping; no tax-authority API pre-approval or digital stamp is required. E-invoicing is permitted under Art. 26a of the Swiss VAT Ordinance (MWSTV) if the system guarantees authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility; a PDF invoice sent by e-mail is accepted. Domestic invoices must contain the statutory elements of Art. 26 MWSTG (seller name/address, VAT ID 'CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx MWST', date, description, net amount, VAT rate and tax per rate); the Swiss QR-bill with QR-IBAN is the mandatory payment slip for Swiss invoices. Invoice datasets must be made available to ESTV on request in an auditable format.

ESTV and the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) jointly audit export substantiation files. Deficient documentation triggers reassessment at the standard VAT rate of 8.1%, recapture of input VAT and statutory interest. Under Arts. 96-98 MWSTG, intentional tax evasion attracts a penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax; negligence attracts a penalty of up to one-third of the evaded tax; procedural violations attract fines of up to CHF 10,000. Qualified criminal fraud is referred to public prosecution. Retrospective clawbacks may extend over the full statutory limitation period (up to 10 years, with longer criminal prosecution periods for fraud). The exporter and any co-contractor who knew or should have known of the abuse are jointly and severally liable; fraudulent export zero-rating also triggers sanctions under the Customs Penalty Act and revocation of privileges in simplified export procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland applies full convertibility on both current and capital account operations, with no SNB or SECO authorisation required for cross-border fund movements. CHF is a freely floating G10 currency; exporters and re-invoicing hubs may contract and settle in CHF, EUR, USD or any other currency and may hold foreign-currency balances domestically or abroad without restriction. Profit remittance is unrestricted at transfer level, although dividends paid by a Swiss AG to non-resident shareholders attract 35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) which is fully or partially reclaimable under double-taxation treaties or refund procedures; no withholding tax arises on commercial re-invoicing payments for goods and services. ISIC 4690 general goods do not require import/export licences in Switzerland, subject to SECO lists on dual-use, sanctions and embargoes. The OECD classifies the Swiss franc as a freely usable currency with no Article XIV restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline, surrender ratio, or conversion obligation for export receipts or trade proceeds. The SNB only collects statistical data on cross-border payments through the bank reporting system; the remitter or beneficiary must truthfully indicate the transaction purpose (for example, goods payment or transit trade/commission business) in the SWIFT message. A Swiss company may keep collections in the original currency indefinitely; no central bank approval or conversion certificate is required for onward disbursement.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank), established under the National Bank Act (SR 951.11); sole issuer of Swiss franc (CHF) banknotes and authority for monetary policy, currently maintaining a low policy rate (sub-0.5%) reflecting disinflationary conditions. The SNB operates the SIC/SIC4 real-time gross settlement system and compiles external payments statistics but does not administer exchange controls. FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) supervises banking and AML/CFT conduct under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA, SR 955.0).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as primary treasury; Medium as a supplemental collection or disbursement rail. Legal constraints: taking public deposits in Switzerland requires a FINMA banking licence under Art. 1 of the Banking Act (SR 952.0); EU/UK EMI licences (for example Revolut, Wise or Qonto) do not passport into Switzerland, and an unlicensed EMI account cannot replace a Swiss bank account for substance or audit purposes. Approved overlay: a Swiss fintech-licensed entity (Art. 1b Banking Act; cap CHF 100 million public deposits, no interest allowed) may provide e-money wallets, and licensed EU EMIs may be used for EUR payouts in a pass-through structure where funds first settle in the Swiss licensed bank account and are then distributed via the EMI to small suppliers, provided KYC controls remain with the Swiss bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity: shareholders remit minimum paid-in capital of CHF 100,000 (nominal capital floor under Art. 632 CO; at least CHF 50,000 must be paid in) via SWIFT MT103 to the bank's capital-formation blocked account. Field 70 must state: 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital [amount] per Board Resolution dated [date]' and the bank codes the transaction for SNB statistics as direct investment/equity capital, since Swiss law prescribes no fixed purpose code registry. The bank verifies against the notarised articles of association and provides the capital confirmation for the Commercial Register entry; funds are released to the operating account only after registration of the AG as a legal entity. Subsequent shareholder loans must be supported by written loan agreements with arm's-length interest documentation and use a clear 'shareholder loan' remittance reference so no equity recharacterisation occurs for tax purposes, and no pledge or round-tripping of funds is permitted.Open-account trade payments are executed by SWIFT MT103 with transaction referencing; documentary trade security via MT700 or MT720 letters of credit, MT707 amendments and MT760 standby LCs; the Swiss bank acts as issuing, advising or confirming bank depending on the corridor. Domestic CHF payments settle in real time over SIC (SIX Interbank Clearing); the EUR leg clears through SEPA/EUR-SIC connectivity; the Swiss payments industry is migrating to ISO 20022 MX formats. For XT-Transfer style virtual-account collection rails, the platform must confirm that funds are held with a licensed partner bank in the name of the Swiss AG, that balances are segregated and protected, and that the platform screens against Swiss SECO, UN and OFAC sanctions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 1,000,000 in aggregate intercompany transactions with a single related party in the tax year triggers contemporaneous local file and master file documentation requirements.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations but applies substantive principles under the Swiss OECD-compliant tax framework, exchange of information standards, and the federal anti-abuse doctrine; a re-invoicing company must evidence a real economic employer, decision-making functions, business premises, qualified personnel (headcount and payroll), and risk-bearing capacity in Switzerland; a mere mailbox Administrative AG without local substance can be re-characterized as a shell or transparent conduit, leading to reclassification of income, denial of treaty and participation relief, and a 35% withholding tax leakage on deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Untrue Disclosures

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax prosecution under the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax and the cantonal tax acts; penalty of one to three times the tax evaded plus default interest; procedural fines of up to CHF 10,000; in serious cases a criminal fine of up to CHF 30,000 is possible; civil reassessment without a final time limit until tax is time-barred.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false filings may face imprisonment of up to three years (or longer for aggravated offenses), travel restrictions, seizure of personal assets, and joint and several liability towards the tax authorities for evaded tax in cases of culpable participation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income by the Swiss tax authorities, with a corresponding 35% withholding tax on the deemed constructive dividend or upward assessment; additionally, double taxation risk without a corresponding adjustment; administrative penalties of CHF 20,000 per documentation failure, plus late interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalty if they have actively directed the transfer pricing policy and ignored documented warnings; social security and old-age pension insurers may initiate personal liability claims for abusively low margins that shortchange the Swiss social security system.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The company may be disregarded as a transparent entity under Swiss practice and the OECD MLC anti-abuse approach; its income is reassessed as attributable to the foreign principal or beneficial owner; treaty and participation relief are denied; the withholding tax of 35% is applied on the entire distributed cash flow or deemed dividend; the competent foreign tax authority is notified through the Swiss exchange-of-information mechanism; liquidation or withdrawal of the Swiss registration may be ordered by the commercial register authority if the company lacks a real seat.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid withholding tax and social security funds under Swiss corporate law (article 757 of the Swiss Code of Obligations and article 12 of the Swiss Withholding Tax Act); they may suffer a personal travel ban to facilitate recovery, have their personal assets attached, and be subject to criminal prosecution for negligent money laundering if the entity's sole function is obscuring beneficial ownership.
Aggressive Tax Planning and Anti-Avoidance Rejection

Statutory sanction: Substance-based general anti-avoidance doctrine under Swiss case law (the Zweckverpflockung test) allows the tax authorities to disregard the legal form and assess tax based on economic substance; legal certainty rulings are denied; the resulting tax assessment triggers compound interest, penalties up to 100% of the advantage and criminal sanctions for qualified fiscal evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in a circular structure exposing the company to insolvency may be personally liable to the Swiss trade creditors for tort and to the tax administration for the amount of the fraudulent advantage; criminal courts may issue custodial sentences up to five years for aggravated fiscal fraud, plus confiscation of the economic benefit and a ban on holding director mandates in Switzerland.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Stripping Circumvention

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid anticipatory tax from the debtors and all recipients of the distribution; penal surcharge of one time the evaded tax in case of negligence, up to three times in case of intent; criminal fines up to CHF 100,000 against the responsible organ; any unlawful refund claim is rejected and triggers a denial of all future relief certificates.

Director personal liability: The board but not the administration is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; they may face automatic lien on personal real estate in Switzerland, blocking of foreign travel and extradition for serious evasion; a criminal conviction in canton Zug may lead to a prison sentence of up to five years for the aggregate offence of fiscal fraud.
