Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The mandatory minimum statutory share capital is CHF 100,000 (Art. 621 OR), of which at least 20% of each share's nominal value, and in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration (Art. 632 OR). Funds must be deposited into a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss-regulated bank and remain at the free disposal of the governing body; the bank releases the funds only upon presentation of the certificate of commercial register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire resistance and evacuation certification from the Cantonal Fire Inspectorate in line with VKF/NKGS fire norms; structural load approval for the UHT process hall, silo tanks, and palletized packaging racks from the Cantonal Building Inspectorate; occupational health and safety clearance under ArGV 3 and the Swiss Labour Law, including 45-hour weekly limits, rest intervals, ergonomic workstations, mandatory accident insurance coverage through SUVA, and designated emergency assembly points.

Paid-in capital rule: The mandatory minimum statutory share capital is CHF 100,000 (Art. 621 OR), of which at least 20% of each share's nominal value, and in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration (Art. 632 OR). Funds must be deposited into a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss-regulated bank and remain at the free disposal of the governing body; the bank releases the funds only upon presentation of the certificate of commercial register entry.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni ATC) – VAT Division and corporate income tax units. Official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to carry out taxable activities in Switzerland (including B2B/B2C supplies of goods or services) must apply for a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) and VAT registration via the ESTV eVAT portal. The registration application must identify the foreign entity, its legal form, beneficial owners, and the proposed Swiss business activity. Non-established taxable persons must ordinarily appoint a fiscal representative resident in Switzerland to receive notifications and settle VAT liabilities (Art. 67 VAT Act). Where the annual worldwide turnover from taxable activities exceeds CHF 100,000, registration is mandatory for taxable supplies made or carried out in Switzerland; where turnover is below this threshold, voluntary registration is available. The UID number, once issued, serves as the Swiss VAT identification number for invoicing and customs purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity intending to carry out taxable activities in Switzerland (including B2B/B2C supplies of goods or services) must apply for a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) and VAT registration via the ESTV eVAT portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; an Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is always a separate taxable legal entity for direct taxes and cannot elect pass-through treatment. For Swiss VAT, a group of closely affiliated legal entities may elect to be treated as a single taxable person under Art. 12 VAT Act (VAT group election), with at least a 50% capital interest and uniform business purpose. For corporate income tax purposes, a Swiss AG may elect to form a tax consolidation group with its 80%-owned domestic subsidiaries (Art. 61(3) of the Swiss Direct Federal Tax Act) for offsetting profits and losses, provided the election is made under cantonal and federal tax law. No elective hybrid mismatch mechanism is available; Swiss tax law applies the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable activities (excluding VAT itself, exempt supplies and certain incidental real estate transactions). A foreign supplier carrying out taxable supplies in Switzerland is required to register once this threshold is exceeded based on worldwide business turnover; voluntary registration applies below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated when the goods are physically exported from Switzerland into a foreign territory and the supplier or the recipient (if supplier-instigated transport) provides satisfactory proof of export within legally defined time limits (Art. 23 VAT Act). The supply of services related to exported goods or services rendered directly to recipients abroad may also be zero-rated where the recipient is not established in Switzerland and benefits from the supply abroad. The taxable person must retain documentary proof showing the goods left Swiss customs territory, the commercial nature of the transaction, and the consideration paid. For UHT oat milk packaging operations under ISIC 1050, export zero-rating applies when finished aseptic packaged oat milk products are sold to a foreign buyer and physically dispatched from Switzerland to the destination market.

  • Swiss export customs declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / export declaration processed through the Swiss Customs eRecipients system)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and transport contract evidencing the sale and dispatch of the goods
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / airway bill showing the goods left Swiss customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/TT wire evidence substantiating receipt of consideration where required

Switzerland imposes no real-time government fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing obligation comparable to the Italian Sistema di Interscambio. Electronic invoices are accepted provided the authenticity of origin and integrity of content are guaranteed by recognised controls (e.g., Swiss QR-bill, ZUGFeRD/eBill or EDI interchange). The Swiss VAT Act prescribes required invoice content, but does not mandate a specific electronic clearing mechanism. Swiss QR-invoices are mandatory only for paper-based payment slips related to Swiss bank payments; they are not a VAT clearance device.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may audit export zero-rating cases by requesting full documentary proof. Missing or false export documentation triggers immediate retrospective reassessment at the standard VAT rate, together with default interest. In cases of intentional tax evasion or misleading documentation, the responsible persons are subject to administrative fines under the Swiss VAT Act and the Federal Act on Administrative Penal Law, and fraudulent claims can lead to criminal prosecution. Documents must be retained for at least ten years; a failure to substantiate the export transaction prohibits the refund of input VAT associated with the exported goods and creates a direct VAT clawback liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. The CHF is fully freely convertible, and there are no exchange control approval requirements for cross-border payments, dividend remittances, or capital repatriation. Dividend and profit remittances by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft are subject to a 35% Swiss federal withholding tax absent treaty relief, but no SNB authorization or FX license is required. Payments to non-resident shareholders are permissible in any currency. Sanctions screening is mandatory but does not restrict currency convertibility as such.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. Export receipts in EUR, USD, or other currencies may be retained, converted, or transferred freely. The only obligations are ordinary commercial record-keeping, VAT/accounting treatment under Swiss law, and compliance with Swiss sanctions and anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) - Schweizerische Nationalbank, in coordination with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) for international financial market policy and sanctions-related oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as the principal corporate banking venue. Swiss-licensed fintech banks and service providers may support limited onboarding and payment functionality, but they do not substitute for a tier-1 commercial bank in respect of full SIX SIC clearing, commercial LC capacity, and large cross-border trade settlements. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not viable as primary operating accounts for a Swiss AG, as accepting deposits or conducting payment business without a Swiss banking licence would contravene Article 1 of the Swiss Banking Act and FINMA circulars.
