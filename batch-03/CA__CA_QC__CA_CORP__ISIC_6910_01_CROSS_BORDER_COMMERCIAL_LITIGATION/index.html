Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal business number, corporate income tax, and GST/HST accounts (portal: CRA Business Registration Online / canada.ca/ben-nmr); Revenu Québec for Quebec corporate income tax, QST, and the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) (portal: Registraire des entreprises du Québec / registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca and clicSÉQUR).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation first obtains a 15-character federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online, selecting the GST/HST account and the federal corporate income tax account. The same BN information must then be disclosed through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ and a 9-digit QST account (Form LM-1-V or the clicSÉQUR online service). Processing normally takes 2 to 6 weeks; registration is compulsory before the entity invoices taxable legal services in Quebec, and the NEQ/BN must appear on all contracts, invoices, and written communications.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign corporation first obtains a 15-character federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online, selecting the GST/HST account and the federal corporate income tax account. The same BN information must then be disclosed through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ and a 9-digit QST account (Form LM-1-V or the clicSÉQUR online service). Processing normally takes 2 to 6 weeks; registration is compulsory before the entity invoices taxable legal services in Quebec, and the NEQ/BN must appear on all contracts, invoices, and written communications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. A business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act is always taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no deemed-partnership or pass-through election exists. For indirect tax, a non-resident carrying on business in Canada may file an election under section 132.1 of the Excise Tax Act to deem a resident agent, nominee, or representative to make and receive all supplies; divisional elections under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act may apply for GST/HST grouping. Anti-hybrid rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act and the Canadian hybrid mismatch arrangements rules under the OECD Multilateral Instrument limit deduction/credit structured tax arbitrage in cross-border litigation fee flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated and financial services) in any calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; the same CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold applies to both federal GST/HST (ETA subsection 240(1)) and Quebec QST (QSTA section 16). A non-resident with a permanent establishment in Quebec must register below the threshold if it makes taxable supplies through that establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports of goods applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when tangible personal property is exported before delivery or is delivered to a common carrier for export. Services supplied to a non-resident, including cross-border commercial litigation support and arbitration services, are zero-rated under section 23 of Part V.1 of Schedule VI where the service is supplied to a person not resident in Canada, is not in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is either performed substantially outside Canada or acquired for consumption or use exclusively in the recipient's business outside Canada. Legal services relating to litigation conducted substantially in Canada, or to Canadian real property, may remain taxable unless the non-resident recipient carries on no business in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and CBSA acceptance evidence
  • Commercial invoice demonstrating export sale, consignee, terms of delivery, and export date
  • Bill of Lading, Waybill, or Airway Bill evidencing physical transportation out of Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice proving receipt of funds from the non-resident client
  • Retainer agreement, conflict-of-interest records, and place-of-supply analysis confirming the non-resident status of the litigation client

No full real-time fiscal clearance is in force at the federal level; CRA operates the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 four-corner interoperability standard. Quebec mandates quarterly electronic transmission of standard B2B and B2C sales data (financial data reporting) to Revenu Québec via EDI or clicSÉQUR for fiscal periods beginning on or after set compliance dates; invoices must display the supplier NEQ and the GST/QST registration numbers. Digital stamping or real-time clearance of individual invoices before issuance is not yet mandatory, but transmitted data must reconcile with QST returns and vouchers.

CRA and Revenu Québec use targeted export-verification programs for professional service firms; failure to substantiate zero-rating within prescribed documentation timelines triggers retrospective reassessment of GST/QST at the combined 14.975% headline rate plus retroactive compound interest. Civil penalties include an 8% misstatement penalty for net-tax misreporting (ETA section 280), a 50% gross-negligence penalty on understated net tax (ETA section 285), and penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act for QST misreporting. Criminal exposure includes fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 12 months for evading or attempting to evade GST/QST (ETA sections 327 and 328); fraudulent zero-rating documentation also results in clawback of input tax credits claimed on the export supply, denial of refund claims, and criminal prosecution followed by Civil Remediation Order recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no general exchange-control regime for current or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible in law and in practice. Dividends, management fees, and branch profits are freely remittable subject only to the normal Canadian-source withholding tax, generally reduced by applicable tax treaties, and to anti-avoidance provisions under the Income Tax Act. There are no statutory restrictions on non-resident ownership or on repatriation of invested capital, but financial institutions are required to verify transaction compliance with economic sanctions, proceeds-of-crime rules, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 or carrying a high-risk nexus may be reportable as electronic funds transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement in Canada. Export proceeds are freely retained, convertible, and remittable in any currency. Exporters must maintain business records supporting customs declarations under the Customs Act and may need to report outward or inward financial flows under FINTRAC regulations; however, the exchange rate is fully market-determined and no minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, acting within the overall foreign-exchange and capital-control policy framework of the Minister of Finance; day-to-day compliance administration is shared with FINTRAC, OSFI, CBSA, and the Canada Revenue Agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for non-client treasury operations; Low-to-Prohibited as the principal depository for client trust funds. Offshore electronic money institutions generally lack deposit-taking authorization under the Bank Act and cannot lawfully hold the accounts required for a Quebec law practice. FINTRAC registration as a money service business is mandatory only for entities providing FX or money-transfer services in Canada. For the core litigation-contingency and client-clearing treasury, only an authorized Canadian bank or an authorized foreign bank branch licensed by OSFI under the Bank Act is operationally viable. An offshore EMI may be used exclusively for low-risk operational expenses at arm's length, subject to Treasury Board-equivalent governance and no trust funds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident directors or shareholders domiciled in high-reporting jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-resident directors or shareholders domiciled in high-reporting jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Certified passport copies, board resolution authorizing Canadian account opening, proof of legal entity registration from Québec or federal registry, professional certificate for the litigation chamber, organizational chart with full UBO disclosure, reference letter from a correspondent bank, and a detailed explanation of the commercial purpose and expected wire flows.
Third-party fund deposits into client trust/escrow account

Root cause: Third-party fund deposits into client trust/escrow account

Defense evidence: Executed escrow agreement, underlying litigation or arbitration engagement letter, client fund attribution ledger, source-of-funds declarations for each third-party, notarized independence opinion, and a sworn undertaking by counsel that funds are held strictly under the Barreau du Québec/Chambre des notaires trust rules.
Unusually large inbound wire payment from arbitral award or settlement

Root cause: Unusually large inbound wire payment from arbitral award or settlement

Defense evidence: Final award or court-sealed settlement order, payment instruction, bank MT103 evidence, tax clearance from the Canada Revenue Agency, corporate receipt settlement record, beneficial-offering-party identity, and an anti-corruption/sanctions screening report via OSFI-compliant screening tool.
Use of mixed client-operating account causing fiduciary contamination

Root cause: Use of mixed client-operating account causing fiduciary contamination

Defense evidence: Independent law-society trust accounting report, periodic segregation certificate, transaction currency ledger, monthly reconciliation, dual-authorization mandate, and external auditor attestation that no client funds are commingled with operating funds.
Recurring international fee disbursements to counsel or experts

Root cause: Recurring international fee disbursements to counsel or experts

Defense evidence: Master professional-services agreement, itemized invoices, time-and-cost records, entity bank account details of payees, tax registry identifier of recipient firm, and compliance certification confirming no prohibited person or sanctioned institution is involved.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must be structured through SWIFT MT103 or a participating Canadian bank's direct clearing system. The wire must be annotated with the ISO 20022 purpose code and legal entity identification. For dividend remittance and shareholder loans, the proceeds should be credited to the operating or escrow account only after a purpose-code justification has been retained. For regulatory verification in Canada, the remitting bank must include the Canada entity's CRA business number, the ultimate recipient's legal name, and a precise narrative such as 'Equity injection for Quebec commercial litigation chambers'. Currency conversion is fully permissible at the receiving bank's posted rate, but must be documented in a spot-rate ticket for audit trail completeness. Sanctions screening of the remitting counterparty must occur before acceptance.Outbound trade settlements can be cleared through the Large Value Transfer System for Canadian-dollar payments, SWIFT MT103 for cross-border telegraphic transfers, or letters of credit subject to UCP600 where documentary risk mitigation is required. For litigation disbursements to counterparties or expert-chambers abroad, MT103 is preferred because it provides an immutable cross-border confirmation. Commercial letters of credit may be used when a settlement or retainage is conditioned upon contemporaneous documentary proof of performance by the recipient.
