Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des impôts AFD / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); e-portal at www.estv.admin.ch, including the dedicated VAT online services portal (VAT-Online) for registration, filing, assessments, and refund applications.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Swiss Aktiengesellschaft in the cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister / Registre du commerce); the Federal Statistical Office automatically allocates the Swiss Business Identification Number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) upon registration. Step 2: The AG must separately register for VAT with ESTV if its worldwide turnover from taxable supplies exceeds CHF 100,000 per calendar year; registration is filed electronically through the ESTV VAT portal using the official registration form and becomes effective retroactively to the commencement of the taxable activity. Step 3: For a foreign-controlled AG, the statutory VAT identification is the UID followed by the MWST/TVA/IVA suffix (for example CHE-116.037.634 MWST); foreign enterprises without a Swiss registered seat must appoint a Swiss fiscal representative and provide a Swiss service address before the UID/VAT number can be activated. Step 4: The UID must be shown on all invoices, customs declarations, and business correspondence, and must be renewed or derecognised only through the official Swiss business register platforms.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Aktiengesellschaft is a resident corporate taxpayer with unlimited tax liability on worldwide commercial profit at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. Income and capital gains derived through a foreign permanent establishment or from foreign real estate are allocatively exempt in most double tax treaties and, unilaterally, benefit from income allocation rules; no pass-through status is available; the AG remains a separately taxed legal entity notwithstanding 100% foreign shareholding.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or elective hybrid-mismatch regime exists in Swiss federal tax law. Available elections and statutory mechanisms relevant to an AG: (1) VAT group election under Art. 12 VAT Act allowing financially, economically, and organisationally interconnected Swiss enterprises to file as a single taxable person; (2) profit and loss transfer agreement (Organschaft) under Art. 733b Code of Obligations for income-tax consolidation; (3) cantonal tax status elections such as mixed company or domicile company status where the relevant canton grants reduced cantonal taxation of foreign-source income; (4) patent box election under Art. 24b of the Tax Harmonisation Act (valid in cantons adopting the IP box); (5) notional interest deduction election on excess equity in cantons that have legislated it; (6) election under Art. 34 VAT Act to compute VAT on received consideration (cash accounting basis) where annual VAT turnover does not exceed CHF 5 million; and (7) voluntary VAT registration by a below-threshold or foreign-only-supply business in order to claim input VAT deduction rights.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 turnover per calendar year (Art. 10 VAT Act; for foreign businesses, CHF 100,000 of turnover sourced from Swiss territory). Voluntary registration is permitted for entities supplying only zero-rated or non-subject services in order to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Leasing of ISO shipping containers is treated as a service related to movable tangible property located at the customer's seat under Art. 7(3)(f) VAT Act (B2B rule). Where the lessee is established abroad, the supply falls outside the Swiss territorial scope; the Swiss lessor applies 0% output tax and retains the full input VAT deduction on costs, provided it holds documentary proof of the customer's foreign seat and container deployment abroad. Export of containers as goods with physical removal from Swiss customs territory qualifies for 0% zero-rating under Art. 23 VAT Act only upon customs-verified export.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed in the e-dec customs clearance system of the Federal Customs Administration (EZV)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/AWB) evidencing physical removal from Switzerland
  • Commercial lease agreement and lease schedule identifying the FCL container box numbers and the foreign lessee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit confirmation) evidencing receipt of lease rental proceeds from the foreign lessee

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control requirement applies under Swiss VAT law. VAT returns must be filed electronically through the ESTV VAT portal. Mandatory e-billing is limited to the healthcare sector (insurance carriers and medical service providers, fully mandatory from 2026). Standard commercial practice uses Swiss eBill / QR-bill and Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 formats for B2B e-invoicing, but paper invoices remain legally valid. Cross-border container leasing invoices must contain the supplier UID, customer foreign VAT identification, lease details, and either a zero-rating note or a statement that the place of supply is abroad.

ESTV and cantonal audit authorities scrutinize export evidence and place-of-supply documentation. Non-substantiated zero-rating is retrospectively reversed with VAT assessed on the gross rental for up to ten years, plus statutory default interest at 5% p.a. (Art. 36(4) VAT Act). Deliberate VAT evasion triggers penalty surcharges of up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 58 VAT Act) and criminal fines up to CHF 30,000; forged documents (e.g., false Bills of Lading, simulated inward remittances) are prosecuted under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 251, art. 252 and 314a) with imprisonment up to five years. Switzerland has no general foreign-exchange control; the 180-day repatriation period is a documentary benchmark rather than a statutory capital-control deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland grants full currency convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The Swiss franc is freely convertible; there are no general exchange-control approvals, no withholding limitations on the transfer of lease rental income, no restrictions on dividend cross-border payment, and no impediments to capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders, provided that Swiss anti-money-laundering rules and the statutory 35% withholding tax on dividends are observed. The 35% dividend tax can be reduced or refunded under an applicable double-taxation treaty or under the Swiss Notification Procedure for treaty relief. A Swiss AG engaged in intermodal ISO container leasing may hold USD, EUR, CNY, and other convertible receivables in foreign-currency current accounts without any SNB licence or FX allocation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists. Switzerland imposes no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the container-leasing company may retain lease proceeds indefinitely in any freely convertible currency or offshore. Only the statistical reporting regime of SR 941.141 applies, under which payments exceeding CHF 50,000 may be selected for sample-based balance-of-payments reporting, which is purely informational and does not constitute an approval, conversion, or surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), established under the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank (SNB Act, SR 951.11), is the sole monetary and exchange-rate authority; foreign-exchange controls, balance-of-payments reporting, and statistical notification obligations are codified under the Foreign Exchange Ordinance (SR 941.141). Banking conduct supervision is co-executed with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium for low-value operational treasury functions; Low for serving as the capital escrow account or as the primary trade-settlement institution. Legal constraints: the EU/EEA financial services passport does not extend to Switzerland, so a foreign electronic money institution must either hold or route funds through a Swiss banking partner or apply for a FINMA fintech licence under Art. 1b para 2 of the Banking Act, which caps public deposits at CHF 100 million and prohibits interest business and maturity transformation. For a cross-border leasing treasury, the compliant architecture is a Swiss commercial bank core account for escrow, SWIFT settlements and letters of credit, with an EMI used only for small-value disbursements, expenses, and multi-currency operational float.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound subscription and capital-increase funds must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's own bank account into the Swiss company's escrowed capital account. The payment narrative and ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g., EQUI, Equity capital) must reference the subscription resolution, the company's Swiss UID number, and its Handelsregister identification number. Supporting documentation comprises the notarised capital-increase deed under Art. 650 OR, the extraordinary general meeting minutes, and the updated Commercial Register filing; the CHF 100,000 statutory minimum capital must remain unencumbered until final registration. No SNB approval is required, but the bank submits statistical balance-of-payments notifications under SR 941.141 for inflows exceeding CHF 50,000 when sampled. Inbound USD or EUR wires are traced via SWIFT gpi with UETR and may be converted to CHF at the bank's spot rate, or held in a multi-currency capital account where the capital increase so provides.Clearing is anchored on SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with UETR tracking for lease settlements, container purchase payments to PRC or Korean box manufacturers, and depot and maintenance disbursements. Irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 are the standard rail for new container manufacturing payments, complemented by documentary collections under URC 522 for used-container resales. Same-day CHF payments are effected through the Swiss Payments Platform (SPP); euro-denominated flows clear through SEPA; for recurring domestic CHF collections, the LSV+ direct-debit rail (the Swiss ACH equivalent) is applicable, whereas the North American ACH-debit rail is limited to client debits in the United States. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A Swiss Stock Corporation (AG) must submit to an ordinary statutory audit if, in two consecutive fiscal years, it exceeds at least two of the following criteria: balance sheet total CHF 20 million; annual sales revenue CHF 40 million; 250 full-time employees. Even below those thresholds, a limited audit is required if shareholders holding at least 10% of the voting rights so request; a small AG with fewer than 10 full-time employees may waive audit entirely upon unanimous shareholder consent under Art. 727a CO.
