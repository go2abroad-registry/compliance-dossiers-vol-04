Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a société en commandite; contributions are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and may be cash or in kind. No escrow account is required by any Quebec statute for formation.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire safety certificate applies to a low-risk retail or warehouse occupancy below 1,200 m²; if premises accommodate employees, obtain an attestation of conformity with the Québec Building Code fire safety chapter from the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ) and register the workplace with the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) for occupational health, first aid, and workers' compensation coverage.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a société en commandite; contributions are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and may be cash or in kind. No escrow account is required by any Quebec statute for formation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST, federal income tax, and Business Number (BN) administration via My Business Account portal; Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST), Quebec income tax, and provincial tax registration via Quebec Government Online Services / Revenu Québec CliC/My Business Account; Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) – enterprise registration and NEQ issuance for Quebec legal persons and partnerships.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, obtaining the NEQ (Quebec enterprise number); no minimum capital requirement, and non-resident limited partners are permitted but the general partner must maintain a real establishment in Quebec. 2) Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the My Business Account portal, and open the GST/HST program account (program identifier 'RT') under the partnership's own BN, because for GST/HST purposes a partnership is treated as a separate person from its partners. 3) Register with Revenu Québec for the QST account (program identifier 'TVQ') using the NEQ, including completion of the Revenu Québec registration form; no Quebec provincial corporate income tax entity-level registration is needed because the LP is fiscally transparent, but each partner is required to self-report their share of Canadian-source income. 4) Enroll in CRA GST/HST NETFILE and Revenu Québec 'Services en ligne' for electronic filing, and appoint an authorized representative (each partner may do so) for access and filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election parallel to the US regime. A Quebec LP is automatically transparent for federal and provincial income tax purposes; no election can convert it into an entity-level taxable corporation. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is a distinct taxable person and may elect under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act (and analogous QST rules) to treat a branch or division as a separate small supplier division. The partnership may also elect a non-calendar fiscal period in certain cross-border cases under the Income Tax Act, and partners may jointly elect valuation rules on property contribution to the partnership. No hybrid mismatch election is available; Canada applies OECD hybrid mismatch rules transaction-by-transaction, and non-resident partners must accept the transparent classification for Canadian tax reporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 – GST/HST small-supplier threshold and QST small-supplier threshold; the threshold is based on taxable supply revenues from Canada and elsewhere in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Once exceeded, the Quebec LP must register for both GST/HST and QST. For sales of goods delivered outside Canada, zero-rated export revenues count as taxable supplies for threshold purposes but are not subject to tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Quebec LP selling maternity and baby supplies through e-commerce qualifies for 0% GST/QST on export supplies where the goods are delivered to a non-resident customer at a destination outside Canada, shipped by the vendor, or delivered by a common carrier to an export point as part of the supply. The goods must be exported without further manufacturing, processing, or incorporation into other goods in Canada, and the export must occur within a reasonable time after the supply is made. Services ancillary to exported goods may be zero-rated if they are part of the original exported supply. The LP must maintain contemporaneous evidence establishing the timing, destination, and payment for the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration – Canada Export Declaration Form B13A/AEM (Automated Export Declaration) filed through CBP/CBSA e-Manifest for automated export reporting; for low-value e-commerce mail/express consignments, shipment-level documentation and courier manifests are used
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / warehouse export receipt – signed proof of safe arrival of goods at a destination outside Canada, including tracking status from the carrier or freight forwarder
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment/FX bank record – evidence of receipt of proceeds from the non-resident purchaser, such as SWIFT MT103, PayPal/Stripe settlement report showing cross-border payment, or a bank confirmation of foreign currency repatriation

None – Canada and Quebec do not mandate real-time electronic invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) systems. Vendors may issue paper or electronic invoices. Mandatory invoice and record-keeping content includes supplier name, GST/HST/QST registration numbers, date, customer identification, description of goods, price, and tax amounts. Invoices and underlying export documentation must be retained in CRA/Revenu Québec prescribed form for at least six years; however, no API-based government clearance or pre-approval of invoices is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rating claim within the normal four-year reassessment window, extended to seven years where a taxpayer has made misrepresentation due to neglect or carelessness, and with no time limit where fraud is proven. If the export documentation is insufficient, the GST/QST previously zero-rated will be clawed back, input tax credits claimed in respect of the exported goods will be denied, and all resulting tax will be subject to statutory interest from the original filing date. Penalties include: under the Excise Tax Act, a false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD $100 and 25% of the amount misstated on a GST/HST return; a separate gross negligence penalty of a similar nature; and provincial penalties under the Quebec Act respecting the Quebec sales tax. Criminal prosecution is possible for intentional tax evasion, including under the Criminal Code and the Excise Tax Act, leading to fines and imprisonment. The partnership and, to the extent required by Quebec civil law, its general partners may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid QST; limited partners are generally protected to the extent of their unpaid capital contribution, but only where the partnership was validly registered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, partnership profit allocations, or repatriation of capital by non-residents. However, cross-border wire transfers must comply with FINTRAC reporting thresholds (10,000 CAD or more for incoming/outgoing international electronic funds transfers), and banking counterparties may impose internal sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification. The Limited Partnership is not subject to foreign exchange surrender obligations; capital account remittances are processed as normal business transactions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to compliance with Quebec income tax withholding obligations on payments to non-resident partners under the Taxation Act and Part XIII-type non-resident withholding under Canada's Income Tax Act, as applicable, plus any treaty restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds applies in Canada. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, converted freely, or used directly for trade settlement. For monitoring, Canadian financial institutions must report any incoming international electronic funds transfer of 10,000 CAD or more to FINTRAC. The enterprise should maintain a standard treasury policy for netting and conversion timing, but no time limit is imposed by the Bank of Canada or any Quebec authority.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for AML/CFT oversight. The Foreign Exchange Administration is not a standalone regulator; Canada historically deregistered exchange controls under the Canada-U.S. Free Trade Agreement era, and current policy is free floating with no currency approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium. Canada does not grant an 'Electronic Money Institution' license analogous to the EU model. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions, EU EMI or MiCA-licensed stablecoin operators) cannot obtain a Canadian payment-service license solely through that status; they may operate only as unregulated foreign payment processors if they do not hold Canadian funds, but any Canadian-facing activity involving holding funds, executing payment transactions, or operating a payment account triggers the Retail Payment Activities Act. As of the current regulatory timeline, the Bank of Canada has begun registering payment service providers. For treasury-grade liquidity clearing rails, the enterprise should rely on tier-1 chartered bank accounts; offshore EMIs may be used as supplementary collection rails for e-commerce settlement (Stripe and PayPal fallback) but not as the primary treasury account or FX hedging counterparty. Potential legal blockers include RCMP and FINTRAC registration expectations, provincial money-services business licensing under Quebec's Act respecting money-services businesses (if applicable to certain remittance services), and bank KYC policies that blacklist funds transfer from unregulated cryptocurrency-linked or non-bank EMI intermediaries.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident Limited Partners holding 25% or more of partnership interests, especially where the entity structure shows a chain of offshore companies without direct natural-person disclosure.

Root cause: Non-resident Limited Partners holding 25% or more of partnership interests, especially where the entity structure shows a chain of offshore companies without direct natural-person disclosure.

Defense evidence: Complete chain-of-title documentation: (1) certified registered partnership agreement and certificate of limited partnership from the Quebec Registraire des entreprises, (2) legal opinion from a Canadian counsel confirming pass-through ownership, (3) for each intermediate entity, the corporate register excerpt, shareholders register, and ultimate beneficial owner declaration, (4) biographical information, government-issued ID, proof of residential address, and source-of-wealth statement for each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner, and (5) recognized sanctions and politically exposed persons checks for each jurisdiction of residence.
E-commerce payment structure showing reliance on Stripe / PayPal as the only collection method, with no underlying commercial contracts or procurement documents and with funds aggregating from multiple third-party payment gateways.

Root cause: E-commerce payment structure showing reliance on Stripe / PayPal as the only collection method, with no underlying commercial contracts or procurement documents and with funds aggregating from multiple third-party payment gateways.

Defense evidence: Detailed treasury and flow diagram: (1) executed online merchant agreements with Stripe and PayPal, (2) settlement reports matching the last three months of sales to inventory purchase records, (3) issued invoices to customers or order-level transaction logs, (4) logistics contracts with third-party fulfillment centers or courier partners, (5) a board/general-partner resolution identifying that payment rails are a form of disintermediation and that the operational account is the sole concentration account.

Use of omnibus settlement accounts or interference from foreign EMI virtual accounts causing commingled funds and obscuring the identity of the original counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Quebec Limited Partnership receiving foreign capital, the compliant routing is: (1) initiating bank (in the overseas jurisdiction) sends an international wire in CAD or USD via SWIFT to the Canadian chartered bank's Montreal or Toronto clearing branch; (2) the instruction must specify the beneficiary as the Limited Partnership's full legal name and account number, with the beneficiary address being the registered office in Quebec; (3) the remitter is the partner or subscribing investor; (4) ask the foreign bank to include a clear remittance purpose code consistent with OECD CPC / SWIFT category — for capital contributions, use 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' in Field 70 (Remittance Information) or, where country-specific purpose codes are mandated by the originator's jurisdiction, the equivalent code for 'inward direct investment' or 'other investment'; (5) the Canadian receiving bank does not assign a purpose code but will require the beneficiary to complete a FINTRAC-compliant electronic funds transfer report for amounts equal to or greater than 10,000 CAD and may request a reason code; (6) transfer size, FX conversion (if any), and liquidity impact should be pre-hedged using a spot-futures ladder. To avoid delay, the LP should provide to the bank prior to the first wire: the certificate of limited partnership, taxpayer identification numbers (Quebec and federal), and a capital call resolution.For purchasing maternity and infant care commodities from overseas suppliers, the primary outbound clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 single-payment credit transfer for high-value consignments requiring immediate final payment — the MT103 is processed via the Canadian bank's correspondent network, with clearing through the U.S. where USD is used or direct CAD lines; (2) Documentary commercial letter of credit (also called a 'documentary credit') for medium-ticket steady-state purchases, using irrevocable L/C forms subject to UCP 600, issued by the bank to the supplier's confirming bank, with seller presentation of invoices, bill of lading, insurance certificate, packing list, and certificate of origin; (3) SWIFT MT700/MT707 messaging for issuance and amendments; (4) Disbursements for operating expenses (warehouse, freight, platform fees, marketing) are typically handled by same-day CDSX / Lynx clearing in CAD for domestic legs and SWIFT for international legs. For USD purchases, the Canadian bank may route through the Federal Reserve's Fedwire / CHIPS system via its U.S. correspondent. As of the current regulatory timeline, the Bank of Canada has begun registering payment service providers. For treasury-grade liquidity clearing rails, the enterprise should rely on tier-1 chartered bank accounts; offshore EMIs may be used as supplementary collection rails for e-commerce settlement (Stripe and PayPal fallback) but not as the primary treasury account or FX hedging counterparty. Potential legal blockers include RCMP and FINTRAC registration expectations, provincial money-services business licensing under Quebec's Act respecting money-services businesses (if applicable to certain remittance services), and bank KYC policies that blacklist funds transfer from unregulated cryptocurrency-linked or non-bank EMI intermediaries.
