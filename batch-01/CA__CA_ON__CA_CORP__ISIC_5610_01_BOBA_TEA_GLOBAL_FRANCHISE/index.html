E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate or register extra-provincially under the Canada Business Corporations Act (via Corporations Canada, online at ised-isde.canada.ca) or under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (via the Ontario Business Registry online); both are recognized as CA_CORP (Inc./Corp.) with limited liability. Step 2: Obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO), by filing Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, or by contacting the CRA Business Registration telephone service; the BN is the single statutory tax identifier used by the CRA and the Ontario Ministry of Finance. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian-resident Business Corporation (federal or Ontario) is a separate person and taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA); pass-through treatment is not available for corporations regardless of shareholder count or ownership. Resident status is statutorily deemed for any corporation incorporated in Canada after April 26, 1965 (ITA s.250(4)); therefore CA_CORP is a Canadian resident corporation subject to full worldwide taxation. Federal corporate tax and Ontario corporate tax apply (Ontario tax is collected by the CRA); the corporation will ordinarily qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) unless it is controlled by one or more non-residents or by public corporations, which makes it eligible for the small business deduction on first CAD 500,000 of active business income (subject to clawback rules for associated corporations and adjusted aggregate investment income). Territorial scope: worldwide income taxation for resident corporations with a foreign tax credit system; controlled foreign affiliate rules (ITA s.91-95) apply only where the Canadian corporation holds 10% or more equity in a foreign affiliate. Tax exemption zones: none apply in Ontario, except for specific enumerated statutory exemptions (e.g., municipal or federal crown entities), which are irrelevant to a boba tea franchise.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification for federal and Ontario tax purposes is governed by the ITA and the CBCA/OBCA. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (1) ITA s.85 rollover election to transfer eligible property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (2) ITA s.256(9) and s.156/156.1 elections to allocate the aggregate CCPC business limit among associated corporations; (3) ITA s.115/216 elections for non-residents to elect net-basis taxation on certain Canadian-source rental, timber, or mining income (not typically relevant to franchise royalties, which are subject to Part XIII withholding on outbound payments); (4) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.156 joint election to treat supplies between GST/HST registrants who are related or closely related as made for no consideration, enabling cash-flow simplification in the corporate group; (5) ETA s.227.2 Quick Method election for small GST/HST registrants with worldwide taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (6) ETA s.212 streamlined accounting for charities (not applicable); (7) hybrid mismatch recharacterization rules under ITA s.94B and s.94C, which may disallow deductions or include amounts in income where cross-border payments give rise to double-deduction, deduction/non-inclusion, or tax-transparent mismatches with a non-resident affiliate; (8) Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules under ITA s.18.2/18.21 and 18.23 for taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2023, which restrict net interest and financing expenses exceeding CAD 250,000 to a fixed ratio of tax EBITDA (a group-level election may alter the allocation of the group ratio); (9) elections under ITA s.159/220(2.1) for late-filed and amended elections where the CRA general anti-avoidance or waiver provisions apply. All elections must be filed with the CRA within prescribed statutory deadlines (typically three years after the filing-due date of the relevant return) to be valid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Both current-account items (franchise royalties, management fees, trade payables, and supply-import disbursements) and capital-account items (shareholder equity injections, intercompany loans, dividends, and capital repatriation) may flow freely across borders without exchange-control licensing or central-bank approval. Profit remittance is subject to ordinary corporate law, sanctions screening, FINTRAC wire-reporting thresholds, and Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act where dividends, rents, royalties, or interest are paid to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation window, or mandatory conversion ratio for export or royalty receipts. A bubble-tea franchise treasury may retain USD, CNY, or other currencies in approved accounts or convert to CAD as commercial need dictates. Records must nevertheless substantiate the commercial substance of every transaction for GST/HST, corporate income tax, customs valuation, and FINTRAC monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the issuing and central monetary authority; OSFI supervises all federally chartered Canadian banks; FINTRAC enforces AML/CTF reporting. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration or currency-control commission; foreign-exchange policy is implemented through an open, floating-rate framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for payment-orchestration and low-value remittance; Low for primary treasury settlement. Under the Bank Act, only a chartered bank or an authorized deposit-taking institution may accept deposits in Canada; offshore digital EMIs therefore cannot lawfully open a Canadian deposit account. Such platforms may operate only as licensed or registered payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and provide payment functions without banking privileges. A franchise treasury should use a Schedule I or Schedule II bank as the principal clearing bank; fintech EMIs may be used solely for store-level payout automation, franchisee reimbursements, and e-wallet fronting where the underlying funds are not treated as deposits and no derivative credit risk is taken.
