Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec - https://www.revenuquebec.ca

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity incorporating in Canada obtains a federal Business Number (BN) by registering online through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by mailing Form RC1 for a GST/HST account, payroll account, and importing/exporting account. For Quebec-specific GST/HST and QST administration, the corporation must separately register with Revenu Québec (Form RM-1 or online via Revenu Québec's business registration service) to obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and QST number. Required information typically includes the certificate of incorporation, registered office address in Canada or Quebec, full director/officer details, a Canadian resident representative for service of process, and constitutional documents. The BN and QST registration are TIN-equivalents for corporate tax input tax credit, remittance, and cross-border compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election. A Canadian business corporation is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: section 85 corporate rollover on contributions of property; section 216 election by non-residents to file a net-basis return on Canadian rental income; sections 83(2)/83(2.1) capital dividend elections; amalgamation rollover under section 87; and Quebec provincial elections mirroring federal rollover provisions. Hybrid mismatch and anti-arbitrage restrictions apply under sections 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act, limiting aggressive cross-border structured payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports under the Excise Tax Act applies to goods exported from Canada and to services supplied to non-residents that are not consumed or used in Canada. For ISIC 7020 tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory, 0% GST/QST applies when the supply is made to a non-resident corporation, the service is not performed for a person in Canada, and the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. The supplier must maintain adequate records demonstrating the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of supply, and the absence of Canadian use or enjoyment. Cross-border advisory services may also be zero-rated under the various special rules for supplies made to non-residents where the primary beneficiary is outside Canada.

  • Customs export declaration (Form B13A or CBSA-approved electronic export declaration / Canadian Automated Export Declaration system)
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Commercial invoice
  • Purchase order or service contract clearly identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Inward remittance certificate or corporate bank transfer statement evidencing offshore payment and FX repatriation
  • Relevant customs coding (HS for goods, NAICS / service description for advisory services)

No real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance, API fiscal stamping, or immediate digital stamp duty exists at the federal or Quebec level. Invoices must comply with CRA and Revenu Québec information requirements under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax, including vendor name and registration number, recipient details, date, description, and tax charged. Mandatory electronic filing and remittance applies under GST/HST for registrants with gross annual taxable supplies above C$1.5 million, and Quebec QST returns are generally required to be filed electronically through Revenu Québec's portal. Large businesses must retain electronic records and support digital audits, but there is no per-invoice government approval or clearance mechanism.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reverse zero-rating if discharge-of-export evidence is deficient, incomplete, or fraudulent. Penalties include 25% of the misstated tax claimed as gross negligence penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285, plus accrued interest; civil reassessments can claw back refunds or input tax credits for up to four years (or longer in cases of misrepresentation). Criminal prosecution for knowingly making false statements may result in fines and imprisonment, and Quebec equivalents under the Tax Administration Act impose substantial monetary penalties and personal director liability for wilful participation in false refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account transactions (intra-entity fees, interest, royalties, management charges) and capital-account flows (equity injections, shareholder loans, portfolio investments) are free from exchange-control restrictions and are not subject to pre-approval, minimum holding periods, or repatriation limits. Profit remittance is not blocked by FX law; however, outbound dividends, interest, and royalties to non-residents incur Part XIII withholding tax at treaty and domestic rates, and the legal solvency test under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act applies before any dividend payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirements. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency earnings, invoice in CAD or any freely-convertible currency, and maintain foreign-currency bank accounts with Canadian banks. There is no statutory timeline for conversion of export receipts into CAD; FX conversion is at the exporter's commercial discretion, subject to FINTRAC reporting for any cross-border wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is not centralized in a dedicated FX-control agency, but the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) collectively oversee monetary policy, AML/CTF reporting, and bank solvency/liquidity for cross-border funds flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Canada permits non-bank fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) to operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, but such entities cannot accept deposits or provide retail lending without holding a bank or trust-company license under the Bank Act or Provincial Trust and Deposit legislation. Freestanding foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise) may offer cross-border payment, FX conversion, and card issuance through Canadian MSB registration, but a Schedule I bank account is required for rigorous treasury control, capital escrow, and trade-related outbound clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound subscription proceeds from foreign parent shareholders or investors should be routed via the SWIFT network with standard Correspondent Banking messages. For payment purpose codes, use 'D' (Direct Investment) or 'G' (Equity Capital) in the SWIFT remittance field; although Canada does not assign mandatory FX purpose codes, FINTRAC requires a report for each international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. To verify the legal origin, the receiving Canadian bank will ask for the payment instruction, corporate authorization (board resolution), tax identification number of the remitter, a copy of the signed subscription/funding agreement and the Quebec corporate registry attestation showing issuance of shares or shareholder loan documentation. Routing should be direct through the bank's correspondent network to avoid intermediary banks that might reject the 'capital injection' wording; do not commingle operational revenue with capital funding in the same message.Trade and consulting disbursements are performed primarily through SWIFT MT103 (Customer Transfer) for straightforward cross-border fee payments, which must contain the legal entity identifiers, client reference, and clear invoice number. For larger procurement or mandates requiring documentary security, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is the standard clearing rail, issued pursuant to UCP 600, with the Canadian bank confirming the applicant's commitment. For recurring international tax-consulting invoices, an MT103 is the most efficient rail, accompanied by a digital copy of the invoice and signed engagement. Disbursements to Quebec and intra-group accounts can be cleared through Interac e-Transfer for small amounts or EFT via the Canadian automated clearing system (ACSS) for CAD; foreign-currency payables (USD/EUR) are cleared via correspondent banking or SWIFT RGP (Real-Time Gross Settlement) equivalents.Plain-vanilla FX Forward Contracts (CAD against USD, EUR, GBP, JPY) to lock manage consulting fee conversion flowsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies lacking active onshore delivery (e.g., certain emerging-market currencies) to hedge cross-border exposure without settling the principalCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for matched funding and multi-year tax-structuring fee settlementsVanilla Currency Options (FX Calls/Puts) for contingent capital injections, cross-border acquisitions, or treasury advisory engagements
