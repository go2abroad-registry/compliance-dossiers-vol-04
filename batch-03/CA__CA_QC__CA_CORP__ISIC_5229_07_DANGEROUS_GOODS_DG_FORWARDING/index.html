Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax administration (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency, Business Registration Online portal); Revenu Québec - Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial corporate income tax (www.revenuquebec.ca); Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) - provincial business registry (www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - import/export program accounts and customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or paper, concurrently selecting the GST/HST program account (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) accounts. Where the corporation carries on business in Quebec, it must register at REQ to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ). For QST, the corporation registers with Revenu Québec through the single-window REQ/CLICK system or Form LM-1-V, providing the NEQ, federal BN, anticipated taxable supplies, and proof of Quebec permanent establishment. Non-resident corporations must also file Form RC1410 (information return for non-resident corporations) and may require a section 116 certificate for dispositions of taxable Canadian property. The DG hazmat forwarder additionally must obtain a CBSA carrier/forwarder identifier and comply with Transport Canada TDG registration and dangerous goods permit requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act is a taxable Canadian corporation; resident corporations are subject to federal Part I tax and Quebec Taxation Act tax on worldwide income; corporations are not eligible for pass-through/check-the-box treatment. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source business income under Part I and Part XIII withholding. Quebec levies an additional capital tax on financial institutions only; ordinary DG freight forwarding corporations are outside that scope. No tax-exempt-zone status applies in Quebec.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (i) ITA section 85 tax-deferred rollover of eligible property to a corporation (Form T2057); (ii) ITA section 256.1 share-for-share exchange election; (iii) ITA section 88 amalgamation/winding-up elections; (iv) ETA section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, with Quebec QSTA section 392.1 mirror election; (v) ITA section 261 functional-currency reporting election; (vi) treaty-based hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.2-18.4 (OECD BEPS Action 2) that deny deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements. Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime; entity classification is statutory under the ITA and ETA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over any consecutive four calendar quarters (GST/HST small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148; QST threshold aligned, QSTA s. 414). A DG freight forwarder engaged in cross-border transportation ordinarily exceeds this threshold and must register; voluntary registration is available for zero-rated exporters to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V to: (i) supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier (s. 1); (ii) services performed wholly outside Canada (s. 7) or in respect of exported goods (s. 9); (iii) transportation services of goods from Canada to a destination outside Canada and freight-van/freight-forwarding services forming part of such international movement (ss. 16-17); and (iv) supplies of freight and customs brokerage services for exported goods where the recipient is non-resident. For DG hazmat forwarding, the 0% GST/HST and 0% QST rate applies where the supplier obtains documentary evidence of export within the prescribed period (generally two years from the filing due date of the return), including proof that the dangerous goods were physically delivered to a foreign consignee. QST zero-rating mirrors the ETA under QSTA Schedule IV.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED) or electronic EDI export reporting
  • Commercial invoice with HS codes, UN number, and IMO/ICAO dangerous goods classification
  • Bill of Lading (Sea Waybill / Air Waybill AWB) or multimodal transport document evidencing foreign destination
  • Cargo manifest and carrier proof of exit (port clearance, customs stamp, border crossing record)
  • Signed FIATA/forwarder contract of carriage with non-resident consignee and incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing settlement of the export invoice in foreign currency
  • Packing list, shipper's declaration for dangerous goods (DGD), and IMDG/IATA compliance certificates

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force as of 2025. CRA supports voluntary e-invoicing through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network and requires electronic filing (NETFILE/GST34) for registrants; electronic records must be retained in readable format per ETA ss. 286-286.1. Quebec has announced a phased mandatory e-invoicing mandate for B2B transactions (expected post-2026) and currently requires EDI/e-filing for large enterprises. For DG freight forwarding, CBSA mandates electronic advance commercial information (ACI/eManifest) for all modes and electronic customs export declarations (B13A via EDI) with UN/IMO dangerous goods data.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment and input tax credits retroactively where export documentation is insufficient or fraudulent, with standard reassessment periods of four years (ETA s. 296(6)) extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation. Penalties include: (i) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax, plus 6% per annum interest (ETA ss. 285.1 and 280); (ii) fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for fraudulent rebate/credit claims (ETA s. 327.1); (iii) QST false-statement penalties up to 100% of the amount claimed (QSTA s. 991.1); (iv) CBCA/QBCA director liability for unremitted taxes; (v) CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 25,000 per violation for export misdeclaration under the Customs Act; and (vi) suspension of GST/HST account, mandatory security posting, and publication of taxpayer penalties under CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program rejection. DG substances exported without valid TDG documentation attract additional penalties under the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992 (up to CAD 50,000 for individuals and CAD 250,000 for corporations per offence).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has no foreign exchange control statute, no restriction on profit or capital repatriation, and does not require central bank approval for inbound equity, loan, or treasury transactions. The Investment Canada Act imposes notification or review obligations solely for acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses, not for the movement of funds. Quebec-level corporate registration and Canadian tax residency for the Quebec subsidiary are the only practical hurdles before commencement of dividends or loan repayments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-period obligation on export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in offshore or onshore accounts. However, institutions must report cross-border electronic fund transfers exceeding CA$10,000 to FINTRAC; cash transactions above CA$10,000 trigger large cash transaction reports. The Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, S.C. 1992, c. 34, and IATA DGR impose documentary compliance on cargo but no FX rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Currency Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52, together with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange policy and the OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for prudential bank supervision; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; in Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises money service businesses.
