VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15.47375%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. The federal GST/HST small-supplier threshold applies to taxable supplies of CAD 30,000 worldwide under sections 123 and 148 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA); the QST threshold for supplies made in Quebec is also CAD 30,000 under section 415(9) of the Quebec Sales Tax Act. Zero-rated supplies count toward the threshold, while exempt supplies (including most factoring services treated as financial services under Schedule V Part VII ETA) do not. For factoring entities whose supplies are exclusively exempt financial services, registration is voluntary rather than mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V ETA, subject to documentary proof of export; services are zero-rated when performed wholly or substantially outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident. Financial services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated under Part VII item 21 of Schedule VI ETA if the service is a regulated financial service or is made in respect of a non-resident's financial instrument, and the service does not relate to Canadian real property, Canadian tangible personal property, or a supply of a service rendered in Canada. Reverse-factoring fees charged to a non-resident obligor or an offshore special purpose vehicle may therefore qualify for zero-rating, whereas factoring fees charged to a Canadian resident exporter on domestic receivables remain exempt and confer no input tax credit entitlement.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API validation, or digital stamping regime applies in Canada or Quebec. Invoicing and record retention follow ETA section 223 and Quebec Taxation Act section 321, with a six-year retention period. The Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol network is voluntary; CRA and Revenu Québec have adopted interoperability standards but have not imposed a continuous transaction control requirement. GST/HST and QST remittances are periodic return-based filings, and certain registrants must file electronically using CRA GSTRET and RQ-certified transmitter software.

Canada imposes no literal 180-day statutory FX repatriation deadline; however, export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days will attract heightened CRA audit scrutiny as an indicator of export documentation insufficiency or engineered hybrid financing. False or unsupported export zero-rating claims trigger: (i) ETA subsection 280(1)(c) gross negligence penalties of 25% of the net tax deficiency; (ii) criminal prosecution under ETA section 281 with penalties up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment; (iii) Customs Act and EIPA sanctions including penalties up to CAD 25,000 per false export report and seizure of goods; (iv) clawback of input tax credits and rebates with interest at CRA prescribed rates (basic rate plus 4% for corporations); and (v) an extension of the normal four-year reassessment window in cases of misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations, with no prior approval required for current or capital transactions, no surrender of export proceeds, and no Quebec-specific restrictions on outbound dividend, interest, royalty, factoring-settlement, or capital repatriation. For a Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite) undertaking trade-supply-chain factoring, profits and capital may be freely remitted to foreign partners and creditors. The only statutory frictions are tax-compliance certificates (Revenu Québec attestation de conformité fiscale) when contracting with public bodies or prior to large distributions, plus FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting at or above CAD 10,000 and normal treaty-based withholding refills.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951 via repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act, and there is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts or factoring collections. A Canadian LP may hold multi-currency operating and escrow accounts at Schedule I banks, invoice foreign obligors in USD/EUR/RMB and retain balances offshore or onshore at its discretion without Bank of Canada or OSFI conversion approval. Factoring clients must, however, comply with general FINTRAC record-keeping and EFTR thresholds and ensure trade-based money-laundering red flags are mitigated through underlying invoice and shipping documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and note-issuing authority; however, Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency. Residual exchange-control authority rests with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act, while prudential supervision of authorized deposit-taking institutions is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Clearing and settlement infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada (Lynx for high-value CAD, the Real-Time Rail for immediate payments), and FINTRAC administers AML/ATF and cross-border wire reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not issue a UK-style EMI (Electronic Money Institution) charter; fintech payment providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex must register as money-services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC but are not CDIC-insured deposit takers and lack direct Lynx/SWIFT settlement capacity. They are viable only as secondary disbursement or FX-conversion channels for small value trade payments, not for capital escrow, factoring collection, or high-value settlement, which should be anchored at a prime Schedule I bank to preserve CDIC coverage, regulatory credibility, and counterparty acceptance for commercial letters of credit.

Canada does not issue a UK-style EMI (Electronic Money Institution) charter; fintech payment providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex must register as money-services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC but are not CDIC-insured deposit takers and lack direct Lynx/SWIFT settlement capacity. They are viable only as secondary disbursement or FX-conversion channels for small value trade payments, not for capital escrow, factoring collection, or high-value settlement, which should be anchored at a prime Schedule I bank to preserve CDIC coverage, regulatory credibility, and counterparty acceptance for commercial letters of credit.

Turnover: No statutory independent audit is compulsory for a Quebec limited partnership as such. An audit obligation arises only where (i) the partnership agreement or unanimous partner consent mandates it, (ii) a lender, factor, or investor covenant requires audited financial statements, (iii) the limited partnership is a reporting issuer under Canadian securities legislation, or (iv) a specific regulator such as the Autorité des marchés financiers imposes an auditor's report under an applicable provincial lending statute. A private commercial trade-factoring limited partnership in Quebec will not normally meet any mandatory-audit trigger.
