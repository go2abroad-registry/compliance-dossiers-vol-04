Virtual office services providing only a mailing address are not permissible as the statutory registered office (siége social) for a licensed dangerous-goods battery safety testing laboratory in Quebec. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online portals, administers the federal business number, corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and information-return programs. Revenu Quebec administers Quebec income tax for corporations, Quebec sales tax (QST), employer-source deductions, and related Quebec filings through clicSÉQUR and its online services. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the applicable certificate of incorporation and corporate registry number. 2. Apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or the applicable non-resident registration process. 3. Open the relevant CRA program accounts, including RC corporate income tax and RT GST/HST; payroll and information-return accounts are opened when applicable. 4. Register with Revenu Quebec for Quebec corporate income tax and, where required, QST, employer source deductions, and other Quebec tax programs. 5. Register for CERS access and obtain the required export reporting credentials if the entity exports commercial goods. 6. A non-resident corporation generally provides incorporation documents, legal name, registered address, directors, authorized representative information, business activities, fiscal year-end, Canadian activities, and supporting identity documents. GST/HST registration may be mandatory when the non-resident is carrying on commercial activities in Canada and exceeds the small-supplier threshold, and may be voluntary below the threshold. QST registration may be mandatory where the entity carries on business in Quebec and meets the applicable QST rules. There is no single Canadian VAT identification number; the GST/HST registration number is generally the BN followed by the RT program identifier, while a Quebec QST account is separately administered by Revenu Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for corporations. A Canadian corporation is generally a separate taxpayer, while partnerships are generally fiscally transparent and trusts are subject to separate statutory rules. Available elections may include subsection 85(1) or 85(2) rollover elections for qualifying transfers of property to a corporation, section 86 share-reorganization elections, amalgamation and wind-up rules, functional-currency reporting elections for qualifying corporations, and specified GST/HST or QST elections. Canada has enacted hybrid-mismatch rules that can deny deductions or exempt amounts in specified cross-border arrangements; these are statutory anti-avoidance mechanisms rather than elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, generally measured over the relevant single-quarter and preceding four-quarter periods, is the principal small-supplier threshold for GST/HST registration. Quebec generally applies a CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST purposes, subject to statutory rules and special registration obligations. Certain non-resident suppliers, taxi and commercial ride services, and other specified businesses may be required to register under special rules. A business below the threshold may register voluntarily and must generally remain registered for the applicable minimum period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may generally be zero-rated for GST and, where applicable, QST when supplied for export and the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or transfer outside Canada or Quebec and retention of documentary evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify for zero-rating where the applicable statutory conditions are met, but exclusions apply, including services relating to Canadian real property, certain tangible personal property situated in Canada, litigation or dispute proceedings, and services performed in respect of goods or activities in Canada where the legislation excludes zero-rating. For a battery-safety and UN38.3 testing laboratory, testing performed in Canada for a non-resident customer is not automatically zero-rated; the customer status, contractual recipient, location and use of the testing, connection with Canadian goods or property, and all statutory exclusions must be reviewed. Input tax credits or QST input tax refunds generally require valid tax documentation and a direct connection with commercial activities.

  • Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration or other applicable CBSA export-reporting record
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, consignee, description, quantity, value, currency, terms of sale, and destination
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier transport document
  • Packing list and shipment records
  • Proof of export or delivery outside Canada, including carrier confirmation, customs exit evidence, or destination import documentation where available
  • Sales contract, purchase order, testing agreement, and customer records establishing the non-resident recipient and contractual scope
  • Certificate of origin or other origin document where required by the destination country or a trade agreement
  • Inward remittance or bank-payment evidence where relevant to commercial substantiation; Canadian GST/QST law does not impose a universal requirement for a specific inward-remittance certificate as a condition of zero-rating

Canada and Quebec do not generally require real-time electronic invoice clearance, government pre-approval, or digital tax stamping for ordinary domestic B2B supplies. Registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed GST/HST and QST information, maintain reliable electronic books and records, and retain supporting documents for the statutory retention period. CRA and Revenu Quebec generally permit electronic records and electronic filing. Certain tax returns, elections, payments, and information returns may be subject to mandatory electronic filing based on taxpayer category, filing volume, or statutory rules. Commercial exports are reported electronically to CBSA through CERS when reporting is required; CERS is a customs reporting platform and is not a VAT invoice-clearance system.

Canada has no general mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal 180-day repatriation rule. CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess GST/QST where zero-rating, input tax credits, or refunds are unsupported, including tax that should have been collected, denied credits or refunds, arrears interest, and applicable penalties. False statements or omissions may attract a penalty generally based on the tax understated or benefit overstated; gross-negligence penalties can generally be 50% of the relevant tax amount or benefit, subject to the governing statute. CRA may reassess beyond the ordinary limitation period in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud. Fraudulent conduct may result in civil penalties and criminal prosecution, including fines and imprisonment. Incorrect or late export reporting may also trigger CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties, seizure or detention consequences, and customs reassessment. Revenu Quebec applies comparable assessment, interest, penalty, and prosecution mechanisms for QST and Quebec tax violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating exchange rate and full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control legislation restricts the inflow or outflow of capital, the repatriation of dividends, or the remittance of profits to non-resident shareholders. However, all cross-border electronic fund transfers of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Banking documentation must articulate the source and purpose of funds for any large inbound remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters are not required to surrender export proceeds or convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars within any statutory timeline. Export proceeds may be held in any freely convertible currency. The only obligation is record-keeping and reporting under the PCMLTFA and the Income Tax Act for tax purposes, including the disclosure of foreign reporting entities and foreign bank accounts where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium as a supplementary payment rail only. Wise is registered in Canada as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and is permitted for low-value cross-border client payments, refunds, and vendor disbursements. However, Wise and other offshore Electronic Money Institutions are not chartered banks and do not offer Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. They cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing account for capital escrow, debt funding, or large trade settlements. An offshore EMI without a Canadian MSB registration is legally prohibited from actively soliciting or providing payment services to Canadian residents; it may only be used if the account is opened outside Canada with no Canadian nexus.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner or director is resident in, or connected to, a high-risk, sanctioned, or FATF grey-listed jurisdiction.

Root cause: Beneficial owner or director is resident in, or connected to, a high-risk, sanctioned, or FATF grey-listed jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Obtain and submit: (1) current corporate registry extracts from the home jurisdiction, (2) notarized source-of-wealth declarations, (3) three years of audited financial statements or bank reference letters from a regulated financial institution, (4) a complete chain-of-title showing all intermediate entities between the ultimate beneficial owner and the Canadian entity, and (5) legal opinion from a qualified counsel confirming no sanctions nexus.
Unclear source of funds for the initial capital injection or capital contribution.

Root cause: Unclear source of funds for the initial capital injection or capital contribution.

Defense evidence: Provide: (1) board resolution authorizing the capital injection, (2) share subscription or shareholder loan agreement, (3) bank statements from the remitting account demonstrating provenance of funds, (4) audited financial statements or accountant's letter attesting to the financial capacity of the parent, and (5) tax clearances/certificates of residence for the parent entity.
Cross-border wire pattern inconsistent with stated business scope as a battery safety testing laboratory.

Root cause: Cross-border wire pattern inconsistent with stated business scope as a battery safety testing laboratory.

Use of a foreign electronic money institution (e.g., Wise) for repeated cross-border transactions without a documented treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For foreign direct investment into the Quebec operating entity, the compliant routing is an inbound SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor's bank to a Canadian dollar clearing account at a Schedule I bank. The payment must reference the purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment in Canada' and include the beneficiary account name, the corporate account number, and a concise payment narrative such as 'Share subscription – Battery Safety UN38.3 Testing Lab Capital Escrow'. All inbound transfers of C$10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reporting. The Canadian recipient bank will verify the source of funds and the underlying board resolution. For investments exceeding a prescribed threshold, additional documentation under the Investment Canada Act may be required if the transaction is notifiable; a thorough legal review should confirm whether Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada notification is necessary.Outbound payments for trade settlements must be executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers using the primary Canadian bank account for high-value transactions. For purchases of precision test instruments and calibration equipment from overseas suppliers, a Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700 series) is the preferred clearing rail where the supplier requires payment assurance. Low-value recurring disbursements such as client refunds, calibration lab fees, or software subscriptions may be settled through Wise, subject to the board-approved treasury policy and FINTRAC compliance limits. All outbound wires must include proper purpose codes, underlying invoices or service contracts, and beneficial-owner information.
