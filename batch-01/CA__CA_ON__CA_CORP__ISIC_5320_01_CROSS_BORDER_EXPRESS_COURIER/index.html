Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); filing portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation engaged in cross-border express courier services must obtain a 15-digit CRA Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN and GST/HST registration identifier. Procedure: (1) complete Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', or use CRA Business Registration Online where electronic access is available; (2) select both the GST/HST program account (RC) and, because goods are imported and exported, the import/export account (RM); (3) indicate non-resident ownership details if applicable and confirm whether the corporation is resident in Canada for income tax purposes; (4) CRA will issue the BN and the GST/HST registration effective date; (5) maintain the CRA confirmation notice, including the BN program account suffix, for customs entries, tax invoices, and CBSA export reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Canada does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' or elective entity classification regime. A corporation incorporated federally or in Ontario is automatically treated as an entity-level taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act, and no election can cause the corporation to be disregarded or pass through to shareholders. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed by mandatory denial rules, not elective mechanisms. Specialized elections such as the Income Tax Act section 216 net-basis election for non-residents with Canadian rental or timber income are available but do not alter corporate entity classification.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 of total taxable supplies from commercial activities, measured on a trailing four-calendar-quarter basis, exclusive of GST/HST; once this threshold is exceeded, a courier operator making taxable supplies in Canada must register for GST/HST within 29 days and charge 13% HST on supplies made in Ontario.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
A GST/HST-registered Ontario courier may apply 0% HST to: (i) supplies of goods exported from Canada by the supplier or by a common carrier, where the goods are exported before use or consumption in Canada and the supplier possesses acceptable proof of export; (ii) international freight transportation services, including time-definite express courier services, where the transportation forms part of a continuous international movement of goods or is provided to a non-resident recipient; and (iii) services performed outside Canada for a non-resident person. If a parcel is delivered to an address in Canada or is otherwise released into the Canadian market before export, the supply is taxable at 13% HST. To substantiate a zero-rated treatment and any corresponding refund of input tax credits, the courier must retain contemporaneous commercial and customs evidence linking the supply to an actual export transaction.

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or a central tax authority invoice-approval requirement analogous to certain Latin American or EU systems. Electronic tax invoices are valid if they are legible and contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, CRA Business Number, date, description, GST/HST rate, and total amount. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or approved CRA software. For export goods, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) mandates electronic presentation of export declarations through the Commercial Export Reporting System (CERS), and high-volume courier operators may integrate with CBSA electronic data interchange or authorized service providers. CRA and CBSA systems operate at transaction and declaration level, not invoice clearance level.

      
CRA and CBSA may conduct joint export-verification audits of courier operators claiming zero-rated export treatment. If export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable at the time of audit, CRA may reassess the supply at 13% HST, disallow input tax credits, and charge interest from the original return due date. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of consideration for registration and return failures, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty for false statements or omissions giving rise to understated tax. In fraudulent cases, criminal tax evasion prosecution can result in substantial fines and imprisonment. The standard four-year GST/HST reassessment period does not protect a taxpayer where the export claim is tainted by misrepresentation, willful default, or fraud, allowing CRA to claw back historical zero-rating and refund positions retrospectively.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both capital and current accounts. There is no prior-approval restriction on dividend repatriation, service fees, royalties, interest, or proceeds from an Ontario-resident corporation. Capital and profit remittances remain subject to Part XIII withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% non-treaty, reduced to 5% on intercorporate dividends and 15% on non-dividend payments under an applicable tax treaty), plus Form NR4/NR6 reporting. No monthly remittance cap or exchange-control permission is imposed; however, large cross-border wire transfers are reportable to FINTRAC when equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and non-resident controlling investors remain subject to Investment Canada Act review thresholds.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts earned by Canadian exporters. Exporters of goods or services, including cross-border express courier companies, may invoice and settle in foreign currency and retain such proceeds abroad, subject only to FINTRAC reporting requirements for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and to ordinary tax attribution rules under Canadian anti-avoidance provisions. No statutory repatriation deadline applies.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF surveillance. Canada has no centralized foreign exchange control administration; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no general exchange-control licence is required.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium-to-low for an unlicensed off-shore digital EMI used as the primary treasury account; high for a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB or an EMI partnering with a Schedule I Canadian bank. Legal constraints: an offshore digital EMI without Canadian retail-payment registration cannot lawfully provide 'retail payment activities' to Canadian users under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), does not benefit from CDIC deposit insurance, and may not clear through Payments Canada's core rails. A compliant structure is to maintain the primary treasury account with a Schedule I bank and use an EMI only for FX conversion and cross-border settlement through a Canadian-regulated custodian.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank account, preferably the operational or capital-escrow account, using SWIFT MT103. The wire reference field (field 70) should state 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – [EXACT CORPORATE NAME] – NON-TRADE CAPITAL' or 'PAID-IN CAPITAL FOR ORDINARY SHARES' to avoid customs or trade-misclassification. The receiving bank should receive an executed board resolution, a signed share-subscription agreement, the Ontario corporate registry extract, UBO identity and source-of-wealth evidence, and a funds-origin statement. For transfers above CAD 10,000, the bank must file or the entity must facilitate a FINTRAC EFT report; in additional due diligence, the bank may request a legal opinion confirming that the capital contribution is non-taxable for GST/HST and not blocked under Investment Canada Act review rules.Outbound trade-related disbursements for courier and logistics settlements should clear via Payments Canada Lynx (for high-value CAD same-day settlement), SWIFT MT103 (for USD, EUR, and other cross-currency wires), and commercial letters of credit (under UCP 600 through a confirming Canadian bank) when a shipment or supply contract requires documentary payment protection. Every outbound MT103 must cite the underlying service invoice, the OSFI/FINTRAC-compliant purpose narrative, and, for related-party payments, the applicable transfer-pricing file. ACH Direct is acceptable for recurring domestic CAD disbursements such as carrier subcontractors and customs-bond fees; international settlement should not use ACH rails except for low-value recurring payments cleared via a FINTRAC-registered payment service provider.Plain-vanilla OTC foreign-exchange forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) documented under a Canadian bank ISDA Master Agreement or master forward confirmationNondeliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted emerging-market currencies executed through the offshore booking centre of a Canadian Schedule I bank, limited to genuine commercial exposureCross-currency swaps (CAD/USD or CAD/EUR) for long-term debt or asset-liability hedging with settlement risk approved by the counterparty credit departmentPremium-bearing FX options and zero-cost collars/participating forwards used for budget-rate protection on freight and courier revenue, provided the underlying exposure is documentedFX swaps/rollover swaps for short-term intercompany funding, executed under a recognized master agreement and subject to regular credit valuation adjustment reporting
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-low for an unlicensed off-shore digital EMI used as the primary treasury account; high for a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB or an EMI partnering with a Schedule I Canadian bank. Legal constraints: an offshore digital EMI without Canadian retail-payment registration cannot lawfully provide 'retail payment activities' to Canadian users under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), does not benefit from CDIC deposit insurance, and may not clear through Payments Canada's core rails. A compliant structure is to maintain the primary treasury account with a Schedule I bank and use an EMI only for FX conversion and cross-border settlement through a Canadian-regulated custodian.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if the aggregate value of cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds CAD 1 million (for services) or CAD 2 million (for tangible goods) in a taxation year. Additionally, a Country-by-Country Report is required if consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act applies to transactions lacking genuine economic substance. Additionally, treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) if the main purpose is to obtain treaty benefits. For cross-border courier operations, substance is evidenced by physical presence, employees, and operational assets in Canada.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty) plus interest; criminal prosecution under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax withholdings (e.g., payroll, GST/HST) under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. For tax evasion, directors can face criminal charges, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment if they participated in or authorized the evasion.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disallowing tax benefits, plus penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (Section 245). Treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in the Multilateral Instrument (MLI), leading to full taxation at statutory rates.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes reassessed under GAAR if they were involved in the planning or implementation of the abusive transaction. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges for aiding in tax evasion.

      

      
      

        AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 1 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals. For courier services, failure to report cross-border shipments of currency or monetary instruments can trigger seizure of funds.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AMPs if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement AML compliance measures. They may face criminal charges, travel bans, and imprisonment for money laundering offenses.
