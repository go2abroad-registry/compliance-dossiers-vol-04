Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital or minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares are issued for consideration authorized by the board and recorded in the corporation’s securities register. No statutory escrow account is required.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing or industrial clearance is ordinarily required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration via the CRA Business Portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency); Revenu Québec (RQ) — provincial corporation tax, Quebec Sales Tax (QST), payroll source deductions, and Quebec enterprise registration via the Registraire des entreprises portal (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca and revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (if federal) or the Quebec Business Corporations Act (if provincial) and obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) from the Registraire des entreprises. Step 2: Register with the CRA for a 15-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: 'RC' for federal corporate income tax, 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RP' for payroll deduction, and 'RQ' or 'RM' for non-resident withholding where applicable. Step 3: Register with Revenu Québec for the QST account (required if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000) and, once the Ontario or Quebec payroll obligations arise, open source-deduction accounts. Step 4: A non-resident-owned corporation carrying on business in Canada must file federal T2 and Quebec CO-17 annual returns; GST/HST and QST registration is compulsory before invoicing Canadian clients unless the small-supplier threshold (CAD 30,000 worldwide) remains unmet.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident corporation is a separate person taxed on worldwide net income under federal Part I tax and Quebec Part I tax, with no pass-through or partnership look-through; a non-resident-owned corporation carrying on business in Quebec is also subject to Quebec and federal branch tax (Part XIV, 25% rate subject to treaty reduction) and branches into Quebec QST nexus as a registrant.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; corporations are per se separate taxable entities for domestic purposes. Available elections relevant to the ASO/SEO localization agency: (i) functional-currency election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s.261 where the Canadian corporation computes income in USD; (ii) capital dividend account election under ITA s.83(2) for private corporations; (iii) section 85 rollover election on incorporation of an unincorporated business; (iv) GST/HST election under Excise Tax Act s.211 (nil consideration for closely related corporations); note that foreign hybrid-mismatch elections have no domestic force and ITA ss.18.4-18.6 automatically deny or recharacterize deductions on hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.14975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports and capital property) before GST/HST and QST registration becomes mandatory; registrants may voluntarily register earlier, and a non-resident with a permanent establishment in Quebec or invoicing Canadian clients in respect of Quebec-based assets or services generally requires registration regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for zero-rating under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI Part V when exported by the supplier and substantiated by adequate evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s.7 when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and who does not acquire the service for use in a Canadian business; the service must not relate to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply, or an individual present in Canada. ASO campaign management, multilingual SEO localization, and App Store optimization delivered to a foreign app developer or offshore publisher normally satisfy the s.7 non-resident service test provided the supporting contract identifies the foreign recipient, the deliverables are performed outside a Canadian permanent establishment, and no Canadian property or Canadian-resident end-user is the direct subject matter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or authorized online export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System) for tangible goods exceeding the CRA/StatsCan reporting thresholds
  • Signed commercial invoice or executed service agreement naming the non-resident recipient and describing the offshore ASO/SEO localization deliverables
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier cargo manifest for physical exports, or delivery and campaign performance records for digital services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/PSP statements (SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B, Payoneer transaction reports) evidencing payment from abroad in foreign currency
  • Foreign customs entry documentation and any third-party verification (Google Play/App Store publisher records) demonstrating the export was completed outside Canada

CRA operates no real-time fiscal clearance, no API stamping mandate, and no mandatory e-invoicing at the federal level; EDI and PDF invoices are acceptable if retained in readable form. Revenu Québec has announced a phased mandatory B2B electronic-invoicing framework beginning from the 2025-2026 transition period for large enterprises, but as of the current compliance vintage no Quebec production mandate or central clearance stamping is in force; exporters must nevertheless preserve full digital audit trails, original billing metadata, and six-year retention records.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day value is the standard tax-engine risk ordinal, while the legal retention rule is six years. CRA and Revenu Québec aggressively audit zero-rated export claims by ASO/SEO agencies; if the exporter cannot produce contemporaneous non-resident service evidence or proof of physical export, the zero rate is reversed retroactively, yielding GST/HST at 5% plus QST at 9.975%, statutory interest, and penalties. ETA s.280 imposes a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; ETA s.327 creates summary and indictable offences with fines up to CAD 20,000 and imprisonment up to five years for fraudulent evasion; ITA s.163(2) adds a 50% penalty for false statements, and Revenu Québec applies parallel QST penalties. The normal CRA reassessment window is four years, extended to seven years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, and unlimited for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital account items; Canada has accepted the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange-control restrictions on dividend, profit, interest, or royalty remittances, nor on capital repatriation. The operative frictions are fiscal rather than exchange-control based: Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible) on dividends and similar remittances to non-residents, plus FINTRAC reporting thresholds for cross-border currency movements, virtual-currency transfers, and large cash transactions generally exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law and Quebec regulation impose no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export or service receipts. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely, manage them through domestic or foreign bank accounts, and hedge freely. Canadian-dollar income reporting and functional-currency tax elections are governed by the Income Tax Act, while Export Development Canada (EDC) provides trade finance and risk-mitigation support without exchange-control conditions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; Canada maintains a liberalized foreign-exchange administration regime with no dedicated exchange-control agency comparable to a SAFE-style authority. Bank supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while cross-border AML/reporting oversight is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High operational viability for Wise B2B and Payoneer as supplementary treasury and settlement rails, but legally constrained by their status as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC rather than Schedule I/II Canadian chartered banks. Funds held on these platforms do not constitute CDIC-insured bank deposits; they are custodial or agent balances reconciled against partner-bank accounts. Recommended architecture: use Wise B2B for multi-currency vendor payouts, non-resident contractor settlement, and USD collection sub-accounts; use Payoneer for marketplace/ad-network receiving and disbursement; maintain the capital escrow, statutory records, and primary CAD liquidity with a Tier-1 domestic bank to satisfy OSFI expectations, audit requirements, and counterparty risk standards.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank instructions) remitted from the foreign shareholder to the Canadian operating or capital escrow account. Remittance purpose must state 'Foreign direct investment — share capital contribution' to align with Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada FDI classification. The foreign shareholder must file the applicable Investment Canada Act notification with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) where a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business above the prescribed value thresholds; otherwise a simple post-establishment notification is required. The Canadian receiving bank attributes the purpose code, records beneficial ownership, and submits a Large Cash/Cross-Border Transaction Report to FINTRAC for transfers at or above CAD 10,000. The issuer must contemporaneously update the corporate share register, allocate contributed surplus, and preserve director resolutions authorizing the issuance.Supplier and trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for final irrevocable remittances, supported by invoice and contract references. Purchase-order-backed transactions may be structured through documentary commercial letters of credit initiated via SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707, with document examination performed at the negotiating bank. Domestic CAD legs clear through Payments Canada's Lynx (LVTS) real-time gross settlement system, while USD legs settle over SWIFT/CHIPS rails. Wise B2B sub-accounts are cleared for vendor, contractor, and multi-currency payouts at reduced FX cost; Payoneer is used for ad-network and marketplace receiving/disbursement.
