Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Business Registration Online portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation conducting Canadian commercial activity must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1/WTB. If taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, the applicant must simultaneously register for a GST/HST account. Foreign entities need to provide articles of incorporation, registered address, directors' details, and describe economic activities; for Ontario additional registration via the Ontario Business Registry may be required for provincial tax accounts (e.g., Employer Health Tax and Workplace Safety and Insurance). Upon approval, CRA issues a 9-digit BN, GST/HST registration number (RT), and customs import/export account (RM) for cross-border trade.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporation taxed on Canadian-source income with territorial limitation) — A non-resident corporation conducting Canadian commercial activity must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1/WTB. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporation taxed on Canadian-source income with territorial limitation)

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. Relevant elections include: ITA s. 216 net-basis election for non-resident rental income; ITA s. 217 election to pay Canadian tax on non-resident employment income; treaty-based relief elections; GST/HST annual remittance election for small suppliers; quarterly/monthly filing options; and hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments under ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, and 20.2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) per calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register upon exceeding this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible personal property qualifies as a zero-rated supply under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if it is exported as or as part of a service and documentary proof of export is retained (e.g., shipment leaves Canada within 60 days of invoice). Supplies of services to non-residents are zero-rated where the recipient is not in Canada at the time the service is performed, subject to exclusions for services in respect of Canadian real property, goods in Canada, and certain advisory or consulting services. For a wholesale dried botanical export operation, the sale of goji berries and herbal botanicals to non-resident buyers will generally be zero-rated if title passes and goods are shipped abroad with supporting evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A via CBSA CAED/eCCF)
  • Bill of Lading or Through-Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing consignee abroad
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing foreign currency repatriation

No real-time e-invoicing/clearance mandate or digital stamping for GST/HST. Registrants must issue valid invoices containing supplier's BN, date, description, and tax amounts; electronic invoices are permitted if authentic and subject to integrity controls. CBSA customs documentary clearance for exports requires electronic export declarations (B13A via CAED/eCCF or portal) and compliance with eManifest rules; no API-based fiscal stamping is required.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply as taxable where the prescribed export documentation is missing or falsified, resulting in collection of GST/HST plus retroactive interest. Civil penalties include 25% of the net tax deficiency for false statements or wilful misrepresentation, plus gross negligence penalties; repeated evasion or aiding fraud under Excise Tax Act s. 327.1(1) may be prosecuted as an indictable offense with imprisonment of up to 5 years. Retrospective clawback can apply to all substantially similar transactions in the audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, the Canadian dollar floats freely, and no prior regulatory approval is required for cross-border capital movements or profit remittances. Dividend or interest remittances to non-resident shareholders remain subject solely to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act withholding tax (25% statutory, typically reduced to 5%-15% under a relevant tax treaty); no FX licensing, no repatriation requirement, and no convertibility restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Export proceeds derived from dried goji berry and herbal wholesale sales are not subject to statutory surrender, mandatory conversion into CAD, or any repatriation deadline. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts. The only applicable obligations are statistical filings to Statistics Canada (Balance of Payments reporting) and FINTRAC reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) for transactions at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) for currency issuance and monetary policy; the Department of Finance (Minister of Finance) together with the Bank of Canada acting as fiscal agent administers the Foreign Exchange Reserves Management Framework; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises all federally incorporated banks. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control authority and no exchange control legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium as an auxiliary payment corridor. A foreign fintech EMI or digital cross-border payments provider must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA before offering services to a Canadian client; unregistered EMIs are prohibited from holding client funds in Canada under the Bank Act. Licensed providers (e.g., Wise and Payoneer operating under FINTRAC MSB registration) may support sub-CAD 50,000 supplier payouts and retail-origin receipts, but they lack Lynx (LVTS) settlement membership, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot provide capital escrow accounts or deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards of institutional size.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate (15% federal general rate after federal tax abatement plus 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Although the tax is federal in form, it is a single 25% rate and is not augmented by a separate Ontario withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The balance-due date is three months after the fiscal year-end for most Canadian-controlled private corporations and two months after the fiscal year-end for large corporations. A nil-revenue corporation must still file the annual T2.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian law does not prescribe a fixed monetary Local File or Master File threshold. Under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act, resident corporations must keep contemporaneous records that permit the arm's-length nature of each cross-border non-arm's-length transaction to be verified. Form T106 must be filed by Canadian corporations when the total value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. The CRA applies OECD-style documentation expectations on a risk-based basis, and the subsection 247(3) transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment is available where the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts and the adjustment is significant.

Economic substance scope: Canada is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the Gulf-Caribbean sense, but economic substance is enforced through the sham doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the OECD principal purpose test under the Multilateral Instrument, and CRA transfer-pricing practice. A goji berry export corporation operating in Ontario must demonstrate real business substance: physical procurement or quality-control operations, actual inventory or supply contracts, local employees, board decision-making in Canada, and bank accounts through which commercial risks are managed. A letterbox Canadian entity used to route profits to a zero-tax or low-tax intermediary while delegating all commercial functions offshore is a high-risk arrangement subject to GAAR, treaty-benefit denial, Part XIII withholding on recharacterized payments, and full reassessment with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Unreported Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Section 239 of the Income Tax Act: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment for up to 5 years. The CRA may also assess a 50% gross-negligence penalty under section 163 on the understated tax, plus compound daily interest. Unreported export revenue also triggers Ontario corporate tax reassessment and can expose the corporation to unremitted Part XIII withholding tax if foreign beneficial owners are involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, participate in, or acquiesce in the evasion are jointly exposed: the CRA can collect unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding from directors personally under subsection 227.1(1), and courts may order restitution to the Crown. Criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, asset freezing, and travel restrictions, including passport surrender or court-imposed bail conditions prohibiting departure from Canada.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement and T106 Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Subsection 247(3) of the Income Tax Act imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment when the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to use arm's-length pricing. CRA may also assess a 50% misrepresentation penalty under section 163, disallow deductions, and apply prescribed interest. The failure to file T106 within the statutory deadline can produce separate penalties and can extend the CRA's normal reassessment period for the affected years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false T2 or T106, participate in transfer-pricing documentation fraud, or cause false intercompany purchase invoices to be issued can be personally implicated in tax evasion and fraudulent misrepresentation. In egregious cases, the corporate veil can be pierced and the directing mind held liable for tax evaded, civil restitution, and criminal penalties; known participation may also attract director liability under the withholding and GST/HST provisions if the arrangement is used to strip assets from the corporation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act allows the CRA to disregard the avoidance transaction and re-characterize the profits as taxable Canadian income. Canada's mandatory disclosure and anti-avoidance rules may also impose penalties for failed avoidance transactions. Treaty relief may be denied under the principal purpose test, and payments recharacterized as dividends or unreported Canadian-source income are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding and gross penalties. If the shell structure is used to hide beneficial ownership, anti-money-laundering penalties under FINTRAC may be added.

Director personal liability: If the incorporation is a legal sham, courts may treat the profits as the personal income of the directing shareholder, making the individual personally liable for all taxes, interest, and penalties. Directors who divert corporate funds to offshore accounts may face civil breach of fiduciary duty claims, oppression remedies, and criminal fraud charges. Conviction for fraud or tax evasion can lead to imprisonment, court-imposed travel bans, and personal asset orders, including receivership, monetary judgment, and in extreme cases criminal forfeiture.
Anti-Money-Laundering / Cross-Border Payment Structure Violations

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes administrative monetary penalties of up to hundreds of thousands of Canadian dollars for failure to report suspicious transactions, large cash transactions, or mandated cross-border wire transfer records. Criminal money laundering under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code is punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years. The CRA can also use the payment records to support a complete income reassessment, gross-negligence penalties, and a criminal recommendation for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Senior officers who authorize, direct, or conceal split payments, false invoicing, or undisclosed beneficial ownership are individually liable for the criminal offence and can be fined and imprisoned. FINTRAC may issue penalty notices against both the corporation and responsible officers, directors, or agents. Criminal proceedings frequently include asset-freezing orders and bail travel bans; upon conviction, restitution orders and confiscation orders can be enforced against personal property.
