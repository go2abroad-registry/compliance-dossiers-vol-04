Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec (RQ); portal: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec's CléNumérique / Mon Dossier d'entreprise

E-government filing portal

Non-resident and resident corporations must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or online through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service; Quebec-based corporations must additionally register with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V (Registration of an Enterprise) to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and provincial tax accounts (e.g., QST, source deductions, health services fund). For Federal/Provincial Business Corporation, articles of incorporation or registration with Corporations Canada or the Quebec Registraire des entreprises prior to tax registration; the BN and NEQ are linked through the integrated business registration workflow; foreign entities appoint a provincial agent for service if no physical Quebec presence, and each separate business activity requires a separate BN program account number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status elections under ITA 125(7) and associated definitions apply; Quebec adopts federal taxable income as base with provincial adjustments; available elections include filing a joint election for deemed disposition of eligible capital property (ITA 14) and Form T2 Schedule 89 for eligible capital property transitional amounts; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA 18.5 and subsection 18.6 restrict deduction of payments subject to hybrid mismatch arrangements consistent with BEPS Action 2; Quebec's Taxation Act mirrors hybrid mismatch provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST (zero-rating exceptions for small suppliers); Quebec QST registration threshold is CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada unless the corporation is a 'large business' (threshold applies individually); once exceeded, mandatory registration effective on the transaction that breaches the threshold; non-resident digital suppliers may be required to register for simplified GST/HST and QST regardless of threshold if engaged in enumerated digital services and supplies to Quebec residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada (ITA Schedule VI, Part V) and to certain digital services supplied to non-residents who are not registered for GST/HST and where the supply is not for use in Canada; remote digital services supplied to a consumer ordinarily resident in a participating province may be subject to HST; zero-rating of a cross-border service generally requires documentation demonstrating the recipient is a non-resident and the service is consumed entirely outside Canada; Quebec QST zero-rating mirrors the GST/HST scheme under the Quebec Taxation Act and regulations.

  • Canada Customs Invoice (CCI) or commercial invoice
  • Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency — G13/Canada Export Reporting)
  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Proof of shipment (rail/air/sea waybill or courier manifest)
  • Evidence of foreign destination (delivery confirmation or customs entry at destination)
  • Foreign exchange wiring confirmation (equivalent to Inward Remittance Certificate) showing crediting of foreign currency to a Canadian bank account

No universal B2B e-invoicing clearance mandate at federal level; CRA accepts electronic invoices if records are accessible in Canada; Quebec has a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing (and B2C e-reporting) regime under Revenu Québec implemented from January 1, 2026 for enterprises with annual turnover exceeding CAD 50 million; PDF/A-3 format with embedded structured XML (Factur-X, ZUGFeRD or Universal Business Language) is required for Quebec e-invoices; no national digital stamping requirement; Pan-Canadian interoperability initiatives under the Council of the Federation may evolve.

Under the Excise Tax Act, a false or misleading statement for zero-rating can result in penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax overstated; Knowingly making a false statement is subject to penalty of 50% of the net tax amount; CRA/QFC may assess additional GST/HST, QST, interest, and administrative penalties for failure to substantiate export zero-rating; criminal prosecution under the Tax Rebate and Calculation of Interest Rules and the Criminal Code may apply for fraudulent refund claims; the taxpayer bears the burden of proof, and clawback periods extend to four years from the date of assessment (or indefinitely in case of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default); exporter records must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely floating; there are no exchange control laws or prior-approval requirements for capital inflows, outflows, profit remittances, dividends, interest, or royalties. Cross-border transfers are subject only to general AML/KYC documentation, FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., CAD 10,000 or equivalent), and tax withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties. Non-resident shareholders may freely repatriate lawful profits subject to procedural tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, repatriate, or hold export proceeds in domestic currency. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign bank accounts. Administrative reporting to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments purposes may apply, but there is no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low for purely offshore EMIs without Canadian registration. Offshore digital banks and EMIs are not directly authorized to provide banking services without being recognized as either a Canadian federal/provincial chartered bank, a licensed foreign bank branch, or a registered money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Additionally, the Retail Payment Activities Act (and related Bank of Canada oversight) requires payment service providers to register. A cross-border EMI may become viable through a partnership with a Canadian Schedule I bank or through establishing an MSB in Canada, but standalone direct account-opening as a bank is legally constrained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan funding from non-resident shareholders is wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian corporation's designated account at a Tier-1 bank. The wire must reference the purpose as 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan/Direct Investment', the beneficiary corporate name, and the corporate registration number. Settlement occurs through Lynx (Canada's high-value clearing system) for CAD, or via USD clearing procedures for USD. No exchange-control approval is required; however, if the inbound investment triggers Investment Canada Act review thresholds, an investor notification or application may be necessary. Standard bank compliance supports the transfer with the incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, shareholder register, board resolutions, and source-of-funds evidence.Outbound disbursements for trade and service settlements may be executed through SWIFT MT103/MT202 for bank wire transfers, CAD clearings via Lynx, and retail payments via ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System). For letter of credit transactions, a commercial LC is opened through a Canadian chartered bank with an advising bank abroad, with documentary compliance required. Foreign currency payments are settled through correspondent banking networks. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (federal 15% general corporate rate + Quebec provincial 11.5% general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation (including a Quebec federal or provincial corporation) to a non-resident shareholder. This rate is a flat gross-basis withholding tax; the rate may be reduced under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US treaty: 5% if the recipient is a company that owns 10% or more of voting shares; otherwise 15%; most treaties allow 15% portfolio dividend/5% substantive holding).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is six months after the corporation’s fiscal year-end; the balance of tax payable is due no later than two months after year-end (three months for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations to the extent of its small business deduction).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Must be filed by the same six-month post-year-end deadline as the federal T2; the Quebec final balance for instalment remittances is due within the same two-month (or three-month eligible) window.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 in aggregate annual value of controlled transactions involving inventory goods; CA$500,000 for controlled transactions involving services rental royalties and interest paid to non-residents; CA$100,000 for dividends? – Actually, the operative CRA-administered threshold is CA$1,000,000 for goods and CA$500,000 for services/intangibles/interest per taxpayer fiscal year. A separate CA$100,000 threshold applies to amounts of loans or advances made by a non-resident to the Canadian entity for which a T106 report must be filed whenever the total loans exceed that amount.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not enforce standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the federal Income Tax Act contains robust anti-avoidance rules (general anti-avoidance rule – GAAR), OECD PCA pillar legislation labelled the ‘anti-hybrid mismatch rules’, and substance over form jurisprudence. Quebec’s Tax Act likewise imposes a civil `abuse of law` doctrine taxing transactions lacking a genuine commercial or economic substance. For a health-fitness app holding entity: retain local development IP, real office/cloud assets, and Quebec employees involved in decision-making. Shell status will trigger GAAR, reassessments, and 100% penalty under subsection 162(1).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Willful omission or misrepresentation of Canadian-source revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Income Tax Act section 239 and Excise Tax Act: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (summary conviction up to 2 years). Quebec under Taxation Act section 81: civil penalties of 50%-150% of the additional tax payable plus daily interest. CRA can also impose a 10% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate source deductions (CRA deemed trust), unpaid QST/GST, and any withheld taxes. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a sham or a “frustrated middleman”; personal orders for restitution, asset-freezing orders under the Income Tax Act, and travel bans are obtainable when a director flees the jurisdiction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disallowing transactions, plus a 5% mispricing penalty under subsection 247(3) where transfer pricing documentation is absent. Canada treats the shell as resident and assesses on worldwide income. Quebec may additionally apply the abuse doctrine and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the under-withheld amount.

Director personal liability: Director liability under the CCQ (Civil Code of Quebec) for fraud or a “masking” act; a court can disregard the corporate veil under the veil-piercing doctrine, making directors liable for all tax deficiencies plus punitive damages. Biometric data misuse during a shell operation also activates medical-health regulatory penalties.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to CA$500,000 for a first offence per day for failing to register, failing to report, or non-identity verification. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec-specific: AMF may revoke the digital-media tax credit if the entity has unpaid AML fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for design and enforcement failures; criminal fines enforced against the director’s personal assets, and prohibition orders block them from managing any Canadian entity for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts intercompany remuneration to arm’s-length and levies a penalty of 10% of the net under-assessed transfer price unless reasonable documentation was prepared and filed within 90 days of request. Additional assessment of 25% withholding on the deemed dividend if the price adjustment is treated as a shareholder benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false price-setting arrangement may be assessed personally for the 10% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 247(3) where the veil is pierced for conspiracy to evade income tax; in egregious fraud, directors face criminal jail terms, travel bans, and confiscation of proceeds of the unhealthy tax-planning scheme.
