Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and, under the Quebec single-administration agreement, Revenu Québec (Agence du revenu du Québec). Digital portals: CRA Business Registration (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency) and Revenu Québec Business Registration (revenuquebec.ca) via Clé d'accès.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: obtain the 10-digit Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) by registering the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ); non-resident LPs must appoint a Quebec attorney (mandataire) for service. Step 2: obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA. Step 3: activate program accounts: GST/HST (BN + RT) and QST (BN + TQ) through Revenu Québec; a single combined return covers both levies for a Quebec establishment. Step 4: open payroll accounts (BN + RP; Quebec QPIP, QHSF, CNESST) if the LP employs staff. Step 5: file the Partnership Information Return (CRA T5013 / Revenu Québec TP-1001) each fiscal year; partners report allocated shares on their own returns. Step 6: register non-resident partners for Canadian identification on the T5013/TP-1001 and source any Part XIII withholding on guaranteed payments or interest to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election: a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent unless it is a 'specified investment flow-through (SIFT) partnership' under Income Tax Act s. 122.1 (units listed on a designated stock exchange), in which case it is deemed a corporation. Applicable elections: (i) voluntary GST/QST registration while below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 240 / QSA s. 347); (ii) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election on contributing property to the LP; (iii) fiscal-period elections under ITA s. 96(3); (iv) hybrid-mismatch documentation substantiation under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 attesting to the non-hybrid character of LP allocations; (v) record-keeping and reporting-frequency elections for GST/QST under ETA s. 230.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person carrying on a business (including an LP) must register for GST and QST once worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies by the LP and associated persons, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148; QSA). Non-resident digital suppliers of Quebec consumers must register without a threshold (2021 GST digital-economy framework; 2017 QST digital-services framework).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rated (GST) and exported (QST) if the LP ships or delivers tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1; the LP must be the exporter of record, delivery must be made to a foreign customer address, and satisfactory proof of export must be obtained and retained within the normal evidentiary window (CRA practice: documentation collected at or near the time of supply, customarily within 180 days). Offshore services, e.g., downloadable tuning software, are zero-rated only when performed for a non-resident recipient and not in connection with real or tangible property situated in Canada. Drop-shipping caveat: possession must transfer to the foreign buyer or a common carrier for immediate export; any domestic fulfilment in Canada followed by Canadian use destroys zero-rating. QST/GST input tax credits on related expenses are claimed on the same combined return.

  • CBSA export declaration or CERS electronic export filing (Form B13 equivalent) naming the LP as exporter
  • Customs commercial invoice with 10-digit HS classification of the auto parts and stated terms of sale (EXW/FCA/FOB)
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / courier consignment note with foreign destination and removal-from-Canada proof
  • Foreign buyer's purchase order, shipping address verification and carrier tracking history exiting Canada
  • Inward foreign-currency remittance evidence — SWIFT credit advice or Stripe/PayPal settlement reports tied to each order
  • LP's export register and reconciliation file linking payment rails, customs declarations and carrier records

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-authorization of individual invoices exists in Canada or Quebec; invoicing remains record-based. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (PDI/PEPPOL) is voluntary (pilot since 2023). Mandatory electronic compliance: (i) GST/HST and QST returns and remittances for Quebec-establishment registrants are filed electronically with Revenu Québec on one combined return; (ii) third-party e-commerce platforms facilitating supply must collect and report GST/QST where applicable; (iii) machine-readable general ledgers, transactional settlement reports from Stripe/PayPal, and API access to records on demand are required in CRA/Revenu Québec audits; (iv) digital records must be retained for six (6) years in a format convertible to XML/CSV on request.

CRA and Revenu Québec will deny zero-rating where the export bundle is missing, unrelated, or fabricated, and will reassess GST (5%) plus QST (9.975%) on the gross invoice value with arrears interest; the normal reassessment window is four (4) years, unlimited with misrepresentation or neglect. Penalties: 25% participation penalty and 50% gross-negligence penalty for false or omitted amounts (ETA s. 280; QST counterpart), plus further penalties for repeated non-compliance; intentional evasion is a criminal offence with fines up to 200% of evaded tax and up to five (5) years' imprisonment (ETA s. 327 and parallel Quebec provisions). The LP's registration may be cancelled, and payment processors (Stripe/PayPal) may be notified and merchants risk termination following tax-evasion flags.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible in CAD and foreign currencies. There are no foreign-exchange controls, permits, or prior approvals for profit remittances to non-resident partners. A Quebec limited partnership may remit profits freely, but must apply non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Quebec Taxation Act, and must provide the paying bank with a valid withholding/waiver certificate. Cross-border capital injections and repatriation of proceeds are unrestricted, though CAD 10,000 and above must be reported to FINTRAC and are subject to standard anti-structuring rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds from online retail of auto parts and accessories may be retained in USD or converted to CAD at commercial rates. Export revenue must be reported in the limited partnership's financial statements and tax returns; cross-border EFT receipts of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary policy and currency administration; foreign exchange regulations are liberal, with oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for regulated financial institutions and FINTRAC for cross-border AML/ATF reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditionally viable with Low-to-Medium feasibility. A foreign digital EMI is not a Schedule I Canadian bank and cannot accept or hold 'deposits' as defined under the Bank Act. Such entities may operate as payment service providers only if registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and, where applicable, authorized by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) in Quebec. For the CA_LP treasury, offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used as ancillary payment-collection or disbursement rails, but the primary capital escrow and operational accounts should be maintained at a tier-1 domestic Canadian bank to mitigate legal and counterparty risk.
