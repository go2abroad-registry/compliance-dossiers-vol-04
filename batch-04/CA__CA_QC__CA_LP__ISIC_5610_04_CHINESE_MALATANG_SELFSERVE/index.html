Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST account and federal Business Number (BN); Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST) account and Québec business registration through Registraire des entreprises.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnership must first obtain a federal BN by filing Form RC160 (GST/HST Application for Non-Resident) or via CRA My Business Account, and simultaneously register for the QST with Revenu Québec in the province of Québec, using the Registraire des entreprises enterprise number as the base identifier. Non-resident partners without fixed place of business may apply with a Canadian agent or address of record; documentary proof of the partnership deed and partner identification must be submitted; registrations are effective from the registration date or a specified earlier date if supply nexus exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No statutory check-the-box election exists in Canada; a Québec société en commandite (limited partnership) is automatically fiscally transparent for federal income tax and Québec tax purposes. Available elections include the GST/HST section 156 election for qualifying closely related corporations, annual vs quarterly filing elections, and a QST joint election with Revenu Québec for consolidated registration; partnerships may elect to classify activities as commercial under QST rules but cannot elect entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Québec within any four consecutive calendar quarters; for GST, CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (other than certain excluded supplies) within the same four-calendar-quarter measure. If the limited partnership's domestic and foreign export turnover exceeds this threshold, mandatory registration applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for zero-rated GST/QST if the goods are physically exported by the supplier or on its behalf and the supplier obtains documentary evidence within the required statutory period. Export of franchise and royalty services to a non-resident franchisee is zero-rated when the service is supplied to a non-resident person, all or substantially all (90% or more) of the service is performed outside Canada, and the recipient is not registered for GST/QST in Canada. The CA_LP must be registered for GST/QST to claim refunds of input tax credits and to issue zero-rated invoices.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency CRI / Form B13 for non-CBSA systems)
  • Bill of Lading or Equivalent Transport Evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Foreign Exchange Wire Record
  • Sales or Royalty Agreement with the Offshore Franchisee
  • Non-Resident Recipient Status Evidence (foreign GST/HST equivalency certificate, VAT registration certificate or signed declaration)

No federal real-time tax clearance or fiscal stamping is required in Canada; CRA accepts electronic invoices if they remain legible and accessible. Québec has introduced a phased electronic-invoicing mandate for large businesses (including B2B transmissions via certified platforms), but there is no generalized API-based clearance or digital stamping for SME fast-casual franchise LPs. Invoices of a CA_LP should nevertheless comply with the required tax invoice content (BN, QST registration number, date, description, and GST/QST breakdown) and may be exchanged via EDI or Peppol-compliant networks when engaging with mandated counterparties.

CRA and Revenu Québec may disallow zero-rating and assess GST/QST on the full invoice amount plus interest if documentary export evidence is not produced on request. Penalties include GST/HST failure-to-file or false-statement penalties of 5% plus 1% per month and gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of understated tax; for Québec QST, similar recapture plus civil monetary penalties; deliberate evasion may be prosecuted as an offence, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment; the same statutes contain a general six-year reassessment window for ordinary transactions and a ten-year window where misrepresentation occurs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a liberalized, fully convertible currency regime with no exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements, and no restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance from a Quebec Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted from an FX standpoint, but is subject to indirect fiscal frictions: Part XIII federal withholding tax and Quebec Taxation Act withholding on royalties, interest, and dividends paid to non-residents, reducible under the Canada-China Income Tax Convention (generally to 10% on royalties and interest). Additional non-FX frictions include FINTRAC reporting thresholds, CRS/FATCA self-certification obligations, and Quebec partnership allocation filings (Schedule TP-31/Relevé 15) for non-resident partners. No exchange-control permit or conversion license is required for capital injection, revenue repatriation, or trade financing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion timeline, and no required conversion ratio for foreign-currency receipts. Export and service proceeds may be retained at 100% in any freely convertible currency. For the Malatang master-franchise Limited Partnership, cross-border receipts such as franchise fees, royalties, and management fees may remain in USD, EUR, or CNY indefinitely, subject only to FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting and source-of-funds documentation at the receiving bank. Physical cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 requires a CRA E677 report.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision of federally regulated banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated centralized foreign-exchange-control administration; exchange rate policy and payments-system oversight rest with the Bank of Canada, while the Minister of Finance retains residual authority under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for receivables and payables clearing, LOW for capital escrow and statutory operating accounts. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing class; non-bank payment providers must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, must obtain a licence under the Act respecting money-services businesses administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Ebury may legally settle inbound and outbound CAD cross-border flows as registered MSBs or foreign MSBs, but they cannot hold partnership capital in CDIC-insured accounts, cannot serve as the sole treasury bank for Quebec payroll or tax collection, and are not eligible for direct SWIFT membership or documentary-credit issuance. Recommended architecture confines EMIs to fast secondary rails for Chinese franchisee collections and China disbursements, while a Schedule I Canadian bank remains the primary system-of-record account holder.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound franchise capital from China should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the Chinese parent's licensed bank to the Limited Partnership's segregated Operational Account with an explicit payment purpose code. Recommended statistical purpose codes follow Canada's Balance of Payments classification: use code 290 (charges for the use of intellectual property) for master-franchise licence-right injections, and classify pure intercompany equity funding as Foreign Direct Investment in Canada. No Bank of Canada or FX-administration approval is required, but the wire must clear OFAC and UN security filters, include complete remitter and beneficiary identifiers, and state a substantive purpose. Any single transfer at or above CAD 10,000 triggers FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting. For injections above CAD 2,000,000, a Canadian legal opinion confirming the funds are capital contributions rather than loans is advisable to avoid Part XIII interest withholding characterization. Physical import of monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 requires a CRA E677 declarational filing. The bank's source-of-funds pack should retain the SAFE remittance voucher, notarized General Partner resolutions, and Chinese business licensing documents to prevent hold-funding delays.Principal outbound disbursements are master-franchise royalty payments to the Chinese licensor and trade payments for imported broth powders, chili-based ingredients, and packaging equipment. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No compulsory audit is imposed on a Québec limited partnership by the Civil Code of Québec or the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises. An independent audit becomes mandatory only if (a) the partnership agreement or a unitholder resolution requires it; (b) the LP is a reporting issuer under Canadian securities legislation subject to NI 51-102 annual audit requirements; (c) CRA or Revenu Québec issues a statutory written demand for audited financial statements; or (d) a secured lender, franchisee agreement, or master licence contract imposes an audit covenant. For a fast-casual franchisor LP, reviewed or audited financial statements are commercially typical to satisfy franchise disclosure expectations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined federal 15% + Quebec provincial 11.5% for general corporate income; note: Limited Partnership is a pass-through entity, income taxed at partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for partnership distributions (pass-through entity); if the entity were a corporation, the statutory rate is 25% for non-resident shareholders, reduced by tax treaties (e.g., 5% for substantial shareholding under Canada-US treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties exceed CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, or CAD 250,000 for other transactions. Master File required if total annual revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act applies. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking economic substance, and the partnership must demonstrate real business purpose and substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Section 239) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in the evasion; travel bans may be imposed under the Canada Revenue Agency's enforcement powers.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the partnership and deny deductions, impose penalties under GAAR (Section 245) of up to 50% of the tax benefit, and potentially reclassify the entity as a corporation for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for the partnership's tax debts if the partnership is deemed a sham; directors of corporate general partners may face civil restitution and travel bans.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities per violation; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be held personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML controls; travel bans may be issued under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
