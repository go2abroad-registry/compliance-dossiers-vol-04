E-government filing portal

Canadian-incorporated entities: a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and corporate income-tax account (RC) are assigned automatically upon articles of incorporation; a GST/HST (RT) account and, in Quebec, a QST (or NDT/TVQ) account are opened by registration with CRA/Revenu Québec (single-window registration). Foreign (non-resident) entities: (1) assess whether they carry on business in Canada or make taxable supplies in Canada; (2) apply for a BN using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) with Form RC148 (non-resident request) or Form RC1, providing certificate of incorporation, foreign tax ID, and registered agent; (3) register for GST/HST through CRA or, in Quebec, through Revenu Québec's single registration (which assigns the QST number and the Quebec registration number (NEQ) for businesses); (4) obtain a CRA non-resident 'corporate income tax' account (RC) where the foreign entity has Canadian-source income from carrying on business in Canada; (5) file any required waiver/proof of permanent-establishment status. A non-resident digital supplier of software through Apple/Google platforms is generally not required to register for GST/HST/QST if the platform operator is itself registered and is the deemed supplier, but voluntary registration may be elected only when carrying on business in Canada and to recover input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a generally open foreign-exchange regime and the Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial, investment, dividend, interest, royalty, and service-payment purposes. Canada does not generally impose capital-account controls, mandatory conversion of foreign currency, exchange surrender requirements, or routine central-bank approval for non-resident ownership, equity funding, shareholder loans, intercompany payments, or profit repatriation. Banks must complete customer identification, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, source-of-funds, transaction-purpose, and anti-money-laundering reviews. Outbound dividends and other related-party payments remain subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, treaty eligibility, transfer-pricing support, thin-capitalization and interest-limitation rules, GST/HST and income-tax compliance, and bank documentary review. Payments to sanctioned persons, prohibited counterparties, or jurisdictions may be blocked or reported. Large international electronic transfers and prescribed transactions may be reported by financial institutions to FINTRAC and CRA without imposing a currency-conversion restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general Canadian statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Canada, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to surrender foreign-currency export receipts to a Canadian bank or the Bank of Canada. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering obligations. Banks may request invoices, contracts, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and beneficial-owner information. The corporation must maintain accurate books and records, report taxable income and foreign assets or transactions where applicable, and respond to CRA, Statistics Canada, FINTRAC, or bank information requests. Specific goods, controlled technology, sanctions, customs, tax, or government-contract rules may create additional reporting or licensing obligations that are separate from foreign-exchange convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with foreign-exchange, financial-sector, anti-money-laundering, payments, and tax compliance administered or supervised as applicable by the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario or Autorité des marchés financiers where provincial registration requirements apply.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment-collection and foreign-exchange services; Low for replacing a Canadian operating bank account or holding Canadian customer funds without the required authorization. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot rely solely on its foreign licence to provide unrestricted payment services, deposit-taking, retail payment activities, or money-services activities in Canada. Canadian operations may require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada, registration as a money services business with FINTRAC, provincial registration or licensing, contractual sponsorship by a regulated institution, and compliance with safeguarding, operational-risk, privacy, sanctions, consumer-protection, and reporting requirements. A foreign EMI may be used as a supplemental treasury or payout provider only after confirming Canadian legal perimeter, customer-fund segregation, settlement access, data-transfer, tax, chargeback, and account-closure risks. Apple and Google platform settlement accounts should be opened in the corporation's legal name where commercially available and reconciled to Canadian bank records.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5% = combined 26.5% general corporate rate on taxable income above the small business threshold. Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) claiming the Small Business Deduction: federal 9% + Quebec 4% (Quebec rate reduced to 3.2% for 2024 and subsequent taxation years under the Quebec Budget 2024-2025) = combined 12.2% to 13% on the first CA$500,000 of active business income. Non-resident-controlled Canadian corporations are not CCPCs and cannot claim the SBD.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA s. 212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. Quebec does not impose a separate provincial withholding tax on dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Return due no later than 6 months after fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end due June 30). Balance-due tax payable within 2 months after year-end generally, extended to 3 months for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction; large corporations whose taxable capital employed in Canada exceeds CA$15,000,000 remit monthly installments, while eligible CCPCs may remit quarterly.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after fiscal year-end; Quebec balance-due payable within 2 months (3 months for a CCPC quebecoise eligible for the deduction pour petite entreprise). The general Quebec corporate rate remains 11.5%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Canadian T106 information return threshold is CA$1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions for the taxation year. The Country-by-Country Report obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CA$750,000,000. Canada has not codified OECD-style Local File/Master File monetary thresholds, but the CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation prepared to OECD standards for any material intercompany transaction, with the level of documentation proportionate to the risk and complexity.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain 'Economic Substance Regulations' comparable to offshore ESR regimes; economic substance is instead enforced through (i) central management and control tests for residency, (ii) permanent establishment attribution rules under ITA s. 115 and OECD PE principles, (iii) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s. 245, and (iv) anti-treaty-shopping and beneficial-ownership jurisprudence (e.g., Alta Energy). A Quebec mobile app company must maintain real and demonstrable substance: Quebec-based developers and product managers, board meetings and decisions in Canada, ownership of IP in Canada, and a commercial rationale for any offshore intercompany arrangements. A 'mailbox' or invoice-only Quebec corporation with no employees or operational substance is exposed to recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and GAAR reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – suppression of app-store revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA s. 239(1) and parallel Quebec legislation: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s. 163(2), plus compound daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in, assented to, or acquiesced in the omission may be jointly assessed; under ITA s. 227.1 directors are personally liable for unremitted employee source deductions and GST/HST; criminal prosecution may result in travel bans as bail conditions, asset freezes, and imprisonment.
Failure to file returns and remit installments

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per month up to 12 months, compounded with daily interest; Revenu Quebec imposes an equivalent penalty regime; continued default can trigger a CRA requirement to confirm, and ultimately a tax collection writ, garnishment, and seizure of corporate and personal assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted federal and provincial source deductions, GST/HST/TVQ, and in Quebec for unpaid corporate taxes where the corporation's failure to file results in a willful default; the CRA may issue director liability assessments without first pursuing the corporation.
