Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a single tax administration agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; there is no separate Ontario GST/HST authority.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a CA_CORP business corporation: (1) file articles of incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the Ontario Business Corporations Act; (2) upon incorporation, Corporations Canada or ServiceOntario transmits the incorporation record to the CRA, which issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) constituting the statutory corporate TIN; (3) open program accounts through BRO or Form RC1, requiring an RM account for GST/HST (mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000), an RP account for federal Part I income tax, and an RZ account for payroll if employees are engaged; (4) Ontario corporate income tax is administered through the same federal T2 corporate return without any separate Ontario registration; (5) for non-resident vendors without a Canadian entity, direct online GST/HST registration as a non-resident is available under the same BN framework through BRO without a Canadian legal footprint.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (resident, taxed on worldwide income) and under the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST purposes; no fiscal transparency or pass-through treatment is available to a Canadian corporation and no US-style check-the-box election exists. Ontario taxes the same federal taxable income base at the provincial rate; GST/HST applies to Canadian taxable supplies, while qualifying exports are zero-rated and therefore excluded from the 13% HST base. — SOP for a CA_CORP business corporation: (1) file articles of incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the Ontario Business Corporations Act; (2) upon incorporation, Corporations Canada or ServiceOntario transmits the incorporation record to the CRA, which issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) constituting the statutory corporate TIN; (3) open program accounts through BRO or Form RC1, requiring an RM account for GST/HST (mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000), an RP account for federal Part I income tax, and an RZ account for payroll if employees are engaged; (4) Ontario corporate income tax is administered through the same federal T2 corporate return without any separate Ontario registration; (5) for non-resident vendors without a Canadian entity, direct online GST/HST registration as a non-resident is available under the same BN framework through BRO without a Canadian legal footprint.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (resident, taxed on worldwide income) and under the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST purposes; no fiscal transparency or pass-through treatment is available to a Canadian corporation and no US-style check-the-box election exists. Ontario taxes the same federal taxable income base at the provincial rate; GST/HST applies to Canadian taxable supplies, while qualifying exports are zero-rated and therefore excluded from the 13% HST base.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election for entity classification; a corporation is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections relevant to this entity: (i) GST/HST grouping election under ETA s.150 for closely related corporations to file a single consolidated GST/HST return; (ii) election under ETA s.211(2) to zero-rate supplies of management or administrative services between closely related corporations; (iii) voluntary registration application to waive the small-supplier exemption; (iv) income tax rollover elections under ITA s.85(1) and amalgamation under ITA s.256 for corporate restructuring; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18(4) to 18(7) and the ETA apply automatically and cannot be elected out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the statutory small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the total value of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export sales of furniture but excluding exempt supplies, exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. For this oversized-furniture online retailer, both domestic HST-taxable sales and zero-rated export sales count toward the CAD 30,000 registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods - oversized furniture sold online: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 if (i) the supplier ships the tangible personal property directly to a destination outside Canada, by or on behalf of the supplier, or (ii) the supplier delivers the goods to a common carrier or consignee for actual export within 60 days of delivery, and the goods leave Canada in the ordinary course of trade. Services, including white-glove assembly or logistics performed abroad: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 when supplied to a non-resident in respect of real property situated outside Canada, in respect of tangible personal property situated outside Canada at the time the service is performed, or when the service is performed wholly outside Canada. The exporter must be able to evidence, at the time of supply, that the recipient is outside Canada and that the goods will be physically exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for most exports of goods valued in excess of CAD 2,000)
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean bill of lading, airway bill, or truck waybill naming the Canadian exporter as shipper and evidencing the non-resident destination and consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank document confirming receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer, converted into Canadian dollars)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing authorization exists in Canada. Invoices must satisfy the prescribed content rules of ETA ss.169(2) and 175(1) (supplier BN, date, description of goods, consideration, HST amount or zero-rated indication), and books and records must be retained for six years under ETA s.286. The CRA Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL network (roadmap to 2026) is voluntary; no API clearance gate is imposed on payment rails such as Stripe or Affirm BNPL.

The CRA GST/HST Export Verification Program audits zero-rated export claims by cross-matching B13A export declarations, carrier waybills, and banking remittance records. Where zero-rating is denied because goods never left Canada, documentation is missing, or goods were sold domestically, the CRA re-assesses the supply at 13% HST, denies or claws back offsetting input tax credits on inventory and freight costs, and charges arrears interest. Gross negligence or false statements attract a penalty of up to 50% of the net tax under ETA s.280; the normal four-year GST/HST reassessment window is extended without limitation where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; and criminal prosecution may follow under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code in cases of fraudulent export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepts International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations and maintains fully liberalized current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and not subject to exchange controls. Dividends, interest, royalties, and branch profits may be remitted to non-resident shareholders without Bank of Canada authorization or foreign-exchange licensing, but remain subject to ordinary tax withholding and Canadian reporting requirements. Direct investment into a Federal/Ontario business corporation, including equity injections from non-resident shareholders, is freely permitted, and non-resident ownership is allowed without exchange-control approval. No profit remittance hurdle exists under foreign-exchange law; the only practical constraints are Part XIII withholding tax obligations, transfer pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and bank-level sanctions/KYC screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio apply in Canada. An ISIC 4791 oversized furniture e-commerce exporter may retain export and online retail sale proceeds in original foreign currencies, hold them in CAD or foreign-currency bank accounts, and convert dollars through the interbank market at any time. FINTRAC reporting obligations may apply to electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction or 24-hour period, but these are AML/CTF reporting rules rather than submission or conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating as the central bank for monetary policy and financial system stability, together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal prudential banking regulator, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CTF supervisor, and the Department of Finance as the exchange-rate and macroeconomic policy authority. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with constraints. Non-resident fintech and electronic money institutions are not authorized to take Canadian deposits or issue insured depository accounts unless they become authorized banks or obtain a provincial credit-union/trust exemption. They may, however, act as cross-border payment processors, merchant acquirers, and clearing intermediaries, subject to the Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime, FINTRAC money service business registration, and sanctions compliance obligations. Such providers are suitable for auxiliary treasury rails and customer-merchant settlement; they are not a substitute for a Tier 1 Canadian bank account when receiving share capital, maintaining an escrow account, or disbursing large trade payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound rail for non-resident equity funding into the Ontario corporation is an SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the foreign parent's bank to the Canadian Tier 1 account, addressed in SWIFT Field 70 to the beneficiary bank with the narrative 'Share capital contribution pursuant to signed shareholder resolution and subscription agreement.' Canada has no statutory local FX purpose code, so where an intermediary bank requires an ISO 20022 External Purpose Code, the remitter should use 'CAPI' (capital payments) or, for a more market-standard corporate description, 'EQUI' (equity-related flows), and must avoid generic consumer codes such as 'GDDS' or 'MAKS'. The receiving bank will verify the beneficiary account name, a certified copy of the federal/Provincial incorporation certificate, share register evidence, the board resolution, and the natural-person UBO declaration before crediting the funds as paid-up capital. The transfer must include the full legal name of the account holder and the Canadian institution/routing components (institution number and transit number) either through the national payment network or through SWIFT with CCY/Amount in Field 32A and the settlement instruction in Field 33B.Supplier, freight, duty, and logistics payments for oversized furniture should be cleared through (a) domestic CAD payments via Lynx, the Bank of Canada high-value same-day system, using an MT103 or direct Lynx payment instruction; (b) cross-border USD via Fedwire/CHIPS with an SWIFT MT103 free-format instruction; (c) cross-border CAD/EUR/GBP via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers; (d) commercial letters of credit via SWIFT MT700/MT710 with the confirming bank in the supplier country; and (e) consumer-refund and inbound e-commerce receipts through Stripe and Affirm settlement rails into the primary operating account. Large, repeating supplier disbursements should be supported by purchase orders, supplier contracts, commercial invoices, and shipping customs documentation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation when the aggregate value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length persons exceeds C$1,000,000 (Local File); a Master File is required for multinational groups with Canadian consolidated gross revenue equal to or exceeding C$850,000,000. Documentation must exist before the T2 filing date to qualify for penalty relief.

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) exists in Canada at the federal or Ontario level, but the Income Tax Act uses a functional equivalent: a corporate entity must have a sufficient Canadian permanent establishment (employees, premises, and decision-making functions) to claim treaty benefits and to avoid the General Anti-Avoidance Rule. For online oversized-furniture retailing, maintaining a Canadian distribution warehouse, a customer-service team, and a registered central management office is the minimum substance to evidence a real Canadian commercial operation and to prevent recharacterisation of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act s.239(1); fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; full reassessment of income with penalties under s.163(2) equal to gross negligence (50% of understated tax) plus daily compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in or authorise the filing of false information lose civil corporate shield protection, become jointly and severally liable for the full arrearage plus interest and penalties, are subject to CRA asset freeze and travel bans under s.224.2, and face criminal imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced wherever the entity is a mere façade for personal financial enrichment.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke s.247(2) to recharacterise transaction terms, apply the GAAR under s.245(2) to nullify tax benefits, impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the understated income, and, if the shell is found to be a sham, disregard the entity for all federal purposes and assess tax directly on the economic beneficiary.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for any tax determined under s.160 as equitable transferees of corporate funds, and the CRA can issue director's liability letters under s.227.1(1) that make directors personally responsible for unpaid source deductions and GST/HST; consistent use of a shell to conceal real ownership triggers criminal 'tax evasion' charges.
GST/HST and Payroll Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid GST/HST plus penalties under Excise Tax Act s.280(1) (10% of the amount plus a $2,000 fine if gross negligence proven); CRA can require personal security, register a lien against all corporate assets, and impose daily interest at prescribed rates; repeated failure is indictable with imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Civil directors' liability is made absolute under ETA s.323 and ITA s.227.1(1) for all unremitted GST/HST and source deductions, regardless of whether the corporation becomes insolvent; CRA may pursue directors individually, seize bank accounts, garnish receivables, and obtain court orders restricting travel; bankruptcy of the corporation does not discharge director liability.
AML / Unlicensed Money Services Business

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), an unregistered MSB is liable to administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for an individual director and C$1,000,000 for the corporation, plus cease-and-desist orders and criminal prosecution; the OFT can also issue a notice of non-compliance and revoke the company's payment processing privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate an unlicensed payment or money-transfer service cannot hide behind the limited-liability shell; they face personal fines, debarment from merchant accounts, seizure of corporate and personal assets, and, if a cross-border component is present, inadmissibility to Canada and the US on criminal grounds.
