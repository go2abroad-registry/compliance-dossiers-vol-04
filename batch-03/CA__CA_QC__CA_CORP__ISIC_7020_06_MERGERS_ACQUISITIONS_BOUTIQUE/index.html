Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (REQ) - Corporate Services Division; head office at 800 place d'Youville, Quebec City, with online services at registraire.gouv.qc.ca.

Governing statute

Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA), CQLR c S-31.1, arts. 8-12 (method and contents of incorporation), art. 25 (registered office), art. 163 (unanimous resolutions); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-44.1, arts. 14-20 (enterprise registration, updates and annual declaration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (statuts constitutifs) filed electronically with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec together with the declaration of registration of the enterprise.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Under the QBCA no minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required; at least one common share may be issued for nominal consideration, and the stated capital account must reflect the actual consideration received as determined by the board of directors.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal business permit and signage/legal signage permit from the local borough; secure the Regie du batiment du Quebec (RBQ) building conformity certificate (structural load, means of egress, ventilation) for the leased office; obtain fire safety certificate and occupancy permit from the competent fire service (e.g., Service de securite incendie de Montreal); file the workplace health and safety declaration with the CNESST and maintain the first-aid and emergency preparedness plan under the Quebec Occupational Health and Safety Act (CQLR c S-2.1).

Paid-in capital rule: None. Under the QBCA no minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required; at least one common share may be issued for nominal consideration, and the stated capital account must reflect the actual consideration received as determined by the board of directors.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) – GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); provincial component: Revenu Québec – QST, provincial income tax, payroll deductions; portal: Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/services/online-services/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign M&A advisory corporation must, before carrying on taxable activity in Canada, apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through an authorized representative; concurrently register for GST/HST via the same process or through My Business Account if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. For Quebec, register with Revenu Québec for QST and payroll deductions by filing Form LM-1 (Demande d'inscription) or through the Registraire des entreprises; obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and, once registered, use the 16-digit QST number. Registration requires legal status documents, directors' identification, and a Canadian address for service.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign M&A advisory corporation must, before carrying on taxable activity in Canada, apply to the CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through an authorized representative; concurrently register for GST/HST via the same process or through My Business Account if worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. For Quebec, register with Revenu Québec for QST and payroll deductions by filing Form LM-1 (Demande d'inscription) or through the Registraire des entreprises; obtain a Quebec Establishment Number (NEQ) and, once registered, use the 16-digit QST number. Registration requires legal status documents, directors' identification, and a Canadian address for service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' pass-through election exists for Canadian business corporations. A corporation is automatically a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Election mechanisms include: s.83(2) election to deem a dividend out of the Capital Dividend Account; s.256(2) deemed control election for Canadian-controlled private corporation status; s.216 election for non-residents on Canadian rental/investment income; hybrid mismatch adjustments under s.18.2, s.18.3 and s.20.15(4) apply mandatory to certain cross-border branch payments and do not require an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for both GST/HST and QST; once exceeded, registration is compulsory

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of an M&A advisory service made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST or QST, where the consideration is entirely attributable to services rendered outside Canada, or, if performed partly in Canada, an apportionment meets the applicable de minimis trading rules; the supply must not relate to Canadian real estate or goods situated in Canada at the time the services are performed. QST zero-rating substantially mirrors the GST/HST rules for exported services, requiring proof of non-residence and foreign benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Form B13A / CBSA CAED data set for goods exports)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or electronic manifest) evidencing export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Canada has no real-time national clearance or digital stamping mandate for B2B invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through CRA-approved software when revenue exceeds CAD 1,500,000; Quebec imposes mandatory electronic filing of QST returns and RL-1/T4 information slips through Revenu Québec's online portal; businesses must retain invoices in prescribed form (paper or structured electronic such as PDF with digital signatures) and issue valid GST/HST/QST invoices; no API pre-clearance is yet compulsory.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated status within the normal limitation period (3 years for corporations, or 4 years for non-arm's length transactions; indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, negligence or fraud). If zero-rating is denied, the taxpayer owes uncollected GST/HST/QST plus interest and arrears; penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.285(1) of the Excise Tax Act for false statements, and criminal prosecution for evasion with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. Provincial counterpart penalties under the Tax Administration Act (Quebec) can reach 50% of the tax due and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates an unmanaged floating exchange rate regime with full current and capital account convertibility. There are no statutory restrictions on conversion of Canadian dollars, foreign currency accounts, capital injections, dividend remittances, or profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders. Remittances and distributions remain fully permissible, subject only to income tax and statutory withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act, Quebec Taxation Act, and CRA reporting obligations. No central bank or Ministry of Finance approval is required for capital account transactions by resident corporations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no prescribed conversion ratio in Canada. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency at authorized financial institutions in Canada or abroad, convert freely at market rates, or use such funds for permissible business purposes. All cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 and wire transmissions of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; tax disclosure under CRA and FATCA/CRS remains required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority) together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers foreign exchange transaction reporting and cross-border currency monitoring. Canada no longer maintains exchange controls under the repealed Foreign Exchange Control Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for disbursement-only operational accounts, Low for capital escrow or trust settlement accounts. Cross-border digital EMIs operating in Canada must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and, if based offshore, maintain a recognized Canadian MSB registration. Such fintech platforms do not provide LVTS membership, do not qualify under most escrow agreements as acceptable safekeeping institutions, and carry heightened pricing, sanction-screening, and continuity risks. They may be used for outbound fee disbursements where low balance and non-accredited funds are held, but not as the core treasury banking rail for an M&A boutique.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Quebec business corporation, inbound capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD) from the foreign investor's account to the corporation's CAD or USD account maintained at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The remittance instruction must contain a clear purpose code such as 'DC' (direct investment), 'INV' (investment-related remittance), or an equivalent bank-specific code, and an explicit narrative stating 'capital contribution' or 'share subscription'. The receiving bank will require a board resolution authorizing the issuance, a subscription agreement and share certificate, updated corporate register extracts, and evidence of exemption from the QST/GST where applicable. Canada imposes no exchange control filing for inbound FDI; reporting obligations arise only under the Statistics Act for the quarterly survey when applicable.Outbound disbursements for an M&A boutique, including cross-border sub-advisor payments, due diligence provider settlements, success-fee payments to non-resident partners, and expense reimbursements, are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 for credit transfers in CAD, USD, EUR, and GBP. Large Canadian-dollar payments clear through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); USD payments clear via SWIFT/CHIPS or correspondent banking channels. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are utilised only where a documentary trade settlement obligation is embedded in the transaction; in such cases, the issuing Canadian bank transmits via SWIFT MT700 and later receives SWIFT MT710/MT710? documentary presentations.
