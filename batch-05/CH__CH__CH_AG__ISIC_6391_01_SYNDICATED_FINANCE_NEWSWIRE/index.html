Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), under the technical supervision of the Federal Commercial Registry Authority in the Federal Office of Justice.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, SR 220), Articles 620–763 (rights of the company limited by shares), in particular Articles 626 (incorporation requirements), 628 (articles of association), 629 (share capital and payment), 630 (incorporation report), 632 (share subscription declaration), 634 (commercial register entry), 716b (inalienable board duties) and 727 (optional audit); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV) Articles 87–101.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation and Articles of Association (Statuten/Acte constitutif), filed electronically via the notary as an Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung gemäss Art. 92 HRegV).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital CHF 100,000, of which at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration; the funds must be deposited on a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank or securities firm and remain blocked until the company is entered in the Commercial Register, upon which the bank releases the funds; contributions in kind must be fully covered by the incorporation report.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal building-use occupancy approval and fire-police inspection certification for the office at Baarerstrasse 21, 6300 Zug; structural load confirmation under SIA 261 for open-plan office use; mandatory employer registration with SUVA under Article 66 UVG for occupational accident insurance; compliance with the Ordinance on Safety and Health Protection at Work (ArGV/SolV), including electrical testing of IT equipment under the Low-Voltage Ordinance (NEV), first-aid provision, and an internal emergency plan; civil-defense requirements are limited to the landlord's building-level obligational measures.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital CHF 100,000, of which at least 20% (CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration; the funds must be deposited on a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank or securities firm and remain blocked until the company is entered in the Commercial Register, upon which the bank releases the funds; contributions in kind must be fully covered by the incorporation report.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 6391 (news agency activities). The Canton of Zug Office for Economic Affairs and Labour (Amt für Wirtschaft und Arbeit, AWA) serves as the administrative point of contact for a formal no-license confirmation; the Federal Department of Finance and FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) have no licensing jurisdiction over non-banking financial news aggregation and syndication services.

Category: No sectoral operating license is required: the definitive Commercial Register extract (Handelsregisterauszug) constitutes the sole operating authorization for the unlicensed financial newswire business. Upon request, FINMA may issue a written no-obligation letter (Nichtbewilligungspflicht) confirming that the activity does not fall under the Financial Institutions Act or Banking Act.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV - Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung; Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC; Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni, AFC), official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax offices separately administer cantonal and communal corporate income tax, capital tax, and certain registration requirements for Swiss permanent establishments.

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG), registration in the Commercial Register automatically allocates the Swiss Business Identification Number (UID; Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the cantonal Commercial Register office and the Central UID Service; the UID serves as the Swiss VAT identifier (MWST-Nummer). For a foreign entity with Swiss VAT obligations, the SOP is: (i) determine whether the foreign entity has a Swiss permanent establishment or a Swiss VAT nexus; (ii) if taxable supplies in Switzerland exceed CHF 100,000 per business year, or if voluntary registration is desired, submit the official VAT registration application to the FTA through the 'SUITE' online portal; (iii) provide constitutional documents, foreign registration evidence, expected Swiss turnover, business description, and, where required, a Swiss fiscal representative; (iv) obtain the Swiss UID / VAT number in the format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx; (v) register any Swiss permanent establishment with the relevant cantonal tax administration for direct tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Swiss AG is a distinct legal person with limited liability, subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax (direct federal tax), cantonal and communal corporate income taxes, and capital tax. There is no pass-through status. The AG is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle; foreign ownership is permitted, but distributions to shareholders are separately taxed at shareholder level under applicable domestic and treaty rules. — For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG), registration in the Commercial Register automatically allocates the Swiss Business Identification Number (UID; Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via the cantonal Commercial Register office and the Central UID Service; the UID serves as the Swiss VAT identifier (MWST-Nummer). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Swiss AG is a distinct legal person with limited liability, subject to Swiss federal corporate income tax (direct federal tax), cantonal and communal corporate income taxes, and capital tax. There is no pass-through status. The AG is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle; foreign ownership is permitted, but distributions to shareholders are separately taxed at shareholder level under applicable domestic and treaty rules.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US-style check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch classification: an AG is per se a corporate taxpayer. Available statutory elections include: (i) Swiss VAT grouping under Art. 13 VAT Act for financially, economically, and organizationally integrated enterprises, subject to five-year commitment rules; (ii) voluntary Swiss VAT registration by opting out of the small-business exemption even where turnover is below CHF 100,000; (iii) in reorganisations, tax-neutral restructurings at book value under the Swiss Merger Act and tax law. Anti-hybrid rules under BEPS Action 2 are mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 gross taxable turnover from taxable supplies made in Switzerland during a business year, excluding turnover from VAT-exempt supplies and certain intra-group services. Foreign enterprises supplying goods or services into Switzerland are also subject to Swiss VAT registration if the same CHF 100,000 turnover threshold is exceeded inside Switzerland. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold by waiving the small-business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods delivered from Switzerland qualify for 0% Swiss VAT if the supplier can prove physical export into a foreign customs territory and the goods remain outside Switzerland. Services, including syndicated financial news and financial data APIs, are either outside the scope of Swiss VAT or zero-rated when the place of supply is abroad under the place-of-supply rules of Arts. 18-20 VAT Act. For B2B services, the decisive criterion is the recipient's place of business or fixed establishment: if the recipient is a foreign taxable person and effective use is not attributable to a Swiss establishment, no Swiss VAT is chargeable. The supplier must obtain and retain business evidence of the foreign recipient's identity, status, and address, together with service contracts, order correspondence, and proof of performance to substantiate the foreign place of supply.

  • Swiss customs export declaration (Zollausfuhranmeldung / e-dec export proof) showing departure from Switzerland and destination country
  • Official customs export certificate, electronic exit confirmation, or certified border-crossing documentation
  • Commercial invoice and final Swiss VAT invoice issued at the 0% rate with all statutory particulars
  • Transport document: bill of lading (B/L), airway bill (AWB), CMR road consignment note, or equivalent multimodal transport evidence
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT confirmation, or bank statement evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign customer
  • Foreign customer identification, including VAT registration number or official company register extract, and the underlying contract demonstrating foreign place of supply

Switzerland does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, SAF-T filing, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for VAT invoices. VAT invoices must satisfy Art. 26 VAT Act substantive requirements: supplier and recipient identification (including UID where applicable), date of invoice, date of supply, nature and beneficiary of the transaction, tax rate or exemption/zero-rating reference, and VAT amount where chargeable. Electronic invoices (eBill, QR-bill, PDF, EDI, XML) are valid if displayed with the statutory particulars. During audit, the FTA may require electronic extraction of invoice data, but there is no pre-clearance approval obligation.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict Swiss Federal Tax Administration scrutiny in ordinary audits and retrospective reviews. If the documentation bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, fabricated, or insufficient to prove physical export or foreign place of supply, the FTA retroactively assesses Swiss VAT at the standard rate (currently 8.1%) together with statutory interest from the due date. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims trigger administrative and criminal sanctions under Arts. 70-71 VAT Act, including monetary penalties up to three times the evaded tax, imprisonment in serious cases, confiscation of proceeds, and loss of the zero-rating privilege. Retention of the complete export file for the prescribed commercial and tax record-keeping period (generally 10 years) is mandatory; the FTA can reopen and claw back incorrectly zero-rated VAT within the applicable limitation periods for intentional or grossly negligent tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is freely convertible on both current and capital account; Switzerland has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, so no restrictions or approvals apply to payments or transfers for current international transactions or capital movements. Profit remittance (dividends) is unrestricted; the technical obstacle is the 35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer), which is reduced or repaid under the applicable double taxation treaty via relief-at-source or refund procedures. Capital contributions, shareholder loans, trade payments, and treasury flows in any convertible currency may be routed without exchange-control permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio (effective ratio: 0%). Export receipts denominated in CHF or foreign currency may be retained, reinvested, or converted at the exporter's discretion. Resident businesses only have statistical balance-of-payments reporting duties to the SNB under its direct-reporting minimum thresholds and must comply with AMLA cash-transaction declarations; no repatriation timeline is legally prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank/Banca nazionale svizzera) is the central bank and monetary authority; there is no separate foreign-exchange control administration. FINMA supervises banks for AML/conduct, and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) handles external financial policy, but no FX approval body exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Standalone viability is Low-to-Medium: foreign fintech and digital EMI platforms are not automatically recognized as banks in Switzerland and may not take deposits or run payment systems without a FINMA banking license, a fintech license (up to CHF 100 million in deposits without interest), or a payments institution license under FinIA/FinSA. Non-EU EMIs lack direct access to Swiss SIC or SEPA clearing and therefore must partner with a licensed Swiss bank or hold an account at a licensed custody agent; use as a treasury gateway is possible for payment aggregation but not as the primary corporate bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% direct federal corporate income tax (on after-tax profit); combined federal/cantonal/communal corporate tax rate: 11.5%–24% depending on canton (e.g., Zug ~11.5%, Zurich ~21.2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends distributed by a Swiss AG; no additional cantonal WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland has no statutory local-file/master-file monetary threshold. Written contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected for all material cross-border intercompany transactions upon FTA inquiry (art. 58 DBG). Country-by-Country Reporting mandatory for consolidated groups with annual revenue above CHF 900 million; Swiss local filing triggers when the ultimate parent with CHF 900m+ revenue has a Swiss subsidiary. Practical local file preparation recommended when related-party revenues exceed CHF 500,000 in a year.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland is not an ESR (EU/UK Economic Substance Regulations) jurisdiction. However, the Federal Tax Administration applies the OECD arm's-length standard and Swiss case-law 'Tatsächliche Leistung' doctrine. A Swiss AG deriving newswire/syndication income must maintain board meetings in Switzerland, substantive staff, business premises, and operating control; a mere mailbox company is denied treaty benefits, is tax resident elsewhere, and can be dissolved by competent authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Swiss direct federal tax evasion penalty: fine equal to 1.5–3x evaded tax plus re-assessment; qualified tax fraud (forged books, false certificates) imposes an additional criminal fine of up to CHF 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under Art. 174 DBG and Art. 186 VStG.

Director personal liability: Board members and finance directors who knowingly sign false returns are criminally liable and can be personally imprisoned up to 5 years; restitution of evaded tax plus VAT is joint and several; severe cases trigger civil damages from shareholders and global travel-bans/arrest.
Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Federal Tax Administration disregards the corporate entity (substance-over-form), allocates income to actual owners, denies DTA entitlement, imposes 35% withholding tax on deemed distributions, and may order compulsory deregistration of the AG under Swiss Code of Obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for unpaid withholding taxes (Art. 12 VStG) and for tax debts if the company becomes insolvent due to false balance sheets; travel bans and criminal prosecution for bankruptcy fraud (Art. 163 SCC) up to 5 years imprisonment can be imposed.
AML & Payment Route Abuse

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Art. 305bis SCC punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years (qualified commercial laundering up to 5 years) and confiscation of proceeds. If the company is not a registered financial intermediary but accepts suspicious payments, FINMA may issue a no-business finding and impose administrative fines up to CHF 1 million; failure to report suspicious transactions triggers criminal liability if the entity cannot rely on the AML exemption for non-regulated media.

Director personal liability: Company directors personally implicated in accepting or executing tainted wire transfers face prosecution, asset freezing, and travel bans; civil liability for damages under Art. 41/50 OR. Regulatory enforcement can extend to permanent management prohibition by FINMA.
