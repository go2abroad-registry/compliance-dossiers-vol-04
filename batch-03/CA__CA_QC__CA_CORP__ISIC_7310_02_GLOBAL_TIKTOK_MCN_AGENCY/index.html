Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST, payroll and Part I corporate income tax; portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency). Revenu Québec — Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), Quebec corporate income tax (CO-17) and employer remittances; portal: Revenu Québec 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' (www.revenuquebec.ca). The two authorities coordinate under the Canada–Québec Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement and the GST/HST–QST joint remittance window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Quebec business corporation registers through the CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) one-window service, obtaining a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts 'RC' (federal corporate income tax), 'RT' (GST/HST) and 'RP' (payroll), plus a provincial Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) issued by the Registraire des entreprises; Revenu Québec then assigns the QST program account under the collection agreement. Non-resident corporations file Form RC1/RC4312 with the CRA to establish carrying-on-business nexus before the program accounts are activated, and the QST account remains subject to Revenu Québec's separate audit jurisdiction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Quebec business corporation registers through the CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) one-window service, obtaining a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts 'RC' (federal corporate income tax), 'RT' (GST/HST) and 'RP' (payroll), plus a provincial Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) issued by the Registraire des entreprises; Revenu Québec then assigns the QST program account under the collection agreement. Non-resident corporations file Form RC1/RC4312 with the CRA to establish carrying-on-business nexus before the program accounts are activated, and the QST account remains subject to Revenu Québec's separate audit jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; a business corporation is invariably a taxable Canadian corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income. Relevant elections include: (i) fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1; (ii) GST/HST and QST 'Quick Method' elections for qualifying small suppliers (Form GST74 / Revenu Québec VD-505); (iii) ETA s. 267 election among closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (iv) going-concern sale election under ETA s. 167 and QST Act s. 202 to zero-rate a transfer of a business universality; (v) hybrid-mismatch integrity rules (Income Tax Act ss. 12.8, 17.3, 21.2, 27.3, 59.4, 63.5, enacted by Bill C-59 in 2024) operate automatically to deny deductions on cross-border hybrid instruments—no election can opt out of those rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating currency with complete capital and current account convertibility under the Currency Act and the dismantled Foreign Exchange Control Act (repealed 1951). No exchange control approval is required for inbound equity injections, dividend remittances, or trade-related payments. Profit repatriation from a Quebec business corporation is unrestricted; however, dividends and deemed royalties paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, reducible under applicable tax treaties), and cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Canada Revenue Agency on Form RC599 or through FINTRAC large-transaction filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Export and service receipts, including USD-denominated TikTok creator settlements and MCN commission income, may be fully retained in domestic or foreign-currency bank accounts. Entities holding foreign property (including foreign bank balances, receivables, or shares of non-resident affiliates) with a cost amount exceeding CAD 100,000 must file Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement). All foreign-held revenue is freely convertible to CAD at prevailing market rates via an authorized Canadian dealer (Schedule I bank or registered MSB).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Department of Finance Canada, and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada). The Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) supervises deposit-taking institutions and derivatives dealing in Quebec.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM — legally viable only as a secondary payment-clearing rail, not as the primary banking relationship. Wise (Wise Payments Canada / Wise B2B) operates in Canada as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB), not a bank, and therefore cannot hold statutory escrow deposits, offer CDIC-insured deposits, or provide lending. Offshore EMIs (e.g., EU-licensed digital banks or e-money institutions without Canadian MSB registration) face severe regulatory friction and are generally NOT viable for a Quebec-incorporated operating entity because they cannot clear in CAD through Lynx or the Canadian retail payment system. Recommended structure: primary relationship at a Schedule I chartered bank or Quebec credit union; Wise B2B used exclusively for low-value cross-border creator micro-payouts and multi-currency receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a foreign shareholder must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank transfers) to the corporation's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or Desjardins caisses. The transfer narrative should specify 'Capital Contribution to Business Corporation' or 'Equity Subscription under Share Subscription Agreement'. Under FINTRAC, the receiving institution must file a Large Virtual Currency Transaction Report or Large Cash/EFT Transaction Report for amounts at or above CAD 10,000, and the CRA requires Form RC599 for cross-border currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 when physically transported; for electronic transfers, the bank's reporting obligation suffices. Supporting verification: signed share-subscription resolution, certificate of incorporation, Quebec EPC escrow deposit instructions (if applicable), and a source-of-wealth letter. No Bank of Canada approval is required; witholding tax for dividends on the injected capital begins only upon actual distribution.Primary clearing rails for creator compensation and MCN operating disbursements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for single high-value creator settlements and licensing fees; (2) Canada's high-value payment system 'Lynx' for CAD-denominated domestic transactions above CAD 100,000; (3) Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value instant CAD payments where deployed; (4) Wise Business API (FINTRAC-registered MSB) for recurring cross-border micro-payouts to creators in multiple jurisdictions; (5) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) only where a supplier of production or advertising services requires documentary credit support — uncommon for MCN agency services and generally not recommended due to documentation cost. Batch disbursements should be submitted with standardized remittance advice referencing the relevant creator agreement to preserve the audit trail for AML review.
