Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000 (Art. 632 para. 1 CO). At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal capital and in any case no less than CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash (Art. 632 para. 2 CO). Paid-up funds are credited to a Swiss bank blocked account (Sperrkonto); the bank's written payment confirmation is delivered to the notary. Contributions in kind require a formation audit report (Gründungsprüfung / rapport de révision) by a licensed audit firm. No separate escrow agent is required beyond the blocked-account mechanism.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification pursuant to cantonal fire protection directives (VKF/AEAI) with structural load and stability inspection of premises under SIA standards 260-267; occupational health and safety clearance under Arts. 31-32 and 49 of the Swiss Labour Act (ArG, SR 822.11), including SUVA accident-prevention directives; for vessels and shore operations: SOLAS/ISM safety certificates issued by a recognized classification society and ISPS security clearance where RoRo terminals or port facilities are operated in Switzerland.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital CHF 100,000 (Art. 632 para. 1 CO). At incorporation, at least 20% of the nominal capital and in any case no less than CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash (Art. 632 para. 2 CO). Paid-up funds are credited to a Swiss bank blocked account (Sperrkonto); the bank's written payment confirmation is delivered to the notary. Contributions in kind require a formation audit report (Gründungsprüfung / rapport de révision) by a licensed audit firm. No separate escrow agent is required beyond the blocked-account mechanism.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first complete Swiss Commercial Register registration with a registered legal address and authorized Swiss representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is an independent legal entity subject to entity-level taxation; there is no US-style check-the-box pass-through election. Available statutory elections include group relief under the Swiss tax grouping regime (Tragrechtsreform / contribution agreements), VAT group membership (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) for fiscal unity, and optional OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch elections where applicable. Switzerland also allows unilateral credit relief for foreign permanent establishment income through the applicable double tax treaty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully and freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (CPIA) imposes no quantitative restrictions, prior approvals, or licensing requirements on inward or outward payments. Profit remittance is lawful without any FX authorization, subject only to the 35% federal withholding tax on dividends (reclaimable under treaty or creditable for resident shareholders) and to corporate-law distributable-reserve requirements under Art. 660 CO. The Swiss AG may freely repatriate income, repay shareholder loans, fund cross-border disbursements, and hold multi-currency treasury accounts without currency-convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A Swiss exporter or Ro-Ro carrier is limited to ordinary financial-reporting duties under Art. 957 CO: export receivables must be recognized at transaction-date FX rates and may remain in foreign currency indefinitely. No central-bank filing, conversion declaration, or reinvestment obligation applies to proceeds received in CHF, EUR, USD, or other convertible trade currencies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse — holding sole statutory authority over Swiss currency and monetary policy under the National Bank Act of 2003. Switzerland maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; the SNB's FX activity is limited to monetary-policy intervention (Art. 5 NBA) and does not include licensing, approval, or surrender oversight of private-sector currency flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury relationship and MEDIUM as an ancillary collections or disbursement channel. Switzerland is not an EEA member, so EU/UK electronic-money-institution passports do not extend into Swiss territory. A foreign EMI must either hold a Swiss banking licence (Art. 1a Banking Act) or a FINMA fintech licence (Art. 1b Banking Act, limited to public deposits of up to CHF 100 million and no interest-bearing accounts) to service a Swiss-resident corporate treasury. Offshore digital EMIs may nevertheless process EUR or USD freight collections from non-Swiss charterers, provided funds are swept to the Swiss tier-1 operating account on a T+1 or T+2 basis; the bank will require full transparency on the EMI's regulatory status, SWIFT ordering-party data, and the ultimate funding route of payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be routed via the SWIFT interbank network using MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV where the funds pass through a correspondent bank before crediting the Swiss beneficiary. The payment-priority and purpose fields must reference the commercial-register amendment and include a precise descriptor such as 'Capital injection — contribution to share capital of CH_AG'. The receiving FINMA-licensed bank verifies the transfer against the notarised board resolution, the registered share-capital increase, and the AML source-of-funds dossier; funds are released to the Operating Account only after internal confirmation and, for a definitive capital increase, certification of payment by the statutory auditor.Outbound operational payments — freight disbursements, port and terminal costs, bunker supply, crewing wages, and agency fees — are settled through SIC (CHF domestic clearing) and euroSIC (EUR pan-European clearing) for continental payments, and through SWIFT MT103 via correspondent banks for USD and other trade currencies. Documentary credit settlements use MT700 (issuance) and MT707 (amendment), governed by UCP600 and ISBP 745. For the Ro-Ro carrier's voyage-desk disbursements, banks require vessel IMO number, port-agent contract, and supporting commercial invoices before executing each outbound MT103, reducing diversion risk and trade-mispricing exposure.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards in CHF/EUR/USD, bilateral or CLS-settled, covered by an ISDA FX Annex or master agreement with standard CSA collateral terms.FX swaps combining spot and forward legs for treasury cash-flow bridging of mismatched freight-receipt and disbursement timings.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS), permitted only where a documented commercial loan, charter obligation, or vessel-financing receivable exists and where the hedge tenor does not exceed the underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), permitted exclusively for receivables or payables in currencies that are not freely deliverable onshore (e.g., emerging-market route currencies), with reference-price fixing per ISDA definitions and cash settlement in CHF, EUR, or USD without physical delivery.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% direct federal corporate income tax (after-tax basis; pre-tax equivalent 7.83%), plus cantonal and communal profit taxes varying between approximately 11.9% and 21.0% combined effective rate depending on canton.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) imposed on Swiss dividends paid by an AG, including liquidating distributions of reserves; under applicable DTA the reduced rate is commonly 0%, 5%, or 15% depending on the beneficial owner, shareholding percentage, and holding period.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed with the competent cantonal tax authority generally within six months after the balance-sheet date; for a calendar-year Swiss AG the practical deadline is regularly 30 June of the following year, with written extensions available on justified application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland imposes no statutory monetary threshold for Local File or Master File, but the Swiss tax authorities require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation when cross-border related-party transactions exist; group Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory where consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CHF 900 million; Swiss tax practice follows OECD Chapters I-III.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has not enacted OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, Swiss administrative and judicial practice applies economic-substance, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse doctrines when determining treaty entitlement, participation exemption, and permanent-establishment attribution. A Swiss shipping AG must maintain real commercial substance in Switzerland: board meetings and decisions, local employees or qualified service agreements, management of strategic shipping risks, and operational control. A passive 'flagged' entity with no substantive shore-based management is exposed to treaty denial, profit reattribution, DTA claim rejection, and retroactive withholding-tax exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung / Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment based on estimated profits; default interest as from each statutory due date; administrative tax-evasion surcharge of up to 100% of the evaded federal and cantonal tax; criminal tax fraud proceedings may additionally impose fines and imprisonment of up to five years; statute of limitations is generally ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or organise disgorged profits may be jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; Swiss tax enforcement can issue travel restrictions (Ausweissperre) against responsible directors; personal declarations of criminal guilt can trigger civil restitution actions and disqualification under the Code of Obligations.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the Swiss legal entity as abusive, reattribute profits to the beneficial owner, deny DTA and participation exemption, recapture Swiss withholding tax at 35% on payments that are recharacterised as dividends, and impose tax evasion penalties on the adjusted basis; the Commercial Register may order dissolution for abandonment of lawful corporate purpose.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for restitution of wrongfully paid dividends and any withholding-tax recapture, face board disqualification, profiled travel bans, and criminal accountability for the misuse of shell-law structures; if insolvency follows tax re-assessment, directors must answer for breach of fiduciary duty under Article 754 of the Swiss Code of Obligations.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory transfer-pricing adjustment against profits, additional federal and cantonal corporate income tax, cumulative compound default interest at circa 4.5% per annum, procedural fines up to CHF 10,000 for documentation refusals, and in aggravated cases criminal tax proceedings for deliberate under-declaration; high-risk adjustments may also expose the group to double taxation relief denial.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for intercompany pricing approvals face personal liability for the additional tax and interest in the event of insolvency through negligent implementation of arm's length principles; Swiss legal doctrine allows extension of tax debts to de facto directors; travel restrictions and disqualification can follow enforcement in civil and criminal proceedings.
Withholding-Tax Abusive Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will reclassify such payments as taxable dividends, imposing 35% withholding tax on the payer, an additional federal and cantonal penalty surcharge of up to 100% for wilfulness, default interest from the date of payment, and refusal of foreign tax credit benefits; criminal prosecution for withholding-tax evasion may include imprisonment and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the abusive distribution become personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax as the statutory withholding agent; enforcement mechanisms include attachment of Swiss assets, prohibition from leaving Switzerland where civil claims are significant, and personal criminal fines or custodial sentences under the Swiss Withholding Tax Act.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal investigation under Swiss embargo, sanctions and anti-money-laundering legislation; assets can be frozen and forfeited; companies and directors receive administrative fines that may reach up to three times the sanctioned value for severe violations; banking and government contracts could be terminated; reputational damage can trigger cross-border enforcement from foreign authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment of up to five years for intentional sanctions offences, personal civil claims for consequential losses, travel bans and international arrest warrants in serious cases, plus individual exposure to confiscation orders; breach of due diligence duties under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act and Code of Obligations sustains direct actions against director personal assets.
