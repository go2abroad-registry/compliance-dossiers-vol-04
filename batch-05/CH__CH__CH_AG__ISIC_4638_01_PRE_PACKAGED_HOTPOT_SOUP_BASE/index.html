Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), VAT Domain; official portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; electronic filing via the Swiss Federal Tax Administration 'eService' and Secure Electronic Data Interchange (SUKE).

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is automatically registered in the Central Business Index (Zentraler Unternehmensindex) and obtains a Swiss Business Identifier (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID) upon commercial registry entry. A foreign enterprise carrying on taxable supplies in Switzerland or receiving supplies that shift VAT liability must obtain a Swiss VAT number by filing the Swiss VAT registration application through the ESTV portal before the VAT obligation arises; if a UID is not yet held, the registration process simultaneously allocates one. Registration threshold: CHF 100,000 in annual non-exempt turnover from taxable supplies. For foreign businesses not resident in Switzerland, the competent cantonal authority may require a Swiss tax representative for VAT purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss AG is a separate corporate taxpayer under corporate income tax and VAT, with resident unlimited tax liability on worldwide income plus preferential territorial treatment (exemption with progression) for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. — A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is automatically registered in the Central Business Index (Zentraler Unternehmensindex) and obtains a Swiss Business Identifier (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer, UID) upon commercial registry entry. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Swiss AG is a separate corporate taxpayer under corporate income tax and VAT, with resident unlimited tax liability on worldwide income plus preferential territorial treatment (exemption with progression) for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate.

Available election forms

Swiss VAT provides: (i) voluntary registration even below the CHF 100,000 threshold; (ii) VAT group taxation under Art. 12 VAT Act allowing controlled legal units to be treated as one taxable person; (iii) option to tax real estate letting transactions that are otherwise VAT-exempt. Switzerland maintains no US-type check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch entity elections under federal corporate income tax; hybrid mismatch implications are instead resolved via source and treaty rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in non-exempt annual global turnover received from supplies of goods and services (Art. 10 VAT Act); taxable purchases exceeding CHF 25,000 or entitlement to an input tax refund beyond the relevant threshold can also trigger deemed registration (Art. 11 VAT Act). Reduced rates: 2.6% for basic foodstuffs and 3.8% for hotel accommodation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically transported out of Switzerland to the recipient or the recipient's agent qualifies for the 0% export VAT rate if the supplier transfers ownership and dispatch of the goods takes place abroad (Art. 23 para. 2 VAT Act), with export substantiated by customs documents or accepted digital customs clearance. Related services (loading, transport, processing, quality control and packaging) supplied to a non-Swiss recipient in direct connection with exported goods may also be zero-rated. In addition, certain services rendered to non-residents are not taxable in Switzerland if the economic benefit is effectively transferred outside Swiss territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Export Customs Declaration lodged through the Swiss customs e-dec electronic system, required for physical export proof)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport evidence (CEFTA receipt, airway bill, truck manifest) proving goods left Swiss customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX confirmation that payment was received from a non-Swiss buyer

Switzerland has not mandated real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC); no central government tax clearance of invoices or digital stamping applies. Invoices may be issued electronically and must be kept audit-ready, but use of Swiss eBill, QR-bill, and PEPPOL BIS formats is voluntary unless agreed between the parties. Mandatory invoice content under Art. 26 VAT Act includes the supplier's identity, UID and VAT number, date, recipient, description, amount, and applicable VAT rate or exemption.

ESTV scrutinizes zero-rating claims transaction-by-transaction against customs export records, transport documentation, payment flows and counterparty substance. For insufficient or fraudulent substantiation, the 0% rate is retrospectively disallowed and the VAT shortfall is assessed with default interest. Deliberate evasion or obtaining of unjustified zero-rating triggers criminal tax proceedings and a fine of up to three times the VAT shortfall under Swiss VAT criminal provisions (Art. 103 ff. VAT Act). Records must be retained for six years, and false export declarations may also expose the company to customs penalties and director liability.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully convertible currency regime with no exchange controls on either current or capital account transactions. The Swiss franc (CHF) may be freely converted, and there are no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is subject solely to Swiss withholding tax of 35% on dividends and similar distributions, which may be reduced or refunded under applicable double taxation treaties. No prior exchange control approval is required for cross-border fund movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation requiring export receipts to be surrendered to the central bank or converted into Swiss francs. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any convertible foreign currency, held in Swiss or foreign bank accounts, and used for trade payments, hedging, or reinvestment at the exporter's discretion. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; statutory timeline: not applicable.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for core treasury functions. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) generally cannot provide commercial bank account services to a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft without holding a Swiss banking licence or a FINMA-licensed fintech licence. Unlicensed foreign EMIs may legally partner with Swiss banks for payment initiation and e-money wallets, but they are not a substitute for regulated corporate bank accounts, and their use raises AML, counterparty, and substance concerns. A Swiss licensed fintech or a fully regulated Swiss bank is the compliant route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing requires a SWIFT MT103 or equivalent covering credit transfer from the investing shareholder's bank, clearly citing a valid purpose code such as 'GFOR' (direct investment) or 'SUPP' (loan extended by parent) in the transaction description. The funds must be received into the Swiss entity's designated account at a regulated bank and settled via SIX Interbank Clearing (SIC) in CHF or through Continuous Linked Settlement (CLS) for eligible FX transactions. Compliance verification must include confirmation that the transferor is the registered shareholder or lender, the contractual basis for the capital injection (equity subscription agreement, shareholder loan agreement), and evidence of commercial reasonableness. Anti-money-laundering checks require matching the SWIFT sender reference against the bank's approved KYC file and maintaining records of the inward remittance advice.Outbound trade settlements are executed predominantly over SWIFT MT103 for direct payments and via SWIFT MT700/MT710 documentary credits (Letter of Credit) under UCP600 for secured trade transactions. For supplier payments to high-volume Chinese FMCG exporters, confirmation can be arranged through a confirming bank and settlement made under a commercial LC at sight or deferred payment basis. Urgent high-value CHF and EUR payments clear real-time through SIC; international USD payments flow via correspondent banking networks (e.g., USD CHIPS/ Fedwire).
