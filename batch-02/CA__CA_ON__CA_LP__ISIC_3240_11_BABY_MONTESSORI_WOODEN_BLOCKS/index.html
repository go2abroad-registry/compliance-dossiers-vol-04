Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), GST/HST and Excise Tax program; portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and RepID for non-resident representatives.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Ontario limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Act with ServiceOntario. Step 2: Submit CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the partnership legal name, principal place of business, and requested program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll). Step 3: Where the LP has no Canadian establishment, appoint a Canadian representative/service agent under ETA subsection 273.1(1). Step 4: CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program-account suffix (e.g., RT0001) serving as the partnership's TIN for indirect tax; the LP itself is not the income-tax filer, as partners report their allocable shares using their own TIN. Step 5: Non-resident partners holding an interest in a central-agency-managed LP obtain an NR account and comply with Part XIII withholding using Form 1056.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Ontario LP is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the federal Income Tax Act (income/loss computed at the partnership level but taxed in the hands of partners), with no federal/Ontario income tax imposed on the partnership itself; the sole exception is a SIFT partnership, which is subject to entity-level tax under section 197 ITA when publicly listed and earning non-portfolio income.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections for a cross-border LP: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners taxing Canadian-source rental income on a net basis; (iii) ETA s. 273 partnership-level GST/HST registration election; (iv) ETA s. 253 clearance election to shift GST/HST obligation to a non-resident contractor; (v) quick-method accounting election under ETA s. 223 for small suppliers; (vi) hybrid-mismatch remedial elections under ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5 and 212.3 for dual-inclusion and imported-mismatch relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory GST/HST registration applies when the worldwide taxable supplies of the person (treating the LP as a separate person for ETA purposes) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)); once registered, the LP must collect and remit 13% HST on all taxable supplies made in Ontario unless zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% rate applies under Schedule VI to the ETA where (a) tangible personal property such as beechwood Montessori blocks is sold and shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada by commercial carrier, or delivered to a common carrier for export, and the supplier obtains proof of export within the prescribed period (generally 60 days after delivery to the carrier); and (b) services are supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and the service is performed or delivered outside Canada. The invoice should state 'Zero-rated — Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 / section 27 ETA' and must be supported by export documentation naming the foreign buyer and foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or approved e-manifest/ACE export confirmation showing the goods left Canada)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill issued by the commercial carrier evidencing delivery to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank/FinTech payment confirmation strictly identifying the export sale proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory continuous-connection system, or digital stamping exists federally or in Ontario; e-invoicing is voluntary and implemented through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network. Mandatory invoice content is prescribed by the ETA Regulations and section 223 ETA (supplier/recipient identity, description, quantity, tax charged, and invoice date). The LP must store all books, invoices, and payment-rail records (Stripe, Amazon Pay) in a readable format for six years under ETA ss. 286-287; Quebec's B2B e-invoicing mandate does not extend to Ontario entities.

CRA conducts targeted export-verification audits that match zero-rated claim files against CBSA export transaction data; unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating of domestic sales results in reassessment of the full 13% HST, daily prescribed interest, and penalties: (i) ETA s. 285(1) false-omission penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax; (ii) s. 280 willful evasion/culpable neglect penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of tax plus doubling on repeat violations; (iii) prosecution under ITA/ETA s. 327 with fines of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment of up to two years; (iv) clawback of previously allowed input tax credits tied to the zero-rated supply stream, and possible revocation of the supplier's electronic filing and refund privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control licensing procedures for trade settlements, capital injections, or profit repatriation. Dividends and partnership distributions to non-resident partners are freely remittable, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% or a lower treaty rate (e.g., 15% under the Canada–U.S. Tax Treaty) and, for the LP, completion of CRS/FATCA self-certification forms. E-commerce receipts denominated in USD or CAD can be converted at market rates. The only friction points are the FINTRAC reporting threshold for cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 and bank-level source-of-funds verification on account opening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriating or converting export receipts. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency denominated operating accounts (e.g., USD) at Canadian domestic banks and convert to CAD only when settlement obligations require it.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administration is liberalized with no dedicated exchange-control bureau, and prudential oversight of clearing institutions is allocated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) with AML/CFT supervision by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability, ancillary only. Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate in Canada as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses rather than banks or EU-style Electronic Money Institutions. They may provide foreign-currency payout rails and mass-pay APIs, but they cannot accept Canadian demand deposits, are not CDIC-insured, and do not replace a Tier-1 operational account. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment service providers that perform end-to-end clearing must register with the Bank of Canada; the LP should maintain these providers only as secondary rails for marketplaces or small-value supplier payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign remitting bank to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian account, with field 70 remittance information reading 'LP capital contribution for working capital under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act' and the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) of the LP included where applicable. The Canadian recipient bank will validate the underlying partnership documentation, beneficial-ownership declarations, and FATCA/CRS self-certifications before crediting the funds. CAD settlement occurs through Lynx (Canada's high-value payment system) or via a correspondent-bank intermediary; the wiring party must complete source-of-funds disclosure for any aggregate EFT exceeding CAD 10,000, and the bank may require a FINTRAC Large Cash/Electronic Funds Transfer Report. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not applicable at the LP entity level because the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (Canada). A corporate limited partner is taxed on its allocated share of Ontario-source income at the combined federal and Ontario general corporate rate of 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%). An eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) limited partner that preserves the small business deduction pays a combined 12.2% (federal 9% + Ontario 3.2%) on its first CAD 500,000 of active business income allocated from the LP. Individual limited partners are taxed at graduated federal and Ontario personal rates on their conforming partnership allocations.
