Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administrator and GST/HST collection agent; Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a Tax Collection Agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; CRA business portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html).

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent incorporates a Canadian subsidiary under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or Ontario Business Corporations Act), then obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the GST/HST account adds program code 'RT' plus a 4-digit reference number (e.g., 123456789 RT 0001). The BN application can be made concurrently with corporate income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and GST/HST registration; a person carrying on business in Canada without incorporation must also obtain a BN and may require an extra-provincial license from Ontario's Ministry of Government and Consumer Services.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent incorporates a Canadian subsidiary under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or Ontario Business Corporations Act), then obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the GST/HST account adds program code 'RT' plus a 4-digit reference number (e.g., 123456789 RT 0001). The BN application can be made concurrently with corporate income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and GST/HST registration; a person carrying on business in Canada without incorporation must also obtain a BN and may require an extra-provincial license from Ontario's Ministry of Government and Consumer Services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) functional currency election to prepare returns in a foreign currency (ITA s. 261); (ii) election under ITA s. 216 for non-residents to report Canadian rental/timber income on a net basis; (iii) GST/HST closely-related corporation election to treat certain internal supplies as outside the scope of tax (Excise Tax Act s. 156); (iv) prescribed elections under the hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3) for affected cross-border entities. Election (iii) is relevant for a cold-storage operator that holds a Canadian warehouse in one legal entity and manages it in another.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold) measured over the four preceding calendar quarters and any single calendar quarter (ETA s. 148); non-resident registrants, including foreign-owned warehouse operators carrying on business in Canada, must register once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may claim 0% GST/HST on (i) supplies of goods actually exported from Canada where the registrant retains documentary proof of export (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 1); and (ii) supplies of services to a non-resident person, including warehousing and cold storage services performed on the non-resident's goods, provided the service is not a supply in respect of real property situated in Canada and does not fall within an excepted service category (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 5). Storage of goods owned by a Canadian-resident customer is standard-rated at 13% HST even if the goods are ultimately exported, unless the supply itself is part of an exported good contract. The claim requires the warehouse operator to hold contemporaneous contracts, invoices, customs declarations, transportation documents, and records identifying the non-resident recipient.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (electronic export data filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System or legacy Form B13A where the goods are physically exported from Canada)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing port of loading in Canada and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated supply contract with the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign exchange receipt where proceeds are settled (not a CRA-stipulated document, but standard corroboration of a non-resident supply)

Canada has no federal real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or mandatory e-invoice clearance platform. CRA accepts electronic invoices where key data fields are retained and records are kept for six years; GST/HST filings must be submitted electronically by registrants with annual worldwide taxable supplies over CAD 1 million, and non-resident registrants filing GST/HST without a permanent establishment are required to file on a basis that may include electronic transmission, security, and information requirements prescribed by the CRA. Ontario does not operate a separate digital stamping system for commercial invoices.

CRA audit programs (including the Export Verification and refund integrity reviews) require proof the goods left Canada within prescribed periods and that the recipient is non-resident. Where zero-rating is disallowed, CRA assesses 13% HST plus interest and, if the misrepresentation was due to neglect, carelessness, or willful default, the four-year reassessment period is suspended indefinitely (ETA s. 298(6); ITA s. 152(4)). Penalties include the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax for false statements (ETA s. 280(1)), up to 50% of the additional tax for gross negligence, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and potential imprisonment. Input tax credits previously claimed on the storage facility can be clawed back where the zero-rated export position is overturned.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar (CAD) operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; there are no exchange controls, prior-approval mechanisms, repatriation permits, or central bank authorization requirements for inbound equity injections, intra-company loans, dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees. The only structural frictions are tax-driven: non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25% statutory, reduced to as low as 5% on dividends under applicable tax treaties with a meaningful shareholding threshold) and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore minimal and documentary, not regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Canada imposes no mandatory conversion ratio and no deadline for repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds from cold-storage service revenue may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts held at Canadian Schedule I banks, converted at commercial bank rates, or netted against import disbursements. The relevant obligations are (i) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting above CAD 10,000 and (ii) books and records maintenance in Canadian dollars for income tax purposes, with foreign-currency gains or losses characterized under section 39 of the Income Tax Act unless the income-account election applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority and Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registrar for payment service providers. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration; residual supervisory mandates rest with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering and cross-border wire reporting, and Global Affairs Canada for sanctions administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM - Legal constraints are material. Offshore digital EMIs cannot act as the primary settlement account because access to Payments Canada clearing rails (ACSS for ACH and Lynx for large-value CAD wires) requires membership or sponsorship through a regulated Canadian bank or a registered payment service provider. Since November 1, 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires non-resident payment service providers to register with the Bank of Canada before offering payment services to Canadian end-users; FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration is separately triggered where foreign exchange dealing is involved. Offshore EMIs remain viable only as supplementary disbursement utilities (e.g., virtual cards for supplier payouts) and must be backed by a Canadian Schedule I settlement account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 annual aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border controlled transactions with each related non-resident; smaller service-related transactions may trigger CRA documentation if individual value exceeds CAD 500,000

Economic substance scope: Canada maintains no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but applies the GAAR (section 245 ITA) and treaty limitations under the OECD Multilateral Instrument. Cold-storage logistics operators must show real physical presence (facilities, employees, management) in Ontario and charge arm's-length prices for all intra-group services or the CRA may recharacterize income and challenge treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239 – fine of 50% to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; CRA gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s.163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or acquiesce to evasion are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debt under ITA s.227.1 (source deductions and GST) and may face civil restitution orders, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Hidden Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment with upward TP adjustments; ITA s.163(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000); daily arrears interest from the original due date

Director personal liability: Officers signing false or incomplete TP documentation may be criminally charged for aiding tax evasion; directors may also face personal civil exposure under section 227.1 if they knowingly allow unpaid tax liabilities.
AML / Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act – FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty for entities up to CAD 2,125,000; Criminal Code of Canada sanctions up to CAD 5,000,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment for laundering offense

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally exposed to FINTRAC penalties if they knowingly directed, sanctioned, or failed to prevent non-compliance; criminal conviction may result in incarceration, a formal travel ban, and disqualification from board service.
