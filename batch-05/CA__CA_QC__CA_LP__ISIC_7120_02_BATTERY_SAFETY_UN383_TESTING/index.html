Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods from Canada and to offshore supplies of services performed entirely outside Canada. Eligibility requires the supplier to export tangible goods or perform services abroad, with the supply not constituting a taxable supply in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration

Taxpayers must file GST/QST returns and remittances electronically via My Account. Digital invoicing is required for certain large taxpayers under Quebec's digital tax administration rules; real-time fiscal clearance is not universally mandated but API integrations and electronic stamping are available through authorized providers.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit export zero-rating claims. Penalties include 10% of the tax evaded plus interest and up to 5% per month (maximum 50%). Fraudulent claims may result in criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment; retrospective clawback of zero-rated amounts occurs if documentation is insufficient or false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating and fully convertible currency. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted: inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners into the Quebec LP, trade disbursements, loan servicing, partnership distributions, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds do not require Bank of Canada approval, exchange permits, or currency allocation. Practical hurdles relate only to (i) FINTRAC EFT reporting for every cross-border transfer at or above CAD 10,000, (ii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act, and (iii) tax compliance, notably Part XIII non-resident withholding on certain distributions to foreign partners, subject to treaty reduction. Banks will classify the battery-safety testing lab as Low_Risk_Standard but will nonetheless document the commercial purpose of each cross-border flow in the bank-general-ledger.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime exists in Canada. There is no legal timeline requiring the Quebec LP to repatriate foreign-currency testing revenues, and no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed on export receipts. Testing fees invoiced in USD or EUR may be retained in foreign-currency operating accounts at any Schedule I bank, used directly for offshore subcontractor settlement, or converted into CAD at commercial spot rates at the LP's discretion. Cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting, but this is a surveillance requirement, not a surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as the monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian bank notes, with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) as the prudential regulator of federally incorporated banks. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border EFT and AML/ATF reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its Regulations. Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control licensing authority or foreign-exchange administration agency; capital-movement oversight is embedded in OSFI supervision, FINTRAC transaction reporting, Global Affairs Canada sanctions administration, and the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - permissible strictly as a linked payment rail, not as the primary treasury host. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc., a FINTRAC-registered money services business) is legally available for low-value cross-border supplier payments, calibration-vendor disbursements, and staff reimbursements in CAD/USD/EUR at mid-market rates, with sub-accounts titled in the exact legal name of the LP. However, a FINTRAC MSB/EMI cannot provide commercial letters of credit, segregated escrow services, CDIC deposit insurance, or full treasury credit facilities; client funds sit in pooled omnibus accounts, and the LP remains exposed to the EMI's operational risk. Under PCMLTFA, using an EMI to receive large partner capital injections without underlying contractual documents risks classification as an unnamed-beneficiary remittance, which banks will reject. Therefore, Wise is approved only for outbound sub-CAD-50,000 operational payments, while all capital injections, equipment capex, and reserve balances must flow through the Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections from non-resident limited partners must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 directly to the LP's master account at the primary Schedule I institution, recommended as National Bank of Canada, Montreal, for Quebec civil-law alignment and local branch coordination. Protocol: (i) issue a formal capital-call notice and provide 24-hour advance notice to the relationship manager; (ii) instruct the remitter to use the LP's exact legal name and REQ registration number in SWIFT fields 50 and 59, and to state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CAPITAL CALL NUMBER [X]' in field 70 as the payment description; (iii) the bank performs sanctions screening, OFAC/UN/Global Affairs checks, and beneficiary-account verification before crediting; (iv) funds settle into the capital escrow account and trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 or above; (v) the LP must deliver the capital-call ledger, deposit ticket, and underlying subscription documents; if the funds arrive in USD, conversion is executed at commercial spot rates. Wise is not an approved channel for capital injections because it cannot provide segregated escrow or evidence of final settlement to the bank's AML officer.Clearing-rail discipline by value tier: (1) Major capital expenditure (EMC test chamber, drop-test tower, thermal cycling units) uses an irrevocable, confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700, with settlement by SWIFT MT103 against conforming documents; (2) mid-value vendor invoices above CAD 50,000 use direct SWIFT MT103 with charges set to OUR and field 70 stating 'PURCHASE OF TEST EQUIPMENT' or 'SUBCONTRACT TESTING SERVICES'; (3) low-value operational payments below CAD 50,000, including calibration and lab consumables, may use Wise disbursements or the bank's EFT batch; (4) Canadian domestic high-value payments settle same-day via Lynx, Payments Canada's high-value payment system, while small domestic payments use the bank's standard EFT batch. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods transactions, CAD 500,000 for services with non-residents; also required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada has General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and specific anti-avoidance rules; for LPs, economic substance is required to avoid recharacterization; Quebec has its own GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution possible with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA can recharacterize transactions, impose penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and assess interest; corporate veil may be pierced

Director personal liability: Directors liable for taxes and penalties; assets may be seized; travel bans and imprisonment for fraud
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to CAD 2 million for individuals and CAD 500,000 for corporations per violation; administrative monetary penalties

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and imprisonment for money laundering
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of net transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest; documentation penalties up to CAD 24,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated; civil restitution and potential criminal charges
