Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST and federal income tax administration; Ontario Ministry of Finance (for the Ontario HST component via CRA). Registration portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST Internet File Transfer; Ontario Business Registry for provincial business registration.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident limited partnerships must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 - Request for a Business Number, and open a GST/HST account if taxable Canadian supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or if voluntary registration is desired. Ontario HST is collected under the same CRA BN account; no separate Ontario TIN is issued for HST. The partnership must file annual T5013 partnership return and allocate income to resident and non-resident partners. Non-resident partners may require Canadian tax identification via Form NR3010 or existing BN if they carry on business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; each partner is taxed on their allocable share of income, and non-resident partners may be subject to Canadian Part I tax or Part XIII withholding on certain Canadian-source amounts.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. The LP may elect under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act to compute partnership income and allocate tax items among partners. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available; OECD BEPS hybrid rules are effectively implemented through specific rules such as section 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act, not elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies during the current or previous four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); e-commerce platform and non-resident rules may require HST registration on Canadian sales exceeding this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier under section 21, Division V of the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier retains satisfactory evidence of export within the prescribed documentation period and the goods are not retained in Canada for consumer or commercial use. The LP must sell in the ordinary course of business, transfer possession to the purchaser outside Canada, and maintain proof that title and risk passed abroad. Digital audio and software downloads are generally treated as intangible personal property and may be zero-rated only when the supply is made to a non-resident who does not use the property in Canada. Musical instruments physically exported qualify for 0% GST/HST.

  • Canada Export Reporting (CBSA Form B13A or electronic export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS))
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail or truck) with proof of destination outside Canada
  • Carrier Certificate or Customs Office Proof of Export (e.g., CBSA-stamped copy)
  • Foreign Bank Inward Payment / SWIFT credit evidence for audit substantiation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. Registrants must provide valid GST/HST invoices containing prescribed formulae or information and retain electronic records in an accessible format for CRA audit. EDI, XML, and PDF invoices are accepted; no API-based fiscal autorization is required.

CRA may disallow zero-rating retroactively and reassess GST/HST at 13% plus interest and penalties if export documentation is incomplete. False export claims attract penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act (15% to 25% of any net tax understated), section 327(1) criminal fines and imprisonment, and potential gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act. While Canadian law does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating, an absence of inward remittance within 180 days is treated as an audit red flag and may trigger full documentation review and clawback if export proof cannot be contemporaneously substantiated.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada abolished formal exchange controls on December 14, 1951 and operates a pure floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No central-bank approval, licensing, or FX purpose-code authorization is required for inbound subscription payments, export repatriation, or outbound trade remittances. Profit and capital remittances by non-resident limited partners are governed exclusively by income-tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.), not by FX control. A non-resident partner may be deemed to carry on business in Canada through the Ontario partnership's e-commerce activities and must file Canadian returns for its allocable share; Part XIII withholding considerations arise only where a Canadian payor remits amounts of a character subject to Part XIII. Currency conversion is available without volume limits from domestic commercial banks at prevailing market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline under Canadian law. Export receipts in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in accounts inside or outside Canada. Mandatory reporting thresholds apply only to physical cross-border currency movement and reportable transactions: a Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report (E677) must be filed with the Canada Border Services Agency when physical cash or bearer instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 (or foreign-currency equivalent) enter or leave Canada; banks are obliged to report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the large-value transaction reporting rules. Mandatory conversion ratios: none.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), together with the Department of Finance Canada as policy authority for exchange-rate and capital-account matters; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers anti-money-laundering compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Retail payment service providers are also subject to registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, s. 177).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Canada does not grant EU-style Electronic Money Institution licences; foreign EMIs that hold balances or issue payment accounts must operate through a Canadian Schedule I bank as program manager or register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. A foreign EMI cannot serve as custodian of the LP capital escrow, as deposit-taking is restricted to regulated financial institutions. An EMI such as Wise may be used as an ancillary treasury rail for low-value outward FX remittances, but the primary operational and escrow accounts must remain with a domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and subscription payments from foreign limited partners should route as SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 messages. The receiving Canadian bank credits the Capital Escrow Account in CAD or in the subscription currency. Recommended ISO 20022 Purpose Code: 'INVE' (Investment in capital); 'LOAN' may be used separately for partner loans. The remittance narrative must state: 'Subscription of LP units; [Ontario LP registration number]; CA_LP; business capital contribution' to enable straight-through processing. Domestic transfers of CAD 50,000 or above clear through LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) with same-day finality; amounts below that threshold clear through ACSS or the retail EFT batch. For inbound USD, funds post through the bank's US-dollar correspondent account via Fedwire or CHIPS. All receipts must be matched to the investor capital ledger and reconciled against source-of-funds documentation before any disbursement.Supplier settlements for musical instruments and studio audio gear use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'GOODS' (trade in goods) or 'SUPP' (supplier payment) and a structured remittance reference containing invoice number, purchase order number, and supplier name. CAD-denominated supplier payments clear through LVTS for amounts at or above CAD 50,000, or through ACSS/EFT for smaller amounts. USD-denominated payments clear via the bank's US correspondent using Fedwire or CHIPS. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) are permissible when a supplier requires payment conditioning on shipment documents, but are atypical in e-commerce retail supply chains and create additional fee drag; open-account or prepayment terms using MT103 are preferred. A multi-currency account package (CAD, USD, EUR, GBP) may be opened to reduce conversion spreads, with standing instructions to sweep surplus balances into the base operating currency.Outright FX forwards (CAD versus USD/EUR/GBP) with a Canadian Schedule I bank or approved counterparty, executed only against committed sales or import invoices and marked-to-market monthly, pursuant to a non-speculative hedging mandate.FX swaps (spot-forward combined structures) used to align settlement dates with Stripe and PayPal payout cycles and supplier payment due dates.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps available only if the partnership holds term debt or investor notes denominated in a foreign currency; requires OSFI-regulated counterparty and ISDA/CSA or CDEA documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) technically permissible for restricted-currency exposures such as CAD/CNY or CAD/BRL where onshore deliverability is absent, but unnecessary for freely convertible currencies and discouraged in standard treasury policy.Currency options and leveraged exotic derivatives are excluded from the default treasury policy; they may be executed only upon express General Partner approval with independent mark-to-model valuation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

19% (combined federal 15% + Ontario 4% general corporate rate; applicable to corporate partners because the Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not itself pay corporate income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; not directly applicable to an Ontario LP because partnership allocations are not dividends, but non-resident partners’ shares of Canadian-source business income are taxed under Part I at applicable corporate or personal rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$2,000,000 aggregate annual value of non-arm’s-length cross-border transactions (purchases, sales, royalties, interest, or services) triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous Local File documentation. Master File (Schedule 33) is required when Canadian gross revenues exceed C$500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s. 245 and common law substance-over-form principles. An Ontario LP engaged in online sales of musical instruments and audio gear must demonstrate real commercial activity through employees, inventory, contracts, and decision-making functions in Canada. Absence of economic substance may cause CRA to disregard the partnership, deny treaty residency benefits, or reassess the non-resident partners as if the LP did not exist.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Suppression of Online Sales

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1) indictable offense: imprisonment up to 5 years plus fines up to 200% of the tax evaded; civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of understated tax; additional Ontario provincial penalties and interest under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act and provincial offence proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly liable for unremitted source deductions under ITA s. 227.1; prosecution may target senior officers and directors personally, resulting in imprisonment, personal fines, seizure of proceeds, travel bans, and removal from office.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's-Length Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA s. 247(2) adjusting consideration to the arm’s length price; transfer pricing penalty under s. 247(3) at 10% of the net adjustment (up to a maximum) when the adjustment exceeds C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and contemporaneous documentation is not produced; accrued interest and late-payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Transfer pricing penalties attach principally to the corporate partner, but directors who knowingly sign false T106 declarations or direct evasion of TP documentation can be drawn into criminal tax evasion cases, face common law fraud liability, and be subject to civil restitution orders.
Shell Company / Sham Limited Partnership

Statutory sanction: Ontario Limited Partnerships Act: failure to file annual declarations can result in cancellation of registration and loss of limited liability; CRA may apply GAAR to recharacterize the whole structure and ignore the LP; for missing foreign-based information, ITA s. 233.3 imposes penalties of $1,000 per month up to $24,000.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for all partnership obligations; courts may pierce the corporate veil if the LP is a sham, rendering limited partners personally liable; directors of the corporate general partner face restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans, and potential extradition proceedings.
Non-Resident Withholding Non-Compliance (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 215(3) assessment of the LP as debtor for the unpaid Part XIII tax, plus penalties and interest; gross negligence penalty of 50% if the failure was due to recklessness or indifference; the underlying transaction is not void but remains fully taxable.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under ITA s. 227.1, regardless of the LP’s assets; CRA may garnish personal bank accounts, require payment before issuing clearances, and impose restrictions on international travel for non-compliant directors.
