Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Quebec Business Corporations Act corporation must maintain a registered office at a physical location in Quebec named in its articles. A virtual office or serviced office is acceptable for registry and service-of-process purposes if a real street address in Quebec and a responsible local corporate agent are in place, but a post-office box alone is not permitted. For ISIC 6201 personal wealth analytics and investment tracking software, no statutory physical branch is required. However, Canadian corporate-residence doctrine requires that central management and control (CEMC), including board decision-making, key contract execution, and actual management functions, remain in Canada; a mailbox-only virtual office without substantive management infrastructure creates elevated CRA residence recharacterization and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) risk. To maintain commercial substance, local resident directors, Quebec-based board minutes, a local service contract, and an active registered-office address should be documented.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec (RQ). Federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and income tax administered by CRA; Quebec provincial corporate income tax and QST administered by Revenu Québec. Portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec's CLIC.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing a Canadian business corporation in Quebec, the standard sequence is: (1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act through Corporations Canada or Registraire des entreprises Québec, obtaining a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and, if federal, a corporate identification number. (2) After incorporation, register with CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by submitting RC1 form; add program accounts RT (corporate income tax) and RC (GST/HST). (3) Register with Revenu Québec for provincial corporate tax and QST, either through the BRO service or Registraire des entreprises, obtaining a Numéro d'inscription au fichier (NIF) and separate QST account. (4) Commence GST/HST and QST registration once taxable worldwide revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 or immediately for voluntary registration, and provide proof of non-resident ownership and provincial place of business for the Quebec business number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable. Canada does not have a check-the-box regime. A Canadian corporation is taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act and cannot elect pass-through taxation. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 to 18.6 and related provisions do not provide elective classification; they impose adjustments on certain hybrid arrangements. Elections limited to internal Canadian tax mechanisms such as section 216(4) elections for non-resident investment income and joint elections for Canadian-controlled private corporation business income allocation within a group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide annual taxable supplies for both GST/HST and QST small-supplier thresholds; once exceeded, mandatory registration. Financial portfolio tracking services are subject to this threshold unless zero-rated as exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian registered supplier is eligible for 0% GST/HST and QST on exported goods and on certain services supplied to non-residents. For the fintech software/portfolio tracking service, zero-rating requires: (a) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and QST in the ordinary course of business in Canada; (b) the service is not, and is not performed on, real or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (c) the service is not a taxable Canadian 'financial service' supplied in Canada to a resident; and (d) evidence that the recipient is non-resident and the service is exported, such as the non-resident's business address and foreign payment method. The fact that payment flows through Stripe or Apple IAP does not in itself determine zero-rating; the 0% rate applies only if the statutory conditions are satisfied and documented. Domestic sales to Canadian residents and sales through a Canadian distribution platform may be taxable at 5% GST and 9.975% QST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or Canada Border Services Agency 'Export Report') containing HS classification and value, where goods are physically exported
  • Signed commercial invoice identifying seller, buyer, export date, service or goods description, and foreign currency amount
  • Contract or service agreement showing the non-resident recipient and the place of supply outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / electronic carrier tracking record for tangible exports, or a service completion/usage record for intangible digital services
  • Foreign exchange settlement evidence from Stripe/Apple IAP or banking records confirming payment received from abroad
  • Where applicable, a Currency Exchange Memo or bank inward remittance statement (not a formal 'Inward Remittance Certificate' under Canadian law, but commonly used for audit substantiation)

No federal or Quebec statutory mandate for real-time continuous control, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance of invoices. Suppliers must retain auditable electronic or paper invoices containing required statutory particulars. Quebec requires certain listed registrants to transmit prescribed data to Revenu Québec for exchange-of-information purposes, but no universal e-invoicing clearance API is enforced. Stripe and Apple IAP receipts do not replace statutory invoices; the selling entity must generate tax-compliant invoices for Canadian VAT/GST registrations, including supplier BN/QST registration number, date, description, GST/HST and QST charged, and terms. Real-time reporting is expected to modernize but is not yet legislated.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and claw back zero-rating claims together with interest and penalties where supporting documents are incomplete, false, or the supplier fails to demonstrate that the supply was exported or delivered outside Canada. Under the Excise Tax Act, sections 285 and 280 impose penalties up to 25% of any net tax understated for knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence making false statements or omissions; section 281 imposes additional administrative penalties. Revenu Québec applies equivalent penalties under the Tax Administration Act. The limitation period for reassessment is normally four years from the date of the original assessment, but is extended to seven years where misrepresentation or fraud is proven. The exporter must retain the documentation bundle, including FX repatriation records, for at least six years from the end of the taxation year. Failure to repatriate foreign funds within a commercially reasonable period, and particularly beyond 180 days without documented justification, may trigger a factual inquiry into whether the transaction was a true export or a sham, leading to denial of zero-rating, re-characterization of the sale as a domestic taxable supply, assessment of GST/QST, interest, penalties, and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating currency with no exchange-control approval regime and no substantive distinction between capital-account and current-account convertibility. Equity injections, loan repayments, dividends, interest, royalties and service fees are fully convertible and remittable in CAD. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax-only: Part XIII federal withholding tax at 25% (treaty-reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for a US corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares) plus the parallel Quebec dividend withholding administered by Revenu Québec; no central-bank permit, waiting period, proportionality test or conversion tax applies. Corporations may freely hold USD/EUR balances in domestic foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export or SaaS/service receipts. Invoice proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian or offshore banks. The only applicable duty is FINTRAC's automatic reporting of international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more, which is filed by the clearing financial institution and does not require the operating corporation to repatriate or convert funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - Canada's central bank and monetary authority. No dedicated foreign-exchange administration exists; exchange-control policy is overseen by the Department of Finance Canada, prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Quebec financial-market conduct by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - ancillary use only. Canada has no Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; offshore digital finance platforms (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Mercury) cannot lawfully hold Canadian statutory capital or serve as the primary bank relationship because they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act. They may be engaged as payment facilitators for cross-border receivables and vendor payouts through partnered Canadian money-services-business (MSB) arrangements, but the principal account, capital escrow and audit trail must sit with a regulated Schedule I Canadian bank.

Quebec Annual Declaration (Déclaration annuelle) under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1) Within 60 days following the anniversary date of the enterprise's registration in the REQ registry (anniversary date of the company's immatriculation). $22 USD REQ publishes a default notice and imposes a late fee; failure to file for two consecutive years leads to administrative strike-off (radiation) and, under the Quebec Business Corporations Act, potential dissolution by the registraire; struck-off corporations lose legal personality and directors may face personal liability for certain corporate debts.
