Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) — federal tax authority with Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business/business-number.html) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); and, for the Province of Quebec, Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) administering the Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), the federal GST/HST for Quebec-based businesses, and the Quebec enterprise registration infrastructure via the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca/en/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-established Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must: (1) register the partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), which serves as the provincial master identifier; (2) apply for the federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, indicating the partnership's general partner as the authorized representative and tax-matters partner; (3) register for GST/HST and QST with Revenu Québec once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (the same threshold applies for both taxes), using the online Clé d'accès business services; (4) for a non-resident partnership without a Canadian physical establishment, follow the simplified CRA/Revenu Québec non-resident digital-supplier registration route, which permits electronic registration using the BN/GST program account; and (5) retain the partnership agreement, certificate of registration from REQ, and evidence of the general partner's Canadian presence or designated service address in Quebec for audit purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election comparable to the U.S. regime. A Quebec limited partnership is recognized as a partnership under the Income Tax Act (s. 96) and the Civil Code of Québec, and its income is allocated to partners on a flow-through basis. The CRA determines entity status factually under Income Tax Folio S4-F3-C1; no domestic election is available to treat the LP as a taxable corporation. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.3–18.5 and 163.6 deny inappropriate deductions and impose filing obligations for structured hybrid-arbitrage arrangements; no additional 'hybrid mirror' election is available. A partnership must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold for GST/HST is determined on the taxpayer's worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated and financial supplies) for the current calendar quarter plus the preceding four calendar quarters; Revenu Québec applies the same CAD 30,000 threshold for mandatory QST registration. Non-resident digital suppliers of apps and telemetry services are subject to the same threshold with CRA/Revenu Québec simplified registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Canada (Schedule VI, Part I), (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient where the service is not performed in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada, and (c) intangible personal property (including fitness-app software, source-code licences, and digital telemetry subscriptions) supplied to a non-resident where the property is not used or enjoyed in Canada. For the health-fitness app sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the Quebec limited partnership is the supplier; each Canadian-resident end-user transaction attracts GST/HST and QST, while transactions with non-resident end-users may qualify for zero-rating upon production of contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the foreign place of supply/enjoyment. Platform commissions and payment rails do not alter the underlying GST/HST/QST characterization.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration — electronic export report filed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), successor to Form B13A, required for physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — transportation document evidencing the physical movement of goods outside Canada for exported-goods zero-rating claims
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/foreign-exchange records — Canadian export zero-rating substantiation may be supported by bank statements, SWIFT MT103 payment confirmations, and foreign-exchange conversion records evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident buyer; a formal inward remittance certificate is not a statutory Canadian requirement

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transactional clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. Electronic tax invoices are permitted if they contain the prescribed information under Excise Tax Act s. 223 (supplier name, GST/HST registration number, date, description, amount, and tax charged) and the corresponding QST rules. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework and PEPPOL network are voluntary. Taxpayers must preserve all electronic records in an auditable, readable format for CRA and Revenu Québec inspection.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment where supporting export documentation is absent or deficient. For GST/HST, a false-statement penalty under Excise Tax Act s. 280 applies equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax evaded, with daily compounding interest on the deficiency. Conscious evasion may result in criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. Revenu Québec enforces parallel administrative penalties and interest for unsubstantiated QST zero-rating claims. The general partner of a limited partnership is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST and QST remittances; a clawback therefore attaches to partnership assets and the general partner. The ordinary reassessment period is four years, but it is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange control restrictions. Profit distributions and capital repatriations to non-resident limited partners are permitted, subject to Canadian withholding tax and Quebec-specific tax reporting. No prior government authorization or central bank approval is required for remittances, provided the transaction is not subject to sanctions, tax non-compliance, or suspicious reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender regime. There is no statutory timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and LPs may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore operating accounts. However, repatriated funds must comply with record-keeping and verification requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and applicable FINTRAC guidance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/CTF obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low legal and regulatory viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are generally not authorized to conduct payment activities in Canada unless registered as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. A foreign EMI may provide ancillary virtual account booking services, but the Quebec LP must maintain a domestic bank account for the settlement of Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds and for statutory record-keeping. Quebec's 'Act respecting the distribution of financial services and payment services' and federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) impose registration requirements that offshore EMIs without Canadian presence are unlikely to satisfy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are routed through standard SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire messages into the LP's designated Canadian-dollar or multi-currency treasury account. The payment instruction should reference a 'Capital Contribution' purpose with no commodity code, and the receiving bank must verify the contributing entity against sanctions lists and complete an enhanced source-of-funds review. Canada does not mandate a specific FX purpose code, but the narration should cite the LP agreement clause and limited partner identification to distinguish capital injection from trade receivables, thereby lowering AML risk.Outbound trade payments and vendor settlements use SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent bank transfers, MT202 COV for interbank cover payments, and documentary letters of credit (LC) through the issuance bank when secured supplier terms are required. CAD payments to Canadian counterparties clear through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch transactions. USD disbursements clear via CHIPS or Fedwire through the bank's US correspondent, with dual-currency accounts reducing conversion friction. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38% federal general statutory rate (28% after federal abatement); combined Quebec/Canada headline rate for a general corporation approximately 26.6% (Quebec 11.5% + federal net 15.1%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

General non-resident withholding tax (Part XIII) is 25% for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation; because the LP is fiscally transparent, no Canadian withholding tax applies to profit allocations qua allocations to partners, but a beneficiary is taxable on Canadian-source income through the partner's own Canadian non-resident filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million aggregate fair-market value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents triggers mandatory contemporaneous T2025/RC-4751 TP documentation (Local/Master File equivalent); consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 750 million triggers Country-by-Country Report filing and notification obligations.

Economic substance scope: Quebec/Canada does not operate an 'ESR' code in the Gulf style; however, substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (section 245 ITA), the 'active business' requirement for FAPI exceptions, and BEPS anti-abuse treaty provisions. The LP must demonstrate real premises, operating employees, bank accounts, decision-making authority, and commercial risk for its fitness app operations to avoid sham/GAAR recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Criminal Fraud (Income Tax Act and Quebec Taxation Act)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution carries a fine of 50% to 200% of taxes evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment; civil penalties: gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of any understated tax plus daily late-filing penalties; Quebec administrative penalties may add another 20%.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for source withholding, GST/QST remittances, and penalties if they directed or acquiesced in the omission; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and personal restitution orders.
Shell Company / Sham Limited Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to cancel tax benefits, reassess all Canadian-source income to the partner, and impose a 50% penalty for false statements; Registraire des entreprises du Québec may dissolve the LP for non-compliance, and all limited-partner limited-liability protections are retroactively lost.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors become personally liable for all partnership obligations; in a sham, limited partners can be treated as exercising control and lose the statutory limited-liability shield, exposing them to tort and tax claims.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess income under subsection 247(2) using arm's-length pricing; a 10% penalty is applied to the net transfer pricing adjustment where documentation thresholds are met; continued failure to provide documentation can trigger a daily penalty of CAD 100 per month up to CAD 25,000 and, for CbC reporting failure, CAD 2,000/day for corporations and CAD 1,000/day for directors/officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the mispricing or who signed false CRA filings face personal penalty exposure under Quebec's director liability regime; criminal sanctions include fraud convictions and quarantine from serving as officers.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 500,000 per violation may be imposed; Quebec civil forfeiture provisions allow seizure of the LP's assets where proceeds are linked to unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for FINTRAC violations if they instructed, authorized, or tolerated the failure; criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years and prohibition from holding commercial registrations.
