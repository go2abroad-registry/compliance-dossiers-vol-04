Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST (TPS/TVH) and income tax administration; online portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online. Revenu Quebec - Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), provincial income tax and employer payroll administration; online portal: Clic Revenu. Entity registration authority: Registraire des entreprises du Quebec (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

(1) File the declaration of limited partnership with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec to obtain the NEQ and registration extract; (2) register the LP with CRA via Business Registration Online or Form RC1 to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT), payroll program account (RP), and, if imports or direct export reporting are anticipated, a customs account (RM); (3) register the LP for QST with Revenu Quebec under the same NEQ to obtain a QST registration number; (4) obtain a CBSA exporter/import program account through the CARM portal for electronic export declarations; (5) open a Canadian settlement bank account in the LP's name and satisfy FINTRAC beneficial ownership verification; (6) have the non-resident general partner and each non-resident limited partner obtain a Canadian tax identification number (CRA individual or non-resident account) for reporting Canadian-source income allocations; (7) secure the CFIA Safe Food for Canadians Act licence (food export) and any provincial food-processing permit, which are prerequisites for lawful export and are referenced in the exporter's CRA/customs profile. No minimum capital is required at registration; a non-resident may hold limited partnership interests without Canadian residency restrictions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Canadian Income Tax Act (ITA) and the Quebec Taxation Act, a limited partnership is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners. For GST/QST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' and must register, collect, and file GST/QST as a distinct registrant. The LP carries on business in Canada and in Quebec through its general partner's management activity and manufacturing establishment, creating a Quebec permanent establishment. Tax-exempt zone status does not apply. Export-oriented manufacturers will typically be in a net GST/QST refund position due to zero-rated export supplies.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime; the LP is statutorily pass-through. Relevant elections include: (i) ITA s. 97(2) election to transfer property into the LP partnership on a cost/agreed-amount basis at formation; (ii) ITA s. 96(3) election for a permitted non-calendar fiscal period; (iii) ITA s. 216(1) net-basis election for a non-resident partner deriving rental income; (iv) ITA s. 152(4) waiver of the normal reassessment limitation period; (v) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules (ITA s. 18.4(2) to s. 18.6, effective 1 July 2022) which override treaty benefits and deny deductions for hybrid and branch mismatch outcomes, with designation/elective notices available in prescribed circumstances; (vi) GST/HST Quick Method election (eligibility generally limited to registrants with taxable worldwide supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000, excluding financial services); (vii) annual GST/HST filing election under ETA s. 238; and (viii) the QST Fast Method election in Quebec whereby eligible SMEs calculate net QST liability on a simplified basis. The LP's general partner must maintain the partnership books and file the prescribed partnership information returns (TP-600/RP or T5013) and allocate income to partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (identical threshold for GST/HST and QST). Mandatory registration takes effect on the first day of the month following the quarter in which the threshold is exceeded. A manufacturer exclusively exporting zero-rated goods may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits/refunds on inputs; the small-supplier exemption can be renounced by filing Form RC1 or its Quebec equivalent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of manufactured instant rice noodle products qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (section 5 for goods delivered by the supplier to a carrier or consignee for export, or delivered outside Canada), provided the goods are physically exported, sold, and paid for by a non-resident in the normal course of trade. QST zero-rating applies concurrently under section 23.1 of the Regulation respecting the Quebec Sales Tax, which imports the federal zero-rating categories. Services supplied to a non-resident that relate to exported goods or constitute offshore services may also be zero-rated under Part V, section 7, provided the customer is a non-resident, not registered under the GST/QST, and the service is not performed in relation to real property situated in Canada or in respect of tangible personal property destined for a consumer in Canada. Domestic sales of instant noodles may independently qualify as zero-rated basic groceries (Schedule VI, Part III) unless the product is classified as a taxable pre-packaged snack; export zero-rating under Part V is available regardless of the domestic classification. Input tax credits and refunds are available for GST/QST paid on manufacturing inputs, giving zero-rated exporters a net-refund position; the supplier must retain supporting export evidence within the normal two-year record-keeping cycle and make the evidence available upon CRA or Revenu Quebec demand.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA CARM electronic export report (formerly B13A) submitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System for goods exceeding CAD 2,000, including the exporter account identifier, H.S. classification (H.S. 1902/2008 category mapping for rice noodles), quantity, value, destination, and reason-for-export code, plus any applicable CFIA/agricultural export documentation.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - a clean multimodal or ocean/air transport document issued by a common carrier evidencing that the goods were received in Canada and consigned to the non-resident customer at the foreign destination, showing the shipping mark, port of loading, port of discharge, INCOTERMS, and container/seal numbers.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank evidence of settlement in Canadian dollars or convertible foreign exchange equivalent (SWIFT MT103, telegraphic transfer advice, or letter-of-credit realization statement issued by the Canadian receiving bank) evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer and completing the commercial payment audit trail within the expected settlement window.

Canada does not currently operate a real-time tax clearance, fiscal stamping, or tax-authority API invoice approval system. CRA and Revenu Quebec accept electronic invoices provided the invoice data meet the statutory content requirements of the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act (supplier name, BN/QST registration number, date, description, quantity, taxable amount, GST/QST shown separately for transactions above the CAD 30-invoice threshold). The federal Pan-Canadian e-invoicing initiative promotes voluntary e-invoicing through the PEPPOL network (CRA pilot). Quebec has enacted a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime: enterprises with annual turnover of CAD 50 million or more must be able to receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026 and issue from 1 September 2027; enterprises with turnover between CAD 10 million and CAD 50 million must receive from 1 September 2027 and issue from 1 September 2028; obligations for smaller enterprises, including new SME manufacturers, are phased in under dates to be prescribed. Exporters, irrespective of size, must file electronic export declarations with the CBSA through the CARM system for goods exported with a value exceeding CAD 2,000.

CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of misrepresentation, wilful default, or fraud. Where the exporter cannot produce the export documentation bundle or where the documentation is internally inconsistent, the zero-rating will be reversed, net tax and QST will be assessed with compound daily interest at prescribed rates, and the supplier will lose entitlement to input tax credits. Civil penalties under ETA s. 280 (and the mirroring Quebec Tax Administration Act provisions) apply for false or deceptive statements and omissions: the greater of CAD 8,000 and 25% of the tax evaded or of the rebate/refund improperly claimed. Egregious misstatement penalties under ITA s. 163.2 may also be asserted against the general partner. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 (fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 2 years) is available for wilful evasion or fraud in export-claim transactions. CRA and Revenu Quebec scrutinize circular exporter-customer flows, abnormally long payment terms, related-party offshore purchasers, and high-risk food-trade corridors; export proceeds received beyond the 180-day evidentiary benchmark without documented commercial credit terms will heighten audit risk and may trigger immediate suspension of refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with a free-floating currency and full de facto convertibility for both current account items (trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties) and capital account transactions (direct investment, loans, portfolio flows). No exchange control permits are required for inward or outward remittances. Practical profit remittance hurdles for a Quebec limited partnership are limited to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25% on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties; reduced by tax treaties to 5-15% on dividends, 15% on interest, and often 0% on certain arm's length royalties) and the need for the partnership to file tax information returns (T5013 or NR4) and to obtain treaty-based relief (CRA Form NR301) to prevent over-withholding on distributions to non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Canada. Exporters are not required to convert export proceeds into Canadian dollars or to deposit them with the central bank within any timeline. Export receipt foreign currency balances may be held indefinitely in foreign currency bank accounts at Schedule I Canadian banks. For income tax and GST/HST purposes, export proceeds are translated into Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada's noon spot rate (or the rate stipulated by the CRA) on the date of receipt; there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no FINTRAC threshold beyond the standard CAD 10,000 large cash transaction reporting requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); supporting regulators: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. An offshore digital EMI or money services business (MSB) may operate as a limited payment/clearing conduit for a Canada-facing business only if it is registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). It must maintain a Canadian presence or agent, implement client identification, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting, and cannot hold Canadian deposits. Due to the PCMLTFA and Quebec's Money-Services Businesses Act, a foreign EMI without a FINTRAC MSB registration is not a viable banking rail for the Quebec LP. Unregistered foreign EMIs should be used at most as an auxiliary collection channel, never as the primary corporate treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections are remitted by SWIFT MT103 wire with a clear payment narrative identifying the sender as the investing partner, the beneficiary as the Quebec LP's bank account, and the purpose as 'Capital Contribution' or 'Paid-in Capital'. The Canadian beneficiary bank will require the underlying executed partnership agreement, the contribution schedule, a source-of-funds declaration, and, if the transfer exceeds the FINTRAC large transaction threshold, a large cash transaction report is filed through the bank. Where applicable, a SWIFT MT202/MT202COV may cover the interbank leg. To facilitate clearing, the beneficiary bank account is referenced by the Canadian bank's institution number, bank/transit number, and SWIFT BIC. An opinion letter confirming the capital contribution is not a loan and will be escorted by a statement of shareholder/partner equity may be necessary.Outbound supplier and trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open account or TT payments; for documentary trade, the preferred rail is an advised or confirmed irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank and governed by UCP 600, payable by sight draft or deferred payment terms. Payments to suppliers in China, Vietnam, or other Asian markets can be made in CAD, USD, EUR, or RMB using the agreed settlement currency. Canada's payment infrastructure, including the Canadian High Value Clearing System (HVCS) and its successor Real-Time Rail (RTR), supports the domestic leg of such payments; cross-border transactions settle via SWIFT/CHIPS or correspondent banks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. An offshore digital EMI or money services business (MSB) may operate as a limited payment/clearing conduit for a Canada-facing business only if it is registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). It must maintain a Canadian presence or agent, implement client identification, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting, and cannot hold Canadian deposits. Due to the PCMLTFA and Quebec's Money-Services Businesses Act, a foreign EMI without a FINTRAC MSB registration is not a viable banking rail for the Quebec LP. Unregistered foreign EMIs should be used at most as an auxiliary collection channel, never as the primary corporate treasury account.
