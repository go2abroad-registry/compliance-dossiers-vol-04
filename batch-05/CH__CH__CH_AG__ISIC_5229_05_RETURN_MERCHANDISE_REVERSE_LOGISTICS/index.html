Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the canton of registered office, supervised by the Swiss Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt) under the Federal Office of Justice.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR), Art. 620 et seq. (stock corporation); Art. 626-632 (formation, shares and capital); Art. 640 (organs); Art. 642-643 (registered shares); Art. 720 (independent proxy).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation (Gründungsurkunde) together with Articles of Association (Statuten), registered through the official commercial register application form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash and deposited in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank. The blocked amount can only be released after the commercial register registration; the board must prove free disposal of funds.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from cantonal fire prevention authority (Feuerpolizei); structural load inspection for storage racking and building static integrity by cantonal building inspectorate; workplace safety and health clearance under SUVA and SECO (occupational safety and health). Depending on the premises, a civil defense shelter waiver may be required for large warehouses.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash and deposited in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) with a Swiss bank. The blocked amount can only be released after the commercial register registration; the board must prove free disposal of funds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; commercial registration is administered through the cantonal commercial register offices and the Federal Commercial Register (Zefix portal).

E-government filing portal

For a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft, the universal business identifier (UID) and VAT identification number (MWST-ID) are obtained through Swiss Commercial Register admission and subsequent ESTV VAT registration. The company must first be entered in the Cantonal Commercial Register and receive a UID; if the annual taxable turnover reaches CHF 250,000 worldwide, or if voluntary VAT registration is chosen, the AG must file the ESTV VAT registration form online and obtain a separate MWST-ID. A foreign entity without Swiss registered office must establish a Swiss branch or a Swiss AG, obtain UID through the commercial register, and complete VAT registration with the ESTV before incurring Swiss taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. A Swiss AG is treated as a taxable corporate entity at federal, cantonal, and communal level. Relevant elections include VAT group taxation under Art. 13 VAT Act, the small-business exemption for turnover below the threshold, and optional flat-rate VAT methods for certain eligible businesses. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective; Swiss tax authorities apply anti-avoidance rules, substance requirements, and OECD BEPS-aligned principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover effective 1 January 2025; previously CHF 100,000; voluntary registration remains available below the statutory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods physically leave the Swiss customs territory, the supplier proves export by customs and transport documents, and the invoice is issued to the foreign purchaser. Offshore services, including reverse logistics quality-grading, repair, or handling of movable goods located outside Switzerland, may qualify for zero-rating under Art. 23(2) VAT Act when the place-of-supply and export-performance conditions are met; the recipient's foreign location alone is generally insufficient.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration submitted electronically through e-dec/e-dec R or the NCTS system, including the customs clearance reference or export certificate (Ausfuhrbescheinigung)
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from Swiss customs territory: sea bill of lading, multimodal waybill, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or courier shipment record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment in the Swiss exporter's account

Switzerland does not operate a continuous transaction control system, real-time fiscal API clearance, or mandatory digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is legally admissible under the Swiss VAT Ordinance and EIDI-VO where the invoice content and authenticity are verifiable; common B2B channels include eBill, Peppol, and PDF/A. Taxpayers must retain invoices and source documents for at least 10 years, with ex-post validation by the ESTV rather than pre-clearance.

The ESTV and customs authorities may retroactively examine export proof. Where zero-rating is disallowed due to missing documentation, false customs declarations, non-repatriation of foreign exchange proceeds, or abusive triangular transactions, the supplier is reassessed at the standard VAT rate with default interest. Penalties under Art. 96 VAT Act may reach up to four times the evaded tax; fraud may additionally trigger criminal fines, exclusion from VAT registration, and reassessment for up to 10 years in cases of intentional evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF enjoys full and unconditional convertibility on both current and capital account. Switzerland maintains no foreign-exchange control statute; cross-border transfers of CHAG share capital, shareholder loans, distributions, and trade payments are unrestricted under the Currency and Payment Instruments Act (CPIA/CCVA) and the free-movement provisions of the OECD Capital Movements Liberalisation Code. Profit remittance from a Swiss AG is subject only to Swiss withholding tax (35%) unless reduced by DTT and does not require central-bank approval or conversion licenses. Practical impediments are limited to banking due-diligence (source-of-funds) validation, not regulatory permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to Swiss export or reverse-logistics proceeds in CHF or any foreign currency. Exporters and logistics providers retain full discretion over currency denomination and offshore retention. A statistical reporting duty exists only for enterprises with annual cross-border payment flows exceeding CHF 50 million, which must report aggregate balance-of-payments data quarterly to the SNB; this is a data obligation, not an exchange-control requirement. Returned-goods import/export documentation under the Swiss Single Window (CITES, Swiss Customs) must evidence the merchandise flows that mirror treasury payment flows.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank) in conjunction with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking supervision; the Federal Department of Finance (FDF) holds no exchange-control role.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for operational clearing, Low for statutory capital escrow. Airwallex (the designated payment-rail target) holds an EMI/remittance license (incl. EU EMI passporting and FCA scope) but is not a Swiss bank deposit-taker; it cannot issue the bank confirmation certificate (Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) required for AG capital formation under OR 632. Airwallex is highly effective for inbound client refund/return disbursements at reduced FX margins, multi-currency virtual IBANs, and API-driven batch payouts, provided onboarding uses Airwallex's entity-level KYC in parallel with a Swiss bank relationship. Legal constraint: Airwallex cannot clear SIC CHF direct debits or act as principal for Swiss corporate credit; all statutory capital and major trade settlements must rest on a regulated Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal direct tax on net profit after tax (7.83% pre-tax equivalent), plus cantonal and communal profit taxes resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 11.9%–21.0% depending on the canton (e.g., Zug ~11.9%, Zurich ~18.2%, Geneva ~21.0%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35%% federal anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) withheld at source on all dividends distributed by a Swiss AG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Federal direct tax return due 31 March of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; cantonal deadlines typically 30 June. Extensions of up to 6 months are routinely granted upon timely request without penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation (Local File, Master File and country-by-country data) is mandatory where the multinational group has consolidated annual turnover above CHF 80 million. Below this threshold, the Swiss entity must still maintain contemporaneous arm's length documentation covering the functional, asset and risk profile of its cross-border reverse-logistics transactions, deliverable at the request of the cantonal tax administration.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not maintain a zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations regime. Equivalence is achieved through: (1) Swiss tax law's substance-over-form doctrine requiring real functions, assets and risks for profit attribution to the Swiss entity; (2) beneficial ownership and limitation-on-benefits provisions in Swiss DTAs; (3) OECD/G20 Pillar Two global minimum tax legislation effective 1 January 2024 imposing a 15%% top-up tax on groups with worldwide revenue of at least EUR 750 million. A Swiss reverse-logistics company must demonstrate locally exercised management, qualified operational staff, physical premises and independent decision-making to avoid profit reattribution and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Wilful Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to 250%% of the evaded tax, arrears interest at 4.5%% p.a., and administrative surcharges; the sanction is public in cases of significant amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several civil liability for the unpaid tax and fines; criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years; escalation to tax fraud (Steuerbetrug) with forged documents increases imprisonment to up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under federal debt enforcement.
Constructive Dividend / Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the excess amount as a taxable dividend; assessment of 35%% anticipatory tax payable by the Swiss company including arrears interest; forfeiture of participation relief and possible 50%% penalty on the hidden distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the anticipatory tax if the company cannot pay; civil restitution with compound interest; enforcement certificates and travel bans; criminal prosecution may follow for aiding tax evasion.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Swiss DTA benefits under beneficial ownership and limitation-on-benefits clauses; reattribution of profits to the foreign beneficiary jurisdiction; imposition of 35%% withholding tax on deemed distributions; potential commercial register dissolution action.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for corporate taxes and withholding taxes where the shell structure is deemed abusively organized; travel bans and asset seizures under federal debt collection; imprisonment up to 5 years if forged documents were used in tax fraud.
AML Negligence / Money Laundering Offence

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) and Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis: organizational fine of up to CHF 500,000, confiscation of illicit proceeds, and revocation of any license; regulatory publication of the sanction may occur.

Director personal liability: Directors who negligently fail to implement AML protocols face imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines; disqualification from board positions for up to 5 years; unlimited civil liability for consequential damages; imposed travel bans and freezing orders during proceedings.
