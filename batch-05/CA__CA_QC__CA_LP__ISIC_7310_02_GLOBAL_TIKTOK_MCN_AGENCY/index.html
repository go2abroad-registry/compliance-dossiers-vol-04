Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal for federal Business Number and GST/HST accounts; Revenu Quebec - Cl@ss Affaires portal for Quebec Sales Tax (QST) registration and Quebec provincial income tax administration

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), available to non-resident foreign entities. Step 2: Open a GST/HST program account (RT) with the CRA; a non-resident MCN agency may register voluntarily regardless of the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, and voluntary registration is advisable to claim input tax credits on Canadian operating costs. Step 3: Register separately for QST with Revenu Quebec using Form LM-1 or the Cl@ss Affaires online service; QST registration is mandatory if taxable supplies in Quebec exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or may be done voluntarily. Step 4: If the non-resident LP has no place of business in Canada, appoint a Canadian representative using Form RC59 'Business Number Account Resolution or Cancellation Request' or a CRA-authorized agent. Step 5: All Canadian limited partnerships must elect a fiscal period and file Form T5013 'Statement of Partnership Income' for income tax; the LP is granted its own BN as a deemed person for GST/HST and QST while remaining fiscally transparent for income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - A Canadian limited partnership formed in Quebec is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes: the partnership computes income at the entity level, files Form T5013, but pays no income tax itself; each partner (resident or non-resident) is taxed on its allocable share. Resident partners are taxed on worldwide income (full territorial reach), while non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income, including income from a business carried on in Canada through the partnership. For indirect tax, the LP is treated as a separate 'person' or registrant under the Excise Tax Act and the QST Act, distinct from its partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A Canadian limited partnership formed in Quebec is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes: the partnership computes income at the entity level, files Form T5013, but pays no income tax itself; each partner (resident or non-resident) is taxed on its allocable share. Resident partners are taxed on worldwide income (full territorial reach), while non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income, including income from a business carried on in Canada through the partnership. For indirect tax, the LP is treated as a separate 'person' or registrant under the Excise Tax Act and the QST Act, distinct from its partners. The same entity may be classified as opaque (entity-level taxation) in a foreign jurisdiction, potentially triggering Canadian hybrid mismatch rules.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (a) Income Tax Act s. 249.1(4) - election to use an off-calendar fiscal period for the partnership; (b) Excise Tax Act s. 156 - election for closely related persons (a corporation and an LP, or two LPs under common control) to treat supplies between them as made for nil consideration for GST/HST, eliminating tax on inter-entity transfers; (c) parallel QST election under Revenu Quebec rules for closely related persons to the same effect; (d) hybrid mismatch disclosure - filing CRA Information Return RC4640 'Information Return for Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements' for entities that are fiscally transparent in Canada but non-transparent offshore, with associated adjustments under Income Tax Act ss. 18.4-18.6; and (e) voluntary GST/HST and QST registration election, permitting a below-threshold non-resident LP to register and charge tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - taxable supplies made within Canada (excluding zero-rated exports) must not exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for small-supplier status; exceeding this threshold requires mandatory GST/HST registration with the CRA and, for Quebec supplies, mandatory QST registration with Revenu Quebec; non-resident suppliers may register voluntarily at any time

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: tangible personal property exported from Canada qualifies for 0% GST/HST and 0% QST under ETA Schedule VI, Part I and parallel QST rules if the goods are shipped by the supplier, the supplier maintains documentary evidence of export (customs declaration, bill of lading), and the goods are exported within a reasonable time after delivery - CRA administrative practice generally expects export within 30 to 60 days. Services: a supply of a service made to a non-resident person is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 and corresponding QST provisions if the service is not in relation to real property situated in Canada, not in relation to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and not a service the non-resident consumes in Canada. For a TikTok MCN agency (creator incubation, multi-channel talent representation, storyboard/editing support, payout facilitation, royalty collection), services supplied to an offshore platform entity or foreign creator corporation, with the benefit consumed wholly outside Canada, are zero-rated at 0%. Services performed in Canada on behalf of Canadian resident creators, or relating to Canadian-situated property, are taxable at 5% GST and 9.975% QST. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier reflects standard commercial settlement terms for creator payouts, not a CRA legal requirement, and Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income whether or not repatriated.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) framework exists in Canada. The CRA requires prescribed invoice content under the Excise Tax Act Information Regulations (e.g., vendor name, BN with RT account, date of invoice, total consideration, tax rates, and amounts), but electronic invoices in PDF, EDI, or structured XML are accepted without pre-clearance. Quebec has enacted mandatory B2B electronic invoicing under the 2023 reform: registrants with annual turnover exceeding CAD 300 million must issue structured e-invoices via the PEPPOL network and file e-reports to Revenu Quebec, with obligations phased in starting September 1, 2026 and full effect by 2027; smaller registrants, including mid-sized MCN agencies, are not yet subject to the mandate but may adopt PEPPOL voluntarily. No API-based tax-authority clearance authorization is required for invoice issuance.

CRA and Revenu Quebec audits of zero-rated export claims are document-intensive; failure to produce the prescribed export or non-resident-use evidence within the audit window results in reassessment of the supply at the full 5% GST and 9.975% QST rate. Penalties include: Excise Tax Act s. 280(1) false-statement penalty of CAD 250 or 10% of understated tax, whichever is greater; s. 285(1) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax amount; s. 285.01 misrepresentation penalty; compounded daily arrears interest under s. 329. Revenu Quebec imposes parallel penalties under the Quebec Tax Administration Act, including 50% gross-negligence penalties and interest. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion is available under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act and Quebec equivalent provisions. Retrospective clawbacks may extend to partners of the limited partnership, who are jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST and QST amounts; the CRA may also cancel registration numbers, revoke voluntary registration, and impose director or general-partner liability for unremitted tax in cases of fraud or intentional zero-rating overclaims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement. Non-resident limited partners may freely inject capital, repatriate profits, and redeem partnership interests without exchange control approval or central bank clearance. Legal impediments are limited to standard tax withholdings under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and provincial levy obligations in Quebec; no foreign exchange licensing or convertibility restriction is applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters and service providers may retain receivables in foreign currency offshore or repatriate at their commercial discretion. Reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act (Foreign Income Verification Statement) and FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting arise when thresholds (CAD 10,000 or other prescribed amounts) are exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary and systemic regulation; cross-border currency scrutiny and AML/ATF administration are jointly conducted by OSFI, FINTRAC, and the Autorité des marchés financiers (Québec) for MSBs

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut) are operationally viable for outbound creator payouts and small-scale FX conversion, but strict legal constraints apply. A foreign entity providing money services in Quebec must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and obtain an additional licence from the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) under Quebec's Money-Services Businesses Act. Without Canadian licensure, such an EMI cannot maintain a payment gateway for Canadian-source treasury flows. Tier-1 chartered banks generally do not confer full domiciliary account status to unlicensed foreign fintech providers, and banks may de-risk relationships involving pass-through EB2B settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the Canadian institution/transit/account number of the Quebec LP. The payment narrative must contain the capital contribution identifier and reference the partnership agreement article authorizing non-resident funding; a typical purpose code is 'S130' (direct investment) or '8000' (capital transfer) for customs/BOP classification. The bank requires pre-funding KYC verification of the remitting partner, a signed capital contribution notice, and confirmation that the funds originate from an account in the partner's own name. For inbound transfers exceeding CAD 100,000, the treasury team must notify the bank's financial crimes unit at least 48 hours in advance and submit a wire instruction memo with source-of-funds evidence.Creator payouts and trade settlements are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border fee/flat-rate creator payments in USD/EUR/GBP, (ii) the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for CAD-denominated batch payouts to domestic creators, and (iii) Wise B2B API or equivalent regulated fintech rails for nano payouts under CAD 500. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate rate; not applicable to an LP, which is fiscally transparent with tax borne at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

N/A for a Limited Partnership: distribution of profits to partners is not a dividend and is not subject to Part XIII withholding. By contrast, dividends paid by a Canadian taxable corporation to a non-resident are subject to a statutory 25% withholding tax under Part XIII.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory threshold for Local File / Master File preparation in Canada. The CRA expects contemporaneous documentation for all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions. Penalties for transfer pricing misstatements apply where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue (s.247(4)). Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance and primarily undertaken to avoid tax. A Quebec LP that is a mere shell with no real employees, no office, and no actual business activity will be disregarded or recharacterized as a conduit, exposing the arrangement to reassessment, interest, penalties, and potential criminal exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1)(a): fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the taxes evaded, plus up to 5 years imprisonment. Civil penalties under s.163(2) equal 50% of the understated tax. Québec imposes additional penalties of 30% to 150% of evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1. For tax evasion, if a director knowingly participates, they can be personally assessed for the corporate penalty, subject to a restitution order, a travel ban, and imprisonment up to 2 years.
Unacceptable Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3) is 10% of the adjustment amount that exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. Thin capitalization rules under s.18(4) disallow interest on debt exceeding a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio (for fiscal years commencing after March 28, 2023). The CRA will recharacterize the transaction and deny deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors may not be personally liable solely for a transfer pricing adjustment, but if they knowingly participate in a sham documentation, they may incur civil liability under s.239(2) up to 100% of the penalty and be barred from crossing the border if a travel ban is imposed.
Non-Filing / Late Filing of Information Returns

Statutory sanction: CRA penalty for late T5013 is CAD 25 per partner per day, up to CAD 2,500. Québec penalty for each missing RL-15 is CAD 50, up to CAD 2,500, increasing to CAD 5,000 for a second violation within five years. Additionally, the partners' information allocations are considered late and subject to interest.

Director personal liability: Under the Québec Civil Code, a general partner is personally liable for the partnership's obligations, including regulatory penalties. If the general partner is a corporation, its directors may be held personally liable for the corporation's failure to enforce compliance, if they voted for or acquiesced to the omission.
Artificial Tax Planning / Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: The CRA will apply GAAR under s.245 to deny the claimed tax benefit, impose a 15% penalty on the denied benefit under s.245(6), and may add a misrepresentation penalty under s.163(2). Where intentional sham is proven, the Income Tax Act s.239 criminal penalty applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate a GAAR-tainted structure are jointly and severally liable for the income tax and penalties under s.160 and s.245.3, and may be subject to a personal civil claim by the CRA, a travel ban, and imprisonment for up to 2 years for contempt of court in repeated non-compliance.
