Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) portal at www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) by registering via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts). For non-resident corporations, additional documentation is required, including certified copies of constitutional documents and, where applicable, a completed Non-Resident Corporation Information Return. The BN is the sole tax identification number; there is no separate VAT ID in Canada. The GST/HST account is opened as part of the same BN registration process and carries a separately issued GST/HST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate an IRS-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections for cross-border corporate structures include: (i) subsection 216(1) ITA election permitting non-residents to file a net-basis return for Canadian rental income; (ii) subsection 261(1) ITA functional-currency election; (iii) subsection 217 ITA election for non-residents with Canadian employment income; (iv) corporate tax reorganizations under subsections 85, 86, and 88(1) ITA; and (v) special hybrid-mismatch and foreign affiliate elections under sections 93, 113, and 5907 of the Income Tax Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold determined over any single calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration once exceeded)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated if the supplier maintains conclusive proof of export within the statutory timelines under section 72 of the Excise Tax Act (as amended), including export documentation and shipment records. Services are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI, section 7, when the supply is to a non-resident and the service is performed outside Canada; intangible personal property (including software and digital content) qualifies for zero-rating under section 23.2 of Schedule VI when the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is for consumption outside Canada. For SaaS exports to non-resident customers, zero-rating applies only where the customer is not registered for GST/HST and the supply does not relate to Canadian real estate or tangible goods delivered in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or SWIFT proof of payment received)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Electronic invoices are permitted provided they satisfy the CRA’s record-keeping and invoice content requirements under section 223(1) of the Excise Tax Act and section 286 of the Act. Mandatory electronic GST/HST return filing applies where turnover exceeds CAD 1.5M; regular electronic filing is available for all registrants.

CRA is entitled to reassess claims within a standard four-year period, or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Unsupported zero-rating and export refund claims attract a 25% penalty under subsection 289(2) of the Excise Tax Act, plus statutory interest. Willful evasion penalties can reach 200% of the net tax evaded, and criminal liability under section 293 of the ETA includes fine amounts up to twice the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to five years. Refunds already issued may be clawed back through reassessment, together with interest and penalties, where documentation fails audit or where the export is subsequently found to be non-qualifying.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full Article VIII convertibility: zero exchange controls on current or capital account transactions and a freely floating CAD with no requirement for central-bank approval before remitting profits, dividends, interest, or royalties. The only substantive hurdles are tax-administrative: non-resident withholding tax under Income Tax Act Part XIII (default 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% on substantial US dividends), T106 related-party reporting, Part XIII remittance forms, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds for electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within 24 hours. Multi-currency USD/CAD treasury accounts are widely available, and no repatriation quota or prior-exchange-approval regime applies; the business can therefore invoice in USD via Stripe/Paddle and sweep settlement proceeds into a CAD operational account at any Schedule I bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender, no window within which repatriation must occur, and no mandatory conversion ratio (0% surrender, 0-day statutory timeline). AIGC video-generation SaaS export receipts from the United States and other markets may be retained in USD or any invoiced currency indefinitely. The only compliance overlay is: (i) CRA translation of foreign-currency revenue to CAD at the Bank of Canada published daily or monthly exchange rate, or a subsection 261(1) Income Tax Act functional-currency election for the corporation; (ii) FINTRAC EFT report filing by the bank for any single international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) CBSA cross-border currency reporting for physical or negotiated instruments exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary, currency issuance, and retail payments oversight authority; exchange-control administration is functionally shared with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for tax-based FX reporting, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) for foreign-investment admission under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with a strict legal boundary. Foreign EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Sila) can lawfully provide USD/EUR virtual IBANs and execute outbound vendor payments, and Canadian corporates are permitted to use them as secondary FX-holding wallets. They cannot, however, function as the sole CAD settlement rail for Stripe/Paddle payouts because CAD clearing requires membership or sponsored access to Payments Canada systems (ACSS and Lynx) and, where the fintech acts as a payment service provider to Canadian customers, registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The prudent treasury construct is a hybrid: Schedule I operational account as the primary CAD settlement venue, foreign EMI used only for currency assortment of international receivables and small-value cross-border disbursements, never for capital injection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: the foreign shareholder transmits a SWIFT MT103 to the Ontario corporation's CAD-denominated account at a Schedule I bank (recommended: BMO or CIBC); Field 59 names the legal corporation and account number; Field 72 carries the instruction code INVCAP plus the subscription-agreement reference; the bank's transfer purpose code must classify the inflow as a capital contribution (paid-up capital subscription), not as commercial merchandise. The remitting shareholder must furnish the bank with source-of-funds documentation, and the receiving bank will require the corporation's director resolution authorizing the booking before crediting funds. Where the subscription exceeds CAD 500,000 and control transfers to a non-Canadian, ISED notification under the Investment Canada Act applies, with particular sensitivity in the AI sector. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Ontario corporate income tax rate (15% federal general rate plus 11.5% Ontario general rate) on active business income; federal rate includes the 10% provincial abatement and 5% general rate reduction calculations in the T2 return.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident company to a non-resident shareholder, before application of any income tax treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period; balance-due payment is generally due 2 months after the fiscal year-end (3 months for an eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation), and non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada follow the same 6-month filing rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal Canadian Local File/Master File threshold is prescribed; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, but a Form T106 disclosure is specifically required when the aggregate fair market value of transactions between the corporation and a non-resident non-arm's-length person exceeds CAD 1 million in the taxation year; a full OECD-aligned Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least CAD 800 million, and CRA may request the underlying TP documentation in audit even below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, CRA tests commercial substance under the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245) and transfer pricing rule (ITA s.247). An Ontario-resident AI avatar software corporation must maintain Canadian employees, board decisions, bank accounts, R&D activities, customer contracting, and substantive ownership of its core intellectual property; otherwise, CRA may recharacterize intercompany arrangements, deny treaty benefits, and assess the shareholder directly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years if prosecuted on indictment; s.163(2) civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax, plus compound daily arrears interest; any conviction can result in cancellation of the federal business number and inability to obtain CRA clearances.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized or participated in evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA ss.227.1 and 227.05 for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and corporate tax arrears; criminal exposure includes imprisonment, personal fines, restitution orders, asset forfeiture, and restrictions on international travel pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: CRA will re-determine income using the arm's-length principle under ITA s.247(2) and may impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment when the adjustment exceeds applicable statutory thresholds, plus arrears interest; failure to file T106 separately attracts penalties under ITA s.162(10) and daily penalties for repeated non-filing.

Director personal liability: A director can be assessed personally for a corporation's unremitted tax balances; knowingly signing false TP documentation can trigger criminal liability for tax evasion and the CRA may seek director assessments for unpaid Part XIII withholding where the director was responsible for the withholding system.
Shell Company and Sham Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the corporation as a sham or apply the GAAR (s.245) to reallocate income to the shareholder; the corporation can lose treaty benefits under the OECD MLI and the applicable DTA's limitation-on-benefits clause; continuous failure to file T2 returns also gives the federal Director under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the provincial Director under the Ontario Business Corporations Act the right to dissolve the corporation by certificate of dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors of a dissolved shell remain liable for unpaid corporate tax and unremitted source deductions; courts may pierce the corporate veil when the shell is used to evade tax, causing the director/shareholder to be personally liable for civil tax liabilities and subject to shareholder-level criminal charges, travel restrictions, and orders for restitution.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Infrastructure Violations

Statutory sanction: Failure to keep proper books and records as required by ITA s.230(1) is an offence punishable by summary conviction and daily fines; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an unregistered money services business or a person carrying on a reportable transaction with missing/reporting failures is subject to administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 100,000 per violation (and up to CAD 500,000 for a subsequent violation for money laundering/financial transactions), while Criminal Code participation in laundering the proceeds of crime is an indictable offence punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly execute or benefit from structured settlements and false bookkeeping may be criminally liable for laundering the proceeds of crime or for fraudulent concealment of tax; courts can order seizure and forfeiture of corporate and personal assets, prohibit bank accounts, and freeze personal foreign travel until the individual appears for proceedings.
