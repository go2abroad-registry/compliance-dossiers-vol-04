Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Electronic client portals: CRA My Business Account (www.canada.ca/cra-business-account) and RQ Online Services for Businesses / CLIC (www.revenuquebec.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) declaration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) before commencing activity. Step 2: Register the partnership with CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) — online via 'Register a business with the CRA' or Form RC1 — and open the GST/HST account (and, if applicable, payroll and import/export accounts). Step 4: Activate CRA 'My Business Account' and RQ 'CLIC' credentials for portal filing and direct-debit remittance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Canadian/Quebec limited partnership is a fiscally transparent flow-through entity; the LP itself is not subject to federal or provincial income tax. Each partner (including non-resident limited partners) is taxed on its share of income as earned; the partnership is treated as a separate registrant/person for GST/HST and QST purposes to the extent it carries on a commercial activity.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or elective entity classification regime; flow-through treatment is statutory and mandatory. Practical statutory elections affecting the LP and its non-resident partners include: (i) the subsection 216(1) ITA election permitting a non-resident to file a Canadian income tax return on net rental/business income, (ii) elections to treat Canada–U.S. or other treaty partners' hybrid entities consistently under Canada's OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules, and (iii) GST/HST elections such as the 'single joint election' for parties to a joint venture (where relevant).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — mandatory GST/HST and QST registration where the taxable supply threshold (taxable supplies made in Canada / in Quebec in either a single calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters) exceeds C$30,000; non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada through a Canadian establishment, or supplying real property situated in Canada, are generally required to register without regard to the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Registered suppliers may zero-rate export supplies of 3D game art, sculpting and rigging services where the recipient is a non-resident person, the service is not performed in relation to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and the digital deliverables are supplied for use wholly outside Canada. Physical goods exported directly to a non-resident are zero-rated when shipped out of Canada and supported by prescribed export evidence. Pure electronic delivery of game assets does not require customs clearance, but the supplier must retain contract, non-resident identity, delivery and payment documents to substantiate the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or CBSA ACI eManifest) — required where tangible goods ship across the border; not applicable to pure digital services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a named foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 / bank foreign-exchange conversion record evidencing receipt of foreign currency export consideration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction control or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA and Revenu Québec support Peppol-based e-invoicing (BIS 3.0) on an opt-in basis; paper and PDF invoices remain valid. Mandatory electronic obligations: GST/HST registrants with annual revenue over C$1.5 million must file returns electronically and remit funds electronically when net tax payable exceeds C$10,000; exporters must file advance customs declarations through CBSA ACI/eManifest.

Without adequate export evidence, CRA/Revenu Québec will reassess the zero-rated supply as taxable, recapture related input tax credits, and assess arrears interest plus penalties (ETA s.280; AQRT equivalent). False statements, neglect or misrepresentation on returns invoke a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax at issue; wilful evasion is criminally prosecutable with fines up to C$100,000 (indictment) and imprisonment, and the normal four-year reassessment period extends to ten years. For limited partnerships, general partners are jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST debt; limited partners who participate in the control or management may forfeit limited-liability protection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. No exchange control licence, prior approval, or repatriation consent is required for inbound capital contributions, outbound trade settlements, or profit distributions by a Quebec societe en commandite (limited partnership). Profit remittance mechanics: because the LP is tax-transparent, distributions to non-resident partners are not dividends and are therefore not subject to the Part XIII 25% withholding tax that applies to dividends, interest, royalties, rents, or management fees. Instead, each non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on its allocable share of the partnership's Canadian-source business income, and the partnership is required to file a prescribed non-resident partner return and to remit the associated tax (the section 194 regime of the Income Tax Act); failure to remit creates joint and several liability for the partnership. A further remittance hurdle arises only on disposition of a partnership interest if the interest constitutes taxable Canadian property, in which case a purchaser-side clearance certificate under section 116 may be required; for a game-art studio holding largely working capital, receivables, and goodwill, the interest will typically not be taxable Canadian property. In practice, the only structural hurdle is the LP's obligation to register with Revenu Quebec and the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec, and to provide CRA/Revenu Quebec with partner-level allocation schedules (T5013/RL-15 equivalents).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The LP may retain USD/EUR receivables offshore or in a domestic USD account, and may convert at the time of its choosing. Reporting obligations are limited to: (i) a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) for international electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction; (ii) a FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) for cash receipts exceeding CAD 10,000; (iii) cross-border currency/ monetary instrument reporting to the CBSA for physical movement of currency exceeding CAD 10,000; and (iv) ordinary accounting translation of FX gains/losses to partners under IFRS/ASP for tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary authority and note-issuing central bank. Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control agency equivalent to a SAFE-style administration; FX policy is liberalized and administered through prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and trade/sanctions enforcement by Global Affairs Canada (formerly Foreign Affairs and International Trade Canada). Retail and corporate FX dealing is additionally regulated at the provincial level in Quebec by the Autorite des marches financiers (AMF) where a money-services business is involved.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign-incorporated digital EMI/neo-bank platforms (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Ebury, Mercury) can be used as supplementary treasury rails but are not a substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account. Legally, an EMI that solicits or accepts Canadian clients must either be a registered Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, hold an authorization or be an agent/partner of a Canadian chartered bank, and comply with Quebec AMF money-services rules where applicable. For a Canadian LP, the prudent structure is: (i) a primary corporate account at National Bank of Canada or Desjardins for CAD liquidity, statutory payments, and tax-compliance evidence; (ii) a Wise Business or Payoneer account used strictly for collecting small-balance PayPal/aggregator receivables and for low-value cross-border vendor disbursements; and (iii) a standing instruction that any EFT over CAD 10,000 must clear through SWIFT on the primary bank rail to preserve the audit trail and FINTRAC EFTR compliance. The LP's partnership agreement should expressly authorize the use of EMIs and prohibit commingling of partner funds.

Compliant protocol for partner capital injections: (1) each non-resident partner remits from its own verified bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated CAD operating account at the primary bank; (2) the remittance information field must read: 'Capital contribution to [LP legal name], partner [legal name], dated [date], ref [capital call notice number]'; (3) Canada has no mandatory BOP or purpose-code regime, but the bank's reason-for-payment dropdown should indicate 'other capital investment' or 'capital contribution to limited partnership' where prompted; (4) if funds arrive in USD, a simultaneous spot FX deal ticket must be recorded with the bank at the executed rate; (5) the LP must maintain a partner capital ledger with partner-by-partner lines, the signed capital call notice, the executed partnership agreement, and the bank payment order as AML proof; (6) partner loans used to fund working capital must be supported by a written loan agreement and labeled 'shareholder loan' to prevent recharacterization as a capital contribution or taxable income; (7) where a general partner resolution authorizes the account signatories, a certified copy must be provided to the bank with each new signatory appointment; and (8) all inbound injections exceeding CAD 10,000 will be captured by the bank's FINTRAC EFTR filing, so the LP should pre-emptively provide the source-of-funds package referenced in the KYC defense.Clearing and settlement for outbound trade payments: (1) primary rail for vendor invoices above CAD 10,000 is a SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) via the LP's Schedule I bank, using beneficiary IBAN/account and SWIFT/BIC, with purpose text 'payment for digital art services under contract no. real-time payments may be executed via Interac e-Transfer or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value urgent amounts; (4) USD disbursements to US-based artists should route via ACH through the bank's cross-border USD platform or via SWIFT for priority, with supplier portal confirmations; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign-incorporated digital EMI/neo-bank platforms (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Ebury, Mercury) can be used as supplementary treasury rails but are not a substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account. Legally, an EMI that solicits or accepts Canadian clients must either be a registered Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, hold an authorization or be an agent/partner of a Canadian chartered bank, and comply with Quebec AMF money-services rules where applicable. For a Canadian LP, the prudent structure is: (i) a primary corporate account at National Bank of Canada or Desjardins for CAD liquidity, statutory payments, and tax-compliance evidence; (ii) a Wise Business or Payoneer account used strictly for collecting small-balance PayPal/aggregator receivables and for low-value cross-border vendor disbursements; and (iii) a standing instruction that any EFT over CAD 10,000 must clear through SWIFT on the primary bank rail to preserve the audit trail and FINTRAC EFTR compliance. The LP's partnership agreement should expressly authorize the use of EMIs and prohibit commingling of partner funds.
