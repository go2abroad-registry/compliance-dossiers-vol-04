Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) at the registered seat of the company, under the functional supervision of the Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Amt für das Handelsregister, EHR) of the Federal Office of Justice; electronic filing via the eGRIS platform and publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SHAB).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR; SR 220), Fifth Part, Articles 620–763, in particular Article 626 (minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000), Article 627 (mandatory content of the Articles of Association), Article 632 (minimum paid-up cash capital and blocking requirement), Article 634 (in-kind contribution report), Article 640 (commercial register filings) and Article 698 (general meeting); supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV; SR 221.411) and, for the regulated product scope, the Medical Devices Ordinance (MedDO; SR 812.213).

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister — Application for Entry in the Commercial Register, filed together with the notarized deed of incorporation and the Articles of Association (Statuten).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Aktiengesellschaft requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000 (approx. USD 112,000). At least CHF 20,000 (20% of the nominal capital) must be paid up in cash on a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) opened in the company's name at a licensed Swiss bank or financial intermediary before registration; the bank's confirmation (Liberierungsbestätigung) that the funds are at the company's free disposal must accompany the application, and the funds are released to the Board only after the constitutive Commercial Register entry. In-kind contributions require a special audit report under Article 634 OR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists. An AG is automatically an independent taxable person subject to Swiss federal, cantonal and communal corporate income tax. Statutory election mechanisms available include the Qualified Participation Deduction for dividends and capital gains on qualifying qualifying participations, the election for Swiss VAT group registration under Art. 21 VAT Act, partial VAT deduction election for real estate, and the deemed liquidation election on relocation/emigration under Swiss international taxation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies for Swiss-resident businesses (Art. 10 VAT Act); non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Switzerland are subject to VAT regardless of any threshold and must register or appoint a fiscal representative unless an exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% VAT) applies to the physical export of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks sold by a Swiss taxable person to a foreign buyer, provided the goods are transported directly from Switzerland to a foreign destination, the exporter retains a valid customs export declaration, the goods leave Switzerland unconditionally and definitively, and the export transaction is recorded in the seller's business records. The export must be supported within the statutory deadline by customs and transport evidence; the recipient must not be domiciled in Switzerland and the commercial terms must place transfer of ownership abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Ausfuhrdeklaration validated by the Swiss Federal Customs Administration (BAZG / EZV), including the export transaction number (NCTS/export accompanying document)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or CMR road consignment note showing physical dispatch from Switzerland to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice for the dental zirconia blocks (ISIC 3250) indicating 0% VAT and the exporter's Swiss VAT ID
  • Packing list, purchase order, and export sales contract evidencing the foreign buyer and delivery terms
  • Bank SWIFT wire or TT payment credit advice, or an Inward Remittance Certificate from the Swiss bank, proving actual receipt of the foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, government clearance API, or mandatory digital stamping is currently required for Swiss VAT e-invoicing. Electronic invoices are permissible if authenticity, integrity and readability are guaranteed for the 10-year retention period. Domestic payment and e-billing may use the Swiss QR-bill standard, but the ESTV does not require pre-approval of invoices through a national clearance portal.

If the export zero-rating cannot be substantiated with valid customs and logistics documents, the VAT has to be retrospectively assessed at 8.1% plus statutory default interest from the date of supply. Knowingly false export declarations or fictitious documents trigger criminal proceedings under Art. 82-85 of the Swiss VAT Act and the Federal Customs Act, with fines up to CHF 30,000 or imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud, forfeiture of the tax advantage, denial/recovery of input VAT deductions, and, in serious cases, an administrative penalty not exceeding three times the evaded VAT. In addition, legal and beneficial ownership of the goods and SWIFT/TT payment provenance are scrutinized to counter sham exports and circular cash flows.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Switzerland maintains no exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements for profit repatriation, and no restrictions on dividend or capital transfers. Cross-border payments in CHF, EUR, and USD are free-flowing; the only friction is the 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends, which is fully or partially reclaimable under applicable double taxation treaties. No convertibility hurdles, no negative interest regime at present.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into CHF. Exporters may freely retain, credit, or convert foreign currency receipts. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the SNB apply for quarterly transactions above CHF 10 million or annual turnover thresholds; no treasury restriction is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), Schweizerische Nationalbank, with banking supervision under the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility for Swiss FINMA-licensed fintech institutions operating under Art. 1b Banking Act (Fintech/Sandbox license), which may hold public deposits up to CHF 100 million without full banking license. Offshore foreign EMIs without Swiss FINMA supervision are practically non-viable: Swiss banks routinely decline to serve as correspondent for unsupervised EMIs, and mandated Anti-Money Laundering representatives are unwilling to accept liability for non-supervised beneficiaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed as statutory paid-in capital: funds wired via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'GDSK' or 'INVC' into a designated capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) prescribed by the Swiss notary; the bank verifies the share subscription agreement, notarised deed, and board capital payment resolution against the wire before releasing credit to the operating account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal direct corporate income tax assessed on post-tax net profit (equivalent to 7.83% of pre-tax profit); combined with cantonal and communal profit taxes in the Canton of Zug, total effective burden approx. 14.6% of pre-tax profit (2024-2025 published rate for the municipality of Zug, including federal direct tax, after capital tax offset).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within six months after the close of the statutory business year (Art. 125-126 Federal Act on Direct Taxes, DBG); for a company with a 31 December year-end, the ordinary deadline is 30 June of the following year, with individual extensions routinely granted upon non-taxable request until 31 October; further extension only upon written motion showing grounds. Tax is assessed by notice of assessment and is not self-assessed.
