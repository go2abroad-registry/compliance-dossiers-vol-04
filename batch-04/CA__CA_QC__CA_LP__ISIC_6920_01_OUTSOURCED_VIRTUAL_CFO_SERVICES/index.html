Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None — the Civil Code of Québec imposes no minimum paid-up capital, government escrow, or capitalization threshold for a limited partnership. Contributions (apports) of the commandité and commanditaire are fixed freely in the partnership agreement; a limited partner must contribute money or property in exchange for its interest, not mere services, and the aggregate contribution sum is only optionally published in the REQ register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: certification of the leasehold under the Safety Code (CQLR c. Structural and accessibility clearance: building inspector approval for office occupancy (type A) under the Construction Code, including barrier-free access. Occupational health: CNESST establishment registration under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. S-2.1), a written prevention program, first-aid kit, and telework policy covering home-office workstation ergonomics and incident reporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar floats freely. Profit remittances, dividends, partnership distributions, interest, royalties, and repayment of shareholder/intercompany loans are not subject to prior exchange-control approval. The only statutory hurdles are procedural: non-resident withholding taxes under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act, transfer-pricing rules under Section 247, CRS/FATCA reporting, and provincial registration requirements in Quebec. Adequate contemporaneous documentation is required to support the tax character of each flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export earnings. Corporate exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts in domestic or offshore bank accounts. For customs valuation and GST/QST compliance, export invoices and settlement records must be maintained in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; no central-bank surrender or approval is necessary.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with functional currency administration by the federal Minister of Finance. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange control or exchange-rate management agency; OSFI supervises federally chartered banks and authorized foreign banks, while FINTRAC enforces anti-money laundering and transaction-reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for non-escrow, operational, cross-border payment purposes. Offshore digital EMI services such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not chartered banks and cannot provide CDIC deposit insurance or statutory trust-account integrity required for a Canadian limited partnership's escrow arrangements. These services may be used for lower-risk disbursements and remote payments after FINTRAC MSB registration (if the EMI is deemed to be carrying on business in Canada) and, where applicable, registration with the Quebec Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). However, the core treasury architecture should include at least one Schedule I Canadian bank account to satisfy legal, regulatory, and client-fiduciary expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is achieved via SWIFT MT103 from a recognized foreign financial institution to the partner's / LP's RBC or National Bank account in Montreal. In the SWIFT narrative, the sender must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — PARTNERSHIP ACT OF QUEBEC' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN — ARM'S LENGTH' together with the Department of Finance transaction code CA tax identifier, and should identify the underlying partnership agreement reference. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, CNB / FINTRAC is triggered; the bank will submit an FTR. The LP must maintain a capital sub-ledger showing name of contributor, date, amount in source currency, Canadian-dollar equivalent at Bank of Canada rate, and supporting wire confirmation. No Form 91-type frills or certificates are required.The primary rail for cleared outbound trade disbursements is SWIFT MT103; for procurement activities requiring documentary security, a commercial letter of credit (UCP600) confirmed by a Canadian Schedule I bank is used. Where the client pays in multiple currencies, internal settlement must use a dual-currency ledger integrated with Wise or Airwallex merely as a FX vehicle; however, the legal pay-out instruction should always originate from the bank's regulated treasury workstation to preserve a clear audit trail. Payments to suppliers in non-SWIFT jurisdictions may be effected via correspondent banking arrangements in the beneficiary's domicile, but such indirect routing must be documented under FINTRAC Rule 4.
