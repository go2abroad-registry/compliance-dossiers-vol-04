Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax (Income Tax Act s. 96) and Ontario tax purposes; the partnership itself is not an income tax taxpayer, and each partner, wherever resident, includes its allocated share of partnership income/loss in its own return. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is deemed a 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and holds its own BN/RT registration. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the partnership are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty relief (e.g., Canada-US Treaty Art. VII permanent establishment threshold); non-resident limited partners may also face Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions attributable to Canadian sources. Limited partner loss deductibility is statutorily capped at the partner's 'at-risk amount' (ITA ss. 96.2.1, 96.3, 143.2). Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed only on Canadian-source income, with treaty override; the LP's Ontario situs does not by itself create a permanent establishment in Ontario for a foreign general partner if the drop-shipping platform activity is carried on entirely through independent agents or foreign servers, but Canadian fulfillment-center inventory creates an Ontario permanent establishment risk. Ontario has no partnership-level capital tax; Ontario HST is administered federally.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' election; entity classification is fixed by statute. Available elections and designations relevant to a Canadian LP in automated dropshipping: (i) Fiscally transparent entity designation under Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(6)(c) (and equivalent provisions in other treaties) allowing US partners to claim treaty benefits on a look-through basis; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 216(1) election for non-resident partners to compute Canadian rental/business income on a net, self-assessed basis instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (iii) Partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96(3)/Reg. 1100(1) establishing a common taxation year-end for all partners; (iv) Hybrid mismatch provisions (ITA ss. 18.2-18.4, enacted by Budget Implementation Act 2023, effective for transactions after July 1, 2022) which operate by default with specific elective carve-outs where the partnership demonstrates arm's-length commercial substance and no imported hybrid mismatch benefit; (v) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 225(2)(b)) for simplified accounting and remittance when annual taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 400,000; (vi) GST/HST annual filing and alternative fiscal-year elections (ETA s. 226); (vii) Election to have the marketplace facilitator (e.g., an e-commerce platform) treated as the deemed supplier for GST/HST on facilitated supplies, shifting revenue-recognition for HST purposes to the platform.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a floating exchange rate regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted and are not subject to prior governmental approval or quantitative limits. Canada complies with IMF Article VIII, Sections 2, 3, and 4 obligations. Profit remittances by an Ontario Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are lawful and may be wired abroad without Bank of Canada authorization; in practice, the paying bank may require an updated partnership capital account statement, evidence that the entity is not insolvent, and confirmation that the distribution does not inadvertently create a taxable dividend payment requiring Part XIII withholding. No waiting period or exchange-control approval is statute-imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable - Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory surrender ratio, and no currency conversion requirement for export or dropshipping proceeds. Exporters and LPs may retain proceeds indefinitely in CAD or any foreign currency. However, banks must report to FINTRAC every large electronic funds transfer or cash transaction of CAD 10,000 or more and must file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) for any structuring or unusual inward/outward flow. The Ontario LP should therefore segregate CAD operating receipts from USD and CNY trade settlement flows to enable clean reconciliation during FINTRAC and OSFI-required periodic reviews.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank for monetary policy and, since the Retail Payment Activities Act, also supervises retail payment service providers. There is no separate Canadian Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange rate policy is a Department of Finance Canada mandate. Canadian financial institutions are prudentially regulated by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility assessment: Medium - permissible as a supplemental settlement rail, but not as the primary deposit-taking backbone. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Mercury are not OSFI-licensed banks and do not qualify for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. They may legally hold funds for a Canadian LP provided that: (i) the EMI is properly licensed or authorized in its home jurisdiction; (ii) any Canadian payment-processing activity is covered by either a FINTRAC money services business registration or a Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration once fully phased in; and (iii) the LP discloses all EMI accounts to its Canadian bank at onboarding to prevent false-positive STR filings. The primary Canadian bank should remain the Lynx/ACSS-connected institution for payroll, tax, and correspondent-clearing purposes.

Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Mercury are not OSFI-licensed banks and do not qualify for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (federal 15% plus Ontario provincial 11.5% on taxable income eligible for the federal small business deduction); otherwise combined general rate 26.5% for active business income, subject to provincial abatement, with the federal general rate 15% and Ontario general rate 11.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident individual shareholder; where the LP is a limited partnership, the partnership itself is not liable for Part XIII withholding on distributions to non-resident partners unless the distribution is characterized as a dividend sourced from a Canadian corporation; non-resident partners are instead subject to either Part I tax on Canadian-source business income or Part XII.2 tax on excessive distributions from a SIFT trust or partnership

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Canada Revenue Agency does not prescribe a single fixed monetary threshold for mandatory transfer pricing documentation; documentation is required when the aggregate value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length persons exceeds CAD 1 million for the fiscal year and the transactions are not covered by the small-business transitional relief rules (which exempt taxpayers with under CAD 1 million in cross-border transactions or that are not resident in Canada and have no permanent establishment in Canada); the contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the fiscal year-end and maintained for six years; a separate Local File and Master File are required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and the transactions exceed CAD 1 million

Economic substance scope: Ontario and Canada do not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions; however, the Canada Revenue Agency may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act to disregard a transaction lacking genuine economic substance, and the Ministry of Finance may apply the anti-hybrid and foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules; a limited partnership used in a cross-border dropshipping operation must demonstrate real business activity in Ontario, including inventory logistics, order fulfillment decision-making, and platform management, to sustain its tax classification as a partnership and to counter any assertion that it is a conduit or shell entity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Misreporting Revenue or Concealing Offshore Dropshipping Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded plus 100% of the tax evaded; maximum fine of CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties under subsection 163(2): gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the Ontario limited partnership are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, interest, and penalties if they failed to exercise due diligence; the CRA may issue a director's liability assessment under subsection 160(1) and pursue personal assets; travel bans and non-payment of tax can be enforced under the Tax Enforcement Act, and an individual may be imprisoned for contempt for failure to comply with a court order to pay arrears
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) denies the tax benefit and imposes reassessment with full tax, plus arrears interest and a 10% penalty for misrepresentation attributable to negligence; section 247 transfer pricing penalty: 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment exceeding CAD 50,000

Director personal liability: The general partner of an Ontario limited partnership is personally liable for all partnership debts and obligations if the limited partnership is found to be a sham, because the limited liability protection is void when the limited partnership has no real commercial substance; the court may pierce the corporate veil and order the individual general partner to pay the full tax liability, plus punitive damages; a non-resident partner who knowingly participates in a conduit arrangement may be subject to an order for civil restitution of all amounts received
AML / KYC Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; for money laundering as a criminal offence, imprisonment up to 14 years and fines at the discretion of the court; failure to report a suspicious transaction may result in a penalty of up to CAD 250,000

Director personal liability: The general partner or corporate director is personally liable if they knowingly failed to establish the required AML policies and procedures, or if they condoned or participated in the transaction structuring; the FINTRAC may issue a compliance order, and a conviction may lead to a separate personal fine and an order for restitution to victims; a Canadian criminal court may impose a travel ban as a condition of bail or probation
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 247(3), a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment assessed for the year, where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 50,000 and 10% of gross revenue; interest and arrears are also assessed from the date the return was due; a failure to provide documentation may lead to an adverse inference on the accuracy of the pricing

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to joint and several liability for the penalty if they knowingly participated in the failure to provide documentation or in making the misstatement; the CRA may impose a director's penalty and may seek a court order prohibiting the director from leaving the jurisdiction until the tax debt is secured
Cross-Border Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Part XIII, the withholding agent is liable for the full 25% statutory withholding tax not remitted, plus interest and penalties; failure to deduct and remit continues to accrue interest at the prescribed rate plus a 40% failure-to-withhold penalty on the amount withheld; criminal penalties for deliberately failing to withhold may also be imposed

Director personal liability: The general partner of the LP is the withholding agent and is personally liable for the unremitted amounts; a director of a corporate general partner can be assessed under subsection 227.1(1) for the full amount of the unremitted withholding tax, including interest and penalties, with no defence of good faith; the CRA may issue a garnishment order on personal bank accounts and a lien on personal real property
