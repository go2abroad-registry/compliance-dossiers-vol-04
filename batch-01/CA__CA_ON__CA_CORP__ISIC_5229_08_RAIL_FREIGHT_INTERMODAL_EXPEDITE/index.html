Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and the GST/HST 'Represent a Client' portal for non-resident applicants.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident corporations seeking to supply rail freight or intermodal expeditement services in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account. Apply online through Business Registration Online or file Form RC1 with CRA; provide corporate legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered and mailing addresses, director information, fiscal year-end, and expected annual taxable revenue. CRA will issue the BN and GST/HST registration number without requiring creation of a Canadian legal entity, subject to the non-resident carrying on business in Canada determination under the Excise Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident business corporation taxed on worldwide income under Income Tax Act, with non-residents taxed only on Canadian-source income attributable to a permanent establishment; no provincial tax carve-out for GST/HST as Ontario imposes HST jointly administered by CRA.

Available election forms

Canada does not employ an IRS-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include the Income Tax Act s.216 election for net-basis taxation of non-resident rental income, s.219 deemed-disposition election for non-resident corporations, and the Excise Tax Act s.156 joint election for GST/HST common-use property and services. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are governed by ITA ss.18.6 and 18.7, and no check-the-box entity reclassification is available for Canadian federal or Ontario provincial income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per single calendar quarter in worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada; once the threshold is exceeded or likely to be exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. Non-residents with a presence in Ontario are assessed on the same small-supplier rules under the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are eligible for the 0% GST/HST rate when the property is delivered to a non-resident purchaser and exported from Canada as soon as practicable, or when the supplier is contractually obligated to export the goods. Transportation and freight-forwarding services related to export goods may be zero-rated under Part VII of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are for shipment outside Canada. Offshore services performed in Canada for a non-resident person may qualify under Part VI of Schedule VI provided the direct benefit does not accrue to a person in Canada. The rail and intermodal expedited export transaction must maintain contemporaneous proof that the goods left Canada and that the support services relate to the exported cargo.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada currently mandates no real-time national fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control API for GST/HST invoicing. Registrants must retain source documents and issue invoices containing prescribed particulars under the Excise Tax Act Regulations. Electronic filing and electronic remittance are mandatory for certain large registrants; participation in the voluntary Pan-Canadian and Peppol-based e-invoicing framework is available but not statutorily required.

CRA auditors will scrutinize export documentation, carrier records, terminal transfer receipts, rail waybills, destination customs entries, and foreign currency repatriation trailing 180 days. Misdeclared or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger reassessment and revocation of the zero rate, plus penalties under Excise Tax Act ss.280, 285 and 285.3, gross negligence penalties, interest accruing from the filing due date, and backward clawback of input tax credits. Directors and senior officers may face joint and several liability under s.323 ETA for unremitted amounts. Repeated contravention or documentary fabrication may also result in criminal culpability under s.327.1 ETA and civil enforcement by the Public Prosecution Service of Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible under both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements, and no quantitative limits on cross-border wire transfers, dividend repatriation, capital subscriptions, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittances by a non-resident from a Canadian corporation are subject to Part XIII withholding tax unless reduced under an income tax treaty, but no FX conversion or permit hurdle exists. Foreign-exchange transactions are conducted at market-determined rates through regulated Canadian financial institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio. Exporters may hold receipts in CAD or any foreign currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts at their discretion. The Canada Border Services Agency may require commercial export documentation for customs and statistical purposes, but the Bank of Canada and FINTRAC do not mandate any FX conversion or escrow of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering and anti-terrorist financing supervision. Canada does not have a dedicated foreign-exchange control administrative agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium for domestically licensed fintech and payment service providers, and Low for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs). A Canadian-regulated fintech or a foreign EMI registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and supervised under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPSA) is viable for low-volume, low-risk operational transactions. Offshore EMIs that are not FINTRAC-registered MSBs may not lawfully establish a Canadian client account, clear CAD-denominated payments, or process commercial logistics disbursements. Key legal constraints include MSB registration, RPSA licensing, and the requirement that all beneficial ownership and source-of-funds information remain transparent to Canadian banking regulators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be remitted through a compliant SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire transfer from the parent company's bank to the Canadian operating account. The remitter must include a clear purpose descriptor in Field 70, using wording such as 'EQTY INJ / SHAREHOLDER LOAN – CA__CA_ON__CA_CORP' and, where applicable, the umbrella parent's reference. The Canadian receiving bank will perform FINTRAC watchlist screening and may request the share subscription agreement, director resolution, and source-of-funds documentation. For USD injections, funds can be routed through a Canadian bank's US dollar correspondent, but the beneficial ownership and purpose code must remain consistent. No federal approval is required solely for the capital injection; however, if the entity is a resident of Canada and the amount exceeds CAD 10,000 per transaction or per 24-hour period, the bank must report the incoming transfer in accordance with FINTRAC's travel rule under the Regulations in Respect of Reporting Requirements for transactions occurring over a 24-hour period.Trade settlements to rail operators, intermodal carriers, terminal handlers, customs brokers, and overseas freight forwarders should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent-bank wire transfers, or via commercial letters of credit using SWIFT MT700/M707 messaging for documentary credit issuance and amendment. CAD-denominated domestic payments can be cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value payment system (the successor to LVTS); USD or EUR payments are cleared via the respective correspondent networks. To ensure auditability, each payment must carry a unique invoice number in the remittance reference field, a correct beneficiary bank account number, and a commercial invoice or contract as backup.
