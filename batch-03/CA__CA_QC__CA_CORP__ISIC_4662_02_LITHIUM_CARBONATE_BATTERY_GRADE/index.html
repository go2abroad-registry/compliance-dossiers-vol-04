Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; a Canadian-incorporated Business Corporation is per se resident and taxable at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available election mechanisms relevant to a battery-metals trading corporation: (i) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 227(9)) for qualifying small and medium businesses to reduce compliance burden — generally available for corporations with worldwide taxable turnover ≤ CAD 400,000 per year; (ii) QST simplified accounting election at Revenu Québec for annual taxable supplies ≤ CAD 400,000; (iii) small supplier election under ETA s. 156(2) allowing elective registration before the CAD 30,000 threshold; (iv) section 216(1) election to compute income on a net-rental basis (not applicable to trading but available to non-resident shareholders disgorging rental/foreign-source income); (v) joint election under Income Tax Act s. 156(2) (not for GST) — more relevantly, the section 159(1) remission election for amalgamations, fiscal period elections under s. 34, and the election under s. 216.1 for non-residents electing under the Canada-Qatar/Canada-UAE treaty; (vi) hybrid mismatch neutrality is governed legislatively, not electively, by s. 18.4(2) and s. 18.4(3) of the Income Tax Act, which disallow deductions in respect of hybrid mismatch arrangements for Canadian payers; no check-the-box for Canadian corporations is lawful. For critical minerals (lithium), the entity may elect under the CLEERS regime (s. 127(9) ITA) for the refundable Critical Mineral Exploration, Development and Extraction Tax Credit if it holds qualifying mineral resource expenses — applicable to the battery-metals supply chain but not to a pure procurement arbitrage vehicle.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange-control licensing, quantitative limits, or central-bank pre-approval for foreign-currency transactions. The CAD, USD, EUR, CNY (offshore only) and other G10 currencies may be freely deposited, converted and remitted across Canadian borders. Capital inflows (equity injections, shareholder loans) and outflows (trade settlement, dividends, loan repayments) are unrestricted; however, non-resident dividend remittances are subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (currently 25% under the Income Tax Act, potentially reduced by treaty) and must be reported via forms NR4 and NR301 (where applicable) to the Canada Revenue Agency. No 'meaningful use' or economic-substance veto applies to remittance of profits, but banks apply internal due diligence to validate the underlying commercial purpose of any large cross-border transfer. For battery-metal arbitrage, no special import/export license is required; however, Import and Export Permits Act and Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission regulations apply only to restricted or controlled goods (not applicable to battery-grade lithium carbonate under HS 2836.91.00; the material is dual-use-sensitive but not listed on Canada's Export Control List).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or time-limited repatriation requirements exist. Canadian resident exporters and trading companies may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency, either in a designated foreign-currency (USD/CAD/EUR) operating account at a domestic Schedule I bank or in offshore accounts, without mandatory conversion to CAD. There is no deadline for repatriation and no minimum percentage of export receipts that must be monetized domestically. For banking compliance, exporters must simply record the export transaction in the Commercial Invoice, Bill of Lading, and customs entry via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for statistical purposes. FINTRAC requires the reporting of all incoming international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within 5 business days, but this is an informational threshold, not a surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the sovereign monetary authority, acting pursuant to the Bank of Canada Act, in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction-level AML/ATF compliance. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control agency; exchange-rate policy is a freely floating CAD regime under the IMF Article VIII obligations (Canada accepted Article VIII status on July 15, 1994).

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (limited to operational sub-accounts and treasury collateral not requiring credit extension); Legal Constraints: Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) (in force for ongoing supervision since September 2024 with transitional registration until September 8, 2025), payment service providers (including Wise Platform, Payoneer, Corpay/Centroid, and Airwallex) must be registered with the Bank of Canada. These EMIs are not Schedule I chartered banks and therefore cannot issue letters of credit, provide FX forward credit lines, or hold corporate funds under CDIC deposit insurance. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., non-registered Singapore/Lithuania-licensed EMIs) are legally constrained: they may not hold Canadian currency deposits, offer CAD settlement accounts to Canadian resident corporates, or clear CAD wires through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant Protocol: (1) The remitting entity issues a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) to the Canadian beneficiary account, with Field 50K (ordering customer) being the parent/contributing legal entity and Field 57A (beneficiary bank) being the Schedule I Canadian bank's receiving ABA/BIC; (2) Beneficiary Account Number (Field 59) must correspond to the dedicated 'Capital Escrow Account' (USD-denominated or CAD-denominated) opened in the name of the Quebec operating corporation; (3) Remittance Information (Field 70) must contain explicit purpose codes: for equity contributions, the ICC/ISO 20022 external purpose code 'CAPT' (Capital Transfer between related companies) or 'EQUI' (Equity/Share Capital); for shareholder loans, 'LOAN' or 'INTL' (Intercompany Loan); (4) Canadian banks additionally require a statement such as 'CONSIDERATION FOR SHARES/APPORT DE CAPITAL' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN / PRÊT ACTIONNAIRE' in the field to reconcile with the corporate resolution on file; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (limited to operational sub-accounts and treasury collateral not requiring credit extension); Legal Constraints: Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) (in force for ongoing supervision since September 2024 with transitional registration until September 8, 2025), payment service providers (including Wise Platform, Payoneer, Corpay/Centroid, and Airwallex) must be registered with the Bank of Canada. These EMIs are not Schedule I chartered banks and therefore cannot issue letters of credit, provide FX forward credit lines, or hold corporate funds under CDIC deposit insurance. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., non-registered Singapore/Lithuania-licensed EMIs) are legally constrained: they may not hold Canadian currency deposits, offer CAD settlement accounts to Canadian resident corporates, or clear CAD wires through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). They are viable only as a payment-rail complement for low-value supplier disbursements and FX conversion at wholesale mid-market rates; they are expressly not viable for the primary capital escrow, LC-backed trade, or large-amount vendor settlement (above CAD 100,000 per transaction) where a domestic Schedule I clearing account is mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if gross revenue > CAD 50 million and related-party transactions exceed CAD 1 million; Master File if gross revenue > CAD 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires substance for treaty benefits; no specific ESR but substance is key.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of tax evaded; criminal fines up to CAD 100,000; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax liabilities; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; compliance program mandatory.
