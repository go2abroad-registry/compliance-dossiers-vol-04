Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html) and CRA My Business Account. Quebec: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) administers Quebec corporate income tax, the Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ), and payroll-type levies; the Registraire des entreprises du Québec issues the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO), by fax or mail using Form RC1, or through a registered accounting agent. A non-resident corporation must provide certified copies of foreign registration documents, director/officer identification, and a Canadian address for service. Step 2: Register for GST/HST under the same BN; registration is mandatory for a non-resident carrying on business in Canada and voluntary for non-residents making zero-rated offshore supplies to recover input tax credits. Step 3: For Quebec, register the enterprise with the Registraire des entreprises to obtain an NEQ; the Registraire then facilitates opening Revenu Québec accounts (QST, corporate income tax, employer, and information returns). Quebec's small-supplier CAD 30,000 threshold does not prevent voluntary registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification; a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is automatically taxable at the entity level on worldwide income. Available statutory elections and tailored mechanisms include: (i) non-resident corporation's section 216 Income Tax Act election to report Canadian-source real-property rental income on a net basis after gross-basis withholding; (ii) controlled-foreign-affiliate status elections under ITA section 95(2.1); (iii) capital dividend account election under Form T2054; (iv) associated-corporation grouping elections for the small business deduction; (v) hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance provisions aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2 that may deny deductions or recharacterise an entity for arrangements using two jurisdictions. A Canadian business corporation cannot elect partnership-style pass-through, disregarded status, or fiscal transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold of worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (aligned for federal GST/HST and Quebec QST). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must register above this threshold and is mandatorily required to register even below CAD 30,000 if it solicits orders in Canada or supplies goods or services in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 23 of the Excise Tax Act zero-rates a supply of services where the supplier charges no GST/HST on an exported supply made to a non-resident person (other than a GST-registered non-resident) and the service is not for use, consumption or enjoyment in Canada. For this overseas PR and communications firm (ISIC 7310 / NAICS 541820), global media relations, crisis communications, campaign management and intangible communication rights supplied to non-resident clients qualify as zero-rated exports when performance and consumption occur outside Canada and the contract and work-product evidence substantiate offshore usage. Quebec's QST zero-rating rules mirror the federal export rules, so no QST applies to qualifying exported services. The exporter must be a GST/HST/QST registrant to claim input tax credits and refunds associated with the zero-rated export activity.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A/CED) filed electronically via the CBSA CARM/CAED portal for ancillary physical goods exported as part of PR campaigns; not required for services-only supplies
  • Commercial invoice or executed agency retainer contract with the non-resident client, explicitly stating that the service is supplied to a non-resident person and is not for consumption in Canada
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier statement documenting outbound movement of any printed collateral, promotional media, or event-related materials
  • Bank wire or remittance confirmation from the overseas client evidencing receipt of fees and repatriation of foreign currency converted to Canadian dollars (or equivalent forex conversion record)
  • Internal export substantiation file retained for six years, including work-product delivery logs, digital engagement records proving offshore use, and zero-rating eligibility checklists

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-authorisation stamping, cryptographic digital stamping, or mandatory fiscal API for invoices. Suppliers must issue invoices with prescribed GST/HST elements under the Excise Tax Act (sections 221–224) and the matching Quebec Tax Administration Act, and must retain the invoices as required. Quebec is phasing in a mandatory PEPPOL-compliant electronic invoicing obligation under its Tax Administration Act, beginning with the largest enterprises (phased acceptance and issuance obligations from 2026 forward), and will progressively cover all QST registrants. No real-time CRA/Revenu Québec invoice clearance is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal three-year reassessment period, with an unlimited reassessment window where misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default is established. Denial of zero-rating triggers reassessment of uncollected GST/HST and QST, disallowance of related input tax credits, refund clawback, and accrual of arrears interest at prescribed CRA/Revenu Québec rates. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act include 5% of unremitted tax plus 1% per month of default (capped at 12 months) and false-statement penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the misstated tax for gross negligence; parallel Quebec penalties apply. Under the Customs Act, false, misleading or reckless export declarations attract administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) of up to CAD 25,000 per contravention. Criminal liability for fraudulent refund claims under the Excise Tax Act carries fines and imprisonment of up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and globally traded; there are no remittance ceilings, no profit-repatriation approvals, and no central-bank quotas on conversion of retained foreign-currency income. Cross-border liquidity frictions are therefore tax-architecture frictions rather than FX-control frictions: Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (frequently treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% for dividends under the Canada-United States treaty) applies to outbound dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees; section 247 arm's-length and transfer-pricing documentation is required for intercompany financial and service transactions; Quebec GST/QST registration and reporting must be current; and wire or currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the bank to FINTRAC. Non-resident ownership of a Quebec or federal business corporation is fully permitted, and share capital may be subscribed in any convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion percentage, and no export-proceeds repatriation requirement for Canadian service exporters. A Quebec-based international public-relations and crisis-communications firm may retain USD, EUR, GBP, or other freely convertible professional fees from overseas clients in its corporate bank accounts indefinitely, or adopt the functional-currency provisions of the Income Tax Act. Taxation of such receipts is based on the Bank of Canada published exchange rate or the company's approved functional currency; invoices for services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated for GST/QST under the usual export rule where the benefit is enjoyed by the non-resident outside Canada; and any incoming or outgoing cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must pass through the reporting bank or be declared to FINTRAC or customs where cash is involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in Ottawa; foreign-exchange administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act, while OSFI supervises bank prudential soundness and FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT and cross-border currency reporting. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime without a dedicated foreign-exchange-control commission or exchange-licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, and only as an ancillary collection and payout rail rather than the primary treasury anchor. Canada does not issue a standalone electronic-money-institution licence comparable to the UK regime; a foreign EMI serving Canadian counterparties must register as a money services business with FINTRAC and, for retail payment activities, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by OSFI, with settlement-risk and safeguarding assessments effective 2024. Foreign EMIs cannot clear directly through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS or ACSS settlement systems without sponsorship by a Schedule I chartered bank, and their Canadian-dollar balances are contractual claims against the EMI's pool rather than federal deposit-insurance protected deposits. The overseas-PR operating model may use Wise Enterprise, Airwallex, or similar for small overseas retainers and low-value vendor payments in exotic currencies, but share capital, statutory reserves, and tax remittances must be held at a tier-1 Canadian bank to satisfy REQ, FINTRAC, and creditor-protection expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections or shareholder advances from an overseas parent should be routed as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers to the bank BIC and the company account, with the beneficiary name matching the registered corporate name, a narrative field identifying the transaction as share-capital subscription or intercompany loan, the corporate-resolution reference approving the capitalisation, the ISO 20022 purpose and function code accepted by the receiving bank for capital movements or intercompany financing, and UBO identifiers consistent with the bank's KYC record. Canada requires no exchange-control certificate or import licence for inward investment; the receiving bank must file a FINTRAC large-value wire report for movements of CAD 10,000 or more, and injection amounts above CAD 100,000 will normally trigger a bank credit-side request for the notarised resolution, updated REQ beneficial-ownership register, and a capitalisation table. Funds may settle in the designated deposit currency or be converted at the firm's selected spot or forward rate; no prior central-bank approval is required.Outbound settlements are cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system and Automated Clearing Settlement System for intra-Canada Canadian-dollar transfers, using the three-digit institution number, five-digit transit branch, and account number; through SWIFT MT103 wires to foreign beneficiaries via the bank's correspondent network, with charge allocation in MT103 field 71A (OUR/BEN/SHA) and USD flows clearing through New York CHIPS/Fedwire, GBP through London CHAPS, and EUR through TARGET2; and through commercial letters of credit or documentary collections where media-production, event-logistics, or large-vendor contracts exceed CAD 50,000 and documentary risk control is desired. The treasury blueprint should include a USD concentration account at a New York correspondent and a EUR concentration account in London or Frankfurt when recurring media disbursements justify them, and recurring Canadian subcontractor retainers should be paid over Payments Canada EFT/ACH. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, and only as an ancillary collection and payout rail rather than the primary treasury anchor. Canada does not issue a standalone electronic-money-institution licence comparable to the UK regime; a foreign EMI serving Canadian counterparties must register as a money services business with FINTRAC and, for retail payment activities, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by OSFI, with settlement-risk and safeguarding assessments effective 2024. Foreign EMIs cannot clear directly through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS or ACSS settlement systems without sponsorship by a Schedule I chartered bank, and their Canadian-dollar balances are contractual claims against the EMI's pool rather than federal deposit-insurance protected deposits. The overseas-PR operating model may use Wise Enterprise, Airwallex, or similar for small overseas retainers and low-value vendor payments in exotic currencies, but share capital, statutory reserves, and tax remittances must be held at a tier-1 Canadian bank to satisfy REQ, FINTRAC, and creditor-protection expectations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (15% federal Part I + 11.5% Quebec provincial), applicable to a non-CCPC Business Corporation with a Quebec permanent establishment; CCPC rate on first CAD 500,000 active business income is 14.8% combined.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on gross dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and CRA Form T106 filing; large MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million are additionally subject to OECD Country-by-Country reporting, and CRA will expect Local and Master File documentation where audit risk exists.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) comparable to offshore financial centres, but substance is central to tax compliance: the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245), statutory interpretation, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test in tax treaties all require genuine commercial substance. For an overseas PR communication firm, economic substance is proved by Canadian office premises, employees, client contracts, invoices, management decisions, and real business risk; a pure shell with no operational functions will be recharacterized, lose treaty benefits, and be open to severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239 and the Quebec Taxation Act: fine of up to 200% of any tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA section 163 of 50% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized or acquiesced to false returns face joint and several liability for corporate tax and unpaid withholdings under ITA sections 227.1 and 227.5; criminal conviction can impose travel bans, restitution orders, personal asset freezing, and incarceration.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may recalculate transactions to the arm's-length amount under ITA subsection 247(2); a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment applies under ITA subsection 247(3), plus interest; documentation failure removes the reasonable-effort defense.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length intercompany contracts without support may be liable for the 10% TP penalty as a result of causing the false return; in asset-stripping cases, the corporate veil may be pierced and directors exposed to civil recovery plus criminal charges if intent to evade is shown.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule reassessment under ITA section 245: deductions denied, income recharacterized, interest charged, and an avoidance penalty equal to 25% of the tax benefit may be imposed in the case of wilful participation; professional-level misconduct also attracts monetary penalties under the Excise and Income Tax rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish and operate a conduit shell with no substance can face personal civil liability for the resulting tax debt and penalties through fraudulent conveyance law; criminal tax evasion charges may also lead to travel bans and imprisonment.
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code section 462.31 (money laundering) carries imprisonment up to 10 years and unlimited criminal fines; PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for a reporting entity and CAD 100,000 per violation for individuals; civil forfeiture under Quebec's law applies to proceeds of unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or participate in laundering are treated as principals; personal assets are subject to forfeiture, assets may be frozen under Mutual Legal Assistance, and serious designated offences can trigger a travel ban via an Interpol red notice.
