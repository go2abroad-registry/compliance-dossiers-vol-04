Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; self-service portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT registration and filing via ESTV’s SUISA/MWST portal and e-service suite.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not resident in Switzerland that makes taxable supplies in Switzerland or imports goods subject to Swiss VAT must register as a taxable person. The foreign principal must apply to the ESTV by written or electronic form and, unless exempted, appoint a fiscal representative resident in Switzerland for VAT purposes. Application requires proof of foreign registration, business purpose, expected turnover, and representative mandate. The ESTV assigns a Swiss VAT ID (MWST-Nr/IDE) after verification. Prior corporate income tax registration is not triggered solely by passive inventory distribution unless a Swiss permanent establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. An Aktiengesellschaft is, by default, a fully taxable legal entity subject to federal, cantonal, and communal profit tax on worldwide income with unilateral credit, subject to Swiss territorial limitations for foreign permanent establishments and real estate. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by the Swiss general anti-abuse rules and the 2020 Multilateral Instrument; no elective hybrid mismatch regime exists. Group taxation is limited to the Swiss tax unity concept (Tragfähigkeitsprinzip) under specific cantonal/ordinary federal rules, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (CHF 100,000 principal threshold; reduced thresholds for charities and certain nonprofit supplies); foreign suppliers may have mandatory registration from first taxable supply in Switzerland if not covered by threshold relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of goods qualifies for the 0% Swiss VAT rate if the goods are physically exported to a foreign destination by the supplier or a third party on behalf of the supplier, and the export is covered by customs documentation. Supply of services is zero-rated where the recipient is located abroad in accordance with Articles 23(2)/24 Swiss VAT Act, including services physically performed abroad, supplies of rights and immaterial goods to foreign recipients, and ancillary services to exports. Passive inventory distribution of electronic components qualifies if title risk passes abroad and the goods leave Swiss customs territory.

  • Swiss export declaration/export customs declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration) or certified alternative proof of customs transit
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport from Switzerland
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract referencing ISIC 4652 passive component supplies
  • Evidence of receipt by foreign recipient (delivery confirmation, import acceptance, or forwarding agent certificate)
  • Inward remittance certificate or payment evidence only where tax administration requires proof of consideration; Swiss practice permits bank transfer records

Swiss VAT does not require real-time government clearance or digital stamping. Taxable persons must issue invoices containing prescribed mandatory particulars; QR-bill (Swiss QR-IBC) and e-invoice formats satisfy documentation requirements. Since 2024, structured electronic invoicing is accepted but not obligatory unless parties agree. VAT returns are filed electronically through ESTV’s secure platform; no continuous transaction controls are in force. Import VAT is collected via the Swiss customs system and may be declared in the periodic VAT return under the import VAT settlement procedure.

Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating is treated as tax evasion or attempted tax evasion under Articles 96-99 Swiss VAT Act. Interest at 4-5% per annum applies retroactively from the filing date, and penalties may reach up to three times the evaded tax; aggravated cases may be referred to criminal courts. Failure to substantiate export documentation within the statutory retention period (10 years) leads to reassessment at the standard 8.1% rate with reclaimable input tax denied, and late filing fines and administrative sanctions can additionally apply. ESTV audits export substantiation files routinely, including third-party customs data, transport tracking, and payment flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully convertible, freely floating currency regime with no legal distinction between current and capital account transactions. There are no licensing, approval, or prior-authorisation requirements for cross-border fund transfers, dividend remittances, loan principal payments, or repatriation of invested capital. The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully deliverable in both spot and forward markets. The only practical hurdles in profit remittance are fiscal in nature rather than exchange-control based, namely the Swiss dividend withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) at a standard rate of 35%, reduced or refundable under applicable double taxation treaties and subject to the rules on substantial participation, and the absence of any requirement to obtain a commercial bank or SNB permission. Creditor protection rules under the Swiss Code of Obligations (e.g., capital preservation for distributions out of paid-in capital under Articles 671 and 675 OR) apply independently of currency transfer legality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender regime in Switzerland. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into CHF, have no mandatory timeline for repatriation, and may freely retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts at Swiss banks or abroad. The only binding obligations are statistical: enterprises with material cross-border assets or liabilities must report to the Swiss National Bank under the SNB survey framework, and all cross-border payment instructions routed through Swiss banks are captured by the banks' mandatory reporting responsibilities to the SNB. No surrender ratio, waiting period, or conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank; statutory basis: Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank (National Bank Act, NBG, SR 951.1) together with the SNB Survey Ordinance and related statistical reporting regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury relationship, Medium as an ancillary payments channel. An offshore digital EMI or electronic money institution that is not licensed or recognised by FINMA may not lawfully take deposits or maintain a resident Swiss transactional account; deposit-taking from Swiss persons triggers Banking Act licensing or the FinTech licence under Article 1b of the Banking Act (deposits capped at CHF 100 million). Cross-border EMIs (e.g., UK or EU licensed providers) may be used for virtual-IBAN receivable collection, expense cards, or payout aggregation if the commercial risk is accepted, but all final CHF clearing and corporate liquidity must be held and settled with a FINMA-licensed bank connected to the SIC/euroSIC system. Legal constraints include the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA), the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), and the requirement that any payment service offered to Swiss customers be operationally anchored in a Swiss-regulated institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are made by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) in CHF, EUR, or USD to the CH_AG's account at a SIC participant. The payment instruction shall carry an explicit purpose reference (Zahlungszweck), for example 'Kapitalerhöhung' (capital increase), 'Agio' (share premium), or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan) to support accounting treatment and, if applicable, the capital escrow attestation. No SNB permit or exchange-control approval is required. Inbound funds are credited immediately once the bank validates the beneficiary name and account. The board resolution, notarial deed for the share-capital increase (where registered capital is increased), and, if applicable, the shareholder loan agreement must be retained. Where the aggregate external assets and liabilities of the enterprise exceed the SNB statistical thresholds, the receiving bank will automatically forward the transaction to the SNB for balance-of-payments and external-asset statistics; no separate filing is required by the customer.Outbound trade funding for inventory purchases of capacitors and resistors should be executed through (i) direct SWIFT MT103 transfers for open-account settlement, with a payment purpose code describing 'purchase of electronic components' and full beneficiary bank detail; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600, with the Swiss bank acting as issuing or confirming bank, particularly when dealing with Asian and emerging-market suppliers; and (iii) SEPA credit transfers in EUR for counterparties located in the European Economic Area and participating SEPA countries. Domestic Swiss payments are cleared through the SIC system or QR-bill payments. All international transactions run through SWIFT with the bank performing sanctions screening against SECO, FINMA, and international (OFAC/UN) lists. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., UK or EU licensed providers) may be used for virtual-IBAN receivable collection, expense cards, or payout aggregation if the commercial risk is accepted, but all final CHF clearing and corporate liquidity must be held and settled with a FINMA-licensed bank connected to the SIC/euroSIC system. Legal constraints include the Swiss Financial Institutions Act (FinIA), the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), and the requirement that any payment service offered to Swiss customers be operationally anchored in a Swiss-regulated institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File apply to Swiss group members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CHF 900 million or more, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2020 (ESTV TP documentation circular of 2021); below that threshold no standing documentation is mandatory, but the taxpayer must be able to produce transfer pricing documentation, benchmarking studies and intercompany contracts within 30 days of a tax audit request; a de minimis safe harbour exists for annual controlled transactions below CHF 10 million

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a rigorous economic-substance doctrine rather than a standalone ESR registration system; under TRAF 2020 and the ESTV royalty/substance doctrine, the Swiss AG must maintain a real physical office, qualified operational staff, independent management discretion, and actual risk-bearing functions in Switzerland to claim treaty benefits, patent-box relief, notional interest deductions, or to avoid reclassification as a shell holding vehicle; pure pass-through or nominee structures are rebutted, treaty relief is denied retroactively, and hidden profit distribution adjustments apply

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax plus a penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded amount (DBG Art. 175); default interest at 4.5% p.a.; criminal fine up to CHF 30,000; in aggravated cases custodial sentence up to 3 years under DBG Art. 186

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally liable as principals or accomplices; the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution of the evaded tax, personal criminal records are imposed, non-resident directors may face preventive arrest or exit restrictions during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment is possible in severe cases
Hidden Profit Distribution (Verdeckte Gewinnausschuettung)

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the excess as a dividend: additional tax assessment, 35% withholding tax on the constructive dividend with interest, and a negligence or evasion penalty of up to 3 times the additional tax; the hidden distribution is not deductible at either federal or cantonal level

Director personal liability: Under Art. 12 VStG the board of directors is jointly and severally liable with the company for the withheld but unpaid 35% tax unless each director proves absence of fault; the company may claim restitution against the directors under company law, and a criminal conviction triggers personal asset seizure and potential bankruptcy proceedings against director estates
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief with retroactive full 35% withholding on all distributions; reassessment of income with imputed notional profit; penalties of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax for taxpayers; civil fines up to CHF 500,000 under anti-money-laundering rules for concealed beneficial ownership

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal liability under the Swiss Code of Obligations for organizational deficits, disqualification from board service, criminal liability for negligent supervision under Art. 102 of the Swiss Criminal Code, and freeze or seizure of personal bank accounts if treaty abuse is held to be intentional fraud
Withholding Tax Non-Payment (Verrechnungssteuer)

Statutory sanction: The company must pay the full 35% from its own funds plus a 5% administrative surcharge and interest at 4.5% p.a.; failure to pay triggers criminal proceedings with fines up to 3 times the unpaid tax and potential imprisonment of the responsible signatory in aggravated cases

Director personal liability: Board members are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under Art. 12 VStG; civil execution may reach directors' personal assets, and criminal default sentences may include travel bans, house arrest, or imprisonment; directors who knowingly ordered the non-payment face criminal conviction as co-principals
