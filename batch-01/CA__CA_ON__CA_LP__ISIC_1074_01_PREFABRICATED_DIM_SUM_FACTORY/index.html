Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for federal GST/HST and Income Tax Act matters; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs and export reporting; Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario Part II limited partnership tax and Employer Health Tax. Principal portals: CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); Ontario Business Registry and ON-tario Tax portal at ontario.ca.

E-government filing portal

L.16, through the Ontario Business Registry, naming a general partner with a registered head office address in Ontario. Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN), Canada's business tax identifier, via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; a non-resident LP must disclose legal name, foreign registration details, Ontario permanent establishment address, and a Canadian contact or agent for service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — but subject to material statutory carve-outs. For federal Income Tax Act purposes the LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96 and pays no entity-level Part I tax; income is computed at partnership level and allocated to partners. For GST/HST, the ETA s.272(1) deems the partnership itself to be a separate person and the LP is the registrant. For Ontario Taxation Act, 2007 Part II, the LP is mandatorily deemed to be an individual resident in Ontario and must file a Part II limited partnership return; this tax is imposed on the LP rather than its partners. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income allocated through the LP unless the ITA s.194/195 election path applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; classification is statutory and mandatory. Available elections relevant to this structure: (i) ITA s.195 election — non-resident limited partners may elect to file a net-basis Part I return for Canadian-source income instead of the flat 15% withholding on gross allocations imposed under ITA s.194; (ii) ETA s.273 branch election — treat the Ontario manufacturing division as if it were a separate person for GST/HST registration and filing; (iii) ETA voluntary registration election — a small supplier may elect to register before the threshold is hit to recover input tax credits on pre-production capital costs; (iv) ETA s.227 simplified accounting method is available but generally not advisable for an export-oriented manufacturer that will claim substantial input tax credits; (v) no election exists to avoid or modify the Ontario Part II limited partnership tax; (vi) no customs election exists — export reporting obligations under the Customs Act are unconditional.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (other than financial services and sales of goodwill) measured both in any single calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies from the day the limit is surpassed. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold. A non-resident LP carrying on commercial activity through the Ontario factory is subject to the same threshold, and once registered the LP remains a registrant for at least 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when the goods are (a) shipped by the supplier or a common carrier to a destination outside Canada, (b) actually exported within a commercially reasonable period — CRA audit practice generally expects documentation within 60 days of the supply, and (c) supported by 'evidence satisfactory to the Minister' under ETA s.23. Frozen steamed dim sum and dumplings qualify for 0% on export even though sales of prepared/heated food in Ontario would be taxable at 13%; exports of food products from the factory are zero-rated as goods regardless of the basic-grocery rules. Since the LP supplies through a permanent establishment in Ontario, the zero-rating claim must be documented at the CRA and CBSA level. Canada has no foreign-exchange repatriation controls or statutory deadline; the 180-day value in the mandatory_fx_repatriation_deadline_days field is retained solely as the schema default and is not a legal requirement under Canadian law.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) statistical export declaration (legacy Form B13A/G13) for goods valued above CAD $2,000 consigned to destinations other than the United States; exports to the US generally require no statistical declaration unless the goods are controlled.
  • Commercial Invoice / Canada Customs Invoice — full commercial invoice showing HS tariff classification (typically HS 1902 stuffed pasta goods or HS 1602/1604 for meat- or seafood-based dim sum), Incoterms 2020 delivery terms, quantities, unit values, and the consignee's foreign address.
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air cargo) — transport document evidencing international movement of the goods to the foreign buyer or consignee.
  • Packing List and Container Manifest — supporting the commercial invoice with itemized carton/container details and seal numbers for containerized frozen exports.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Proof of Payment — bank SWIFT credit advice, TT wire confirmation or letter-of-credit settlement record; a bank-issued IRC is not a statutory CBSA document but is routinely used to evidence the commercial reality of the export sale.
  • Proof of Export Evidence File — internal file compiling the above plus any carrier certificate or freight forwarder certification, maintained in Canada for CRA audit inspection under ETA s.286.

Canada does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) or real-time fiscal clearance regime; there is no digital stamping or tax-authority blockchain validation of individual invoices. The pan-Canadian e-invoice framework is voluntary and aligned with the Peppol PINT/BIS standard, used mainly for business-to-government transactions. The CRA accepts electronic invoices in EDI and XML formats and requires GST/HST returns to be filed electronically, with mandatory electronic remittance for larger registrants (annual gross taxable revenue above CAD $1 million) via GST/HST NETFILE. Invoices must satisfy ETA s.223 content rules (vendor and recipient identity, description, consideration, tax amount) and books and records must be retained and produced to the CRA on audit under ETA s.286.

The CRA can reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period under ETA s.298 and, in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default, at any time. Where the CRA is not satisfied with the exporter's evidence, zero-rating is clawed back and the supply is reassessed at the full Ontario 13% HST, with compound daily prescribed interest from the original reporting period. Civil penalties: ETA s.285.1(1) imposes 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (minimum CAD $250) for knowingly making false statements or omissions; ETA s.285.1(2) imposes 50% for gross negligence. Criminal prosecution for evasion under ETA s.327 carries, on summary conviction, a fine up to CAD $25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months and, on indictment, a fine up to CAD $100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Audits of frozen-food exporters focus on 'phantom exports' and circular trade, requiring a clean chain of documentary evidence from factory production records, CFIA/SFCR export licences, refrigerated container tracking, B/L matching, and bank settlement — domestic deliveries to trading intermediaries claimed as exports are the highest clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Canada maintains no exchange controls, no approval requirements for capital movements, and no restriction on profit or dividend remittance. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely and is quoted on global FX markets. Canada has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; capital injections, loan repayments, and repatriation of LP profits are not subject to foreign-exchange licensing. Only statistical and tax reporting obligations apply, including Statistics Canada direct-investment surveys and Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) information returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. The Ontario LP may retain, convert, or disburse USD, EUR, CNY, or other export revenue freely. Frozen-dim-sum exporters are subject only to standard FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for cross-border remittances of CAD 10,000 or more and to Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting thresholds; no Central Bank approval is required for maintaining foreign-currency operating accounts at a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-rate and monetary policy authority. Prudential conduct of banks is supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for cross-border transaction surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Foreign digital payment institutions and offshore EMIs are not authorized deposit-taking entities in Canada under the Bank Act; they cannot open a domestic chequing/escrow account in the LP's name or access the Lynn high-value clearing system. Such entities are restricted to operating as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) for ancillary cross-border transmission of funds, and their services cannot replace a Schedule I or Schedule II bank account for capital contributions, letters of credit, or XOF/CAD trade settlements. Secondary use is acceptable only where the digital EMI holds a Canadian MSB registration and maintains segregated settlement accounts at a Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: inbound SWIFT MT103 from each limited partner to the LP's designated Capital Escrow Account at the escrow bank; SWIFT purpose code TRF (Transfer) with narrative 'Limited Partnership Capital Contribution'; CAD settlement cleared through Lynn (Canada's high-value payment system) for CAD-denominated legs; verification pack includes executed partnership agreement, capital-account ledger, ISC register, source-of-funds declarations, and bank reference; for USD-denominated injections, clear via the US correspondent through CHIPS/Fedwire; FINTRAC reporting triggers at CAD 10,000 or equivalent per transaction; Statistics Canada direct-investment surveys must be completed when non-resident equity exceeds the statutory threshold. Capital escrow release occurs only upon issuance of the CFIA licence and municipal permits.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic-transfer payments to raw-ingredient and packaging suppliers; (ii) UCP 600-compliant Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (usance or sight) for capital equipment imports such as quick-freeze tunnel freezers and dumpling-forming machinery; (iii) wire disbursements via the bank's correspondent network for CNY/USD corridors routed through a U.S. clearing bank or Chinese renminbi settlement bank. Payments are executed through the lead bank's treasury desk with spot FX conversion; cash concentration is managed via zero-balance account structures and a multi-currency pooling notional overlay for CAD, USD, and CNY.Vanilla deliverable FX Forward Contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/CNY, CAD/EUR) with tenors up to 24 months executed under an ISDA/Canadian Master Agreement with a Schedule I bankFX Swap Contracts (swap of near-leg and far-leg FX forwards for rolling hedging coverage)Cross-Currency Swaps (CAD/USD interest-rate and principal exchange) for long-term financing of the factory build-outNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for CAD-denominated exposures because the CAD is fully convertible and an onshore deliverable forward market exists; OSFI-regulated banks do not offer customer NDFs against CAD as primary hedge instrumentsPlain-vanilla European FX options (CAD/USD and CAD/CNY) for premium-limited risk management

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Foreign digital payment institutions and offshore EMIs are not authorized deposit-taking entities in Canada under the Bank Act; they cannot open a domestic chequing/escrow account in the LP's name or access the Lynn high-value clearing system. Such entities are restricted to operating as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) for ancillary cross-border transmission of funds, and their services cannot replace a Schedule I or Schedule II bank account for capital contributions, letters of credit, or XOF/CAD trade settlements. Secondary use is acceptable only where the digital EMI holds a Canadian MSB registration and maintains segregated settlement accounts at a Canadian bank.
