Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Ottawa (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); in Quebec, Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) administers both GST/HST and QST/TVQ for businesses established in Quebec; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export controls.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act and obtain the NEQ from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec. Step 2: Register for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or, for Quebec businesses, through Revenu Québec's ClicSÉQUR single-window system. Step 3: Activate program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and the Quebec QST/TVQ file. Step 4: Open a CBSA importer/exporter account for plant machinery and export shipments. Step 5: Non-resident ownership is permitted with no minimum capital; Canadian directors are required under CBCA/QBCA. Electronic filing of GST/HST/QST returns is mandatory for registrants above the CAD 1.5 million threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; a business corporation is always taxed at the entity level. Available elections: (i) short first taxation year-end election under ITA s. 249(3); (ii) functional-currency (USD) reporting election under ITA s. 261; (iii) GST/HST annual-filing and Quick Method elections under ETA ss. 238 and 227 for eligible small suppliers; (iv) Canada's OECD hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) operate mechanically without an elective classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold tested over the current or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies; registration for GST/HST and QST becomes mandatory once exceeded. Exporters of zero-rated goods are strongly advised to register voluntarily even below the threshold in order to recover input tax credits on plant construction, UHT packaging equipment, and raw materials.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of UHT aseptic oat milk qualify for 0% GST/QST under Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act (mirrored in Quebec's QST statute) when: (1) the supplier sells and ships goods to a non-resident purchaser at an address outside Canada; (2) the goods are exported no later than 60 days after consideration becomes due or is paid without having become due; (3) the non-resident purchaser is not registered for GST/HST at the time of delivery; (4) no supply or use occurs in Canada before export. Domestic sales of plant-based beverages may also be zero-rated as basic groceries under Part III, Schedule VI, but export treatment is the operative regime for this UHT plant.

  • Customs export declaration (B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System, or the CAD 2,000 exemption record for US-bound shipments)
  • Bill of lading or air waybill consigned to the overseas non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice (HS 2202.99, NAICS 311999) stating INCOTERMS and final export destination
  • Inward remittance certificate / Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (bank SWIFT MT103) evidencing repatriation of export sale proceeds
  • Packing list, terminal receipts, and forwarding instructions proving physical departure from Canada
  • Evidence of non-residency of the purchaser, such as foreign incorporation certificate or tax residence proof

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital-invoice validation regime exists in Canada or Quebec. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) interoperable with CRA systems. Revenu Québec does not operate pre-clearance digital stamping; audit reliance is placed on commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and bank remittance records rather than government pre-approval of invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec will deny the 0% rate if the documentation bundle is incomplete; the full 14.975% combined GST/QST is reassessed on gross sale consideration plus interest from the original due date. Statutory penalties include: ETA s. 285.1 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax); s. 280 gross negligence penalty (25% of net tax); and s. 327 tax-evasion prosecution with fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. All associated input tax credits are clawed back retroactively. The normal reassessment window is 4 years, extended indefinitely where misrepresentation was attributable to carelessness, neglect, or fraud. Canada imposes no codified 180-day FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects trade-finance norms and transfer-pricing prudence, and unrepatriated intercompany export receivables remain under ITA s. 247 arm's-length scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-float regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals, no registration requirements, and no statutory restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, management-fee, or capital remittance. Non-resident profit repatriation is governed solely by Part XIII withholding tax: 25% statutory, typically reduced to 5% for direct corporate dividends under the Canada-United States Tax Treaty and similar bilateral conventions. The Investment Canada Act requires a net-benefit review for direct acquisitions of Canadian businesses above the C$1.3 billion WTO-investor threshold and a separate national-security review for sensitive sectors; the inbound equity capitalisation of a new Quebec oat-milk manufacturing subsidiary should file the standard ICA notification and confirm that review thresholds are not triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Canada. There is no mandatory conversion timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export earnings to a domestic account; exporters may retain 100% of received proceeds in USD/CAD foreign-currency accounts with a Schedule I domestic bank. Applicable obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers exceeding C$10,000 and large-cash transaction reporting, plus accurate SWIFT trade narrative describing the underlying export sale of UHT plant-based beverages and material inputs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision and Payments Canada for clearing-system administration; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority equivalent to a SAFE-style regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Canada is not an EU-style EMI passporting jurisdiction; non-bank payment providers must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and may lawfully deliver cross-border payments, FX conversion, and virtual prepaid access without a banking licence, but deposit-taking is prohibited under the Bank Act. Fintechs such as Wise and Payoneer are viable for low-value collections, payroll, and vendor disbursements, yet they cannot support documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, or capital escrow mechanics. The core operating account and any escrow/injection account must therefore sit at a Tier-1 Schedule I bank, with fintech rails used only as auxiliary disbursement channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent's bank to the Quebec branch of RBC or NBC using the beneficiary account's three-digit institution number, five-digit transit number, and account number, with the beneficiary BIC quoted in the wire instruction. For shareholder loans, the narrative must read Intercompany Shareholder Loan - Arm's Length Terms and be accompanied by a signed loan agreement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million gross revenue for mandatory Master File and Local File; CAD 1 million in cross-border transactions (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services, interest, royalties) for T106 reporting

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but requires economic substance for treaty benefits and to avoid the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR). The CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial substance, and the courts have upheld that treaty benefits require a genuine business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty) plus interest; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit source deductions, or if they signed false returns. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment are possible for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties under GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) of 25% of the tax benefit. Interest and penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax benefit denied if they were involved in the scheme, and may face civil penalties and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties up to CAD 2 million for administrative violations; criminal penalties include fines and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to comply, and may face imprisonment and fines. Travel bans may be imposed during investigations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation is inadequate), plus interest. For large corporations, penalties can be substantial.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing, and may face civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty.
