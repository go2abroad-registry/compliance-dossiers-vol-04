Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online; for Quebec-specific taxes (QST, provincial income tax deductions), Revenu Québec / Services Québec online portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada/Quebec must apply for a Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC348. The BN serves as the GST/HST registration number for the partnership itself; in Quebec, separate registration for QST is effected through Revenu Québec, generally using the same BN and a distinct TVQ account. Non-resident suppliers are required to register once their taxable supplies in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate over the preceding four consecutive quarters. For the Quebec QST, the equivalent threshold applies. The LP is a separate 'person' for GST/QST purposes but is not a taxpayer for income tax purposes; each partner must report its allocable share on its own Canadian tax return, and non-resident partners may need a CRA tax account for instalment remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is characterized by law and remains fiscally transparent for Income Tax Act purposes. No entity-level classification election is available for a Canadian LP. For cross-border hybrid mismatch scenarios, the Income Tax Act hybrid entity rules (ss. 18.1 and 18.2) apply de jure to disallow double deductions or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes, and no advance election is needed for these anti-avoidance provisions to operate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000: GST/HST registration is mandatory for any person (including non-resident suppliers and online sellers) making taxable supplies in Canada above this threshold in either a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Quebec imposes a parallel C$30,000 threshold for QST registration. For non-resident e-commerce operators, a simplified GST/HST and QST registration regime applies to taxable supply categories such as remote goods supply and digital services where Canadian-source sales exceed C$30,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of tangible personal property supplied to a non-resident and shipped by or on behalf of the supplier from Canada to an outside-Canada destination are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, of the Excise Tax Act, provided prescribed documentary evidence is retained and the goods leave Canada within statutory timelines. Services: supplies of services to a non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, if the services are not in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada and are not reasonably expected to be received by a person in Canada. For Quebec QST, analogous zero-rating provisions apply. In a beauty DTC e-commerce context, each export sale must be supported by ordered goods, shipping documentation, and proof that risk and possession passed outside Canada.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or CAED / G7 export report)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading, airway waybill, or Canada Post/courier waybill for low-value shipments
  • Proof of export (carrier attestation, customs export permit, eManifest receipt, or port stamp)
  • Bank transfer/remittance record or payment processing statement evidencing receipt of foreign consideration and FX repatriation

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed GST/HST/QST information; however, those invoices are not pre-validated by the CRA or Revenu Québec. Electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns is mandatory for large registrants and available to all registrants through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE/API and Revenu Québec's E-services, but no government fiscal invoice approval API is involved.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal reassessment period of four years, extended to seven years for misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default. If export substantiation is inadequate or fraudulent, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale, exposing the LP to assessment of 5% GST and 9.5% QST (14.975% combined) plus interest, failure-to-file penalties, and, for gross negligence, a penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for willful tax evasion or false statements may result in fines up to C$10,000 on summary conviction or up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment, in addition to full reassessments and retrospective zero-rating clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating, and no prior exchange approval is required for profit remittance, loan repayments, intercompany settlement, or capital distributions to non-residents. For a Quebec limited partnership, the sole legally relevant hurdles are tax reporting and withholding consequences, not currency regulation: non-resident partners allocated Canadian-source business income may be taxable under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Quebec Taxation Act, with treaty relief available under an applicable tax treaty. Contemporaneous transfer pricing, partnership allocation schedules, and substance documentation are required to avoid reassessment under anti-avoidance rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no minimum holding-period requirement for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into CAD through a Canadian Authorized Foreign Exchange Dealer. Reporting obligations are limited to Canada Border Services Agency export declarations for goods, FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reports on monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, and routine income tax and provincial sales tax compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. Financial supervision is exercised by OSFI for banks and by FINTRAC for currency-flow reporting and money-services-business compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for non-bank fintech EMI payment facilitation, but low suitability as the primary or sole CAD clearing account. A foreign EMI not licensed as a bank under the Bank Act (Canada) has no direct membership in Payments Canada and cannot clear or settle CAD-denominated transactions on its own. An overseas EMI may operate in Canada only through a FINTRAC-registered money-services-business, with settlement ultimately occurring through a Canadian chartered bank. Such EMIs are acceptable as a secondary disbursement rail, provided the main treasury liquidity account remains at a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Canada and Quebec do not impose a domestic FX purpose code, but the originating jurisdiction may require a purpose code: for such jurisdictions, use the category denoting direct investment or capital contribution, and add the remittance narrative 'Capital contribution to a Canadian limited partnership.' The receiving bank will perform beneficiary scrutiny, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds checks; supporting documentation should be delivered in advance, including the executed partnership subscription agreement, capital call notice, shareholder or partner resolution, source-of-wealth evidence, and the LP's bank-account opening package. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is imposed on a Quebec limited partnership by the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises or the Civil Code of Quebec solely because of size or revenue. An audit becomes compulsory when triggered by: (i) the limited partnership agreement or unanimous-partner covenants, (ii) a lender, investor or franchisor requiring audited statements, (iii) the LP being a reporting issuer or otherwise publicly accountable, or (iv) an order of a court or regulator. Market practice: an annual audit is prudently introduced when gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000, when third-party debt is obtained, or when a corporate general partner's governance rules require it

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Quebec (11.5%) general corporate income tax rate; a Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level tax — income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level (the 26.5% rate applies to a corporate general partner resident in Quebec).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act, applied to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; distributions by a Limited Partnership to a non-resident limited partner are not legally dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding — they are taxed as allocable Canadian business income on the non-resident partner's own return.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed no later than the last day of the sixth month after the partnership fiscal period end; TP-600 must be filed within three months after the fiscal year-end if all partners are Canadian resident individuals, otherwise within six months.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate taxation year applies — both T2 and CO-17 are due six months after the corporate partner's fiscal year-end; taxation year of the corporate partner does not necessarily coincide with the LP fiscal period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 — Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of cross-border transactions with related non-resident persons exceeds CAD 250,000 in a taxation year; comprehensive OECD-compliant Local File and Master File documentation is expected under CRA administrative policy when aggregate related-party transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 and the consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, and Quebec's parallel anti-avoidance provisions, permit reassessment and denial of tax advantages where transactions lack genuine economic substance and are carried out primarily to avoid tax. Additionally, Quebec civil law provides that a limited partner who takes part in the management or control of the Limited Partnership loses limited liability and is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years; Quebec Tax Administration Act: additional civil penalties of up to CAD 100,000 plus prosecution costs and compound interest on unreported liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner face criminal prosecution and are personally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions and GST/QST under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; civil asset seizure, travel bans, and restrictions on leaving Canada may be imposed through Revenu Québec enforcement measures, and fraudulent conveyance of partnership assets can pierce the corporate veil.
Shell Company and Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of interposed entity treatment under the GAAR; reassessment of profits to the Canadian beneficial owner; accuracy-related penalties of up to 25% of the misstated amount under section 163(2); Quebec administrative penalties of up to CAD 50,000 for false statements or omissions.

Director personal liability: Limited partners found to have exercised control or management authority are stripped of limited liability under the Quebec Civil Code and become jointly and severally liable for all partnership obligations; directors of a foreign corporate general partner may be held personally liable for Canadian taxes arising from a sham structure, with extended civil restitution and, in criminal cases, extradition exposure where a tax treaty permits.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and T106 Omission

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under section 247(2) to the arm's-length price; penalty under section 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where documentation is absent or inadequate; interest and arrears running from the original assessment date; Quebec may apply an equivalent reassessment and penalty regime aligned with the federal adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize underpricing or who grossly negligently fail to obtain proper documentation may be named in a transfer pricing penalty assessment; if the partnership or corporate partner defaults, directors can be liable for unremitted withholdings, and director residency restrictions may be ordered by the CRA where a taxpayer's principal residence is outside Canada.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for failure to maintain a compliance program, report suspicious transactions, or keep required records; criminal penalties are also available, including fines and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner may be subject to personal penalties where they failed to implement reasonable compliance measures, including personal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment; the CRA and Revenu Québec may also levy civil penalties against directors who knowingly fail to report taxable revenues underlying the payment flows.
