Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV / FTA), Division for VAT and Excise Duties; portal https://www.estv.admin.ch; UID portal https://www.uid.admin.ch; VAT e-services via https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/vat

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Aktiengesellschaft with the Cantonal Commercial Register, including articles of association, capital confirmation and board resolutions; 2) The Commercial Register automatically grants the legal entity a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / ID économique / identificatore d'impresa), which serves as the federal TIN; 3) For VAT, file form 035 'Registration for VAT' with ESTV or use the ESTV e-service after commercial registration, attaching the statutes, commercial register excerpt and projected turnover; 4) ESTV issues a separate VAT number in the format 'CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST' for taxable entities; 5) The UID is used for income tax, federal and cantonal levies, social security and statistics.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Register the Aktiengesellschaft with the Cantonal Commercial Register, including articles of association, capital confirmation and board resolutions; 2) The Commercial Register automatically grants the legal entity a UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / ID économique / identificatore d'impresa), which serves as the federal TIN; 3) For VAT, file form 035 'Registration for VAT' with ESTV or use the ESTV e-service after commercial registration, attaching the statutes, commercial register excerpt and projected turnover; 4) ESTV issues a separate VAT number in the format 'CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST' for taxable entities; 5) The UID is used for income tax, federal and cantonal levies, social security and statistics.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective entity classification exists in Switzerland; taxation follows the statutory legal form. An Aktiengesellschaft is automatically subject to corporate income tax at federal, cantonal and communal level and to VAT at federal level. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Switzerland implements BEPS Action 2 through domestic anti-hybrid rules and its OECD MLI positions, without elective deduction or classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies made in Switzerland (zero-rated exports count as taxable turnover for the threshold); voluntary registration from the first franc is available, particularly to recover input VAT. Foreign suppliers without a Swiss registered office must register if they generate at least CHF 100,000 from supplies to Swiss customers and do not benefit from an exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Switzerland to a foreign destination qualify for the zero rate under Art. 23 VAT Act when the removal from Swiss customs territory is proven by a customs export declaration or equivalent electronic customs release. Services are zero-rated under Arts. 23 and 24 VAT Act when the place of supply is outside Switzerland, particularly services provided to a foreign recipient in the course of the recipient's business, or services directly connected to exported goods or goods located abroad. For cosmetics e-commerce, export consignments must leave Switzerland via customs-approved parcels and be traceable to the foreign recipient. Swiss law does not set a statutory FX repatriation deadline; administrative practice for export documentation completion is benchmarked at 180 days.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / déclaration d'exportation) with customs stamp or electronic customs release record
  • Commercial export invoice prepared in accordance with Swiss VAT invoice requirements
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or equivalent transport document showing consignee abroad
  • Retail sales or delivery record for each export consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Foreign recipient's VAT ID or commercial register extract for offshore B2B services

Switzerland does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoice API clearance, or digital stamping regime. Invoices, whether paper or electronic, must contain the statutory content under Art. 26 VAT Act: supplier and recipient identification, type and volume of goods or services, date of supply, consideration, tax rate and tax amount. Tax-relevant electronic records must be archived and made available to ESTV for ten years. The QR-bill and eBill standards are common but not compulsory.

ESTV and cantonal tax authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims for completeness and authenticity of documentary evidence. If proof of export is absent or insufficient, the supply is retrospectively taxed at 8.1% with default interest from the original due date. Deliberate misuse, false declarations or forged documentation constitutes VAT evasion under Art. 97 VAT Act, punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded and, in serious cases, criminal penalties including imprisonment. The limitation period for concealed VAT violations is ten years, and any reassessment of exported supplies also triggers reversal of related input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittances, dividends, interest, or capital repatriation. Dividend distributions to foreign shareholders are subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35%, which may be reduced or refunded under applicable double tax treaties. Cross-border payments are freely effected through regulated banking channels and may be captured in SNB statistical payment reporting, but require no prior regulatory approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export proceeds surrender or conversion obligation. Export receipts may be retained freely in any currency, held offshore, or converted as desired. Mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; no statutory surrender timeline exists. The only passive requirement is statistical reporting of cross-border payments by the resident bank to the SNB for monetary statistics purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — the sole monetary authority; banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight delegated to FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority); no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — offshore digital EMIs may serve only as ancillary payment gateways; they cannot legally execute CHFin clearing or hold customer deposits without a Swiss banking license. Under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act, a fintech license allows FINMA-compliant custodial deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million without full banking licence conditions. An unregulated offshore EMI must route client funds through a licensed Swiss correspondent bank and cannot clear SIC transactions directly. For Shopify/PayPal-based retail, an offshore EMI may collect card proceeds abroad, but all CHF treasury must ultimately be settled via a licensed Swiss bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate profit tax: 8.5% on after-tax profit (7.83% on pre-tax profit). Combined federal and cantonal/communal headline rate at registered office ranges from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to 21.0% (highest cantons); typical CH_AG for online cosmetics retail seated in Zug or Zurich faces an aggregate rate of 11.9% to 18.2%. Input metadata flags has_sub_tax_distinction=false, but Switzerland de facto imposes bi-level taxation: federal direct tax plus cantonal/communal profit and capital tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends paid by the Swiss AG; reduced to 0% for distributions to Swiss-resident qualifying corporate holders with at least 10% participation and no Swiss permanent establishment issue; for DTA-covered foreign shareholders, the 35% is withheld and subsequently refunded by the ESTV to the treaty rate (typically 15% portfolio, 5% for qualifying holdings of 10% or more depending on the treaty; 0% for certain EU/DTA cases).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the fiscal year-end per Art. 126 DBG and cantonal implementing law; for calendar-year AGs the cantonal deadline is typically 30 September of the following year; extensions are granted on request if audited financial statements are pending.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax on dividends distributed must be declared and remitted at 35% of gross distributions within 30 days after the end of the relevant calendar quarter; maximum extension to 60 days upon prior application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland follows the OECD three-tier documentation standard: Country-by-Country Report is required where the ultimate parent's consolidated revenue reaches CHF 900 million; the ESTV expects a Master File and Local File for Swiss taxpayers with annual turnover of at least CHF 80 million or with intra-group cross-border transactions exceeding CHF 10 million; a simplified Local File (functional and risk analysis) is advisable for small and medium e-commerce AGs with recurring cross-border related-party purchases. Statutory codification of documentation duties is currently under federal legislative process with projected entry into force in 2025; current basis is Art. 58 DBG, Art. 57 DBG, the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, and ESTV circular (Kreisschreiben Nr. 4) on transfer pricing.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the sense of the GCC or BVI regimes, but substance is enforced through (i) the tax-residence test under Art. 50 DBG, which attributes corporate residence to the Austrian? otherwise to the place of effective management in Switzerland; (ii) FTA substance checks applied when issuing tax-residence certificates and processing DTA refunds; (iii) the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT), which is applied in Swiss tax treaties and denies treaty benefits to letterbox companies; and (iv) case law confirming that an AG without employees, premises, and local decision-making may be treated as a sham for direct tax and withholding tax purposes. For the ISIC 4791 online cosmetics retail AG, demonstrable Swiss substance includes a physical office or fulfillment base, at least one resident director supervising sales operations, and the exercise of management and control in Switzerland for all purchasing, marketing, pricing, and order-processing decisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Direct Federal and Cantonal Corporate Taxes

Statutory sanction: The evaded tax is due with interest at 4.5% per annum; a fine of one-third up to one and a half times the evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG is imposed; qualified evasion based on forged or falsified documents (Art. 176 DBG) carries imprisonment of up to three years; cantonal law applies parallel surcharges and fines.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable under Art. 754 CO for the corporate tax deficit where the company is insolvent and the loss results from a breach of their duty of care; criminal convictions bar the director from holding further board positions; in aggravated cross-border cases, Swiss and foreign mutual legal assistance may trigger surrender proceedings, international travel alerts, and precautionary seizures of personal bank accounts.
Withholding Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The 35% tax is assessed on the gross amount with default interest; evasion penalties reach five times the tax evaded (Art. 70 VStG); repeated or high-value evasion is a crime with imprisonment of up to five years under Art. 71 VStG; the FTA may also withdraw the AG's eligibility for simplified payment procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors are answerable to the ESTV for the unimpeded 35% tax when the corporate payer is unable to pay and the default is attributable to their omission; willful personal concealment triggers criminal liability and the potential freezing of personal Swiss bank accounts; foreign travel bans are routinely ordered while the criminal investigation is pending.
Unpaid Employer Social Security Contributions (AHV/IV/EO/ALV and BVG)

Statutory sanction: The outstanding AHV/IV/EO/ALV contributions are recovered with arrears interest; additional administrative surcharges apply; criminal sanctions under Art. 87 AHVG include monetary penalties of up to CHF 30,000 and, in cases of fraudulent conduct, deprivation of liberty; the company may also be debarred from public procurement and lose its tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Under Art. 12 AHVG the directors and the liquidator are personally liable for unpaid employer social security contributions, even after liquidation; personal assets of board members can be executed directly; flight risk or asset disposal abroad leads to Swiss travel bans, arrest warrants, and extradition based on social security fraud.
Shell Company Structure and Abuse of Treaty Benefits (PPT/LOB)

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities re-attribute the profits to the place of effective management and assess federal, cantonal, and communal tax at the full combined rate with default interest; treaty benefits are denied under the PPT, causing the 35% Swiss withholding tax to remain in full; hidden distributions reclassified from the excessive intercompany payments suffer the 35% WHT plus penalties; the company loses access to cantonal privileged regimes and NID.

Director personal liability: Directors face qualified tax-evasion prosecution for the artificially created tax advantage, including imprisonment up to three years; personal restitution claims from the tax authorities and minority shareholders under Art. 754 CO can strip board members of personal assets; the Federal Tax Authority may initiate criminal proceedings with travel restrictions and, in severe cases, issue an international arrest warrant through Interpol for the principal director.
