Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC); federal portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html). Quebec counterpart: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca), administering the Quebec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) and Quebec corporate income tax under the Taxation Act (Loi sur les impôts).

E-government filing portal

A foreign business corporation engaged in e-commerce in Quebec must obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1/RC143; non-residents use Form RC413. The BN is the national TIN. The corporation must also register with Revenu Québec through 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' using the enterprise's NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to open a QST account; an import/export account may be added if goods are imported for Canadian fulfilment. Non-resident applicants must submit certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, a Canadian fiscal representative appointment where required, and evidence of Quebec nexus (place of business, resident agent, or warehouse/fulfilment centre).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; a business corporation is always taxed at the entity level. Available elections: functional currency election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 261; GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156; QST joint election under s. 196.1 of the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax (deemed nil consideration for intercompany supplies); and non-resident rental income election under ITA s. 216. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2–18.4 address BEPS Action 2 structures and are not elective, though taxpayers may make timing adjustments through structured arrangement disclaimers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual taxable-supplies threshold for both GST/HST and QST (small-supplier rule). Once worldwide taxable supplies to Canadian persons exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter, foreign e-commerce vendors must register for GST/HST; once Quebec taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, registration for QST is required. Combined Quebec rate is 14.975% (GST 5% + QST 9.975%). Non-resident vendors with goods delivered from Canadian fulfilment centres or sold through online platforms are deemed to carry on business in Canada for GST/HST purposes and must register before the threshold is exceeded. Registration is prospective and idempotent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V and the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax (ss. 100 ff.) applies where goods are exported by the supplier in the ordinary course of business, title passes to a non-resident recipient, and shipment is made to a destination outside Canada. For e-commerce pet-product exports, each sale must be made to a non-resident person, physically exported via common carrier, and supported with contemporaneous export documentation. Services supplied to non-residents qualify where performed wholly outside Canada or substantially related to foreign property. Intangible supplies electronically delivered to non-residents are zero-rated as well. Claiming 0% GST/HST/QST requires the vendor to hold reasonable evidence of export at or before the time of filing the return for the reporting period.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CBSA-approved low-value export entry, or carrier-reported electronic export information for courier consignments
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Canada Post/courier label showing Canadian origin and foreign destination address
  • Commercial invoice (HS classification, quantity, unit price in CAD, Incoterms, country of destination, seller and buyer identification)
  • Carrier proof of delivery or customs clearance certificate confirming arrival in the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/MT103 credit advice, or merchant-platform payout statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Export-warehouse and order-fulfilment documentation (picking slips, courier manifests, and import-export statistics reports where applicable)

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, government invoicing stamping, or publication-service API clearance regime. CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10,000; Revenu Québec mandates electronic filing and remittance for GST/HST/QST above prescribed thresholds. Commercial e-invoices must be retained in machine-readable format (XML or PDF) for 7 years. Since 2024, payment service providers including Stripe and PayPal are subject to ETA Part V.1 registration and annual reporting obligations regarding foreign merchants. Online marketplace operators are deemed to make taxable supplies and must collect tax at source when vendors sell through a marketplace such as Amazon. No pre-clearance stamping or government invoice validation applies in Quebec.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/HST and QST within 4 years of an assessment, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Improper zero-rating triggers reassessment at 14.975% combined, daily compound prescribed interest, and penalties: ETA s. 285 failure-to-remit penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 10% of net tax), ETA s. 280 gross-negligence false-reporting penalty (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax unreported), and the mirror QST penalty provisions under the Act respecting the Quebec Sales Tax. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating may result in fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Documentary insufficiency, split shipments to domestic intermediate addresses, or absence of evidence of foreign-currency proceeds within 180 days will trigger clawback of zero-rating and recharacterisation of the supply as taxable domestic; Revenu Québec applies analogous administrative fines and may exclude habitual non-compliant exporters from simplified reporting programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The federal Exchange Control era has been repealed, so no government authorization is required for the inflow or outflow of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, shareholder loans, or e-commerce sales revenues. The Quebec corporation may hold CAD, USD, and other foreign-currency accounts at a Canadian chartered bank. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject only to standard withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and relevant double-tax agreements, not to FX control. No stamp duty, exchange permit, or pre-approval applies. The only practical hurdle is bank-level due diligence demonstrating that funds are legitimate and not routed through sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender foreign currency to the central bank or to any government agency, nor does it set a mandatory timeline or minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. A Quebec pet-supplies retailer may benefit from foreign-exchange gains and retain foreign currency in trade accounts; however, the Income Tax Act requires that inventory purchases and revenues be reported in Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for tax filings. Exports of pet products are not subject to special surrender or conversion formalities, but all cross-border receipts must be documented for Canadian customs lodgment and sales-tax (GST/HST/QST) reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential oversight, FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision, and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for Quebec financial-services conduct.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Quebec-incorporated entity receiving parent-company equity or shareholder loan from abroad, the compliant rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Canadian bank account. The beneficiary account is the corporate Operational Account or a designated Capital Escrow sub-account. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must identify the economic nature, e.g., 'Share capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan' to permit accurate book/tax characterization. Canadian banks do not use official exchange-control purpose codes, but the bank will require supporting documents on file: board resolution, share subscription agreement, loan agreement, and proof of source of funds. The bank will verify that the remitting financial institution is not on any sanctions list, and if the incoming currency is not CAD, the bank executes the conversion at its prevailing commercial exchange rate. For sums above CAD 10,000, the bank must file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) with FINTRAC, and in cases of large or unusual transactions, it may request the Beneficial Ownership Register and source-of-wealth evidence.Routine operating disbursements, including supplier payments, warehousing fees, and refunds, are cleared through the Canadian payment system: the high-value Lynx system for SWIFT MT103 and domestic CAD wire; the retail Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for EFT and bill payments; and card-network rails for Stripe/PayPal disbursements. For international trade settlements with overseas vendors of pet products, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) with the invoice reference in the remittance field. For larger purchases (typically above CAD 50,000 total risk) where the vendor requires a bank undertaking, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) drawn under UCP 600 is permissible, opened by the Canadian bank in favour of the supplier. The LC route guarantees payment against compliant shipping documents (commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and certificate of origin).
