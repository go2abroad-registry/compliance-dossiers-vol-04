Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); principal digital portals: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and 'Business Registration Online' (BRO). Provincial: Ontario Ministry of Finance; Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement, and the Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry) manages corporate registrations. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export reporting for indirect-tax substantiation.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act through Corporations Canada (or under the Ontario Business Corporations Act through the Ontario Business Registry), or register as an extra-provincial corporation if incorporated elsewhere. 2) Obtain the federal CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit identifier, and any required program accounts via BRO, by telephone, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Key program accounts for a SPAC: RC for corporate income tax (T2) and RT for GST/HST; RM is added only if importing/exporting. 3) The Ontario corporate tax account is issued automatically when the T2 return includes the Ontario portion; no separate Ontario tax ID is needed for corporate income tax. 4) For a non-resident corporation: appoint a Canadian address for service or an authorized representative (Form RC59 Business Consent), and provide identity documents for directors and ultimate beneficial owners; compliance with the Ontario Business Registry extra-provincial licence is a precondition to provincial registration. 5) Expected timelines: online incorporation in 1-3 business days; BN issued immediately upon online application (paper Form RC1 generally processed in 5-10 business days). 6) Registration for GST/HST is mandatory only if taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or if the entity is a 'financial institution' under Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 149(1); a SPAC should obtain an RT account only if it makes taxable supplies, although voluntary registration may be appropriate to recover input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident (Income Tax Act section 250(4)) and is mandatorily taxed at the entity level under Part I. Available elections relevant to a SPAC: ETA section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration; ETA section 211 and related elections for financial institutions; Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident corporations to file a return on Canadian-source rental income (rare for a SPAC); and the informational associated-corporation allocation election under Income Tax Act section 256(3) (not applicable to a public SPAC). Canada's hybrid mismatch and anti-arbitrage rules (enacted in the 2021-2022 budgets) generally deny deductions for certain hybrid arrangements and do not provide elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148(1): a SPAC must register once the total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding supplies of financial services, capital real property, and goodwill). Financial institutions, including certain 'investment plans', are required to register regardless of this threshold. Interest earned in a SPAC trust or escrow account is generally exempt financial service income, so no GST/HST is collected and input tax credits are correspondingly denied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, supplies of goods exported, and services supplied to non-residents (if not relating to Canadian real property, not for goods located in Canada at the time the service is performed, and not excluded personal services), may be zero-rated. A SPAC's typical income streams, namely dividends, interest on escrowed funds, and fees for arranging acquisitions, are exempt financial services rather than zero-rated supplies; therefore zero-rating and input tax credit recovery are not generally available. If the SPAC provides a taxable management or consulting service to a non-resident target, zero-rating requires documented evidence that (a) the recipient is a non-resident, (b) the service is performed entirely outside Canada or relates to property located outside Canada, and (c) the supply is not a 'financial service' within ETA section 123(1).

  • CBSA export declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / RMD or ACI eManifest export data) when tangible goods cross the border
  • Commercial invoice with exporter and consignee identification, Incoterms, value, and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (ocean or air transport document) evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Inward remittance records: SWIFT MT103 wire transfer confirmations and bank statements evidencing payment by the non-resident recipient (Canada does not issue a statutory Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Written supply agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, place of performance, and non-Canadian property
  • GST/HST invoice (if issued) containing the supplier's BN, date, description, zero-rated indicator, and consideration

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate applies in Canada or Ontario. CRA requires invoices to contain prescribed information (Input Tax Credit Information Regulations section 3: supplier name, BN, date, description, consideration, and GST/HST amount or rate). The federal government is phasing in a mandate for suppliers to federal departments and agencies to submit electronic invoices through the pan-Canadian PEPPOL network (EN 16931-compliant; phase-in from 2024), but this does not create a CRA clearance requirement. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA (GST/HST NETFILE/TELEFILM); Ontario has no separate HST filing interface.

CRA can reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (ETA section 298; Income Tax Act section 152(4)), which is unlimited for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Where zero-rating is unsupported, the CRA will assess unremitted HST at 13%, disallow input tax credits, and charge prescribed arrears interest (basic rate plus 4 percentage points, compounded daily). Penalties include: ETA section 280 (failure to file - 1% of unremitted net tax plus 0.25% per full month up to 12 months); ETA section 285(1) (failure to register - greater of CAD 250 and 1% of taxable consideration); and ETA section 285.1 (false statements knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence - greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount in question). The 180-day repatriation benchmark reflected in this dossier is a compliance best practice; Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating, but supporting records should be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains an independently floating CAD exchange-rate regime and has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement: the CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No exchange control legislation impedes cross-border payments, profit remittance, or dividend distribution; the sole frictions are Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (base 25%, treaty-reduced), FINTRAC reportable thresholds, and Investment Canada Act net-benefit review where foreign control of a Canadian business is acquired. A SPAC's IPO trust monies and de-SPAC merger consideration are freely transferable in CAD or USD without Bank of Canada or OSFI prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable: Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Remaining reporting duties are: FINTRAC mandatory electronic-funds-transfer reports for CAD 10,000+ (with 24-hour aggregation), CBSA physical currency declaration for CAD 10,000+ crossings, and Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada international-transaction surveys. SPAC IPO proceeds are capital inflows, not export proceeds, and carry no CAD conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank and monetary authority; prudential regulation of chartered banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and financial intelligence rest with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); legal tender and currency issuance are governed by the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration agency in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW. Canada does not license EU/UK-style electronic money institutions; the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, registers payment service providers but confers no deposit-taking or trust-safeguarding authority. TSX/NEO SPAC listing conditions require underwriter IPO proceeds to be deposited into a segregated escrow with a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank or a licensed trust company; an offshore digital EMI cannot act as escrow depositary, and using one for trust capital would breach OSC prospectus undertakings and trigger FINTRAC suspicion. An EMI-style account may support only insignificant OPEX wires.
