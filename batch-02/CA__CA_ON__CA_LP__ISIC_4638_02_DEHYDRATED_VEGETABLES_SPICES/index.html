Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); official business portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership that is carrying on business in Ontario or makes taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before activating tax program accounts. The application is submitted through Business Registration Online (BRO) or directly to the CRA Non-Resident Income Tax Section. Once the BN is issued with a 9-digit base number, the partnership activates the GST/HST 'RT' program account if its worldwide annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or if voluntary registration is elected. An Ontario provincial account may be added only for employer health tax or other provincial statutory obligations. The same BN is referenced on GST/HST returns, customs export documents (B13A), and non-resident income tax filings. Foreign partnerships should provide a Canadian representative or authorized agent with a completed 'Authority for a Representative' to prepare and file returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box election; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes unless the partnership itself carries on business as a deemed person for GST/HST in limited circumstances. Election mechanisms relevant to LPs include: (i) the Income Tax Act s. 97(2) election to transfer eligible property to a partnership on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) the Excise Tax Act s. 273 election between closely related persons to treat supplies and acquisitions as non-taxable; (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election for non-resident partnerships under the small-supplier provision; and (iv) hybrid mismatch elections or determinations under Income Tax Act ss. 12.6 and 18.6 with respect to deduction denial for foreign hybrid instruments. No 'partnership check-the-box' election analogous to IRS Form 8832 is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods, including bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper, qualify for the GST/HST zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier sells and ships the tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada, or supplies it to a non-resident purchaser who immediately exports it, and the supplier retains evidence of export. The goods must be exported 'as soon as reasonably possible' after delivery to the carrier. Storage or trans-shipment within Canada before export may continue to qualify if the exporter controls the goods and the export actually takes place. Directly exported goods must be supported contemporaneously with transport documents; the exporter must not have knowledge that the goods will be diverted to the Canadian market.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canada Border Services Agency export reporting where goods exceed CAD 2,000 or are controlled)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual physical export from Canada
  • Commercial Invoice evidencing sale to a non-resident purchaser and terms of sale (e.g., FOB, CIF)
  • Packing List describing dehydrated vegetables and spice commodity codes
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT wire confirmation proving payment settlement from the foreign buyer

Canada does not impose a national real-time fiscal clearance e-invoicing mandate, nor does Ontario require digital stamping or continuous transaction control through government APIs. GST/HST registrants must issue commercial invoices containing the prescribed information under Excise Tax Act sections 221 to 223, including supplier BN, purchaser’s name, date, description of goods, tax amounts, and zero-rated indication. Electronic invoices in PDF, PEPPOL BIS, or EDI 810 formats are accepted voluntarily. Returns and schedules may be transmitted electronically through CRA’s GST/HST NETFILE or XML upload; there is no CRA-operated transactional clearance gateway.

The CRA may reassess a purported zero-rated export as taxable where the documentary evidence is insufficient, causing the registrant to remit uncollected HST at 13% (5% federal + 8% Ontario component) plus prescribed interest. False export claims or deliberate misstatements to obtain refunds or avoid tax attract civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including up to 25% of the amount assessed, and gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act if refund claims flow through to partner returns. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or omission. Criminal prosecution may be initiated under the Criminal Code for fraudulent evasion of tax, including fines and imprisonment. The CRA audited export zero-rating claims ordinarily require retention of all export, freight, customs, and settlement records for at least six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully free-floating and convertible currency on both current and capital account. No exchange control legislation exists, and there are no statutory requirements for prior approval on capital inflows/outflows or profit remittances. Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII. Profit remittance from a Canadian Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is subject to Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25% absent treaty relief) and may require a Canadian Revenue Agency T2062 form for dispositions of partnership interests. No conversion hurdle or proof-of-origin restriction applies beyond general anti-money-laundering reporting and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion to CAD for export proceeds of a Canadian entity. Exporters are free to retain or deploy foreign currency. However, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any incoming international electronic funds transfer (IEFT) of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported by the receiving financial institution to FINTRAC. Export documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs export declaration in the Canadian Export Reporting System) must be retained for six years. No timeline or ratio is mandated for converting export receipts, but the Income Tax Act requires all revenue to be reported in Canadian dollars at the prevailing rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and clearing-house supervisor; foreign exchange monitoring and compliance administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility medium-high for treasury efficiency but not a replacement for an OSFI-regulated chartered bank account. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and PCMLTFA, any non-bank payment service provider operating in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed or registered in Canada cannot lawfully provide CAD-denominated accounts or clearing services to resident businesses. Canadian-regulated platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or dedicated FX platforms such as Corpay, Bloomberg FXGO) are permissible for cross-border receipts/payments and offer reduced FX spreads, but they must be linked to a primary commercial bank account for capital base and clearing issuance.

Offshore digital EMIs not licensed or registered in Canada cannot lawfully provide CAD-denominated accounts or clearing services to resident businesses. Canadian-regulated platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or dedicated FX platforms such as Corpay, Bloomberg FXGO) are permissible for cross-border receipts/payments and offer reduced FX spreads, but they must be linked to a primary commercial bank account for capital base and clearing issuance.
