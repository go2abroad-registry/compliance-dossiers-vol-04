Competent corporate registry

Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug (Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug), Switzerland; electronic filing through the federal EasyGov / commercial register entry portal; official publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt, SHAB).

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Nominal share capital minimum CHF 100,000 (OR Art. 621). At the moment of registration at least 20% of the nominal capital, and in all cases not less than CHF 50,000, must be paid in cash (OR Art. 632 para. 2). The funds must be deposited on a Swiss bank account in the exact future company name and must remain irrevocably at the free disposal of the board of directors, confirmed by the bank's blocking confirmation (Sperrbestätigung). The shares may be issued as registered shares; a capital band (Kapitalband, OR Art. 653s et seq.) may be adopted later.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV / AFC), Bern, Switzerland; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; business registration portal: www.easygov.swiss; VAT registration and reporting portal: SuisseTax (FTA); cantonal tax administrations handle direct income/capital tax assessments and issue cantonal tax numbers.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) obtain statutory tax identification through the Swiss Business Identifier (UID) system. Step 1: register the AG in the Commercial Register of the canton of registration (Handelsregisteramt); the UID-CH number is assigned automatically and serves as the general tax ID. Step 2: apply for VAT registration with the FTA via SuisseTax if the entity performs taxable activities in Switzerland or exceeds the turnover threshold; non-established foreign supply chain entities must appoint a Swiss VAT tax representative. Step 3: register with the relevant cantonal tax administration for direct tax; the canton issues a cantonal tax number for corporate income/capital tax purposes. A separate 'TIN' does not exist; the UID-CH doubles as the tax identification number for VAT and customs purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Aktiengesellschaft, AG, is a per se taxable legal entity subject to Swiss federal, cantonal and communal corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident entities are taxed only on Swiss-source income; no pass-through treatment is available; territorial source limitations apply to foreign activities through a permanent establishment or real estate).

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a 'check-the-box' entity classification election; classification is determined by legal form. Available statutory elections include: Swiss VAT group election for closely linked independent taxable persons (Art. 21 VAT Act); participation relief/deduction election for substantial shareholdings (qualified participation exemption) under Art. 69 Federal Direct Tax Act and cantonal participation regimes; patent box treatment election under Swiss Tax Reform Act; capital contribution relief election for tax-neutral return of paid-in capital; no hybrid mismatch check-the-box election exists under Swiss domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies (excluding VAT-exempt turnover; Art. 19 and Art. 20 Swiss VAT Act). Cantonal and communal direct tax registration applies regardless of turnover once the AG is incorporated in the Commercial Register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is claimable for export of tangible goods if the supplier transfers ownership to a recipient abroad, the goods are physically transported out of Switzerland to a foreign destination, and the supplier holds absolute export evidence; the place-of-supply rules determine the zero-rated treatment of cross-border services, including Swiss gaming anti-cheat middleware and software-related services supplied to foreign recipients whose benefit is located abroad; services directly related to exported goods or supplies of offshore services to foreign customers also qualify under Art. 23(4) Swiss VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / Zollanmeldung, validated by Swiss customs)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Switzerland
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment / reinsurance of consideration from foreign counterparty
  • Commercial Invoice

Switzerland has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no e-invoicing statutory clearance gateway, and no digital stamp regime. E-invoicing is voluntary; acceptable Swiss electronic formats include Swiss eBill (SIX), QR-bill, EDIFACT, and PEPPOL BIS. VAT taxpayers must issue ordinary invoices/credit notes containing the legally required data fields; records of invoices and export substantiation must be retained for 10 years.

The FTA and Swiss customs cross-check export declarations, e-dec data, transport documents, bank inflows and counterparty ledgers. If export zero-rating is applied without legally valid proof, the output VAT must be re-assessed, the input VAT deduction on export purchases becomes recoverable only through a separate credit procedure, and the FTA may impose default interest under Art. 87 VAT Act and an administrative fine of up to four times the evaded tax for wilful tax evasion under Art. 60 VAT Act; criminal penalties for tax fraud also apply. Retrospective clawback limitation is 10 years, and serious audit findings can trigger extended fiscal records reviews and mutual assistance with foreign tax authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account, with no general foreign-exchange approval, filing, conversion or rationing requirements. The Swiss franc is freely convertible under both current-account and capital-account transactions, and Swiss-based corporate treasuries may hold, convert, repatriate or disburse CHF, USD and EUR without central-bank authorisation. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are likewise free, subject only to Swiss federal withholding tax at 35% on dividend payments, which may be reduced or refunded under an applicable double-tax treaty. The SNB no longer operates a minimum exchange-rate regime; the CHF free float has been in force since 15 January 2015. Treasury hedging and cross-border liquidity are therefore governed by ordinary banking, AML/KYC, tax disclosure and financial-markets rules rather than exchange-control permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts, licence fees, subscription revenue or software disbursements. A Swiss AG may lawfully retain receivables in foreign currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical and tax-aggregate disclosures, principally the SNB’s external-assets and balance-of-payments data collections and the ordinary Swiss corporate accounting rules. No SNB or FINMA consent is required for an anti-cheat gaming middleware company to receive export revenue or to hold dollar and euro balances in its operational accounts.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank) is the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange reserve manager. Banking, anti-money-laundering and market-conduct supervision is delegated to the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). International financial and investment-policy competence relevant to cross-border capital flows rests with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment-collection channel only; Low as a substitute for a tier-1 Swiss bank relationship. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI or e-money institution without a Swiss banking licence cannot hold CHF-denominated corporate deposits or engage in deposit-taking activities from Switzerland. Under the Swiss Banking Act, any entity taking public deposits from a Swiss treasury is required to hold a FINMA banking licence or a FINMA fintech licence (Art. 1b Banking Act). The Swiss fintech licence permits public deposits of up to CHF 100 million provided no interest is paid on the deposits, but it does not permit broader credit intermediation or fully equivalent commercial banking. A non-Swiss digital EMI may be used only as a foreign-currency collection node through a FINMA-regulated partner bank, and funds must be routed onward into the Swiss AG’s principal UBS or cantonal-bank account. For the Swiss AG’s core capital escrow and treasury clearing account, a licensed tier-1 Swiss bank remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection into the Swiss AG’s capital escrow or reserve-capital account should be executed by SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA corridors and by SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR remittances. Where the transfer originates from a subsidiary, shareholder or parent holding company, the remittance information field must reference the capital-increase resolution, the Swiss commercial-register number (CHE-xxx), and the designation ‘Paid-in capital / Agio’ to permit the receiving bank to classify the funds clearly. The wire should be routed through the AG’s principal tier-1 Swiss bank, and the bank’s KYC department must receive simultaneous delivery of the notarised capital-increase deed, shareholder register excerpt, source-of-funds documentation and Board minutes. Since Switzerland has no exchange-control filing requirement, no central-bank permit is required; the operational requirement is purely bank AML/KYC verification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal profit tax (direct federal tax); combined effective federal, cantonal and communal rate typically 11.5% to 21.0% depending on the canton of residence, with Zug at approximately 11.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% gross Swiss federal withholding tax on dividend distributions by a Swiss AG; full refund available to qualifying Swiss residents and partial or zero-rate relief available under an applicable double taxation agreement (DTA) for non-resident beneficial owners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return is due within six months after the end of the fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers the ordinary deadline is generally 30 June of the following year unless a formal extension (maximum one year) is granted by the cantonal tax administration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss TP documentation obligations apply in line with the Swiss Transfer Pricing Documentation Ordinance; mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation is generally required where cross-border controlled related-party transactions exceed CHF 1,000,000 in a tax period and a Master File is ordinarily required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CHF 50,000,000; the ESTV may nevertheless require documentation for smaller material transactions where an arm's-length issue is identified.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but Swiss tax law and DTA anti-abuse practice require genuine economic substance, beneficial ownership, and business purpose; a Swiss AG holding anti-cheat IP must demonstrate actual staff, premises, decision-making, technical development, and commercial risk management to secure treaty relief and avoid treaty-shopping recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax plus default interest; under Article 175 of the Swiss Direct Federal Tax Act (DBG) the fine may amount to the evaded tax; under Article 186 DBG, tax fraud through falsified documents carries imprisonment up to five years and fines; cantonal tax laws impose equivalent penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign false returns or knowingly participate in evasion are personally prosecuted; the corporate veil does not shield them from criminal conviction, civil restitution to the tax authority under Article 41 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, asset seizure, travel bans where flight risk exists, and imprisonment.
