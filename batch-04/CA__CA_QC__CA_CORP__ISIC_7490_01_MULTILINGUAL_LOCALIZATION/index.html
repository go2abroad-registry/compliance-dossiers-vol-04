Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (federal GST/HST, payroll, and income tax accounts); Revenu Québec Clic Revenu portal (Quebec Sales Tax registration, Quebec corporate income tax, and employer accounts).

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-controlled or resident business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act must register with CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN), then establish a separate GST/HST account (RT), payroll account (RP), and corporate income tax account (RC). A non-resident entity engaging in commercial activity in Canada may register under ETA s. 240 and Regulation 301. For Quebec QST, registration is made separately with Revenu Québec using the BN-based QST account. Registration is completed online via CRA Business Registration Online and Revenu Québec, which allows electronic signature, authentication of directors' personal identifiers, and immediate issuance of tax account numbers. BC/QC corporate income tax registration is integrated with incorporation filings and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec must be notified within statutory timelines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law; a business corporation is a statutory taxpayer subject to Part I federal tax and Quebec corporate tax under the Taxation Act (Quebec). Available elections include: s. 216 ETA election for non-resident claimants, s. 256 amalgamation and winding-up elections, Quebec QST election for closely related corporations, and ITA s. 216(1) election respecting Canadian-source non-business income. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are restricted by OECD BEPS Action 2 rules incorporated into Canadian tax treaties and the general anti-avoidance rule, with no elective classification mechanism.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
14.975%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240(1); mandatory QST registration under the Quebec Sales Tax Act (L.Q. 1991, c. D-9.1) is aligned with the same CAD 30,000 threshold when the activity is carried out in Quebec. Registration below the threshold is voluntary but requires a minimum two-year commitment.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Services exported by a multilingual localization agency qualify for 0% GST/QST where: (1) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST/QST, or was outside Canada during the course of receiving the service; (2) the service is performed wholly outside Canada; or (3) the service is a supply of professional translation, localization, dubbing, and linguistic services supplied to non-resident publishers or media platforms under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 (services relating to intangible property) and the QSTA corresponding provision (QSTA Annexe 6, Partie V). Zero-rating does not exempt the supplier from input tax credit entitlements; full input tax credits are claimable on business inputs attributable to zero-rated export supplies. Documentation must demonstrate the non-resident status of the customer and the offshore place of enjoyment or delivery.

Canada and Quebec do not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or digital stamping mandate. Invoicing must comply with ETA s. 223 and QSTA invoicing requirements, including bilingual content in Quebec (French language predominance under the Charter of the French Language). CRA accepts electronic invoices subject to integrity controls; Revenu Québec requires disclosure of QST separately for intermediate consumers. A localization agency billing B2B clients in Canada must retain invoices, agreements, delivery evidence, and exchange of electronic records to substantiate place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 142.1 and QSTA Titre I, Chapter IV.

      
Claiming zero-rating without substantiation exposes the filer to reassessment under ETA s. 296 and QSTA enforcement provisions, interest on unremitted tax at prescribed rates, and penalties: (i) 5% of the net tax amount plus 50% of interest for late remittance per ETA s. 280/281; (ii) penalty for false statements or omissions of CAD 500 or 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, whichever is greater, under ETA s. 285.1 and Quebec equivalent; (iii) criminal prosecution for knowing misrepresentation carrying fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment under ETA s. 327; (iv) retrospective clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on export inputs if zero-rating is denied. CRA's International and Large Business Directorate audits zero-rated service exporters with heightened scrutiny on transfer pricing, intercompany localization services, and chargeable offshore delivery evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on payment of dividends or repatriation of capital, and no prior approval requirements for inbound or outbound investment. Profit remittances by a Quebec corporation to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted, subject to normal corporate tax obligations and the requirement to provide appropriate tax residency documentation (e.g., NR4 reporting, Part XIII withholding tax compliance). All transactions must be reported to FINTRAC only for anti-money laundering surveillance thresholds (electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more), not as a convertibility limitation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. There are no surrender rules, and export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, held in foreign-denominated bank accounts in Canada or abroad, or converted at the commercial discretion of the entity. Reporting obligations arise solely under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for specified monetary transactions over CAD 10,000, including the filing of a Large Cash Transaction Report or Electronic Funds Transfer Report, as applicable, to FINTRAC.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the regulatory framework administered in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility Rating: High for supplemental settlement, Low as a primary treasury rail. Wise B2B and Payoneer operate in Canada as regulated Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, but they are not chartered banks and cannot provide deposit insurance (CDIC) or full treasury custody. Legal constraints: (i) such EMIs may not hold Canadian-dollar settlement balances on behalf of a corporation as a core banking relationship; (ii) their funds are typically pooled in omnibus accounts with third-party banks, exposing the corporation to counterparty and clearing risk; (iii) ability to issue Canadian-dollar bank drafts or commercial letters of credit is non-existent. Recommended use is for receiving client payments and making small vendor disbursements in multiple currencies, but all official treasury accounts, capital escrow, and material cross-border settlements must be held with a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Newly incorporated small business with non-Canadian directors and no historical financial statements
        
Root cause: Newly incorporated small business with non-Canadian directors and no historical financial statements

        
Defense evidence: Provide (i) certificate of incorporation (federal or Quebec), (ii) current corporate the articles and bylaws, (iii) Registraire des entreprises attestation (Quebec), (iv) passport copies and proof of residential address for each director, (v) a detailed business plan describing the multilingual localization services, expected client jurisdictions, and forecast transaction volumes, and (vi) a copy of the trust or corporate registry search from the home jurisdiction of any 25%+ shareholder.

      

      
      

        High transaction velocity with multiple foreign counterparties (typical of localization agencies)
        
Root cause: High transaction velocity with multiple foreign counterparties (typical of localization agencies)

        
Defense evidence: Maintain and present a treasury policy (segmenting trade payments to vendors vs. employee payments), a customer due diligence (CDD) file for material clients, a list of beneficial owners, and a FATCA/CRS self-certification (Form W-8BEN-E for US-source counterparties or equivalent). Include a narrative describing the localization supply chain and a sample invoicing contract to evidence commercial substance.

      

      
      

        Use of digital EMI rails (Wise B2B / Payoneer) as primary receiving accounts
        
Root cause: Use of digital EMI rails (Wise B2B / Payoneer) as primary receiving accounts

        
Defense evidence: Demonstrate that the EMI accounts are merely collection accelerators, with funds swept daily to the tier-1 Canadian bank. Provide signed agreements with the EMI providers, a transaction-flow diagram, and a confirmation from the EMI that the account is held in the corporation's name and subject to FINTRAC registration. Banks will require that all client invoices reference the Canadian bank as the official settlement account.

      

      
      

        Direct disbursement to high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions for outsource subcontracting (automatic subtitling or dubbing houses)
        
Root cause: Direct disbursement to high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions for outsource subcontracting (automatic subtitling or dubbing houses)

        
Defense evidence: Implement an OFAC/UN/EU/Canada sanctions screening procedure and provide evidence of a sanctions-screening engine covering all counterparties. For sanctioned or embargoed jurisdictions, produce a legitimate-services legal opinion and obtain Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy / Global Affairs Canada permit where required. Maintain a dual-control payment approval matrix.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant inbound capital injection for the Quebec corporation is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent/funder bank to the Canadian clearing account (transit number + institution number + account number, or via the correspondent banking network). For capital contributions, use purpose code 6010 (Investment in Canada — capital contribution to a Canadian entity) in the optional purpose code field; for intercompany loans, use 6020 (Intercompany loan). The receiving Canadian bank will verify the source of funds against the corporate register of directors and shareholders. For injections exceeding CAD 2,000,000, the transaction may be reported to the Bank of Canada under the balance-of-payments surveillance, but no prior authorization is required. All remittances must be in the name of the corporation, with the non-resident shareholder identified on the instruction to ensure clean FATCA/CRS reporting.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements (payments to foreign dubbing studios, linguists, audiovisual platforms) are executed via SWIFT MT103 remittance transfers through the Canadian bank's clearing network. For purchases of goods or services from non-resident vendors, the bank requires the vendor invoice, a purchase order, and, where the aggregate amount exceeds CAD 1,000,000, a board resolution or treasury authorization. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are permissible and typically issued by the Canadian bank's trade finance desk, in accordance with UCP 600 rules, for large infrastructure or media-platform procurement contracts; however, most localization services are settled on open account or 30-day net terms. The Canadian bank may use the cross-border USD clearing rail through the U.S. Federal Reserve system (Fedwire or CHIPS for USD funds), and through SWIFT for EUR/GBP. For recurring small-value payments, the corporation can use the bank's batch FX payment service or a dedicated payment portal, but all payments must be reported to FINTRAC if they are electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000.FX Forwards (deliverable) up to 12 months from a Canadian chartered bank, requiring a standard ISDA Master Agreement with a Canadian counterparty and supporting credit facilityFX Currency Swaps (cross-currency swaps) between CAD and USD/EUR for treasury management of intercompany loans or acquisition financing, permitted under OSFI rulesFX Options (plain vanilla OTC options) on CAD crosses, with hedge accounting treatment available under IFRS 9, provided the transaction is with a Schedule I Canadian bankCurrency Collars and participating forwards (bank-quoted structured products) permitted for the translation agency's natural hedging of client inflows and linguist outflowsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are NOT permissible for CAD, as CAD is a fully convertible, freely deliverable currency; NDFs for CAD/CROSS are not offered by Canadian banks and would be prohibited as a non-bona-fide transaction under OSFI guidanceCurrency swaps under the Bank of Canada's standing liquidity facility are not available to private corporations; such instruments are restricted to major dealers, and the corporation must rely on bank-intermediated hedging

Legal constraints: (i) such EMIs may not hold Canadian-dollar settlement balances on behalf of a corporation as a core banking relationship; (ii) their funds are typically pooled in omnibus accounts with third-party banks, exposing the corporation to counterparty and clearing risk; (iii) ability to issue Canadian-dollar bank drafts or commercial letters of credit is non-existent. Recommended use is for receiving client payments and making small vendor disbursements in multiple currencies, but all official treasury accounts, capital escrow, and material cross-border settlements must be held with a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
Combined statutory general corporate income tax rate (federal + Quebec): 26.5%, composed of the federal general rate of 15% (statutory 38% less the 10% provincial abatement and the 13% general rate reduction) plus the Quebec general provincial rate of 11.5%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% federal Part XIII withholding tax applies to Canadian-source dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Quebec coordinates a provincial withholding component of 12% under section 1015 of the Taxation Act (Quebec), and the combined federal-provincial collection is administered so that the treaty-reduced rate, once properly documented, is the effective ceiling for the shareholder.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
The federal T2 return and the Quebec CO-17 return are both due no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due June 30 of the following calendar year). The balance of tax payable is due on the same deadline. Quarterly instalments of estimated tax are due on the last day of the 3rd, 6th, 9th, and 12th months of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions with non-resident related parties per taxation year triggers the mandatory T106 information return and the expectation of contemporaneous documentation (Local File). A Master File is expected where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000, and Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime of the style found in zero-tax offshore jurisdictions; instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act and the parallel provision in section 1079.1 of the Taxation Act (Quebec) deny tax benefits obtained through transactions lacking genuine economic substance or commercial purpose. The CRA and Revenu Quebec may re-characterize artificial arrangements, and the foreign accrual property income rules and anti-hybrid mismatch rules operate to tax passive income parked in low-tax non-resident entities. A localization agency with no physical premises, no Quebec employees, and only a post-office mailbox cannot sustain intra-group service fees or royalty deductions.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation
        
Statutory sanction: Subsection 163(2) civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated; subsection 239(1) criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus interest at the prescribed rate of 9% compounded daily; imprisonment of up to five years on indictment or two years on summary conviction.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who direct or authorize the evasion are jointly and severally liable at civil law for restitution and cannot rely on the corporate veil where fraud is proven; courts may issue travel bans, passport seizures, and asset freezes; criminal fraud under section 380 of the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 14 years.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Shell Company Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) transfer-pricing adjustment restores arm's-length income; section 247(4) penalty equals 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable efforts and documentation are absent; GAAR under section 245(2) denies the tax benefit and imposes a 25% penalty on the benefit amount under subsection 245(5); interest accrues at 9% per annum.

        
Director personal liability: Directors can be assessed under section 160 of the Income Tax Act for property transferred to non-arm's-length persons at less than fair market value; courts pierce the corporate veil on a finding of sham; personal liability for the adjusted tax, penalties, and interest is collectable against director assets.

      

      
      

        Non-Resident Withholding Tax Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Subsection 215(2) imposes a penalty of 10% of the unreported gross amount plus such multiplicand; subsection 227(8) holds the payer liable for the unremitted withholding tax as a trust obligation, with interest at 9% compounded daily; criminal prosecution for intentional non-remission is available under subsection 227(9).

        
Director personal liability: Subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act makes directors personally liable for unremitted Part XIII tax withheld or required to be withheld, with only a narrow due-diligence defense; Revenu Quebec has a corresponding director liability regime, and directors may face travel restrictions and extradition proceedings where the arrears are substantial.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA) / FINTRAC Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 500,000 for each violation by a corporation; failure to report a suspicious transaction or implement a compliance program carries penalties up to CAD 100,000; criminal money laundering under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code attracts up to ten years' imprisonment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and designated AML officers may be named personally in FINTRAC enforcement notices; the corporate veil does not shield senior officers who knowingly authorize laundering transactions; individuals face travel bans, seizure of proceeds, and imprisonment; the low-risk classification of a localization agency does not negate the obligation to report suspicious transactions occurring through its corporate bank and payment accounts.
