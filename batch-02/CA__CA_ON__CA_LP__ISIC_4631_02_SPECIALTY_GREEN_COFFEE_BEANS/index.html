Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; GST/HST portal is CRA My Business Account (MyBA) via cra-arc.gc.ca

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or non-resident carrying on business in Ontario must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA online registration (Business Registration Online), followed by a separate GST/HST account registration using Form RC1 or the online portal. The Limited Partnership itself may register for GST/HST in its own legal name and capacity, and each partner must report the partnership's income allocations under their own respective tax filings. Proof of Canadian nexus, director/partner identification, and a Canadian contact address or authorized representative may be required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian income tax purposes, no check-the-box elective regime exists; the Limited Partnership is statutorily treated as a flow-through/transparent entity. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a separate person and may be required to use the partnership as a single registration unit. Certain elective mechanisms apply under Part XVI and section 156/157 of the Excise Tax Act (e.g., election by closely related corporations, or election to have deemed supplies), and hybrid mismatch rules under s. 12.6 ITA require deferral adjustments where deductions are not matched with inclusions. Non-resident partners may trigger withholding and Part XIII tax on certain distributions or allocations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter, or in any twelve consecutive calendar months, unless the provincially-administered Ontario HST threshold rules apply to the same carrying-on-business nexus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of exported goods qualify for zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the exporter, as the supplier, retains evidence that physical possession, transportation, and exportation of the goods was completed within prescribed timelines, or the goods are delivered by the supplier to a common carrier for export outside Canada. Offshore services supplied to non-resident persons may be zero-rated under Part V Schedule VII, subject to a limitation where the supply is not in respect of real property in Canada, consumables/intangibles used in Canada, or services part of a Canadian business. Green coffee bean distribution to foreign buyers must include a written contract, freight terms, and export documentation evidencing transfer of risk and exportation.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CERS electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill issued by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or bank wire/LC settlement proof from the non-resident buyer
  • Written supply/contract documentation evidencing foreign destination and payment terms
  • Packing/proof of export under CRA Memorandum 17-4 or GST/HST Memoranda establishing export

No general mandatory B2B/B2C electronic invoicing clearance or continuous transaction control regime is imposed in Canada; GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information, retain electronic records in Canada for six years, and file returns via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or EDI-based receiver programs. The CRA may issue assessment notices electronically and requires that e-commerce operators remit GST/HST under the digital economy registration framework where applicable.

CRA can reassess a zero-rating claim, deny the exemption, and assess GST/HST plus interest and penalties at 5% per month of the net tax rate (s. 280 Excise Tax Act) up to 25%, with further gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the overstated amount under s. 285.1, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent claims or knowingly making false statements. Retrospective clawback periods for ordinary assessment are four years, extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established. Export documentation must be retained for six years; failure to produce contemporaneous evidence will invalidate zero-rating and trigger full 13% HST recovery from the exporter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible and freely floating. Canada maintains no general exchange control act, no individual approval regime for current account transactions, and no capital account licensing requirement for equity injections, trade settlements, or loan disbursements. Profit remittance from Canada is legally unrestricted, but a Canadian Limited Partnership distributing Canadian-source income to non-resident partners is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at a statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced under an applicable Canada tax treaty, with compliance effected via CRA NR6 undertaking, NR4 information returns, and monthly or quarterly remittances. Non-resident partners may also face deemed-deduction obligations under subsection 135(2) or section 212.1 anti-avoidance rules if value is stripped disproportionately. No other convertibility or remittance hurdle applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply in Canada. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Export and import receipts may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency at commercial bank level. The operative compliance duties are statistical and anti-money-laundering: Canadian balance of payments reporting to Statistics Canada, customs declarations through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and FINTRAC reporting for any cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Since the target entity imports Specialty Green Coffee Beans (ISIC 4631 / NAICS 424490), the relevant compliance focus is on import payment verification and documentary trade evidence rather than export surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank; Department of Finance Canada as the foreign exchange administration authority under the Currency Act; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the financial intelligence unit governing cross-border wire reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary supplier payouts and operational disbursements; LOW for primary treasury and clearing use. Canada does not operate a dedicated EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime. Foreign digital EMIs operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and frequently hold pooled client balances through Schedule I banks. They can provide foreign exchange conversion, expedited cross-border wires, and virtual account management, but they do not provide Canadian domestic Lynx settlement access, documentary Letters of Credit, commodity trade desks, or Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation participation. The entity may integrate EMIs for low-value coffee sample payments or freight disbursements, but the primary liquidity rail must sit with a tier 1 domestic commercial bank to support ISIC 4631 import letter-of-credit requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended narrative: Capital Equity Injection - Ontario Limited Partnership with partner reference. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined Canadian federal (15% general) + Ontario (11.5% general) corporate rate; the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent so tax is assessed at the partner level on allocated income (individual Ontario partners face graduated rates up to 53.53%; corporate partners are subject to the 26.5% aggregate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income paid or allocated to non-resident limited partners; the partnership must withhold on distributions and on the allocated net income of foreign partners even where no cash distribution is made

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate gross revenues from all specified non-arm's-length cross-border transactions in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Master File, Local File, and entity-level document retention); failure to produce the documentation on an audit demand results in a 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CAD 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations exist in Ontario; substance is assessed under the Canadian General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) and the s.55(2) anti-dividend-stripping rules; CRA audits foreign-controlled LPs for genuine procurement activity, physical inventory, and trade-finance presence in Ontario; a shell LP holding a bank account without real business functions will be re-characterised and the foreign partner deemed to carry on business in Canada under s.253

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) on the net understated tax; Criminal (indictable): fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; cumulative s.162(8) late-filing penalties; CRA may reassess the partnership and all partners jointly for the evaded tax plus daily compounded arrears interest at the prescribed rate

Director personal liability: General partners and directing minds are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1; personal restitution orders may attach to personal assets; conviction triggers a travel ban under the Criminal Code and potential extradition to Canada under mutual legal assistance treaties; directors may be disqualified from serving as officers of any corporation for up to 5 years
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse (Income Tax Act s.245 and s.55(2))

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterisation: denial of the tax benefit and income adjustment to the arm's-length standard; 25% surcharge on the tax benefit under the 2024 GAAR amendments when the avoidance arrangement spans more than 3 years; s.55(2) re-characterises deemed dividends as taxable capital gains or income; reassessment windows extended to 7 years for grossly negligent filings

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in the sham may be assessed for the full tax benefit denied under s.245; punitive damages may be awarded in derivative litigation; the CRA may impose penalties exceeding CAD 100,000 for egregious corporate-shop abuse; individuals are disqualified from managing corporations under s.91 of the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act while civil judgements remain unpaid
Withholding Tax Evasion (Part XIII Non-Resident Distributions)

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of 10% of the unwithheld amount plus the full 25% tax is payable by the partnership under s.227(8); the amount is immediately payable with daily compounding interest; repeated failures trigger a CRA compliance halt, registration freeze, and funds garnishment against the LP's account receivables and bank balances

Director personal liability: Under s.227.1 ITA, directors and general partners are personally liable for the unremitted withholding; personal restitution is demandable within a 2-year limitation window; the CRA may issue a Director's Certificate and pursue personal assets including primary residences; a travel ban is imposed pending resolution of the 25% obligation
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC / Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals per violation; criminal money-laundering conviction under Criminal Code s.462.31 carries up to 10 years imprisonment; offence-related property, including the coffee inventory and shipping containers, is subject to forfeiture

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or wilful blindness of trade-based money laundering are criminally liable as parties to the offence; personal travel bans and restraint orders may be imposed under the Criminal Code; compliance officers face professional disqualification and mandatory restitution of the laundered amounts
