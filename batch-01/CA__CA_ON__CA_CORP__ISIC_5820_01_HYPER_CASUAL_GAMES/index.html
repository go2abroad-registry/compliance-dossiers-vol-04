Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an OBCA private corporation; the authorized share capital is set by the Articles and shares may be issued for nominal consideration (e.g., C$0.01 per share). No escrow deposit requirement is imposed on a software-publisher vehicle; escrow capital rules apply only to certain regulated sectors (insurance, securities dealing, trust companies) and are not applicable to ISIC 5820. Non-resident ownership is permitted, and Ontario has removed the resident-Canadian director majority requirement for non-offering OBCA corporations effective 2021.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required under the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07): annual fire alarm testing, extinguishers, emergency signage, and a written fire safety plan filed with the local fire department. Structural load inspections are not separately enforced for standard office leaseholds; the municipal Building Division occupancy permit issued for the leased suite serves as the structural clearance. Employers must register with the WSIB (Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, 1997) and maintain occupational health and safety compliance, including a supervisor awareness module where employees work from the office; the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) requires an accessible customer service policy and accessibility compliance filing for employers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an OBCA private corporation; the authorized share capital is set by the Articles and shares may be issued for nominal consideration (e.g., C$0.01 per share). No escrow deposit requirement is imposed on a software-publisher vehicle; escrow capital rules apply only to certain regulated sectors (insurance, securities dealing, trust companies) and are not applicable to ISIC 5820. Non-resident ownership is permitted, and Ontario has removed the resident-Canadian director majority requirement for non-offering OBCA corporations effective 2021.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Ontario Ministry of Finance for Ontario corporate income tax and Employer Health Tax via the Ontario Business Registry.

E-government filing portal

A federally or Ontario-incorporated business corporation receives a nine-digit Business Number (BN) automatically from the CRA upon incorporation. The GST/HST program account (RT) is opened by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online (BRO); the corporate income tax program account (RC0001) is likewise issued through the same integration. A non-resident foreign publisher without a Canadian presence may obtain a BN and register for GST/HST through the CRA Non-Resident GST/HST Registration Portal, which applies to digital-service suppliers exceeding the small-supplier threshold. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under a federal-provincial collection agreement using a permanent-establishment allocation; Ontario Employer Health Tax registration follows separately through the Ontario Business Registry when payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a statutory, non-flow-through taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act and the Ontario Corporations Tax Act. It is liable on world-wide income if tax-resident in Canada; eligible active business income may access the small business deduction (federal and Ontario limits shared), intercorporate dividend deductions, and foreign tax credits. No partnership or disregarded-entity pass-through classification exists for an incorporated entity.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Canadian federal income tax; classification is statutory. Statutory elections applicable include: (i) the Excise Tax Act s.156 closely-related corporation election to treat intercompany taxable supplies as non-taxable for GST/HST; (ii) the Income Tax Act s.216(1) election for non-residents deriving Canadian-source property income; (iii) the s.93(1) joint election on foreign affiliate surplus distributions; (iv) excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) group financing elections under ITA ss.18.2 and 18.7 for large CFC groups; and (v) provincial allocation elections for Ontario corporate income tax apportionment based on permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold is met when taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies made by the person and any predecessor, exceed CAD 30,000 in total consideration either in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the same threshold applies to non-resident digital-service suppliers under the July 2021 GST/HST regime for digital economy supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian GST/HST registrant publishing hyper-casual mobile games may zero-rate outbound supplies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: (i) services rendered to a non-resident person (Part V s.7), if the supply is not a specified service in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada and is not primarily for persons in Canada - this covers ad-network and monetization services rendered to foreign AdMob and AppLovin entities; (ii) supplies of intangible personal property, such as game software and IP licences, where the IP may be used only outside Canada or the recipient is a non-resident with no Canadian use (Part V s.10); (iii) exported tangible goods (Part V s.19). Digital-game supplies to non-resident consumers are deemed made outside Canada under place-of-supply rules based on the consumer's usual place of residence, consequently outside the scope of HST. The registrant must support each zero-rated supply with contemporaneous written evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and absence of Canadian use, in line with CRA published policy P-051.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or CAED electronic export reporting) for physical exports from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the goods left Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment-provider remittance statement evidencing payment from a non-Canadian source
  • Written supply contract and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident counterparty, licensed game IP, and service description
  • Two pieces of contemporaneous written evidence of the recipient's non-resident status per CRA Policy P-051, such as a foreign corporate registry extract, certificate of incorporation, or government-issued identification
  • IP licence or end-user agreement showing the game content and advertising inventory are used, hosted, and consumed outside Canada
  • Ad-network payout statements (AdMob Payout, AppLovin Payout) evidencing the foreign payor, settlement amounts, and tax-residence data

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax clearance currently applies in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE with payment due within one month after the end of the reporting period. The 2024 federal budget announced voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoice standard; no legislative mandate is in force for B2B digital invoicing. Records and platform-generated tax invoices (AdMob Payout, AppLovin Payout statements) must be retained in English or French for six years under ETA record-keeping rules.

Canada imposes no explicit statutory FX repatriation deadline for software or service exports; the 180-day convention is referenced here as a prudent intercompany settlement and documentation-currency benchmark. CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period (ETA s.298(1)); where a false statement or omission is attributable to carelessness, neglect, wilful default or fraud, the period is unlimited (ETA s.298(3)). Deficient export zero-rating documentation permits CRA to recharacterize the supply as taxable, assessing uncollected 13% HST plus prescribed arrears interest, and to claw back any GST/HST refund or credit claimed. Penalties include: (i) a 10% misstatement penalty under ETA s.280(1); (ii) a 50% false-statement penalty under ETA s.285; (iii) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of additional tax and transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the adjustment under the Income Tax Act (ss.163.2, 247(3), and foreign accrual property income rules on foreign ad-network payors); and (iv) criminal prosecution under ETA s.327 for wilful evasion, with fines and imprisonment. Freezing injunctions and collection actions may issue without prior notice where the Minister suspects jeopardy.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency for both capital and current account transactions. There are no general exchange control restrictions on inbound foreign direct investment, dividend remittances, interest payments, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident shareholders. Profit repatriation from an Ontario business corporation is structured as a dividend subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., U.S.-Canada treaty rate). Anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening apply at the payment processor level rather than through FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts in foreign currency may be maintained in Canadian-chartered bank accounts offshore or onshore, converted at commercial market rates, or used directly to settle foreign supplier obligations, subject only to bank reporting thresholds and FINTRAC monitoring obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Monetary Authority); Foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with OSFI supervising banking prudential conduct and FINTRAC administering AML/CTF transaction reporting; exchange control functions are not centralized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Foreign digital EMI/neobank license holders may not carry on a regulated banking business in Canada without being authorized as a Canadian bank or a federally/ provincially recognized credit union. Non-bank EMI platforms may operate only as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and they cannot access the Canadian payments clearing infrastructure unless they partner with a Schedule I bank. Cross-border EMI payment aggregation for corporate treasury is permissible for remittance flows but is not a substitute for a full-service Canadian corporate bank account; core liquidity clearing and capital escrow functions require a domestic authorized financial institution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate per non-resident connected person (T106 reporting threshold); Country-by-Country Report filing threshold is CAD 750 million consolidated group revenue (or USD 750 million equivalent)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) and the transfer-pricing arm's length rule (section 247) effectively require real economic substance. CRA will disregard or recharacterize arrangements involving a Canadian shell corporation that receives game publishing income but lacks Canadian employees, operational decision-making, business records, or risk exposure. A non-resident parent that centralizes all IP, marketing, and development outside Canada while routing revenue through an Ontario entity will lose treaty benefits and be subject to reassessment at the full provincial and federal rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustment under ITA section 247 restoring taxable income to the arm's length result; transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount in excess of the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; gross negligence penalty under section 163(2) at 50% of the understated tax; late T106 penalties of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000; daily compounded interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved false transfer pricing documentation are exposed to civil liability for the corporation's unremitted withholding taxes under section 227.1; criminal prosecution under section 239 for tax evasion may result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; serious cases trigger court-ordered travel restrictions and passport seizure during the investigation.
Tax Evasion / Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on a gross-up basis; false omission penalty under ITA section 163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understatement; section 162 late-filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per full month (up to 12 months); criminal fines equal to 100% to 200% of the amount evaded; CRA may freeze bank accounts and impose a collection-ordered travel ban.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or instruct the omission are subject to personal joint liability for unremitted Part XIII source deductions and corporate tax under section 227.1; criminal conviction under section 239 can attract imprisonment of 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment, plus restitution orders for civil damages.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) denying the tax benefit; recharacterization of the Canadian entity as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent; withholding tax reassessment at 25% on deemed dividends or management fees; penalty for failure to file foreign-source information (T1134/T1135) ranging from CAD 2,500 to CAD 25,000 plus ongoing daily penalties for forced compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the corporation to operate as a sham can have the corporate veil pierced; they face joint and several civil liability for corporate tax debts, CRA can obtain an order imposing travel bans, and directors who sign false declarations of Canadian residency may be prosecuted for perjury and tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML / Digital Payment Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for regulated entities; tax reassessment including unreported income, 50% gross negligence penalty, and Part XIII withholding on any disguised payments; Criminal Code anti-money laundering offences carry fines and imprisonment; Professional disciplinary actions are not insurers.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners who knowingly launder proceeds derived from digital ad fraud or unreported gaming revenue face criminal liability under Criminal Code section 462.31 with maximum imprisonment of 10 years; civil liability extends to all funds moved through personal accounts, and court-ordered travel bans are common during forfeiture and prosecution proceedings.
