Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized currency regime: the Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible G10 currency, and there are no exchange controls under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Foreign Exchange Control regulations. Capital-account and current-account transactions require no Bank of Canada conversion authorization. Profit remittance (dividends, management fees, and salvage net earnings) is unrestricted in form, but is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) — statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5%–15% under applicable Canadian income-tax treaties — and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer (EFT) reporting for each remittance of CAD 10,000 or more. Marine salvage and towage receivables denominated in USD may be retained offshore or converted freely, without surrender obligation; the practical compliance hurdles are thin-capitalization (s.18(4)), transfer-pricing documentation (s.247), and the EIFEL excessive-interest and financing-expenses limitation (s.18.2).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada, and there is no statutory repatriation timeline or Central Bank conversion percentage for deep-sea tug towage and salvage export receipts. The only binding statutory obligations are: (i) FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting for every outgoing/incoming transfer of CAD 10,000 or above; (ii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting for tug and salvage equipment deployed beyond Canadian waters; (iii) IFRS/ASPE translation of foreign-currency receivables into the functional currency; and (iv) s.247 contemporaneous transfer-pricing records where salvage revenue is earned through, or booked in, a foreign related corporation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada (Ministère des Finances Canada) as ultimate monetary and exchange-policy authority; prudential supervision of Canadian banks and FX settlement risk is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and transaction-level cross-border funds-flow surveillance is carried out by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). There is no dedicated Canadian foreign-exchange administrative body equivalent to an emerging-market capital-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High as a supporting rail, Low as a primary rail. Non-resident digital EMIs that lack a Canadian Schedule I/II chartered-bank license cannot directly settle on Payments Canada rails (Lynx RTGS and ACSS batch). Such providers may operate in Canada only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (PCMLTFA, s.190) or as deposit agents of a regulated Canadian bank. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced offshore towage/salvage treasury profile, an MSB-style EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) is viable for small-value USD-CAD conversion and routine cross-border disbursements, but escrowed Lloyds-form salvage awards, tug-charter performance security, and capital injections must run exclusively through a Schedule I chartered bank and Lynx/correspondent clearing rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where a taxpayer has cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in excess of CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate; a comprehensive Local File/Master File set is expected for material transactions, and large multinational groups must maintain a global Master File when total consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations to domestic corporations; however, CRA assesses contractual allocation, management, employees, vessels, insurance and revenue records to ensure a capital/income economic presence. Pure invoice-shell arrangements, off-book salvage fees, and the absence of active towage operations will be attacked under GAAR, residence doctrines and the permanent-establishment threshold, with penalties and non-recognition of the corporate form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Attribution of Salvage Income

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment with interest; gross negligence penalty of 50% under s.163(2); transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3) of up to 10% of the transfer-pricing correction; criminal prosecution under s.239(1) with fines between 50% and 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment for up to two years (or five years on indictment).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and unpaid corporate tax under s.227.1; civil restitution orders, seizure and garnishment of personal assets, travel bans pending extradition, and imprisonment up to two years for deliberate evasion.
Shell Corporation / Sham Corporate Structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment (s.245) with a 25% penalty plus interest; sham doctrine permits CRA to allocate income to the real economic owner; transfer-pricing penalties and loss denial; registration may be stripped under the Canada Business Corporations Act if the company is inactive for two consecutive years; fines up to CAD 200,000 for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil; directors who directed invoicing through sham offshore entities face personal assessments, punitive damages in tax fraud actions, Canadian travel bans, and imprisonment for wilful non-reporting.
AML / Counterfeit Invoicing and Unregulated Lloyd's Wire Transfers

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of crime (up to 10 years' imprisonment); seizure of proceeds and disgorgement; CRA partnership with FINTRAC may use the AML findings as evidence of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally named in AML penalty proceedings and restitution claims; conviction may lead to travel bans, extradition, director disqualification, and imprisonment for up to five years for laundering; CRA director's liability for penalties may reach personal bank accounts.
