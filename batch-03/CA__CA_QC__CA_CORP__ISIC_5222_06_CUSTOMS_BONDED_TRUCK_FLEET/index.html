Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) for GST/HST and Part I corporate income tax. Quebec: Revenu Québec - Mon dossier d'entreprise (https://www.revenuquebec.ca) for QST and provincial corporate tax under the Act respecting the Québec sales tax (CQLR c T-0.1). Corporate registry: Corporations Canada (federal) / Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ). CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) administers customs bonded carrier licensing and eManifest.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Québec Business Corporations Act; obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and, if incorporated in Quebec, the 11-character NEQ. Step 2: Register for GST/HST through CRA Business Registration Online using Form RC1 (RC1A for non-residents); the BN receives an RT program account code (e.g., 123456789RT0001). Step 3: Register for QST at Revenu Québec via the combined entreprises-québec portal using Form VD-1; the QST account is NEQ-based. Step 4: Apply separately to CBSA for a Customs Bonded Carrier Authorization and, where applicable, sufferance warehouse capacity to operate the GPS-sealed feeder routes. Step 5: Non-resident carriers must appoint a Canadian representative for service and keep books and records in Canada as required by ETA s.286 and the QSTA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer on worldwide income where central management and control is in Canada (ITA s.2 and s.250); no pass-through election is available for a corporation. Quebec taxes allocated Canadian-source income at the corporate level. Non-resident-owned corporations remain subject to Part XIII withholding on outbound dividends/interest and ITA s.115 Canadian-source tax on the Quebec trucking business.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; classification follows the corporate statute. Available elections for the bonded carrier: (i) ETA s.156 election to treat supplies between closely related registrants as made for nil consideration (GST/HST grouping); (ii) ETA s.227 Quick Method election for small registrants; (iii) ETA s.238 annual filing election for small suppliers; (iv) ITA s.256 associated-corporations agreement allocating the Small Business Deduction business limit; (v) QST mirror group election under the QSTA; (vi) instalment and remittance frequency elections; (vii) hybrid-mismatch elections - not yet enacted in federal Canadian law (OECD hybrid rules remain in draft), so reliance is placed on treaty and domestic FAPI/thin-capitalisation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for both GST/HST (ETA s.148) and QST, measured on worldwide taxable supplies of the registrant and associated persons. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada (including soliciting orders and delivering goods) is registrable without the threshold; voluntary registration is strongly indicated for a bonded carrier to recover input tax refunds on fuel, GPS telematics, tolls and fleet maintenance. Payroll and provincial health-services fund registrations trigger once the employer-establishment test is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/QST applies where the GPS-sealed bonded trucking constitutes an international transportation service or a service in respect of exported goods within ETA Schedule VI (Parts V and VII), mirrored by the QSTA: (1) the goods must be destined for export or in transit between places outside Canada, evidenced by an airway bill/house manifest; (2) where goods are supplied for export, export must occur promptly - the CRA administrative benchmark is 60 days from the supply; (3) the feeder leg must be performed under CBSA customs supervision with a valid bonded carrier authorization and seal records; (4) the invoice and contract must identify the foreign destination and the international carriage obligation; (5) services supplied to a non-resident for goods located outside Canada at the time of supply also qualify. Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline for zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the standard export-letter-of-credit collection cycle.

  • CBSA eManifest (ACI) cargo and conveyance data transmission; PARS/RMD release records
  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian International Trade Single Window (for goods valued at or above CAD 2,000, or controlled goods)
  • Master Air Waybill / House Air Waybill evidencing foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and transport contract evidencing continuous international carriage

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices is mandated in Canada. Mandatory electronic filing: GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; Quebec imposes similar electronic-filing rules for QST. CBSA eManifest (ACI) mandates advance electronic cargo and conveyance data for bonded highway movements, including PARS/RMD release submissions and GPS seal/trailer integrity reporting; CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalties (AMPS) apply to non-compliant transmissions. Voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-aligned e-invoicing is available, and records must be retained for six years under ETA s.286 and the QSTA.

CRA/Revenu Québec may reassess within 4 years (ETA s.298; unlimited for misrepresentation or omission). Defensive claims of export zero-rating are high-risk audit triggers: (i) reassessment of uncollected GST/QST plus prescribed-rate interest; (ii) 25% gross-negligence penalty on the net tax understated (ETA s.285); (iii) failure-to-register, collect or file penalties (ETA ss.280-282); (iv) criminal prosecution for false statements under the Excise Tax Act offence provisions (fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years); (v) CBSA AMPS of up to CAD 25,000 per violation and revocation of the customs bonded carrier licence; (vi) ITA s.247 transfer-pricing adjustments and ITA s.239 prosecution if export proceeds are diverted; (vii) clawback of ITC/ITR claims on invoices lacking the required destination or export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; no exchange control, prior approval, or repatriation restriction regime exists. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5% for dividends and 15% for interest/royalties under the Canada-United States Tax Convention and similar instruments); (ii) source-of-funds and know-your-client documentation required by OSFI-regulated banks; and (iii) Statistics Canada cross-border transaction reporting for payments exceeding CAD 1 million per reporting period. Dividend payments to non-resident shareholders must be effectuated by a Canadian resident agent and reported on NR4 forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or designated account mandate for export receipts. Export proceeds are freely retainable, convertible, and re-deployable. Applicable obligations: (i) FINTRAC large cash transaction report (CTR) for cash of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report (EFTR) for cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export reporting and Canadian International Merchandise Trade (CIMT) statistical filings; and (iv) operational compliance of the bonded carrier licence, including Cargo (ACI) manifests and bonded warehouse seals.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange administration jointly administered with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cross-border digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury) can serve as secondary operational rails for low-value supplier disbursements and payroll, but they are money service businesses (MSBs) supervised by FINTRAC rather than OSFI-regulated Schedule I chartered banks. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI soliciting Canadian clients must register as an MSB with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; (ii) balances held at an EMI are not CDIC-insured and are not eligible for capital escrow or letter-of-credit facilities; (iii) CAD routing via SWIFT/BIT is supported, but trade documentation, CBSA duty payments, and airport authority settlements require a Canadian bank account. Use EMIs only for <15% of monthly treasury throughput.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for cross-border transactions with non-residents (for large corporations). Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million. Local File required if transactions exceed CAD 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require genuine economic substance. The CRA may deny treaty benefits if the entity lacks substance (e.g., no real office, employees, or business activity). For customs bonded trucking, the entity must demonstrate operational capacity, physical assets, and active management in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) and Quebec Taxation Act (s. 1019). Penalties: 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act) if they fail to withhold and remit. Civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, and impose transfer pricing penalties (10% of net adjustment). GAAR can apply to recharacterize transactions. Penalties up to CAD 10,000 for failure to file information returns.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. Personal liability for tax arrears and potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations, CAD 100,000 for individuals. Criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to implement AML controls. Travel bans and asset freezes possible under FINTRAC enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty: 10% of the net adjustment (if adjustment exceeds CAD 1 million). Additional penalties for late filing of T106 (CAD 100 per month, up to CAD 2,500).

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading documents. Personal liability for gross negligence penalties (up to 50% of tax understated).
