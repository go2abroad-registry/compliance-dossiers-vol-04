Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) — sole federal tax and GST/HST administrator; digital portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance — provincial HST component and Ontario corporate/partnership income tax administration under the 2010 Great Western Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement (portal: Ontario Business Registry / ONT-TAXS). For the Ontario limited partnership, the CRA is the single counterparty for GST/HST registration and return filing; the 8% Ontario HST portion is collected by CRA on behalf of the Province of Ontario, and no separate Ontario sales tax account is required.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Confirm legal capacity: the Ontario limited partnership is a 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and must hold its own Business Number (BN) for GST/HST even though income tax flows through to partners. Step 2 — File the registration application, executed by the registered general partner, either electronically via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; attach the partnership agreement, Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration certificate, certified identity of the general partner, and the principal address of the partnership's central mind and management. Step 3 — For a non-resident general partner or non-resident limited partners, complete Form NR301 'Request by a Non-Resident for a Reduction in the Amount of Non-Resident Tax Withheld' and, where applicable, Form GST72-NR, so that Part XIII withholding is applied at the treaty-reduced rate (typically 0–15% depending on the jurisdiction). Step 4 — The CRA issues a 9-digit BN with a two-letter program suffix: 'RT' for GST/HST; add the 'RM' program account if goods are physically exported and 'RP' for Canadian payroll. Step 5 — After registration via BRO, the GN (GST/HST account) is active within 1–2 business days; paper Form RC1 processing averages 4–6 weeks. The Ontario Ministry of Finance does not issue a separate TIN: corporate partners file Ontario corporate income tax using the BN and T2 program account, while the partnership files a T5013 Partnership Information Return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Income Tax Act s. 96, the Ontario LP is fiscally transparent: the partnership computes income and loss at the partnership level, then each partner — whether resident or non-resident — includes its allocated share in its own tax return; the LP itself has no federal or Ontario income tax liability. Territorial scope: a non-resident limited partner does not create Canadian nexus solely by holding an LP interest, but if the LP carries on business in Ontario through employees, a fixed base, or a general partner acting in Canada, the partnership has a permanent establishment in Canada, and non-resident partners become taxable on the Canadian-source business income. For indirect taxes, the LP is an independent registrant 'person' and is the taxable supplier of the financial newswire/API data service, which is a taxable supply of a service or intangible personal property, not a 'financial service' exemption. No tax-exempt zone classification applies: Ontario offers no free-trade-zone VAT derogation; HST applies to all Canadian-use supplies unless zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; the partnership is irrebuttably a pass-through entity. Elections available to the CA_LP: (1) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) to adopt a non-calendar year-end for the partnership's taxation year; (2) ITA s. 85 rollover election permitting the tax-deferred transfer of the financial news data contracts or API subscriber book from the general partner (or an affiliate) into the LP at adjusted cost base; (3) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for nil consideration on inter-entity supplies if the LP is closely related to a Canadian service subsidiary, and the s. 150 election to reduce the frequency of GST/HST remittance; (4) hybrid-mismatch compliance: Canada's domestic hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.2 and 18.21, in force since 2020) deny the deduction of hybrid amounts unless the corresponding foreign payment is subject to equivalent foreign taxation — where the US partner is a flow-through LLC under US check-the-box, the LP must file a hybrid-mismatch schedule with the T5013 to identify the deduction and preserve cross-border deductibility; (5) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident partner to be assessed on a net Canadian-source basis instead of gross Part XIII withholding. All elections require filing with CRA before the statutory deadline, typically the due date of the T5013 return (March 31 or six months after fiscal year-end).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 306. Registration is mandatory when cumulative taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. For a non-resident supplier actively soliciting orders or providing electronic subscription services to Canadian residents (e.g., the financial newswire API sold into Canada), the same threshold applies; the registrant obligation arises at the end of the fourth calendar month following the quarter in which the threshold is exceeded, regardless of physical Canadian presence. Where the CA_LP exclusively serves non-resident syndicated finance institutions, the threshold is not triggered by export supplies, but any incidental Canadian-based subscriber volume above CAD 30,000 forces full HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (1) s. 7 — supply of a service to a non-resident who is not registered under the Act, where the service is not supplied in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property physically located in Canada at the time the service is performed; the financial news aggregation and API feed service qualifies when delivered electronically to the non-resident subscriber's foreign servers or office systems. (2) s. 21–23 and s. 10 — supply of intangible personal property (financial data content licensing) to a non-resident non-registered recipient where the rights are acquired for use exclusively outside Canada; the newswire content license qualifies when the subscriber is a foreign syndicated finance arranger using the feed solely for offshore analysis and distribution. Zero-rating is self-assessed by the supplier, requires no prior CRA approval, and must be declared on the GST/HST return with supporting evidence retained for audit. The zero rate does not apply where the non-resident recipient is registered for GST/HST, where the service is performed on tangible personal property located in Canada, or where the supply is to a Canadian branch of a foreign bank.

  • CRA GST/HST return and supporting 'zero-rated exports' worksheet evidencing the export classification
  • Executed international subscription/license agreement with the non-resident financial institution, including governing law, place of performance, and exclusive foreign jurisdiction clauses
  • Commercial invoice referencing the zero-rated supply under Schedule VI Part V (item 7 or 23) of the Excise Tax Act, showing the LP's GST/HST registration number and the foreign billing address
  • SWIFT MT103 wire instruction and bank statement evidencing inward foreign currency remittance from the non-resident subscriber to the LP's Canadian bank account
  • Non-residency evidence pack: certificate of incorporation from the foreign registry, foreign tax residence certificate, beneficial ownership/CRS self-certification, board resolution confirming off-Canada use, and IP-address access logs showing the API feed consumed outside Canada
  • Where any physical deliverable is exported (e.g., data drive, appliance, or damaged media replacement): CBSA B13A Export Declaration / Automated Export Declaration, commercial invoice, and Bill of Lading or Air Waybill

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or split-payment mandate for e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is lawful, provided the invoice contains all elements of Excise Tax Act s. 223 and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations: supplier legal name, BN and GST/HST registration number, date of invoice, description of the financial news feed/API subscription, consideration payable, HST rate applied, and total amount; the recipient's name and registration number are required where the invoice exceeds CAD 150. The federal government has adopted the Pan-Canadian E-invoicing framework based on Peppol BIS 3.0 on a voluntary basis, so the LP may exchange e-invoices via a certified Access Point with non-resident counterparties. GST/HST returns are filed through CRA Net-File using EDI/XML or CRA's GST/HST web filing; there is no pre-clearance step. The LP must retain all e-invoice records in a legible electronic format for six years as documentary evidence of zero-rating.

CRA may reassess the LP's zero-rated claims for four years from the date of filing the GST/HST return (Excise Tax Act s. 310), and without time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, fraud, or wilful default is attributable to the LP or any partner. Penalties include: (1) gross-negligence penalty under s. 285.1 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated where zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof; (2) interest under s. 280 compounded daily at prescribed rates retroactive to the original due date of the return; (3) denial or clawback of input tax credits claimed on Canadian-origin expenses where the export claim is fraudulent or the LP fails to retain the foreign-remittance and non-residency evidence; (4) criminal prosecution under Excise Tax Act s. 327 (evasion) and Income Tax Act s. 239 (tax evasion against resident partners) with fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years for wilful false statements. Ontario will reassess the 8% provincial HST component in lockstep with CRA under the coordinated provincial VAT framework. Transfer pricing rules (ITA s. 247) apply where the LP supplies the newswire feed at nil or below arm's length price to a foreign affiliate; CRA may impute revenue, recalculate HST at 13%, and impose the same penalty and interest regime on the additional assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible without exchange-control approvals. Both current-account and capital-account transactions, including inbound equity contributions, cross-border investment, profit allocations, and cash distributions, are unrestricted under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada imposes no statutory profit-remittance licensing or exchange-rationing mechanism. The practical hurdles for an Ontario limited partnership are tax-driven, not exchange-control-driven: non-resident partners are subject to Canadian income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act on allocable business income of the partnership; the partnership must file T5013 partnership information returns; GST/HST registration may be required for taxable digital newswire services; and certain cross-border funds flows must meet FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting thresholds. None of these constitutes a convertibility or repatriation obstacle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose mandatory surrender of export receipts, statutory conversion ratios, or time-limited retention rules for proceeds earned from cross-border newswire syndication and financial-data API services. Export earnings may be invoiced in USD, EUR, CAD, or any other freely negotiable currency and may be held indefinitely in a non-resident foreign-currency account or a Canadian-dollar account. However, any electronic funds transfer equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, moving across the Canadian border, must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the underlying international transaction purpose code must be disclosed to the Canadian financial institution for balance-of-payments reporting to Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance. Canada does not maintain a dedicated Foreign Exchange Control Administration; the Bank of Canada administers the national high-value payment system (Lynx) and issues Canadian dollars, while market-determined foreign exchange conversion is available through all domestic Schedule I banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the European-style 'electronic money institution' licence; a foreign fintech that issues electronic money, holds client funds, or provides payment services on behalf of Canadian customers may be treated as a foreign money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must register with FINTRAC before operating in or to Canada. A non-resident EMI can be used as a settlement rail for collecting subscription fees if it is licensed in its home jurisdiction and maintains strong AML/ATF controls, but it cannot function as the primary treasury bank for an Ontario limited partnership without Canadian banking charter. Using an offshore EMI as a sweep account adds legal and counterparty risk; therefore the treasury blueprint prioritises a Schedule I domestic bank account for core liquidity and settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from non-resident limited partners will be routed through the global SWIFT network using Payment Message Type MT103, with the Canadian Schedule I bank acting as the beneficiary bank. The wire instruction must clearly identify: (i) the Ontario limited partnership's legal name and account number; (ii) the underlying purpose as 'inbound capital contribution — equity participation in Ontario Limited Partnership' or 'newswire subscription/licence revenue' for operating receipts; (iii) an international transaction purpose code consistent with Canada's Statistics Act balance-of-payments reporting framework (capital contributions are classified under equity capital/direct or portfolio investment categories; subscription revenue is classified under services and financial-information licencing); and (iv) complete originator and beneficiary Travel Rule data under FATF Recommendation 16, including names, account numbers, addresses, and identifiers. Any inbound wire transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 is reported to FINTRAC as an electronic funds transfer. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the European-style 'electronic money institution' licence; a foreign fintech that issues electronic money, holds client funds, or provides payment services on behalf of Canadian customers may be treated as a foreign money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must register with FINTRAC before operating in or to Canada. A non-resident EMI can be used as a settlement rail for collecting subscription fees if it is licensed in its home jurisdiction and maintains strong AML/ATF controls, but it cannot function as the primary treasury bank for an Ontario limited partnership without Canadian banking charter. Using an offshore EMI as a sweep account adds legal and counterparty risk; therefore the treasury blueprint prioritises a Schedule I domestic bank account for core liquidity and settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal (15%) and Ontario (11.5%) general corporate rate; the Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so allocated income is taxed at the partner level, not at the entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory withholding rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; distributions from an Ontario LP are not 'dividends' — the non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on allocated Canadian-source business income, and the LP is jointly and severally liable for the partner's Part I tax on that income under the administrative withholding regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Due no later than 5 months after the partnership fiscal year-end for fiscal periods ending after December 30, 2022; the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act also requires an annual declaration with the Ontario Ministry (within 60 days of the registration anniversary).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; corporate partners with gross tax payable exceeding CAD 3,000 must pay tax in quarterly installments, with the first installment due on the last day of the first quarter of the taxation year (2-month filing deadline applies to CCPCs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is mandatory where the total value of reportable transactions with any single non-arm's length non-resident exceeds CAD 50,000 in a fiscal year; comprehensive contemporaneous Local File documentation is expected by CRA where gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million or intercompany transaction value exceeds CAD 10 million; Master File and Country-by-Country reporting apply to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 900 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, CRA applies ITA s. 245 GAAR, the common law sham and substance-over-form doctrines, and the s. 237.3 hallmark-based mandatory disclosure rules to disregard transactions lacking genuine economic substance; under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, a limited partner who participates in control of the business loses limited liability, and CRA may reclassify a substance-less LP as a general partnership or taxable association.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 50%–200% and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; interest and full reassessment of taxes owing.

Director personal liability: General partners and controlling directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted tax withholdings; CRA may obtain an 'unpaid tax certificate', register liens on personal property, and issue travel-ban orders under s. 226.1 for debts over CAD 50,000; the corporate veil is pierced where a director directed or acquiesced in the evasion.
Shell company / artificial partnership

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) denies the tax benefit and reassesses with arrears interest; s. 163.2 third-party civil penalty of up to CAD 100,000 plus CAD 1,000 per false statement; reclassification of the LP as a taxable association or corporation under the 'partnership versus association' test; denial of treaty benefits under LOB rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly liable for the reassessed taxes and penalties; CRA can enforce against personal property and bank accounts; criminal charges under s. 239 are available where the artificial structure was designed to mislead; key personnel may face entry/exclusion restrictions when departing Canada.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(4) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to determine arm's length prices; full denial of the deduction plus arrears interest at the prescribed rate; secondary adjustments under s. 247(12) may recharacterize the adjustment as a dividend or shareholder contribution.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed or approved false transfer pricing certificates are jointly and severally liable for the s. 247(4) penalty; CRA may assess directors personally where the corporation is insolvent and the penalty relates to unremitted withholding; criminal liability under s. 239 may attach for deliberate falsification of transfer pricing records.
AML / FINTRAC and payment-rail non-compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporate non-compliance and CAD 500,000 for individuals; separate penalties for record-keeping and reporting violations; criminal sanctions for knowingly failing to report a suspicious transaction include imprisonment; FINTRAC can also suspend, restrict, or revoke an MSB registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for FINTRAC AMPs where they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the non-compliance; banning orders may prohibit an individual from operating an MSB; for underlying money-laundering predicate offences, Canadian courts can impose travel bans, extradition, asset freezing, and personal fines plus imprisonment under the Criminal Code and the PCMLTFA.
