Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The AG requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000. At formation, at least 20% (i.e., CHF 20,000) of the nominal capital must be fully paid up in cash. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) that remains frozen until the Commercial Register confirms the registration, after which the capital is released to the company's free disposal.

Funding & unlock path: No specialized fire-safety or civil-defense permit is required for e-commerce and low-density warehouse operations. The company must satisfy cantonal fire-prevention requirements for its office/storage premises (smoke detectors, escape routes, portable extinguishers) and comply with SECO workplace safety and health guidelines, including SUVA accident-prevention rules for office and logistics staff.

Paid-in capital rule: The AG requires a minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000. At formation, at least 20% (i.e., CHF 20,000) of the nominal capital must be fully paid up in cash. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) that remains frozen until the Commercial Register confirms the registration, after which the capital is released to the company's free disposal.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), tax portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch; VAT and UID e-services: https://www.e-service.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Swiss seat or permanent establishment must register with the ESTV for Swiss VAT if it makes taxable supplies in Switzerland and its annual worldwide Swiss-taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. Procedure: submit the official VAT registration form (Formular 100 / Formulaire 100) via e-service or post, attach proof of incorporation (company extract/charter), evidence of Swiss business nexus (supply source, import/export customs records, local warehouse or agent), and appoint a Swiss tax representative if the entity has no residence in Switzerland or reciprocal recognition. Upon approval, ESTV issues the Swiss VAT identification number (MWST/TVA/IVA UID number, in practice UID-CH-XXXX-XXX-X). The UID also serves for VAT identification; TIN for direct tax matters is generally the same UID number. Registration is effective from the date of taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. For Swiss VAT, entities may elect voluntary VAT registration despite falling below the CHF 100,000 threshold; may elect VAT grouping (Art. 67 VAT Act) for economically integrated Swiss entities; and may apply to use the excess-input-tax method or simplified accounting methods under Art. 37 VAT Act. For direct federal, cantonal and communal taxes, the Aktiengesellschaft is taxed at entity level; no pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally recognized absent permanent establishment substance; certain group relief/notional interest deduction mechanisms do not apply in Switzerland except within cantonal tax conditions. Charitable non-profit entities may apply for tax-exempt status under Art. 56 lit. g LIFD.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible. Switzerland imposes no exchange controls on either capital-account or current-account transactions under the Federal Act on Currency and Means of Payment (Currency Act), the Federal Act on the Swiss National Bank, and the Foreign Exchange Ordinance. Inbound capital contributions, outbound profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, and trade settlement payments are all free of SNB authorization requirements. The only material friction is the statutory 35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions and certain interest payments; the effective burden is typically reduced to 0-15% via double taxation treaties using Swiss Forms 822/823 relief-at-source or refund procedures. FATCA and the Swiss-AEOI disclosure regime require transparency but do not restrict convertibility. There is no requirement to obtain a capital-export license and no residual limitation on repatriating proceeds of online retail sales.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligations exist under Swiss law. Export receipts arising from the ISIC-4791 online retail of musical instruments and audio equipment may be held indefinitely in any convertible currency, retained in offshore accounts, or converted to CHF at the entity's discretion. There is no prescribed repatriation timeline and no minimum conversion ratio. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the SNB applies when cumulative cross-border flows exceed defined reporting thresholds (customarily CHF 10 million), and even then the filing is informational rather than a currency-control measure.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, with prudential supervision of banks exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) under the Federal Act on the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMASA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary treasury functions; LOW for primary corporate banking. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex can lawfully be used by a Swiss AG for FX conversions, foreign-supplier disbursements, and receiving e-commerce aggregations from Stripe and PayPal in EUR, USD, GBP, or CNY. However, EMIs cannot serve as the exclusive banking backbone because: (i) they lack direct access to the Swiss SIC/SIC5 CHF clearing rail, making Swiss payroll, AHV social-security contributions, and Swiss VAT (MWST) payments impracticable; (ii) FINMA Circular 2016/07 and the Anti-Money Laundering Act require any institution holding client funds with Swiss nexus to be licensed as a Swiss financial intermediary, so an unauthorized EMI cannot legally provide inward payment collection from Swiss card acquirers on behalf of the AG; and (iii) the notary's capital confirmation for the statutory CHF 100,000 minimum capital (at least CHF 50,000 paid in) must be issued by a Swiss bank regulated under the Federal Banking Act, not by an EMI. The optimal blueprint is therefore an anchor relationship with a Swiss commercial bank backed by supplementary EMI accounts for currency corridors only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the statutory CHF 100,000 initial capital: the shareholder wires at least CHF 50,000 into a notarial escrow account (Sperrkonto) held at a Swiss banking counterparty, using SWIFT MT103 from the source bank with Remittance Information (field 70) reading 'Kapitalerhoehung / Gesellschaft CH_AG' plus the notary engagement reference. The internationally preferred purpose-classification is ISO 20022 Purpose Code INTC (intra-company/capital infusion) or the narrative 'equity contribution' where free-text is permitted. No SNB approval is required. Banks should be given one business day's notice to effect a spot CHF/USD or CHF/EUR conversion before value date so that the credit lands in CHF to meet the paid-in-capital threshold. The notary relies on a bank confirmation letter (Kontobestätigung) issued to the company; after public notarization of the deed of incorporation and commercial-registry entry, the escrow block is lifted and funds are moved to the operational treasury account via SIC5 internal transfer. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary treasury functions; LOW for primary corporate banking. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex can lawfully be used by a Swiss AG for FX conversions, foreign-supplier disbursements, and receiving e-commerce aggregations from Stripe and PayPal in EUR, USD, GBP, or CNY. However, EMIs cannot serve as the exclusive banking backbone because: (i) they lack direct access to the Swiss SIC/SIC5 CHF clearing rail, making Swiss payroll, AHV social-security contributions, and Swiss VAT (MWST) payments impracticable; (ii) FINMA Circular 2016/07 and the Anti-Money Laundering Act require any institution holding client funds with Swiss nexus to be licensed as a Swiss financial intermediary, so an unauthorized EMI cannot legally provide inward payment collection from Swiss card acquirers on behalf of the AG; and (iii) the notary's capital confirmation for the statutory CHF 100,000 minimum capital (at least CHF 50,000 paid in) must be issued by a Swiss bank regulated under the Federal Banking Act, not by an EMI. The optimal blueprint is therefore an anchor relationship with a Swiss commercial bank backed by supplementary EMI accounts for currency corridors only.
