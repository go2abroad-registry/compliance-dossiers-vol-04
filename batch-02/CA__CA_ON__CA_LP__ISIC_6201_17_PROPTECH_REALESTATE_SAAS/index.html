Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topic/registering-your-business.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming or carrying on business through a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mailing Form RC1, Business Number Registration. The LP itself must be registered for GST/HST if its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold (CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters), and may optionally register if below. Each partner's share of partnership income is reportable by the partners, not the LP, but the LP must file its own GST/HST return and may be required to provide partner identification. Non-resident partners without other Canadian reporting obligations may need an individual or business BN for withholding and reporting purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity forming or carrying on business through a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mailing Form RC1, Business Number Registration. The LP itself must be registered for GST/HST if its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold (CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters), and may optionally register if below. Non-resident partners without other Canadian reporting obligations may need an individual or business BN for withholding and reporting purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership is generally not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes; it is a pass-through vehicle. No check-the-box election is available for federal income tax, but the partnership may elect under subsection 96(3) to compute income on a fiscal-period basis. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a separate 'person' and may elect under section 177 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain closely related members as a single GST/HST registrant. Hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.4 and draft hybrid mismatch legislation may disallow certain partnership deductions or impose withholding adjustments where there is a hybrid mismatch arising from foreign entity classification differences; an entity may file prescribed forms to elect to align its Canadian and foreign tax characterization in limited circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident businesses, the same threshold applies based on supplies attributable to carrying on business in Canada, with special rules for online platform operators

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act ( exports of goods ) and section 7 of Part V ( services supplied to non-residents ) for supplies made by a GST/HST registrant. For software-as-a-service PropTech services, the supply must be made to a non-resident person, the recipient must be outside Canada, and the supply must not be made to a resident of Canada, and must not be performed in Canada to a significant extent. If the SaaS is delivered over the internet to a non-resident customer and no Canadian-resident employee or contractor performs services in Canada for the recipient, zero-rating is generally available. For goods, zero-rating requires physical export pursuant to a contract of carriage with DCS, proof of export, and retention of export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or equivalent ACE/AES data)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment record demonstrating foreign-currency payment
  • Contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Canada Border Services Agency export proof documentation
  • Supporting records of digital service delivery and IP address / geolocation evidence

No federal real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists. CRA does not require a specific e-invoicing format; taxpayers may issue electronic invoices in XML, EDI, or PDF. Effective April 2024, CRA requires reporting of certain GST/HST and payroll information through digital channels, but there is no fiscal stamping or real-time tax clearance requirement. Quebec has announced mandatory electronic invoicing for QST purposes under Revenu Québec with phased implementation beginning for large businesses in 2026, but Ontario does not impose a provincial e-invoicing requirement.

CRA may reassess permitted periods (usually four years, or longer for taxpayers using the voluntary disclosure program), deny zero-rating where export substantiation is deficient, and recover unpaid GST/HST plus interest and penalties under sections 280 to 285 of the Excise Tax Act. False or negligent statements on GST/HST returns attract penalties of up to 25% of the tax or amounts claimed, in addition to interest charges. Willful evasion under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act can lead to penalties of up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks include the reassessment of the full net tax and suspension or revocation of registrations. CRA audit practice in the digital-services sector requires robust evidence of non-resident status, payment routing from foreign banks, and absence of Canadian consumption benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no statutory approvals for payment or repatriation of profits, dividends, partnership distributions, interest, royalties, or capital subscriptions. Capital flows are subject only to general corporate, income-tax withholding, and sanctions/AML compliance. A non-resident limited partner's profits/distributions are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, reducible by applicable income tax treaty, unless allocated through a Canadian partnership and filed under Part I/Part XIV as relevant. Transactions at or above CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC as applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no prescribed conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters and SaaS providers may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian or foreign-denominated accounts, and convert when commercially required. Documentary retention for Income Tax Act audits, CBSA customs declarations for goods, and FINTRAC records for transactions over the reporting threshold remains mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Canada has no designated exchange-control authority and does not administer foreign exchange surrender or licensing requirements. Prudential banking supervision is exercised by OSFI; anti-money-laundering transaction monitoring is supervised by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, subject to Canadian licensing. An Ontario fintech or electronic money institution is not recognized as a bank; it must register or be licensed as a money-services business/payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada) and maintain a FINTRAC AML program. A pure offshore digital EMI without a Canadian registration, clearing agent, or OSFI-recognized banking relationship has low viability for direct CAD settlement and is generally restricted from accessing the Canadian automated clearing system, Interac, and LVTS/Real-Time Rail. The recommended route is a tier-1 Canadian bank account for CAD settlement and a foreign-currency sub-account or EMI for non-CAD disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be wired to the dedicated LP capital-escrow account using SWIFT MT103 (gpi) with the beneficiary naming the Ontario LP and the field-70 remittance information stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_LP' and the capital-call reference. US-dollar subscriptions from US investors will generally be cleared through the Canadian bank's US-dollar correspondent via Fedwire or via SWIFT to the bank's New York branch; CAD wires clear through the Canadian Payments Association LVTS. Any conversion of USD to CAD must be executed at the bank's spot or forward rate, with the buy/sell currency, amount, and settling instruction recorded for the account's FX file and tax audit trail.For settlement of software licensing, procurement, and PropTech vendor obligations, the preferred clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103/MT202 gpi network for cross-border wires, with commercial invoices and PO numbers embedded in fields 70/72. For physical equipment transactions requiring performance and payment security, a commercial letter of credit should be issued by the Canadian bank under SWIFT MT700 and settled by MT202/MT103 on presentation of compliant documents.
