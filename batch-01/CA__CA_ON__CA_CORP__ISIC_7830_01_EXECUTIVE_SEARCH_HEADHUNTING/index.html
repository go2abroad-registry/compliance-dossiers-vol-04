Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal tax administration portal for Ontario corporate income tax and GST/HST: 'My Business Account' (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/business-registration-online.html).

E-government filing portal

The statutory TIN is the nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts (RC0001 corporate income tax; RT0001 GST/HST; RP0001 payroll). New Ontario business corporations incorporate under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act followed by extra-provincial registration in Ontario), then obtain the BN via Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; the CRA issues the BN generally within five to fifteen business days. A non-resident foreign corporation carrying on or deemed to carry on business in Canada (including deemed activity under Income Tax Act s. 253) must register for a BN and GST/HST account through the CRA's non-resident registration pathway, submitting a certificate of foreign incorporation or certificate of corporate status, the home-jurisdiction tax ID, and Canadian agent-for-service information where required. The same BN integrates Ontario corporate income tax, Federal Part I tax, GST/HST, and, if applicable, payroll source deductions, all administered by the CRA on behalf of Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Ontario business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Federal Part I corporate income tax and Ontario provincial corporate income tax administered by the CRA; no pass-through or disregarded-entity classification exists for Canadian incorporated entities. Corporation tax residence is determined by central management and control (ITA s. 250); income is computed at the entity level, and tax losses cannot flow through to shareholders. A U.S. LLC shareholder's domestic check-the-box election is not binding on Canadian characterization; the CRA and the Canada-U.S. tax treaty may re-characterize a hybrid LLC for Canadian tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available to an executive-search corporation include: (i) ITA s. 85 corporate rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of eligible property to a corporation; (ii) ITA s. 256 corporate-group tax allocation and loss-consolidation elections; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election treating closely related corporations as a single person so intercompany administrative services are outside GST/HST; (iv) ETA s. 273 joint election for nil-value supplies between closely related registrants; and (v) ITA s. 249.1 election to vary the taxation year with CRA concurrence. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, 94B, and 212.3) apply mandatorily and are not elective; they deny deductions for imported hybrid mismatches involving hybrid entities or hybrid transfers such as interest paid by a Canadian borrower to a U.S. hybrid LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148). An executive-headhunting corporation must register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export services but excluding exempt financial services and supplies of capital property) exceed C$30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current calendar quarter; mandatory registration takes effect on the day the threshold is exceeded, and the registrant must thereafter charge and remit 13% HST on taxable Canadian supplies. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Executive search and headhunting services (ISIC 7830) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 when a registrant supplies the service to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST under Subdivision d of Division V, provided the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Cross-border C-suite search, market mapping, candidate assessment, and success-fee services supplied to non-resident U.S., EU, and Asia-Pacific technology clients therefore qualify for 0% HST even where the recruitment work is physically performed in Canada, because the value and benefit of the service accrue to the non-resident's foreign business (CRA GST/HST Policy P-051). Where the non-resident client is itself a GST/HST registrant, the supply is taxable in Canada and the place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 143.2 determine the applicable HST rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration - not applicable to service exports; executive search is an intangible service for which no Canada Customs export declaration, AES filing, or CAED document is required; the commercial invoice and signed contract serve as the export evidence equivalent.
  • Bill of Lading - not applicable to services; substitute the executed executive-search retainer agreement, statement of work, and candidate-search deliverables evidencing the cross-border supply to the non-resident recipient.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - Canada does not issue an inward-remittance certificate; substitute SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit advices, Wise for Business payment receipts, and corporate bank statements evidencing receipt of the foreign client's retainer and success fees in the registrant's Canadian bank account.

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime. Registrants with annual taxable revenue above C$1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE or EFILE); others may file on paper. Tax invoices must satisfy ETA s. 223 particulars (supplier name and BN, date, description of service, HST rate and amount, or an explicit zero-rated export notation). Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-standard e-invoicing is voluntary; there is no CRA API pre-clearance, no digital stamping, and no immediate transaction transmission requirement.

CRA audit analytics profile executive-search exporters for mismatches between zero-rated export declarations and inbound remittance evidence; where documentation fails, the CRA retroactively revokes zero-rating and assesses 13% HST on the full retainer and success-fee revenue as uncollected tax payable by the registrant out of pocket, plus daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed rate. The normal reassessment window is four years from the return filing date (ETA s. 298); it is extended to six or more years for misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default and is unlimited in cases of fraud. Civil penalties include ETA s. 285(1) (25% penalty on a false statement or omission made knowingly or under gross negligence), the late-filing penalty of 1% of unremitted net tax plus 0.25% per full month up to 12 months, and criminal liability under ETA s. 297 (fines up to C$50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years for wilful evasion). Canada imposes no independent statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline on service exports; the 180-day window reflected in this dossier is a compliance best-practice metric, and prolonged non-repatriation may trigger place-of-supply and permanent-establishment inquiry but is not by itself a separate clawback trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account items. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency, and no advance approval is required for dividend remittances, interest payments, management fees, shareholder loans, or equity capital inflows. Profit repatriation is unrestricted under the federal Exchange Control regulations, which were abandoned in the 1950s. The principal external control gates are the Investment Canada Act review (for foreign non-Canadian acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above statutory thresholds) and Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act for dividends, interest, royalties, and certain service fees, which may be reduced by an applicable tax treaty. No exchange-control moratorium, surrender requirement, or surplus-cash retiming restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory repatriation, surrender, or conversion of export proceeds. Receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained in a Canadian or offshore foreign-currency account indefinitely. There is no statutory timeline for conversion into Canadian dollars, and no export-proceeds settlement ratio is prescribed. The only compliance obligations are transactional recordkeeping, FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers at or above C$10,000, and standard CRA tax reporting based on the tax year in which the proceeds are earned.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central monetary authority and operator of the high-value payment system (Large Value Transfer System). The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates banks. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the financial intelligence unit. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; the Minister of Finance has residual policy authority, but no foreign-exchange approval or licensing regime exists for ordinary cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High practical viability for low-value operational flows only, with specific legal constraints. Registered Canadian Money Services Businesses (MSBs) such as Wise, Payoneer, and Ebury are authorized by FINTRAC as money services businesses and can transmit cross-border B2B retainers under a limited-operating palette. They are, however, not banks under the Bank Act, do not have direct access to the LVTS settlement system, are not members of CDIC, and cannot issue a conventional Canadian business chequing account. Offshore digital EMIs operating outside Canada but seeking to serve a Canadian entity generally require a Canadian MSB registration or partnership with a licensed Canadian bank; otherwise, they violate requisite money-services licensing and payment-clearance rules. For this executive search entity, Wise B2B is acceptable for sub-CAD 50,000 retainers and expense refunds, but the primary operating account and any retainer escrow account should remain at a Schedule I bank. Large invoice settlement and capital movements must run through SWIFT/Canadian clearance rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity or shareholder loans are executed by international SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer from the foreign shareholder's bank to the corporation's Canadian operating account. Canada does not apply a statutory foreign-investment purpose code, but the originator's bank will require a narrative purpose code or description to appear in field 70 of the SWIFT message; examples include 'ACH - Additional Paid-In Capital', 'SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE', or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - CAD INTEREST AT NIL'. For inflows exceeding C$10,000, FINTRAC reporting obligations attach to both the Canadian receiving bank and the originator's bank, and a source-of-funds questionnaire may be invoked. The recipient must ensure the payment is allocated to a designated paid-up capital or shareholder-loan schedule under the corporate ledger, and must be prepared to provide a corporate resolution, a signed subscription agreement, and a written confirmation from a Canadian chartered accountant that the injection does not create a taxable benefit. No exchange approval or economic benefits test applies to capital injection under Canadian foreign-exchange rules.Outbound disbursements for executive search and headhunting fees are settled using the SWIFT MT103 message standard for single customer credit transfers, with the invoicing reference, client identification, and payment amount carried in fields 20, 50, 59, and 70. For interbank funding cover, an MT202 COV is used where the remitting bank does not maintain a direct nostro account with the beneficiary's bank. Commercial letters of credit (documentary LC) are generally unnecessary for services, but may theoretically be structured where a client insists on an LC; in such case, an irrevocable transferable LC issued by a Canadian bank and advised via SWIFT MT700 with a confirmation instruction from a foreign correspondent bank is the appropriate rail. Domestic CAD settlements clear through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and high-value flows over CAD 100,000 through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High practical viability for low-value operational flows only, with specific legal constraints. Registered Canadian Money Services Businesses (MSBs) such as Wise, Payoneer, and Ebury are authorized by FINTRAC as money services businesses and can transmit cross-border B2B retainers under a limited-operating palette. They are, however, not banks under the Bank Act, do not have direct access to the LVTS settlement system, are not members of CDIC, and cannot issue a conventional Canadian business chequing account. Offshore digital EMIs operating outside Canada but seeking to serve a Canadian entity generally require a Canadian MSB registration or partnership with a licensed Canadian bank; otherwise, they violate requisite money-services licensing and payment-clearance rules. For this executive search entity, Wise B2B is acceptable for sub-CAD 50,000 retainers and expense refunds, but the primary operating account and any retainer escrow account should remain at a Schedule I bank. Large invoice settlement and capital movements must run through SWIFT/Canadian clearance rails.
