Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portals: CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online, and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Canadian corporation must first complete federal or provincial incorporation, then apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account through CRA's Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The application must state the Ontario business address, directors, authorized signatory, expected Canadian taxable supplies, and, for non-resident directors, a Canadian resident agent may be required. CRA issues the BN/RT program account, which serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration number, before taxable commercial activity begins.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign-owned Canadian corporation must first complete federal or provincial incorporation, then apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account through CRA's Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The application must state the Ontario business address, directors, authorized signatory, expected Canadian taxable supplies, and, for non-resident directors, a Canadian resident agent may be required. CRA issues the BN/RT program account, which serves as the TIN and GST/HST registration number, before taxable commercial activity begins.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election and no hybrid-mismatch entity election; a federally or provincially incorporated Canadian corporation is categorically taxed as a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Available elections relate to transactions, not entity classification: GST/HST joint elections under sections 155 to 157 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related persons, section 216 and 217 elections for certain non-resident income, and the GST/HST quick method election for small registrants. None of these alter the default corporate entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000; calculated on worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, once the non-resident corporation carries on business in Canada or has a permanent establishment in Ontario. Registration becomes mandatory after exceeding the 30-day or 12-month threshold in a calendar quarter or fiscal year; voluntary registration is available for small suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 1, 2 and 5 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a specialty tea salon may zero-rate supplies of tea leaves, tea products, and ceremonial goods only when the goods are physically exported by the supplier or on behalf of the customer as part of the supply, ordinarily within a reasonable time, and the supplier retains clear documentary proof that the goods left Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI if the service is performed in Canada but delivered to a non-resident, or where the supply relates to tangible personal property exported from Canada. On-premises tea service and lounge sales remain subject to Ontario HST at the standard rate.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / eDeclaration for shipments above the legal reporting threshold)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada
  • Customs/external transport record (carrier proof of pick-up and foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign exchange transfer record evidencing final payment from the foreign buyer
  • CBSA release documents or transportation broker reports on vessel/aircraft departure

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval clearance, or digital stamping is mandated in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed tax invoices in paper or electronic form and retain them for audit. Most registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or CRA-certified software; integrated filers may use CRA's GST/HST Web Services API or XML formats. Invoices are not filed with the CRA before issuance.

CRA may disallow zero-rating if export documentation is incomplete or inconsistent; the full 13% HST is then reassessed on the exporter, with input tax credits clawed back and refunds reversed. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act can include a 6% assessable error amount plus monthly arrears interest, 25% gross negligence penalties, and summary conviction or indictable fines for fraudulent statements, with a retrospective reassessment period extended beyond four years where a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation that is negligent or amounting to fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no foreign exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittances. Dividends, management fees, and royalties may be repatriated freely, subject to ordinary corporate authorization, CRA non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, and the bank's own KYC/AML verification of underlying transaction validity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists in Canada. Export receipts for tea inventory, ceramics, or franchise/royalty inflows may be retained in foreign currency or repatriated without central bank approval. The only obligations are commercial invoicing, customs declarations through the CBSA, and FINTRAC reporting for transactions over the statutory threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of Schedule I banks by OSFI; AML/ATF supervision by FINTRAC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Canada has no dedicated 'EMI' banking license. A foreign fintech/EMI offering digital accounts is legally not a substitute for a Schedule I bank; it cannot take deposits unless licensed under the Bank Act. Such an EMI may operate as a money services business registered with FINTRAC, but for cross-border settlement, FX conversion, and Ontario treasury operations, a Schedule I bank remains the primary clearing entity. Foreign EMIs may be acceptable for low-value sales channels, but they create additional compliance risk and are not suitable for capital escrow or trade LC disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital injections are cleared over the SWIFT network into a Schedule I bank corporate account. No exchange control permit is required. The remitter should use SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary as the Ontario entity, and Field 70 should clearly state 'shareholder capital injection' or 'working capital loan.' The receiving bank will request supporting documents such as the board resolution, shareholder loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that the transaction does not trigger investment review thresholds under the Investment Canada Act. For a specialty tea salon, a direct capital injection below the review threshold is generally lawful and immediately available after standard KYC verification.Trade disbursements for imported loose-leaf tea, premium ceramics, or franchise payments should use SWIFT MT103 for lower-value settlement, commercial letters of credit through a Schedule I Canadian bank for high-value inventory purchases, and cross-border card rails for small e-commerce payments. Payment instructions must include the underlying commercial invoice and, where applicable, a clear description of goods. Canada does not require a foreign exchange permit, but the paying bank will screen for sanctions and the beneficiary must be an authorized payee.
