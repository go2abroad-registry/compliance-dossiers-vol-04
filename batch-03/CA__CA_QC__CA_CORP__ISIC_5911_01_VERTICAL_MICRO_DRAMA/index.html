Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed; incorporation is permitted with nil or nominal capital. No escrow or deposit account requirement applies to standard business corporations; the board may fix the consideration for issued shares at its sole discretion.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required from the local municipal fire service under the Fire Safety Act (CQLR c S-3.4) and the Building Act (CQLR c B-1.1); building occupancy and structural load clearance must comply with the Construction Code. Workplace health and safety registration with the CNESST is mandatory when employees are engaged, including a prevention program for workforces of 20 or more. Electrical and ventilation inspections must be completed pursuant to the Construction Code before the premises are occupied.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), official portal https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; for Quebec provincial taxation, Revenu Québec, official portal https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation (including a vertical micro-drama producer/distributor) must first either complete federal/provincial incorporation or extra-provincial registration in Quebec. Thereafter: (1) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, indicating 'GST/HST' as the required program account; (2) where total taxable supplies from carrying on business in Canada exceed the small-supplier threshold, the corporation must register for GST/HST through the same BRO portal or CRA account; (3) for Quebec, the same entity must register for the Quebec Sales Tax (QST) with Revenu Québec by using the 'Registrations for a business' module in Revenu Québec's online portal or by filing the applicable registration form (Form LM-1 if not already assigned a Quebec enterprise number to the sole establishment); (4) obtain the QST account identifier linked to the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (5) update the registrations within 30 days of any material change in legal name, address, ownership, or business activity. Voluntary registration is available for non-residents carrying on business in Canada even below the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer resident in Canada, taxed under the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; it is not a pass-through entity, and no entity-level classification election is available to reclassify it as a flow-through vehicle.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or entity-classification election akin to the U.S. regime. A corporation is always a separate taxable person. Hybrid mismatch limitation rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4, 94.1–94.3) apply to certain foreign entity structures, and Quebec adopts equivalent tax characterization principles. Available elections relevant to the entity include capital cost allowance elections, fiscal-period elections for wholly-owned Canadian corporations, and associated-corporate allocations; none alter the fundamental entity-level tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person must register for GST/HST if their total taxable supplies in Canada (excluding zero-rated and financial supplies) are more than CAD 30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or the aggregate of the previous four consecutive calendar quarters. Revenu Québec applies a parallel CAD 30,000 threshold for QST registration. Supplies made to non-residents for export are generally zero-rated and may be excluded for threshold purposes in certain circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST purposes, a supply of services made by a Canadian resident to a non-resident is zero-rated where the service is supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada and the service is not received in or performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is supplied. Digital micro-drama deliveries to foreign subscribers through an app are generally zero-rated if the non-resident status of the subscriber is evidenced and the service is delivered outside Canada. For QST, Revenu Québec applies a materially equivalent zero-rating rule for exported services and intangible personal property supplied to non-residents. The supply of goods exported from Canada is zero-rated upon evidence of physical export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency A/CASPER export declaration or Form B13) for physical goods movement
  • Commercial invoice and Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment out of Canada, where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer / SWIFT payment evidence demonstrating receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Electronic evidence of non-resident customer status for digital services, including IP address, billing address, contractual terms, and non-resident declaration
  • App-store settlement reports and subscriber records evidencing the source of revenue abroad

Canada has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing regime as of the current tax roadmap. Electronic invoices and records are acceptable if legible and supported by originals; no digital stamp or API clearance is required. Quebec imposes standard billing data requirements under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax, but there is no pre-clearance or continuous transaction control mandate. This is relevant to cross-border digital platform payments such as Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe.

CRA and Revenu Québec may audit zero-rated export claims indefinitely where fraud is alleged; the normal reassessment period is four years, with longer periods for gross negligence or misrepresentation. If zero-rating is denied, the taxpayer is liable for uncollected GST/HST/QST, interest, and penalties. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act can reach up to 25% of the amount at issue or CDN 25,000, and criminal penalties may include imprisonment. Revenu Québec imposes parallel penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act, including monetary fines and penal remedies. Documentary deficiency, circular invoicing, or inadequate non-resident verification will trigger retroactive clawbacks and denial of export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible, and Canada makes no operational distinction between capital-account and current-account transactions for FX purposes. Dividends, profit remittances, interest, royalties, management fees, intercompany loan repayments and shareholder capital repatriation to non-residents are not subject to central-bank approval, exchange licensing, compulsory conversion, repatriation quotas or foreign-investment FX screening. The remaining legal hurdles for a Quebec business corporation are the Part XIII non-resident withholding tax regime under the Income Tax Act (Canada), the Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds where a share subscription results in a new Canadian business or acquisition of control, and FINTRAC cross-border wire and large-cash transaction reporting. The practical liquidity hurdle for the vertical-micro-drama entity is therefore bank-channel policy on USD-denominated Apple IAP, Google Play Billing and Stripe receivable flows, not any statutory restriction on outward conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts or foreign service revenues. A Quebec business corporation may lawfully retain USD and other foreign currency indefinitely in onshore or offshore operating accounts without central-bank approval. Treasury discipline is limited to recognition of unrealized and realized foreign-exchange gains/losses under IFRS and the Income Tax Act, disclosure of foreign-currency assets on financial statements, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting where wire transfers individually meet or exceed CAD 10,000. No conversion is required before paying suppliers, financing production, or settling distribution profit-share obligations worldwide.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), headquartered in Ottawa, is the central monetary and currency-issuance authority for the Canadian dollar (CAD). Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control statute; prudential regulation of federally chartered banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT supervision plus financial-intelligence reporting is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Quebec-incorporated entities are also subject to the civil-law corporate requirements of the Companies Act (Quebec) as administered by the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as an auxiliary treasury rail and Low as the primary treasury solution. Canadian federal law does not issue the EEA-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence, and deposit-taking in Canada is reserved to banks, trust companies, and loan corporations regulated by OSFI or provincial regulators. Offshore digital EMIs (for example entities holding UK FCA, Lithuanian, Singaporean or Hong Kong licences) may be used operationally for vendor payouts and exotic-corridor settlements, but they cannot lawfully accept Canadian deposits, originate Canadian payroll, or function as the statutory capital-escrow account provider. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), payment service providers carrying on retail payment activities in Canada are required to be registered with the Bank of Canada, and foreign PSPs that hold Canadian funds are increasingly captured by that regime. For the vertical-micro-drama treasury, the optimal architecture is a Tier-1 Canadian bank as the primary banking hub, a Stripe Treasury or Stripe payout rail as the merchant settlement layer, and an offshore EMI only as a narrow complement for jurisdictions with no Canadian-bank correspondent presence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injections by shareholders, the compliant routing is a high-value SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder bank to the entity designated capital escrow or CAD operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, denominated in CAD to preserve traceability and eliminate conversion at receipt. The wire must carry the ISO 20022 Category Purpose Code EQUI or an equivalent equity-financing code, and the remittance information must reference the share-subscription agreement, board resolution, and the share-count issued. The receiving bank will require the executed subscription agreement, corporate minutes confirming issuance, the completed register of directors and directorial residency, and the UBO register for FINTRAC-compliant identity verification. Wire transfers meeting or exceeding CAD 10,000 must not be structured in tranches to remain below large-transaction reporting thresholds; the company should preserve full auditability. Recommended practice is a segregated capital-escrow sub-account so that equity sources, share-issuance documents, and the eventual transfer to the USD operating account are documented in a clear capital-introduction audit trail. Concurrently, operating inflows from Apple IAP, Google Play Billing and Stripe should be channeled to the USD operating account, and intercompany or intra-treasury sweeps between CAD and USD accounts should be executed only with internal FX tickets for transparent pricing.Outbound trade disbursements will consist of USD and key content-market currencies (EUR, KRW, INR, JPY, BRL) paid to production studios, licensing agents, ad-tech platforms, local distribution partners and freight or compliance vendors. The primary clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for vendor payments, royalties, licensing advances and profit-share settlements; (b) SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover payments where the beneficiary institution requires an intermediary cover; (c) documentary letters of credit issued or confirmed under UCP600 for milestone-based production financing against delivery of master recording files or launch documentation; and (d) standby letters of credit serving as performance guarantees to app-store or distribution partners. Canadian-dollar disbursements settle through Lynx, the high-value payment system administered by Payments Canada, while USD elective legs settle through CHIPS or Fedwire via the Canadian relationship bank U.S. branch or correspondent.
