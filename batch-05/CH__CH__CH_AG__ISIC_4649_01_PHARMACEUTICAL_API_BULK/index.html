Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), federal VAT and direct tax authority; official portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT e-filing and registration portal: eservices.estyv.admin.ch; Cantonal Tax Authority of Zug (for Zug-registered AG) for cantonal/communal income and capital tax; Federal Customs Administration (FOCBS/BAZG/Douane) for import VAT, export declarations and e-dec customs clearance; UID register via Federal Office for the Commercial Register (Zefix).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Swiss AG must: (1) be incorporated and registered in the Commercial Register of the company's canton (Zug) with a minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 (20% paid up); upon commercial registration the Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which is the single statutory business tax identifier for all Swiss federal, cantonal and VAT purposes, replacing any separate TIN; (2) register for VAT with the ESTV Main Division for Value Added Tax by filing Form 001 (or Form 003 simplified registration for persons not resident in Switzerland for permit purposes) once the entity's worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 or upon voluntary registration, thereby obtaining the MWST number (MWST/TVA/IVA 9-digit code); (3) obtain a Swissmedic wholesale/distribution authorization for pharmaceutical APIs before engaging in import/export, as the customs and VAT clearance process requires the UID and the pharmaceutical licence; (4) register in the FOCBS e-dec customs portal using the UID as the importer/exporter identifier; (5) open a Swiss bank account for import VAT settlement, export remittance documentary support and SWIFT LC operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the AG is a body corporate with statutory seat/management in Switzerland; subject to federal direct tax, cantonal/communal income tax and capital tax on its total worldwide net profits for residence purposes; foreign business profits of a Swiss-resident company are taxable in Switzerland unless attributable to a foreign permanent establishment or foreign real estate, in which case Switzerland grants an exemption with progression - modified territoriality; no pass-through of the AG's tax attributes to shareholders exists; profits are taxable at the entity level and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to a 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax, reducible or refundable under tax treaties; participation exemption relief applies to qualifying dividends and capital gains on 10%+ participations).

Available election forms

Switzerland has no US check-the-box election; the relevant statutory/administrative elections are: (i) VAT group taxation election (Art. 13 MWSTG) for financially and economically integrated entities, allowing consolidated VAT filing; (ii) participation exemption election (substantiated 10% holding, one-year holding period or 1-year future intent) to render qualifying dividends and capital gains tax-free at federal and cantonal levels; (iii) election to apply the net turnover/input VAT apportionment method or the presumptive input VAT deduction rates for documentation-poor wholesale trades; (iv) advance tax ruling (verbindliche Auskunft) with the Zug Cantonal Tax Authority and ESTV securing treatment on permanent establishment triggers, transfer pricing, anti-hybrid classification and export margin structures; (v) under the OECD MLI anti-hybrid framework, no domestic election is available but the taxpayer must reconcile foreign entity classification mismatches against Swiss transparency rules, with the ability to elect grouping for VAT purposes only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover from taxable supplies (excluding VAT and exempt items), effective since 1 January 2018; the threshold applies to turnover performed in Switzerland for a foreign-owned Swiss AG; for non-resident suppliers of electronic services, telecommunication services and distance-sales of imported goods, mandatory Swiss VAT registration is also triggered by Switzerland-generated turnover exceeding CHF 100,000 (special notification obligation under Art. 11 MWSTG); below the threshold voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Swiss VAT-registered AG may zero-rate (exempt with input VAT deduction) export supplies of pharmaceutical APIs under Art. 23(1)(1) MWSTG where physical goods leave Swiss customs territory and are delivered into a foreign customs territory at the recipient's destination. Eligibility requires: (i) valid Swiss VAT registration and UID identification on the invoice; (ii) actual physical dispatch/transport of the API goods abroad, evidenced by a customs export declaration, transport document and the contractual allocation of risk/title transfer to the foreign buyer; (iii) complete bookkeeping records including the export invoice, packing lists, GMP/Swissmedic export permits and proof of payment retained in Switzerland; (iv) the goods must not be re-imported into Switzerland without a valid import declaration and VAT payment; (v) services ancillary to the exported goods (freight, insurance, handling) qualify for zero-rating only if the principal export supply meets the same evidentiary test; services rendered directly to a non-Swiss recipient in the B2B sector are zero-rated under the recipient-location rule of Art. 18 MWSTG; constructive exports, triangular flows and consignment stock arrangements require a prior ESTV ruling or strict documented chain-sale proof.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (e-dec entry submitted to FOCBS, with export assessment/Ausfuhrnummer and customs registration number confirming the physical exit of the goods)
  • Commercial invoice (in Swiss francs or currency of sale) showing the Swiss exporter's UID and MWST number, description of the API, NET unit price, Incoterms and explicit statement of zero-rating 'Exempt without input deduction' / '0% MWST - Export'
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), CMR/talon de route (road) or multimodal bill evidencing carriage to the foreign destination and the foreign consignee as party of title
  • Swift MT103/MT202 or inward remittance certificate (bank credit advice) evidencing inward FX transfer of the export proceeds within the 180-day documentary period; bank statements of the Zug-registered Swiss AG account
  • Swissmedic export licence/permit and GMP certificate for the API batch, plus the wholesale distribution records of the Swiss AG proving lawful possession and chain of custody

Switzerland imposes no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no real-time tax authority clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing and no digital stamp regime under VAT law. Statutory VAT e-filing (Teletax/ESTV eServices) is mandatory for tax returns and the e-dec electronic customs declaration system of FOCBS is mandatory for exports and imports. Invoices must satisfy the substantive content requirements of Art. 26 MWSTG (supplier identity and UID, recipient identity, type and quantity of goods, countervalue, VAT rate and amount); QR-bills and Swiss e-invoicing standards (eBill/EBICS) are commercially dominant in pharmaceutical wholesale trade but are not government-mandated clearance documents. For export transactions, the customs export declaration is validated by the FOCBS risk engine, but no invoice clearance or advance fiscal approval occurs at the invoice level.

FOCBS and ESTV jointly audit export substantiation; if export proof is missing, incomplete or contradictory, the zero-rating is retrospectively revoked, the full standard VAT rate (8.1%) is re-assessed on the invoice amount, interest of 4.5% p.a. accrues from the original tax period, and the input VAT deduction for the export period is clawed back. Under Art. 96(1) MWSTG intentional VAT evasion attracts a fine of up to 25% of the tax evaded; negligent non-payment or unjustified zero-rating under Art. 96(4) MWSTG attracts a fine of up to 10%; serious fraudulent export refund schemes are prosecuted as criminal tax offences (Art. 186-188 DBG analogously via the MWSTG penal regime, imprisonment up to 3 years in aggravated cases). The statutory reassessment limitation is 10 years for VAT in the presence of a criminal offence and 5 years for ordinary assessments; an exporter whose goods are shown to have been diverted back to a Swiss customer or whose documents were back-dated or fabricated faces a full reversal of all related VAT exemption, forfeiture of input VAT credits, a surtax and the possible suspension of the UID customs privileges. Although Swiss law does not set a statutory FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day period is the documented banking/SWIFT LC benchmark; exceeding it without explanation is treated by auditors as an indicator of an uncompleted export and triggers the same clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility. No exchange control filings or approvals are required for foreign direct investment, trade payments, or profit repatriation. Capital inflows and outflows are unrestricted under the Swiss Federal Constitution and the Free Trade Act. Profit remittance in the form of dividends or branch profits is legally unrestricted, but a 35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) applies at source; relief or refund is available under double tax treaties upon proof of beneficial ownership. The principal hurdle is documentary, not regulatory: the distributing entity must evidence the dividend is paid out of taxed earnings and the recipient is the beneficial owner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export receipts in Switzerland. Exporters may keep proceeds in any convertible currency, hold foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, and settle trade obligations freely. Commercial records, VAT filings, and Swiss customs declarations must separately document export transactions for statistical and tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-low. An offshore digital EMI cannot offer core treasury banking to a Swiss AG without a FINMA-recognised banking or financial technology sandbox licence. The Swiss legal framework does not recognise the UK-style e-money institution for corporate transaction accounts; a foreign EMI may provide ancillary payment services only through a licensed Swiss bank or a licensed fintech with a banking licence (FinIA / FinSA). For a high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale entity, reliance on offshore EMIs is undesirable for documentary credits and large-value settlements. A domestic tier-1 bank is the viable primary rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal statutory corporate income tax rate; plus cantonal and communal taxes. Aggregate headline rate for a Swiss AG equals approx. 11.9% to 21.0% depending on the registered office.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer); reduced to 0% under most DTA participation articles when the beneficiary is resident in treaty jurisdiction, remains final for non-residents absent treaty relief.

Cantonal deadline generally six months after the fiscal-year end; a 31 December financial year normally must be filed no later than 30 June of the following year; extensions are available on written application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss law sets no explicit CHF threshold for a Local File; arm's-length documentation under OECD TPG and Art. 58 of the Swiss Federal Act on Direct Taxation must be prepared for controlled transactions whenever material. CbCR and Master File documentation become mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds CHF 900 million.

Economic substance scope: The Swiss economic-substance doctrine is applied through statutory residence tests (registered office or central administration in Switzerland) and through anti-abuse provisions in DTA/tax-privilege practice. A wholesale AG selling pharmaceutical APIs must show local offices, employees, board decision-making, and substantive business assets; entities lacking substance are ineligible for cantonal principal-company/holding relief or treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as non-resident.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false corporate return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment and interest; tax-evasion penalty up to the tax evaded, increased to up to 300% for deliberate concealment; tax-fraud provision under DBG carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a criminal fine; repeated violations may lead to suspension of the wholesale pharmaceutical licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve false returns risk joint liability, criminal prosecution, asset seizure/restitution, imprisonment, director disqualification, and travel-bans/flight-risk measures under Swiss criminal procedure.
Shell company / artificial substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; cancellation of tax rulings and favourable cantonal status; reclassification of the Swiss AG as management-shy, with allocation of profits to foreign principal and mandatory withholding-tax collection; penalties for providing false information to tax administration.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for unpaid corporate tax under veil-piercing where the corporation is undercapitalized and substance is illusionary; criminal prosecution for bankruptcy/wilful mismanagement and possible passport seizure or international travel ban where the board misleads creditors and tax authorities.
AML / sanctions exposure in pharmaceutical API trade

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering Act administrative fines up to CHF 500,000, blocking/freezing of assets by Swiss authorities, criminal penalties under Swiss Criminal Code Art. 305bis/305ter (up to 5 years imprisonment for money laundering and 3 years for lack of diligence), and possible withdrawal of the statutory wholesale trading permit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted, fined and imprisoned; there is personal confiscation of illicit financial gains; they may be barred from serving on boards or as beneficial owners in financial-registries; cross-border cooperation (MLA/Interpol) exposes them to extradition and travel restrictions.
Transfer pricing / arm's length violation

Statutory sanction: Swiss tax administration adjusts taxable income to arm's length under Art. 58 DBG, imposes late interest and, in abusive designs, applies anti-abuse penalty surcharge; cross-border adjustments may provoke double taxation; failure to deliver required TP documentation may result in evidentiary burden reversal and penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors signatory to deficient transfer-pricing policies can be held personally liable in restitution claims from the tax authority where corporate insolvency prevents payment; personal responsibility for civil damages and, on proof of deliberate false documentation, the same imprisonment/fine exposure as tax fraud.
