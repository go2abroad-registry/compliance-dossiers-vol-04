Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; CRA My Business Account portal for GST/HST and corporate income tax; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM Client Portal for customs (RM) accounts.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident sulfur trading entity: (1) Confirm whether the corporation "carries on business in Canada" under ITA s. 253 (deemed carrying on business includes soliciting orders or offering goods for sale in Canada through employees or agents) and whether a permanent establishment exists. (2) Obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1; the BN is the single statutory business identifier used for income tax, GST/HST, payroll and import/export accounts. (3) Register for the GST/HST (RT) program account; registration is mandatory for any person making taxable supplies in Canada other than a small supplier; a non-resident small supplier without a Canadian permanent establishment is not required to register but may elect to do so under ETA s. 242 to recover input tax credits. (4) Open the CBSA import/export (RM) account through BRO or the CARM Client Portal. (5) If the corporation creates an Ontario establishment, file extra-provincial registration through the Ontario Business Registry; no separate Ontario corporate income tax return exists (federal-provincial single corporate tax filing), but Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) registration applies if employees are located in Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Canada; all Business Corporations are taxed at the corporate level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA). Available statutory elections: (i) functional currency election under ITA s. 261 (USD reporting); (ii) closely related corporations election under ETA s. 156 (supplies between 90% related corporations deemed made for nil consideration for GST/HST); (iii) non-resident election under ETA s. 242 (deemed registration for input tax credit recovery); (iv) common GST/HST filing period and refund election. Hybrid mismatch results are mandatory under ITA ss. 18.2/18.21 and cannot be disapplied by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the four most recent calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under ETA s. 123(1)/148); non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Canada must register unless they are non-resident small suppliers without a Canadian permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of granular elemental sulfur (HS 2503) exported by the supplier are zero-rated (0% HST) under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA) as tangible personal property exported pursuant to the contract of sale. Conditions: (a) the supply is a sale of goods to a non-resident purchaser; (b) the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or on the supplier's account as part of the supply within a reasonable time; (c) the supplier retains prescribed export documentation evidencing physical export; (d) freight and brokerage services supplied to a non-resident for the exported cargo are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the services relate to goods outside Canada or are performed for a non-resident customer. Valid zero-rating preserves full input tax credit recovery on acquisition costs, inland freight, marine freight, and brokerage commission inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CERS electronic export declaration (formerly Form B13A) filed with CBSA where the fair market value of the export exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled; the CERS confirmation number is the primary export evidence.
  • Bill of Lading — original negotiable ocean through bill of lading evidencing that the granular sulfur was laden on board an ocean vessel at a Canadian port, consigned to the foreign buyer or their order, and discharged outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank-issued certificate or SWIFT payment confirmation (LC settlement MT-202, bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds; for CAD-denominated settlement, a bank statement showing receipt under the letter of credit is acceptable.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list — matching HS 2503, quantity, weight and Incoterms (CIF/C&F), together with the sales contract and charter party or freight contract, retained in the CRA GST/HST books and records.

No real-time tax authority clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file and remit GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE). E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA is aligned with the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing standard. Export transactions must be reported electronically to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); import/export accounting is processed through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform.

CRA applies the ETA s. 169(4) evidence rules at audit; failure to produce the CERS export declaration and transport documents within the reasonable-time window causes a retrospective clawback of the zero-rating, with HST reassessed at 13% on the full consideration, compounding daily arrears interest (ETA s. 280) and remittance-failure penalties of 5% (first offence) and 10% (subsequent offence) under ETA s. 280. False or grossly negligent claims attract a false-statement penalty of up to 25% of the tax sought to be avoided (ETA s. 285) and, where income tax filings are also affected, a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2)). Criminal tax evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327 may result in fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment of up to five years, plus permanent loss of zero-rating privileges for the affected account.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account, and Canada has remained in uninterrupted compliance with IMF Article VIII since 1952. No exchange-control approval is required for profit remittances, dividends, royalties, interest, or repayment of shareholder loans. The only practical frictions are documentary: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced) and FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; neither constitutes a convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender certificate for export receipts. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained offshore or in CAD/USD bank accounts at the exporter's discretion. Export compliance is limited to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS): goods exported from Canada valued at CAD 2,000 or more (CAD 2,500 or more for shipments to the United States) must be reported using a valid exporter Business Number (BN) and accurate export declarations/HS codes; no mandatory FX conversion applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority responsible for currency issuance and monetary policy, but Canada operates no central-bank exchange-control licensing or approval regime. Prudential supervision of banking institutions rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and large-value transaction reporting rest with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); derivatives and ISDA market conduct are overseen by provincial securities regulators, including the Ontario Securities Commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payments, Low for primary treasury clearing. Canada does not recognise a dedicated 'EMI' licence; payment fintechs operate as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, once fully in force, will require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. MSB/PSP status does not permit deposit-taking, lending, or direct membership in Payments Canada clearing. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise or Airwallex) may provide FX conversion and bulk payment execution through a Canadian correspondent, but the CA-ON entity's primary treasury account should be held at a Schedule I bank to guarantee settlement finality, commercial LC issuance, payroll configuration, and Canada Revenue Agency tax remittance routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan proceeds are routed as a straightforward SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or UBO account to the Ontario corporation's operational account at a Schedule I bank. The MT103 narrative and purpose-code field should state 'Share subscription / paid-in capital' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement'; Canada does not maintain a statutory purpose-code list, so the bank relies on the stated reference and underlying documentation. The bank's compliance team must receive: (i) the corporate resolution authorising share issuance or the loan; (ii) a subscription agreement reflecting the capital amount; (iii) a certified copy of the CBCA/OBCA securities register entry; and (iv) for loans, a signed shareholder loan agreement with interest and repayment terms. Single electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more automatically trigger FINTRAC large-value reporting, so the compliance narrative must be retained in the client file. After receipt, the share certificate must be issued and the CBCA/OBCA securities and beneficial-ownership registers updated.Trade settlements for bulk sulfur purchases, freight, stevedoring, port charges, and demurrage are cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value real-time gross settlement (successor to LVTS) for CAD payments, alongside ACSS for batches and cheques; USD payments flow through the bank's US correspondent via Fedwire/CHIPS. Cross-border disbursements use SWIFT MT103 for open-account high-value transfers and irrevocable documentary/commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 and reimbursed under MT103/MT202, with confirmation advised for higher-risk counterparty jurisdictions. The LC documentary package must be drafted on Incoterms 2020 terms and include: commercial invoice, clean-on-board ocean bill of lading, certificate of analysis for granular sulfur purity, independent inspection certificate, certificate of origin, and drafts as required by the issuing bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal statutory Local File or Master File monetary threshold exists in Canada. CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under s.247(4) whenever a taxpayer carries on cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length parties. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more, reported on form RC5107; supporting Local and Master File records must be produced within three months of a CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Ontario does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the central management and control test for corporate residence, treaty benefit limitations under the Multilateral Instrument, and CRA transfer pricing rules. A sulphur ore trading vehicle must maintain real employees, active trading decisions, genuine inventory risk, bank accounts, board minutes, and physical records in Canada; a pure booking or conduit entity without commercial substance will be recharacterized and denied treaty or tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess beyond the normal three-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Civil penalty: 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2). Criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1) and Criminal Code s.380 can result in fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 14 years for fraud exceeding CAD 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in, direct, or assent to fraudulent filings face criminal prosecution and can be personally fined or imprisoned. Where the corporation failed to remit source deductions or Part XIII withholding, directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 regardless of corporate insolvency. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reallocate income to the true economic owner, impose Part XIII withholding on disguised dividends, and assess interest and penalties. Treaty benefits may be denied under the principal purpose test contained in Canada's tax treaties implementing the Multilateral Instrument.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.227.1. If the shell structure is used for fraudulent evasion, directors are exposed to criminal liability and CRA may issue director assessment, garnishment, and travel restrictions.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can trigger FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties, and Criminal Code s.462.31 makes laundering proceeds of crime an indictable offence punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly facilitate laundering transactions are subject to criminal prosecution; personal bank accounts may be frozen, international travel may be restricted, and confiscation orders may attach to personal assets.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Defalcations

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess the Ontario corporation for the full unremitted Part XIII amount plus interest and penalties. Under ITA s.215 and s.226, CRA may enforce collection against the payer corporation's assets. Criminal charges may follow where the failure is willful.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding tax, even if CRA first attempted collection from the corporation. Directors can also be made personally liable for GST/HST collected on domestic taxable supplies and for employee source deductions. Criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment and a conviction-based travel ban.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to the arm's-length price under s.247(2). A transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies where the net adjustment exceeds the statutory threshold, typically CAD 5,000,000, plus late-payment interest. The gross negligence penalty may also apply in cases of culpable conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or implement artificial transfer prices without commercial substance and without documentation can be subject to civil penalties and director liability for unremitted amounts where the adjustment reveals an unpaid withholding obligation. In aggravated cases, participation in transfer pricing fraud can support criminal tax evasion charges.
