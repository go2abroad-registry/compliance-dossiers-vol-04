Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), administered under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) for GST/HST and the Income Tax Act (ITA) for federal income tax, via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account; Ontario Ministry of Finance and Ontario Business Registry (Companies Office) for provincial Limited Partnership registration and Ontario provincial tax programs administered through the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Ontario Limited Partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act through the Ontario Business Registry online filing system and obtain the Ontario LP registration number. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); the BN is a nine-digit number issued to the partnership, with the general partner acting as the applicant. Step 3: Open CRA program accounts as required, including GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and corporation income tax (RC) where applicable. Step 4: Register for GST/HST with the CRA; registration is mandatory where the partnership's worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold in a calendar quarter or the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, and voluntary registration is available below that threshold for persons carrying on business in Canada. Step 5: File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return together with federal and Ontario schedules; partners report their allocated shares on their own income tax returns and are not required to obtain a BN solely by reason of partnership participation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. An Ontario Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under ITA section 96 and files a T5013 information return allocating income, losses, and credits to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a distinct 'person' eligible for registration under ETA section 240, and may elect the closely related corporation election under ETA section 156, elect a non-calendar fiscal period for GST/HST remittance, or use the Quick Method where eligible. Hybrid mismatch and excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules under ITA sections 18.2, 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and deduction/deduction mismatches involving cross-border partnership and hybrid entity arrangements; no elective check-the-box hybrid election exists under Canadian federal tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available for non-residents carrying on business in Canada and for partnerships that have not exceeded the threshold but wish to claim input tax credits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A vendor may claim a 0% HST export rate under ETA Schedule VI, Part V for: (1) goods exported by the vendor or delivered to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada, provided the goods leave Canada before the supply is considered to have been made; (2) services performed wholly or substantially outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7); and (3) services performed on movable property that is subsequently exported (section 8). For the custom plush mascot OEM, supplies of stuffed animals manufactured in Ontario and shipped to foreign customers, including U.S. toy importers, qualify for zero-rating where the partnership retains the export declaration, bill of lading, freight records, commercial invoice, and payment trace. Input tax credits on inputs used to produce exported goods remain fully recoverable. A failure to substantiate the export makes the supply taxable at 13% HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or electronic CAED transmission via the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank records evidencing foreign currency repatriation of the sales proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List referencing 0% HST zero-rated export status
  • Proof of foreign customs clearance or third-party logistics delivery confirmation

Canada and Ontario do not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API-based electronic stamping, or digital seal requirements for invoices. Vendors making taxable supplies in Ontario must issue an invoice containing prescribed particulars under ETA section 221 and the corresponding regulations: supplier name and Business Number, invoice date, description of goods or services, consideration paid, HST amount, and the basis for any zero-rating. Electronic invoices are permissible where they can be reproduced in a readable form and retained for at least six years under ETA section 286. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service Provider Network (PCEIP), which uses PEPPOL-interoperable service providers, is a voluntary federal initiative only and does not constitute a clearance mandate. Quebec's mandatory electronic billing regime for large enterprises does not apply to Ontario.

CRA may deny zero-rating and assess HST at 13% plus compounded arrears interest where export documentation is incomplete. Under ETA section 298(2), an assessment may be reopened outside the normal four-year reassessment period where a zero-rating claim is based on a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. A false statement or omission made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence attracts a penalty under ETA section 282(1) equal to the lesser of CAD 25,000 and the total of CAD 100 plus 25% of the understated tax or improperly claimed refund, applied per claim. Retrospective clawbacks of input tax credits claimed on export inputs and repayment of previously issued refunds are imposed on audit. Wilful tax evasion under ETA section 327 is an indictable offence punishable by a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; the Canada Border Services Agency may also levy administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per false export declaration under the Customs Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible and fully floating. Canada maintains neither exchange controls nor capital-account authorisation for inbound equity capital into an Ontario limited partnership or for outbound trade remittances. Current-account transactions (export/import of plush toys, embroidery machines and raw materials) and capital-account transactions (capital contributions by non-resident limited partners and distribution remittances) are unrestricted, subject to tax withholdings and AML due diligence. Remittance friction is principally tax-driven: a non-resident partner of an Ontario LP carrying on business in Canada is liable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its Canadian-source business income, and the LP may be required to withhold Part XIII tax (generally 25%, reducible by tax treaty) on management fees, interest, rents or royalties paid to non-residents. Distributions of tax-paid Canadian-source operating profits can be remitted freely via SWIFT once the applicable Canada Revenue Agency partnership information return and Part XIII withholding remittances are completed; no central-bank permit or FX conversion approval is required, but the remitting bank must receive a purpose narrative consistent with the partnership agreement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion or holding-period rule for export proceeds. An Ontario LP exporter of plush mascots may retain receipts in CAD, USD or any convertible currency in a domestic or foreign bank account. The Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA), administered by Global Affairs Canada, requires permits only for controlled, military or sanctioned goods; ISIC 3240 plush toys and embroidery machinery are non-controlled. Export compliance is limited to CBSA export declarations for goods valued above C$2,000 (or for controlled goods), accurate commercial invoicing, valuation at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for tax conversion, and the mandatory FINTRAC reporting by banks of international electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more; no mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank and monetary authority) together with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. OSFI regulates federally chartered banks for prudential FX-risk matters and FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT reporting on cross-border funds transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). An offshore digital EMI (e.g., an FCA-authorised EMI in the United Kingdom or an EU-licensed electronic money institution) may lawfully be used by the Ontario LP as an FX collection and disbursement wallet, but such non-bank EMIs cannot accept deposits in Canadian dollars from Canadian residents unless they are registered as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (administered by the Bank of Canada) and as money service businesses with FINTRAC. Cross-border CAD settlement through an offshore EMI must be routed via a Canadian correspondent bank or through equivalent Platform Pay rails; therefore, the LP should maintain its primary operating and escrow accounts with a Canadian Schedule I bank to access Lynx/ACH clearing, trade finance and CAD/USD banking services. PayPal Business and Stripe are viable Platform Pay rails for low-risk e-commerce because the AML risk tier is standard/low, but the LP must present business-licence and beneficial-ownership records and reconcile platform payouts monthly to avoid treatment as third-party payment processing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident limited partner's capital contribution is a SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 pacs.008 credit to the LP's Canadian operational account, with settlement on Lynx (Payments Canada) for CAD and via SWIFT for foreign currency. The wire must include: (i) the LP legal name exactly as registered; (ii) the general partner or authorised signatory as account beneficiary; (iii) a clear payment purpose, using an accepted bank purpose code such as CREC (capital contribution) or a free-text narrative stating capital injection into Ontario limited partnership; and (iv) the ultimate originator identity to support the bank's FINTRAC large-EFT report if the transfer is C$10,000 or more. The supporting verification package comprises the executed capital-call notice, article references in the LP agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and beneficial-ownership declarations for partners owning 25% or more of the capital; no central-bank approval is required, but the funds must not be routed through sanctioned entities or mixed with trade receivables.Supplier payments for embroidery thread, polyester fabric, filling machinery and shipping settle via SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR) or domestic Lynx/ACH for CAD; recurring platform disbursements may be executed through PayPal/Stripe Business settlement into the operational account. For large confirmed distributor orders, the standard rail is a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued by the buyer's bank and advised/confirmed by a Canadian Schedule I bank, subject to UCP 600, with payment against a full set of transport documents (clean-on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and required Canadian export declarations). CAD/USD drawings are cleared via the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) for USD and via Lynx for CAD; the LP must maintain its CBSA export declarations and Ontario registration in good standing and reconcile all outward wires against corresponding commercial invoices to prevent AML false-positive reviews.Deliverable vanilla FX forward contracts (CAD-USD, CAD-EUR and CAD-CNY) with tenors customarily up to 24 months, documented under the bank's ISDA Master Agreement or domestic FX forward template.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CAD-USD) for matching long-term OEM receivables against machinery-import payables, subject to OSFI margin and risk-management eligibility tests.Plain-vanilla FX options (call/put, purchased or collared) for protecting export order-book margins; options must satisfy the bank's product-suitability assessment for a pass-through partnership and must not be used for speculative uncovered positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only where the hedging leg is a non-convertible or restricted currency (for example, an offshore CNH NDF against emerging-market receivables); because CAD is freely convertible, NDFs referencing CAD are economically unnecessary and are not offered as a standard permissible instrument by Canadian Schedule I banks.
