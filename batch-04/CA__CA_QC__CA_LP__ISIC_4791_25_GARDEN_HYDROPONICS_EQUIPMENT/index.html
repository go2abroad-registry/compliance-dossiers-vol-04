Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - Federal tax administration; principal portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). In Quebec, Revenu Québec administers provincial tax registration and the Quebec Sales Tax (QST).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must first apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT), using Form RC1 or through My Business Account. For Quebec provincial sales tax (QST), the partnership must register with Revenu Québec via Form LM-1-V or the online Quebec business registration portal, obtaining a QST number (ND). Supporting documents typically include: the LP declaration / partnership agreement, foreign registration extract, proof of non-resident status, a valid power of attorney or authorization, banking information, and, where applicable, a Canadian representative for service of process. Registration is mandatory before making taxable supplies unless the small-supplier threshold is not exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must first apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT), using Form RC1 or through My Business Account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; allocations of income and loss are made in accordance with subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act. There is no US-style check-the-box election available. A partner may elect under subsection 97(2) to transfer property to the partnership on a rollover basis. Hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act) restrict the deduction or inclusion treatment of hybrid arrangements involving partnerships and hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (or the current quarter if carrying on commercial activity in Canada); the Quebec QST registration threshold is aligned at CAD 30,000 under the same small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property, including garden hydroponics equipment, is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier sells to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported from Canada as part of the supply, or when delivered to a common carrier for export. The supply must not be conditioned on use in Canada, and documentary proof of export must be obtained. Services directly related to the exported goods (e.g., logistics, installation or assembly outside Canada) may also be zero-rated. Digital supplies delivered electronically to a non-resident purchaser may qualify as zero-rated services when the recipient is outside Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (submitted through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System/eManifest or Form B13A as applicable)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Waybill showing the carrier, consignee, and export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank / payment processor settlement statement evidencing receipt of foreign consideration
  • Commercial Invoice / Sales Contract referencing the exported goods and purchase order
  • Packing list and HS/ISIC classification documentation substantiating the nature of the goods

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime applies in Canada at the federal or Quebec level for standard online retail. Electronic invoices in PDF/EDI formats are valid if they meet the integrity and traceability requirements stated in CRA Information Circular IC 106-6R. Quebec has certain sector-specific digital reporting obligations (e.g., accommodation platforms), but none are applicable to hydroponics equipment e-commerce. Registered businesses must retain all invoicing and export records for at least six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess a zero-rated export within the normal four-year limitation period; the period is unlimited in cases of false statements, misrepresentation, or fraud. A false export zero-rating claim attracts penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act of up to 100% of the net tax deficiency, plus arrears interest. Criminal tax evasion prosecution can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Exporters must retain contemporaneous export evidence for six years; failure to do so triggers reversal of the zero-rating, additional GST/QST assessment, and clawback of input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a liberalized and fully convertible currency regime without exchange controls. Both capital and current account transactions are freely permitted, and there are no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or profits for qualified non-resident investors. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily tax-driven rather than regulatory: dividends or partnership distributions to non-residents are generally subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, typically 25% absent an applicable income tax treaty, with treaty rates frequently reduced to 5%–15% for direct and indirect interests. For a Quebec Limited Partnership, careful characterization of distributions as return of capital versus allocated income is essential to optimize withholding and treaty eligibility. The Bank of Canada does not impose prior approval, surrender, or conversion mandates, but Canadian financial institutions require proper source-of-funds documentation and beneficial ownership disclosure for all cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters are free to hold, use, or remit foreign currency proceeds subject only to standard commercial banking arrangements and FINTRAC threshold reporting. For goods sold online, export documentation such as commercial invoices, customs declarations, and shipping evidence must be retained for tax and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange and anti-money laundering reporting oversight also exercised by the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) can be used as supplementary payment corridors for cross-border receipts and disbursements, but they are not licensed as Canadian banks and cannot provide full domestic clearing or CDIC-insured deposit services. Any entity providing payment services in Canada is typically required to register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Reliance on a non-Canadian EMI as the primary treasury account is discouraged due to limited regulatory comfort, potential KYC friction, and insufficient credit facilities. A dual-rail structure with a Canadian chartered bank as the principal account and an EMI as a settlement accelerator is operationally viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from non-resident partners or parent entities are executed via SWIFT MT103 transfers to the Canadian LP's capital escrow or operational account at a Canadian chartered bank. Reference must clearly state 'Limited Partnership Capital Contribution' and, where applicable, 'Foreign Direct Investment'. Canadian banks may require supporting documents such as the partnership agreement, the board or partner resolution authorizing the capital call, and a declaration of source of funds. No exchange control approval is required, but wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC. For statistical reporting to Statistics Canada, the transaction may be classified as a direct investment equity liability. Settlement is typically in CAD, with conversion from USD or EUR handled at the receiving bank's commercial rate or via a pre-agreed client FX forward.Outbound supplier and trade payments are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent banking transfers, especially for international suppliers. For high-value or repeat bulk purchases, commercial letters of credit (documentary credits confirmed by the Canadian bank) provide secure payment against shipping and inspection documents. Domestic suppliers are settled via EFT/Interac or wire transfer. For cross-border disbursements, the bank will require an underlying invoice, purchase order, and in certain cases a certificate of origin for tariff classification. Currency conversion on outbound payments is executed at spot or via a booked forward, with SWIFT payment details containing the applicable corporate purpose code or descriptive payment narrative aligning with the goods sold and ISIC 4791 classification.Deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts with Canadian chartered banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or restricted emerging-market currenciesCross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term CAD/USD exposurePlain vanilla foreign exchange options (call and put) for downside protection
