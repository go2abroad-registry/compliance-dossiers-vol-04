Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal tax authority for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export program accounts. Digital portal: CRA My Business Account () and Business Registration Online (BRO) used for Business Number (BN) and program-account activation. Ontario Ministry of Finance administers Ontario-specific taxes (e.g., Employer Health Tax), but the 8% Ontario HST component is collected by the CRA through the harmonized return.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Constitute the limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act and register it with the Ontario Business Registry (online filing of Declaration of Limited Partnership). Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a Business Number through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the LP obtains a 9-digit BN and program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) if staff are employed, and RM (import/export) if condiments are exported. Step 3: The designated member (usually the general partner) completes the registration, declaring the Ontario permanent establishment, the nature of the commercial activity (ISIC 5610 full-service dining), and estimated taxable supplies. Step 4: Foreign limited partners do not require a separate GST/HST account for allocations but must have Canadian tax filing capacity and must withhold on any partnership distributions subject to Part XIII tax. Step 5: Open the GST/HST account before the first taxable supply; provide the BN to merchants, acquirers, and suppliers; register with the CBSA for export reporting (CAERS); and, where applicable, obtain a Safe Food for Canadians Act (SFCA) licence and CFIA export certifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (Income Tax Act section 96): no entity-level federal or Ontario income tax is imposed; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income or loss. For GST/HST, the Excise Tax Act subsection 2(1) treats the partnership as a separate 'person' and a 'registrant', enabling the LP to charge 13% HST and claim input tax credits. Income from an Ontario permanent establishment is taxable in Ontario in the hands of partners, including non-resident partners, subject to treaty relief. The LP files an annual T5013 information return.

Available election forms

1) Voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA subsection 240(1.1), allowing a small supplier to register before exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold. 2) ETA section 273 joint election for deemed nil-consideration supplies among closely related registrants (e.g., corporate limited partners and the LP). 3) Income Tax Act subsection 97(2) rollover election permitting tax-deferred contribution of capital property to the partnership. 4) Income Tax Act subsection 96(3) elections regarding fiscal period, accounting method, and deemed year-end on partnership reorganization. 5) Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by legal form, treaty interpretation, and CRA administrative policy, and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 94I to 94O) may deny benefits for cross-border arrangements lacking commercial substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full IMF Article VIII convertibility applies; the Canadian dollar floats freely and Canada maintains no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. There is no regulatory approval, quota, or conversion quota for repatriating profits, paying cross-border dividends, or settling intercompany balances. Fiscal frictions are the only material hurdles: (i) a non-resident partner's share of the Ontario LP's income is Canadian-source only to the extent the LP carries on business through a Canadian permanent establishment, so the foreign-host restaurant's active earnings are generally taxable in the host jurisdiction rather than in Canada for non-resident partners; (ii) Canadian-resident partners remain taxable on worldwide income and, if the restaurant is held through a foreign corporation, a Foreign Accrual Property Income (FAPI) current inclusion may arise on passive income while distributions out of exempt surplus return tax-free under ITA section 113; (iii) Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reducible) applies to Canadian-source royalties, management fees, interest, and certain partnership distributions characterized as passive Canadian-source amounts. No additional convertibility or profit-remittance licensing applies to the Canadian side.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished mandatory export-proceeds surrender with the dismantling of the Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951. There is no statutory timeline for converting export receipts into CAD, no minimum conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate foreign currency to a Canadian bank. Residual compliance duties: (i) FINTRAC EFT reports are mandatory for every cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the CBSA B13A export declaration must accompany condiment and goods shipments leaving Canada; and (iii) GST/HST and corporate or partnership income reconciliation must be completed for repatriated foreign revenue.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for the administration of the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act and for foreign-exchange policy; prudential banking supervision is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada). FINTRAC, as the financial intelligence unit, supervises AML/CFT compliance and cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting.
