Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (QUE) — Registraire des entreprises; electronic filing portal CLIC at https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca.

Governing statute

Quebec Business Corporations Act (QBCA), CQLR c S-31.1, ss. 5 (incorporation), 8 (articles), 15 (existence), 16 (certificate), 105-109 (directors and officers), and 214-216 (corporate records); implemented together with the Act respecting the legal publicity of sole proprietorships, partnerships and legal persons (CQLR c P-45).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Statuts de constitution) filed electronically through the Registraire's CLIC system, including the automatic registration (immatriculation) of the legal person in the Quebec enterprise register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 29 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow-account requirement under the QBCA. The Articles must authorize at least one class of shares; the board may lawfully fix a nominal consideration (e.g., CAD 1 per common share) or approve payment in property. For banking and franchise operations a commercial opening capital of CAD 1,000–10,000 is recommended.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: obtain a fire safety permit from the local municipal fire service (Service de sécurité incendie), verify occupancy classification and emergency egress standards; Structural Load Inspection: engineering sign-off on mezzanine storage racks, heavy syrup pallet loads, and HVAC equipment anchoring; Occupational Clearance: register as an employer with CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail), complete a workplace risk assessment, affix the 'Avis de conformité' and retain a CNESST prevention program file in the workplace.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow-account requirement under the QBCA. The Articles must authorize at least one class of shares; the board may lawfully fix a nominal consideration (e.g., CAD 1 per common share) or approve payment in property. For banking and franchise operations a commercial opening capital of CAD 1,000–10,000 is recommended.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO/IE) and My Business Account; concurrent Quebec registration with Revenu Québec / Registraire d'entreprises du Québec (REQ) via the CLIC 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' portal for QST, QPIP, CNESST, and provincial payroll accounts.

E-government filing portal

A foreign franchisor establishing a Quebec Business Corporation must: (1) obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) by F.1 submit to CRA via Business Registration Online (www.canada.ca/business-registration) or Form RC1; (2) request a GST/HST 'RT' program account automatically with the BN; (3) file Quebec incorporation/registration with REQ (Form AR-1) and open a Revenu Québec file to obtain a QST account 'Telechargeur' ID; (4) appoint a Canadian resident representative (agent for service) if no place of business in Canada; (5) register for payroll, RDTP, QPIP, and CSST accounts if domestic employees exist; (6) obtain the unique 'NEQ' (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) as the single provincial business identifier aligned with the BN. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must also register under ETA section 240(1) even below the small-supplier threshold in certain cases (e.g., soliciting orders or maintaining an establishment).

Canada taxes worldwide income of resident corporations on a territorial-plus basis with foreign tax credits; no check-the-box pass-through regime exists for corporations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canada/Quebec Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA Part I (Federal 15% general rate) and Quebec Tax Act Part I (11.5% base rate after general reduction), with permanent-establishment apportionment in Quebec. Canada taxes worldwide income of resident corporations on a territorial-plus basis with foreign tax credits; no check-the-box pass-through regime exists for corporations. Quebec is not a designated tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Canada has no federal check-the-box election. Available entity-level statutory elections include: (1) subsection 85(1) and 85(2) rollover elections on incorporation/transfer of property; (2) section 256/256.1 amalgamation election and 88(1) wind-up rollover; (3) subsection 216(1) election by non-residents to compute Canadian rental income tax on a net basis; (4) Part XIV (non-resident Part XII.6) 25% branch tax election; (5) foreign affiliate 'surplus' elections (S.90, 113, 128-131) and FAPI grouping under 91(4); (6) hybrid mismatch structural elections — Canada's EIFEL regime (section 18.2/18.3) denies excessive interest and financing expense where group entities treat the same arrangement inconsistently (dual inclusion/omission), with election to align timing under 18.2(6) for specified entities; (7) GST/HST section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat limited supplies as non-taxable; (8) QST/GST combined filing consistency election and B2B 'net tax' election; (9) section 159 election to reduce withholding tax on a Canadian corporation paying management fees to non-residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148(1)); CAD 30,000 taxable supplies in Canada for QST (TAA s. 375); once exceeded, mandatory registration, with effect from the calendar quarter in which the threshold is exceeded or, for a new supplier, the day the first supply is made. For franchise royalties cross-border: non-resident franchisors licensing to Quebec franchisees must register for GST/HST and QST even below the threshold where they solicit orders, maintain an establishment, or derive Canadian-source royalties (agency/permanent-establishment rules), and must collect QST on royalty installments unless the supply is zero-rated (export) or subject to self-assessment by the Quebec registrant under the reverse-charge for services (TAA s. 87.1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V (s.1) where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a non-resident customer outside Canada and the goods are exported 'as soon as practicable' (in practice within 60 days) by the supplier or at the direction of the supplier via a common carrier for export; processed tea/syrup and franchise boba equipment shipped to US or foreign franchisees qualify if the exporter obtains evidence of export (customs declaration, bills of lading) before the return is filed. Services: zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V (s.7) where the supply is made to a non-resident and the service is not performed in Canada; franchise licensing, royalties, training, marketing support, and supply-chain coordination services supplied to foreign franchisees are zero-rated if the royalty or service is 'in respect of' a business carried on by the non-resident outside Canada and no Canadian situs of performance exists; if part is performed in Canada (e.g., R&D or Canadian franchisee services), apportionment is required. Royalties from foreign franchisees are not subject to QST where the franchise agreement is a genuine export supply, and Revenu Québec applies a similar non-resident + no use in Quebec test. Digital supply of software or cloud-based franchise management systems follows OECD/ETA e-commerce rules: zero-rated when delivered to the foreign franchisee's business address outside Canada and not used by a Quebec establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A / Canada Customs Invoice / GCA Export Declaration or certified carrier exit confirmation; for US-bound goods, the ACE/AES filing accepted as export evidence)
  • Commercial Invoice and Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (showing foreign consignee, Incoterms EXW/FOB, description, quantity, invoice value, country of origin CAN, EORI or US importer number)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or signed proof of FX receipt from the non-resident franchisee (bank SWIFT/MT103, wire confirmation denominated in CAD or foreign currency showing funds credited to the Canadian exporter's account)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or 'valeur fiscale' digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Instead: (1) mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST net-file or Revenu Québec ITGS for registrants filing monthly or above the threshold; (2) pan-Canadian e-invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework (Canada's 'E-invoicing' service, launched by the governments of Quebec and Ontario in 2023) but not a legal mandate; (3) invoice content must satisfy ETA s. 223 and Quebec TAA s. 435 minimum data (supplier name, BN, date, description, GST/QST payable or included, rate); (4) POS terminals in boba outlets must issue receipts showing the GST and QST components; (5) simplified (point-of-sale) QST/GST invoice rules permit combined display only if both taxes are shown when the transaction is taxable; (6) in Quebec, a registrant must issue an invoice within 30 days if requested and preserve records for 6 years (CRA) / 7 years (Revenu Québec).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada operates an open capital account with no quantitative restrictions on foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividends, royalties, or loan repayments. Quebec imposes no provincial capital controls; funds may be remitted freely through chartered banks. Non-resident shareholders face no withholding tax beyond the standard Part XIII rates under the Canada–Quebec tax framework, subject to treaty relief. Profit remittance is accomplished via ordinary wire transfers with proper tax forms (NR4/income declaration) and no prior approval from the Bank of Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts in Canadian Schedule I banks and repatriate earnings at market rates. FINTRAC only requires reporting of cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. The boba tea franchise's syrups and equipment export revenue may be held in USD or CAD without conversion timetable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) acts as the monetary and exchange-rate authority; exchange controls are not administered by the Bank. Foreign-exchange and cross-border transfer monitoring is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and prudential oversight of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). The Canadian dollar is legal tender under the Currency Act, and there is no central foreign-exchange licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital banks are not permitted to carry on the business of banking in Canada unless licensed or chartered under the Bank Act or operating as an authorized foreign bank branch with OSFI approval. Offshore EMIs may offer payment-processing or prepaid-card rails to the Canadian entity only via partnerships with federally regulated Canadian financial institutions or money-services businesses registered with FINTRAC. AML/CFT obligations require EMI transactions to flow through a Canadian clearing partner to access Payments Canada rails. For this low-risk franchise structure, an EMI can serve as a supplemental collection node but not as the primary liquidity account.

Compliant wire transfer protocol for the initial franchise capital escrow from non-resident parent or shareholders: settle via SWIFT MT103 into the Canadian entity's capital escrow account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank. Use Fedwire/CHIPS or SWIFT single-customer-credit-transfer formatting; include a purpose-of-payment code corresponding to 'Foreign Direct Investment in enterprise capital' to satisfy the reporting thresholds under Canada's Balance of Payments reporting to Statistics Canada via the Bank of Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) if above CAD 50,000. Provide a board resolution authorizing the inward transfer, a UBO declaration, and a simple narrative reference 'Franchise capital contribution for ISIC 5610 operation' to reduce bank holds. For sums above CAD 10,000, the bank files an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC. No exchange-control approval is required.
