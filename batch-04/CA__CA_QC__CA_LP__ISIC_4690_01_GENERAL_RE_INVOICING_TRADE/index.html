Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec Enterprise Registrar) — the official state registry for legal publicity of enterprises, accessible through the 'Espace entrepreneurs' e-filing portal under the Ministère de l'Économie, de l'Innovation et de l'Énergie.

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec (CQLR c. CCQ), arts. 2246–2266 (société en commandite / limited partnership); Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c. P-44.1), ss. 1–30, 94–105.1; Regulation respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c. P-44.1, r. 3); Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96 governs federal pass-through taxation. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not apply to a Quebec limited partnership.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation — Déclaration de société en commandite (Declaration of Registration of a Limited Partnership), e-filed with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec and evidenced by the Certificat d'attestation (attestation of registration) and the 10-digit Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Quebec limited partnership; capital is fixed solely by the partnership agreement (contrat de société), and contributions may be money, property, or services. No escrow or capital deposit is mandated. First-year state fees total approximately CAD 126 (CAD 33 registration fee plus CAD 93 annual fee), equivalent to about USD 92.

Funding & unlock path: Standard municipal fire prevention inspection certificate for the office premises (e.g., Service de prévention des incendies de Montréal for a Montreal registered office); compliance with Chapter I of the Quebec Building Code governing structural loads, means of egress, and fire separations for the leased space; registration of the employer with the CNESST through the employer declaration if staff are hired, together with the mandatory orientation and training program prescribed by the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. S-2.1). No special physical clearance applies to a pure re-invoicing operation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and federal income tax administration; Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST), provincial corporate income tax, and payroll duties. Portals: CRA My Business Account; Revenu Québec CLIENT (Services en ligne des entreprises).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST where required, using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or online via CRA My Business Account. For Quebec nexus, the partnership must also register with Revenu Québec through the Registrant Registration Service (formerly Form LM-1) to obtain a QST registration and separate QST number. The LP must appoint a resident agent for service of process when no permanent establishment exists, and provide partnership identification including all general and limited partners. The BN/GST/HST registration must be completed before commencing commercial activity in Canada or within one month after reaching the taxable-supply threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST where required, using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or online via CRA My Business Account. For Quebec nexus, the partnership must also register with Revenu Québec through the Registrant Registration Service (formerly Form LM-1) to obtain a QST registration and separate QST number. The LP must appoint a resident agent for service of process when no permanent establishment exists, and provide partnership identification including all general and limited partners. The BN/GST/HST registration must be completed before commencing commercial activity in Canada or within one month after reaching the taxable-supply threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act. The partnership must file an information return (Form T5013) allocating income/loss to partners. Non-resident partners may elect under the Income Tax Act s.216 to file a return for Canadian-source rental or timber income, but for trading income the standard withholding regime applies. For GST/HST, the partnership or a designated member may elect to register as a group or as a participating partnership under s.178 of the Excise Tax Act. In Quebec, the registrant may elect with a partner to make that partner the QST registrant for the partnership's activities under the Taxation Act (Quebec). Hybrid mismatch elections under Canadian tax law are not available as a distinct election, but treaty-based and applicable domestic allocation rules may mitigate double taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (for both GST/HST and QST small-supplier rules; non-residents may be required to register earlier if carrying on business in Canada).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property that are exported from Canada in accordance with the Excise Tax Act Schedule IX Part V ss.16-20 and, for Quebec, the QST system mirroring GST export rules. For general re-invoicing trade where the intermediary never takes possession or the buyer is a non-resident and goods are shipped directly from a foreign supplier to a foreign buyer, the Canadian LP must establish that the supply is made to a non-resident and the property is not delivered or made available in Canada. Offshore services supplied to non-residents concerning goods located outside Canada may also be zero-rated under Schedule IX Part V s.7. To claim zero-rating the LP must hold contemporaneous evidence confirming the direct export, including shipping instructions, commercial terms, and transfer of ownership abroad.

  • Canada Customs Invoice or commercial invoice indicating zero-rated supply
  • Form B13A – Canada Customs Export Declaration or approved CAED electronic equivalent
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, air waybill, or truck manifest)
  • Proof of export from Canadian territory (carrier certificate, customs stamp, outward presentation notice)
  • Foreign exchange record and inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation proving payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Controller/provincial QST and federal GST/HST remission documentation, if applicable

Canada and Quebec do not mandate continuous transaction controls, real-time tax clearance, or digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is permitted and encouraged via the Pan-Canadian Peppol network (CanBII) for B2B transactions. Businesses must issue compliant invoices under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act, but no government API clearance is mandatory. A re-invoicing hub must retain all electronic records in Canada or make them available electronically to CRA/Revenu Québec upon audit; failure to provide e-records on request may lead to assessment.

CRA and Revenu Québec aggressively review zero-export claims. A taxpayer who cannot substantiate physical export or the foreign destination will be reassessed to collect uncollected GST/HST or QST, with interest retroactive from the reporting period. For knowing or grossly negligent misrepresentations, penalties of 50% of the tax avoided under the Excise Tax Act s.285 and Taxation Act provisions, plus criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code, can apply. The normal reassessment period is four years for GST/HST and, for income matters involving non-arm's length partners, seven years; the period is unlimited where fraud or misrepresentation is proven. A re-invoicing structure that lacks economic substance may also be challenged under GAAR, leading to full clawback of zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no exchange controls on current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, partner distributions) or capital-account transactions (FDI, shareholder loans, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds). The Quebec Limited Partnership may freely receive capital contributions, invoice foreign customers, and repatriate profits to non-resident partners without Bank of Canada approval or declaration. Practical constraints are limited to (i) FINTRAC reporting of international electronic fund transfers (IEFTs) of C$10,000 or more by the financial institution, (ii) OSFI Guideline B-8 enhanced due diligence on non-resident-owned entities, and (iii) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on payments to non-residents, subject to treaty relief — a tax matter, not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no export-proceeds surrender regime, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. The Quebec LP may hold, invest, and disburse USD, EUR, or other foreign currency proceeds freely in domestic or offshore accounts. Applicable obligations are limited to (i) accurate invoicing, transfer-pricing documentation, and customs valuation with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for goods physically crossing the border; and (ii) bank record-keeping and FINTRAC EFT reporting obligations. Where re-invoicing involves direct shipment from a third-country supplier to the end customer without entry into Canada, the transactions fall outside CBSA scope but still require full documentary support to satisfy bank AML/KYC review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); AML/CFT supervision by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); retail payment system oversight under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency; the Minister of Finance retains residual approval powers only under the Investment Canada Act for foreign investment, not for FX flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer) operate in Canada as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC rather than as chartered banks; balances are not CDIC-insured and the EMI is not a direct SWIFT member. Since 2024, payment service providers must additionally register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. A Quebec LP may use an EMI for ancillary receivables collection or FX conversion, but the main liquidity and clearing rail should be a Tier-1 Canadian bank account to obtain direct SWIFT membership, documentary LC capability, and an auditable trail for the re-invoicing model. Inbound transfers routed from an unregulated offshore EMI into a domestic bank account will trigger enhanced due diligence and may be placed on hold pending full commercial invoice documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound partner capital via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank for nostro repositioning) to the LP's designated operational account held at the Canadian Tier-1 bank. Beneficiary name must exactly match the LP's registered legal name under REQ. Remittance Information (field 70) should read 'Partner Capital Contribution — [Partner Name] — [Date]'; for intercompany loans, use 'Shareholder Loan — [Agreement Ref]' and attach the loan agreement. Canadian banks automatically report all international transfers of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC as IEFTs; this is a reporting obligation, not an approval gate. To avoid delays, deliver to the bank in advance: the partnership agreement, the GP board resolution authorizing the capital call, source-of-wealth documentation for each contributing partner, and a statement of the contribution's commercial purpose (working capital for trade settlements, LC collateral cover, or operating expenses). Avoid generic purpose codes such as 'fees' or 'services' that do not match the underlying trade flow.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers to suppliers and end-customers, using structured remittance data referencing invoice numbers and purchase order numbers; (2) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 from the Quebec LP's bank in favor of suppliers, governed by UCP600, payable at sight or by acceptance; (3) MT202 for interbank repositioning to cover LC reimbursement claims under URC522 where documentary collections are used; (4) domestic CAD payments via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR) for Quebec operating expenses. The LP should operate a payment factory from a master operational account with segregated CAD, USD, and EUR sub-accounts, executing FX conversions only at settlement time under the bank's electronic banking portal. Every outward payment must be matched to the trade document package (invoice, PO, proof of delivery, customs entry) to avoid FINTRAC suspicious transaction reporting and to preserve transfer-pricing defensibility.USD/CAD deliverable outright forwards (standard onshore market, maturities up to 24 months, offered by all Tier-1 Canadian banks without complex ISDA documentation for plain-vanilla tenors)USD/CAD FX swaps (spot-forward and forward-forward combinations) for managing rolling settlement liquidityCross-currency swaps (USD/CAD, EUR/USD, USD/CNH) for matched long-dated invoice and payable cycles under ISDA/CSA master agreementsVanilla FX options (USD/CAD calls and puts) for contingent or bi-laterally uncertain trade flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for third-country non-convertible legs (e.g., USD/CNY, USD/INR, USD/BRL) executed with offshore counterparties; CAD NDFs are neither offered nor necessary because the CAD/USD onshore deliverable market is fully functionalMoney-market forward-rate agreements and swap-point trading against LVTS and correspondent balances for short-term liquidity optimizationForeign-currency deposit sweeps and multi-currency notional pooling to reduce idle currency risk across the CAD/USD/EUR account structure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer) operate in Canada as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC rather than as chartered banks; balances are not CDIC-insured and the EMI is not a direct SWIFT member. Since 2024, payment service providers must additionally register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. A Quebec LP may use an EMI for ancillary receivables collection or FX conversion, but the main liquidity and clearing rail should be a Tier-1 Canadian bank account to obtain direct SWIFT membership, documentary LC capability, and an auditable trail for the re-invoicing model. Inbound transfers routed from an unregulated offshore EMI into a domestic bank account will trigger enhanced due diligence and may be placed on hold pending full commercial invoice documentation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined for corporate partners (federal 15% general corporate rate + Quebec 11.5% general corporate rate); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and not a taxable entity.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Income Tax Act Part XIII on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident person (portfolio rate). For the Quebec Limited Partnership itself, profit distributions are not dividends; they are allocated partnership income taxed under Part I. If the LP is used with a Canadian corporate vehicle, any dividend distribution from that vehicle attracts 25% statutory withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory monetary threshold for preparing contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; however, CRA penalty-safe-harbour thresholds are CAD 1,000,000 for cross-border goods and services transactions and CAD 50,000 for guarantee fees. Country-by-country reporting is mandatory for Canadian ultimate parent entities with consolidated group revenue equivalent to EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec do not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and transfer-pricing standard (s.247) apply substance-over-form and commercial rationality tests. A re-invoicing LP that lacks employees, premises, business assets, or meaningful decision-making functions will be disregarded for lack of economic substance, and its profit allocations may be re-allocated to the parties that actually perform functions and bear risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction under Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Quebec Tax Administration Act imposes additional fines equal to up to 100% of the evaded duty and daily penalty accruals.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted taxes and penalties; asset seizure, including personal bank accounts and real estate, may follow; conviction triggers travel bans, disgorgement, and extradition exposure in cross-border schemes.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may reprice all transactions to arm's length, adjust income upward, and apply a 10% gross-negligence transfer-pricing penalty under s.163(2) on the net understated income; interest is compounded daily and treaty adjustments may be grossed up under s.247(10).

Director personal liability: Directors may be named on directors' liability certificates and held personally liable for amounts the corporation fails to remit; participation in deliberate mispricing may trigger criminal liability and personal monetary sanctions in civil courts.
Part XIII Withholding Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the full unwithheld amount plus interest and a 10% penalty under s.227(9); Revenu Quebec may impose equivalent obligations under the Quebec Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 ITA for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII amounts; travel bans may issue, personal assets can be garnished, and director disqualification may be ordered.
Shell Company / AML Abuse

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties for corporations up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code s.462.31 money laundering carries imprisonment up to 10 years, asset forfeiture, and court-ordered fines; Canada Border Services Agency and FINTRAC may coordinate asset freezes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or ignore laundering indicia face personal criminal liability, travel bans, extradition risk, director disqualification, and substantial civil restitution for victims and governmental authorities.
