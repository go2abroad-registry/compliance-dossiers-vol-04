Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope (federal income tax on worldwide basis for resident partners; partnership itself is fiscally transparent for income tax under ITA s.96; GST/HST treats the LP as a separate 'person' registrant; Ontario HST place of supply applies)

Available election forms

No Canadian 'check-the-box' entity election exists; the LP's partnership classification is statutory under Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act and ITA s.96. Available elections: (1) partnership fiscal period election under ITA s.249.1(4) where all partners are non-residents; (2) GST/HST Quick Method election for eligible registrants under ETA s.227 and s.228? actually let me simplify; (3) simplified annual ITC method election for registrants with qualifying revenue not exceeding CAD 500,000; (4) related-party election under ETA s.273 for non-application of GST/HST on supplies between closely related corporations (limited utility for LPs); (5) non-resident drop-shipment certificate mechanism under ETA s.179 to support zero-rating on goods consigned to a non-resident. Hybrid mismatch: Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.4–18.6, as amended by 2023?) apply mandatorily with no elective relief; partnerships relying on deferral must document arm's-length substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same CAD 30,000 registration threshold; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration; a bonded warehouse in a free trade zone must generally register even below the threshold because it holds a CBSA license and makes taxable supplies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Sch. VI Part V, zero-rating applies when: (i) tangible goods are exported from Canada as soon as is reasonable after the supply (ss.1–3); (ii) goods are sold to a non-resident who exports them in the course of commercial activity; (iii) services supplied to a non-resident (s.7) unless the service is rendered in respect of goods situated in Canada — a deemed 0% applies where the tangible property is exported within 60 days after the service is performed and the supplier retains export evidence; (iv) the drop-shipment rules (ETA s.179) allow a non-resident registrant's supplier to zero-rate goods subsequently exported if the supplier holds a signed drop-shipment/certificate and appropriate documentation. For bonded warehouse operations: storage of goods that remain in Canada is generally taxable at 13% HST even for non-residents, but warehouse and handling services can be zero-rated where the goods are exported within 60 days after each service and the operative documentary evidence is preserved; goods sold from the bonded depot and shipped outside Canada qualify for 0% on the export supply. Claimants must hold secondary evidence (CBSA export declaration, bills of lading, Cargo Control Documents, freight invoices, proof of payment) to sustain the zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Canadian Export Reporting System / CES, successor to Form B13A, with CES control number and unique shipment identifier)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing departure from Canada and foreign destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer/Payment evidence to substantiate commercial reality and FX repatriation in support of the underlying export

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada for GST/HST invoices; electronic invoices are permitted under ETA s.223.1 if legible and accessible. Import/export cargo is processed through CBSA's CARM platform (mandatory for commercial accounting since 2024); exporters must file electronic Export Declarations (successor to B13A) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) integrated in CARM; CBSA Single Window Initiative (SWI) allows electronic data submission; bonded warehouse licensees use CBSA Memorandum D8-1-1 inventory control procedures, with agricultural/GST relief linked to electronic warehouse transaction records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no requirement for central-bank approval of inward direct investment, and no quantitative limits on profit remittances. An Ontario Limited Partnership may freely receive capital contributions and pay distributions in Canadian dollars or foreign currency. However, distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners are generally subject to withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), with relief available under an applicable tax treaty such as the US-Canada Treaty. FINTRAC requires that all international electronic funds transfers in or out of Canada in amounts equal to or exceeding $10,000 CAD be transmitted with prescribed identifiers to allow cross-border monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Bonded warehouse operators may retain export and re-export proceeds in foreign currency accounts at any authorized Schedule I bank or authorized foreign bank branch. Reporting obligations apply under the Bank of Canada Exchange Rate Reporting System and under FINTRAC for international electronic funds transfers of CAD $10,000 or more; certain commercial receipts must be disclosed on the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Customs ledger when used to offset Canadian duty obligations within a customs bonded warehouse.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administration is shared between OSFI, Finance Canada, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering and cross-border movement reporting. No separate Crown 'Foreign Exchange Administration' exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Canadian-regulated payment fintechs operating as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and foreign digital EMIs are not viable for the core treasury of an ISIC 5210 bonded warehouse depot. OSFI-authorized deposit-taking institutions are required for custodial duties, customs bonds, standby letters of credit, and CBSA obligations. A foreign EMI may be engaged solely as a payment aggregator for low-value client remittances, but it cannot legally hold escrow funds, execute high-value RTGS wires, or issue commercial letters of credit. Such arrangements must be ancillary and isolated from the operational liquidity buffer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the Ontario LP must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 into a Lynx-enabled Canadian Schedule I bank account. The remitter is required to state the Bank of Canada 'International Transactions Reporting System' direct investment code for inward non-resident investment in the SWIFT MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) and reference 'Ontario Limited Partnership – Capital Contribution'. All beneficial owners must be identified. The receiving bank will apply enhanced due diligence to any FINTRAC-triggered wire equal to or above CAD $10,000. If funds are in USD, the bulk settlement is cleared through the Canadian Direct Settlement participant's US Dollar Clearing Service or an approved Carib-Clear correspondent. A pre-approved partner capital call notice and LP resolution must be placed on file with the bank before the first injection. The account currency must match the LP's functional capital currency; multiple segregated sub-accounts are recommended for partner capital, export proceeds, and cashed customs duty deposits. There is no central bank exchange-control permission required, but the transaction must not involve a comprehensively sanctioned jurisdiction.Outbound disbursements for bonded depot trade settlements must utilize SWIFT MT103 for international transfers routed through Lynx for high-value CAD remittances; within Canada and for USDCAD cross-border flows, use Lynx or Payments Canada's EFT retail clearing for small-value payments. Customs duty, taxes, and government charges must be paid exclusively through the CBSA authorized payment gateway (i.e., CADE e-payment, trade import/export cash stream). For clients requiring deferred payment terms, the deposit must issue irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/Cs) under UCP600 confirmed by a Canadian Schedule I bank. Standby letters of credit are required by CBSA in respect of the free trade zone bonded depot for customs bonded liabilities.
