Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Share capital of at least CHF 100,000 must be subscribed and stated in the Articles of Association; at least CHF 50,000 (20%) must be paid in cash and held in a blocked account (Sperrkonto) opened in the future corporate name before registration. Contributions in kind must be fully covered and justified by an audit report (Art. 634 OR). No escrow release is permitted prior to the Commercial Register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate for office and warehouse from the Zug municipal fire brigade; structural load clearance for storage racks and shelving issued by the building inspector; occupational safety and health clearance established through SUVA workplace inspection under Art. 82 of the Swiss Accident Insurance Act and Art. 3 of the Occupational Safety Ordinance.

Paid-in capital rule: Share capital of at least CHF 100,000 must be subscribed and stated in the Articles of Association; at least CHF 50,000 (20%) must be paid in cash and held in a blocked account (Sperrkonto) opened in the future corporate name before registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Swiss German: Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV; French: Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Bern. Central VAT registration, assessment, and enforcement portal: www.estv.admin.ch; VAT-dedicated portal: www.vat.admin.ch; official central business/UID register: www.uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss AG obtains its company identification number (UID, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) automatically upon entry in the Cantonal Commercial Register. For VAT, the AG must submit a VAT registration declaration to the ESTV once its worldwide annual taxable turnover (excluding supplies with foreign place of supply) exceeds CHF 100,000, or it may elect voluntary registration. Foreign entities without a Swiss registered office that perform taxable supplies in Switzerland must register with the ESTV through the online registration form and, where required, appoint a Swiss fiscal representative; the UID serves as the VAT ID and must appear on invoices. Federal direct tax, cantonal, and communal income taxes are administered through the cantonal tax authorities of the canton of registered office.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Swiss AG obtains its company identification number (UID, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx) automatically upon entry in the Cantonal Commercial Register. For VAT, the AG must submit a VAT registration declaration to the ESTV once its worldwide annual taxable turnover (excluding supplies with foreign place of supply) exceeds CHF 100,000, or it may elect voluntary registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box entity classification regime; the AG is statutorily a taxable corporation. Available elections include: (i) VAT group election (Art. 21 VAT Act) permitting closely linked group members to be treated as a single VAT taxpayer; (ii) voluntary VAT registration despite turnover below the statutory threshold where supplies are predominantly made to VAT-liable businesses; (iii) annual or quarterly VAT filing frequency elections under the ordinary semi-annual regime; (iv) cantonal notional interest deduction (NID) election on excess equity; and (v) patent box election for qualifying intellectual property income. Anti-hybrid rules (BEPS Action 2) are enacted, so no hybrid mismatch election that produces double non-taxation is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover (CHF 150,000 for non-profit and charitable entities), excluding supplies of goods and services whose place of supply is abroad; a legislative revision proposing an increase of the threshold toward CHF 250,000 is in preparation and must be verified for the applicable tax year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies where tangible goods are physically transported outside Swiss customs territory and the supplier holds verifiable proof of export (Swiss customs export declaration with exit confirmation). Services: 0% applies to services directly related to exported goods, services supplied to recipients abroad, and other B2B services whose place of supply is abroad, provided the conditions of Art. 23 of the Swiss VAT Act and the record-keeping requirements are satisfied. The supplier bears the burden of proof and must demonstrate the actual movement of goods abroad within a commercially reasonable and documentable period. Export supplies qualify for full recovery of input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Swiss electronic customs export declaration (e-dec/ATIS) with Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and exit confirmation evidencing physical departure from Switzerland
  • Transport Document - Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), Air Waybill (air), or registered postal/courier dispatch proof with signed proof of delivery where available
  • Commercial Invoice - supplier invoice bearing the Swiss AG's UID/VAT number, item descriptions, quantities, export destination, and the foreign buyer's address and VAT number where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank statement or SWIFT/MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign payment in CHF or convertible currency from the export buyer

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, transactional API reporting, or digital stamping regime for VAT. VAT is self-assessed through periodic returns (generally semi-annual). The standardized electronic invoice format is the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) containing embedded IBAN and QR code; eBill is the bank-channel e-invoicing standard. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is under legislative preparation but not yet in force; no continuous transaction control (CTC) API connection to ESTV exists to date.

ESTV conducts documentary checks and risk-based VAT audits of export zero-rating; the supplier must preserve the substantiation bundle for at least 10 years. Failure to substantiate triggers retrospective VAT reassessment at 8.1% with default interest from the original due date; assessment is statute-barred after 10 years, extended to 15 years in cases of tax evasion (Art. 93 VAT Act). Administrative fines reach up to CHF 10,000 for record-keeping and procedural violations; intentional tax evasion is penalized with a fine of up to 30% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 2 years (Art. 58 VAT Act), while negligent evasion attracts a fine of up to 10% of the evaded tax. Fraudulent zero-rating claims for fictitious exports may additionally trigger criminal tax fraud proceedings and loss of export privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a comprehensively liberalised capital and current account regime. The Swiss franc is freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements, and no mandatory licensing for cross-border payments, dividends, royalties, or capital movements. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: dividends and interest are subject to 35% Swiss withholding tax, but treaty relief or full refund mechanisms are available to qualifying foreign shareholders. No expatriation authorisation is required for retained earnings, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Swiss law imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the SNB or any other authority. Exporters may retain, hold, or convert foreign currency balances freely. There is no mandatory conversion ratio. Export invoices may be denominated in any currency, and receipts may be used directly for trade settlement; ordinary commercial documentation must be maintained solely for banking compliance and balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB), acting with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) and the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market and monetary statistical matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility of offshore digital EMIs is Low for direct Swiss account domiciliation because the provision of payment services in or into Switzerland requires a Swiss banking licence, a fintech licence under Art. 1b Banking Act, or a payment services authorisation under the revised Financial Services Act / AMLA framework. EU- or UK-authorised EMIs cannot operate as primary account holders for a Swiss AG without FINMA recognition or a licensed Swiss partner. Licensed Swiss fintech banks and payment institutions are viable for low-risk retail e-commerce payment processing, but the core corporate treasury account should remain at a traditional Tier 1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the AG is executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's own bank account into a blocked capital escrow account held in the company's formation name at a Swiss bank. The payment reference must identify the company name, the purpose 'Share capital contribution', and be supported by the certified deed of incorporation and notarised share subscription documents. Switzerland does not operate a national payment purpose-code registry; banks rely on the SWIFT remittance information field and their internal CDB 20 compliance checks. No regulatory approval is required for foreign direct investment, but the receiving bank must verify the remitter's identity and the ultimate beneficial owner as part of standard money-laundering due diligence.Supplier settlements are routed through SWIFT MT103 for direct commercial payments and MT202 for interbank cover orders where required. For documentary trade, Swiss banks issue and confirm commercial letters of credit under UCP600, transmitted by SWIFT MT700; documentary collections follow URC522. Settlement currency may be CHF or any freely convertible foreign currency. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if intercompany transactions exceed CHF 10 million; Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds CHF 1 billion. Documentation must be prepared annually and submitted upon request.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but requires genuine economic substance for tax residency and to prevent treaty abuse. Companies must have real business activity, physical presence, employees, and decision-making in Switzerland. Shell companies with no substance may be denied tax benefits and face penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 3 times the tax evaded; imprisonment up to 3 years; interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, reclassification of income, additional taxes, and penalties up to 20% of additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CHF 500,000; imprisonment up to 5 years; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally prosecuted; personal fines and imprisonment; travel bans and asset freezes.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax, interest, and penalties up to 20% of the additional tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil liability for damages to the company.
