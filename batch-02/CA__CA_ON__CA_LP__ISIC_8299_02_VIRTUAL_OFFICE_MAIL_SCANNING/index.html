Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Ontario HST is administered federally by the CRA (Ontario 8% provincial component remitted through the federal HST return); separate Ontario registration of the Limited Partnership itself is filed with the Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery under the Limited Partnerships Act (RSO 1990, c. L.20).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario LP must first complete Ontario LP registration under the Limited Partnerships Act and then obtain a federal CRA Business Number (BN) by filing online through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts); non-resident applicants may use the CRA non-resident registration channel with an authorized representative. Because the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada (mail-receiving, opening, and scanning services performed in Ontario), it must open a GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT') once worldwide taxable turnover exceeds the CA$30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted and advisable to claim input tax credits. The LP must appoint a designated partner for CRA correspondence and, if carrying on business in Canada, file the T5013 Partnership Information Return; the 15-character multi-program account linking structure of the BN serves as the national tax identifier for federal GST/HST and Ontario HST obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; an Ontario LP is statutorily flow-through for federal and Ontario income tax purposes under ITA s. 96(1), while, for GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate deemed person required to register and account for tax in its own capacity. Available elections include: (1) ITA s. 97(2) partnership contribution rollover election for assets contributed by partners; (2) ETA s. 265.1 election for the partners to assume joint and several liability for the partnership's net HST; (3) ETA s. 156(2) closely-related election (Form GST25) where the general partner is a wholly-owned corporate subsidiary of the group; and (4) administrative elections respecting partnership fiscal periods under ITA s. 249.1. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA s. 18.8 and s. 18.9 operate automatically on deduction/non-inclusion outcomes arising from inconsistent foreign characterization of the Canadian LP interest and cannot be elected around; treaty-based double-dip is further constrained by the principal-purpose limitation of Art. 29 of the Canada-US Treaty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold; measured on the person's global taxable supplies; registration is mandatory where the person exceeds CA$30,000 in the immediately preceding calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, computed on supplies made in and outside Canada)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, subsection 9, a supply of a service to a non-resident of Canada who is not registered for GST/HST is zero-rated unless the service is in respect of (a) real property situated in Canada, (b) tangible personal property (TPP) situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or (c) certain Canadian-use intangible, admission, or hotel services. The core virtual-office mail-receiving, opening, and scanning service is performed on physical mail (TPP) situated in Ontario at the time of performance; therefore it falls within the exception and is taxable at 13% HST even when invoiced to a foreign customer, with the supply deemed made in Canada under ETA s. 142(2). Only a separately chargeable pure digital document-management service with no physical mail or other TPP located in Canada at the time of performance may qualify for the 0% export rating, provided contractual terms, place-of-supply analysis, and the customer's non-resident status are documented. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is an internal compliance benchmark aligned with CRA's four-year normal reassessment cycle, bank AML transaction monitoring, and typical creditor covenants.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (required only where physical mail, documents, or goods are actually exported from Canada; not required for services-only digital scanning delivery)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (where a tangible mail-or-document export occurs; must be retained to support any zero-rating claim on the goods component)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice (evidencing remittance receipt from the non-resident customer in the invoiced currency)
  • Executed master services agreement, commercial invoice with zero-rating or HST line, and CRA place-of-supply documentation (GST/HST Memorandum 4-6 and 12-10 retentions) demonstrating non-resident status and the TPP-in-Canada analysis

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API fiscal device exists in Canada. Taxable supplies with a consideration exceeding CA$30 must be supported by an invoice containing supplier name, Business Number, invoice date, recipient name, description of supply, consideration, applicable rate, and HST charged (ETA s. 286 and GST/HST Memorandum 12-10). Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework based on Peppol BIS 3.0, and CRA accepts Peppol-transmitted electronic invoices as supporting records; participation is voluntary and CRA performs no pre-clearance or fiscal stamping of invoices.

CRA GST/HST audit programs scrutinize zero-rated export claims and non-resident invoices. Where the CMRA supply is found taxable in Canada, CRA may assess unremitted 13% HST with interest from the original filing date, apply failure-to-register and failure-to-file penalties, and impose the ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CA$100 or 25% of the tax sought to be evaded. Fraudulent export zero-rating, fictitious foreign invoices, or deliberate omissions trigger prosecution under ETA Part XI on summary conviction or indictment, and the normal four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298(1)) is extended without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is proved (ETA s. 298(2)). For LP structures, a corporate general partner's directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted HST under ETA s. 323(1), and non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP may face federal and Ontario assessment, NR4/T5013 reporting, and Part XIII withholding exposure, with clawbacks accruing from the original statutory due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no foreign-exchange permits, no central-bank prior-approval requirements, and no limitations on dividend-like drawings or partnership distributions. Profit remittance and capital repatriation may be executed freely through Canadian banks pursuant to ordinary KYC, tax reporting, and FINTRAC reporting rules. Non-resident partners remain subject to Canadian tax compliance, including Part I tax on Canadian-source business income where applicable, but no exchange-control barrier exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls in the early 1950s. Export proceeds, trade receivables, and service receipts may be retained in CAD or any permitted foreign currency. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no compulsory conversion ratio. FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies to transfers of C$10,000 or more, but this is an AML transparency obligation, not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); functional supervision is complemented by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and provincial securities and business registries. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary multi-currency settlement and low for primary treasury custody. Offshore digital EMIs may offer euro-area e-money accounts or multicurrency payment platforms, but they cannot hold themselves out as Canadian chartered banks or, in general, take Canadian-dollar deposits without compliance with the Bank Act. As a cross-border operational tool, a FINTRAC-registered money services business or a compliant entity facilitating payment services may be used for collections and disbursements; however, bank-tier custody, capital escrow, and commercial-lending services should remain with a Canadian CDIC-member bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed by SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers using the Ontario LP's designated Canadian bank account, including the Canadian financial institution number, transit branch number, account number, and the receiving bank's SWIFT/BIC. Canada does not maintain a mandatory government FX purpose-code list; the remittance field is populated with a clear economic descriptor such as 'Capital contribution – Ontario Limited Partnership' and, where applicable, a bank-specific purpose code for direct investment. Beneficiary verification and compliance sign-off are required before acceptance. FINTRAC EFT reports are triggered at C$10,000 or more. The bank may require the underlying partnership agreement, capital call resolution, beneficial-ownership attestation, and confirmation that the funds will be used for the CMRA/mail-scanning operating business. USD contributions may be received into a USD sub-account to avoid conversion latency, or converted into CAD using a deliverable spot FX trade with best-execution documentation.Outbound treasury disbursements for trade settlements and operating expenses use SWIFT MT103 payment instructions for supplier payments, service fees, salaries, and other invoice-based outflows. For goods or documentary trade settlements, a confirmed or unconfirmed commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) through a Canadian bank is appropriate. Cross-border USD payments are cleared via correspondents participating in CHIPS or Fedwire; CAD high-value payments use Lynx (the successor to the Large Value Transfer System); lower-value CAD payments may use ACSS or the modernized Real-Time Rail when applicable; recurring subscription and credit-card receipts settle through Stripe and card-rail acquirers and are transferred by automated clearing or interbank settlement into the operating account.
