Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Québec limited partnership. Each limited partner must contribute only the amount agreed in the partnership agreement; contribution may be cash, property, or services. The limited partner's liability is limited to the unpaid balance of the promised contribution. No mandatory escrow deposit is imposed by the Registry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance certificate under the Québec Fire Safety Code for any occupied commercial premises; municipal certificate of occupancy attesting structural load and safe egress; and an occupational health and safety program under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c. S-2.1) applicable to employees.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/businesses/business-number-registration.html); for Quebec provincial tax and enterprise registry: Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca).

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) or an extra-provincial limited partnership carrying on business in Quebec: (1) file the declaration/registration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ); (2) register for the federal CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online; (3) register for GST/HST by filing Form RC1 with the CRA unless the partnership qualifies as a small supplier; (4) register separately with Revenu Québec for QST using the NEQ, completing the registration questionnaire and, where applicable, electing a representative for service of documents; and (5) maintain all partnership constituent documents, general partner identities and ownership records in English or French for verification by CRA or Revenu Québec. The LP itself is not a legal person under Quebec civil law, but for GST/HST and QST purposes it is a separate registrant; therefore the registration must be in the partnership's own name and administered by the general partner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election; tax classification is determined by legal form. A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax: income or loss is allocated to partners, each of whom computes tax in his/her/its own jurisdiction. For GST/HST and QST, however, the partnership is a 'person' liable for registration, collection and remittance. There is no elective transparent/opaque classification for Canadian income tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Canada applies anti-hybrid rules which may deny deductions where partnership transparency interacts inconsistently with a foreign tax system. Relief-elected periods or fiscal-period elections may be available only in the narrow circumstances prescribed under the Income Tax Act, principally for partnership fiscal years; non-resident partners should separately assess the section 253 deemed-carrying-on-business rule and any applicable treaty permanent establishment protection.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST and QST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, with QST effectively requiring CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies attributable to Quebec; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, and optional below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on supplies of goods exported directly by the supplier and on qualifying exported services/intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered under GST/HST, where the supply is not made in Canada under the place-of-supply rules. For social-casino and virtual-slots digital gaming services, zero-rating will generally require documented evidence of the recipient's residence outside Canada, including payment-rail address, billing country, IP/geolocation data, and dedicated backend records. Supplies made to Canadian-resident consumers through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing are taxable in Canada at the applicable GST/HST plus QST rate.

  • Canadian customs export declaration (B13A/CERS electronic export declaration
  • Bill of lading or equivalent transport/waybill evidence showing export from Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange/conversion record establishing receipt of funds from the overseas buyer

There is no generalized real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control regime, or mandatory digital stamping for GST/HST or QST in Canada/Quebec. The CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoicing, but require prescribed invoice particulars for GST/HST and QST, including seller/registrant identification, transaction description, tax rates and amounts. Periodic GST/HST and QST returns are electronically filed through CRA and Revenu Québec portals, and certain high-volume registrants may have enhanced electronic reporting obligations. Apple/Google payment rails do not perform tax clearance or stamping; the regulated supplier remains responsible for tax compliance on each simulated casual-game transaction.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny a zero-rated export claim or refund where the supporting export/remittance documentation is incomplete or internally inconsistent. Under the Excise Tax Act and Quebec QST Act, making a false or misleading statement, failing to provide records, or participating in a fraudulent zero-rating scheme can result in interest, penalties, gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax claimed or avoided, reassessment beyond the normal four-year limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or wilful default, and prosecution. Clawbacks typically include the full zero-rated amount originally claimed, disallowed input tax credits, interest computed from the balance-due date, and any assessed penalty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act. There are no exchange controls restricting profit remittance, capital inflows, or liquidation proceeds. The Canadian dollar trades freely; however, FINTRAC and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) impose mandatory reporting on cash, currency, and monetary instruments equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and cross-border wire transfers are subject to record-keeping and sanctions-screening protocols.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds indefinitely or convert them at commercial discretion. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to large cash transactions and international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; CBSA requires physical cross-border reporting of currency or monetary instruments at CAD 10,000 or above.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange oversight is conducted under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) by FINTRAC and by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for regulated financial institutions. There is no dedicated foreign exchange control administration in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury banking; MEDIUM as supplementary payment facilitation. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) licensed in the EEA or UK are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions in Canada. They can operate only if registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and may not open CAD clearing accounts unless through a Canadian bank partner. For the LP's treasury, direct use of an offshore EMI is legally constrained; Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement requires clearance into a Canadian-chartered bank account or a FINTRAC-registered payment processor.
