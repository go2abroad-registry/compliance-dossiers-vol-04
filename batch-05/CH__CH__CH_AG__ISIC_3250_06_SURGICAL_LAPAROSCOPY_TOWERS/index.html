Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC); official national portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch. The VAT Division of the ESTV administers Swiss value added tax affairs centrally; cantonal tax administrations (Kantonales Steueramt) act as assessing authorities for cantonal and communal corporate income tax while applying the harmonized Swiss federal direct tax framework.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporation: the Cantonal Commercial Register office registers the new Aktiengesellschaft (AG). Step 2 - UID assignment: the Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the company-wide identification number (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / UID, format CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX), which also serves as the master tax number for federal and cantonal direct tax files. Step 3 - VAT registration: the AG files the official VAT (MWST) registration application through the FTA e-services portal, attaching the Commercial Register excerpt, articles of association, the intended business description (manufacture of rigid endoscopes and surgical laparoscopy tower systems, ISIC 3250) and the projected annual Swiss turnover; the FTA issues the VAT ID in the form CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX MWST and assigns the filing frequency (quarterly with prepayments, or monthly where taxable turnover exceeds CHF 5,000,000). Step 4 - Foreign non-resident manufacturers: foreign enterprises delivering taxable goods or services into Switzerland must apply through the same e-portal, designate a Swiss addressee for formal service, attach their home-country registration certificate and power of attorney, and after acceptance obtain the UID/MWST number; input VAT refunds for foreign suppliers without a Swiss fixed establishment are handled under the simplified FTA refund procedure once their Swiss place-of-supply nexus is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide an elective classification regime comparable to the United States check-the-box or EU hybrid mismatch options; the Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available statutory elections include: (i) Swiss VAT group taxation under Art. 11 of the Swiss VAT Act (Mehrwertsteuergruppierung) for financially, economically and organizationally integrated Swiss group entities; (ii) the election of flat-rate VAT deduction rates (Saldo-Steuersätze) for smaller businesses; (iii) the election of the VAT filing period (annual, quarterly or monthly); (iv) the federal and cantonal patent-box election for income from qualifying patents; (v) the cantonal R&D super-deduction election under the 2020 Swiss Tax Reform Act (USR 2020); and (vi) the participation exemption, which applies automatically to qualifying dividend and realized capital gain income without elective recharacterization. No election can transform the AG into a pass-through entity; federal direct tax, cantonal and communal corporate tax, capital tax and withholding tax on distributions remain structurally applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; no exchange-control permits, no SNB pre-authorisations, and no conversion quotas apply to trade payments, inbound capital, dividends, interest, or profit remittances. Capital-account liberalisation is complete; a Swiss manufacturing AG may freely receive equity injections and shareholder loans and may freely pay dividends and royalties, subject only to corporate-law profit-distribution rules (Art. 660 of the Code of Obligations, CO), AML/KYC documentation, the OECD BEPS transfer-pricing standard for related-party flows, and double-tax-treaty limitations. The only administrative duty is the SNB payment-statistics report by Swiss-resident enterprises for cross-border payments exceeding CHF 10 million per period; this is informational and does not require approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline apply to export receipts. Swiss exporters of surgical laparoscopy towers and related medical devices may retain USD, EUR, or CHF proceeds indefinitely in multi-currency accounts at Swiss banks. Legal duties are limited to (i) proper invoicing and bookkeeping under Arts. 957-958f CO, (ii) substantiating the economic background of payments above CHF 15,000 under Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) Art. 8 CDD, (iii) transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany trade under Art. 58 Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax, and (iv) SECO export-control licenses and Swissmedic declarations for regulated medical devices.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank) as monetary authority; financial-market supervision by FINMA and the Federal Department of Finance (FDF). Switzerland has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration and no exchange-control agency; the SNB monitors foreign-exchange flows solely for monetary policy, inflation, and balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for supplementary collection/disbursement rails; Low as a sole treasury infrastructure. Legal Constraints: (i) foreign UK/EU e-money institutions (EMIs) do not issue Swiss IBANs and cannot clear through the SIC system, leaving all CHF cash-flow settlement outside Switzerland; (ii) taking CHF deposits from the public requires a FINMA banking licence or a fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Banking Act (max. CHF 100 million of deposits, no interest), which most offshore EMIs do not hold; (iii) EMI virtual IBAN and SWIFT MT103 overlay accounts can be used only for USD/EUR collections in specific export corridors, with a daily sweeping arrangement to UBS/ZKB and a strict prohibition on end-of-day balances residing outside Switzerland; (iv) FINMA-supervised Swiss payment institutions provide a compliant overlay for e-commerce or distributor collections, but all significant liquidity must sit in a Swiss systemically protected bank.
