Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), portal canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html and My Business Account; Provincial (Quebec): Revenu Quebec, portal revenuquebec.ca and CLICK. Since 2013, Revenu Quebec administers both federal GST/HST and Quebec QST for Quebec businesses under a single-administration agreement; customs/export reporting is handled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or provincially under the Quebec Business Corporations Act (Registraire des entreprises du Quebec, REQ), obtaining the unique provincial NEQ identifier. Step 2: Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) - automatic for federal incorporations; for Quebec incorporations, apply through CRA Business Registration Online or the REQ/Revenu Quebec one-stop service. Step 3: Register program accounts under the BN: RT (GST/HST), RM (customs import/export), RP (payroll), and RD (excise) as applicable. Step 4: Through Revenu Quebec one-stop online registration, open the QST (TVQ) account, the provincial payroll account, and the federal GST/HST RT account; Revenu Quebec issues the QST registration number and coordinates the federal RT account with CRA. Step 5: Enrol in CBSA's CERS and validate the RM customs account for goods exports. Step 6: Non-resident foreign entities may voluntarily register for GST/HST and QST by submitting the foreign incorporation certificate, board resolution, foreign tax identifier, and a Canadian address for service; registration is mandatory where the non-resident carries on business in Canada and taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Books, records, and the BN must be retained normally for six years from the end of the relevant reporting year.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Quebec provincial corporate income tax; no pass-through eligibility exists under Canadian law. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (with foreign tax credits for foreign business income); non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income, generally through Part I tax and Part XIII withholding. Quebec maintains no general tax-exempt zone, and the ISIC 3240 novelty fidget toy compression-molding business qualifies for no statutory tax holiday.

Available election forms

Canada prohibits US-style check-the-box elections; entity classification follows statutory corporate law. Relevant statutory elections for this scope: (1) closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156 and its QST counterpart, deeming intercompany supplies at nil consideration to eliminate GST/QST friction; (2) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 (e.g., USD for the exporter); (3) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents with net-basis Canadian rental income; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, 91.6, and 247.11-247.13 (with Quebec counterparts) operate as mandatory BEPS Action 2 overrides - deduction disallowance or income inclusion - and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148 and provincial equivalents. Registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies of the person and associated persons exceed CAD 30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or over the four immediately preceding consecutive calendar quarters; registration is voluntary below the threshold and is available to non-residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: A supply of silicone stress-relief fidget toys manufactured in Quebec is zero-rated at 0% GST and 0% QST where it is a sale of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or recipient under ETA s. 1 of Part V, Schedule VI (QST equivalent), i.e., the goods are delivered to an export carrier or transferred to the recipient for export from Canada and are exported as soon as reasonably possible (CRA administrative position generally expects export within 60 days from sale/delivery), with sufficient written evidence. Services: separately supplied services to a non-resident are zero-rated under ETA s. 7, Part V, Schedule VI only if the non-resident is not in Canada when the service is performed or the service is performed wholly outside Canada. Failure to substantiate converts the supply to taxable at 14.975% with risk of ITC reversal. No trade permit or special license conditions zero-rating for this novelty toy product.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada or Quebec. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for monthly and quarterly filers (NETFILE/e-file); Revenu Quebec requires electronic transmission of the combined GST/QST return via CLICK. E-invoicing is being adopted voluntarily under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework based on PEPPOL/BIS, with no government invoice clearance or API pre-validation. Taxpayers must retain commercial invoice data and export evidence in machine-readable form for six years.

Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; however, ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing principles require arm's-length collection terms for intercompany receivables, and overdue balances can attract attribution adjustments. CRA and Revenu Quebec may deny zero-rating and reassess at 14.975% (GST 5% + QST 9.975%) with clawback of claimed ITCs/RTIs where export documentation is missing or inconsistent. The normal reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298), extended without limitation in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. Civil penalties include a late-filing penalty of 5% of the net tax plus 5% per complete month up to 12 months (maximum 65%) and gross-negligence penalties for false statements equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax payable (ETA s. 285.1; Quebec Tax Administration Act ss. 59-60); interest compounds daily at the prescribed rate. Criminal exposure arises under ETA s. 327 and the Customs Act for tax evasion or false export statements, carrying corporate fines (up to CAD 25,000 per count on indictment), imprisonment of directors/officers, forfeiture, and exclusion from federal programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency with no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or invested capital. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is governed exclusively by Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% unless reduced by treaty, e.g., Canada-United States Income Tax Convention, and further reduced for provinces such as Quebec where abatement applies); applicable corporate rates of 5-15% apply depending on the shareholder's jurisdiction of residence. The practical hurdles are documentary rather than regulatory: source-of-funds evidence, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and Quebec's registraire des entreprises (REQ) good-standing attestation are routinely required by Canadian financial institutions to evidence the legitimacy of cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation windows. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency proceeds in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts, invoice in USD or any freely convertible currency, and convert at commercial rates. The only compliance duties are indirect: accurate export declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs and trade statistics, and FINTRAC reporting of every incoming or outgoing cross-border currency transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, together with record-keeping obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy and oversight are administered jointly with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with cross-border currency reporting enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium (ancillary use only). The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), overseen by the Bank of Canada, now requires any payment service provider operating in Canada to be registered; however, offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot lawfully open primary internal settlement accounts for a Quebec business corporation. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be used as supplementary collection and disbursement corridors for marketplace payouts and small-ticket supplier payments, provided such providers hold a valid RPAA registration or operate through a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank partner. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control or central-bank pre-approval is required. Inbound shareholder capital is remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian operational account with Field 70 reference line clearly stating 'Paid-in capital / shareholder equity contribution' and the corporate name. The remitting shareholder must remit from their own bank account, and the Canadian bank may request source-of-funds evidence for credits of CAD 10,000 or more. Every cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is subject to FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be used as supplementary collection and disbursement corridors for marketplace payouts and small-ticket supplier payments, provided such providers hold a valid RPAA registration or operate through a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank partner. Primary treasury liquidity and statutory capital escrow must reside with a regulated Canadian bank or provincial credit union.
