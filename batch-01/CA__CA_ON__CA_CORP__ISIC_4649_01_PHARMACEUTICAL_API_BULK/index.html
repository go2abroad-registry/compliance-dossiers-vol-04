Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a United States-style check-the-box regime; a business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or equivalent provincial statute is mandatorily a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level income tax. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border pharmaceutical wholesale structures include: (i) Income Tax Act (ITA) section 85 rollover elections for qualifying transfers of property to a corporation; (ii) ITA section 216(4) election for net-basis taxation of non-resident rental and timber income; (iii) ITA section 261 functional-currency election; (iv) ITA sections 93 and 95 elections respecting foreign affiliate surplus and foreign accrual property income; and (v) treaty-based classification elections for hybrid entities under applicable Canada income tax conventions. Hybrid mismatch countermeasures, including ETA section 18.12 excessive debt interest disallowance and ITA section 247 transfer-pricing adjustments, are mandatory and not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during any calendar quarter and preceding three quarters for resident persons; non-resident suppliers of taxable goods delivered in Canada are also subject to this small-supplier threshold but must register if the threshold is exceeded. A pharmaceutical API wholesale distributor with delivery into Ontario will apply the 13% Ontario HST rate. Ontario Employer Health Tax applies only to employers with Ontario payroll exceeding CAD 1,000,000, with a separate exemption-level filing duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered GST/HST person may claim the 0% rate on supplies of goods that are exported from Canada under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, and on certain pharmaceutical API supplies qualifying as prescription drug ingredients under Schedule VI, Part I. The zero-rating applies where ownership of the goods is transferred to a non-resident purchaser, the goods are delivered by the supplier to an address outside Canada, or the goods are made available to a common carrier for direct export. The goods must be exported as soon after the supply as is commercially reasonable, and the supplier must retain contemporaneous documentary proof sufficient to satisfy CRA audit standards.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Commercial invoice describing the pharmaceutical API, unit quantities, value, and buyer/seller identities
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing foreign destination and export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment received from the non-resident buyer
  • Carrier instructions and forwarder confirmation linking the Canadian point of origin to the export gateway
  • Packing list and certificate of analysis for the API lot
  • Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence and any export permit required for controlled drug ingredients

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transactional tax authority pre-approval of invoices. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1,500,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE, GST/HST TELEFILE, or EDI. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and the federal e-invoicing roadmap align progressively with the PEPPOL/CIUS interoperability standard, but there is no statutory API-based invoice clearance mandate as of the current fiscal period. CBSA export reporting acts as the de facto transactional control for exported goods.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export within the normal four-year GST/HST reassessment window, extended to seven years in cases of carelessness, neglect, or wilful default, and without limitation in cases of fraud. Where documentary proof is absent or false, CRA reverses the 0% rate, assesses 13% Ontario HST on the supply, charges compounded daily arrears interest, and may impose the 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA section 285 for a false statement or omission. Wilful evasion can additionally expose responsible officers to fines up to CAD 25,000 on summary conviction and higher amounts on indictment, together with imprisonment. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day administrative benchmark heightens audit risk and burdens the taxpayer with enhanced substantiation requirements, although no separate GST/HST penalty attaches solely to delayed FX repatriation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange-control permit regime, no repatriation quota, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency balances. Profit remittance is impeded only by Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25% on dividends, reduced under the Canada-US and Canada-India/China treaties) and by ICA filing thresholds for acquisitions. CAD is freely floating and settles directly in CLS for major currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Canada. API export receipts may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency, provided FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports are filed for cross-border transfers above C$10,000 and the CRA foreign-asset/foreign-affiliate disclosures (T1134/T1135) are made where applicable. No BOP currency-conversion certificate is required for wholesale trade remittances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with FINTRAC (AML/CFT and cross-border reporting supervision), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation, and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) for inbound investment screening under the Investment Canada Act (ICA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency; FX supervision is embedded in the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Canadian Payments Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Canadian law does not recognise a separate 'EMI' deposit-taking licence; a foreign digital EMI can only operate as a money-services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, cannot take deposits or rely on deposit insurance, and must hold a Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) licence before controlling end-user float. For a High-Risk_Special_License pharmaceutical API wholesaler, most Schedule I banks will reject an offshore EMI as the settlement account, although EMIs remain acceptable for low-value procurement cards or non-trade disbursements.
