Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Quebec limited partnership. Capital contributions are governed by the partnership agreement; no escrow account is required. Limited partners' liability is confined to the unpaid balance of their agreed contribution, provided they do not take part in the control or management of the enterprise (CCQ arts. 2247-2249). Contributions must be disclosed in the declaration of registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec for Quebec provincial taxes, QST, and corporate tax administration; CRA Business Registration Online and Revenu Québec's Registraire des entreprises are the applicable portals.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online or a paper RC1 form; simultaneously or subsequently register with Revenu Québec for a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), QST account, and provincial payroll and tax accounts. Registration requires legal entity proof, foreign registration documents, Canadian agent or place of business details, and appointment of a representative for tax purposes, with the BN granted immediately and provincial accounts linked thereafter.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: A limited partnership (LP) is not a separate taxable entity for Canadian income tax purposes; each partner is taxed on their allocated share of income, with limited partners having liability protection but no active participation in management, and non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding, Part XII.2 tax on specified partnership distributions, and income tax under Part X.3 or withholding on allocated income as applicable. Territorial scope: full Canadian and provincial tax nexus, with Quebec-specific QST registration where the LP carries on commercial activity in Quebec, unless the $30,000 threshold relief applies. — A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online or a paper RC1 form; simultaneously or subsequently register with Revenu Québec for a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), QST account, and provincial payroll and tax accounts. Registration requires legal entity proof, foreign registration documents, Canadian agent or place of business details, and appointment of a representative for tax purposes, with the BN granted immediately and provincial accounts linked thereafter.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A limited partnership (LP) is not a separate taxable entity for Canadian income tax purposes; each partner is taxed on their allocated share of income, with limited partners having liability protection but no active participation in management, and non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding, Part XII.2 tax on specified partnership distributions, and income tax under Part X.3 or withholding on allocated income as applicable. Territorial scope: full Canadian and provincial tax nexus, with Quebec-specific QST registration where the LP carries on commercial activity in Quebec, unless the $30,000 threshold relief applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide check-the-box elections akin to the US; however, available elections include: (1) filing a designated partnership election under section 186 to treat a partner's share of certain income as business income; (2) electing under subsection 216.1 to treat non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source income as income from a business other than through a permanent establishment for treaty relief; (3) choosing to be a passive limited partner under subsection 34.2 to accelerate deductions; (4) making the partnership's taxation year election under subsection 34.1; and (5) electing under the hybrid mismatch rules in subsection 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 to conform or adjust the tax treatment for foreign hybrid entities, ensuring no deductible foreign tax credit mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the general threshold is CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies; for QST, the Quebec threshold is also CAD 30,000 of taxable turnover within Quebec, subject to mandatory registration once exceeded or for certain activities (e.g., non-residents carrying on commercial activity in Quebec may be required regardless of threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada by the LP to a non-resident for use outside Canada, provided title and risk pass outside Canada; services rendered to non-residents where the LP performs all or substantially all of the service at an address outside Canada; or optional zero-rating for certain toll conversion and freight services. Supplies of dental aligner sheets to export markets are zero-rated only upon documentation proving physical exportation, or direct contractual terms with a foreign orthodontic laboratory or clinic.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or equivalent electronic export reporting through Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA))
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing shipment to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of foreign currency proceeds in the LP's or its authorized foreign exchange intermediary's account
  • Commercial invoice matching the exported goods' description, quantity, and value
  • Customs Officer's endorsed proof of departure where applicable

Canada and Quebec do not impose a centralized real-time tax clearance or digital stamping mandate for invoices. B2B invoices issued by a GST/QST registrant must contain the registrant's BN and QST registration number; electronic invoices must be securely transmitted and preserved in a compatible audit trail. Filing is electronic via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, and Revenu Québec's provincial online services, with specific API-enabled bulk filing available for large filers, but no immediate fiscal clearance before invoice issuance.

CRA and Revenu Québec can, upon audit, re-classify zero-rated exports as taxable supplies if documentation is incomplete or the goods were not demonstrably exported. Penalties include: reassessment of GST/QST due plus interest from the settlement date; a 6% penalty under subsection 280(2) of the Excise Tax Act on numeric GST errors; gross negligence penalties of up to $5,000 or 10% of the tax sought, and 50% of unpaid tax for misstatements; and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims under section 327.1 of the Excise Tax Act, leading to fines up to CAD 100,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years. For QST, similar penalties apply under the Quebec Tax Administration Act, including a 50% penalty for false statements and seizure of refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, no requirement for Bank of Canada approval on capital injections, and no restriction on profit remittances or dividend/distribution repatriation by a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident limited partners, provided the remittance is made through a regulated Canadian financial institution. Tax compliance obligations, including Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners and Quebec TP-1021 reporting, apply independently of FX rules. Large cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 or equivalent are reportable to FINTRAC, and any non-resident partner funding above applicable thresholds must be supported by source of funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to Canada. There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rate or mandatory conversion timeline. Exporters retain 100% of foreign currency receipts and may hold them in foreign currency accounts with domestic chartered banks. However, FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting applies to any cross-border receipt of CAD 10,000 or more, and export documentation must be preserved for the Canada Revenue Agency and customs audits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low immediate viability for offshore digital EMIs. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not passportable into Canada; such entities cannot operate as banks and cannot directly provide CAD-denominated clearing. An offshore EMI may only provide ancillary services if it becomes a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB), maintains a Canadian agent or physical presence, and complies with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once applications become mandatory. Due to the LP's bankability requirements, a Canadian Schedule I bank or Desjardins is the legally preferable treasury corridor.
