Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for a limited partnership under the Civil Code of Québec; each limited partner must contribute the agreed cash or property and is liable only up to that contribution (art. 2246 C.c.Q.); contributions in kind must be disclosed in the declaration; no escrow account is mandated by ordinary Quebec law, but any partnership funds deposited for port operations are subject to contractual trust or bond provisions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the municipal fire prevention service, structural and crane-load inspections certified by an engineer under the Professional Code (with wharf loading clearance issued by the Port Authority), CNESST occupational health and safety programme for stevedores, WHMIS training, first-aid coverage, and port security clearance under the Marine Transportation Security Regulations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec; CRA Business Registration Online portal for federal Business Number (BN), GST/HST account and import/export account; Revenu Québec's Registraire des entreprises for the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and QST registration.

E-government filing portal

For a Quebec Limited Partnership formed by foreign entities: (1) file a declaration in the Quebec registre des entreprises to obtain the NEQ; (2) apply to CRA for a Federal Business Number using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, checking the box for GST/HST and import/export accounts; (3) obtain a CRA business number (BN) incorporating a standard 9-digit business number and a two-letter program suffix; (4) register with Revenu Québec for QST using the same NEQ; (5) complete partnership income tax identification (Partnership Information Return) as required under the Income Tax Act. The LP itself does not receive a separate tax identification number for income tax purposes; partners are allocated taxable income and are separately liable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and Quebec income tax; income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who bear tax according to their own status and tax home. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is a separate registrant when carrying on a taxable supply activity; the general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership tax obligations unless the partnership is designated as the registrant.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; limited partnerships are inherently pass-through under the Income Tax Act. Partners may allocate income in accordance with the partnership agreement subject to anti-avoidance rules, and may file prescribed designation forms in respect of the partnership using a fiscal year end. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada has enacted hybrid mismatch integrity rules under sections 18.4 to 18.6, 20.5, and associated definitions of the Income Tax Act, which disallow deductible amounts on a structured arrangement where there is hybrid financial instrument duplication or hybrid entity duplication; affected foreign partners may be required to provide information and documentation to the CRA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold; registration becomes mandatory for GST/HST and QST when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-residents carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies, special rules may require registration from the first supply depending on permanent establishment presence and supply characteristics).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property made by a registrant to a non-resident for delivery directly before export from Canada, or to services performed on goods destined for export, including stevedoring and terminal bulk cargo loading, provided the registrant obtains and retains documentary evidence of export within the required time frame. The supply must be made to a non-resident who is the exporter or the freight contract holder, and the goods must be delivered to a carrier or loaded onto an ocean vessel for export. Services such as unloading, weighing, grading and marshalling of bulk cargo at a marine dock for export can qualify as zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the analogous QST provisions, provided the services are performed in respect of tangible personal property supplied to a non-resident and not in respect of unexported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) or a border customs export declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where required
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or rail bill showing consignee and foreign destination)
  • Commercial invoice or service invoice identifying the stevedoring charges and the exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of payment and conversion of foreign currency (including SWIFT credit confirmation)
  • Cargo manifest or port terminal loading report evidencing the transfer of goods to the vessel
  • Freight contract, booking note or charter party reference linking the non-resident shipper and the export destination

No national real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or digital stamping regime applies to stevedoring or bulk port supplies. Tax invoices must contain prescribed particulars under the Excise Tax Act and the Quebec Tax Administration Act; electronic invoices are acceptable if complete, legible and retained in a processable format. Businesses are required to file GST/HST and QST returns electronically above prescribed thresholds, and must retain records supporting the zero-rating of exported goods for at least six years. Revenu Québec may require certain large registrants to transmit return data electronically through its secure portal.

The CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year assessment period, or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. If zero-rating is denied due to insufficient export documentation, the registrant becomes liable for uncollected GST/HST and QST on the full invoice value, plus federal and provincial interest and arrears penalties. Knowingly making a false or deceptive statement or claim under the Excise Tax Act is an offence punishable, upon summary conviction, by fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to two years, and upon indictment, by higher fines and imprisonment up to five years; civil penalties may be assessed of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded or of the net tax understated, and QST penalties analogous in Quebec apply. Repeated failures or fraudulent export zero-rating claims can trigger complete retroactive revocation of the zero-rating, recapture of refunds and denial of input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating and freely convertible currency (CAD) with no exchange control statute or administrative FX approval regime. Current account payments (profits, royalties, professional fees, stevedoring service fees) and capital account flows (equity injections, partner capital repatriation, liquidation proceeds) may be executed without Bank of Canada authorization or Minister of Finance license. Constraints arise only from financial crime controls: FINTRAC large-value international EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 and above, OSFI margin and credit rules on the banking counterparty, and the Income Tax Act section 233.1 foreign property reporting obligations of the Quebec Limited Partnership. Profit remittances from Quebec are unrestricted, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (which may be reduced under the Canada-US Tax Treaty if the recipient is a US resident) and to the Quebec taxation representative appointment rules for a partnership carrying on business in Quebec.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion ratio. The Quebec bulk-port stevedoring entity may retain proceeds in any foreign currency (USD, EUR) in a foreign currency-denominated account at a Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch in Canada. Compliance obligations are limited to accurate record-keeping under the Income Tax Act, FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds, and the OECD Balance of Payments statistical reporting administered by Statistics Canada. No Bank of Canada approval is required to fund offshore operating accounts or to pre-settle trade liabilities at Foreign Exchange.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation, and FINTRAC for AML compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border electronic money institutions (EMIs) and foreign payment service providers are rated Low-to-Medium on the treasury viability scale for this Quebec stevedoring mandate. Unlicensed non-resident EMIs cannot serve as primary settlement or escrow rails because Canada has enacted the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), under which payment service providers must be registered directly with the Bank of Canada; the registration transition window began September 2024 and existing providers must hold an RPAA registration or transitional status to operate into September 2025. The Quebec LP may lawfully use a registered RPAA intermediary for small-value disbursements (crew payments, per-diem allowances) but the Capital Escrow Account and the port-authority clearing relationship must reside with a prudentially regulated Schedule I bank or a licensed Quebec credit union. Legal constraints: foreign EMIs are not authorized to accept deposits under the Bank Act, their balances are not CDIC-insured, and the Export Development Canada trade settlement programs do not accept EMI counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injections to the Quebec LP capital escrow and operating accounts follows SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 where interbank positioning is required) from the subscribing partner's bank to the Schedule I receiving institution in Montreal or Quebec City, with receipt credited through Payments Canada Lynx or ACSS. The instruction must reference the partnership bank account IBAN-equivalent number, SWIFT BIC of the receiving branch, CAD or USD settlement currency, and an information line containing the NEQ registration, the subscription agreement number, and the UBO declaration. Canada does not employ a uniform outward cross-border purpose code system comparable to the China BOP scheme; however, the receiving bank maps the narrative to an internal purpose reason code and must apply FINTRAC EFT record-keeping rules for international transfers of CAD 3,000 or more, and EFT reporting for CAD 10,000 or more. The bank will require pre-funding clearance letters, an executed escrow agreement for initial shareholder capital, and confirmation that the funds are free from prescribed drug or criminal proceeds before credit enhancement or interest accrual.Outbound stevedoring trade settlement rails comprise: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct customer wires for charterer remittances, international agent fees, and foreign supplier invoices; (2) SWIFT MT202COV two-sided bank positioning for substantial disbursements; (3) commercial documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600 and eUCP for bulk-handling equipment, marine bunker purchasing, and terminal capex; (4) domestic CAD clearing via Payments Canada ACSS and Lynx for compliant disbursements of payroll for stevedores, dock supervisors, equipment mechanics, and wharfage charges to the port authority; and (5) multi-currency netting through an OSFI-regulated custody account where the LP maintains operational USD and CAD ledgers. Where the counterparty is a Quebec port terminal operator, Port_Authority_Clearing designations apply, requiring bank confirmation of port concession agreements and tenant licence credentials before releasing funds to the dock operator. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5% = 26.5% combined general corporate rate; LP is fiscally transparent, no entity-level tax, income taxed at partner level

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII (ITA s. 212(2)) on payments characterized as dividends; LP profit allocations are not Part XIII dividends — non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source business income (up to 53.31% top marginal rate for Quebec individuals; 26.5% combined for corporate partners); guaranteed interest or management fees paid to non-resident partners are subject to 25% Part XIII

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate reportable transactions with non-arm's length non-residents (T106 filing threshold); Master File required where consolidated group revenue is at or above C$500,000,000 and cross-border reportable transactions exceed C$5,000,000; documentation must be produced within 6 months of a CRA request under s. 247(4)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Caribbean-style ESR, but s. 245 ITA GAAR and sham doctrine void transactions lacking commercial substance; a stevedoring LP must demonstrate real presence through terminal assets, dock cranes, licensed labour, port authority contracts, invoicing, and Quebec bank accounts to preserve pass-through integrity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s. 163(2) ITA); criminal conviction under s. 239(1) ITA: fine from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; Quebec parallel penalties under the Tax Administration Act

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for restitution; criminal convictions may impose travel bans, seizure of terminal assets, revocation of port licences, and imprisonment
Abusive Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment denying the tax benefit; s. 245(7) GAAR penalty of 25% of the denied benefit (effective for transactions after April 2023); Quebec GAAR under s. 1075.2.1 of the Taxation Act

Director personal liability: Under Civil Code of Quebec art. 317 the corporate veil is pierced where the LP is a sham; directors personally liable for the full reassessed tax, interest, and penalties
Transfer Pricing Misstatements

Statutory sanction: s. 247(2) TP adjustment; s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the adjustment where it exceeds C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; late T106 filing penalty of C$100 per day up to C$24,000

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in false TP documentation face criminal prosecution under s. 239; CRA may apply s. 227.1 to impose director liability for unremitted amounts flowing from the adjustment
Withholding Tax Default (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: s. 227(8) ITA: 10% late-withholding penalty plus daily interest; s. 227.1: directors jointly and severally liable for up to 15% of the unremitted amount; Revenu Québec imposes the same regime

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable, without proof of intent, for unremitted Part XIII and interest; Quebec LAF provides parallel director liability and may bar directors from leaving the province pending settlement
AML / Shell-Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalty up to C$2,000,000 per violation; suspension of the port operator licence; CRA sham-entity reassessment with full tax restitution

Director personal liability: Directors liable under PCMLTFA s. 74(1) for fines up to C$500,000 (individuals) and imprisonment up to 5 years; travel prohibitions and extradition exposure under Canada's Mutual Legal Assistance arrangements
