Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce) of the canton of the registered office, operating under the supervisory authority of the Federal Commercial Register Authority at the Federal Department of Justice and Police (Bundesamt für Justiz, BJ).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft); incorporation formalities per Arts. 626-637 OR; registration and legal personality per Art. 643 OR and Arts. 62-95 of the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV); name-control rules per Arts. 950-951 OR.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation (Errichtungsakt) including the Articles of Association (Statuten), accompanied by the Board's Declaration of Acceptance (Annahmeerklärung) and the bank's blocked-account capital confirmation, filed through the e-register/eBAS electronic filing portal of the competent cantonal registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 46 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount must be confirmed by a Swiss-regulated bank via a blocked capital deposit account (Sperrkonto), with funds held at the company's disposal and subject to the Swiss Anti-Money-Laundering Act verification of all ultimate beneficial owners.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory fire-safety certification from the municipal Feuerpolizei confirming compliance with the cantonal fire-protection ordinance; structural-load and occupancy clearance evidenced by the landlord's valid commercial building permit and lease; registration with SUVA or a licensed private accident insurer under the UVG for occupational coverage; occupational-health clearance for screen workplaces under the Labour Act (ArG) including the 4-hour maximum continuous screen work regime, workplace ergonomics, lighting and ventilation standards.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of each share, and in aggregate no less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up amount must be confirmed by a Swiss-regulated bank via a blocked capital deposit account (Sperrkonto), with funds held at the company's disposal and subject to the Swiss Anti-Money-Laundering Act verification of all ultimate beneficial owners.

(2) The registered office of the Company is adopted and established at [Street Address], [City], Canton of [Canton]. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial licensing board is applicable to commercial CGI and 3D animation production under ISIC 5911; general municipal business supervision (Gewerbepolizei) and the Cantonal Department of Economic Affairs (Amt für Wirtschaft und Arbeit) provide only routine registration and labor-market oversight.

Category: No statutory operating or manufacturing permit is required for an animation/VFX studio under the Swiss Federal Act on Radio and Television (RTVA), which governs broadcasting rather than content production; the company operates under its Commercial Register entry, the municipal business-registration confirmation, and any applicable work/residence approvals for non-EEA creative personnel.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / ESTV — Administration fédérale des contributions / AFC), official portal: www.estv.admin.ch (ePortal for VAT returns and registrations)

E-government filing portal

A foreign company without a Swiss registered office that performs taxable supplies in Switzerland must apply online to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration for a Swiss VAT number (UID/MWST-Nr. / UID/TVA). The application is submitted through the FTA's ePortal (Login mit SwissID/CH-LOGIN) using the 'Formular 100 Änderungsmeldung / Registrierung' or the official ePortal registration module. The company must state its foreign registration, actual activity in Switzerland, estimated Swiss turnover, and appoint a Swiss tax representative if it has no Swiss establishment. The UID is generally granted within days; there is no mandatory local incorporation requirement solely for VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign company without a Swiss registered office that performs taxable supplies in Switzerland must apply online to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration for a Swiss VAT number (UID/MWST-Nr. / UID/TVA). The application is submitted through the FTA's ePortal (Login mit SwissID/CH-LOGIN) using the 'Formular 100 Änderungsmeldung / Registrierung' or the official ePortal registration module. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide a 'check-the-box' regime. The AG is an independent legal subject taxed at entity level. Elective arrangements are limited to group tax consolidation at cantonal level ('Steuerkonsolidierung'), group value-added tax registration under Article 15 of the Federal VAT Act (close economic, financial and organisational links), and advance tax rulings issued by the competent cantonal tax administration and the FTA (including rulings on withholding tax, VAT and direct tax treatment of international service structures). No hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 per year of taxable turnover from supplies made in Switzerland; foreign enterprises without a Swiss place of business are also liable if their Swiss-taxable supplies exceed CHF 100,000, or, in the case of certain cross-border services, if they exceed the worldwide CHF 100,000 threshold and the Swiss threshold is met. Voluntary registration is possible below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (tax-exempt with input VAT deduction) is available for: (i) goods physically transported out of Switzerland by the supplier, the recipient or a third party, evidenced by a Swiss Customs export declaration; (ii) services that are actually and economically used in a foreign country by a foreign business recipient or by a foreign private recipient, provided the recipient is established abroad and the service is not connected to Swiss domestic assets; (iii) services relating directly to exported goods (e.g. handling, transport, freight forwarding, and ancillary services) and typically also digital or animation/VFX services supplied to an overseas client where the benefit is obtained abroad. The supplier must as a rule obtain written confirmation from the foreign recipient, supporting transaction correspondence, and payment evidence to substantiate the foreign business purpose and the actual use abroad.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Aussgangszollanmeldung / déclaration en douane de sortie) filed via e-dec/Rhea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document or equivalent proof of shipment
  • Confirmation of foreign arrival or importation (foreign customs entry document, when obtained)
  • Bank transfer or SWIFT payment evidence from the foreign buyer / inward remittance evidence

Switzerland does not operate real-time VAT clearance or continuous transaction controls. No API-based fiscal stamping is required. Electronic invoices are permitted if they satisfy Swiss VAT invoice content requirements (Art. 26 of the Swiss VAT Act): supplier's identity and VAT UID, date, recipient, nature of supply, consideration and tax rate. For federal public procurement contracts, B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via the SIMAP portal in Peppol BIS 3.0 format. Invoices and electronic records must be retained for ten years.

False zero-rating claims are recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies; the FTA will retrospectively assess VAT at the applicable rate, disallow input VAT deductions, and impose default interest (4.0% per annum). Intentional or negligent tax evasion under Articles 70, 96 and 97 of the Swiss VAT Act attracts penalties up to double the tax amount evaded, plus administrative fines up to CHF 30,000; document falsification or fraudulent claiming of export exemptions can further trigger criminal prosecution and clawback of input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Swiss franc (CHF) is freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on inbound or outbound capital flows, and no statutory FX approval for ordinary cross-border transactions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are unrestricted but trigger a 35% federal withholding tax, potentially reduced under an applicable double tax treaty. Practical frictions arise only from bank-level AML/KYC and sanctions screening, not from statutory FX regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Switzerland. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely. Only statistical reporting obligations for identified cross-border payment thresholds and general tax/AML documentation requirements must be observed.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) acting together with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking supervision and payment system oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise B2B, Revolut Business, Airwallex) may be usable as supplementary cross-border collection and disbursement rails, but under Swiss law they cannot hold customer funds or provide main payment accounts to Swiss residents unless FINMA-authorized under the Banking Act or FinTech licence regime. A Swiss-licensed AG should not rely solely on an unregulated offshore EMI for primary treasury. Feasibility for low-value international vendor payments is Medium; for primary Swiss clearing and capital accounts it is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 using remittance text 'CAPITAL INCREASE – SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – [LEI/ENTITY REFERENCE]'. The foreign subscribing shareholder must instruct its bank to credit the Swiss AG's account with a final and irrevocable equity payment. The Swiss receiving bank will require: notarized capital-increase resolution, application for commercial register entry, auditor's confirmation of capital payment where applicable, source-of-funds evidence from the shareholder, and final beneficial ownership identification. For newly formed AGs, funds are held in a designated 'capital contribution account' and released only after commercial registration. Avoid blended purpose codes or loan/equity ambiguity; use a clean purpose code such as 'EQUITY CAPITAL ISSUE' to prevent routing delays.Service-based disbursements for subcontracting, licenses, royalties, and animation production are cleared most efficiently through SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking and, where applicable, SEPA credit transfer in EUR/CHF. For high-value or contractually material settlements, direct SWIFT wires with a full reference narrative are preferred; Wise B2B and comparable EMI rails can supplement low-value vendor payments provided each transaction carries a compliant purpose code and audit trail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal profit tax rate on net profit after federal tax; equivalent to approximately 7.83% on pre-tax profit; combined federal plus cantonal/communal headline rates for a Swiss AG typically range from 12% to 21% depending on the canton of residence.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal anticipatory withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) on any direct, constructive or liquidating dividend paid by an AG, regardless of the shareholder's residence; participation relief reduces the company's corporate income tax base but does not eliminate the withholding obligation on distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 10 million aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions is the general administrative threshold triggering the Swiss tax authority's request for Local File and Master File documentation; Country-by-Country Reporting becomes mandatory at CHF 900 million group consolidated revenue, with a CbCR notification filing.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no codified Economic Substance Regulation comparable to offshore centres; however, substantive corporate presence, local board decisions, qualified employees, operational premises and assumption of commercial risks are economic-substance criteria applied by the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and cantonal tax authorities to deny treaty benefits and prevent abusive shell structures, particularly for IP-holding and financing companies in Zug and Geneva.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Federal Tax Evasion and Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax plus default interest; administrative fine of up to the amount of the evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG; in serious tax fraud under Art. 186 DBG, fine of up to three times the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to three years.

Director personal liability: Board members who knowingly participated in the false declaration can be personally fined and imprisoned; they may also be held jointly civilly liable for the corporate tax shortfall and face attachment of personal assets or travel measures during criminal tax proceedings.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Hidden Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Mandatory arm's-length reallocation of profits and disallowance of excessive expense deductions; recharacterization as a hidden profit distribution triggers 35% federal withholding tax plus a 5% default interest charge; additional tax evasion penalties apply when intent is shown.

Director personal liability: Directors approving false intercompany pricing or secret profit transfers are subject to director liability under Art. 754 CO for damage to the company and tax creditors; they may also face criminal liability for forgery and unlawful distribution of share capital, including imprisonment and travel bans in ongoing fraud investigations.
Withholding Tax Default on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The withholding tax remains owed by the Swiss company and is compulsorily collected by the ESTV together with default interest of approximately 5% per annum; procedural fines and, in cases of constructive distribution, gross-up of the distribution are also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors can become personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax when they knowingly ordered the payment; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to five years under aggravated tax fraud and, in cross-border cases, an automatic passport or travel-ban measure pending final judgment.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and AML Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty reductions and refunds; retroactive assessment of 35% withholding tax on hidden distributions; tax-reassessment penalties; under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act and FinIA, shell-type structures without adequate UBO screening can attract fines of up to CHF 500,000 and mandatory reporting to the MROS.

Director personal liability: Foreign shadow directors and ultimate beneficial owners can be treated as de facto directors and subjected to Swiss criminal sanctions for money laundering, forgery and unlawful asset management; civil liability reaches personal wealth when the corporate veil is pierced for abuse and personal guarantees, with travel bans and executives' residence restrictions imposed during active proceedings.
