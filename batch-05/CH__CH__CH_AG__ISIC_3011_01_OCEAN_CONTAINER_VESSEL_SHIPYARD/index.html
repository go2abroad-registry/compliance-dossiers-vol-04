Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), supervised by the Swiss Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenossisches Handelsregisteramt) within the Federal Office of Justice.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, OR; SR 220), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft / societe anonyme), in particular Arts. 625 (minimum capital), 626-628 (articles of association), 629 (paid-up capital at incorporation), 630-631 (deed of incorporation), 643 (registered office), 698 (general meeting powers), and 716b (non-transferable board duties); supplemented by the Swiss Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV; SR 221.411).

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of incorporation (Errichtungsakt / acte constitutif) executed before a licensed notary public of the Canton of Zug, together with the Articles of Association (Statuten / statuts) and the application for commercial registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung / avis d'inscription).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

CHF 100,000 minimum nominal share capital; at least 20% of each subscribed share, in aggregate not less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash at incorporation. The paid-up capital must be credited to a blocked escrow account at a FINMA-supervised bank in Switzerland and may be released only after entry of the company in the Commercial Register; a bank blocked-account certificate (Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) must accompany the registration application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Zug Cantonal Building Insurance (Gebäudeversicherung Zug) covering fire-resistance ratings, escape routes, and flammable-liquid storage; structural static clearance for dry dock, slipway and gantry crane loads by the Cantonal Building Inspectorate (Bauamt Zug); occupational safety audit by SUVA and the cantonal labor inspectorate covering noise, welding fume control, fall protection, and confined-space protocols prior to commissioning; civil defense conformity under cantonal construction law (shelter obligations) where the shipyard includes underground areas.

Paid-in capital rule: CHF 100,000 minimum nominal share capital; at least 20% of each subscribed share, in aggregate not less than CHF 50,000, must be paid up in cash at incorporation. The paid-up capital must be credited to a blocked escrow account at a FINMA-supervised bank in Switzerland and may be released only after entry of the company in the Commercial Register; a bank blocked-account certificate (Sperrkonto-Bestätigung) must accompany the registration application.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV/FTA – Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung), VAT and federal income tax divisions, together with the competent cantonal tax administration for cantonal and communal profit taxes; official e-portal: www.estv.admin.ch and the VAT online service 'Mein Konto / Services en ligne'.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Aktiengesellschaft is taxed as a separate corporate taxpayer at federal, cantonal and communal levels; it has no pass-through status under Swiss tax law. For Swiss VAT the AG is an independently taxable legal person and a taxable person under Art. 10 VAT Act. The worldwide scope of direct taxation applies to a Swiss-resident AG; exports are handled through the indirect tax regime.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide US-style check-the-box or entity-classification elections. Existing elections relevant to a Swiss shipyard AG include: (i) Swiss VAT group taxation ('Unternehmensgruppe') for financially, organisationally and functionally consolidated Swiss entities (Art. 12 para 3 VAT Act); (ii) election between effective input-tax accounting and the simplified flat-rate 'saldo' method (Art. 59 VAT Act); (iii) voluntary VAT registration to recover input VAT, and simplified registration for foreign suppliers of services (Art. 64 VAT Act); (iv) election to pay out Swiss capital contribution reserves (KER/EAC) tax-neutrally to shareholders; (v) optional notional interest deduction-type relief to the extent available under cantonal tax law for financing structures. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not available; stricter Swiss anti-abuse and OECD-aligned hybrid mismatch avoidance rules (HEIV) prevail.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover for Swiss-resident businesses; a foreign shipyard must register for Swiss VAT when its taxable supplies in Switzerland exceed the CHF 100,000 threshold or when it voluntarily opts for registration to claim input VAT recovery. Foreign service suppliers in Switzerland may use the Art. 64 simplified registration procedure if their Swiss receipts are below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT with full input tax credit) applies when the Swiss shipyard delivers a newly built ocean-going container vessel out of Swiss customs territory to a foreign buyer, provided the seller, buyer or a third-party carrier physically transports the goods abroad and economic ownership passes overseas (Art. 23 para 1 let. a VAT Act). Contractual shipbuilding works, outfitting and repair services performed on movable goods for a foreign recipient may also be zero-rated under Art. 23 para 2 let. a/e VAT Act if the recipient is established abroad and the benefit is enjoyed abroad. Delivery to a Swiss-connected buyer, a Swiss port or a customs-free zone without actual export fails zero-rating and triggers the standard 8.1% VAT rate on the full consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Ausfuhrbescheinigung (Swiss Customs NCTS declaration with export date, vessel identification and customs value)
  • Bill of Lading / Port Delivery Acceptance Certificate proving delivery and discharge at a destination outside Switzerland (original or certified copy)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Currency Credit Advice from a Swiss authorised bank evidencing USD/EUR payment by the overseas buyer within 180 days
  • Original Commercial Invoice and shipbuilding contract (e.g. Norwegian Sale Form / SHIPSALE) with export terms, Inco determination and vessel ID
  • Swiss VAT export audit form (MWST export proof) where required by ESTV, plus class survey certificate or proof of newly constructed vessel status.

Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based clearing obligation. Mandatory rules instead: (i) every tax invoice must contain the Art. 26 VAT Act details (supplier and recipient VAT numbers, invoice date, description, consideration and applicable VAT rate); (ii) paper and PDF invoices must bear the Swiss QR-bill / QR-payment slip replacing conventional payment slips since 30 June 2020; (iii) B2G invoicing to Swiss federal authorities is executed through PEPPOL-compatible eBill/e-invoicing; (iv) export declarations are filed through the Swiss customs system (NCTS/Landesverwaltung) independently of invoicing; (v) invoices and export substantiation documents must be retained for ten years.

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration and cantonal audit authorities rigorously challenge zero-rating claims lacking coherent customs, transport and payment evidence. Consequences of insufficient or fraudulent export substantiation include: (i) denial of the 0% rate and retrospective reassessment at 8.1% on the full contract price; (ii) arrears interest at 4.5% per annum on the VAT shortfall; (iii) administrative fines of up to three times the evaded tax for intentional tax evasion under VAT Act Arts. 96-97; (iv) criminal prosecution for tax fraud if the vessel export documentation is wilfully falsified; (v) retrospective clawback over the standard five-year limitation period, extended to ten years for deliberate concealment or fraud, exposing all prior export years to reassessment; and (vi) loss of input VAT recovery until restitution is made. Maritime instalment LC or escrow mechanics do not cure documentary insufficiency.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account flows. There is no exchange-control regime, no authorised-dealer intermediation, and no SNB or SECO approval requirement for inbound equity, shareholder loans, trade credits, or outbound profit repatriation by a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft. Art. 27 of the Federal Constitution and Art. 3 of the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments guarantee free movement of capital. Dividend and interest distributions by the shipyard AG are subject to Swiss federal withholding tax at 35% (Verrechnungssteuer), fully refundable for Swiss-resident shareholders and substantially relieved under double-taxation treaties for non-resident owners; otherwise, no convertibility restriction, no minimum onshore retention period, and no residency-based limitation applies. For ISIC 3011 vessel construction, invoicing in USD or EUR is unrestricted, proceeds may be held abroad indefinitely, and the SNB operates no minimum exchange-rate target since the 2015 unpegging. Swiss policy rates are in a positive corridor, and there is no penalty or negative interest on non-resident CHF or foreign-currency balances under current SNB policy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export receipts. A Swiss ocean-container-vessel shipyard may retain USD and EUR proceeds in onshore or offshore accounts indefinitely, apply them to imported steel, engine, and auxiliary-equipment payables, or convert them to CHF at freely negotiated market rates. The only mandatory requirements are documentary: FINMA anti-money-laundering rules and CDB20 oblige the receiving bank to retain origin-of-funds documentation and the underlying shipbuilding contract, and transactions exceeding the enhanced-CDD threshold (normally CHF 50,000 in one or two linked payments) trigger internal record-keeping obligations; no SNB reporting of export earnings is required.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse), operating under the Federal Constitution, the National Bank Act (SR 951.11), and the Federal Act on Currency and Payment Instruments (SR 941.10), with anti-money-laundering and banking conduct supervision by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), sanctions and export-control enforcement by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), and counterparty due-diligence standards imposed through the Swiss Bankers Association Due Diligence Agreement CDB20.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for the mandated payment rails. A Swiss fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act or FINMA-recognised payment-institution SRO membership does not permit issuance of commercial documentary credits, maritime instalment LCs, or bank refund guarantees; those require a full category-B Swiss banking licence. Licensed digital-asset banks (Sygnum Bank, SEBA Bank) provide FINMA-graded banking substance and cross-border settlement but do not underwrite vessel-construction trade finance and have low integration with SWIFT-based marine letter-of-credit infrastructure. Unregulated foreign EMI providers (Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise) cannot hold the construction-milestone escrow balance or issue standby LCs and are restricted by FINMA Circular 2022/14 substance and pass-porting rules. Such EMIs are legally usable only as supplementary FX-settlement wallets for minor supplier payments; core inbound capital escrow, instalment LC collection, and shareholder-loan repayments must run through UBS or ZKB to preserve IFRS audit integrity and CDB20 compliance.

Defense evidence: Bank-issued advance-payment/refund guarantee (URDG 758) payable to the owner; construction-progress certificate by the classification society covering steel-cutting, keel-laying, engine installation, and launching dates; auditor's interim report quantifying burn rates on steel procurement and slipway utilisation; supplier invoices from certified steelworks and engine OEMs; copy of the builder's all-risks and marine construction insurance confirming hull value.

Defense evidence: Full UBO ladder to natural persons via CDB20 Form A-B with documentary evidence for each intermediate entity; Swiss commercial-register excerpt with board signatures; nominee declarations waiving fiduciary capacity; tax-residence certificates of each UBO and intermediate jurisdiction; negative press and regulatory search results; sworn declaration confirming absence of PEP status or, where applicable, an origin-of-wealth protocol satisfying the bank's PEP policy.
Sanctions-adjacent counterparties, grey-fleet vessel bunkering, Russian or isomorphic connections, or dual-use equipment acquisition for the yard

Defense evidence: End-to-end real-time sanctions screening of the vessel sale chain, charterparties, and supplier flags against SECO, EU, and OFAC consolidated lists; SECO export-control certificate for the yard's procurement scope; attested dual-use end-use statements for engines, thrusters, and sonar instrumentation; insurance and bunker-licence compliance from the operator; negative Russian-interest attestation and board resolution prohibiting onward transfers; external AML advisory opinion from a Swiss law firm.
Reliance on shareholder loans rather than paid-in equity for capitalisation, creating source-of-funds ambiguity and thin-capitalisation suspicion

Root cause: Reliance on shareholder loans rather than paid-in equity for capitalisation, creating source-of-funds ambiguity and thin-capitalisation suspicion

Defense evidence: Signed shareholders' loan agreement with stated commercial terms, interest rate, and drawdown schedule; bank statements of the lending shareholder proving the wired amounts; tax-clearance certificate from the shareholder's residence jurisdiction; statutory declaration that no Swiss debt-equity ratio threshold is breached; board minutes approving the loan facility; undertaking to provide annual financial statements certified by an auditor licensed under Art. 727a of the Swiss Code of Obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (cover payment) to the UBS Capital Escrow Account, with ISO 20022 remittance information stating 'EQUI – Capital Increase per Board Resolution DD.MM.YYYY' for share capital or 'LOAN – Shareholder Loan Agreement DD.MM.YYYY' for subordinated debt. For any credit above CHF 50,000, the receiving bank requires: a notarised capital-increase resolution, updated commercial-register excerpt, account purpose statement, CDB20 funds-origin form, and beneficial-owner attestations from the payer's bank. Escrow release is permitted only after confirmation that the capital contribution is recorded in the Swiss Commercial Register and sanctions, PEP, and FATF-recommended monitoring flags are cleared. Cross-border advance instalments under the vessel contract enter by MT103 with purpose 'Export proceeds – vessel construction contract 2024-XX', attract no SNB conversion obligation, and may be maintained in CHF, USD, or EUR per treasury policy.Outbound settlements for steel slabs, main engines, switchboards, and propeller systems run through (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for open-account disbursements against supplier invoices with ISO 20022 remittance data, and (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by UBS or ZKB subject to UCP600 and ISBP 821 for major capex procurement. EUR legs clear via TARGET2/EURO1 and USD legs via the issuing Swiss bank's US correspondent network in New-York-dollar wires (CHIPS/Fedwire) under the bank's own liquidity lines. The maritime collection structure uses staged irrevocable instalment LCs (steel-cutting 10%, keel-laying 15%, launching 25%, delivery after sea-trial protocol 50%) under UCP600, paid against classification-society certificates, commercial invoices, drafts, and refund-guarantee certificates; proceeds are swept daily via zero-balance structures into the UBS operational concentration account. Standby LCs under ISP98 secure refund obligations to the buyer for milestone slippage, and SWIFT MT760/MT799 messages provide the documentary backbone for guarantees. No exchange-control or SNB approval is required for any outbound trade payment from Switzerland.Plain-vanilla Deliverable FX Forwards (USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, and USD/EUR) under an ISDA 2022 Master Agreement with credit support annex, admissible for forecast vessel-sale proceeds up to 24 months forward without SNB authorisationCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) converting long-dated USD/EUR export receivables into CHF operational liquidity at fixed or floating legs, hedge-accounted as fair-value hedges under IFRS 9FX Options and zero-premium collars/participating forwards for milestone payments and tender-bid protection, executed only after board treasury-committee approvalNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on USD/CHF, technically traded in the interbank market but economically redundant while CHF remains freely convertible and deliverable; reserved for legacy or restricted-capacity corridorsExchange-traded CHF/USD and CHF/EUR futures cleared via Eurex Clearing for tail hedges and reduction of counterparty credit-risk concentration below ISDA thresholdsFX Swaps (spot-forward rollovers) for short-term working-capital bridging between instalment receipts and disbursement peaks, including USD/EUR swaps avoiding physical conversionForward-starting swaps and swaptions in USD/CHF, permitted in-house when the underlying construction-cost payable is evidenced by the documentary LC pipeline

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the mandated payment rails. A Swiss fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act or FINMA-recognised payment-institution SRO membership does not permit issuance of commercial documentary credits, maritime instalment LCs, or bank refund guarantees; those require a full category-B Swiss banking licence. Licensed digital-asset banks (Sygnum Bank, SEBA Bank) provide FINMA-graded banking substance and cross-border settlement but do not underwrite vessel-construction trade finance and have low integration with SWIFT-based marine letter-of-credit infrastructure. Unregulated foreign EMI providers (Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise) cannot hold the construction-milestone escrow balance or issue standby LCs and are restricted by FINMA Circular 2022/14 substance and pass-porting rules. Such EMIs are legally usable only as supplementary FX-settlement wallets for minor supplier payments; core inbound capital escrow, instalment LC collection, and shareholder-loan repayments must run through UBS or ZKB to preserve IFRS audit integrity and CDB20 compliance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax: 8.5% of after-tax profit (equivalent to 7.83% of pre-tax profit); combined federal + cantonal + communal effective rate ranging from approximately 11.9% (Zug) to 21.0% (Vaud) depending on the canton of the registered office; the rate for the Zurich cantonal seat commonly applied to an industrial shipyard is approximately 19.7%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividend distributions of an Aktiengesellschaft, payable by the AG to the Federal Tax Administration, charged to the shareholder-beneficiary subject to refund/treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The return must be filed within the period set by the cantonal authority, ordinarily by 31 March of the year following the tax period (Art. 124 para. 1 Federal Act on Direct Tax, DBG); an automatic or granted extension is available on written request up to 30 September (Zurich) or 31 October (Geneva) for corporations with audited accounts, and in any event before the tax period is assessed by a final decision.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Switzerland does not codify OECD Local File/Master File thresholds in domestic statute; the ESTV Transfer Pricing Circular (Verrechnungspreis-Rundschreiben, November 2020) applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the documentation duty arises as soon as cross-border related-party transactions are declared in the tax return; in administrative practice contemporaneous TP documentation is expected whenever the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds CHF 1 million per counterparty or the AG belongs to a group; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for Swiss-headquartered (and secondary/ surrogate) groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million (OECD BEPS threshold), filed electronically with the ESTV within 12 months of the fiscal year-end and exchanged under the multilateral competent authority agreement.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has not enacted Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) legislation in the Caribbean/Emirati sense; substance is protected through: (i) corporate residence determined by statutory seat or place of effective management (Art. 50 DBG); (ii) the statutory general anti-abuse rule (Steuerumgehung, Art. 219 DBG) by which structures lacking economic purpose, erected solely to save taxes, are recharacterised — for a shipyard this means that any holding or invoicing entity without personnel, shipyard capacity or decision-making in Switzerland is disregarded; (iii) the anti-hybrid and Limitation of Benefits clauses embedded in DTA relief, and (iv) the Pillar Two Minimum Tax Act (Mindeststeuergesetz, in force 1 January 2024): Swiss-resident groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million are subject to a 15% qualified global minimum tax through IIR/UTPR and a Swiss qualified domestic top-up tax (QDMTT); the shipyard must model the GloBE effective rate and file the GloBE information return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) / Federal and Cantonal Direct Tax

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to the amount of tax evaded (Art. 175 DBG); in particularly serious cases up to three times the evaded tax (Art. 176 DBG), plus default/evasion interest on the reassessed tax, and exclusion of the deductibility of the fine. Cantonal law attaches parallel penalties up to similar multiples.

Director personal liability: The CEO/CFO and the director responsible for accounting are jointly targeted as the acting organ: criminal fines are imposed personally, the share register does not protect the signatory, and the Federal Criminal Court may order a professional ban (Berufsverbot) of up to five years and seizure of assets; civil piercing of the veil (Durchgriffshaftung) applies where the AG is used as a mere facade for the shareholder's personal affairs (e.g., BGE 138 III 195).
Tax Deception (Steuerbetrug) and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to two years or fine (Art. 186 DBG); withholding tax evasion: fine of up to five times the withheld tax under Art. 72 of the Federal Withholding Tax Act and liability for the tax at 100% plus 5% per annum interest; in international group cases the evasion may qualify as criminal money laundering under Art. 305bis SCC with imprisonment up to three years (five years in aggravated cases), confiscation of the proceeds and freezable assets in multiple jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable for false certification of the annual accounts and for the failure to deduct the withholding tax (Civil and criminal liability in solidum: the unpaid tax, penalty and interest can be recovered from the signatory directors; travel bans and international arrest warrants can be issued by the Office of the Attorney General once flight risk is demonstrated).
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse & Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under Art. 219 DBG (Steuerumgehung): consequent assessment of the tax as if the economic substance were at the place of management; repayment of treaty relief; 35% withholding tax on the disguised dividend plus 5% default interest and penalties; pursuant to the DTA limitation-of-benefits clauses, the foreign parent may lose all treaty protection on the repatriated stream.

Director personal liability: The directors who signed the substance-less agreements are jointly liable for the additional corporate tax, withholding tax and procedural fines; in aggravated abuse the court may set aside the legal entity (veil-piercing) and order restitution of the tax advantage from the shareholder individually; board members may also face disqualification from serving as administrators for up to five years.
Anti-Money Laundering & Sanctions (Trading with a 'Medium-Risk, Enhanced' profile)

Statutory sanction: As a merchant in goods (AMLA Art. 2 para. 3 scope), the shipyard is a financial intermediary only when receiving cash ≥ CHF 100,000; violation of due diligence and reporting obligations is sanctioned by imprisonment up to one year or a fine; money laundering under Art. 305bis SCC is punishable by imprisonment up to three years (five for aggravated cases) and total confiscation of assets; breach of Swiss embargoes (Embargo Act) entails imprisonment up to ten years in qualified cases and blacklisting of the company from public procurement and export financing.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are prosecuted as perpetrators or accomplices; personal imprisonment up to five years, prohibition on exercising management functions, seizure of personal real estate and bank accounts, issuance of an international wanted notice, and joint and several civil liability to the company for losses caused by the violation (Art. 717 Code of Obligations duty of care).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income by the canton with reconstitution of an arm's length profit, additional corporate tax plus interest; hidden profit distribution reclassification triggers 35% withholding tax on the excess interest or royalty paid to the foreign shareholder; the ESTV may impose the procedural fine under Art. 174 DBG (up to CHF 10,000) for non-production of records; for groups >EUR 750 million revenue, penalty for missing GloBE Information Return and top-up tax up to 15% effective rate.

Director personal liability: The finance director and management board are jointly liable for the tax adjustment and the punitive withholding tax payment; in the presence of fraudulent documentation, the matter crosses into tax deception with personal criminal liability (up to two years imprisonment) and, in cross-border cases, the secondment of the case to the Federal Criminal Police for money-laundering assessment of fund flows.
