Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. For Quebec-specific QST, provincial tax returns, and provincial registration: Revenu Québec; portal: revenuquebec.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including a non-resident limited partnership, must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, normally through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service using Form RC151 (or by mail where online access is unavailable). Registration for the GST/HST program account is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a rolling four-quarter period. For QST, registration with Revenu Québec is required under analogous small-supplier thresholds; the partnership must also obtain a Québec enterprise number (NEQ) through the Registraire des entreprises if it carries on business in Québec. No minimum capital or resident ownership condition applies to LP registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (income-tax status as a fiscally transparent partnership; the LP itself does not pay federal/provincial income tax, but files an information return and allocates income to partners). For GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate registrant and must collect tax on taxable supplies. — A foreign entity, including a non-resident limited partnership, must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, normally through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service using Form RC151 (or by mail where online access is unavailable). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (income-tax status as a fiscally transparent partnership; the LP itself does not pay federal/provincial income tax, but files an information return and allocates income to partners). For GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate registrant and must collect tax on taxable supplies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal or Québec income tax law. A limited partnership is transparent by statutory default; if all partners are Canadian residents it may qualify as a 'Canadian partnership' under Income Tax Act s.102(2). Hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss.12.7, 18.4-18.5, and 20(1) apply to cross-border arrangements involving hybrid entities. For indirect tax, the LP may voluntarily register for GST/HST and QST before exceeding the threshold and, once registered, has the usual elections for streamlined accounting and related-party supplies where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST threshold for small suppliers; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST under Revenu Québec rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of industrial MRO goods may be zero-rated as an export under Excise Tax Act Part V of Schedule VI where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a non-resident buyer outside Canada, possession and title pass outside Canada, and the goods are exported within the required period (generally within 60 days after the supply or within a longer period accepted by the CRA). The supplier must hold documentary commercial evidence of export and of the non-resident destination, including purchase orders, shipping records, and payment from the foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/CAED/eManifest export report filed with the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods and destination
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / carrier document proving physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT/wire payment confirmation from the non-resident customer
  • Purchase order or sales contract with the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoice mandate in Canada or Québec. Invoicing is returns-based; commercial electronic invoices are accepted if they meet record-keeping standards. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million, and Revenu Québec requires electronic filing for applicable QST registrants. Records must be retained for six years.

CRA and Revenu Québec apply strict evidentiary tests before accepting zero-rating. If export documentation is missing or defective, the supply is recharacterized as taxable and the supplier is assessed for uncollected GST/HST and QST, with interest from the original remittance date and no offset for invoice underestimation. Penalties include false-statement penalties under Excise Tax Act s.285, gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the net tax under s.285.1, and an extended reassessment period of up to seven years in circumstances of misrepresentation or neglect (ETA s.298). Revenu Québec may impose analogous QST penalties. Wilful evasion or fraudulent export zero-rating can lead to criminal prosecution under federal tax statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the IMF Article VIII framework. There are no general exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no licensing or administrative authorization for routine foreign-currency transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and deliverable in interbank markets. Profit remittances by a Quebec limited partnership to non-resident partners are permitted without Bank of Canada or AMF approval, but are subject to federal Part XIII withholding tax on deemed distributions to non-residents and to treaty-based rate reductions where applicable. The Investment Canada Act imposes a separate national-security and net-benefit review for certain foreign acquisitions of control, but this is not an FX control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized foreign-currency accounts at domestic or foreign banks without mandatory conversion timelines and there is no prescribed conversion ratio. Cross-border transaction reporting obligations arise only for statistical and AML purposes, principally through the Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) data collected by financial institutions and under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) or Canada Border Services Agency export reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator; no dedicated foreign exchange administration exists, and the Minister of Finance and the Currency Act exercise residual authority over monetary and exchange-rate matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for direct use of offshore digital EMIs as the sole treasury clearing institution. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, any payment service provider serving Canadian end-users must register with the Bank of Canada and maintain prescribed safeguarding regimes; deposit-taking remains an exclusively Schedule I bank activity. An offshore EMI may be used for merchant acquiring or ancillary payment processing only through a registered Canadian PSP or in partnership with a Canadian chartered bank. Capital injection escrow, statutory settlement, and the core liquidity ledger must remain at an authorized Canadian bank or a wholly-owned foreign bank subsidiary in Canada. Quebec's Autorité des marchés financiers may also require provincial money-services-business registration in the event of remittance or currency-exchange services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for inbound capital is a SWIFT MT103 international wire from the foreign parent or investor to the Quebec LP's CAD current account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The beneficiary account must be in the legal name of the LP, and the wire instruction should include the phrase 'Limited Partnership Capital Contribution' in the payment details field to facilitate automatic ITRS classification, with the beneficiary bank's CIF code and the LP's Canadian business number. Banks will record the balance-of-payments purpose as direct investment equity in a Canadian enterprise; no FX approval is required, but the bank may request the capital call, partnership resolution, and closing balance sheet to substantiate the transaction. For U.S.-dollar injections retained for USD trade settlement, the financial institution may designate a USD domestic settlement account serviced through CIBC, RBC, or BMO U.S. branches, subject to the same source-of-funds documentation.Cross-border trade disbursements for industrial MRO procurement and settlement will use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for correspondent-bank settlements where necessary. Commercial letters of credit for recurring supplier financing should be issued through the Canadian account bank's trade desk in Montreal or Toronto, advised and confirmed through the beneficiary's bank, with documents compliant under UCP 600.
