Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to carry out taxable activities in Switzerland must register for Swiss VAT using Form 1520 or the Swiss VAT portal; the cantonal tax authority assigns corporate income tax identifiers upon registration of the Swiss AG or its branch. The Swiss VAT number and tax UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) are aligned. A non-resident company may apply directly for VAT registration once turnover or voluntary VAT registration is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is a resident corporation subject to federal, cantonal, and communal corporate income tax on worldwide taxable income, with treaty and territorial limitations for foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate. Pass-through treatment is not available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Swiss law. Available elections include VAT group taxation under Art. 52 ff. VAT Act for closely linked taxable persons, small business VAT exemption below the statutory threshold, and partial FIFOS-related timing elections under the VAT Act. Hybrid mismatch elections are generally not available; Switzerland applies OECD-aligned anti-hybrid rules and preferential regimes have been abolished.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 in annual worldwide taxable turnover excluding VAT-exempt supplies; voluntary VAT registration is available for businesses below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating without input VAT deduction loss applies to the export of goods to destinations outside Switzerland and to certain offshore services supplied to recipients abroad. The supplier must substantiate the physical export through customs documents and prove that the recipient is a foreign person or validly carrying out offshore activities. Film licensing royalties paid by foreign licensees generally qualify as exported services under the Swiss VAT Act if the recipient is outside Switzerland.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR transport document
  • Sales contract or licence agreement
  • Commercial invoice issued to foreign acquirer
  • Evidence of cross-border payment, such as SWIFT credit advice
  • Foreign acquirer VAT registration or business identity document where applicable

Switzerland does not mandate fiscal clearance or real-time tax authority API stamping. Electronically issued invoices are legally recognized, and the QR-bill standard is the national structured invoice format. No immediate online VAT reporting to the tax authority is required at invoice level; VAT returns are filed periodically.

Swiss tax and customs authorities may conduct retroactive audits and reassess zero-rated export supplies where documentation is deficient. Fraudulent claims for export zero-rating are subject to value-added tax clawback plus default interest at 4% per year, and penalties under Art. 82 and 83 of the Swiss VAT Act of up to four times the VAT evaded. In serious cases imprisonment is possible and controlling persons may be held primarily liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible with a free-floating regime; Switzerland imposes no capital controls and no separate exchange-control approval is required for current-account royalty flows or capital-account equity injections. Profit and royalty remittance from a Swiss AG is unrestricted at the FX/treasury layer; however, banks will require documentary evidence of the underlying licensing contract, beneficial ownership, and tax status because the 35% Swiss withholding tax on distributions is collected at source. Capital vs current account classification follows IMF BPM6; both are open, but high-value cash transactions above CHF 100,000 trigger AMLA identification duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law mandates no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts or copyright royalty proceeds. Corporate exporters may hold foreign currency balances freely in any bank; reporting obligations are imposed only on banks for SNB balance-of-payments statistics (annual corporate registration with the SNB is required only when a Swiss resident is a lending/borrowing relationship exceeding CHF 10 million, which is not applicable to standard film licensing treasury).

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank, governed by the National Bank Act 2003 and the Federal Act on Currency and Means of Payment; prudential conduct supervised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Swiss digital banks regulated as fintech licensees under FINMA (e.g., Yuh, Neon, SMNT) permit rapid remote onboarding and SEPA/SWIFT-adjacent transfers, but cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and typically cap high-value treasury volumes. Non-Swiss EU EMIs may only operate via a FINMA-approved branch or Swiss outsourcing arrangement under the Banking Act Art. 1b and FinIA; therefore, an offshore EMI as sole treasury rail is legally insufficient for LC settlements and is contraindicated; use only as a supplementary collection conduit for micro-royalty receipts.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax rate levied on post-tax profit (effective pre-tax equivalent approximately 7.83%); combined cantonal/communal rates vary by jurisdiction, producing an aggregate effective rate of approximately 12%–24% for most profit-active companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends paid by a Swiss stock corporation (AG). Reduced rates may apply under Swiss double tax agreements (DTAs) up to the withholding tax cap in the relevant treaty; under the Switzerland-EU Savings Agreement, certain EU parent corporations may be eligible for a reduced 0% rate on dividends under the parent-subsidiary directive parity provision.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed within six months after the end of the fiscal year; cantonal extensions are granted upon request, but the formal deadline cannot generally exceed 12 months after fiscal year-end. Interim payments of federal and cantonal corporate income tax are due quarterly based on prior assessments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and submitted upon request by the tax authority if the annual gross revenue of the Swiss taxpayer exceeds CHF 10 million; Master File is required when the group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds CHF 50 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies to Swiss-parented groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CHF 900 million and for Swiss subsidiaries of foreign groups with similar group thresholds. Transfer pricing documentation must be produced in German, French, or Italian (or accompanied by an official-certified translation into the tax authority's language).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime of the type found in the UAE or other tax havens. However, Swiss tax law and anti-abuse rules require a corporation to have actual economic substance, including a registered office at its declared Swiss seat, a valid VAT and employer registration (where applicable), physical business premises, business bank account, records, bookkeeping, and able directors with authority. DTA benefits are denied under the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and the OECD principal purpose test (PPT) where an arrangement was designed to obtain treaty benefits without genuine economic substance. The Swiss Federal Tax Administration may disregard a corporate structure or reclassify transactions as a permanent establishment if substance is insufficient.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Active tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) and fraudulent misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to three times the amount of evaded tax, plus default interest and the full back-tax assessment for up to ten years (longer in cases of fraud). In aggravated cases involving fraudulent conduct, the tax administration may order criminal prosecution before cantonal courts.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or are otherwise complicit in false declarations can be personally liable for the tax arrears and fines; the criminal penalty may include imprisonment for up to three years (up to ten years for repeat or large-scale fraud) and travel bans may be imposed by the cantonal tax authority.
Transfer pricing mispricing and documentary default

Statutory sanction: Praxis adjustment by the tax authority increasing taxable income by the arm's-length price differential, assessment of 5% late-payment interest on the additional tax, and penalties for incomplete or late disclosure of transfer pricing documentation, generally up to CHF 10,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the additional Swiss tax and penalties if they intentionally instructed or approved the mispricing, had knowledge of the deficiency, and failed to take corrective measures. Swiss insolvency law provides for creditor clawback claims against directors where corporate assets were depleted by unlawful transactions.
Shell corporation lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief, requalification of income as distributed to the ultimate beneficial owner, and reinstatement of 35% Swiss withholding tax on the undeclared distribution. The tax authority may also reassess the company as a managed or foreign-controlled permanent establishment and apply Swiss tax on the entire net base.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid withheld tax and company tax debts under Swiss tax debt liability rules (Art. 12 ff. Federal Act on the Collection of Taxes). Directors who knowingly maintained a sham company may be subject to criminal penalties for aiding and abetting tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment, and may be prohibited from managing Swiss entities by the commercial registry in cases of repeated non-compliance.
Non-collection and non-remittance of Swiss withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the full 35% withholding tax on gross payments, plus default interest of 5% per annum. Willful failure to remit WHT constitutes a criminal offense under Art. 186 ff. of the Federal Direct Tax Act, with a fine of up to CHF 500,000 and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax if they were knowingly involved in the decision and the company is unable to pay. Swiss criminal law provision for tax offenses allows fines against directors acting in the corporate interest, and travel restrictions may be imposed pending criminal investigation.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) transparency violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), financial intermediaries and companies carrying out commercial transactions in cash of over CHF 100,000 have reporting obligations; violations are punishable with fines up to CHF 500,000. If the film licence income is traceable to tax evasion or corruption, the assets can be frozen and confiscated under the Swiss Criminal Code proficiency law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted under the Swiss Criminal Code for failing to conduct the required identification of beneficial owners, failure to report suspicious transaction reports to the Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (MROS), and for participating in a criminal organisation; personal sanctions include imprisonment of up to five years, seizure of personal assets, and permanent exclusion from serving as a company director.
