Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST, income tax of certain partnerships, and custom reporting; Revenu Québec for Quebec QST and provincial tax registration. Digital portals: CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec Cliquer pour affaires.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada/Quebec must register for GST/HST with the CRA and QST with Revenu Québec. Procedure: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with CRA via Form RC1 or online registration; (2) open a GST/HST account under the BN using the same BN if not already added; (3) register separately with Revenu Québec for a QST number using the business registration form or online portal; (4) if the LP has no Canadian permanent establishment but is registered for GST/HST as a non-resident supplier, designate a Canadian representative for service of documents. There is no minimum capital or statutory paid-up capital requirement for a Canadian limited partnership, and non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A limited partnership is fiscally transparent; the partnership itself is not subject to income tax, and each partner is allocated a share of the partnership income or loss. Non-resident partners are taxable on their Canadian-source share of income from carrying on business in Canada, subject to applicable treaty and withholding provisions.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. Tax classification follows the legal form under provincial and federal law. Partnerships may make a fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1; a GST/HST substantial-presence election may be available for newly incorporated/registered entities; hybrid mismatch and foreign tax credit limitation elections may apply under ss. 18.4–18.5 and related provisions; QST relief and input tax refund elections may be made with Revenu Québec for selected transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST small supplier threshold; CAD 30,000 for QST small supplier threshold. Non-residents and digital-economy suppliers may be required to register regardless of threshold if they make taxable supplies to persons in Quebec or Canada outside treaty-covered exporting activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada and to certain supplies of services/intangibles made to non-residents, under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) and corresponding provisions of the Quebec Taxation Act (QST). To claim 0% for battery-grade lithium carbonate powder, the supplier must establish that the goods were shipped from Canada to a foreign destination as part of the sale, the buyer is a non-resident, and the sale/transfer is not a sham or artificial attempt to obtain input tax credits. Services qualify as exported only where performance occurs primarily outside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident, subject to look-through rules.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or electronic export declaration via the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, quantity, unit price, H.S. classification (ISIC 4662), and reference to zero-rated export supply
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing Canadian port of loading and foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire/SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Purchase order or sales contract setting out incoterms and title transfer (e.g., FOB, FCA, CIF)
  • Customs broker or carrier records confirming physical export (including PARS/ACI export transmission or cargo control documents)

Canada does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital tax stamping for GST/HST/QST. Registered suppliers may issue electronic invoices provided the relevant documentary and information requirements under the Excise Tax Act and Revenu Québec rules are satisfied. Filing and remittance are generally periodic through CRA and Revenu Québec portals. Quebec is progressively developing mandatory e-invoicing reforms for future implementation, but no operational real-time clearance mandate applies at this stage.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny the zero-rating if the required export evidence is missing or inconsistent. Consequences include reassessment of GST/HST and QST at the applicable rates, interest from the time of the original return, and penalty under Excise Tax Act s. 280 (6% of tax or refund amount) or s. 285 (20% for repeated failures), plus false-statement penalties under s. 285.1 and s. 285.2 for gross negligence. Criminal prosecution for wilful tax evasion under s. 327.1 may result in fines and/or imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks of input tax credits or GST/QST refunds can extend beyond the normal assessment period where misrepresentation or fraud is established, with no limitation period in the case of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and OECD codes. CAD floats freely with no restriction on inward/outward remittances, profit repatriation, or foreign-currency accounts. Regulatory friction arises solely from documentary and sanctions-compliance requirements: FINTRAC reporting, CRA Section 233.3 foreign-asset disclosure (Form T1135) for specified foreign property above CAD 100,000, and anti-money-laundering KYC for each wire. Partnership allocations to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax unless treaty-reduced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds in Canada; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely. For trade-reporting purposes, export entries are filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Act; proceeds held outside Canada must be reported on CRA Form T1135 if aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; no exchange-control quotas or repatriation deadlines apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; foreign exchange and cross-border money-services compliance administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and by Global Affairs Canada for economic sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplementary payment-rail accelerator, but legally restricted from true treasury custody. A foreign digital EMI cannot operate a deposit-taking business in Canada unless authorized under the Bank Act or Quebec's deposit-institution rules; at most it may register with FINTRAC as a money-services business (MSB) for localized conversion/payment services. For LC issuance, escrow of capital, or commodity trade settlement, an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank is mandatory. EMIs can usefully handle intra-group liquidity for minor euro/dollar expenses but cannot provide the banking-grade trade finance instruments required for lithium-carbonate import settlement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined headline rate (15% federal Part I general corporate rate + 11.5% Quebec general corporate rate) applied at partner level; the Quebec Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96 and pays 0% entity-level corporate income tax. Non-resident partners are taxable on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income and are subject to a 15% refundable withholding deposit under Income Tax Regulations s.805.
