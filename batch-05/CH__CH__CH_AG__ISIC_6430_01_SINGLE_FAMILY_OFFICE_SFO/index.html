Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

CHF 100,000 minimum nominal share capital; at least 20% of the nominal capital (in all cases a minimum of CHF 50,000) must be paid up and verified by the board of directors. The paid-up amount must be held in a blocked escrow account with a licenced Swiss bank until registration.

Funding & unlock path: The lessee or landowner must obtain a fire safety approval certificate from the cantonal fire protection authority for ongoing workplace use. A structural suitability certificate from the cantonal building inspectorate is required if the premises exceed load thresholds or are retrofitted. Workplace health and safety compliance under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG, Art. 6) and Ordinance 3 to the Labour Act is required; if the SFO employs more than 50 persons or operates particular workplaces, an occupational physician and safety specialist must be engaged (EKAS guidelines).

Paid-in capital rule: CHF 100,000 minimum nominal share capital; at least 20% of the nominal capital (in all cases a minimum of CHF 50,000) must be paid up and verified by the board of directors. The paid-up amount must be held in a blocked escrow account with a licenced Swiss bank until registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions, ESTV/AFC), Eigerstrasse 65, 3003 Bern; VAT matters are exclusively governed by its VAT Division. Income and capital taxes of a Swiss AG are administered by the cantonal tax authority of the statutory seat (e.g., Kantonales Steueramt Zug, Neugasse 2, 6300 Zug). The official electronic filing infrastructure is the ESTV e-tax-office portal (www.estv.admin.ch) for VAT and the cantonal tax e-filing systems for profit/capital tax. Commercial register data flows to the tax authorities through the central UID directory.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign-owned Swiss AG: (1) Notarize the articles of incorporation and register the AG with the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister), which simultaneously triggers automatic issuance of the Swiss business identification number UID-CH/IDE through the Federal Statistical Office. The UID-CH functions as the statutory Swiss tax identification number/TIN for legal entities and is used for all tax authorities and VAT returns. (2) Assess VAT entrepreneur status under Art. 10 Swiss VAT Act: a passive holding SFO that merely holds participations and collects dividends is not an entrepreneur; an SFO that invoices management, custody-oversight, or investment-advisory services to group entities or the family in excess of CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover must file the VAT registration form (e-office/UID portal) with the ESTV VAT Division. (3) For a non-resident entity without Swiss business seat but with Swiss VAT obligations, a professional fiscal representative domiciled in Switzerland must be appointed under Art. 66 VAT Act. (4) For ordinary corporate income tax, the AG files annual tax returns with the competent cantonal tax administration; the UID-CH is the master identifier and the Federal Tax Administration assigns a separate federal tax-reference number if required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Aktiengesellschaft is a limited-liability corporation with independent legal personality; it is a resident taxpayer subject to Swiss federal profit tax (8.5% of pre-tax profit after federal tax deduction), cantonal profit tax and cantonal/communal capital tax. No pass-through or look-through to shareholders is permitted for corporate-income-tax purposes. Territorial scope: Swiss-resident companies are taxed on profits economically connected to Switzerland plus profits attributable to domestic and foreign permanent establishments; profits of foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are unilaterally and treaty-exempt (territoriality principle). SFO holding structures benefit from statutory participation relief on qualifying dividend/capital-gain income; the former cantonal holding privilege has been repealed under the 2020 Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (STAF).

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election: the AG legal form is mandatory for limited-liability business vehicles. Available statutory elections/regimes: (i) participation relief election for qualifying participations (≥10% shareholding or acquisition cost ≥ CHF 1 million) under Art. 70 DBG, removing dividend and capital-gain income with a pro-rata corresponding expense deduction; (ii) shareholder/company election under the 'ordinary procedure' to maintain and repay reserves from capital contributions free of Swiss withholding and income tax; (iii) optional VAT group treatment (Unternehmensgruppe, Art. 21 Swiss VAT Act) for closely-integrated affiliated entities under unified management; (iv) patent-box (IP box) cantonal election for self-developed qualifying IP; (v) no consolidated fiscal group exists in Switzerland, so each AG is separately assessed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 per annum of taxable turnover from supplies (Art. 10 para 2 lit. a MWSTG), computed net of VAT. A pure passive SFO that performs no commercial supply is not a VAT entrepreneur. If the SFO charges custody, reporting, controlling, administrative, or investment-advisory services to the family, the single-family-office services are generally standard-rated (8.1%) unless they constitute exempt management of collective investment schemes under Art. 21 para 2 no. 19 MWSTG. Consequently, a fee-charging SFO crosses the CHF 100,000 threshold and must mandatorily register; registration is also required for all non-exempt supplies. For non-resident service suppliers with reverse-charge supplies to Switzerland, separate nexus and fiscal-representative rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Swiss customs territory if the export is evidenced by a customs declaration and the recipient is abroad (Art. 23 para 1 MWSTG). Services: a zero-rated service supply under Art. 23 para 2 MWSTG requires that the recipient is domiciled/resident abroad and the benefit of the service is actually and economically used abroad; additionally, services directly connected to exported movable assets may be zero-rated. For the SFO, asset-management, advisory, controlling, and fiduciary services rendered to non-resident family shareholders or foreign SPVs qualify as zero-rated export services where the place of effective benefit lies outside Switzerland. The SFO must pragmatically evidence foreign use by contract stipulations, foreign VAT/TIN references of recipients, board minutes, and payment flows from foreign bank accounts.

  • Customs export declaration recorded in the Swiss customs system (e-dec web/export declaration with movement reference number) proving removal of goods from Swiss customs territory.
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure: Bill of Lading, Airwaybill, or CMR consignment note issued by the independent carrier.
  • Export commercial invoice and underlying contract; for export services, the service agreement with a foreign place-of-benefit clause, the foreign recipient's confirmation/assessment, and cross-border remittance records from the private-banking rails (SWIFT/KBV credit advice) substantiating the foreign consideration.

Switzerland imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory government API connection, and no digital stamping of invoices for VAT purposes. The ESTV accepts paper, PDF, and structured e-invoices; electronic invoicing follows voluntary private standards (Swiss XML, SIX eBill, and the Swiss QR-bill payment slip where applicable). A tamper-proof certified cash-register system is required only where annual cash receipts exceed CHF 500,000, which is irrelevant to a bank-wire/custody-based SFO. VAT returns and audit files are transmitted through the ESTV e-tax-office portal; an audit trail of underlying transactions, records, and export proofs must be retained for ten years.

Switzerland maintains no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied by Swiss banks and tax auditors as the evidentiary reference for commercial payment cycles and continued substantiation of cross-border supplies. Where zero-rated export supplies are claimed without valid customs documentation or foreign-use evidence, the ESTV retroactively reassesses the standard VAT rate, adds default interest at 5% p.a., and imposes tax-evasion penalties of up to three times the evaded tax for wilful misconduct. The correction/limitation period is ten years for tax fraud and five years for ordinary omissions. Fraudulently manufactured export documents also trigger criminal prosecution under the Swiss Criminal Code (Art. 146 StGB) and, for qualified VAT fraud, potentially imprisonment; participation in sham offshore planning does not cure the civil clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full bilateral convertibility on both current-account and capital-account items. The CHF is freely floating and fully convertible; there is no exchange-control statute, no repatriation approval regime and no restriction on non-resident ownership of CHF assets. Practical profit-remittance frictions are purely fiscal and corporate-law based: 35% Swiss federal withholding tax on dividends and certain interest payments (reduced to 0–15% under applicable double-taxation treaties, refundable under the statutory 3-year applicant window, or eliminated for qualifying parent shareholders meeting participation conditions), plus the AG's statutory distributability test (accumulated profits and audited reserves under Art. 660–671 CO). No central-bank pre-authorisation, FX allocation or beneficial-interest clearance is required for a Single-Family-Office AG to pay dividends, service shareholder loans, repay intercompany debt or repatriate liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Receipts from exports, divestments and asset sales may lawfully be retained indefinitely in the invoicing currency, in any convertible foreign currency, onshore or offshore. The only compliance layer is statistical: Swiss-resident legal entities must submit direct declarations to the SNB (Grenzüberschreitender Zahlungsverkehr forms and balance-of-payments direct surveys) when cross-border payment turnover or foreign assets/liabilities exceed the SNB's reporting thresholds. In practice the relevant Swiss clearing bank reports the substantial majority of such flows on a delegated basis, so direct filing applies principally to proprietary foreign accounts and intercompany balances maintained outside the tier-1 Swiss banking perimeter.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank (Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera) — sole monetary and foreign-exchange-administration authority; exchange-rate policy and balance-of-payments statistics are executed by the SNB in coordination with the Federal Department of Finance (FDF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: LOW for core treasury, capital-escrow and custody rails; MEDIUM only for ancillary, low-value cross-border disbursements (e.g., operational expenses, travel, small vendor payments). Legal constraints: foreign digital e-money institutions (UK/EU/Malta/Lithuania EMIs) are not passportable into Switzerland and hold no FINMA banking licence; under the revised Swiss Anti-Money-Laundering Act (AMLA/GwG revision in force 2024–2025), payment-service providers operating commercially into Switzerland require FINMA authorisation. An SFO AG may act as a customer of an offshore EMI for small-scale payments, but no Swiss tier-1 bank will accept an EMI as correspondent or settlement agent, and SNB cross-border-payment statistics cannot be discharged through an unregulated EMI. Consequently, EMI rails are unsuitable for the CHF capital-escrow function, for fiduciary securities-custody flows and for the MT103/SSI-controlled trade-payment architecture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the CHF share-capital injection: SWIFT MT103 (or SIC/euroSIC for CHF/EUR payment instructions) addressed to the SFO's blocked Capital Escrow Account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the CH_AG at UBS Switzerland AG or Zürcher Kantonalbank. The payment reference must state the statutory purpose: 'Capital contribution – subscription of shares pursuant to Art. 629–632 CO / Einzahlung auf das Aktienkapital' and, where available, the SWIFT purpose-code category S001 (capital subscription). The receiving bank issues the Sperrkonto-Bestätigung (escrow confirmation) to the Swiss notary for the Commercial-Register capital filing. For inbound foreign-sourced funds the bank additionally requires: (i) identification of the remitter and of the remitter's own bank with full MT103 transparency; (ii) the underlying board resolution approving the capital increase and naming the subscriber; (iii) proof that the funds correspond to the subscription table; and (iv) a declaration consenting to SNB cross-border-payment statistics (Grenzüberschreitender Zahlungsverkehr) reporting. If the inbound currency is not CHF, the bank effects a spot conversion at or near the SNB reference rate; no SNB licence or approval is required for the conversion itself.Approved clearing rails for outbound trade, investment and treasury disbursements: (a) onshore CHF/EUR liquidity through the SNB-operated SIC and euroSIC systems; (b) cross-border multi-currency payments via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) and MT202 COV (interbank cover payments), with standard settlement instructions (SSI) locked into the bank's treasury system to eliminate fraud; (c) commercial and standby Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600 / ISP98 for goods-and-services trade underlying the family office's real-asset investments; (d) securities and custody settlement via SIX SIS (Swiss central securities depository) with Euroclear/Clearstream links for cross-border holdings; and (e) CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) for major-currency FX spot and forward legs to mitigate Herstatt risk. SEPA direct debits and credit transfers are used for low-value European operational disbursements. Each outbound instruction is matched against the approved treasury plan and the KYC-documented beneficiary registry.Vanilla Outright FX Forward (CHF/USD, CHF/EUR, CHF/GBP and other freely convertible pairs) for transactional hedging of holdings and operational exposuresFX Swap / short-dated rollover (spot-forward combination) for treasury liquidity and funding managementCross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCS/CIRS) for long-term foreign-currency liabilities and investment-holdings financingNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) restricted to genuinely convertible but non-deliverable emerging-market currencies, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement / Swiss Master Agreement with a regulated counterparty; permissible because the SFO qualifies as a non-financial counterparty (NFC) below the FMIA (FinfraG) mandatory-clearing thresholdsOTC Vanilla European-Style FX Options (puts/calls on institutional counterparties) for premium-limited hedging, subject to the same ISDA/framework documentation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: LOW for core treasury, capital-escrow and custody rails; MEDIUM only for ancillary, low-value cross-border disbursements (e.g., operational expenses, travel, small vendor payments). Legal constraints: foreign digital e-money institutions (UK/EU/Malta/Lithuania EMIs) are not passportable into Switzerland and hold no FINMA banking licence; under the revised Swiss Anti-Money-Laundering Act (AMLA/GwG revision in force 2024–2025), payment-service providers operating commercially into Switzerland require FINMA authorisation. An SFO AG may act as a customer of an offshore EMI for small-scale payments, but no Swiss tier-1 bank will accept an EMI as correspondent or settlement agent, and SNB cross-border-payment statistics cannot be discharged through an unregulated EMI. Consequently, EMI rails are unsuitable for the CHF capital-escrow function, for fiduciary securities-custody flows and for the MT103/SSI-controlled trade-payment architecture.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% flat federal direct tax (charged on net profit after tax; equivalent to 7.83% on a pre-tax base), plus Zug cantonal and communal profits tax at a combined marginal rate of approximately 11.9% to 13.5%; total effective CIT envelope for a typical SFO-AG ranges between 11.9% and 14.0% depending on profit level and local multipliers.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer / impôt anticipé) imposed on all dividend distributions, profit shares and constructive profit distributions of a Swiss AG (Art. 4 VStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Default statutory deadline is 31 March following the fiscal year-end; the Zug tax authority routinely grants extensions to 30 June and, upon application, to 31 October; the federal element is time-aligned with the cantonal submission in the combined return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: mandatory when the consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CHF 80 million in the preceding fiscal year; Local File: mandatory for a Swiss taxpayer whose cross-border controlled transactions exceed CHF 10 million per annum; Country-by-Country Report: mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least CHF 900 million; intra-group financing transactions of more than CHF 5 million in any case trigger detailed documentation.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply UAE-style coded Economic Substance Regulations; substance is instead enforced through (i) the effective place of management residency test (Art. 50 DBG); (ii) the general anti-abuse clause of Art. 20 DBG which disregards legal arrangements lacking economic reality; (iii) the formal conditions for participation relief, WHT refunds, private tax rulings and treaty benefits requiring a registered Swiss office, active board management, genuine investment decision-making, adequate staffing, premises and business records of the SFO; and (iv) OECD/G20 BEPS standard on beneficial ownership and exchange of information (Art. 26 OECD Model). A letter-box SFO-AG without substantive activities in Zug will be denied reliefs, its rulings revoked and its treaty claims rejected retroactively.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Art. 175 DBG: fine equal to the amount of the evaded tax (minimum 25% of the evaded tax; increased to 150% for gross negligence); default interest at 4% p.a.; denial of loss carryforwards, participation relief and treaty deductions for the assessment years; restitution of the underpaid tax and penalties plus prosecution costs.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false return are jointly and severally liable for the fine and evaded tax unless exonerated by full delegation to a competent and independent tax adviser; criminal conviction of the director is recorded in the criminal register and may be relied upon in civil restitution suits by the canton.
Tax Fraud (Steuerbetrug) and Falsification of Documents

Statutory sanction: Art. 186 DBG (fraud): custodial sentence of up to five years or monetary penalty; confiscation of criminal proceeds under Art. 18 of the Federal Act on Administrative Criminal Law; public announcement of the judgment in the official gazette; automatic disqualification from serving as a director of a Swiss company or from acting in a regulated financial function.

Director personal liability: Direct criminal liability on the natural person who executes or orders the forgery; the corporate veil does not shield the officer; restitution of the full tax shortfall is ordered civilly, and the canton may attach personal assets of the director; a final conviction prevents the director from holding mandates in any Swiss regulated enterprise.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Art. 20 DBG anti-abuse: retroactive annulment of participation exemption, capital tax favours, WHT refunds and any private tax ruling; reassessment of the full tax deficiency with 4% p.a. interest; penalty of up to 10% of reassessed tax; possible forced deregistration from the commercial register after the substance verification; fines under Art. 174 DBG for each omitted filing.

Director personal liability: If the company is dissolved or assets are distributed without liquidation formalities, directors are personally liable for all outstanding taxes and debts of the AG (Art. 756–760 CO); criminal travel bans and asset freezes are issued under international mutual assistance where funds are concealed; liability may extend to shadow directors and family trustees.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Hidden Dividend Distribution

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the Swiss profit base by the Federal Tax Administration under Art. 58 DBG; recharacterisation of the excessive payment as an upstream dividend distributions subject to 35% WHT with the Swiss AG as debt-paying entity; fine of 25%–100% of evaded tax under Art. 175 DBG; interest 4% p.a.; documentation penalty of up to CHF 10,000 and loss of evidentiary weight of all accompanying agreements.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length fees owe restitution to the company for the entire excess amount plus interest (Art. 754 CO); personal criminal liability for evasion if they had knowledge of the profit shift; underlying documentation is used by both civil liquidators and the tax prosecutor; in aggravated cases, imprisonment up to three years.
Money Laundering / Unlicensed Financial Market Activity

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code Art. 305bis: up to five years imprisonment for intentional money laundering; Art. 305ter: up to three years for negligent conduct; Art. 44 AMLA: up to five years for unlicensed financial intermediation; fines up to CHF 2 million for legal persons under Art. 12 of the General Administrative Criminal Law; FINMA profit disgorgement, asset confiscation, prohibition of the individuals and revocation of authorisations; all linked assets are attachable.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable as officers and may be imprisoned; civil liability under Art. 754 CO requires full restitution of laundered amounts and lost taxes; the court may issue travel bans and passport seizure under Art. 239 of the Swiss Criminal Procedure Code; disqualification from all financial market functions for up to ten years is automatic upon conviction.
