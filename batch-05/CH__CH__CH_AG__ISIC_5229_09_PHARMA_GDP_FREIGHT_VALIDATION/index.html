Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Zug (Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zug), supervised by the Federal Commercial Register Office (Eidgenössisches Amt für das Handelsregister, EHRA).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (OR/CO), Arts. 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft); specifically Art. 621 (minimum capital), Art. 626 (contents of Articles of Association), Art. 629 (list of subscribers and audit statement), Art. 640 (registration application), and Art. 643 (legal effect of commercial register entry); Commercial Register Ordinance (HRegV), Arts. 33-48.

Formation / constitutional filings

Public deed of incorporation (Statuten / acte constitutif) authenticated by a Swiss notary public, filed through the Commercial Register application form (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) for an Aktiengesellschaft.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 (or 20% of the nominal value attributable to the relevant share tranche, whichever is greater, but never below CHF 50,000) must be fully paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up funds, or the valuation report for non-cash contributions, must be deposited in a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) maintained with a Swiss bank or branch, with a written banker's confirmation of availability at the board's free disposal; such funds remain blocked until the Commercial Register entry is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni ATC), official e-services portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch. VAT, federal corporate income tax, withholding tax and stamp duties are administered federally; cantonal tax offices (e.g., Cantonal Tax Office of Zug or Zurich) concurrently levy cantonal and communal profit taxes. The federal business identification register is accessible via https://www.uid.ch.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent incorporates a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft by notarial deed before the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) of the canton of the registered office; upon registration, the Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer (UID, format CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx), which is the statutory federal tax ID. VAT registration is filed separately with the ESTV via the electronic VAT registration portal; the ESTV then issues the combined VAT identification number (CHE-xxx.xxx.xxx MWST/TVA/IVA) and sets the effective VAT registration date. A non-resident enterprise without a Swiss commercial register entry (i.e., no Swiss permanent establishment) registers directly with the ESTV under the simplified foreign-entity procedure, submitting foreign certificate of incorporation, proof of foreign business seat and turnover declaration. Payment of the VAT registration is settled via Swiss bank or postal account; standard processing time is 5–10 business days. After registration, the company must file periodic VAT returns (quarterly or semi-annual) and annual federal and cantonal corporate tax returns for the approved tax period.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — the Swiss AG is an opaque corporation taxable at entity level and is never pass-through. Resident AGs have unlimited federal and cantonal tax liability on worldwide profit (with cross-border attribution based on Swiss permanent establishment rules). Non-resident foreign enterprises are limited-taxable only on Swiss-source income (Swiss PE, Swiss real property, and statutory source categories). No income tax-exempt zone exists in Switzerland; bonded warehouses and free ports (e.g., Zurich Free Port) merely suspend import VAT and customs duties. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — the Swiss AG is an opaque corporation taxable at entity level and is never pass-through. Resident AGs have unlimited federal and cantonal tax liability on worldwide profit (with cross-border attribution based on Swiss permanent establishment rules). Non-resident foreign enterprises are limited-taxable only on Swiss-source income (Swiss PE, Swiss real property, and statutory source categories). No income tax-exempt zone exists in Switzerland; bonded warehouses and free ports (e.g., Zurich Free Port) merely suspend import VAT and customs duties. Combined effective corporate income tax rates (2025) range from approximately 11.9% (Zug, with patent box benefits) to about 21.0% (Geneva/Bern), depending on canton and financing structure.

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no US-style check-the-box election; company-law form is conclusive and hybrid entity mismatches are curtailed by domestic anti-hybrid rules implementing BEPS Action 2. Available statutory elections and confirmed treatments include: (i) VAT group election (Mehrwertsteuergruppe) allowing closely held Swiss group entities to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) direct federal tax group election (Steuergruppe) enabling intra-group loss offset between Swiss group companies as one taxable entity; (iii) participation relief election (Beteiligungsabzug) for dividends and capital gains from qualifying participations of at least 10%; (iv) notional interest deduction (NID) election on safe-harbor Swiss equity, currently applied at rates around 2.0%–2.5% in most cantons; (v) cantonal patent box and R&D super-deduction elections under the TRAF reform; (vi) binding advance tax ruling procedure (Vorbescheid) to confirm cross-border and hybrid-mismatch treatment in advance; and (vii) capital contribution reserves election (KAG) permitting tax-free distribution of qualified paid-in equity. OECD Pillar Two transitional safe harbor and top-up calculations apply to Swiss-headquartered groups with global revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 of annual worldwide taxable turnover (art. 10 VAT Act) for Swiss-resident enterprises. Non-resident enterprises have no registration threshold exemption: they must register from the first taxable supply carried out in Switzerland unless the reverse-charge mechanism shifts VAT liability to the Swiss recipient. Voluntary registration from CHF 0 is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under art. 23 VAT Act applies to: (1) goods supplied and physically exported to a foreign country by the supplier or a commissioned transporter, with proof of export evidenced by the Swiss customs electronic export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung/e-dec); (2) services directly connected with exported movable goods, including cross-border freight, transit, handling, customs brokerage, and ancillary GDP logistics (temperature-controlled pharma transport, chain-of-custody services), provided the direct economic and documented link to the physically exported goods is proven; (3) services rendered entirely abroad, including the non-Swiss leg of international freight performed by a Swiss carrier or subcontractor; and (4) repairs or processing of movable goods after which the goods are exported. For GDP pharma freight validation, the zero-rating fails unless the logistics provider evidences the client purchase order, temperature-excursion records, route and consignee documentation, delivery timing, and customs exit validation. Domestic warehousing, storage, and mono-Swiss logistics without subsequent physical export remain taxable at 8.1% (or 2.6% for qualifying food/medicines).

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) filed electronically via e-dec/NCTS, including the validated export document (Ausfuhrschein) and Tares tariff/statistical code
  • Proof of physical exit: Bill of Lading (B/L or Sea Waybill), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR road consignment note, or CIM rail consignment note evidencing dispatch from Swiss territory to the foreign country
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the Swiss VAT invoice number, the MWST/TVA/IVA identification number, and the art. 23 zero-rating statement
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or ACH payment confirmation showing collection and FX repatriation of export proceeds to the Swiss bank account
  • GDP compliance record package: GDP transport SOPs, temperature-monitoring printouts, quarantine/pharma chain-of-custody notes, and appointment-time documentation
  • Customs presentation accepted by the border office or, for postal/relief consignments, the postal customs declaration (CN23/CP72) and proof of release

Switzerland does not currently operate a mandatory Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) regime, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API validation process. Electronic invoices are legally recognized under arts. 68–69 VAT Ordinance when sent with a qualified electronic signature (QES) or under an agreed EDI procedure. The commercial standard is the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) anchored in Swiss Payment Standards, while the SIX eBill platform dominates B2B/B2C digital invoicing. VAT-relevant records must be retained for 10 years and be readily accessible to the ESTV. B2G e-invoicing obligations are being phased in, and a federal consultation on mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing is open, but no fiscal clearance mandate is in force as of the current filing season.

ESTV and customs authorities conduct desk and on-site matrix audits of zero-rated pharma logistics chains; the taxpayer bears the burden of proof for the export throughout the statutory record-keeping period. If the export evidence is absent, incomplete, or internally inconsistent, the zero-rating is reversed retroactively via the VAT assessment (Nachsteuer) at the standard 8.1% rate, plus default interest of 5% per annum; previously refunded or offset VAT is clawed back with accrual interest. Limitation periods run 10 years for tax evasion and 5 years for simple under-declaration. False or deliberately fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under arts. 96–97 VAT Act read with the Federal Administrative Criminal Law Act (VStrR): fines of up to three times the evaded tax, an aggregate criminal fine cap of CHF 500,000 per levy, custodial penalties in aggravated cases, and exclusion from simplified export procedures. Concurrently, Swiss Customs Act sanctions (fines up to CHF 500,000 and forfeiture of goods) may be imposed for false customs declarations. Each GDP shipment file should therefore be immutable, audit-ready, and include tamper-evident temperature logging to withstand retrospective clawback review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc (CHF) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Switzerland complies with IMF Article VIII and OECD capital movement liberalisation codes; no exchange control approval or licence is required for profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or trade settlement in any currency. Non-resident investors may freely inject, hold, and repatriate capital. The SNB only conducts statistical cross-border payment reporting; this is not a permit or an authorisation requirement. For the GDP pharma freight company, no restrictions apply on treasury operations in CHF or foreign currency. Banks may apply operational due diligence for payments to high-risk jurisdictions, but there is no regulatory currency conversion barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be retained in foreign currency accounts either in Switzerland or abroad at the exporter's discretion. No statutory timeline exists for converting export earnings into CHF and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Under the National Bank Act, Swiss banks have a statistical duty to report cross-border payments, typically above a threshold, to the SNB; the exporter itself is not required to file if payments are routed through a Swiss bank. The underlying GDP freight services do not affect this treatment.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca nazionale svizzera

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – EU/EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) have no passporting rights in Switzerland because Switzerland is not an EEA member. Non-Swiss digital EMIs generally require a FINMA license or a partnership with a licensed Swiss payment provider to hold funds, execute payments, or issue IBANs for Swiss corporates. While offshore EMIs may be used as auxiliary gateways for foreign-currency disbursements, they cannot serve as the principal treasury account without a Swiss booking entity. FINMA licensing costs, substance expectations, and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations make such a route legally complex and operationally fragile; statutory pharma logistics payments customarily require a Swiss banking relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CHF 80 million group revenue threshold for mandatory TP documentation; contemporaneous Local File required for cross-border controlled transactions above CHF 5 million; Master File required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CHF 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Swiss substance doctrine under OECD TP Guidelines and Art. 58a DBG; no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to UAE, but substance requirements apply to secure DTA benefits and avoid recharacterization. Minimum substance indicators for a GDP pharma freight company: local board decisions, qualified staff, active logistics-oversight functions, bank accounts, and physical or contractual control of distribution/validation activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax up to 3x the evaded amount; criminal fine up to CHF 100,000; imprisonment up to five years under Art. 186/187 DBG.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and withholding tax; criminal conviction triggers personal restitution, travel bans, and up to five years’ imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DTA benefits denied; entity treated as sham; transactions recharacterized; taxes recalculated at full statutory rate; penalty for tax abuse and possible forced dissolution.

Director personal liability: Board or management subject to veil-piercing: personal liability for restored taxes, fines, asset seizures, and prohibition from acting as directors if evidence of fraudulent intent exists.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CHF 500,000; confiscation of proceeds; regulatory sanctions by the Swiss AML authority; revocation of required licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers face criminal liability under Art. 305bis/305ter StGB up to five years’ imprisonment; imprisonment extended to ten years if operating within a criminal organisation.
