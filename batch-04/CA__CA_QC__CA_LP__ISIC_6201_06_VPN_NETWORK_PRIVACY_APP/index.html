Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and federal income tax registration portal at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Revenu Québec — QST (Québec Sales Tax), payroll, and provincial corporate/personal income tax portal at www.revenuquebec.ca. A limited partnership receiving Canadian-source business income is generally subject to GST/HST and QST registration requirements as a separate 'person' for sales tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership (CA_LP) established in Quebec: (1) Apply for a federal Business Number (BN) electronically via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, selecting GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. (2) Register for QST with Revenu Québec via the 'Business Registration Online' joint service or directly through Revenu Québec's 'Mon dossier d'entreprise' portal, using the federal BN as the base identifier. (3) If the partnership has no permanent establishment in Canada but carries on business through a Canadian-based partner or agent, the partnership must still register for GST/HST and QST once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; if the supplies are 'specified digital services' or if the partnership is a non-resident that is required to collect Quebec consumption taxes on remote services, separate simplified registration may apply. (4) For income tax, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent; the partnership itself does not file a T1/T2 return unless an election is made, but each partner must report their allocable share. Non-resident partners may be required to file a T3 Partnership Information Return with CRA and Revenu Québec, and may be subject to Part XIII or Part I withholding tax on distributions or on allocated Canadian-source income under ITA 215 and Quebec Taxation Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A limited partnership is not a taxpayer for Canadian income tax purposes; income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners. For GST/HST and QST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' (registrant) when engaged in commercial activity in Canada, and must carry its own registration, returns, and remittances. The territorial scope is determined under the permanent establishment rules for federal income tax allocation and Quebec Corporations Tax rules; for sales tax, place of supply rules under the GST/HST and QST framework apply. The partnership may also qualify for 'territorial non-resident' treatment if it has no Canadian establishment; however, indirect tax registration may still be required on supplies of registered downloadable/virtual network services to Canadian or Quebec consumers under the 'specified digital services' / 'service supplied in Quebec' rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' entity classification election comparable to the US IRS regime; the tax classification of a limited partnership is statutory and mandatory. However, several statutory elections are relevant: (a) election under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act (ITA) for non-resident individuals/partners to file a return on Canadian rental or timber income instead of Part XIII withholding tax; (b) election under section 150(3) for a partnership to be deemed a separate corporation for certain withholding obligations? — in correct practice, a partnership may elect under ITA 34 to have a specified percentage of income taxed for certain professionals, but for LPs not relevant; (c) election for GST/HST under section 177 of the Excise Tax Act to use a 'substantial performance' point, or elective enhanced GST/HST rebate treatment for new housing not relevant; (d) an election under the Quebec Taxation Act to compute income in Canadian dollars for a partnership with foreign functional currency; (e) hybrid mismatch elections under section 18.2/18.3 ITA and related rules are not elective in the traditional sense but can be shaped through partnership agreements to avoid denied deductions. For indirect tax, parties may jointly elect to treat a joint venture as a separate GST/HST registrant under ETA 273, but a limited partnership itself cannot elect out of entity-level sales tax registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the small-supplier threshold for a person (including a limited partnership) is CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) unless the person is a non-resident supplying 'specified digital services' or is already a 'large business' with more than CAD 10 million. For QST, the small-supplier threshold is also CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Quebec, with separate threshold computation. A non-resident LP supplying VPN/network privacy SaaS services to Quebec consumers may be required to register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST and QST regimes for 'specified digital services' regardless of the CAD 30,000 threshold if the services are supplied from a location outside Canada in the ordinary course of business and the recipient is in Canada. Additionally, if the partnership has a permanent establishment in Quebec, all registrable supplies must meet the registration threshold; conversely, absent a Canadian establishment, the international services and intangible personal property zero-rating rules under ETA 25(c) will generally apply if the service is supplied to a non-resident individual, unless the supply is made to a resident of Canada or in connection with Canadian real property.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Quebec-registered limited partnership supplying VPN network privacy software as a digital service (other than advertising services or other excluded services) may zero-rate the supply under ETA 25(a) or 25(c) if all the following conditions are met: (i) the recipient is a non-resident individual who is outside Canada, or a non-resident corporation that is not registered for GST/HST and is not using the service in connection with a business carried on in Canada; (ii) the supply is not made to a resident of Canada, and no legal or factual linkage exists to Canadian real property or goods; (iii) the supply is 'made in Canada' under the place-of-supply rules, with the province of supply being Quebec for QST purposes if the address of the recipient is in Quebec; and (iv) the service is not considered a 'supply of a service that is rendered in Canada' to a Canadian recipient under the deemed supply rules for intangibles. If the recipient is a GST/HST registrant in Canada, or a Quebec resident individual, the supply is subject to GST/HST at 5% and QST at 9.975%. For exports of physical goods (if any), zero-rating under ETA 1 requires proof that the goods have been exported within 60 days of the invoice. The LP should also obtain documentary evidence of the non-resident status of each foreign counterparty, such as a signed certificate of residence, a foreign passport or a tax identifier for non-resident billing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Reporting System CERS / B13A Form)
  • Commercial Invoice and Customer- or Partner-Specific VPN Service Agreement with evidence of the foreign recipient's address and non-resident status
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Waybill for exported physical goods, or a written export certificate for intangibles
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer/wire confirmation evidencing the receipt of foreign currency consideration from outside Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance regime (no continuous transaction controls or digital tax stamping). Under the ETA and Quebec's Act respecting the legal framework of information technology, electronic invoices are legally valid if they are provided in a readable format that accurately records the prescribed information; the CRA and Revenu Québec require registrants to issue invoices containing the BN, GST/HST and QST registration numbers, date, supplier/receiver names, description, tax amounts, and rate. Revenu Québec operates a 'Relevés électroniques' program for certain financial data but no transactional clearance. For sales of VPN network privacy apps as digital supplies, there is no obligation to use API-based e-invoicing; however, if the LP uses Stripe or Paymentwall as payment services, those platforms are not tax-remittance intermediaries. The registrant must maintain full audit trails in an electronic format, and business records must be retained for six years per ETA 282 and Quebec Tax Administration Act. Digital invoicing without XML clearance is sufficient for compliance.

CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinize zero-rated export claims in the digital service context for artificial structures designed to avoid tax. If the LP cannot substantiate that the supply was made to a non-resident or that consideration was received from outside Canada within a reasonable period (administratively accepted as 180 days), the zero rating is denied and GST/HST plus QST become payable, with assessed interest. Under section 285 ETA, knowingly making a false statement or evasion is subject to penalties up to 25% of the assessed amount plus fines up to CAD 50,000, and upon conviction for tax evasion, a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Revenu Québec imposes equivalent penalties under sections 1160, 1161, and 1162 of the Taxation Act and section 40 of the Tax Administration Act. Retroactive clawback may extend four years for normal assessments and indefinitely where misrepresentation is involved. Additionally, if the LP fails to register for GST/HST and QST while making taxable digital supplies to Quebec consumers, it may be assessed for uncollected taxes under section 323 ETA, plus penalties for failure to register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. No foreign-exchange permits, nationality restrictions, or prior approvals apply to capital injections, dividend payments, partnership distributions, or profit remittances. For a Quebec Limited Partnership, a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP may be taxable on the Canadian-source partnership income, and treaty-based allocation rules under the Canada-US or other applicable tax treaties must be observed. Part XIII withholding may apply to remittances characterized as interest, royalties, or deemed periodic payments, but such fiscal frictions are tax obligations, not currency-control restrictions. The principal practical barriers are bank KYC/EDD, source-of-funds substantiation, and sanctions compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules, mandated conversion timelines, or minimum conversion ratios exist in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency and used freely for operational or treasury purposes. Reporting obligations apply only through anti-money-laundering channels: any incoming or outgoing wire transfer of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and all export receipts must be supported by records sufficient for Canadian income tax and GST/QST purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary authority with no statutory exchange-control mandate. Cross-border currency reporting and AML/ATF monitoring are administered by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), not by a separate foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium in Quebec. Foreign fintech EMIs are not recognized as domestic banking institutions under Canadian law; they cannot accept deposits or act as cash custodians without registration or bank sponsorship. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment service providers performing core retail payment functions in Canada are required to register with the Bank of Canada. Foreign EMIs must therefore either establish a Canadian registered entity and obtain the applicable regulatory authorization, or operate through a Tier 1 Canadian bank sponsor. For this LP, foreign digital EMIs should be used solely as SaaS subscription merchant gateways (e.g., Stripe, Paymentwall, or PayPal) and never as primary treasury collateral or liquidity providers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident investor's bank to the LP's Tier 1 Canadian bank account in Montreal or Toronto. The remittance advice must state 'LP capital contribution / partnership capital call' and reference the applicable capital call number. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC prior approval is required, but the receiving bank will require the executed capital call, a partnership agreement extract showing capital account mechanics, source-of-funds evidence, and FINTRAC EFT reporting for any transaction of C$10,000 or more. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium in Quebec. Foreign fintech EMIs are not recognized as domestic banking institutions under Canadian law; they cannot accept deposits or act as cash custodians without registration or bank sponsorship. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment service providers performing core retail payment functions in Canada are required to register with the Bank of Canada. Foreign EMIs must therefore either establish a Canadian registered entity and obtain the applicable regulatory authorization, or operate through a Tier 1 Canadian bank sponsor. For this LP, foreign digital EMIs should be used solely as SaaS subscription merchant gateways (e.g., Stripe, Paymentwall, or PayPal) and never as primary treasury collateral or liquidity providers.
