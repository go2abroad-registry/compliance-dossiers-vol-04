Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including CRA Business Registration Online and CRA My Business Account. Ontario Ministry of Finance and ServiceOntario administer the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registration, but GST/HST and federal income tax are administered federally by the CRA; Ontario's 8% provincial HST component is collected through the CRA as part of the 13% Harmonized Sales Tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Ontario Limited Partnership obtains its federal tax identifier by registering through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. A non-resident applicant may also use CRA Form RC459 for GST/HST registration. CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus a two-letter program account code; the GST/HST account code is RT. Ontario limited partnership registration is completed separately through ServiceOntario. GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold, though voluntary registration is allowed before threshold crossing to claim input tax credits. CRA may require an authorized Canadian representative or security from non-resident registrants where necessary to secure collection.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. An Ontario Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Canadian federal income tax law pursuant to section 96 of the Income Tax Act. Income is allocated to partners and taxed in their hands; the limited partnership itself does not pay entity-level Canadian income tax. For GST/HST purposes, however, the limited partnership is a separate legal person and may itself be a registrant, supplier, and recipient. Supplies made in Ontario are generally subject to HST at 13%, subject to zero-rating and exempt supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not have an elective check-the-box entity classification regime. Tax classification is determined by the legal form of the entity under Canadian law; an Ontario LP is always a flow-through partnership. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 12.7 and 18.4 to 18.8 of the Income Tax Act apply mandatorily to cross-border arrangements and offer no general election to disapply them. Available GST/HST elections include the closely related person section 156 election, selected financial institution elections for financial services, and the quick method of accounting for GST/HST remittance; none of these converts a limited partnership into a corporation for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters, including the current quarter; this is the small-supplier threshold. Once taxable Canadian supplies exceed C$30,000, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Registration may be voluntary below the threshold, and lower or special thresholds apply to certain listed financial institutions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported as part of a sale are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are physically delivered outside Canada and the supplier retains prescribed export evidence. Outsourced virtual CFO, bookkeeping, advisory, and management accounting services supplied to a non-resident recipient are generally zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI unless an exclusion applies, such as services relating to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services rendered to an individual present in Canada. The recipient should be a non-resident, should not be acquiring the service for consumption in Canada, and the agreement must clearly evidence an offshore service supplied from Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export report or Form B13A where required, including Canadian Export Reporting System records)
  • Commercial invoice containing full supply and consideration details
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or cargo control document showing destination outside Canada
  • Packing list and transportation records
  • Proof of payment and foreign-currency remittance, including SWIFT or bank transfer evidence; Inward Remittance Certificate where obtained
  • Non-resident status evidence and offshore recipient contract for service export substantiation

Canada does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, digital tax stamping, or API-based fiscal clearance for GST/HST. Registrants may issue paper or electronic invoices, provided the invoice contains the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and regulations, including the registrant's Business Number, rate and tax charged, and description of the supply. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1,000,000, and CRA may require electronic records and audit access. Ontario does not operate a separate digital clearance regime because HST is administered by the CRA.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated export refunds where export evidence is inadequate, false, or fraudulent. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, but may be extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Penalties include late-filing penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, failure-to-remit penalties, gross negligence penalties, interest accrual from the original due date, and criminal prosecution in serious cases. CRA expects actual receipt and documented repatriation of consideration within a commercially reasonable period; 180 days is the compliance benchmark used for foreign-currency export payments. Failure to substantiate payment will generally result in denial of zero-rating, reassessment of HST, interest, penalties, and in egregious cases prosecution for fraudulent export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The Exchange Fund Account held by the federal Minister of Finance is reserved for discretionary exchange-market intervention and does not serve an approval or rationing function. Capital account openness is absolute: inbound capital contributions to the Ontario Limited Partnership and outbound profit remittances require no approval from the Bank of Canada or the Department of Finance. The only frictions are bank-level AML/KYC due diligence and, for non-resident limited partners, Part XIII withholding tax on allocated Canadian-source income (default 25%, potentially reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty where the non-resident partner does not carry on business through a permanent establishment).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or any Ontario statute. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or foreign-source receipts. The LP may hold, convert, or remit foreign currency freely at market rates, and may establish multi-currency operating accounts with any Schedule I bank. For Canadian income tax filing, all foreign-currency revenue and capital contributions must be translated to CAD using the Bank of Canada daily/annual exchange rates; no maximum foreign-currency holding period applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa. The Bank of Canada is the sole monetary authority issuing CAD and operating the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for commercial clearing. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; the regulatory perimeter comprises: (i) OSFI (prudential supervision of banks under the federal Bank Act); (ii) FINTRAC (AML/CFT supervision and mandatory registration of Money Services Businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, PCMLTFA); and (iii) the Ontario Securities Commission for any securities-linked treasury activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for payment disbursement efficiency, subject to material legal constraints. (i) Wise (the target rail) operates lawfully in Ontario as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business through Wise Payments Canada Inc., with settlement infrastructure provided by Community Trust Company, a CDIC-member Schedule I bank. A Wise Business multi-currency account is acceptable for receiving client fees and executing FX-converted outbound payments; however, it is not a chartered bank account under the Bank Act. Funds are pooled, comprehensive CDIC deposit insurance applies only during the pass-through at Community Trust, and holding idle balances above CAD 100,000 is commercially imprudent and outside the expected deposit-taking framework. (ii) Stripe Billing functions as a payment aggregation platform remitting settlement to a domestic Canadian bank account in CAD; Stripe is registered as an MSB in Canada but does not provide treasury or credit-balance services. (iii) Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Relay, Intergiro) cannot serve as the primary operating account for the Ontario LP: they are not regulated by OSFI, will fail the counterparty due-diligence test of regulated Schedule I banks under PCMLTFA, and create unremediable audit and CRA inter-period reporting risk.

(ii) classify the transaction for the receiving bank and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting as a direct investment capital contribution to a Canadian partnership; (iii) for any wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank must file a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report, and the LP must supply its CRA Business Number and the partner's date of birth and address if not previously on file; (iv) convert the foreign currency at a same-day spot rate under a negotiated FX threshold (e.g., TD FX365 or BMO FX Advanced), or direct the inflow to a USD sweep sub-account to enable discretionary timing of conversion; (v) credit the contribution to a dedicated partner capital account in the LP's books, segregated from operating revenue, to prevent any CRA recharacterization of capital as service income; and (vi) record the transaction in the partnership's capital reconciliation schedule with supporting FX translation notes for T5013 filing.For trade payments (software subscriptions, contractor invoices, international marketing spend): (a) SWIFT MT103 is the default cross-border rail for institutional transfers exceeding CAD 2,500, clearing through LVTS in CAD, and is recommended for vendor payments above CAD 10,000 where a full audit trail to the CRA fiscal-year-end is required; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit are not available and are not appropriate for pure services transactions under Canadian banking practice — UCP600 LCs are restricted to documentary sale-of-goods contracts; for service contracts requiring payment assurance, use an irrevocable payment undertaking or bank payment undertaking instead; (c) for cost-efficient disbursements below CAD 50,000 per transaction, Wise Business multi-currency local rails (ACH Same-Day in USD, SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR, Faster Payments in GBP) offer superior execution rates relative to bank wire desks; and (d) the LP must implement a dual-approval treasury control for all outbound transfers exceeding CAD 15,000, with a daily reconciliation matching Stripe and Wise payouts to the sub-ledger using automated bank-feed imports.Deliverable Foreign Exchange Forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD) with tenors up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with the dealing bank; permitted only where the LP can evidence firm contracted USD or EUR revenue commitments to satisfy the commercial-loss test and achieve income-account hedge treatment rather than speculative mark-to-market trading.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for receivables or payables denominated in restricted or illiquid currencies (e.g., CNY, BRL, INR) where a deliverable forward is unavailable due to local exchange-control infrastructure in the counterparty jurisdiction; net-settled in CAD and confined to underlying contracted cash flows.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for matched funding where the LP carries foreign-currency denominated financing or tax-basis liabilities; constrained to interest-rate-neutral structures to preserve hedge accounting designation under IFRS 9 and to avoid unintended Part XIII withholding exposure on any taxable swap payments.Vanilla Foreign Exchange Options (call or put on USD/CAD) written by the LP's dealing bank, precisely matched to committed conversion cycles in the virtual CFO subscription model; option premiums must be capitalized as hedging costs and fully documented in the IFRS 9 cash-flow hedge designation file.CORRA-linked Cross-Currency Basis Swaps, permitted only where the LP operates a multi-currency treasury pool with an average monthly balance exceeding CAD 500,000; otherwise economically disproportionate and disallowed where the notional amount exceeds audited contracted annual turnover.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for payment disbursement efficiency, subject to material legal constraints. (i) Wise (the target rail) operates lawfully in Ontario as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business through Wise Payments Canada Inc., with settlement infrastructure provided by Community Trust Company, a CDIC-member Schedule I bank. A Wise Business multi-currency account is acceptable for receiving client fees and executing FX-converted outbound payments; however, it is not a chartered bank account under the Bank Act. Funds are pooled, comprehensive CDIC deposit insurance applies only during the pass-through at Community Trust, and holding idle balances above CAD 100,000 is commercially imprudent and outside the expected deposit-taking framework. (ii) Stripe Billing functions as a payment aggregation platform remitting settlement to a domestic Canadian bank account in CAD; Stripe is registered as an MSB in Canada but does not provide treasury or credit-balance services. (iii) Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Relay, Intergiro) cannot serve as the primary operating account for the Ontario LP: they are not regulated by OSFI, will fail the counterparty due-diligence test of regulated Schedule I banks under PCMLTFA, and create unremediable audit and CRA inter-period reporting risk.
