Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) and Revenu Québec. Corporate registry: Corporations Canada (federal) or Registraire des entreprises du Québec (Quebec enterprise number - NEQ). Digital portals: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online and Revenu Québec Cliquez Affaires.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Quebec Business Corporations Act; deliver articles of incorporation; obtain the incorporation number and, for Quebec, the NEQ (Numero d'entreprise du Quebec). Step 2 - Apply for the federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; a federally incorporated company is issued a BN automatically. Step 3 - Add CRA program accounts: RC for T2 corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, and RP/RD accounts if payroll or installments are required. Step 4 - Register with Revenu Quebec for QST and provincial payroll accounts; QST registration is aligned with the CRA BN and results in a separate QST number (format 1000-0000-00). Step 5 - For a foreign-owned SPV, provide evidence of foreign incorporation, head office address, and a Quebec agent for service; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST and QST if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or where a mandatory registration obligation applies. Step 6 - Maintain the registers and file the annual declaration with the Registraire des entreprises du Quebec within the prescribed statutory period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no statutory check-the-box regime analogous to U.S. Form 8832; entity classification follows the legal form under its jurisdiction of creation, and a corporation is always an entity-level taxpayer in Canada. Relevant elections available to the PE buyout SPV: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act section 273 and the parallel QST election under An Act respecting the Quebec sales tax, permitting taxable supplies between a parent and a 90%-or-more owned subsidiary to be made for nil consideration; (ii) associated corporations joint election under Income Tax Act section 256 to allocate the small business limit, generally unavailable to a non-CCPC SPV; (iii) section 216(4) election for a non-resident receiving Canadian rental income; (iv) foreign affiliate and controlled foreign affiliate elections relevant where the Canadian SPV holds non-resident loan or equity investments; and (v) transitional election for grandfathered hybrid mismatch instruments under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.21 (post-30 June 2022 OECD hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory for new instruments).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. Both current-account and capital-account operations—equity injection, loan drawdown, LBO disbursement, dividend and redemption repatriation—are unrestricted by exchange control. Profit remittance is subject only to a 25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends/interest paid to non-residents (treaty-reducible), FINTRAC EFT reporting above CAD 10,000, the Income Tax Act EIFEL limitation capping net interest deductibility at 30% of EBITDA post-2023, and, where the ultimate acquirer is foreign-controlled, Investment Canada Act notification/net-benefit review. No currency rationing, central-bank prior approval, or convertibility certificate exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil—no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation applies to export, service, or investment receipts under Canadian law. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency and in any jurisdiction. The only applicable controls are FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and, for controlled goods, Export and Import Permits Act licensing administered by Global Affairs Canada; neither imposes any conversion or surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and payments authority under the Bank of Canada Act; the foreign-exchange administration function is decentralized. Department of Finance Canada sets exchange-control policy, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises authorized banks, and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers tax and reporting duties. There is no dedicated FX-control agency or exchange-licensing regime under Canadian law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury use. Canada has no e-money institution licence comparable to the UK/EEA EMI regime; offshore digital EMIs cannot take deposits or access the Lynx high-value clearing system unless registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force 30 September 2024, with registration and compliance obligations phased through 2026) and holding a sponsor-bank settlement account at a Schedule I bank. An offshore EMI may serve as a supplemental FX quote or expense-payments node, but Bank Act s. 54 deposit-taking prohibitions, FINTRAC obligations, and OSFI sponsor oversight render it unviable as the SPV's principal clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

CAD-denominated equity commitments settle through Lynx—Payments Canada's ISO 20022 high-value SWIFT-compatible system—with immediate irrevocable finality at the clearing member. USD commitments route via Fedwire or CHIPS to the bank's US correspondent account and are either held in a USD sub-account or spot-converted to CAD. Verification stack comprises: (i) pre-wire sanctions screening of sender and beneficiary against Global Affairs Canada, OSFI, OFAC (for USD) and UNSC lists; (ii) FINTRAC EFT filing, automatically effected by the bank for every cross-border transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) SWIFT field 50K/59A matching against the pre-verified UBO and LP register; (iv) ISO purpose code 'CAPD' (capital payment) with narrative referencing 'equity contribution per subscription agreement dated [date]'; (v) for commitments above CAD 100M, head-office treasury pre-funding or a segregated CAD liquidity facility agreed in the account mandate, with same-day Lynx settlement after internal compliance release.Primary clearing rails: (1) CAD payments to Quebec and Canadian sellers and lenders via Lynx, achieving same-day real-time settlement; (2) cross-border purchase-price wires via SWIFT MT103 with an MT202/COV cover leg, executed through the bank's US/EU correspondents under SWIFT GPI tracking, with a 12:00 ET cut-off for same-day value and T+0 settlement in the US afternoon session; (3) syndicated loan drawdowns under the committed term facility via ISO 20022 pacs.008 loan-funding instructions reconciled to the facility agent; (4) residual target trade obligations paid by documentary commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) issued by the Canadian clearing bank, confined to post-closing working-capital flows.
