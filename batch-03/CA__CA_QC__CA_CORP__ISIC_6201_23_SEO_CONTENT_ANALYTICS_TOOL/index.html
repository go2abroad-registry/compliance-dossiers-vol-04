Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office is mandatory. A commercial virtual-office address may be acceptable if it is a real address in the required jurisdiction, capable of receiving official documents, and supported by appropriate corporate records. A mailbox-only, forwarding-only, or accommodation address is insufficient. For a Quebec enterprise, the declared establishment and correspondence information must be accurate and current. For a federal corporation, the registered office must comply with the Canada Business Corporations Act and applicable provincial address requirements. A virtual office is generally unsuitable as the sole operational location for banking, payment processing, employment, tax-residency substantiation, controlled technology, regulated activities, or businesses requiring customer-support and data-processing substance. For the proposed non-regulated SEO, content analytics, and MarTech SaaS business, a virtual registered office is generally legally permissible, subject to provider, bank, tax, privacy, and payment-processor requirements.

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued for any lawful consideration approved by the board. Quebec does not generally require a blocked escrow account for incorporation or ordinary share subscriptions; subscription funds should be paid to the corporation and recorded in its accounting and securities registers.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal occupancy, fire-prevention, and building-code approvals for leased or owned premises where required. Register with CNESST when applicable, implement workplace health and safety procedures, and complete ergonomic and electrical risk controls. No special industrial clearance is expected for ordinary office and cloud-software operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Revenu Québec

E-government filing portal

Canadian corporations obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA through the My Business Account online portal by submitting articles of incorporation and related corporate documents. Quebec entities must additionally register with Revenu Québec to obtain a Quebec enterprise number (NEQ) and register for QST if applicable. The SOP is fully online and requires proof of address and incorporation.

The SOP is fully online and requires proof of address and incorporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require approval for foreign investment, dividend remittances, intercompany loans, royalty payments, or service-fee payments. Banks may require commercial rationale, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, sanctions screening, and tax documentation. Outbound payments may be affected by Canadian withholding tax, treaty eligibility, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, shareholder-benefit rules, sanctions, court orders, insolvency restrictions, and lender covenants. Quebec corporations must also maintain accurate corporate, tax, and accounting records and comply with applicable Quebec language, privacy, and consumer rules where relevant to their operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline applies to Canadian exporters. Export receipts may generally remain in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts and may be converted at the holder's discretion. The corporation should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, payment records, and tax records supporting the commercial purpose and accounting treatment of receipts. Financial institutions may impose internal settlement, documentation, sanctions, or transaction-monitoring requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine capital controls. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada administers federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing compliance for reporting entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and disbursement, subject to product, residence, currency, and underwriting eligibility. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide services only within the scope permitted by its home-country authorization and Canadian law. A foreign payment service provider carrying on in-scope payment functions for end users in Canada may require registration with FINTRAC as a money services business or foreign money services business and must satisfy Canadian AML, sanctions, recordkeeping, reporting, and travel-rule obligations. It may not represent itself as a Canadian deposit-taking bank or accept deposits as a bank without the required Canadian authorization. Stripe and Paddle may be used where the Canadian entity passes merchant, beneficial-owner, product, chargeback, tax, and restricted-business underwriting; their services are not substitutes for a Canadian operating bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route funding from the verified shareholder, parent, or lender account directly to the Canadian corporation's account through a regulated bank or licensed payment institution. Use a wire transfer with complete originator, beneficiary, account, and intermediary information. Canada generally does not use mandatory universal FX purpose codes for ordinary corporate wires; the payment narrative should state the legally accurate purpose, such as equity subscription, shareholder loan, intercompany loan, or working-capital funding. Before receipt, provide the bank with the capitalization or loan agreement, board approval, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and frequency, originating-bank details, and tax treatment. Reconcile the bank receipt to the general ledger, issue any required share documentation, record related-party balances, and retain records for statutory and AML purposes. Do not route capital through unrelated third-party accounts or split transfers to avoid monitoring thresholds.Use Canadian bank wires through SWIFT for foreign suppliers, contractors, cloud providers, and other commercial counterparties. An MT103 or equivalent wire should contain accurate ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and payment-purpose information. Use commercial letters of credit where supplier risk, documentary control, or shipment conditions justify them; the issuing bank should apply the applicable documentary-credit rules, commonly UCP 600, and documentary collections may use URC 522 where appropriate. For SaaS and digital services, establish recurring supplier payments, invoice approval controls, dual authorization, beneficiary-change verification, and monthly bank-to-ledger reconciliation. Payments must be screened for sanctions, fraud, tax withholding, transfer-pricing, and anti-bribery issues.
