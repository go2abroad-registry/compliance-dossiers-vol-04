Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated enterprise carrying on business in Canada obtains a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) from CRA as the statutory tax identifier for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs. Registration is initiated online through CRA My Business Account or by submitting Form RC1, Request for a Business Number. The Ontario provincial corporate income tax account is created under the same BN. A separate GST/HST program account (RT) is opened at registration. A non-resident corporation must provide certified charter documents, proof of registration/extract, the address of the registered agent, and may appoint a Canadian representative for CRA correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election; a federal/provincial business corporation is mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Available elections include the section 219(1) branch tax election available to non-resident corporations, the section 216(1) election for non-resident rental and disposition of Canadian real property income, and associated-company loss/allocation elections for the small business deduction. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are generally restricted under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the final hybrid mismatch rules proposed under the Income Tax Act (see 2023 legislative proposals).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (worldwide) per calendar quarter and over the preceding four calendar quarters; mandatory GST/HST registration applies from the transaction quota being exceeded unless the non-resident supplier's taxable supplies in Canada are below the threshold and no registrant status is otherwise required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to suppliers of goods that are actually exported and delivered outside Canada pursuant to Excise Tax Act sections 21-23, and to certain services supplied to non-residents under section 25 where the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. A cross-dock/3PL reconciliation warehouse performing domestic fulfillment services in Ontario on goods located in Canada is supplying a taxable service in Canada and generally cannot claim zero-rating for those services; zero-rating may apply to export transportation or services relating to goods in transit out of Canada, subject to prescribed documentary proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canada Customs Coding Form)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document evidencing departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/credit confirmation) proving receipt of export proceeds

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime is currently enforced by CRA. Electronic invoices are acceptable if preserved in legible form and available for audit. CBSA customs procedures require electronic export/import declarations via the Canada Single Window / Commercial System, but this is not a VAT clearance mechanism.

CRA can revisit zero-rating claims within a four-year normal reassessment period (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud exists), reverse the zero-rating, and assess uncollected HST plus compound arrears interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act for false statements or evasion include a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and up to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided, failure-to-register penalties, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act/Excise Tax Act with fines and imprisonment. The exporter must retain all export substantiation records for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully floating exchange-rate regime and full current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No exchange-control approvals, licences, or repatriation permits are required for dividend distributions, branch-profit remittances, or capital withdrawals. The only compliance encumbrances are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory ceiling 25%, treaty-reducible, e.g., 5-15 percent under the Canada-United States Tax Convention) and FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers at or above CAD 10,000. Capital-account transactions are likewise unrestricted as to convertibility, although all cross-border flows remain subject to ordinary bank sanctions screening, FINTRAC EFT reporting, and OSFI prudential oversight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender obligation exists under Canadian law. There is no statutory deadline for converting export receipts into CAD and no mandated conversion ratio. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts with Canadian Schedule I banks, retain proceeds offshore, and freely net settlement with foreign suppliers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Bank of Canada Act and coordinating with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Payments Canada as the statutory clearing-rail operator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for auxiliary liquidity-and-FX rails; Low as a sole primary banking relationship. Canada does not licence EU-style electronic money institutions (EMIs). Cross-border fintech providers available to Ontario corporations (Airwallex, Wise Platform, Corpay, and niche Banking-as-a-Service issuers) operate under FINTRAC money-service-business registration and rely on sponsor banks for underlying clearing. They afford multi-currency collections, virtual IBAN-equivalencies, forward FX, and marketplace settlements, but do not provide CDIC deposit insurance, indigenous UCP 600 commercial-credit infrastructure, or Payments Canada Lynx membership; therefore a Canadian Schedule I deposit account remains the treasury anchor.

Statutory deliverableDeadline window Government feeDelinquency sanctions
Ontario Annual Return (Ontario Business Corporations Act and Corporations Information Act) Within 2 months after the anniversary of incorporation each year $60 USD Late filing may attract additional administrative fees; failure to file for two consecutive years permits the Director to administratively dissolve the corporation under OBCA s. 241 after statutory notice.
Federal Annual Return (Canada Business Corporations Act - applicable if the entity is a federal CBCA corporation registered in Ontario) Within 60 days after the anniversary of incorporation each year $0 USD Failure to file for two consecutive years permits administrative dissolution under CBCA s. 212 after statutory notice; no separate government late fee applies to online filings.
T2 Corporation Income Tax Return (federal and Ontario corporate income tax administered jointly by the CRA) Within 6 months after the end of the corporation's fiscal year $0 USD Failure-to-file penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months (10% plus 2% per month for repeat failures); interest accrues on unpaid balances; no direct registry strike-off but CRA enforcement and director liability may follow.
