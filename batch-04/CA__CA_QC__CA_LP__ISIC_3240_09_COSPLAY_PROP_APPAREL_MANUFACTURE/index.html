Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal competent authority for the Business Number (BN) and GST/HST — accessed via the CRA My Business Account portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html). Because the LP has a Quebec establishment, the federal-provincial Single Tax Administration Agreement designates Revenu Québec as the sole administrator of both GST/HST and the provincial Quebec Sales Tax (QST); Revenu Québec operates the Clic Revenu / ARGENT digital portal (revenuquebec.ca). The Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) remains the upstream mandatory business registry for the juridical existence of the limited partnership.

E-government filing portal

1) Complete REQ registration of the LP to obtain the Quebec establishment number (NEQ). 2) Apply for tax accounts through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or the Revenu Québec entrepreneur platform; authorize a representative with a valid mandate. 3) CRA issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN); the GST/HST program account (RT prefix) opens concurrently and Revenu Québec assigns the QST registration number (RQ file number) in a single data exchange. 4) The LP registers in its own name as a 'person' for GST/QST purposes even though it is fiscally transparent for income tax. 5) Registration is mandatory on the day total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively in the four preceding quarters; voluntary registration may be requested earlier. 6) On failure to register, CRA may register the LP retroactively under ETA s. 240(2) and assess penalties from the date registration was required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Quebec limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: income, losses, capital gains, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners per the partnership agreement (ITA s. 96; Quebec Taxation Act). The LP files only an information return (T5013 federal and the Quebec partnership information return) and pays no entity-level income tax. By contrast, for GST/HST and QST the LP is a separate registrant and is itself the taxable supplier; partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax obligations. Territorial scope: resident partners are worldwide-taxed; non-resident partners are subject to Canadian and Quebec tax on their allocable share of the LP's Canadian-source business income and are deemed to have a permanent establishment in Canada via the partnership (ITA s. 253.1).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for a Canadian LP; the entity cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 (Form T1139) permitting a non-calendar partnership year-end when all partners' taxation years coincide; (ii) closely related election under ETA s. 156(2) to alter the deemed-nil-consideration rule and make taxable supplies between the partnership and related entities; (iii) no prophylactic hybrid mismatch election is granted — Canada applies BEPS Action 2 rules that re-characterize certain foreign check-the-box entities and hybrid financial instrument deductions, and any structuring involving non-resident limited partners must be validated against those anti-avoidance provisions before reliance on pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable worldwide supplies made in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the four preceding calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold; ETA s. 148; the QST threshold is identical by administrative agreement). Upon crossing the single-quarter threshold, registration is compulsory effective that same day; upon crossing the four-quarter test, registration is effective at the beginning of the first month after the threshold is exceeded. The LP may also register voluntarily as soon as it makes any taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods — cosplay costumes, licensed theatrical prop apparel, and synthetic wigs — may be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, para. 1 when: the supplier and the non-resident recipient deal at arm's length; the goods are delivered in Canada to the non-resident recipient; the goods will not be further processed or incorporated into goods supplied in Canada; and the goods leave Canada as soon as is reasonably possible after delivery (CRA administrative benchmark: generally within 60 days). Direct-to-consumer international shipments by mail or courier to a foreign address likewise qualify. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., design or web-store services) qualify under Part V, para. 7 only where they do not relate to real property or tangible property situated in Canada. The QST zero-rating rules for Quebec mirror the GST rules; Canada imposes no exchange-control or FX-repatriation requirement, so the operative deadline is the 60-day export window.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA CERS electronic export-reporting confirmation with export reference number
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Ocean Waybill evidencing shipment of the costumes or wigs to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order naming the non-resident buyer and destination country
  • Inward remittance or bank settlement certificate evidencing payment in convertible currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Freight-forwarder certificate, insurance policy, or courier tracking report evidencing exit from Canada, together with the destination customs entry if available

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government API-based invoice stamping, no digital seal, and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing platform as of 2025; Quebec's planned mandatory electronic invoicing reform remains indefinitely suspended. CRA and Revenu Québec accept electronic invoices that contain all prescribed invoice content. However, for exported goods, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires electronic filing of the export declaration (B13A) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000 or subject to permits/controls, generating an export confirmation reference that must be preserved for tax audit purposes.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess GST/QST within four years, extended to ten years where a return was filed with misrepresentation, carelessness, or fraud (ETA s. 298 and Quebec parallel rules). Zero-rating claimed without substantiation is reassessed at the full 14.975% effective combined rate, plus daily-compounded prescribed interest from the original filing date. Civil penalties include 5%-10% of unremitted tax plus fixed penalties for failure to register, file, or remit (ETA s. 280), and a substantial false-statement penalty of up to 25% of the amount at issue for negligent claims (ETA s. 285). Fraudulent export zero-rating or forgery of export documents is a criminal offence under ETA s. 327, punishable by fines of up to 100% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. CRA operates an Export Verification Program that targets high-risk exporters; records supporting zero-rating must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility for the Canadian dollar (CAD). The currency is freely floating with no exchange controls or surrender requirements. Capital contributions and profit remittances are not subject to central bank approval; however, payments of certain income streams to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (base statutory 25%, generally reduced by an applicable tax treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in a foreign currency, converted at market rates, or credited to a Canadian-dollar account. All international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial intermediary under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator for federally licensed banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF financial intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail, not a substitute for a Schedule I bank relationship. Legal constraints: foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Payoneer, Wise Business) are not Canadian banks, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot clear CAD directly through Payments Canada. They may be used for e-commerce payout aggregation and cross-border supplier payments, but the core capital escrow, FX, and LC infrastructure must sit with an OSFI-regulated or provincially regulated (Quebec) deposit-taking institution.
