Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni AFC), Eigerstrasse 65, CH-3003 Bern; VAT e-Services portal accessible via 'Mein Konto / Mon compte / Il mio conto' at www.estv.admin.ch; Swiss central business identifier issued through the Swiss central UID register at www.uid.admin.ch (Federal Statistical Office, BFS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (i) obtain a Swiss Business Identifier (UID/IDE/IDI) via the central UID register portal for use in all federal and cantonal tax filings; (ii) file a Swiss VAT registration application with the ESTV VAT Division (Form 1015; 'Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuer-Registrierung') — a non-established foreign supplier is registrable once annual Swiss-source taxable turnover exceeds CHF 100,000, while below the threshold it may remain unregistered and the Swiss customer is instead liable for reverse-charge 'acquiring tax' (Bezugsteuer) on services; (iii) apply for the simplified VAT procedure (SST) if the Swiss turnover is limited and no Swiss input VAT deduction is required; (iv) where the AG maintains a permanent establishment, branch or warehouse in Switzerland, must also register the establishment in the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and obtain the cantonal direct-tax number from the competent cantonal tax administration for corporate income tax, capital tax and municipal tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime; the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) is per se a separate taxable legal person for Swiss federal, cantonal and communal income taxes, as well as a taxable person for Swiss VAT. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT group election under Art. 21 VAT Act allowing a Swiss controlling parent and its Swiss subsidiaries to be treated as one taxable person; (ii) Swiss domestic group taxation for corporate income tax under Art. 9(3) DBG (in force since 1 January 2024), allowing an 80%-or-more Swiss parent to consolidate the taxable results of its Swiss group companies with prior cantonal tax authority approval; (iii) hybrid-mismatch rule elections under the DBG and SHIG, which deny deduction for hybrid financial instruments or entities unless the corresponding income is subject to tax in the counterparty jurisdiction; (iv) simplified VAT procedure (SST) for foreign suppliers; (v) option to waive zero-rating on exports in exceptional invoicing cases is not permitted, but the taxpayer may elect ordinary vs. net-tax/input-VAT deduction methods in VAT returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover for Swiss-established businesses (including worldwide export turnover); CHF 100,000 Swiss-source taxable turnover for non-established foreign businesses (Art. 10 VAT Act). Reverse-charge 'acquiring tax' applies to services supplied by a non-registered foreign supplier to a Swiss VAT-registered business; mandatory registration is required upon threshold exceedance from the date the threshold is objectively foreseeable to be met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Art. 23 VAT Act for (i) goods physically exported by the supplier or a third party from the Swiss customs territory to a foreign destination; (ii) services ordered and used in the territory of the customer abroad, subject to the place-of-supply rules; and (iii) transport services in international traffic. For the silicone fidget toys AG, the sale is zero-rated if goods leave Switzerland under a valid Swiss customs export declaration, the buyer/consignee is located outside Switzerland, title and risk pass pursuant to Incoterms 2020, full consideration is received and demonstrably repatriated, and the exporter retains complete shipment documentation. Shipment to a Swiss free port, bonded warehouse or to a Swiss dealer does not qualify; the zero-rating is subordinate to the genuineness of the foreign delivery.

  • Swiss Electronic Customs Export Declaration (e-dec / e-dec web or NCTS transit declaration) with customs clearance confirmation (Ausfuhrschein / document de sortie)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical dispatch from Switzerland to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with full Incoterms 2020 terms and reference to the export declaration number
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign buyer
  • Supply contract or binding purchase order identifying the foreign buyer, delivery point and payment terms

Switzerland does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, API-based stamping or continuous transaction control regime. Since 1 January 2020, suppliers invoicing the Swiss federal administration must transmit structured electronic invoices (XML/eBill/EBICS-compatible) for B2G settlement; the Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) format has been mandatory for payment slips issued by domestic businesses since 30 June 2020 to enable digital payment processing. E-invoices are VAT-deductible when they satisfy the content and authenticity requirements of Art. 26 VAT Act, e.g., via qualified electronic signature or EDI/EDIFACT secure exchange (network operated by SIX B2B and PEPPOL-compatible platforms). No legal obligation exists for a foreign exporter to use a specific local e-invoicing platform, provided the documentary proof of export is preserved in auditable form.

The ESTV conducts risk-based post-clearance audits of export zero-rating claims. Missing, incomplete, contradictory or fraudulent export evidence triggers retrospective annulment of the 0% rate, re-assessment at the standard 8.1% Swiss VAT, late-payment interest (default administrative interest currently 4.0% per annum), and, in cases of intentional tax evasion, criminal fines under Arts. 69–71 VAT Act of up to three times the tax evaded; negligent violations attract fines of up to the full amount evaded. Customs export irregularities additionally engage the Swiss Customs Act (ZG) penalties, including fines up to three times the duties evaded, confiscation of goods and potential prosecutorial referral; individuals may be disqualified from board membership and the entity's beneficial-owner reputational standing is impaired in Swiss commercial registries.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland operates a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime. Neither the SNB nor the Federal Department of Finance imposes exchange controls, quantitative restrictions, surrender obligations, or prior authorisation requirements on capital-account or current-account transactions. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (CH_AG) may freely receive shareholder equity injections, service trade debts, and remit dividends abroad without FX approval. The only fiscal friction on outbound profit flows is the statutory 35% Swiss anticipatory withholding tax on dividends, which is fully refundable or reducible under double-taxation treaties and regularly neutralised at the shareholder level; it is a tax collection mechanism, not a convertibility barrier. Practical hurdles are confined to bank-internal compliance screening and SNB balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds, not regulatory currency permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rule applies in Switzerland. A Swiss exporter of food-grade silicone tactile fidgets may retain 100% of export receivables in foreign currency (EUR, USD, CNY) in any onshore or offshore account without conversion to CHF. The sole binding obligation is statistical: under the SNB Ordinance on the Statistical Recording of International Transactions, a Swiss resident enterprise must report monthly cross-border assets and liabilities to the SNB once its cumulated foreign receivables/payables or international trade volume exceeds the statutory threshold (currently CHF 10 million per reporting month); this is a passive monitoring duty with no conversion or repatriation mandate.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque Nationale Suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera, acting in conjunction with the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) for banking and exchange-control counterparty supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium when channelled through a Swiss-licensed sponsor, otherwise Low. Because Switzerland is not an EEA/EU member, the EU EMI passporting regime does not extend into the Swiss market. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., an authorised UK or Maltese e-money institution) cannot offer direct domestic CHF IBAN accounts or SIC clearing access without holding, or contracting under, a Swiss banking licence. Practical structures are: (i) a FINMA fintech banking licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act for public-deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million, bricked with a full onboarding and AML operation; or (ii) a white-label sponsorship by a FINMA-regulated bank under which the EMI provides the global interface while clearing settles through the sponsor's SIC/SWIFT membership. For platform-pay concentration and small-ticket marketplace flows, a Swiss fintech such as Yapeal, Zak, or Neon via a ZKB or UBS sponsorship is viable, but custody of the CH_AG's corporate funds should remain in a tier-1 licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections are wired via SWIFT MT103 Priority Transfer over the SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) rail into the designated CHF capital escrow account at ZKB or UBS. Remittance must carry a clear BOP statistical category — direct investment equity (category 5013), or intra-group financing loan (category 5014) — in the transaction reference, with the beneficiary name matching the commercial register entry exactly. No SNB prior approval is required; the bank performs an FX-transaction purpose attestation under the Geldwäschereiverordnung. EU customer collections are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer/Instant on TARGET2; US and Asian disbursements flow via correspondent banks (typically JPMorgan, HSBC, or Citi). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% federal corporate income tax on after-tax profit (effective 7.83% pre-tax); plus cantonal/communal profit taxes yielding typical combined rates of 14.9% (Zug) to 19.7% (Geneva)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on gross dividends from a Swiss AG

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit statutory threshold for Local File/Master File; administrative guidelines consider contemporaneous TP documentation material for intercompany transactions above CHF 500,000 per transaction category (goods/services/intangibles/financial transactions); for Country-by-Country reporting, consolidated group revenue threshold is CHF 900 million

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no switchboard-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as seen in the UAE, but the 'Swiss substance' doctrine applies: a CH-AG must have actual physical presence, qualified decision-making, and operational substance to enjoy treaty benefits, patent box relief, and defend transfer pricing positions. The Federal Tax Act provides no formal substance test but tax rulings and exchange of information require real commercial activity commensurate with revenue

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CHF 100,000 per act (federal tax law), plus full tax arrears with default interest (4.5%) and a penalty surcharge of 1–3 times the evaded tax; per cantonal law additional fines up to CHF 10,000

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal liability for the corporate tax claim if non-declaration was deliberate; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 3 years (Art. 175–177 DBG); travel bans and asset seizures may be imposed during investigation
Unpaid Withholding Tax on Distributions

Statutory sanction: The company becomes debtor of the full 35% WHT (takeover liability), plus 5% default interest per annum and possible fine up to 10% of unpaid amount (Art. 174 DBG); the distribution remains taxable for the recipient

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable for the withheld amount if they knowingly participated in non-declaration; criminal liability under substitution law can lead to imprisonment up to 1 year and a directive to travel restricted pending settlement
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting Without Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax re-assessment with upward adjustment of Swiss profit, application of hidden profit distribution classification (27.4% effective corporate income tax increase in downside), late payment interest of 4.5%, and a civil penalty up to 100% of extra tax; TP adjustments are subject to cantonal capital tax and are not deductible

Director personal liability: Directors can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty; if they approved ransfer pricing below arm's length causing tax loss to the company, he/she is personally liable for the shortfall; criminal consequences include imprisonment up to 2 years for abetting tax evasion, a travel ban, and exclusion from managing other Swiss companies
Money Laundering / Shell-Company Operations

Statutory sanction: Under the Swiss Criminal Code and AMLA: imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine of up to CHF 1,000,000; unauthorised relocation of taxable profits can trigger confiscation of illicit assets; additionally, the company may be struck off and liquidated if deemed a shell without economic substance

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or facilitate MLAT/AMLA violations bear criminal liability with up to 5 years imprisonment (Art. 305bis SCC); the corporate veil is pierced for restitution and forfeiture; travel bans to ensure their appearance and prohibition from engaging in commercial activity may be imposed
