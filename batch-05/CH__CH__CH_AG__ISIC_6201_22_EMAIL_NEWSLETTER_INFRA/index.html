Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt / Registre du commerce) of the canton of the registered office, here proposed for the Canton of Zug, operating under the supervisory oversight of the Federal Department of Justice and Police (EJPD) and the Federal Commercial Register Section.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Articles 620-763 (Aktiengesellschaft); Article 719 (public deed of incorporation); Article 626-627 (mandatory content of articles); Article 631-632 (founders' declaration and capital deposit); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRegV) Articles 26-28; Federal Law on Data Protection (DSG/FADP) for processing compliance.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Statuten / Acte constitutif) together with the Commercial Register incorporation application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung / Requête d'immatriculation) and the founders' declaration (Deklaration / Déclaration des fondateurs).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital is CHF 100,000. At least CHF 50,000 (50%) must be fully paid up in cash or in kind prior to registration; cash subscriptions must be deposited in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) held at a Swiss licensed bank, and the blocking is released only upon certificate of registration. The remaining unpaid portion may be called in later by the board of directors.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace compliance under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and Labour Ordinance 3 (ArGV 3) is mandatory for any office space; a simple fire safety self-declaration is required from the Cantonal Building and Fire Insurance Authority (Gebäudeversicherung Zug) for service offices under 1,000 m², typically without structural load inspections. Upon hiring the first employee, SUVA requires compulsory accident insurance coverage and a basic workplace inspection checklist; no civil defense shelter obligation applies for purely administrative digital services.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung – ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions – AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni – AFC), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern; e-services and VAT filing portal: ESTV ePortal / estv.admin.ch. Cantonal tax authorities administer Swiss corporate income and wealth taxes for the AG.

E-government filing portal

A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft is incorporated in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and automatically receives a national business identification number, the UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), in the format CHE-123.456.789. The UID is also the Swiss TIN for tax purposes. For VAT, the AG applies to ESTV for an MWST/TVA/IVA VAT number when annual turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 or upon voluntary registration; registration is made via the ESTV ePortal supported by notarized articles of association, commercial register excerpt, board resolutions, and beneficial ownership details. A foreign entity without a Swiss registered office that is required to pay Swiss VAT must appoint a Swiss tax representative under Art. 67 MWSTG and submit a formal VAT registration application, together with the foreign business extract, power of attorney, identity evidence of directors/owners and projected Swiss turnover. The UID/TIN is then allocated by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office/UID-Register, and VAT registration is merged with the same UID identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident Swiss AG is a separate taxable legal entity, subject to unlimited Swiss income tax on worldwide profits with foreign tax credit relief, and separately subject to VAT. Swiss federal corporate income tax is levied at 8.5%; cantonal and communal profit taxes and capital tax apply in addition. Shareholders are not taxed on entity profits until distribution; there is no pass-through eligibility for an AG.

Available election forms

Swiss law does not provide US-style check-the-box or hybrid pass-through elections. The AG is mandatorily classified as an opaque corporation. Available elections include VAT group taxation under Art. 12 MWSTG for financially, economically and organizationally integrated enterprises; the participation deduction for qualifying dividend and capital gains at federal tax level (Art. 69 DBG); the Swiss group relief mechanism for cross-border losses in the form of finance-branch rules; and certain simplified VAT assessment methods for smaller taxpayers. Hybrid mismatch avoidance is governed by Swiss acceptance of foreign entity opacity based on its civil-law form; no domestic statutory hybrid mismatch election exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility both on current account and capital account. The Swiss franc is a freely floating, freely transferable currency; Switzerland maintains no exchange-control legislation, no inward/outward licensing, no conversion quotas and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment or equity repatriation for an Aktiengesellschaft. Practical frictions are limited to: (i) SNB FX-market interventions that affect the CHF level and forward points; (ii) SNB statistical reporting of large cross-border payments, generally a bank-side declaration duty for payments above CHF 10 million per transaction; (iii) FATCA/CRS documentation, so that all foreign-source income is transparent to the tax authorities; and (iv) the 35% Swiss withholding tax on dividend distributions, which is refundable or reducible under applicable double-taxation treaties but is a treasury-planning item rather than a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no prescribed conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline and no holding restrictions on foreign-currency receipts. Exporters and SaaS/software vendors may retain USD, EUR or other convertible receipts in any FINMA-regulated domestic bank or in a foreign bank account. The only administrative duty is statistical: banks automatically report cross-border SWIFT/SEPA payment flows to the SNB for balance-of-payments and direct-investment statistics, and the company must be able to produce invoices and underlying contracts on request. Service export invoices under ISIC 6201 are not subject to customs, foreign-trade or payment-licence declarations.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank, French: Banque nationale suisse), headquartered in Zurich and Bern. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration; the SNB is the sole monetary, currency and payment-system authority under the National Bank Act and the Federal Constitution. The Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervises banks for prudential and AML/CFT purposes, while the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) coordinates international financial and exchange-rate policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. FINMA-licensed domestic fintech institutions under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act (FinTech licence, public-deposit cap of CHF 100 million), for example Wise Payments AG, Yapeal or Alpian, are viable as secondary operating and settlement accounts, including USD/EUR collection from SaaS clients, but none can fully substitute a Tier-1 bank for the statutory share-capital escrow confirmation, treasury credit lines or complex ISDA hedging. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud or Mercury are legally usable for working-capital sweeps and low-value vendor disbursements, but they are not FINMA-supervised; heavy reliance on such rails creates correspondent-banking friction, counterparty concentration risk and AML-reputational exposure. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The statutory share capital (minimum CHF 100,000 nominal, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid in) is wired via unrestricted SWIFT MT103 into the AG's capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) in Switzerland before Commercial Register registration. The remitting bank must include the AG's IBAN and a free-text purpose such as 'Einzahlung Aktienkapital / Capital Contribution'; for parent-subsidiary injections the SWIFT Category Purpose Code is 'INTC' (Intra-Company Payment), while SEPA/EUR remittances use ISO 20022 Purpose Code 'EQUI' or the receiving bank's internal 'capital contribution' mapping. The escrow bank blocks the funds, issues the statutory bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) required by the notary, and releases the balance to the AG's operating account only after the Handelsregister entry is completed. No exchange-control pre-approval is required; the only automatic notification is the SNB statistical declaration for payments above the applicable threshold, generally CHF 10 million per transaction, which the receiving bank files on the company's behalf. For pre-incorporation wiring, the founders may open a Vorgründungskonto in the future company's name and subsequently transfer the balance to the registered AG.The AG's principal disbursement rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 and, where applicable, MT202 COVER for USD, GBP and other non-SEPA payments (cloud hosting, software licences, foreign contractors, dividends), with full beneficiary SWIFT/BIC-IBAN data and matching purpose codes; (2) SEPA Credit Transfers for recurring EUR vendor payments and customer refunds; (3) Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC) RTGS for CHF domestic transfers; (4) card-network settlement via Stripe, paid out to the AG's merchant settlement account using SEPA credit or domestic clearing; and (5) for high-value goods-related supply contracts, irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600 remain structurally permissible, although intangible SaaS-service disbursements are executed more efficiently by invoice-backed MT103 transfers. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Art. 727 CO, an ordinary (full) audit is mandatory if, for two consecutive fiscal years, the company exceeds any two of the following thresholds: balance sheet total of CHF 20 million; sales revenue of CHF 40 million; 250 full-time employees on annual average. It is also compulsory for public companies, companies with listed debentures, companies subject to financial-market supervision, and companies whose shareholders holding at least 10% of the capital so request. If those thresholds are not met but the company has more than 10 full-time employees, a limited audit (review) under Art. 727a CO is mandatory; below this, shareholders may unanimously waive any audit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: CHF 10,000,000 of annual cross-border controlled transactions with related non-resident parties for the same tax year. Master File and Country-by-Country Report: CHF 900,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold under Switzerland's implementation of the OECD BEPS Action 13 reporting framework.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies a real economic substance requirement derived from art. 58 DBG and the international effective-management doctrine. For an ISIC 6201 bulk transactional email API provider, the Swiss AG must demonstrate substance through a board of directors meeting in Switzerland, employees with meaningful functions and duties, an operational office or infrastructure node in Switzerland, independent contract negotiation, and direct retention of business risk. No separate Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations exist in Switzerland; however, the Swiss tax administration and DTAs require substance to qualify for treaty residence, transfer pricing adjustment avoidance and participation exemption. The platform's Swiss-held customer contracts, mail domain ownership, peering arrangements and credit management must be evidenced in the TP documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (federal/cantonal direct taxes)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 100% of the evaded tax plus default interest at 3.5% federal (higher cantonal rates apply); the tax administration recomputes the assessment with a penalty surcharge and retroactively cancels preferential deductions under the patent box, NID and participation exemption. Failure to cooperate in a Swiss tax audit can raise fines to CHF 10,000 or more.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false tax return are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax debt under arts. 53 and 173 DBG; a finding of deliberate evasion supports personal civil restitution orders and asset seizure. Swiss criminal courts may impose disqualification from board service, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to two years for simple evasion or up to three years for the qualified offence.
Shell company / sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of income under art. 58 DBG: the Federal Tax Administration attributes revenue to the actual place of management, cancels the Swiss tax residence benefits, denies treaty relief and imposes withholding at source up to 35% on deemed distributions. Fines for non-observance of tax declaration duties reach CHF 100,000 together with the administrative penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors expose the Swiss corporation to piercing the corporate veil; the court may order them to restore concealed profits to the AG and pay personal restitution for artistic misuse. Directors may face a Swiss travel ban, denial of re-entry under viewed criminal enforcement, forfeiture of personal Swiss bank accounts, and imprisonment where the shell is a vehicle for tax evasion or fraud.
AML/economic sanctions red flag

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) and SECO/OFAC financial sanctions ordinances. Fines up to CHF 750,000 and imprisonment up to five years; FINMA may impose additional administrative sanctions, freeze the AG's bank accounts, and revoke the AG's ability to maintain banking and payment-processing infrastructure.

Director personal liability: Natural-person criminal liability under art. 30 AMLA applies directly to directors as data processors and payment-transfer decision-makers. Directors are subject to disqualification from regulated email and payment activity, denial of Swiss residence permits for non-citizens, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, and personal confiscation of any benefit obtained through sanctioned business.
Transfer pricing documentation breach

Statutory sanction: The Swiss tax authorities adjust profits under art. 58 DBG based on arm's length principle, with corresponding disallowance of deductions and a 35% withholding tax on deemed hidden profit distributions. A procedural penalty up to CHF 10,000 is applied, and the taxpayer loses the evidentiary weight of its transfer pricing documentation; criminal tax evasion prosecution follows if the adjustment arises from willful misstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors signing the tax return with full cognizance that TP documentation is absent or materially false may be personally liable for the additional withholding tax assessed on deemed constructive dividends. In administrative enforcement, directors may be made personally liable for the section of the corporate tax debt associated with non-arm's length expense claims, and intentional concealment triggers personal civil damages following a criminal conviction.
