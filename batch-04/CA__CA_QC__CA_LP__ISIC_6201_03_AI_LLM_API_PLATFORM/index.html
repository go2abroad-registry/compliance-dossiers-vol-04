Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), within the Ministère de l'Économie, de l'Innovation et de l'Énergie (MEIE).

Governing statute

Civil Code of Québec (CQLR c CCQ-1991), arts. 2209-2262 (société en commandite / limited partnership), supplemented by the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (CQLR c P-44.1), ss. 27 and 33-40, on registration of the declaration of registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation (Declaration of Registration) filed under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None required. Quebec law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for limited partnerships; contributions are contractually fixed in the partnership agreement and may be monetary or in kind. No escrow account is mandated, but the general partner must maintain a contribution receipt in the partnership books (Civil Code of Québec, art. 2236).

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Code (CQLR c B-1.1, r. 10): landlords must provide fire-safety conformity certificates for leased premises; structural-load inspections apply only to constructed facilities, not to service-tenancy software operations. Under the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1), the general partner must maintain a workplace risk assessment, first-aid kit, and CNESST establishment registration once five or more workers are employed.

Paid-in capital rule: None required. Quebec law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for limited partnerships; contributions are contractually fixed in the partnership agreement and may be monetary or in kind. No escrow account is mandated, but the general partner must maintain a contribution receipt in the partnership books (Civil Code of Québec, art. 2236).

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); Revenu Québec — CliquezAffaires business registration portal (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/registering-your-business/) and Registraire des entreprises du Québec (https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca/)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine nexus. A limited partnership formed in Québec or an extra-provincial LP carrying on business in Québec must register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) under the Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises (Civil Code of Québec arts. 2188-2236) to obtain the Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ). Step 2: Register for federal income tax and program accounts. Obtain the CRA 9-digit Business Number (BN) and register the RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RC (corporation income tax, if applicable) accounts via Business Registration Online or Form RC1 / RC391 (for non-residents). Step 3: In Québec, Revenu Québec co-administers the GST/HST; the registrant must therefore also register with Revenu Québec for the GST/HST, the QST (Form LM-1, via CliquezAffaires), and provincial source deductions. Step 4: Obtain the QST registration certificate referencing the NEQ and BN. Step 5: Foreign non-resident partners must supply identity, residential and beneficial-ownership documentation; a non-resident partner without Canadian residence may be required to appoint a representative (Income Tax Act s. 273; Excise Tax Act s. 294.3) for service of process and compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 96 and the Taxation Act (Québec) ss. 599-600, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: the LP is not a taxpayer and all income, gains, losses, tax credits and capital are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners. Non-resident partners are taxable on their Canadian-source share and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on distributions (treaty-reducible). For GST/HST and QST purposes, the partnership is treated as a distinct 'person' / registrant in its own name. Québec is a civil-law jurisdiction for private law, but Canadian income tax and indirect tax legislation apply federally and provincially in a harmonized manner.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; transparency is statutory. Available elections include: (1) Income Tax Act s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to file a return on net Canadian-source rental income; (2) s. 216.1 deemed-dividend election converting non-resident partner allocations into dividends; (3) s. 96(1.1) and 96(6) partnership allocation designations for hybrid-mismatch alignment; (4) s. 156 GST/HST election for closely related corporations/partnerships to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (5) corresponding QST nil-consideration election under the Act respecting the Quebec sales tax; (6) hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act Part 3.3 (ss. 18.4, 20.4, 91.7) addressing reverse hybrids and imported mismatches; and (7) irrevocable GST/HST registration election to accelerate registration ahead of the small-supplier threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (Excise Tax Act s. 148; Quebec Sales Tax Act s. 28): a person ceases to be a small supplier once worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies but excluding financial services, capital property and goodwill, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. For the AI/LLM API platform, monthly recurring token-based API fees count as taxable supplies for the threshold. Non-resident digital-supplier simplified rules (ETA Division IV.1) impose registration with no threshold in respect of Canadian consumers, but an LP carrying on business through a Québec permanent establishment follows the ordinary regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 6 of the Excise Tax Act where goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a carrier for direct export, supported by satisfactory evidence of export. Services and intangibles: a supply of a service or a supply of intangible personal property (including access to an AI/LLM inference engine, model weights, or API token rights) made to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 7 and 10, provided the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. The QST zero-rating rules (Act respecting the Quebec sales tax and its regulations) are substantially harmonized with the GST. An AI API token supply qualifies as an exported intangible/service when the customer is a non-resident non-registrant and the platform does not or cannot reasonably determine that the supply is acquired for consumption or use in Canada. The supplier should obtain destination-based evidence (customer address, IP/geolocation, contract terms, payment origin) to support non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System / eManifest G12) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Cargo Control Document) evidencing physical export and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / payment confirmation) evidencing cross-border FX repatriation
  • Signed non-resident declaration from the customer confirming non-registrant status and non-Canadian use of the AI API services
  • Service or licensing agreement with the non-resident counterparty describing the supply, place of supply, and payment terms
  • Electronic usage and billing records (ticket-level token consumption, metering logs, IP geolocation) substantiating that the digital service was rendered to the non-resident
  • Corporate register extracts or beneficial ownership evidence for non-resident partners where the LP is claiming zero-rating on internal partner supplies

Canada and Québec operate a post-audit fiscal model with no real-time clearance, no government fiscal stamp, and no API-based stamping mandate. E-invoicing is permitted (PDF, XML, EDI, Peppol-based formats) provided the invoice is legible and contains the prescribed information: supplier legal name (the LP, not the partners), registration number for GST/HST and QST, date of invoice, description of the supply, consideration, and tax amounts. The CRA accepts structured electronic invoices, and Canada is progressively adopting the Pan-Canadian e-Invoice Interoperability Framework based on Peppol on a voluntary basis; business-to-government e-invoicing was launched in 2024-2025 but remains voluntary. Québec requires that a QST-registered supplier show its QST number on invoices and retain electronic books and records in conformity with the Regulation respecting books and records. No digital clearance or nightly validation is imposed.

Canada imposes no codified 180-day FX repatriation deadline for services; the 180-day parameter reflects the administrative benchmark used by CRA and Revenu Québec for producing bank-repatriation evidence during audit. Penalties on fraudulent or undocumented export zero-rating: (1) reassessment of the full 14.975% combined GST/HST+QST retroactively where the supplier fails to sustain the non-resident or export documentation burden; (2) gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (Excise Tax Act s. 285.1); (3) false-statement penalty and failure-to-file penalties (ETA ss. 281.1, 280); (4) corresponding QST penalties under the Quebec Sales Tax Act, including 25% gross-negligence penalties and penal fines; (5) criminal prosecution for evasion (ETA s. 327) punishable by fines of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; (6) interest compounded daily on all reassessed amounts; and (7) the normal 6-year reassessment limitation period is waived where misrepresentation is attributable to fraud, neglect, or wilful default (ETA s. 298(1)(d)). Records and zero-rating support must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full currency convertibility under both current and capital account classifications, consistent with Article VIII of the International Monetary Fund Articles of Agreement. There are no statutory exchange controls, no mandatory conversion quotas, and no Central Bank approval obligation for profit repatriation by non-residents. The practical legal hurdles for a Quebec limited partnership distributing income to foreign partners are: (i) Canadian withholding tax on Part XIII payments where the income is sourced in Canada; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting for amounts at or above CAD 10,000; (iii) OFSI consolidated sanctions list and SEMA compliance screening; (iv) business number registration and GST/HST administration obligations if the LP is carrying on business in Quebec; and (v) documentation evidencing entitlement under the applicable tax treaty to reduce withholding on service fees or royalties. For USDC and other crypto-asset receipt flows, the Financial Action Task Force-based AML rules require source-of-crypto wallet verification and the maintaining of blockchain forensic records where convertible virtual currency receipts form part of operating turnover.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no minimum conversion ratios, and no time-limited repatriation obligations for software/cloud API export receipts under Canadian law. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency-denominated bank accounts held at any Schedule I Canadian bank, or in an offshore account subject to Canadian tax reporting on specified foreign property and attribution of foreign exchange gains or losses to the LP's partners for income tax purposes. Physical import or export of currency or bearer monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more triggers Form E311 (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instrument Report) to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Electronic inbound and outbound wire transfers attributable to the LP of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the responsible financial intermediary to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada does not maintain a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration, and exchange control supervision is instead effected through banking prudential rules, AML/ATF regulations, and sanctions administration administered by Global Affairs Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility for using offshore fintech EMIs is 'Medium' with legal constraints. Platforms such as Stripe Treasury, Wise Business, Airwallex and Payoneer can provide payment acceptance and foreign currency collection in Canada without being chartered banks, but they cannot legally serve as the sole repository for partner capital contributions or substantive treasury liquidity unless operated as registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) in Canada under FINTRAC and holding provincial authority in Quebec. Large-value cross-border clearing above CAD 500,000 should exclusively use a Schedule I bank to avoid regulatory uncertainty, sub-custody risk, and PCMLTFA non-compliance. Incoming funds to an EMI may also create double KYC requirements and possible exposure to the unwinding of bank partner relationships. For low-value AI API subscription collections, Stripe as a payment facilitator is viable; for USDC treasury operations, a regulated custodian with Canadian MSB or provincial licensing must be engaged, and the LP should maintain the majority of operating cash in a Tier 1 Canadian bank account.

Large-value cross-border clearing above CAD 500,000 should exclusively use a Schedule I bank to avoid regulatory uncertainty, sub-custody risk, and PCMLTFA non-compliance. Incoming funds to an EMI may also create double KYC requirements and possible exposure to the unwinding of bank partner relationships. For low-value AI API subscription collections, Stripe as a payment facilitator is viable; for USDC treasury operations, a regulated custodian with Canadian MSB or provincial licensing must be engaged, and the LP should maintain the majority of operating cash in a Tier 1 Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital injections into the Quebec LP must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 pain.001 corporate-to-bank message through the originating bank and settled via the Canadian Lynx RTGS or correspondent US Fedwire network. The remittance field (field 70) must contain an unambiguous purpose descriptor, for example: 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION // FDI // ISIC 6201 // AI LLM API PLATFORM // PROJECT [REF] // QUEBEC CA'. For each inbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, the receiving bank is required to file a FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFT). The beneficiary must match the LP name and account mandate exactly; pre-delivery verification includes the payment confirmation, the partner's passport or entity registry, and a copy of the capital call notice issued by the general partner. Statistics Canada may require a Foreign Direct Investment Return if the cumulative Canadian-based assets or revenue exceed established thresholds; prompt response is necessary to avoid administrative penalties. For domestic Canadian disbursements, Payments Canada operating rails are the Lynx Real-Time Gross Settlement System for individual values above CAD 250,000, the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch transfers below CAD 25 million, and the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for lower-value immediate payments. Vendor payments requiring US dollar clearing outside Canada will use the US correspondent network or a bank-to-bank SWIFT gpi track. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million in aggregate transactions with non-residents (or if the partnership is a large entity with gross revenue/assets exceeding CAD 50 million)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have ESR, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply; partnerships must have real economic activity to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and interest on unpaid taxes

Director personal liability: Directors (general partners) may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and interest

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for the tax liability if the partnership is deemed a sham; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, imprisonment for individuals, and suspension of operations

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly participate or fail to supervise; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Transfer Pricing Penalties

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment, plus interest; potential double taxation

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable if they knowingly participate in transfer pricing mispricing; civil penalties and potential criminal prosecution for gross negligence.
