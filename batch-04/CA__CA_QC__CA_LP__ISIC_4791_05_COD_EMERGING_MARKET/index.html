Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no statutorily mandated escrow account apply to a Quebec limited partnership. Contributions are fixed freely in the limited partnership agreement. For this engagement, the agreed initial capital contribution is CAD 100,000, to be deposited into the LP's Quebec operating account or Airwallex settlement wallet; any cash-on-delivery courier escrow is contractual security, not a statutory requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to the office-only formation stage. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal (www.canada.ca) and CRA My Business Account; Quebec sub-authority: Revenu Québec (CLIC portal) and Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ issuance)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-resident partner establishing a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) must: (1) register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, obtaining the Quebec enterprise number (NEQ); (2) apply for the CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Business Number Request), selecting the GST/HST account (RT), partnership income account (RC), and import/export account (RM) as needed; (3) register separately for QST with Revenu Québec, which administers GST and QST in Quebec under a single-administration agreement; (4) file Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return) annually if the LP carries on business in Canada or earns Canadian-source income. No minimum capital contribution is required; non-resident ownership is permitted, but a representative for service of process may be required. The LP's pass-through status is automatic upon formation; the BN serves as the sole federal tax identification number for the partnership.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA): the partnership does not pay income tax; each partner is taxed on its distributive share. Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable under ITA s. 2(3) and Part I / Part XIV on Canadian-source business income attributable to the LP's Canadian business. No Quebec tax-exempt zone applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Quebec LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA): the partnership does not pay income tax; each partner is taxed on its distributive share. Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable under ITA s. 2(3) and Part I / Part XIV on Canadian-source business income attributable to the LP's Canadian business. No Quebec tax-exempt zone applies. For GST/HST and QST purposes, the LP is a distinct 'registrant person' separate from its partners and must account for tax itself.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-entity classification; pass-through treatment is mandatory for income tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) non-calendar partnership fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1, permitted only where no partner is a 'specified member' (partnerships with Canadian-resident partners are effectively forced to a December 31 year-end); (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election for qualifying small suppliers whose annual worldwide taxable revenues do not exceed CAD 400,000; (iii) no hybrid-mismatch election is available — the hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 94.1–94.3) operate by disallowance, not registration; (iv) the ETA s. 156 closely related corporation election is not available to partnerships.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a supplier becomes a mandatory registrant when worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies for QST in Quebec. Once exceeded, registration is mandatory effective from the date the threshold was surpassed. Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Canada (including digital or mail-order sales) are subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold under the place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including mail-order and COD parcels) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) if the supplier exports the goods or delivers them to a common carrier for export to a non-resident purchaser within 60 days of the time of supply, and property in the goods passes outside Canada. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated only where not performed in Canada or performed wholly outside Canada; services relating to Canadian real property or tangible personal property do not qualify. QST zero-rating mirrors the GST under the Quebec Sales Tax Act. COD settlement through a foreign logistics partner or convergent payment rail (e.g., Airwallex) does not disqualify zero-rating, provided the vendor's records evidence delivery to a foreign address and that the goods were not re-delivered into Canada.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (electronic export reporting via Canada Border Services Agency, G13/CAARS confirmation, required for exports exceeding CAD 2,500 or regulated goods)
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract naming the foreign COD buyer
  • Bill of Lading or parcel consignment waybill evidencing Canadian consignor and foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent — SWIFT MT103, Airwallex export collection statement, or the local logistics partner's COD remittance report
  • Packing list and shipping log matching the customs export declaration
  • Proof of non-resident status of the buyer (foreign billing/shipping address, IP or geo-location evidence for COD verification)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous-control invoicing regime is in force in Canada or Quebec as of 2025. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative has adopted the Peppol framework on a voluntary basis, with production availability targeted for 2026. Mandatory electronic filing applies: GST/HST returns must be filed via GST/HST NETFILE (CRA) and QST returns via Revenu Québec's CLIC platform. No API-based pre-clearance or CRA/Revenu Québec validation of individual invoices is required; paper or PDF invoices remain legally valid if they meet ETA s. 223 record-keeping requirements (six years' retention).

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess within the normal four-year period from the filing due date; the period extends to eight years for large businesses (annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 500,000) and is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default is established. Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized for proof of export within the 60-day rule and non-return of goods into Canada; failure to substantiate results in retroactive clawback of the 0% rate and assessment of 14.975% GST/QST, plus interest under ETA s. 280 and the QSTA. Civil penalties for false statements in returns are the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 285.1); false GST/QST rebate or refund claims attract additional penalties of CAD 500 to CAD 10,000. Criminal prosecution for willful evasion under ETA s. 327.02 may result in fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Although the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is a compliance covenant of the Airwallex/local logistics remittance rails rather than an ETA statutory requirement, late or undocumented repatriation may trigger Part XIII withholding exposure where the non-resident logistics partner is deemed to earn Canadian-source service income.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a freely convertible Canadian dollar and no general exchange-control regime. Current-account payments, including trade payments, service fees, interest, dividends, partnership distributions, and other ordinary commercial remittances, are generally permitted without central-bank pre-approval. Capital-account transactions, including capital contributions, loans, acquisitions, guarantees, and repatriation of invested capital, are also generally unrestricted, subject to corporate authority, partnership-agreement terms, sanctions, tax compliance, anti-money-laundering controls, beneficial-ownership verification, transfer-pricing requirements, withholding-tax obligations, thin-capitalization rules where applicable, and lender or investor restrictions. A Quebec limited partnership may distribute cash subject to its partnership agreement, solvency considerations, creditor claims, applicable tax reporting, and confirmation that the distribution is not a disguised payment for an unlawful or restricted transaction. Canadian financial institutions may require source-of-funds evidence, tax residency information, foreign tax forms, contracts, invoices, customs records, and a clear economic rationale before processing large or unusual cross-border transfers. There is no general profit-remittance approval or mandatory conversion into Canadian dollars.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not generally require exporters to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert foreign-currency receipts, or repatriate receipts within a statutory period. Export proceeds may ordinarily be retained in foreign-currency accounts or received through payment processors, subject to contractual banking terms, sanctions screening, tax reporting, accounting records, and anti-money-laundering controls. Cross-border currency or monetary-instrument movements of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction or related transactions within the applicable reporting framework must be reported through the prescribed Canada Border Services Agency process when physically imported into or exported from Canada; financial institutions also have separate FINTRAC reporting obligations for reportable transactions. There is no mandatory export-proceeds conversion ratio. Foreign-currency revenue remains reportable for Canadian income-tax and financial-reporting purposes using applicable exchange-rate and recognition rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign-exchange transaction reporting and anti-money-laundering supervision also involve the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Canada Border Services Agency for cross-border currency reporting, the Canada Revenue Agency for tax administration, and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for Quebec-regulated derivatives and financial-market activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, foreign-exchange conversion, supplier payments, and treasury workflow, but not a complete substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank. Airwallex and comparable foreign electronic-money institutions or payment-service providers may provide multi-currency collection and payout services only within their licensing perimeter, contractual corridors, and correspondent-banking arrangements. Client-money safeguarding or segregation does not necessarily provide Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation protection and may not constitute a Canadian operating deposit account. The provider may decline cash-on-delivery proceeds, marketplace flows, emerging-market counterparties, high chargeback exposure, cash-intensive activity, or unexplained third-party collections. The limited partnership should maintain a Canadian bank as its primary operating and reserve account, use payment accounts only for documented commercial flows, reconcile processor settlements to orders and delivery records, and avoid personal accounts, nominee accounts, undisclosed sub-merchants, and unsupported third-party settlement instructions. Canadian anti-money-laundering, sanctions, consumer-protection, privacy, tax, payment-service, and foreign-licensing requirements may apply depending on the actual activities and customer location.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). The CA_LP is fiscally transparent; no entity-level tax is imposed, and each partner is taxed on allocated income at their applicable rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

37% combined statutory on dividends paid to non-residents of Canada by a payer resident in Quebec (federal Part XIII 25% + Quebec Taxation Act s. 1041.0.1 additional 12%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (RC5107): mandatory for fiscal years beginning after 2022 when total cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000. Master File: required when such transactions exceed CAD 100,000,000. T106 information return: required for reportable transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Country-by-Country Report: required for MNEs with group revenue of at least CAD 1.06 billion (EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies no UAE-style ESR. However, non-resident LPs must demonstrate a permanent establishment (DTA Article V) and real business substance to access treaty benefits. The GAAR (federal Income Tax Act s. 245; Quebec Taxation Act s. 1079.12), the 'carrying on business in Canada' test (s. 253), and CRA substance reviews may re-characterize a shell LP that has no employees, premises, or independent decision-making in Quebec.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax avoided plus arrears interest; criminal conviction under ITA s. 239 and Quebec TAA s. 72: fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (indictable).

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate penalties; personal bankruptcy does not discharge these debts; travel bans and extradition can be ordered under tax-treaty mutual assistance.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization and denial of the fiscal transparency of the LP; reassessment of the full 37% withholding on all repatriated amounts plus interest and penalties up to 50%; Quebec GAAR penalty and fines.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under common-law agency or sham doctrines; general partners are personally liable for the full tax debt of the LP; directors of the corporate general partner face civil restitution and potential criminal prosecution for forgery of documentation.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII and Quebec 12%

Statutory sanction: The full unwithheld amount becomes collectible from the payer under ITA s. 227(8) and Quebec TAA; penalty of 10% of the amount not withheld (s. 227(9)) plus daily interest on arrears; Quebec adds a 15% penalty on unreported withholdable amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 and Quebec TAA s. 278 for all unremitted withholdings, including interest and penalties; this liability survives bankruptcy (super-priority claim) and can trigger personal travel bans during collection.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) under ITA s. 247(3) and Quebec Taxation Act s. 1299.1; CRA may reassess beyond the normal three-year limitation period when transfer-pricing adjustments exceed the specified threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable, but involvement in the preparation of false documentation attracts the 50% gross-negligence penalty (s. 163(2)) and criminal liability under s. 239; penalties are uninsurable and non-dischargeable in bankruptcy.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance (COD E-commerce)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation for enterprises under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal money-laundering conviction: unlimited court-discretion fines and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and partners who directed or authorized the non-compliance face personal criminal liability (PCMLTFA s. 74); FINTRAC may also seek civil penalties against individuals; travel restrictions can be imposed pending criminal prosecution.
