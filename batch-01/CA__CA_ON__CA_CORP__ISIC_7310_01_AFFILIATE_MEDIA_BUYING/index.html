E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders; there is no pass-through regime and no check-the-box election in Canada. A corporation resident in Canada is taxed on worldwide income (Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), s.2(1)); a non-resident corporation is taxed on Canadian-source business income and taxable Canadian property dispositions (ITA s.2(3), s.115, s.253). Ontario levies provincial corporate income tax at 8% on the first C$500,000 of active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) and 11.5% on general corporate income. No tax-exempt zone or industry-specific exemption applies to digital advertising or affiliate media buying in Ontario.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; classification follows incorporation law. Available elections include: (i) ITA s.85(1) rollover on property transfers into the corporation; (ii) ITA s.216 election for non-residents on net Canadian rental income; (iii) ITA s.217 election for non-residents on certain Canadian pension income; (iv) associated-corporation elections under ITA s.256 for allocating the C$500,000 small business deduction; and (v) CCPC elections on the T2 return for refundable dividend tax accounts. Hybrid-mismatch constraints directly relevant to foreign-owned media-buying corporations: ITA ss.18.2 and 18.4 deny deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements; ITA s.18(4) thin-capitalization limits interest deductions on shareholder debt to a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio; and Part II.2 foreign affiliate dumping tax (ITA s.212.3, Form RC4651) may apply to excessive capital injections from non-resident shareholders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, no requirement for central bank approval on dividend repatriation, and no restrictions on converting CAD into major currencies. However, any transfer of funds exceeding CAD 10,000 into or out of Canada is reportable to FINTRAC, and certain outbound payments to high-risk jurisdictions must satisfy enhanced due diligence standards. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is freely permitted after compliance with corporate law solvency tests and tax withholding reporting (Part XIII T5 filings for dividends).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in any permitted currency. Nevertheless, all receipts crossing the border must be traceable through FINTRAC-reporting channels, and underlying commercial documents must be retained to substantiate the source and purpose of export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. Cross-border monetary and AML reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary cross-border payment flows, but not recommended as the sole primary banking relationship. Legally, a foreign offshore digital EMI must be registered as a money-services business (MSB) in Canada if it conducts payment services for Canadian resident entities, or must obtain a limited-services arrangement through a chartered Canadian bank. Wise Canada holds an MSB licence in Canada and can provide multi-currency operating rails. Mercury is oriented principally to US entities and typically does not support a pure Ontario business corporation account; therefore it should be used only for distinctly separate US-based affiliated entities, not for routing the Canadian treasury. The Canadian operating entity must maintain a core account at a tier-1 Schedule I Canadian bank to ensure regulatory certainty, ISO 20022 wire routing, and same-day CAD clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from a foreign parent or shareholder must be routed through SWIFT WT (wire transfer) using the Canadian beneficiary bank's BIC and the Ontario entity's account number. The remittance details must state either 'Shareholder Loan', 'Equity Injection / Paid-in Capital', or 'Intercompany Advance' as the payment purpose; no government-issued transaction code is required in Canada, but the bank may ask for supporting purpose codes in its internal forms. A copy of the loan agreement, corporate resolution, and ownership chart must be provided to the receiving bank to release funds to the capital escrow or operating account without AML holds. For amounts equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, the bank will file a Large Cash Transaction / Cross-Border Transfer Report with FINTRAC; the entity must ensure the remitting account is held in the name of the same corporate beneficial owner or identified arm's-length financier to avoid a suspicious transaction report.Outbound disbursements to international media vendors, affiliate networks, DSPs, and data providers should use SWIFT MT103 for same-day or value-dated cross-border settlement when the beneficiary is not enrolled in Canadian direct clearing. For repeat high-value media buys of over CAD 50,000, a commercial letter of credit (MT700) is advisable where the counterparty requires payment assurance before delivering ad inventory; standby LC (MT760) can also support ongoing media-rate cards. Domestic publisher payments can use EFT/ACH or wire transfer through the operating bank. Each disbursement must include the invoice number, contract reference, and clear description (e.g., 'digital media purchase' or 'performance advertising services') in the remittance field.
