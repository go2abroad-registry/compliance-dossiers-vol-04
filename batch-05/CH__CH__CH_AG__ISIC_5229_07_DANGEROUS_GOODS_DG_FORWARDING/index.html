Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000; at least 20% (minimum CHF 50,000) must be paid up at incorporation. Capital contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank, evidenced by a bank certificate; funds are released only upon successful Commercial Register registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Cantonal Fire Prevention Service and Building Insurance (GVA) under VKF norms; structural load and stability inspection certificate in accordance with SIA 461 for DG staging areas; occupational safety and workplace health clearance issued by Suva (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund), including mandatory medical surveillance and CMR-gas monitoring for DG-handling personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory share capital for an AG is CHF 100,000; at least 20% (minimum CHF 50,000) must be paid up at incorporation. Capital contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a licensed Swiss bank, evidenced by a bank certificate; funds are released only upon successful Commercial Register registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenoessische Steuerverwaltung / Administration federale des contributions / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni), Schwarztorstrasse 50, 3003 Bern, Switzerland. Official portal: www.estv.admin.ch (VAT e-services, DEVA electronic filing). Central business register portal: www.zefix.ch. Swiss UID public register: www.uid.admin.ch. Cantonal tax administrations retain competence for cantonal and communal direct taxes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) by notarial deed, deposit minimum share capital of CHF 100,000 (at least 50% paid in), appoint the board of directors, and file for entry in the competent Cantonal Commercial Register. Step 2: The Commercial Register entry automatically allocates the Swiss business identifier (UID / Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer), which is the master tax identity number for all federal, cantonal and communal tax purposes. Step 3: Register for VAT with the Swiss Federal Tax Administration via Form 'Registration as a VAT taxable person' (eService) or via the UID portal once annual worldwide turnover from taxable supplies in Switzerland exceeds CHF 100,000, or voluntarily for lower turnover. Step 4: A foreign-incorporated enterprise with a Swiss permanent establishment or performing taxable supplies in Switzerland must obtain a Swiss UID through the UID registry, appoint a Swiss fiscal representative for VAT where no Swiss domicile exists, and register with the ESTV VAT division before the first taxable supply. No separate federal tax ID is issued for corporate income tax beyond the UID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Swiss AG is a resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide profits (Art. 52 ff. Federal Direct Tax Act / DBG). Direct federal corporate income tax is levied at a flat rate of 8.5% on profit after tax (effective burden approximately 7.83% on pre-tax profit); cantonal and communal profit taxes apply at combined rates of approximately 11% to 21% depending on the canton of registered seat. Distributions are subject to Swiss withholding tax of 35%, reduced or refunded under applicable tax treaties. No pass-through or partnership treatment is available for an AG; the entity is a per se corporation and cannot be disregarded for Swiss tax purposes. Capital tax is imposed at cantonal level.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not provide a check-the-box or other entity-classification election under domestic tax law; an AG is mandatorily subject to entity-level taxation. Available statutory elections and administrative arrangements include: (i) VAT group election (Art. 12 VAT Act) allowing closely held affiliated companies to be treated as one VAT taxpayer with joint and several liability; (ii) participation-relief election for qualifying dividends and capital gains (Art. 70 and 71 DBG plus cantonal equivalents); (iii) Swiss principal-company ruling election allowing centralisation of international purchasing, sales and logistics functions under a reduced federal and cantonal profit allocation; (iv) patent-box / IP-relief election in participating cantons, subject to a binding tax ruling; (v) election to treat foreign permanent establishments as separate taxpayers or to apply a tax-exemption method, as available under cantonal practice. Hybrid mismatch rules under OECD BEPS Action 2 are implemented statutorily and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide turnover from supplies taxable in Switzerland (Art. 10 para. 2 let. a VAT Act). Foreign-established companies without a Swiss place of business or domicile are subject to compulsory VAT registration from the first taxable supply in Switzerland (no monetary threshold). Voluntary VAT registration is permitted from the first franc of turnover where input VAT recovery is sought. Since 1 January 2018, entry in the Swiss Commercial Register no longer triggers automatic VAT liability; the CHF 100,000 threshold applies uniformly to all taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act, supplies of goods are zero-rated if the supplier proves that the goods were physically exported from the Swiss customs territory and delivered to a recipient abroad. Under Art. 23 para. 2 VAT Act, services directly attributable to the export or import of goods are zero-rated, including international transport, freight forwarding, customs clearance, temporary storage, handling and ancillary logistics services for dangerous goods. For a DG forwarding business, the zero rating applies if the freight forwarding service forms part of a single supply directly connected with the physical cross-border movement of the regulated hazardous goods; the Swiss domestic leg of an international export transport retains the 0% rate where the single-supply and direct-attribution tests are satisfied. The service must be rendered by a Swiss taxable person and the transport chain must be fully documented. Swiss VAT law imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard export-financing documentation and bank / export-credit verification practice rather than a Swiss VAT statutory condition.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Swiss e-dec / e-dec web export declaration (A- or B-number) confirmed by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) evidencing the physical exit of dangerous goods from the Swiss customs territory, including transit documentation where applicable.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR - international contract of carriage plus the freight forwarder's receipt (Spediteur-Uebernahmebescheinigung), the dangerous-goods transport documentation (IMO/IMDG container packing certificate, IATA shipper's declaration for dangerous goods, ADR consignment note, material safety data sheet) and the commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - in Swiss practice, a bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 statement evidencing receipt from the foreign principal in foreign currency; no formal statutory Inward Remittance Certificate exists under Swiss VAT law, but the payment record must be retained in the substantiation file for the 10-year retention period.

None. Switzerland has no real-time fiscal clearance system, no mandatory transaction-level API reporting, no electronic invoice validation and no digital stamping regime for VAT purposes. E-invoicing is standardised but voluntary, using the Swiss QR-bill standard, Swiss Payment Standards 2025 and Peppol BIS 3.0 with Swiss Business Rules. A federal B2G e-invoicing mandate for federal procurement is being introduced progressively starting 2026, but it does not require API-based fiscal clearance. VAT returns are filed quarterly or half-yearly via ESTV e-Service; accounting and substantiation records must be retained for 10 years.

Swiss VAT operates on self-assessment and zero-rating claims are subject to systematic ESTV audit. Consequences of wrongful zero-rating: (i) the VAT shortfall is retroactively assessed at the applicable rate (8.1% standard, 3.8% accommodation, 2.6% reduced) together with default interest from the original due date; (ii) simple tax evasion (Art. 97 VAT Act) is punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; (iii) qualified tax evasion or fraudulent use of false documents (Art. 98 VAT Act) carries imprisonment of up to three years or a fine; (iv) violation of procedural and record-keeping duties is subject to fines of up to CHF 8,000; (v) in fraudulent export chains involving forged customs papers, disappearing exporters or phantom goods, the ESTV may impose joint liability, disallow input VAT recovery and initiate criminal proceedings, with publication of final convictions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility of the Swiss franc (CHF) for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements for cross-border payments, and no restrictions on profit repatriation or capital outflows. The CHF is a freely floating currency; however, transactions must comply with Swiss anti-money laundering (AMLA), sanctions, and reporting obligations under the Federal Act on the Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing. No differentiated convertibility regime exists for current versus capital account items.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios in Switzerland. Exporters are free to retain, use, or invest foreign currency proceeds without any obligation to repatriate or convert into CHF. This liberal regime is subject only to standard banking and tax transparency requirements, including proper bookkeeping and potential documentation requests under double taxation treaties or FATCA/CRS.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Direct Federal Tax (effective 7.83% pre-tax equivalent); combined cantonal/municipal rates yield a statutory head-line range of 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton and commune, e.g. Geneva ~14.0%, Zurich ~21.0%, Zug ~11.9%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Swiss anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer/impôt anticipé) of 35% is withheld on dividend distributions and similar profit distributions by Swiss AGs; this statutory rate is reduced or refunded under DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Swiss tax law does not impose a fixed value-based Local File/Master File threshold for all entities. Mandatory country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of CHF 900 million or more. For local transfer-pricing documentation, Swiss practice requires a Local File for companies with more than 250 employees, total assets above CHF 20 million, or turnover above CHF 50 million, or whenever intercompany cross-border transactions exceed CHF 5 million in a tax year; group-wide Master File is expected above the same group-size safe harbour (SME relief threshold).

Economic substance scope: Switzerland applies an anti-abuse substance doctrine, distinct from EU-style ESR, under which an AG must have real personnel, premises, decision-making, administrative functions, and economic activity in Switzerland. Letterbox or pure domiciliary companies are taxed as fully taxable Swiss entities or substantially denied treaty benefits. For a DG forwarding company, required substance includes a Swiss business address, operational licences under the Swiss Dangerous Goods Ordinance (SDR 741.621), ADR/IMDG-certified personnel, genuine board and shareholder meetings in Switzerland, and actual management of hazardous-goods transport.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / Tax fraud under Swiss direct tax law

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge up to 200% of evaded federal/cantonal tax plus 3.5% per annum interest; criminal tax fraud carries a public-law fine up to the tax evaded plus damages and imprisonment up to 5 years; prosecution within 10 years.

Director personal liability: Members of the Board of Directors and officers are personally liable for deliberate false declarations; sanctions include attachment of private assets (civil restitution to the tax authority), travel bans where criminal proceedings have started, 10-year statute of limitations, and possible custodial sentence of 6 months to 5 years for tax fraud.
Shell company / sham substance and abusive treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of CIT assessment at preferential rates, disallowance of group tax relief, restitution of unfairly obtained treaty benefits, additional taxes of 25–35% plus interest, and licence revocation of the DG transport licence; civil penalties and prohibition to exercise a Swiss commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign substanceless financial statements and tax returns are exposed to personal liability for restitution of lost Swiss taxes, directors' disqualification, seizure of Swiss bank and private assets, and imprisonment in aggravated cases.
Transfer pricing mispricing and non-arm's-length cash flows

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit by the Swiss tax authority; assessment of hidden profit distributions subject to 35% anticipatory tax; back-transfer of tax, penalty surcharge up to 100–200%, and 3.5% arrears interest; criminal fine and mandatory revision of annual accounts if adverse audit report.

Director personal liability: Swiss director can be held civilly liable for corporate tax debts if he/she knowingly authorized abusive TP schemes; board members may be joined as defendants in tax withdrawal procedures, and travel measures (confiscation of passports) may be ordered during criminal tax proceedings.
AML and sanctions violations in hazardous-goods cash flow / trade remittances

Statutory sanction: Anti-money-laundering supervision measures; administrative fines up to CHF 500,000 for serious violations in the financial intermediary sector; confiscation of profits, closure of Swiss banking relationships, reporting to the Money Laundering Reporting Office; parallel customs criminal proceedings and penalties.

Director personal liability: Prolonged disregard of AML and sanctions diligence exposes directors to Swiss AML criminal prosecution (including imprisonment), personal restitution, travel bans and denial of residency/immigration rights, and disqualification from serving as Swiss board members; the corporate veil is not a defence once criminal negligence is proven.
False reporting under the Swiss Anticipatory Tax and Swiss VAT

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax at 35% is shifted to the company; imposition of unpaid withholding tax plus 5% penalty and interest; VAT evasion fines of up to 300% of connected tax; loss of deduction of input VAT; publication of liability in Swiss Official Gazette.

Director personal liability: Executive directors who knowingly omit withholding-tax declaration or VAT payments are personally liable for the company's tax debt and exposed to criminal fines/imprisonment up to 3 years; private assets may be arrested and a commercial prohibition issued.
