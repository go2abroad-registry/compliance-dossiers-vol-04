Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the QBCA; a single incorporator may subscribe for one common share for nominal cash consideration. No escrow account or capital sequestration requirement applies. Shares must be issued for adequate consideration determined by the directors; partly paid shares are restricted and paid-up value must be credited in the securities register.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate per the Fire Safety Act (CQLR c S-3.4); structural adequacy and occupancy verified through the municipal occupancy permit; CNESST workplace clearance including employer registration, health and safety plan, first aid kit, evacuation signage, and compliance with the Act respecting occupational health and safety (CQLR c S-2.1).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll remittances; Revenu Québec (Revenue Quebec) for Quebec corporate income tax, QST, and employer contributions. Portals: CRA My Business Account; Revenu Québec clic Revenu / Mon dossier d'entreprise.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or deriving Canadian-source income must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 or by registering through CRA My Business Account, and must open the required GST/HST and payroll accounts. For Quebec operations, the corporation must first incorporate or extra-provincially register with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain a Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ), then register with Revenu Québec for QST, corporate tax, and employer deduction accounts. Separate provincial registration is mandatory where the corporation maintains an establishment, employees, or taxable sales in Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal/provincial business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the Quebec Taxation Act. A Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income, taxable capital gains on qualifying Canadian property, and certain other Canadian-source income. An employee stock ownership trust may itself be taxed as a resident trust under trust rules, but the sponsoring corporation is entity-level taxed and cannot elect pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid entity election exists under Canadian law. Available tax elections include corporate rollovers under ITA sections 85, 86, 87, and 88, intra-group transfers, and employee stock option timing/deduction elections under section 7. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3) restrict deduction for interest or royalties involving hybrid instruments/entities and are considered where foreign intermediary shareholders are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (not including capital sales and certain excluded supplies) across the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST small-supplier threshold; Revenu Québec applies a similar CAD 30,000 QST small-supplier threshold. Registration is mandatory when the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration may also be elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies may be zero-rated where the exporter substantiates that goods have been physically exported from Canada or that the supply is a service supplied to a non-resident outside Canada, subject to rules against services consumed in Canada. For employee share incentive administration services rendered by a Quebec corporation to a non-resident employer or trust, zero-rating may be available if the services are not used in Canada, delivery occurs outside Canada, and the recipient is neither a Canadian resident nor a person required to register for Canadian GST/HST. Supporting contracts, service provider documentation, and objective commercial evidence must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian B13A form or CBSA electronic export declaration / ACI export reporting, as applicable)
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic sea/air/truck waybill with shipper and consignee details)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX receipt evidencing payment received from the non-resident buyer or trust

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) API, reference number stamping, or pre-approval digital stamping mandate exists in Canada or Quebec. GST/HST and QST invoices may be issued electronically in PDF, EDI, or XML where the parties agree and the invoice contains all prescribed particulars, including registrant identification, invoice date, taxable supplies, tax rate, and tax amount. Revenu Québec requires Quebec business registration numbers to appear on invoices/records and has electronic filing mandates for returns, but no pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

CRA and Revenu Québec scrutinize export zero-rating claims through document-matching and audit. If substantiation bundles are incomplete, the zero-rating is denied and tax is reassessed with statutory interest. Civil penalties may reach 50% of the tax amount for knowingly making false statements or omissions; failure to maintain records can lead to additional penalties and an extended reassessment period beyond the usual 4-year limitation. Criminal referral is possible for fraudulent export schemes, with retrospective clawbacks of refunds and input tax credits, plus Quebec-specific QST parallel penalties under the Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible across both current-account and capital-account transactions. No Bank of Canada or Finance Canada pre-approval is required for inbound capital contributions, equity subscriptions, trust corpus injections, or outbound remittances of dividends and trust distributions to non-residents. The only binding restrictions are tax-based: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% federal-provincial composite, typically reduced by treaty to 5-15% on dividends) and Quebec income tax/QST compliance. There are no exchange-control quotas, 'nesting' limits, or conversion windows; repatriation is unrestricted, subject only to CRA T2/T3 filing and NR4/Part XIII remittance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding period on export proceeds or any foreign-currency receipts. Exporters and trust administrators may freely retain, deploy, and remit foreign currency. The only obligations are (i) CBSA export declarations and Balance of Payments statistical reporting, (ii) FINTRAC declarations for cross-border currency movements and EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) OSFI/CBSA record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Foreign-currency share consideration received by the ESOP trust is therefore fully retainable without conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking supervision by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); AML and payment monitoring by FINTRAC; Quebec money-services business licensing by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, governed by clear legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the EU EMI passport and has no standalone e-money institution licence. An offshore digital EMI may operate in Quebec only as a money-services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, permitted by the AMF under the Money-Services Businesses Act (CQLR c S-16.1). The EMI may serve as a low-value disbursement rail for payroll wires and beneficiary payments, but it cannot lawfully hold deposits, maintain escrow or trust accounts, or act as a settlement bank. Recommended architecture: a chartered-bank operational/escrow account at the top of the structure, with the EMI used solely for downstream distribution efficiency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 using ISO 20022 purpose codes corresponding to 'Capital subscription' (commonly 'CAPT' or 'EQUI', or the bank's proprietary equivalent), with remittance field 70 stating 'ESOP Trust capital subscription - CA-QC EOT' and beneficiary details comprising full bank name, transit and account number, and BIC. CAD-denominated credits clear through Lynx (high-value) or ACSS (standard); foreign-currency credits clear via correspondent banking and may be converted to CAD by the appointed dealer.
