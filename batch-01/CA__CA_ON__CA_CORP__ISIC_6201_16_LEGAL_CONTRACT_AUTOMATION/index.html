Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — 'My Business Account' portal at canada.ca

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or required to collect GST/HST must register with the CRA to obtain a Business Number (BN) and separate GST/HST account. Registration is done online through the CRA's My Business Account portal, by paper Form RC1, or via registered tax representative. The entity must provide legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office address, director/officer details, and description of activities. The CRA issues a nine-digit BN plus two-digit program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST). A non-resident entity may also voluntarily register for GST/HST if it makes taxable or zero-rated supplies in Canada, which permits claiming input tax credits for Canadian expenses. Ontario provincial harmonization: GST/HST registration with CRA covers Ontario's 8% PST component under the HST, as Ontario administers no separate VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None equivalent to US check-the-box. Canada does not allow elective entity classification; corporations are separate taxpayers under ITA, while partnerships are transparent. Certain hybrid mismatch elections may be relevant under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 to align deductions with withholding outcomes, but no general check-the-box election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory once exceeded by a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods (property exported by the commercial exporter as part of a commercial export operation) and to certain services supplied to non-residents where the benefit is consumed wholly outside Canada and the service is not incidental to real property situated in Canada. For LegalTech SaaS, zero-rating generally applies when the supply is made to a non-resident and the service is delivered to the recipient's location outside Canada, provided the exporter retains evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and the intellectual property/copyright for resale rules are met. Legal documentation automation services qualify as either zero-rated services supplied to non-residents or, if otherwise contractual, may require analysis of place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 143.2.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CRA/Statistics Canada Canadian Export Reporting System or B13A if Canadian-controlled specified output)
  • Bill of Lading (or other shipping/carriage document evidencing export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (in Canada, accepted as equivalent bank transfer/remittance proof, though CRA typically accepts bank statements and payment confirmations)

No statutory real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate in Canada. GST/HST invoices must contain specified information (vendor BN, date, amount, GST/HST paid or payable) but can be issued in electronic format. CRA does not require API-based fiscal clearance, though taxpayers must retain invoices and electronic records for audit.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess GST/HST if documentary evidence is incomplete or the exported service/goods were not actually exported. Penalties include GST/HST net tax reassessment, arrears interest, failure-to-register penalties, false statements penalties under ETA s. 285, knowing misrepresentation penalties up to 25% of the amount, and potential criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327/328. For transactions involving deliberate false invoicing or sham exports for refund claims, CRA can apply civil penalties up to CAD 25,000 plus 200% of gross negligence penalty, and refer for criminal investigation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the Canadian dollar floats freely with no exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward transfers. Profit repatriation as dividends is unrestricted at the FX level, but a non-resident shareholder is subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, often reduced to 15% or 5% under treaty), and all wire transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC. A solvency test under the Ontario Business Corporations Act applies to dividend declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in CAD or foreign currency in resident accounts; any voluntary conversion is executed through a licensed financial institution, and cross-border transfers above C$10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and cross-border money-laundering administration is exercised through the Department of Finance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services (e.g., Wise, Corpay, Modern Treasury-linked EMIs) cannot access Payments Canada clearing infrastructure and do not hold a deposit-taking license under the Bank Act. Such providers may be used as supplementary cross-border gateways if they hold an authorized money services license and register with FINTRAC, but they cannot replace a Schedule I bank account for CAD settlement, escrow, or regulated FX. Viability rating: Medium as a secondary payout/collection rail; Low as the primary treasury bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections are routed over SWIFT MT103 to a Canadian Schedule I bank using the recipient bank's SWIFT/BIC, institution number, branch transit number, and account number (Canada uses no IBAN). Remittance messages should state purpose as 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Intercompany Loan', and the receiving bank will verify against a board resolution and updated share register. Large-value credits are settled in central bank funds through Payments Canada's Lynx system; amounts of C$10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC. For shareholder loans, a written term note with arm's-length interest and an Ontario corporate record are required to preserve deduction eligibility.Foreign-currency trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary account details; large CAD disbursements clear through Lynx (real-time gross settlement), and small-value transfers may use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR). Commercial letters of credit are not customary for cloud computing/e-signature service payments; instead, procurement is paid via corporate credit card, ACH/EFT, and SWIFT wires. All disbursements to restricted jurisdictions must pass OFAC, EU, and Canadian Autonomous Sanctions screening, and the bank's anti-money-laundering system will monitor for unexpected payout geography.Bank-offered spot and forward outright contracts for CAD/USD, with maturities up to 12 months under an ISDA or Canadian Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or non-convertible emerging-market currencies (settled in CAD equivalent)Cross-currency swaps (CCS) to hedge multi-year intercompany debt and dividend repatriation cash flowsVanilla FX options purchased for downside protection, without speculative writingZero-cost collars combining a purchased FX put and a sold FX call, subject to treasury policy and bank credit approval
