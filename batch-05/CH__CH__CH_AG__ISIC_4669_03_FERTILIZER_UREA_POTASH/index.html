Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions AFC), Berne; online portal at estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax authorities for federal, cantonal and communal direct tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign AG must first obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via registration in the Commercial Register through Zefix if it establishes a branch or registered office, or otherwise by direct UID assignment from ESTV. VAT registration is then effected electronically via ESTV e-Services; the ESTV issues a separate MWST-Nr/VAT number. A non-resident AG without a Swiss address must appoint a domestic fiscal representative/domicile before filing. Registration must be completed prior to the first taxable supply in Switzerland.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss AG is a separate taxpayer subject to federal, cantonal and communal income tax; foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt with progression; not treated as a pass-through entity) — A foreign AG must first obtain a Swiss UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer) via registration in the Commercial Register through Zefix if it establishes a branch or registered office, or otherwise by direct UID assignment from ESTV. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Swiss AG is a separate taxpayer subject to federal, cantonal and communal income tax; foreign permanent establishments and foreign real estate are exempt with progression; not treated as a pass-through entity)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election in Swiss domestic law. Available elections include VAT group treatment for Swiss VAT purposes (Art. 21 VAT Act), participation deduction election for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income, cantonal capital-tax relief elections, and branch VAT-identification election; OECD anti-hybrid rules apply via the Multilateral Instrument and domestic implementing provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual taxable turnover in Switzerland (Art. 10 VAT Act); non-established foreign suppliers are generally required to register for VAT when making non-exempt supplies in Switzerland even if below this threshold, unless reverse charge or export zero-rating applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) goods physically transported from Switzerland to a foreign territory where the export condition is evidenced by customs export documentation; (ii) transport services directly connected to exported goods; and (iii) services for B2B foreign recipients performed outside Switzerland or otherwise used and exploited outside Switzerland under the benefit-abroad rule. For prilled urea and potassium chloride wholesale charter, the exporter must hold valid export proof, commercial documentation, and evidence of physical movement out of Swiss customs territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Swiss Export Declaration via NCTS/customs system)
  • Commercial invoice and export contract with agreed INCOTERMS (e.g., CIF/FOB)
  • Original Bill of Lading (negotiable maritime B/L) or CMR/Air Waybill for alternative transport
  • Proof of export to third-country territory (Ausfuhrbescheinigung / customs export certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign sales proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or digital stamping in Switzerland. Swiss VAT law prescribes statutory invoice content; payment slips referencing Swiss bank accounts must use the Swiss QR-Bill with QR code and creditor reference. eBill and electronic archiving are permitted; tax-relevant invoices must be retained for ten years.

Swiss Federal Tax Administration and customs authorities may apply retrospective scrutiny to export zero-rating. Incomplete or fraudulent proof causes the 0% rate to be reversed, with 8.1% VAT assessed plus default interest from the supply date. Negligent evasion may incur fines of up to three times the tax evaded; intentional evasion or unlawful obtaining of zero-rating/refunds is criminally penalised under Articles 104, 180 and 183 VAT Act, including imprisonment sanctions. Customs false declarations attract separate penalties, and the assessment limitation period for tax evasion is ten years, allowing long look-back clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Swiss franc is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Switzerland observes IMF Article VIII and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements; there are no exchange permits, no quantitative convertibility limits, no prior-authorisation duties for cross-border transfers, and no profit-remittance barriers. A Swiss Aktiengesellschaft may freely distribute dividends, redeem share capital and repatriate proceeds, subject only to the statutory 35% anticipatory tax (Verrechnungssteuer) which is withheld at source and refunded or reduced under Switzerland's comprehensive double-tax-treaty network. The only practical constraints are (i) the withholding mechanism itself, (ii) SNB statistical reporting thresholds for international transactions, and (iii) the post-negative-rate monetary environment: since June 2022 the SNB policy rate has normalised to a positive range (approximately 1.25%-1.75% as of 2025), restoring conventional CHF treasury optimisation. No capital controls, investment-screening restrictions or current-account barriers apply to a Swiss AG engaged in wholesale fertilizer distribution, including with non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation deadline. All proceeds from physical exports of prilled urea and potassium chloride may be retained in USD, EUR or any freely convertible currency, onshore or offshore, without SNB consent or conversion into CHF. The only mandatory layer is statistical: under the National Bank Statistics Ordinance (SR 951.51), resident exporters must report cross-border receipts through their Swiss clearing bank where aggregated flows exceed the prescribed reporting thresholds, and banks must disclose to the SNB any client FX open position above CHF 3 million per counterparty. Swiss banks may, under their own internal credit policy, require provisioning for export receivables outstanding beyond 240-360 days under IFRS 9 expected-credit-loss rules, but this is a prudential accounting rule, not a statutory surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) — Schweizerische Nationalbank / Banque nationale suisse / Banca Nazionale Svizzera, statutory monetary authority under the National Bank Act of 3 October 2003 (NBA; SR 951.11), acting in coordination with the State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial-market policy and with SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) for dual-use and trade export controls. Switzerland maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control administration; the SNB is the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics, foreign-exchange monitoring and prudential FX position reporting of resident banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW (2/5) for using a foreign EU/UK electronic-money institution (EMI) as the primary treasury bank of the Swiss AG; MEDIUM (3/5) as an ancillary payment corridor. A non-Swiss EMI cannot passport its electronic-money licence into Switzerland; serving a Swiss resident company from abroad for deposit-taking or payment-account services requires a FINMA licence, a Swiss branch licence, or a permitted outsourcing arrangement under the Swiss Banking Act and the Financial Market Infrastructure Act (FMIA). A FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech (for example, Sonar or Yapeal-type licensed entities) can offer multi-currency virtual IBANs, but tier-1 Swiss banks remain mandatory for USD clearing, documentary credit issuance, standby letters of credit and commodity trade liquidity. Legal caveat: holding trade proceeds or conducting LC settlement through an unlicensed foreign EMI creates regulatory non-transparency and elevated anti-money-laundering risk under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA); such rails are therefore strictly complementary, never structural.

A FINMA-licensed Swiss fintech (for example, Sonar or Yapeal-type licensed entities) can offer multi-currency virtual IBANs, but tier-1 Swiss banks remain mandatory for USD clearing, documentary credit issuance, standby letters of credit and commodity trade liquidity. Legal caveat: holding trade proceeds or conducting LC settlement through an unlicensed foreign EMI creates regulatory non-transparency and elevated anti-money-laundering risk under the Swiss Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA); such rails are therefore strictly complementary, never structural.
