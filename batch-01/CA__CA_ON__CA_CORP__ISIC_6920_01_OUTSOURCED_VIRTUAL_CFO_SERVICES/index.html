Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax authority and central digital gateway (My Business Account, Business Registration Online) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Ontario Ministry of Finance — provincial counterpart for Ontario corporate income tax and HST, administered by the CRA through the federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

For CA_CORP (a federal or Ontario business corporation): (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act and obtain the incorporation number; (2) obtain the statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 – Request for a Business Number; the nine-digit BN is the universal Canadian tax identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export program accounts; (3) open the GST/HST (RT) program account through the same BRO registration; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters and voluntary below that threshold; (4) foreign (non-resident) corporations carrying on business in Canada register by filing Form RC1 through the non-resident channel, provide a Canadian agent address, and may be required to post security with the CRA under ETA s. 241; non-resident digital platform suppliers of services to Canadian consumers are subject to a separate mandatory simplified GST/HST registration regime (effective 1 July 2021) without the CAD 30,000 threshold; (5) add the corporate income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) accounts to the same BN; all filings are made through CRA My Business Account and ONT-TAX-ONLINE for Ontario.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; corporations are always entity-level taxpayers under ITA s. 2 and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available elections for the virtual CFO corporation include: ITA s. 85 rollover election (tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation), ITA s. 89/83 capital dividend account election, ITA s. 125(6) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) election affecting the small business deduction, ITA s. 150(2) non-calendar fiscal period election, ETA s. 156 election for nil consideration on supplies between closely related registrants, and ETA s. 167 election for joint GST/HST filing; hybrid mismatch neutrality is governed by ITA ss. 18.4, 18.6 and 20.2 (no elective exclusion, and Canadian courts disregard artificial tax-indifferent structures under the general anti-avoidance rule, ITA s. 245).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: total taxable supplies of CAD 30,000 or more over any four consecutive calendar quarters (for non-residents, taxable supplies made in Canada, with associated-group aggregation and special de minimis rules for carrying on business in Canada); exceeding the threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; voluntary registration is available below threshold to recover input tax credits; once registered, the Ontario HST rate of 13% (5% federal GST + 8% provincial component) applies to the corporation's supplies of virtual CFO, bookkeeping, and advisory services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (1) exported goods — 0% GST/HST applies if goods are exported from Canada with documented proof of exportation; (2) exported services — a supply of a service made to a non-resident is zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 where the non-resident is not registered under GST/HST and the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and is not an excluded agency/transport service. Fractional CFO advisory, virtual bookkeeping, and multicurrency ledger services to offshore clients qualify for zero-rating if the supplier retains evidence of the customer's non-resident status (foreign TIN, certificate of residence, or signed declaration) and records demonstrating delivery of the service to the offshore recipient. Zero-rated supplies remain 'taxable supplies', so the corporation fully recovers input tax credits (ITCs) for related expenses and can claim a net refund if ITCs exceed output tax; supplies to non-resident GST/HST registrants, or services in respect of Canadian real or personal property, are taxable at 13% Ontario HST.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (Customs Export Declaration) for goods, filed via the CBSA e-manifest or customs broker; not applicable to pure cross-border services
  • Commercial invoice describing the zero-rated supply and the non-resident purchaser
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or Cargo Control Document (A6/E6) evidencing physical exportation of goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing the foreign-currency payment receipt
  • Certificate of residence, foreign tax identification number, or signed non-resident declaration substantiating that the service recipient is not a GST/HST registrant
  • Engagement letter, virtual CFO service agreement, and multicurrency ledger delivery records linking the services to the offshore recipient

No mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-clearance regime exists in Canada at the federal or Ontario level. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE, GST-ACCESS file transfer, or XML/EDI) for registrants above prescribed revenue thresholds; invoices must be issued and retained for six years in a human-readable and retrievable format. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing standard operates voluntarily for business-to-government and business-to-business exchange; the Ontario government accepts PEPPOL e-invoices. Stripe and Wise payment rails do not impose any additional fiscal clearance obligation beyond normal GST/HST accounting.

CRA subjects export zero-rating claims to substantive audit review; insufficient documentation results in reassessment of the supplies at 13% Ontario HST, denial of related input tax credits, daily compound interest, and recovery of any refunds previously paid, creating a full retrospective clawback. The standard reassessment period is four years, but misrepresentation, fraud, or unrepresented facts remove the time limit and permit unlimited retrospectivity. Civil penalties include ETA s. 280 failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties (5% of the net tax plus 1% per month, up to 12 months) and ETA s. 285 / ITA s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalties (generally the greater of CAD 500 and 50% of the tax attempted to be evaded). Wilful evasion of GST/HST is a criminal offence under ETA s. 239 and ITA s. 327, punishable by fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years; CRA may also assess the corporation's directors and officers for unremitted HST under ETA s. 323, subject to the due-diligence defence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no exchange-control legislation requiring central bank approval for foreign investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. Dividends and management fees from the Ontario-service entity can be remitted freely in any convertible currency at prevailing market rates, subject only to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and to FINTRAC cross-border reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements in Canada. Exporters are not obliged to repatriate foreign currency balances to Canada or to convert them into Canadian dollars. Tax reporting, however, requires that foreign-denominated revenue be recognized in the Canadian-dollar functional currency in accordance with the CRA functional-currency election rules, where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control and AML oversight functions are shared with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Wise (Wise Payments Canada) and Stripe Billing are viable as supplementary treasury rails for receivable collections and low-value disbursements, but they are licensed as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC rather than banks. They cannot maintain statutory escrow balances, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and are not members of CDIC deposit insurance. Feasibility rating: High for cross-border disbursement and online invoicing rails; Low for treasury concentration, escrow management, or regulatory capital holding.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax rate for active business income (15% federal Part I rate after the 10% abatement and 13% general rate reduction, plus 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate); a 12.2% combined preferential rate (9% federal plus 3.2% Ontario) applies to the first C$500,000 of qualifying active business income earned by a Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (section 212) on dividends paid by a resident Canadian corporation to a non-resident individual, corporation, trust, or partnership, unless reduced by a tax treaty; comprehensive Canadian DTAs commonly reduce the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for dividends paid to a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting shares (e.g., Canada-U.S. and Canada-U.K. treaties).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed on or before the day that is six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year; a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through an Ontario permanent establishment files on the same six-month deadline; the balance-due payment is statutorily due 2 months after year-end (3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the SBD), independent of the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required where a taxpayer participates in non-arm's length cross-border transactions whose aggregate fair market value equals or exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year (the Form T106 reporting threshold); a Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$50 million; the Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4647) is mandatory for multinational enterprise groups whose consolidated group revenue was at least C$750 million in the preceding fiscal year; documentation must be prepared before the T2 filing due date and provided to the CRA within 90 days of a written demand, otherwise transfer pricing penalties may apply.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a dedicated Caribbean-type Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the doctrine of substance over form, the beneficial ownership requirement, and the business-purpose test to deny treaty benefits and tax shelters where transactions lack genuine commercial substance; additionally, the Ontario Business Corporations Act requires at least 25% of directors of an Ontario corporation to be resident Canadians (at least one resident director for boards of one or two directors), and central management and control in Canada will determine tax residence, so a purely virtual or passive-structured corporation may be reassessed, reattributed to the place of effective management, or subjected to GAAR penalties where no real economic substance exists in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) of 50% of additional tax assessed; criminal evasion under subsection 239(1) punishable by a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year corporate reassessment period without limitation in cases of misrepresentation or fraud; daily compound interest continues to accrue on the assessed tax; the CRA may also invoke jeopardy assessment and remit collection to the Federal Court.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1 for unremitted third-party source deductions, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholding irrespective of dissolution; where the corporation is a sham, courts will pierce the corporate veil and hold controlling shareholders and directors personally civilly liable; Canadian courts may issue travel bans and passport surrender orders under section 226 of the Income Tax Act where the Minister establishes grounds to believe an individual will leave Canada to avoid collection; directorial participation in evasion exposes the director to criminal fines and imprisonment.
