Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal tax portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Business Registration Online (BRO). Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) administers Ontario regional taxes, with Ontario corporate income tax collected federally under a federal-provincial tax collection agreement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation must first register for a federal Business Number (BN) with the CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the Ontario jurisdiction code, and simultaneously creating program accounts: GST/HST (RT), corporation income tax (RC), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) where applicable. The Ontario corporate income tax account is activated through the CRA under the Ontario collection agreement. If the foreign corporation will carry on business in Ontario, it must also complete extra-provincial registration with the Ontario Business Registry. The 15-digit BN with RT program account serves as the tax identification number and VAT/GST ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-resident business corporation is a separate taxable entity, not a pass-through; it is taxed on worldwide income at Part I corporate rates and allocated business income to Ontario by permanent establishment. Foreign-incorporated entities may become Canadian-resident by central management and control being in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election. Entity status is statutory and common-law based under Income Tax Act section 250 and central-management-and-control rules. Relevant elections include Income Tax Act section 216 (non-resident net-basis election for Canadian rental income), section 217 (non-resident election for certain Canadian-source retirement amounts), and section 93(1) (foreign affiliate election to treat dividends as proceeds of disposition). Hybrid-mismatch rules in sections 18.2 and 18.3 deny deduction relief for certain hybrid interest and dividend mismatches, with no elective opt-out except de minimis exceptions in the Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — a person becomes required to register for GST/HST once total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a four-consecutive-calendar-quarter period (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada through any platform or direct sales must also register at this threshold; a CRO may nevertheless register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on its Canadian costs while charging zero-rated export services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Parts IV and V, Excise Tax Act, clinical trial CRO management services supplied to a non-resident pharmaceutical sponsor are zero-rated when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada and is not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the services are not supplied in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada upon delivery; (iii) for professional, advisory or research-type services performed in Canada, the non-resident recipient receives the benefit outside Canada and is not in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (iv) the CRO maintains contemporaneous commercial documentation evidencing the offshore supply and foreign payment. Tangible goods, clinical specimens or samples exported as part of the CRO engagement qualify for zero-rating only if they are delivered to the non-resident recipient and exported within a reasonable time, supported by customs documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/B13B) for physical goods, specimens, or samples actually exported; not applicable for pure services
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or courier shipment receipt proving export movement and delivery outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate, wire-confirmation, or bank credit advice evidencing payment received from the non-resident sponsor
  • Signed CRO Master Services Agreement / Statement of Work identifying the non-resident sponsor, scope, and site-work in Canada
  • Non-resident status declaration of the sponsor (e.g., IRS Form W-8BEN-E for US sponsors, or equivalent tax-residency certificate) and confirmation that the sponsor is not GST/HST-registered

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing stamping, or continuous transaction control regime exists in Canada or Ontario. GST/HST registrants may issue electronic invoices in any format provided they contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and Regulations. Records supporting tax returns must be kept in Canada or be accessible electronically from Canada. Filing is made through CRA GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or electronic data interchange/API; no pre-clearance or CRA transaction stamping is required.

If zero-rating is claimed without adequate export substantiation, the CRA will treat the supply as subject to HST, assess the 13% Ontario HST on the full consideration, and add interest from the due date. The normal reassessment window is four years, but in cases of fraud, neglect, or misrepresentation the CRA may reassess without any limitation period. Penalties include the Excise Tax Act section 280 false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated tax, and criminal prosecution under section 285. For wilful evasion, fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment are possible, in addition to director-liability exposure under Excise Tax Act section 323 for unremitted HST where the CRO is insolvent. CRA audit practice requires complete commercial, banking, and logistics documentation before allowing export zero-rating; retrospective clawbacks and gross-negligence penalties are common where foreign-payment trails are missing or invoices misstate the place of performance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible floating currency. Canada maintains an open capital account with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Dividends, royalties, interest, and management fees may be remitted offshore subject to domestic corporate tax and Part XIII withholding tax obligations, but no foreign exchange quota, repatriation license, or prior central bank approval exists for profit remittance. Protections under the USMCA and OECD capital liberalization norms preclude discriminatory FX measures. The principal non-tariff barrier for non-resident-controlled entities is foreign-investment-sectoral screening under the Investment Canada Act, not currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory surrender, forced conversion, or prescribed repatriation deadlines on export receipts. Exporters and service suppliers, including clinical trial CROs, may retain export proceeds in any foreign currency, maintain offshore collection accounts, and remit or convert funds as dictated by internal liquidity policy, subject only to ordinary Canadian tax compliance on worldwide income and transfer pricing rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing regulation by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign investment review under the Investment Canada Act administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the European 'electronic money institution' (EMI) category. Unregulated or foreign-supervised EMIs may operate in Canada only under payment service provider restrictions and cannot hold clinical trial funds in statutory escrow. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Canadian operating company fully or partly owned by a non-resident parent, inbound equity funding must be routed as a one-off SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire. The remitter must cure the beneficiary CAD (or USD) clearing account with a purpose code such as 'Direct investment – equity injection' under BPM6 categories, and the Canadian bank will require a board capital-call resolution, share subscription agreement, a certificate of common share value, an updated corporate registry, and evidence of receipt under the Investment Canada Act (mandatory notification if post-acquisition asset value exceeds the thresholds). The funds may be credited to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account pending issuance of the share certificate, or directly to Operating Account if a certified shareholder resolution so authorizes. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (15% federal Part I general rate + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate; 2024 rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Income Tax Act s. 212(2)), imposed under Part XIII on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return due 6 months after fiscal year-end; balance due 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for a qualified CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction); instalments due on the last day of each quarter; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Ontario through a permanent establishment (PE) file the same T2, with Ontario allocation per Schedule 5

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due 6 months after fiscal year-end, identical to the T2 deadline

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 12 months after the ultimate parent's fiscal year-end; filing obligation arises where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least C$750,000,000

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Master File required under ITA s. 247(4) and CRA Information Circular 87-2R when aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions reach C$2,000,000 in a fiscal year (threshold increased from C$1,000,000 for fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2022); Country-by-Country Report obligation at C$750,000,000 group revenue

Economic substance scope: No stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations exist in Canada, but CRA enforces equivalent principles through the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the common law sham and substance-over-form doctrines, treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions, and the foreign accrual property income rules. A CRO must demonstrate genuine Ontario substance: employees with real decision-making authority, premises, management of clinical trial contracts and risk, and intellectual property development conducted in Canada. Ontario's Business Corporations Act beneficial-ownership transparency registry (operative in 2025) imposes significant reporting obligations on Ontario-inc

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine up to C$25,000 and/or 12 months

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or direct evasion face criminal prosecution, restitution orders, travel bans, and civil liability under s. 227.1 for any unremitted withholdings
Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under s. 163(2): greater of C$100 and 50% of the understated federal tax; Ontario penalty amounts are assessed alongside the federal penalty; management misrepresentation penalty under s. 163.2: minimum C$1,000, maximum the greater of C$100,000 and the gross compensation received

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who signed or acquiesced in false returns may be personally assessed under s. 163.2 and may face director disqualification under corporate and securities law
Unremitted Non-Resident Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer remains fully liable for the withheld amount plus daily compounding interest and penalties under s. 215; CRA can waive penalties only where the non-resident is demonstrably entitled to treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for the entire unremitted Part XIII amount; CRA enforcement may include direct garnishment, seizure of personal assets, and restrictions on leaving Canada
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty on any net transfer-pricing adjustment exceeding C$10,000 under s. 247(3); upward adjustment to taxable income with interest; loss of documentation protection; T106 late penalties as described above

Director personal liability: Directors who approved intercompany pricing without economic analysis may be assessed for negligence under s. 163.2; courts may pierce the veil where the Canadian entity is a mere conduit of a foreign parent
Shell Arrangement, GAAR and AML

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization of the transaction with a 25% penalty (s. 245); sham doctrine voids the arrangement retroactively; PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 per violation for corporations; imprisonment up to 10 years for money-laundering facilitation

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil when the corporation is a facade or agent of the controller; directors face personal liability for treaty-abuse assessments, FINTRAC penalties, and criminal liability for authorizing unreported cross-border fund flows
