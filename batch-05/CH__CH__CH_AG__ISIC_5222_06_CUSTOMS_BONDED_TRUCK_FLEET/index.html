Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt / Office du registre du commerce) competent for the seat canton, operating under the central eGRIS/Zefix platform and the federal supervision of the Federal Commercial Register Law Office (EHRA) of the Federal Office of Justice.

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (SR 220) of 30 March 1911, Articles 620-763, in particular Articles 626-632 (founders and incorporation), Article 629 (minimum capital), Article 630 (paid-up capital), Articles 634-640 (articles of incorporation and reports), Article 716 (board of directors) and Article 718 (power to bind the company); supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (SR 221.411) and the revised Swiss company law effective 1 January 2023.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (Errichtungsakt) together with the Articles of Association (Statuten), plus the electronic registration application to the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) and certified specimen signature cards.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 38 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

An Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum share capital of CHF 100,000, with no nominal share lower than CHF 0.01. At incorporation at least 20% of the nominal capital, but not less than CHF 50,000, must be paid-up in cash and irrevocably placed at the disposal of the company in a notary-controlled blocked account (Sperrkonto); release occurs only upon successful commercial register entry. Contributions in kind (e.g., the bonded truck fleet) require a statutory audit report confirming the asset valuation; no bearer shares are permissible and founders must declare all non-cash contributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification and structural-load inspection for the fleet depot premises issued by the cantonal fire brigade and building inspectorate as a condition of the building permit; occupational-safety clearance under Article 82 of the Swiss Federal Accident Insurance Act (UVG) with SUVA and EKAS; workplace risk assessment covering forklift and maintenance-bay safety, ergonomic lifting and driver-fatigue management; mandatory tachograph and driver-hours audits by the Federal Roads Office (ASTRA) and cantonal police; civil-defence parking-space liability confirmed where the depot exceeds the statutory threshold.

Paid-in capital rule: An Aktiengesellschaft requires minimum share capital of CHF 100,000, with no nominal share lower than CHF 0.01. At incorporation at least 20% of the nominal capital, but not less than CHF 50,000, must be paid-up in cash and irrevocably placed at the disposal of the company in a notary-controlled blocked account (Sperrkonto); release occurs only upon successful commercial register entry. Contributions in kind (e.g., the bonded truck fleet) require a statutory audit report confirming the asset valuation; no bearer shares are permissible and founders must declare all non-cash contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / ESTV), Bern — official portal: www.estv.admin.ch

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Swiss Aktiengesellschaft (AG) must first complete commercial registry registration at the cantonal commercial register office. The cantonal tax authority (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung) automatically assigns the corporate UID (Unternehmens-Identifikationsnummer / IDI) which serves as the VAT ID and tax reference number. For non-resident entities without a Swiss establishment, VAT registration is required under Art. 14 Swiss VAT Law (MWSTG) by submitting Form 32 to the FTA, with a written or online application via the Swiss VAT portal (E-Service VAT). A non-resident company may appoint a Swiss fiscal representative or use the electronic VAT settlement procedure if turnover exceeds the registration threshold or supplies taxable services in Switzerland. No statutory tax identification card is issued; the UID is published in the Swiss UID register.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A Swiss AG is subject to federal corporate income tax (effective flat 8.5% on net profit after tax surcharges), cantonal and communal profit taxes (combined effective rates typically 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton; subject to tax reform rules), and Swiss net wealth tax for the capital stock. The AG is a tax-resident entity if registered office and effective management are in Switzerland. Territorial scope: Swiss taxes worldwide income for resident AGs, but dividends from qualifying participations are exempt under the 10/30 participation relief rule. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Swiss AG is subject to federal corporate income tax (effective flat 8.5% on net profit after tax surcharges), cantonal and communal profit taxes (combined effective rates typically 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton; subject to tax reform rules), and Swiss net wealth tax for the capital stock. The AG is a tax-resident entity if registered office and effective management are in Switzerland. Territorial scope: Swiss taxes worldwide income for resident AGs, but dividends from qualifying participations are exempt under the 10/30 participation relief rule. No pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

Switzerland does not operate a check-the-box election for tax classification. The AG is inherently a corporately taxed legal entity. Certain statutory elections are available: (1) election to form a group for VAT purposes (VAT group registration under Art. 21 MWSTG) enabling consolidated VAT treatment of affiliated entities; (2) election for reduced VAT taxation on specific supplies; (3) election to apply the Swiss domestic margin scheme for used goods and works of art; (4) in cantonal profit tax, an election may be made for the qualifying participation exemption or notional interest deduction on equity capital (NID) under limited conditions; (5) for federal profit tax, a forward-loss carry-forward election is mandatory. Hybrid mismatch elections under Swiss law are limited to explicit anmeldung (notification) of a selected classification for Swiss tax purposes if incompatible with a foreign classification; no generic check-the-box is permitted.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 annual worldwide taxable turnover from supplies (excluding exempt supplies, but including supplies deemed to be in Switzerland; separate threshold of CHF 100,000 for certain franchises). Non-resident suppliers without establishment in Switzerland are not subject to the threshold and are required to register if performing taxable supplies in Switzerland except where the supply is subject to reverse-charge/deemed-taxed rules. A separate mandatory registration threshold of CHF 10,000 for goods deliveries on consignment in the customs warehouse sector applies if the business is not domiciled in Switzerland.

Switzerland does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. E-invoicing is permitted and encouraged under general VAT compliance rules. An e-invoice must contain all mandatory data under Art. 26 MWSTG and be integrity-ensured (e.g., EDI-HAN, XML with qualified electronic signature, or Swiss QR-Rechnung). The Swiss Peer-to-Peer Electronic Invoicing Framework (eBill) is widely used but not legally mandatory. From 1 January 2026, e-billing (Peppol BIS 3.0) will be mandatory for all federal government suppliers, but no national B2B mandate exists. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; no exchange control approval is required for cross-border payments. Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted, but Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35% applies to dividend distributions, with potential relief under double taxation treaties or via refund procedures. For an AG, distributable reserves must be certified and statutory capital preservation rules observed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Switzerland has no forced surrender or conversion of export receipts; proceeds can be held, sold forward, or used in any currency. Enterprises must comply with SNB statistical reporting if their cross-border assets or liabilities exceed reporting thresholds, and SECO economic sanctions require transaction approval for listed persons or countries.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) – Schweizerische Nationalbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Swiss-regulated fintech banks and payment institutions (FINMA licensure, e.g., the 'Fintech-license' per Art. 1b Banking Act) are viable for low-risk logistics payments. Offshore digital EMIs without a Swiss banking or fintech license cannot legally accept Swiss deposits or provide local IBANs; they may be used only as ancillary transaction rails through a licensed Swiss correspondent.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory corporate income tax 8.5% (effective 7.83% after deductibility of the federal tax itself); combined federal, cantonal and communal effective tax burden in Zug approx. 11.9% for a profitable operating AG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss Anticipatory Tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on any dividend distribution by the AG; statutory rate cannot be reduced where the recipient is a Swiss resident company. As of the 2022/2023 reform (OASI reform), a reduced rate of 25% applies to dividends distributed by companies with substantial participations in other Swiss companies when the recipient holds 10% or more for 1 year, but generally the 35% rate applies unless treaty relief is granted.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the end of the business year, i.e. by 30 June for a 31 December year-end (Zug Cantonal Tax Act Art. 53). An extension of up to 6 months is available upon reasoned request; the federal direct tax return follows the same deadline as the cantonal return (VStG/DBG Art. 124).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the Swiss AG's aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (goods, including fuel, spare parts and fleet services, as well as royalties and interest) exceed CHF 10 million in a fiscal year; also required where related-party domestic transactions exceed CHF 10 million. Master File: required when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds CHF 2 billion (OECD BEPS standard). A simplified transactional file (simpler documentation) applies to lower-risk service companies; however, the customs-bonded trucking company is treated as a full-risk operating entity and must file the complete documentation upon request within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland has no Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the BVI/Cayman sense; however, under Federal Direct Tax Act Art. 50 (place of effective management) and FTA administrative practice, an AG claiming treaty benefits and holding the customs-bonded transport licence must prove real economic substance at its registered office: own or rented operational premises in Switzerland, full-time personnel (at least one qualified manager with Swiss residence), the board meeting in and effective management from Switzerland, bookkeeping in Switzerland and valid customs-broker licence (Art. 22 à 25 of the Swiss Customs Ordinance; MOFA/T-Pe operator approval). The absence of substance lets the tax authority either (i) disregard the Swiss entity as a sham and allocate the trucking income to the foreign shareholder's permanent establishment, or (ii) refuse the WHT treaty reduction under the anti-abuse rule. For ISIC 5222, the Swiss customs agency also requires a physical depot, weighbridge access, and a GPS-sealed fleet register; failure to maintain these triggers withdrawal of the customs licence and immediate tax reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal tax evasion (direct taxes)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine of up to the amount of tax evaded (DBG Art. 175); if false invoices or forged documents are used, the qualification is 'criminal tax fraud' (DBG Art. 186) with imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine up to CHF 500,000; full back-taxes plus default interest of 5% p.a.

Director personal liability: The CEO and each board member are criminally liable as 'authors of the tax offense' (unlawful instructions do not protect directors); the court may impose a travel ban and asset freeze (Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 237); the tax authority may pierce the corporate veil and order civil restitution against the directors for the unpaid tax where the company is stripped of assets (DBG Art. 12a in respect of withholding tax responsibility).
Customs and excise fraud (bonded regime)

Statutory sanction: Customs penalty up to 3 times the evaded import duties (Customs Act Art. 118-120); confiscation of the vehicle fleet and the cargo; withdrawal of the bonded warehouse/trucking licence with no re-grant for 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or negligent supervision are subject to personal fines up to CHF 100,000 and disqualification from customs representation; the customs authority may also arrest the responsible director pending extradition if the cargo crosses borders (travel ban under Art. 237 CCP).
Shell company / mailbox-risk (no economic substance)

Statutory sanction: Income re-allocation to the place of effective management abroad; denial of the Swiss DTA relief and of the 0%/15% dividend WHT; capital tax assessment on a fictive equity basis; for the parent, a retroactive withholding tax demand of 35% on the deemed distribution.

Director personal liability: The local nominee director (often resident in Zug) is criminally sanctioned for 'employment of a sham domicile' under the Code of Obligations Art. 62i (non-disclosure of the beneficial owner) and the cantonal tax law; fines up to CHF 20,000, personal restitution obligation for taxes lost, and immediate removal from the Commercial Register.
AML and financial intermediation (client funds in the bonded supply chain)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to CHF 500,000 under the Financial Services Act; for the entity, disqualification from banking relationships; funds are frozen; the Swiss Money Laundering Reporting Office (MROS) report leads to a penal inquiry.

Director personal liability: Board members of the AG incur personal fines and imprisonment up to 3 years (AML Act Art. 9, SFTA), forfeiture of the frozen assets, and a personal travel ban if they reside abroad; the director's personal assets are attached for the restitution of the laundered value.
VAT and social security evasion

Statutory sanction: Value-added tax amendment of up to 10 years (if fraud) plus a fine of up to 3 times the evaded VAT; social security retro-assessment of unpaid AHV (no statute of limitation) plus contributions and default interest.

Director personal liability: Under Swiss VAT Act Art. 12 and AHV Act Art. 52, directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid VAT/social contributions to the degree of their fault if the company becomes insolvent; the social security authority may secure a civil attachment against the directors' personal bank accounts and impose a prohibition on leaving Switzerland until the debt is settled.
