Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both capital and current accounts. There are no exchange controls under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act restricting cross-border capital flows. Profit remittance by a Quebec business corporation is permitted without prior central bank approval; however, dividends, management fees, and intercompany payments must comply with transfer pricing rules under section 247 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec Revenue Agency reporting obligations. Payments to non-residents may be subject to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, but no foreign exchange conversion certificate or approval is required. Foreign direct investment into Canada is reviewed only for net-benefit under the Investment Canada Act for enterprise value thresholds; an EOR staffing agency below the threshold is not subject to review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Canada has no mandatory repatriation or forced conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign currency accounts at Canadian financial institutions without reporting conversion. For goods exported, export declarations are filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for statistical purposes; for services related to an EOR workforce, no foreign exchange conversion or surrender timeline applies. Billing in foreign currency is permitted, and the exporter may settle, hedge, or retain proceeds freely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and, for enforcement administration, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the Foreign Exchange Administration Function resides with the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada for monetary policy

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for core treasury dependency. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity injections from non-resident investors must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from an originating foreign bank to the Canadian bank's correspondent account with a detailed payment reference 'Contribution to capital under the Canada Business Corporations Act / Quebec Business Corporations Act' and the Canadian entity name. For cross-border equity capital of CAD 50,000 or more, the beneficial ownership and source-of-funds information must accompany the wire for FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting. Investment type must be coded for statistical purposes as 'O-Other Investment' or 'Direct Investment – Equity' under the Statistics Canada Balance of Payments reporting framework. All conversions to CAD may be executed at the receiving bank's spot rate; a Canadian withholding tax certificate may be required only for later distributions, not for the inward injection. The bank will also verify that the lender or shareholder is not a 'restricted entity' under the Investment Canada Act where the investment involves a cultural business; an EOR staffing agency is non-cultural and no net-benefit filing is required below the prescribed threshold.Approved clearing rails include SWIFT MT103 for operational payments (supplier invoices, intercompany settlements, dividend distributions), SWIFT MT202 for correspondent-based transfers, Commercial LC (letters of credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank with MT700/MT710 messages) for international trade settlements, and domestic payments via Payments Canada ACSS (G3/G4 clearing files for payroll direct credits). For international contractor payroll, bulk cross-border payments are best executed through a Schedule I bank's international payment gateway using FX-Convert-and-Pay with same-day settlement to ISO 20022-compatible banks. EOR disbursements to contractors located outside Canada should be batch-processed under a standing instruction to the bank's foreign exchange desk, with each beneficiary receiving a separate MT103 containing the payroll reference and the employment contract identifier. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal statutory headline rate 15% general (after 13% General Rate Reduction) + Quebec 11.5% general = 26.5% combined general corporate income tax; Benefiting CCPCs: federal 9% + Quebec 3.2% = 12.2% combined on the first C$500K of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA) on dividends paid or deemed paid by a Canadian (Quebec) resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada; Quebec does not impose an additional separate dividend WHT because the federal Part XIII tax is levied uniformly and the benefit of the federal-provincial collection agreement applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after each fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 fiscal year-end is due June 30 of the following calendar year; if the due date falls on a weekend or statutory holiday, the filing deadline shifts to the next business day under CRA administrative practice). Payment deadline differs: balances are due 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for qualifying CCPCs), and instalments apply when net tax above C$3,000

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 stub = June 30 due date; Revenu Québec applies the same 'next business day' practice as CRA only where expressly published). Quebec balance-due day: within 2 months of fiscal year-end (3 months for qualifying CCPC), with instalment rules coinciding with federal instalments

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 — Form T106 (Information Return on Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed for a corporation's reportable non-arm's length transactions with non-resident related persons where the aggregate fair market value of specified transactions exceeds C$1,000,000 in the taxation year; a Group EOR platform must also file: Form RC4646 Country-by-Country Report where consolidated group revenue ≥ C$750,000,000; Quebec equivalent cross-border documentation regime under the Taxation Act (Revenu Québec TP documentation and CO-1066 filing threshold aligns with the federal C$1M aggregation methodology); contemporaneous TP documentation is expected for all reportable transactions and a full Local File is prudent for all intra-group services above C$100,000; whatever the threshold, CRA and Revenu Québec will disallow deductions on non-arm's length cross-border amounts whose pricing and documentation fail the s. 247(2) 'arm's length principle' — and a 10% TP gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 247(3) applies to adjustment amounts.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not implement the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however the substance-over-form principle is enforced through: (1) GAAR at ITA s. 245 (federal) and s. 1079.3 of the Quebec Taxation Act — an avoidance transaction lacking economic substance can be redrawn; (2) common-law corporate residency tests (central management and control), which determine whether the EOR's 'Canadian' entity truly manages operations in Canada; (3) treaty bilateral anti-abuse provisions (beneficial ownership, Limitation-on-Benefits where present and principal-purpose test per OECD Multilateral Instrument, which Canada has signed); and (4) CRA's criminal-level indicator analysis for 'shell payroll' arrangements. Accordingly the EOR's Canadian entity must maintain real commercial substance: own premises, Canadian-resident directors/officers, local payroll infrastructure, employment contracts and accounting records, or the entire arrangement is re-characterized as a PE or a disregarded conduit, exposing Part XIII WHT at 25% plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Payroll Trust Deviation / Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Penalty under ITA s. 227(8)-(9): 10% penalty on the unremitted amount plus daily compound interest at the prescribed rate; second/subsequent failures within a relevant period attract 20%; CRA can issue a s. 227(16) direction freezing bank accounts, garnish receivables, and revoke the payroll 'RT' account; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under s. 239 ITA carries fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; the equivalent Quebec regime (Taxation Act and Act respecting the ministère du Revenu) adds provincial penalties of 12.5% to 25% plus surcharges for repeated misconduct.

Director personal liability: Director liability is automatic and severe: ITA s. 227.1 renders every director who was in office jointly and severally liable for the entire unremitted withholdings, penalties and interest regardless of fault or mental state; the Crown can assess directors personally, file judgments, seize personal assets, garnish director remuneration, and require travel clearance? on serious matters — no transactional travel clearance under CRA, but criminal arrest warrants effectively impose travel bans where evasion charges are laid; personal insolvency proceedings do not discharge this statutory trust debt.
Worker Misclassification / Payroll Documentation Fraud (T4/RL-1/NR4 Manipulation)

Statutory sanction: CRA determines an employer-employee relationship retroactively and assesses the full payroll deductions, employer penalties (10% of the unreported amounts under ITA s. 227(10)), worker-compensation back assessments by CNESST, unpaid vacation/social-benefit liabilities under the Quebec Act respecting labour standards, and late-filing penalties under ITA s. 162(6) (C$25/day per T4/NR4 slip up to C$2,500, C$100/day per slip where more than 100 slips are late, with a 4-year limitation window for reassessment).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the retroactively-assessed unremitted source deductions under ITA s. 227.1 and the Quebec equivalent, including interest and penalties; where the false classification was knowing (fraud were committed), directors face criminal personal prosecution under s. 239 ITA — imprisonment up to 5 years, criminal fines, civil restitution orders, and travel restrictions via criminal court undertakings.
