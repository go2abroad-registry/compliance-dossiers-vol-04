Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the OBCA; a charitable endowment corporation may file a nominal authorized share capital (e.g., CAD 1) and no escrow account deposit is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — my Business Account portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or Canadian-incorporated corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA via online registration, fax, or mail. A registered charity must additionally file Form T2050 (Application to Register a Charity for Income Tax Purposes) with the Charities Directorate; registration is retroactively effective on the date of application and confers income-tax exemption under paragraph 149(1)(f) of the Income Tax Act. GST/HST registration is effected separately by indicating same on Form RC1 or via the Business Registration Online portal; the BN is also used for payroll, corporate income tax and import/export accounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — non-resident owned or Canadian-controlled private corporation taxed at Part I rates (combined federal 15% + Ontario provincial rates); upon charitable registration, income-tax exempt under ITA s. 149(1)(f), but must file Form T3010 (Registered Charity Information Return) annually and maintain books and records in Canada. — A foreign entity or Canadian-incorporated corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA via online registration, fax, or mail. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — non-resident owned or Canadian-controlled private corporation taxed at Part I rates (combined federal 15% + Ontario provincial rates); upon charitable registration, income-tax exempt under ITA s. 149(1)(f), but must file Form T3010 (Registered Charity Information Return) annually and maintain books and records in Canada.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; optional elections include: (i) election under ETA s. 156 to treat closely related corporations as a single person for GST/HST; (ii) charity/qualified donee can elect with a co-jurisdiction entity for consolidated GST/HST reporting; (iii) deemed Canadian-resident trust election for foreign charitable trusts is not available — hybrid mismatch rules apply via BEAT-like Anti-hybrid Arrangement Rules (sections 18.2/18.3 ITA) to deny double deduction or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(4); once exceeded in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, mandatory GST/HST registration is required; charities selling taxable goods/services must also monitor this threshold (CAS 30,000)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods exported from Canada under ETA Schedule VI Part V (ss. 5-6); for a charitable foundation with endowment management scope, qualifying supplies are primarily 'financial services' (generally exempt, not zero-rated) — therefore any advisory/management services rendered to non-residents must satisfy the ETA s. 9 'substantially all consideration attributable to services performed outside Canada' test, or be zero-rated export services, to benefit from O% GST/HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via CBSA CAED / EDI (CCS) to Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Commercial Invoice or pro forma invoice
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/truck waybill) evidencing Canadian port of loading
  • Proof of export acceptance (carrier delivery report or foreign customs entry documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire record evidencing payment from non-resident counterparty
  • Executed contract and delivery terms (Incoterms) demonstrating risk and title transfer outside Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance/e-invoicing in Canada; CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if they are complete, legible, and in a durable format; voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard is available (Peppol/CIUS) for procurement, and ON Government requires e-invoicing for supplier registration in certain B2G contracts; PDF/EDI updates to CRA via GST/HST NETFILE and CRA My Business Account are required for periodic returns (no continuous 'digital stamping' regime).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a freely convertible, floating-currency regime without central-bank prior approval for current or capital account transactions. Canada's IMF Article VIII status means payments and transfers for current international transactions are not restricted. Dividend, interest, royalty and profit remittances are not FX-controlled but may attract Part XIII withholding tax; for a charitable foundation, the relevant remittance controls are the CRA registered-charity disbursement quota and the requirement that payments not create a private benefit. Cross-border wires are screened for UN/Canadian sanctions and reported to FINTRAC when at or above C$10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None under Canadian law. There is no statutory export proceeds surrender, no prescribed repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio to Canadian dollars. Exporters or recipients may retain foreign currency; the CRA and FINTRAC treat the Canadian-dollar value of foreign-denominated receipts as the basis for reporting and compliance, and any foreign currency account held by a foundation must be consolidated in CAD for T3010 financial information.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada/Banque du Canada; bank prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. A digital EMI cannot operate as a deposit-taking bank in Canada unless authorised under the Bank Act, and an offshore EMI's Canadian exposure would generally require registration as a money services business (FINTRAC) and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act, as a payment service provider with Bank of Canada. Balances held with an EMI are not CDIC-insured and may expose the foundation to unregulated counterparty risk. Use EMIs only as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, not as the primary treasury or capital-escrow account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Notarized Articles of Incorporation or Letters Patent; Ontario Business Registry record; CRA registration application and acknowledgement (T2050) if applicable; foundation bylaws; board resolution appointing signatories; charity purpose statement and constitutional objects.
No individual beneficial owner can be identified because the entity is a private foundation controlled by a board.

Root cause: No individual beneficial owner can be identified because the entity is a private foundation controlled by a board.

Defense evidence: Certificate of incumbency; register of directors and officers; board resolution confirming board control; UBO declaration signed by each director; background screening; sanctions/PEP questionnaires; explanation that foundation assets are held for charitable purposes and not for any private individual.
A foreign donor or grantor sends a significant endowment from an offshore jurisdiction with enhanced AML risk.

Root cause: A foreign donor or grantor sends a significant endowment from an offshore jurisdiction with enhanced AML risk.

Defense evidence: Donation agreement or trust/foundation deed; source-of-funds evidence (audited financials, bank reference, tax filing/statutory declaration, corporate registration and UBO chart); humanitarian or philanthropic rationale; adverse-media/sanctions screening; confirm donor is not a terrorist-listed person or PEP without approval; identify intermediary bank and purpose language.
Cross-border transactions involve third-party payees or unusual grant payment patterns inconsistent with the foundation's objects.

Root cause: Cross-border transactions involve third-party payees or unusual grant payment patterns inconsistent with the foundation's objects.

Defense evidence: Board-approved grant policy; executed grant agreements with each donee; CRA qualified-donee status or equivalency determination for foreign grantees; project due-diligence file incorporating budget, verification report and intended use; audited financials/procurement invoices; FINTRAC large EFT report record.
Rapid in-and-out movements of endowment capital or significant currency conversion with no clear commercial purpose.

Root cause: Rapid in-and-out movements of endowment capital or significant currency conversion with no clear commercial purpose.

Defense evidence: Formal investment and liquidity policy; treasury and hedging mandate signed by the board; capital preservation objectives; project cash-flow forecast; contemporaneous records of swaps/forward contracts and their underlying exposures; accounting entries in the general ledger for CRA audit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is an incoming SWIFT MT103 credit to the foundation's Canadian Schedule I bank account. The sending bank must use the beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC, beneficiary legal name and segregated account number, and purpose field 'Charitable Endowment Contribution / Donation'. The bank will verify the foundation's KYC profile, screen for sanctions and file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC if the transfer is C$10,000 or more. No Bank of Canada exchange-control authorisation is required. Foreign-currency wire proceeds can be held in a multi-currency operating account or exchanged to CAD by the receiving bank's spot rate, then settled into the Capital Escrow/Operational Account.For the foundation, 'trade' clearing refers to grant disbursements and arm's-length procurement payments. Use SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border CAD/USD/EUR payments, with SWIFT gpi end-to-end tracking and beneficiary account validation. Domestic CAD grants should clear through Payments Canada automated settlement; for goods/services, a Commercial Letter of Credit may be opened only where a genuine import contract exists and the bank receives the underlying invoice/documentary evidence. All transfers require a purpose code and contract/grant reference in field 70, and FINTRAC reporting is triggered at C$10,000 or above. The foundation must not send unbacked wire transfers or third-party-authorized disbursements.Spot FX contracts for immediate funding of grants or investment flows.Outright FX forwards, generally with tenors up to 12 months, matched to a committed foreign-currency grant or investment exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where deliverable CAD conversion is impractical, strictly hedge-only.Cross-currency swaps or long-dated FX swaps when the foundation holds a corresponding foreign-currency asset/liability and board approves the hedge policy.
