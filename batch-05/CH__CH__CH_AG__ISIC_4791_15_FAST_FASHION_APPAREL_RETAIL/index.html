Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cantonal Commercial Register Office (Handelsregisteramt) of the canton of the registered office (e.g., Handelsregisteramt des Kantons Zürich); supervisory and appellate authority: Swiss Federal Commercial Register Authority (Eidgenössisches Handelsregisteramt EHRA) in Bern, within the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP).

Governing statute

Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht, SR 220), Arts. 620-763 (provisions on the stock corporation, Aktiengesellschaft). Key formation sections: Arts. 625-638 (incorporation procedure, reports, and payment of capital), Art. 640 (application for registration), Art. 643 (content of the registration application), Art. 716a (non-transferable board duties), Art. 698 (powers of the general meeting). The Ordinance on the Commercial Register (Handelsregisterverordnung, SR 221.411), Arts. 1-94, governs filing mechanics.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Commercial Register Entry (Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister, Form A), submitted together with the notarized Deed of Incorporation (Errichtungsurkunde) and the Articles of Association (Statuten), authenticated by a Swiss notary public.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000 (OR Art. 621); at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash before registration and must be at the board's free disposal (OR Art. 632), evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) from a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank. Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) require a notarized contribution report (Sacheinlagebericht, OR Art. 628). No separate state escrow account is required; the deposit is held in a corporate formation account blocked until registration and released automatically upon entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required from the cantonal fire prevention authority (e.g., Schutz & Rettung Zürich or the cantonal insurance company Gebäudeversicherung) for commercial and warehouse use, including escape-route planning and fire extinguisher installation. A structural load inspection (Statiknachweis) certified by an engineer is required for storage racks exceeding 1.8 m in height. Occupational health and safety clearance must be obtained from the Cantonal Labour Inspectorate (Arbeitsinspektorat) under the Swiss Labour Act (ArG) and Ordinance 3 (ArGV 3), covering lighting, ventilation, rest areas, and ergonomic workstations. Accident prevention certification under EKAS/SUVA is needed for warehousing equipment; if working hours exceed the statutory thresholds of ArG Art. 9, a special work-time permit from the canton is required.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital is CHF 100,000 (OR Art. 621); at least CHF 50,000 must be paid up in cash before registration and must be at the board's free disposal (OR Art. 632), evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) from a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank. Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) require a notarized contribution report (Sacheinlagebericht, OR Art. 628). No separate state escrow account is required; the deposit is held in a corporate formation account blocked until registration and released automatically upon entry.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Cantonal Office for Economic Affairs and Labour (Amt für Wirtschaft und Arbeit, AWA) and the municipal business licensing office (Gewerbepolizei). For ISIC 4791 (electronic retailing of fast-fashion apparel), no industrial manufacturing or sectoral retail license is required; only a municipal business notification (Betriebsanmeldung) and zoning clearance for office and warehousing premises are mandatory.

Category: Not applicable - no sector-specific operating license is prescribed for e-commerce apparel retail under Swiss federal law. A municipal business notification (Betriebsanmeldung) and a building/zoning conformity clearance (Bewilligung) for the registered office and warehouse address must nonetheless be obtained from the respective commune.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV/FTA), Eigerstrasse 65, CH-3003 Bern; online portal estv.admin.ch with eServices/SecurePortal for VAT registration and filings; UID register operated by the Federal Statistical Office at uid.admin.ch; company data accessible via Zefix (zefix.ch). Cantonal income tax is administered by the cantonal tax administration for the seat canton (e.g., Zug: Dienststelle Steuern, zg.ch).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarially execute articles of incorporation and register the Aktiengesellschaft (AG) in the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregister); the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce publishes the entry. Step 2: The Federal Statistical Office automatically issues the Swiss Business Identification Number (UID, format CHE-123.456.789) upon commercial registration; no separate application is required. Step 3: Apply to the FTA for VAT registration via Form 716/eServices within 30 days after the VAT liability arises (worldwide turnover above CHF 100,000 or voluntary registration); the VAT number is the UID suffixed with MWST (CHE-123.456.789 MWST), being the statutory Swiss tax ID. Step 4: The FTA and cantonal tax administration open the corporate income tax file automatically from the commercial register notification; file annual federal and cantonal tax returns. Non-resident sellers without a commercial register entry must obtain a UID via the UID-A application to the competent cantonal administration and register for Swiss VAT through the FTA eServices before commencing taxable supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation for the AG (collective capital company, minimum share capital CHF 100,000 under Art. 621 CO). The AG is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide profits: federal corporate income tax at 8.5% (effective approximately 4.4% after gross-up) plus cantonal and communal profit tax (combined effective rates vary, e.g., roughly 11.9% in Zug) and cantonal capital tax. Swiss law applies a classical-like system with shareholder-level dividend relief (Teilsatzverfahren) and participation exemption; there is no pass-through eligibility and no territorial-exemption election; residence is governed by statutory seat and place of effective management. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for the AG (collective capital company, minimum share capital CHF 100,000 under Art. 621 CO). The AG is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide profits: federal corporate income tax at 8.5% (effective approximately 4.4% after gross-up) plus cantonal and communal profit tax (combined effective rates vary, e.g., roughly 11.9% in Zug) and cantonal capital tax. Swiss law applies a classical-like system with shareholder-level dividend relief (Teilsatzverfahren) and participation exemption; there is no pass-through eligibility and no territorial-exemption election; residence is governed by statutory seat and place of effective management. No tax-exempt zone applies to this entity.

Available election forms

Switzerland has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election; tax classification follows legal form. Available elections: (1) Steuergruppe group taxation under the Tax Reform and AHV Financing Act (TRAF) in force since 1 January 2020, permitting a qualifying parent to elect consolidated federal and cantonal profit/capital taxation of 100% owned Swiss subsidiaries; (2) VAT group election under Art. 12 VAT Act forming a fiscal unity for Swiss group companies; (3) small-business election under Art. 10 para. 2 VAT Act allowing taxable persons below the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold to elect either full VAT liability with input deduction or exemption; (4) election of VAT accounting basis (agreed consideration versus received consideration) subject to FTA approval; (5) foreign-currency accounting election for domestic financial statements under Art. 58 DBG; (6) notional interest deduction on surplus capital (capital contribution privilege, Art. 1 TRAF); and (7) cantonal incentive elections (patent box, R&D super-deduction, status privileges abolished as of 2020). No hybrid mismatch elections exist; Swiss ATAD-aligned rules and the Pillar Two qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (15%, Mindestbesteuerungsverordnung in force 1 January 2024) apply to in-scope MNE groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 worldwide turnover per annum (Art. 10 para. 2 letter a VAT Act); reduced rates: 2.6% for basic necessities (food, books, medicines) and 3.7% for accommodation; a political proposal to raise the threshold to CHF 250,000 is pending and not yet enacted. Non-established remote sellers of goods or digital services to Swiss consumers may be taxable from the first franc of B2C digital supplies under Art. 7 para. 3 VAT Act, while the CHF 100,000 threshold applies to standard goods exports; B2B supplies to Swiss recipients are generally shifted to the recipient via reverse charge under Art. 45 and 51 VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of fast-fashion goods to foreign destinations qualifies for zero-rating (exempt with deduction) under Art. 23 para. 1 VAT Act where the AG proves physical dispatch abroad without undue delay, including e-commerce parcels via Swiss Post or courier with a customs export declaration; ancillary services directly related to exported goods and specified offshore services qualify under Art. 23 paras. 2–4 and Art. 21 para. 2 VAT Act. For C2B e-commerce exports, the exporter must retain destination-country customs clearance evidence; services supplied to recipients outside Switzerland are outside the Swiss VAT scope if not performed through a Swiss establishment.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) lodged electronically in the Federal Customs Administration e-dec system naming CH_AG as exporter and using the applicable export procedure code
  • Transport evidence: ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR international consignment note, or postal/courier export receipt with destination customs clearance tracking (CN23/CN22 for postal parcels)
  • Commercial or VAT invoice stating 'steuerfreie Exportlieferung (Art. 23 MWSTG)' and containing UID (MWST), foreign consignee address, goods description, quantity, and Incoterms
  • Proof of payment and foreign consideration: SWIFT/SEPA credit advice or payment-rail ledger confirmation; Swiss practice substitutes the formal Inward Remittance Certificate with auditable bank and ledger records

None: Switzerland has not introduced a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government-stamping, or statutory B2B e-invoicing clearance platform as of 2025. Electronic invoices (XML, EDI, Swiss QR-bill, eBill hub protocol) are legally valid under Art. 26 VAT Act and Art. 162 VAT Ordinance provided the statutory invoice content is present. The taxpayer must keep auditable electronic books and records for 10 years and grant FTA access to data stored domestically or abroad; no API pre-clearance to a governmental fiscal gateway is required. The Federal Council monitors the EU ViDA framework but has not enacted an equivalent Swiss mandate as of 2025.

Swiss VAT law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day figure is the conservative internal benchmark of the exporting group for assembling the export evidence file. The FTA audits export claims through book and document review under Art. 78 VAT Act; the assessment limitation period is generally 5 years after the tax period, extended to 10 years for intentional tax evasion. Missing or false export proof triggers retrospective assessment at 8.1% with default interest, and penalties under Art. 96 VAT Act of up to three times the evaded tax, or fines up to CHF 250,000 under Art. 100 VAT Act for negligent non-compliance; criminal fraud under Art. 146 Swiss Penal Code and loss of the right to deduct input VAT are further sanctions. The FTA may enforce joint and several liability against directors and group entities for e-commerce export schemes involving reimportation into Switzerland without applicable import VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. CHF is freely convertible; there are zero restrictions on foreign ownership, dividend repatriation, or cross-border payments for non-financial operating companies. No prior exchange control approval is required for inbound equity injections, intra-group loans, trade settlements, or profit remittance. Withholding tax of 35% applies to Swiss dividends (reducible under DTTs); this is a tax matter, not a currency control. For e-commerce flows denominated in EUR, USD and GBP, Swiss banks freely maintain multi-currency operating accounts; balances can be converted to CHF at market rates without notification to the SNB. The only administrative burden is the SNB monthly balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation for transactions above CHF 10 million (cumulative) or as instructed by the SNB data collection framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation timeline, or minimum retention ratio on export receipts, including e-commerce proceeds from cross-border apparel sales. Exporters may hold proceeds indefinitely in foreign currency or convert freely. Reporting is limited to statistical disclosure: SNB Balance of Payments form (Zahlungsbilanzmeldung) for goods sold online abroad when total monthly inflows exceed the de minimis threshold; sales of goods to foreign consumers are declared as 'goods trade' (Warenhandel) and settled via ordinary banking channels without regulatory clearance.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB / Schweizerische Nationalbank) in conjunction with FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) for banking conduct; SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) and SIF (State Secretariat for International Finance) for sanctions and financial market policy. Foreign exchange policy is administered under the Federal Act on Currency and Means of Payment (WZG) and the SNB Act; no dedicated foreign exchange control law exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a treasury concentration venue; High as a payment-only gateway. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not licensed Swiss banks and cannot accept 'deposits' in the banking-law sense under Art. 1 Banking Act. Funds placed with these EMIs are not covered by the esisuisse deposit protection scheme (§37a Banking Act). For ISIC 4791 e-commerce treasury, an EMI may be used for high-volume, low-value supplier disbursements and marketplace payouts, but the primary corporate treasury, escrow of the CHF 100,000 minimum share capital, and FX hedging margin must sit with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank. Contractually, the EMI agreement must be reviewed to ensure no rehypothecation, clear segregation of client funds, and EU e-money license (or equivalent) where the EMI is passporting into Switzerland; cross-border passported EMIs may only provide payment services to Swiss corporates without soliciting deposits if they act through a Swiss domestic bank as settlement agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: shareholder equity or shareholder loan via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent to the Swiss AG's operating account at UBS/ZKB. Mandatory reference fields include the no. 70 purpose code: use 'Equity injection - formation of Swiss AG' (SNB statistical classification code 2301 – establishment of companies and participation in such companies) or for loans use code 2302/2801 (intercompany financial loans). Verify the same-day SWIFT confirmation and provide the bank with an executed capital payment confirmation board resolution. The wire must be denominated in CHF, EUR, or USD with the bank's FX conversion noted; ensure the CHF 100,000 minimum share capital is booked to the separate capital escrow account (Einzahlungskonto/Aktienkapitalkonto) until registration, then released to operating account. For subsequent recapitalisation via debt push-down, prepare a written intercompany loan agreement with arm's-length interest (safe-harbour per OECD TP guidance), Swiss withholding tax analysis if interest exceeds deductibility thresholds, and notify the SNB in the monthly balance-of-payments survey.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for supplier settlements in EUR, USD, CNY, CHF; SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for Euro-zone suppliers under EUR 100,000 per transaction. For large apparel procurements exceeding USD 500,000 or where the supplier requires bank risk, use an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) issued by UBS/ZKB with MT700 dispatch and documents against payment (D/P) via documentary collection (MT400). Optional use of MT202 COV for forwarding interbank credits when supplier and bank differ. To support Klarna/BNPL receivables settlement, set up a merchant settlement account with an acquiring bank connected to the card networks (Visa/Mastercard/SIX Payment Services) and ensure funds are swept daily to the domestic Swiss concentration account. For FX payment conversion, use the bank's standard spot trade (T+2) or an automated currency sweep with a pre-agreed pip spread; avoid HKD/CNY cash-pooling without a licensed NDF if the supplier's currency is non-convertible.Outright FX Forwards (CHF/EUR, CHF/USD, CHF/GBP) - standard, freely permitted, documented under ISDA or Swiss Master Agreement; no SNB authorisation required for non-financial end-usersFX Swaps (roll-over/swap FX forwards) - permitted for treasury liquidity managementCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for medium-term alignment of USD/EUR revenue with CHF operating costs and dividend repatriation - allowed with Swiss bank counterparty under EMIR reporting if above volume thresholdsCall and Put FX Options - permissible (European-style) for flexible hedgingNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - permissible exclusively for illiquid emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY offshore variant, INR); rarely required for CHF due to full convertibility; must be executed with a regulated bank and reported in financial statements as derivative instrumentsInterest Rate/Currency Swaps with embedded FX for financing of cross-border inventory acquisition - permissible but subject to board derivative policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a treasury concentration venue; High as a payment-only gateway. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not licensed Swiss banks and cannot accept 'deposits' in the banking-law sense under Art. 1 Banking Act. Funds placed with these EMIs are not covered by the esisuisse deposit protection scheme (§37a Banking Act). For ISIC 4791 e-commerce treasury, an EMI may be used for high-volume, low-value supplier disbursements and marketplace payouts, but the primary corporate treasury, escrow of the CHF 100,000 minimum share capital, and FX hedging margin must sit with a FINMA-licensed Swiss bank. Contractually, the EMI agreement must be reviewed to ensure no rehypothecation, clear segregation of client funds, and EU e-money license (or equivalent) where the EMI is passporting into Switzerland; cross-border passported EMIs may only provide payment services to Swiss corporates without soliciting deposits if they act through a Swiss domestic bank as settlement agent.
