Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming a Quebec Limited Partnership must: (1) register the LP extra-provincially with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ), filing a signed declaration and appointing an attorney for service; (2) obtain the nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account opening either through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 or, for a Quebec establishment, through Revenu Québec's one-step combined GST/HST and QST registration, which simultaneously creates the QST file; (3) provide evidence of foreign incorporation, the general partner's identity and residence, and the LP agreement; (4) if the LP has no Canadian establishment, use CRA's 'non-resident registrant' pathway, which permits GST/HST registration without a Canadian address and without a permanent establishment, subject to voluntary registration status for zero-rated exporters; and (5) obtain a GST/HST account number and QST number with an effective registration date, which may be backdated up to 30 days (up to one year in exceptional circumstances). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Quebec limited partnerships are fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and Quebec income tax: no entity-level income tax is imposed; income, gains, losses and credits flow through proportionately to partners under ITA section 96 and the Quebec Taxation Act. The LP is nonetheless a separate person/registrant for GST/HST and QST purposes and for remittance and input-tax-credit mechanics. Depending on partner residence and place of business, the LP will be either a 'Canadian partnership' or a non-Canadian partnership; it is not subject to entity-level tax in either case.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity classification system; partnership pass-through is mandatory. Available elections relevant to an offshore vessel-leasing LP include: (i) ITA section 102 election for the partnership to be a 'Canadian partnership'; (ii) ITA section 97(2) partnership rollover election, allowing a partner to transfer vessel property to the LP at an agreed elected amount for tax-deferred carry-over; (iii) ETA section 156 election for closely related persons (for example, a corporate general partner in the same GST group) to be treated as a single person for GST/HST, with the parallel election under the Quebec Sales Tax Act; and (iv) OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 to 18.4, partially in force or proposed) which override pass-through treatment in cross-border arrangements producing deduction-without-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the ETA small-supplier threshold (section 148(1)) and the parallel QST threshold (section 77 of the QSTA), measured on the lesser of the person's worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters and in the current calendar quarter. A non-resident lessor of offshore vessels must register once its taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, and registration is mandatory regardless of threshold where the non-resident solicits orders or carries on a chartering business in Canada. A non-resident whose supplies are exclusively zero-rated may register voluntarily to recover GST/QST paid on Canadian inputs such as harbour services, vessel repairs and crew provisions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST and 0% QST) is available to the offshore supply-vessel leasing business on three alternative statutory grounds: (a) Schedule VI Part I item 17 of the ETA — supply by way of lease of a ship of more than 50 registered tons, which the ISIC 5222 offshore support fleet will ordinarily exceed; (b) Schedule VI Part V items 1, 2 and 3 — supply of a service of transporting goods or passengers from or to Canada or between places outside Canada, respectively; and (c) Schedule VI Part V item 2 — supply of a service to a non-resident person, provided the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada nor in respect of goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. A bareboat charter (no crew) is characterized as a supply of the vessel good; a time charter or crewed charter is characterized as a supply of a transportation service. To sustain the 0% rate the LP must: (i) be a GST/QST registrant; (ii) deliver possession and control of the vessel outside Canadian territorial waters (12-nautical-mile limit); (iii) contract with a non-resident charterer that does not enjoy a Canadian permanent establishment in respect of the charter; (iv) retain documentary proof of the vessel's tonnage, flag and registry; and (v) demonstrate the vessel is not substantially consumed or used in Canadian domestic waters.

  • Customs Export Declaration, transmitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (Canada Export Declaration G12 / CAED / CESARS), evidencing the vessel's export from Canada or repositioning to an international offshore supply operation
  • Bill of Lading, original negotiable form or authenticated sea waybill, evidencing delivery of the chartered vessel and supporting the international transportation character of the fixture
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, comprising the bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or equivalent showing receipt of charter hire from the non-resident charterer into the LP's Canadian bank account within the applicable repatriation deadline
  • Certificate of Registry, proving the vessel's registered tonnage exceeds 50 tons for Schedule VI Part I zero-rating
  • Charter Party Agreement, such as BIMCO SUPPLYTIME, HEAVYCON or BARECON fixture note, identifying the non-resident charterer, flag state, delivery location and intended offshore field

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or pre-clearance authorization is imposed at the federal or Quebec level. CRA and Revenu Québec operate audit-based rather than clearance-based e-invoicing. Mandates in force: (i) electronic filing of GST/HST and QST returns is mandatory for registrants, through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or Revenu Québec's clicSÉUR and EDI platform; (ii) electronic remittance by financial transfer is mandatory where annual net tax exceeds CAD 50,000; (iii) records, including electronic invoices and charter-party documents, must be retained in an accessible, readable format for six years; and (iv) Pan-Canadian Peppol PINT/P28 B2B e-invoicing standards are being adopted voluntarily, with no current statutory requirement to use a specific XML schema or interoperability platform.

Export zero-rating claims are a CRA and Revenu Québec audit priority in the marine charter sector. On audit failure the 0% rate is recharacterized and GST/HST plus QST are reassessed at 14.5% on gross charter hire, with: (1) interest under ETA section 280 and QSTA section 484, compounded daily from the return due date; (2) a false-statement or gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax at risk under ETA section 285 and QSTA section 500; (3) denial or reversal of input tax credits claimed in respect of vessel operating costs where the zero-rating fails; (4) extended reassessment windows of eight years or indefinite where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is established (normal window is four years); (5) criminal prosecution under ETA section 320 and ITA section 239 for evasion, with fines of up to 200% of the evaded federal tax and imprisonment up to five years; and (6) publication of taxpayer names in CRA's tax avoidance 'named persons' list where gross negligence is confirmed. Substantiation deficiencies, including incomplete bills of lading, missing inward remittance evidence, or charters to a Canadian-resident intermediary, trigger automatic clawback of prior refunds and may disqualify the LP from future export ITC claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII status. There are no balance-of-payments restrictions, no routine FX licensing, and no separate capital-account approval mechanism for the Quebec Limited Partnership (societe en commandite). The only material frictions are prudential: Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for foreign acquisitions of control above applicable enterprise-value thresholds and PCMLTFA/FINTRAC reporting duties. Profit remittances to non-resident partners of a pass-through LP are not blocked by exchange control; however, Canadian tax withholding may apply to interest, rents, and royalties under Income Tax Act s. 212, and non-resident partners must file Canadian Part I tax returns or claim treaty relief for allocated Canadian-source business income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Freely retained foreign-currency proceeds from offshore supply-vessel chartering may be held in a CAF$ or foreign-currency account at any authorized Canadian financial institution. Reporting obligations are limited to customs export declarations through CBSA/Canadian Export Reporting System when physical goods depart Canada and FINTRAC Electronic Fund Transfer Reports for each international transfer of CA$10,000 or more in a 24-hour period. No central bank conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; exchange-control and prudential supervision is shared with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada). No separate foreign-exchange administrative board exists. The Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) contain no conversion or surrender mandate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. A non-resident electronic money institution (EMI) cannot take Canadian demand deposits or act as a clearing member of SWIFT/LVTS without being incorporated and authorized under the Bank Act or registered as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23). Foreign fintech EMIs are treated as money services businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA and must register with FINTRAC; they cannot provide a conventional capital escrow account, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot perform maritime trade-clearing settlement in Canada. Therefore, the Quebec LP must rely on domestic Schedule I banking rails for its Stage 4 treasury architecture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and loan contributions from foreign partners must be made via SWIFT MT103 in CAD or another freely convertible currency, with a precise purpose message in Field 70 such as 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION FOR CA_LP / ISIC 5222 OFFSHORE SUPPLY VESSEL LEASING'. The receiving bank will automatically generate and submit the FINTRAC Electronic Fund Transfer Report whenever the amount equals or exceeds CA$10,000 within a 24-hour period. The instruction must include the legal entity name of the Quebec LP, its Registraire des entreprises registration number, and the General Partner's corporate identifier. For dual-currency funding, the commercial bank will apply a contractual FX conversion rate and correspondingly document the spot rate; no exchange-control approval is needed. Supporting verification includes signed subscription/contribution agreements, board/resolution approval of the General Partner, sanctions and PEP screening evidence, and CRS/FATCA declarations.Trade settlements for vessel hire, fuel, port charges, crew wages, and sub-charter payments should be cleared via: (i) SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank transfers for operating disbursements; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit confirmed under UCP 600 for charter-hire collateral and major voyage-related settlements; (iii) CAD-denominated payments through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) with SWIFT gpi tracking for cross-border finality; and (iv) dedicated maritime escrow arrangements requiring the bank to hold voyage proceeds and release funds only upon presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, and signed statement of facts, thereby reducing the risk of double payment and providing audit trail integrity for the CA_LP.Plain vanilla USD/CAD deliverable forward (FXA)Cross-currency interest-rate swap (USD/CAD)Vanilla USD/CAD foreign-exchange option (call or put) and collarExchange-traded USD/CAD futures and options on recognized regulated exchanges (TMX Group or CME Group)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. A non-resident electronic money institution (EMI) cannot take Canadian demand deposits or act as a clearing member of SWIFT/LVTS without being incorporated and authorized under the Bank Act or registered as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23). Foreign fintech EMIs are treated as money services businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA and must register with FINTRAC; they cannot provide a conventional capital escrow account, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot perform maritime trade-clearing settlement in Canada. Therefore, the Quebec LP must rely on domestic Schedule I banking rails for its Stage 4 treasury architecture.
