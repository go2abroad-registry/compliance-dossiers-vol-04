Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); GST/HST electronic services and CRA Business Registration Online. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Ontario–Canada Tax Collection Agreement.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident enterprise incorporating a Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) must file articles of incorporation, obtain the Ontario informational/registry compliance, and then procure the corporation's CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The application opens the GST/HST program account (RT), payroll program account (RP), and import/export account (RM) as needed. Non-resident shareholders are not required to obtain a Canadian TIN; the CA_CORP entity itself is the registered taxpayer. Where the non-resident operates a Canadian branch instead, the non-resident registers for the BN for that Canadian business and is subject to the same GST/HST registration rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident enterprise incorporating a Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) must file articles of incorporation, obtain the Ontario informational/registry compliance, and then procure the corporation's CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; a Canadian resident Business Corporation is always a separate taxable entity and is ineligible for pass-through classification. Relevant statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) functional currency reporting election under ITA s.261 for Canadian corporations maintaining books in a foreign functional currency (USD or EUR); (ii) GST/HST voluntary registration election under ETA s.240 for non-resident suppliers; (iii) election under ETA s.191(3)/(8) for qualifying export supplies to reduce ITC restrictions; (iv) international hybrid mismatch rules in ITA s.18.4–18.6 that disallow certain deduction/income mismatches and generally cannot be elected out of by a wholly-owned Canadian corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies made by the non-resident supplier (excluding sale of certain capital property and zero-rated supplies) per ETA s.148; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters. Ontario does not operate a separate VAT registration threshold because HST is collected federally by the CRA.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods (including aged Scotch and Bordeaux wine from bonded trade) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the vendor ships the goods out of Canada, delivery occurs outside Canada, and the vendor retains contemporaneous export evidence. Supplies to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and who will consume/use the goods outside Canada may also be zero-rated. For bonded alcoholic beverages, federal excise duty relief under the Excise Act, 2001 requires the goods to be entered for export from a licensed bonded/customs warehouse; release for home consumption in Canada would trigger HST and excise duty. Offshore services ancillary to the bonded trade (logistics, storage, brokerage) are zero-rated when delivered to a non-resident and performed wholly or principally outside Canada, or where they relate directly to exported goods.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice showing FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice with full product description, HS/ISIC code, quantity, and value
  • Bonded warehouse removal/export authorization and customs bond documentation

Not applicable — Canada does not operate a continuous real-time fiscal clearance model; there are no API-based fiscal stamping or live invoice-authorization rules at the federal level for GST/HST. Suppliers must issue prescribed GST/HST invoices containing supplier BN, date, recipient, description, tax amounts, and, where applicable, the 'zero-rated export' notation. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns and electronic remittance apply to registrants with annual taxable supplies at or above CAD 1.5 million, and GST/HST registrants may optionally use the Pan-Canadian Peppol B2B e-invoicing standard for procurement and electronic invoice exchange.

The CRA may deny zero-rating, disallow input tax credits, and reassess with interest and penalties for invalid or fraudulent export claims under ETA s.285, 326, and 327; normal reassessment period is four years, extended to ten years (or 12 years for serious misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud). Where goods are falsely claimed as exported while actually sold into Canada, the CRA and CBSA may recover unremitted GST/HST and impose penalties under the Customs Act and Excise Act, 2001, including monetary penalties per false/undervalued declaration and possible criminal exposure; directors and officers may be liable under ETA s.323 for wilful failure or negligence in remitting tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Canada maintains a freely floating Canadian dollar, complies fully with IMF Article VIII and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and has no exchange controls over current-account or capital-account operations. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax and licensing based rather than currency based: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding tax of 25% on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid to non-residents, treaty-reduced to 5-15% for residents of the US (Canada-US Tax Convention), the UK, and France; (ii) AGCO Ontario import permits and the federal Importation of Intoxicating Liquors Act provincial-permit gate for lawful wine/spirit imports; and (iii) FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. No convertibility restriction, surrender regime, or central-bank approval impedes equity injections, trade settlements, or remittance of post-tax profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender, forced-conversion, repatriation, or holding-period rules on export earnings; bonded export receipts of aged Scotch and Bordeaux may remain in USD/EUR/GBP in a Canadian foreign-currency account or be converted at spot freely. The only compliance obligations are: (i) CBSA customs export declarations and in-bond movement records under the Customs Act for goods leaving a customs bonded warehouse; (ii) balance-of-payments reporting by the client's bank to the Bank of Canada/Statistics Canada for transactions above the reporting threshold; and (iii) FINTRAC records and reporting for currency and wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. No portion of export proceeds is payable to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), acting jointly with the Minister of Finance as the monetary and exchange-control authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates domestic Schedule I banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration body, and no official authorization is required for cross-border capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium-Low for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not recognize EU/UK EMI passports, and a non-resident EMI cannot receive deposits or issue client settlement accounts without a Schedule I bank licence under the Bank Act. An offshore fintech may instead register as a FINTRAC Money Services Business (MSB) or partner with a Tier-1 bank, but such entities cannot deliver the mandated Canadian Lynx/SWIFT clearing rails, excise-duty collection, or AGCO/CBSA bonded-transfer settlement. Use for tactical FX conversion and supplier-accelerated pays is feasible; the primary operating and escrow accounts must remain at a chartered Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general rate 15% (Part I, Income Tax Act (ITA), R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)) plus Ontario general corporate income tax rate 11.5%, producing a combined general headline rate of 26.5% on taxable income allocated to Ontario; the separate Ontario rate applies to income earned in Ontario after federal general rate reduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; the 25% rate applies to non-treaty jurisdictions (e.g., a Cypriot or BVI parent) and to the excess portion of any treaty-reduced dividend where the corporate-residency or beneficial-ownership test fails

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not apply a monetary threshold to the substantive arm's length rule (ITA s.247(2)); contemporaneous documentation must be prepared whenever a taxpayer is party to a transaction or series with a non-arm's length non-resident and a reasonable person would conclude the terms differ from arm's length. The information-return triggers are (i) T106 Information Return: aggregate fair-market value of all reportable transactions with any single non-arm's length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year; (ii) Country-by-Country (CbC) Report, Master File and Local File under the post-25-June-2023 documentation regulations: CbC applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 1 billion; a prescribed Master/Local File documentation package is required for group revenue of at least CAD 1 billion with Canadian revenue booked in the group above CAD 10 million (draft regulations as announced in the 2021 Federal Budget and effective for taxation years beginning after 25 June 2023). Note: for a standalone Ontario wholesale corporation with no foreign parent, low TP risk exists except for cross-border purchases/sales of bonded stock, branding royalties, guarantee fees and management charges.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through (1) the common-law central-management-and-control residence test and the statutory deemed-residence rule in ITA s.250(4) (a corporation incorporated in Canada is resident irrespective of board location); (2) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) which denies tax benefits of transactions lacking a bona fide non-tax purpose; (3) anti-hybrid and treaty-shopping rules (including the OECD-style principal-purpose test embedded in amended Article XXIX of the Canada-US treaty), and (4) for alcoholic beverages, the Excise Act, 2001 and AGCO licensing regimes: a bonded warehouse licence requires a physical, bonded premises with continuous CRA supervision, a licensed warehouse owner operating in Ontario, and the posting of a bond/security for excise duties, imposing inherent economic substance on the Ontario entity. A paper Ontario company acting solely as a pass-through invoice platform for non-resident principals will be reassessed and can lose its excise and alcohol licences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Non-Reporting (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years less a day; on indictment: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil consequence: non-disclosure interest, gross-negligence penalty (ITA s.163(2)) equal to 50% of the understated tax, and liability for all reassessed taxes absent any limitation period (unlimited reassessment for si.239 wilful misrepresentation).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize or acquiesce in evasion can be charged as parties under the Criminal Code (s.21); sentencing includes restitution orders, criminal fines, and incarceration. CRA may register a judgement lien; directors may be barred from leaving Canada where an arrest warrant is outstanding (Canada Border Services Agency flag at ports of entry).
Gross Negligence / Third-Party Facilitator Penalties (ITA s.163(2) and s.163.2)

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax understated for false statements (s.163(2)); transfer-pricing adjustments attract the 10% penalty on net adjustments where reasonable-efforts documentation is absent; third-party preparer penalties up to CAD 100,000 (s.163.2(4)) and gross-negligence penalties for planners up to 100% of the benefit

Director personal liability: CRA applies the 'responsible officer' doctrine: directors who prepare or sign false returns with minimal review are jointly liable for the corporate penalty to the extent of the corporation's non-collectability (ITA s.163(3)); civil indemnity claims by co-directors and criminal exposure under s.239 continue where knowledge is shown.
Unremitted Part XIII / Source Withholding & Director Liability (ITA s.215 and s.227.1)

Statutory sanction: The payer remains fully liable for the unwithheld amounts; CRA collects 25% of the gross remittance plus interest and penalties (s.215(3) and s.227(8)); failure to file NR4 attracts CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; interest compounds daily at the CRA prescribed rate (approximately 9% for 2025 corporate credits; GOC quarterly rate varies)

Director personal liability: ITA s.227.1 imposes strict joint and several personal liability on all directors who served at the time of the failure, provided the corporation has not satisfied the debt after CRA has executed against corporate assets; no due-diligence defence is available for a failure to withhold where directors caused the payment to be made without checking residency certificates; directors can be personally assessed without a corporate veil, and travel bans can be sought through CRA collection warrants (Income Tax Act s.225.1 requires judicial authorization to seize assets, and egregious non-payment supports court-ordered travel restrictions).
GAAR / Economic Substance / Shell Company Abuse (ITA s.245, s.250(4) and Treaty Anti-Abuse)

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit and applies a penalty of 25% of the denied benefit (proposed 50% for transactions after 2023); CRA reassesses on a substance-over-form basis, recapturing the small business deduction, disallowing losses, and reattributing income to the non-resident principal; Ontario may revoke or refuse the liquor licence under the Liquor Licence Act and Customs Bonded Warehouse Licence under Excise Act, 2001 section 19 with immediate duty demand, duty equal to the greater of the ex-warehouse duty rate and 200% of the duty evaded

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell entity exposed to fraud, fraudulent conveyance (Ontario Assignments and Preferences Act / Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act s.95-96) and director liability provisions in the Excise Act, 2001; the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation is a mere agent or sham, making directors personally liable for unpaid excise duties, HST and wage claims (OBCA s.130); serious cases are referred to the RCMP Federal Policing, Integrated Proceeds of Crime and the CBSA, and non-resident directors may be declared inadmissible to Canada under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act s.36 for 'serious criminality' where evasion or money laundering is proven.
