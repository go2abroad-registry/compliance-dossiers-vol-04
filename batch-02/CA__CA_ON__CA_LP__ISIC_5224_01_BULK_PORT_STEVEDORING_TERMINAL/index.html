Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Registration and Business Number (BN) Portal; portal: canada.ca/business-taxes; Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial tax matters

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Canada Revenue Agency Program Accounts) with the CRA, or RC1A for non-residents, indicating the GST/HST program account; the CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus an RT/RC program identifier; for a limited partnership, the partnership itself is registered for GST/HST while the partners report their allocated shares, with the partnership’s BN referenced on all returns; completion typically requires legal entity proof, directors'/partners' details, and a Canadian address or agent's address; online registration is available through CRA's 'Register for a business number' service.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Canada Revenue Agency Program Accounts) with the CRA, or RC1A for non-residents, indicating the GST/HST program account; the CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus an RT/RC program identifier; for a limited partnership, the partnership itself is registered for GST/HST while the partners report their allocated shares, with the partnership’s BN referenced on all returns; completion typically requires legal entity proof, directors'/partners' details, and a Canadian address or agent's address; online registration is available through CRA's 'Register for a business number' service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election comparable to the United States; a limited partnership is generally a pass-through under the Income Tax Act (ITA) if it is a 'Canadian partnership' or a foreign partnership treated as non-fiscally transparent; certain deemed-transparent or opaque elections may be available under the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 18.4 and 212.3 for cross-border arrangements; a partnership may elect to have its fiscal year end under ITA subsection 249.1(4), and the partners may jointly elect to use alternative allocation methods; Ontario adopts federal tax classification for income tax purposes, and the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act confirms the pass-through nature unless commercially unusual conditions apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual threshold for GST/HST registrant status); a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over any consecutive four calendar quarters, or earlier if required for export zero-rating; no threshold for 'specially permitted' registrants claiming input tax credits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of stevedoring and cargo-handling services in respect of bulk cargo exported from Canada can be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, provided the services are rendered to a non-resident person and the goods are exported as part of the supply, or the services are in respect of goods being loaded onto an ocean vessel for export; the key conditions are: (i) the supply is of a service expressly listed as zero-rated, (ii) the recipient is a non-resident, and (iii) the service is supplied to or in respect of cargo destined for export; GST/HST registrants may also zero-rate supplies of services rendered to a non-resident that use the services in commercial activity outside Canada; supporting evidence must link the physical cargo movement to the service invoice.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or ACI e-manifest export report)
  • Marine Bill of Lading (through bill or port-to-port bill)
  • Marine Cargo Report / Shipping Note
  • Proof of service performance (stevedoring tally sheets, terminal receipts, weighbridge certificates)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record showing payment from non-resident recipient in foreign currency
  • Final Export Contract / Purchase Order and Container/Vessel itinerary

Canada mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE/EFILE channels, and customs declarations through CBSA's ACI/CCS (Cargo Control System) for marine shipments; there is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance platform for GST/HST, but documentary evidence of export transactions must be retained electronically and be available for CRA audit on a 48-hour demand basis; bulk port terminal operators typically submit marine export declarations through the CBSA Single Window Initiative.

CRA auditors will scrutinize export zero-rating claims to verify the goods were actually exported and that payment was received from a non-resident; under the Excise Tax Act, a registrant that knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence makes a false statement may be subject to a penalty of 50% of the unreported tax; false zero-rating claims can be reassessed with GST/HST clawed back, plus arrears interest and daily compounded penalties; failure to provide export documents may result in denial of the 0% rate, recovery of input tax credits previously claimed, and penalties up to CAD 4,000 per instance plus additional civil penalties for 'misrepresentation' under ITA s. 163.2 if income tax is affected; willful evasion can attract criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible floating currency; Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning there are no current-account exchange restrictions, no capital-account approval permits for non-resident partner capital contributions, and no domestic restriction on converting CAD stevedoring revenues into USD, EUR, or any other freely traded currency. Profit remittance from the Ontario Limited Partnership occurs through partnership distributions made in respect of a non-resident partner's partnership interest; such distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act at the statutory rate of 25%, with treaty-reduced rates available (for example, 5% or 15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention, 1980, as amended, where the distribution is characterised as a dividend for treaty purposes, or relief under the business-profits or other-income articles where the non-resident partner has no permanent establishment in Canada). There are no statutory hurdles to conversion or repatriation of CAD profits, provided the payer remits the correct Part XIII withholding to the Canada Revenue Agency and the non-resident partner obtains a Canadian tax clearance where required. Establishment of the stevedoring terminal by non-residents is subject to the Investment Canada Act, which requires a mandatory notification or, where the enterprise value exceeds the applicable WTO-investor review threshold, an application for review; this is a filing requirement rather than a currency-control constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not impose a statutory timeline for repatriating export/service proceeds, does not mandate any conversion percentage, and does not require surrender of export receipts to the Bank of Canada. The stevedoring LP may invoice vessel operators, port principals, and cargo interests in CAD, USD, or EUR and may maintain multi-currency accounts in Canada and with foreign correspondent banks. The mandatory compliance points are: (i) FINTRAC reporting of every incoming international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more and every outgoing international EFT of C$10,000 or more; (ii) cross-border currency and monetary-instruments declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency (Form E311) for any physical movement of C$10,000 or more in cash, cheques, or other monetary instruments; (iii) recognition of foreign-exchange gains and losses in computing income under section 39 of the Income Tax Act for the partnership and its partners; and (iv) retention of invoicing, bills of lading, and receipt documentation under subsection 230(1) of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border wire reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no foreign-exchange-control act and no central-bank permission is required for cross-border payments, foreign-currency accounts, or profit remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury bank. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised to accept deposits in Canada because they are neither Schedule I Canadian banks nor OSFI-supervised financial institutions under the Bank Act; operating through an unregistered foreign EMI would violate the registration requirements of the PCMLTFA and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations, exposing the LP and its partners to criminal and administrative sanctions. A Canada-incorporated and FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) or payment-service provider is structurally viable only as a supplementary collection rail for small payments, and must sweep balances daily into a regulated Schedule I bank account. All port-authority clearing, commercial letters of credit, stevedoring payroll, and vessel-turnaround settlements must be executed through a Canadian chartered bank or an OSFI-supervised trust company to assure irrevocable settlement, eligibility for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage of up to C$100,000 per insured category, and compliance with the capital-account protections of the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital calls should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian operating or capital-escrow account at a Schedule I bank, quoting the Ontario LP registration number and the purpose narrative 'capital contribution under limited partnership agreement'. Large-value CAD transfers should be routed through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day irrevocable settlement; USD amounts should be settled through the Canadian bank's United States correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS. The remittance instruction must include full originator and beneficial-owner identity fields to satisfy FINTRAC's EFT reporting threshold of C$10,000 and the 24-hour reporting rule. The receiving bank will verify the capital injection against the partnership agreement and the registered Statement of Registration. Where non-resident partners establish a new Canadian business, the Ontario LP must first comply with the Investment Canada Act by filing the mandatory notification for a new business and, where the enterprise value crosses the applicable review threshold, obtaining an approval or an undertaking; the bank should receive a copy of the notification acknowledgement or approval letter before onboarding large inbound wires. The bank will also apply its method-of-payment diligence and, where the originating bank does not provide transparent beneficiary information, may request a correspondent-bank assurance letter confirming the principal of the wire.Outbound disbursements for the stevedoring terminal should be executed across the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 and MT202 messages for vendor, fuel, charterer, and subcontracted-gang payments in CAD, USD, and EUR; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 by the Canadian bank for imported terminal equipment, crane parts, conveyor spares, and marine bunkers; (iii) the dedicated port-authority clearing rail for wharfage, dockage, and other tariff obligations, which integrates with the port's electronic clearing house and settles through LVTS in CAD or through the bank's USD correspondent accounts; and (iv) same-day internal transfers between the LP's CAD operating account and its USD/EUR concentration sub-accounts to minimise conversion spread. Every outgoing international EFT of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and the bank must screen all counterparties against Canadian sanctions regulations under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, administered by Global Affairs Canada, in addition to US OFAC lists for USD-clearing items. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate income tax rate for an Ontario corporate partner (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); the Ontario Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and subject to no entity-level income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding applies to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. For an Ontario LP, the operational profit allocation to a non-resident partner is not a dividend: active business income is taxed under Part I, and the partner may repatriate after-tax profit without further Part XIII withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 5 months after the partnership fiscal year-end (e.g., May 31 for a December 31 year-end). An Ontario LP with corporate partners or non-resident partners is ineligible for an exemption from filing.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after the corporate partner's fiscal year-end; tax instalments are due in monthly or quarterly prescribed amounts. Ontario tax is reported on the same T2 return under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act collection agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold exists under s.247 of the Canadian Income Tax Act. CRA administrative practice requires contemporaneous transfer pricing records for any controlled transaction with a non-resident. A Local File is generally expected when controlled cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in annual aggregate. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the Caribbean/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the CRA invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) and common-law 'sham' and 'artificial transaction' doctrines to disregard partnerships lacking bona fide commercial substance. An Ontario LP with no physical terminal equipment, no employees, and no operational control of the stevedoring site will be re-characterized as a conduit; income will be attributed to the non-resident principal and subjected to Part XIII withholding on all deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under s.238(1) of the Income Tax Act, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years on indictment; summary conviction carries imprisonment up to 2 years. CRA may also assess significant arrears interest and a gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any acting directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withheld taxes. A director or officer who participated in the evasion is personally liable for restitution, fines, and may be subject to imprisonment. CRA can freeze personal bank accounts, seize assets, and obtain a court order restricting international travel when criminal proceedings are initiated.
Transfer Pricing / GAAR / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply s.247 to reprice intragroup transactions and s.245 to disallow the entire tax benefit. Reassessments will impose federal/Ontario tax at 26.5%, add 25% Part XIII withholding on all non-resident distributions, and apply transfer pricing penalties of 10% of the net adjustment. CRA may also void the partnership's limited liability shield by proving the limited partners took part in control.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner sign the T5013 and T2 returns; if they knowingly sign false or incomplete transfer pricing documents, they face personal civil liability for withheld taxes and interest. Under s.380 of the Criminal Code, fraudulent documentation of financial statements can lead to a term of imprisonment up to 14 years. Directors face removal orders and travel restrictions in pending Canadian criminal proceedings.
AML / Port Regulatory Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA can reach CAD 500,000 for a first violation. Criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of crime carries a fine of up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years. The Canada Marine Act and port authority rules permit immediate revocation of the terminal operating agreement, halting all stevedoring operations.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and the general partner have a statutory duty under s.76 of the PCMLTFA to take all reasonable precautions; failure allows prosecution directly against them. Personal fines and imprisonment apply to the individual who directed the omitted reports. Federal courts may impose non-monetary conditions, including surrender of passport, strict bail terms, and prohibition from leaving Canada while charges are pending.
