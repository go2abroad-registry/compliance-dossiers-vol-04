Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Canada's national tax administration for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and excise taxes; administered through the 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' portals (canada.ca). Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the CRA on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance under the federal-provincial corporate tax collection agreement; Ontario tax accounts are integrated with the CRA Business Number.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first completes incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or Ontario Business Corporations Act (provincial), or registers as an extra-provincial corporation in Ontario. The entity then obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online or Form RC1. For GST/HST registration, the same BN is used with a program-account identifier (e.g., RT0001). A non-resident entity is statutorily required to register for GST/HST when carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada, unless the small-supplier exemption applies; it may also voluntarily register to claim input tax credits. Ontario tax accounts (corporate income tax, Ontario HST component, workplace safety, and employer health tax) are administered under the same CRA BN profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate, non-transparent taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and Ontario corporate income tax. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations carrying on business through a permanent establishment in Canada are taxed only on Canadian-source income. The target entity is a taxable Canadian corporation, not a pass-through vehicle.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a United States-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Hybrid and branch mismatch consequences are addressed non-electively through specific hybrid-mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act (including sections 18.2, 18.3, 20.2, and 247) that disallow deductions or include amounts in income to neutralize inconsistent tax treatment. Statutory elections that may be relevant include section 216 (net rental income election for non-residents), section 219 (branch tax election), and the treaty-based election under subsection 126(2.1) for foreign tax credits; these do not change the entity-level taxation default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in consideration for worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada by the entity and associated persons, measured over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters or on an ongoing small-supplier test; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory for the Canadian entity. A non-resident registrant may have a lower practical threshold when making taxable supplies in Canada through a fixed establishment, and non-resident providers of certain digital services must register when global revenues attributable to Canadian consumers exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of software development and offshore engineering services to a non-resident recipient is generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the service is performed outside Canada, or where it is performed in Canada provided the non-resident recipient is not present in Canada at any time while the service is performed and the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada. For the described offshore development center supplying services to a foreign parent or affiliated foreign clients, the export nature must be evidenced by the non-resident status of the recipient, the absence of Canadian consumption, and contractual terms confirming the beneficiary is outside Canada. Where the service is provided to a non-resident registrant and consumed in Canada or in connection with Canadian property, GST/HST may instead be payable; otherwise the service qualifies for 0% HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System) where tangible media or physical deliverables are exported
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical export movement, where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued foreign-exchange/remittance record showing receipt of export consideration in Canadian or foreign currency

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or pre-clearance electronic-invoicing regime, and there is no government digital stamping requirement. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices that contain prescribed registration information and are required to retain electronic and paper records for at least six years. CRA accepts fully electronic invoicing and record-keeping where the information is complete, legible, and supports the transaction audit trail; no API clearance filing is required at the time of invoice issuance.

CRA rigorously audits export zero-rating and may reassess the registrant within the four-year normal reassessment period for GST/HST, with an extended reassessment period where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. If export zero-rating is denied, the full 13% HST is clawed back from the vendor, together with arrears interest and penalties. Civil penalties may include a false-statement penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (minimum CAD 100) and gross-negligence penalties. Criminal prosecution for knowing or willful evasion of GST/HST may result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. Additionally, participation in an artificial export transaction or sham arrangement can trigger the general anti-avoidance rule under subsection 245(2) of the Income Tax Act and parallel provincial penalty provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a floating, fully convertible currency. Both current-account flows (software service fees, salaries, royalties, dividends) and capital-account flows (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, capital contributions) are freely transferable without any exchange-control approval. Profit remittance is permitted through ordinary SWIFT wires, but Part XIII withholding tax, Canadian transfer-pricing documentation, and T106 reporting obligations must be satisfied. No domestic conversion or repatriation quota applies to the offshore development centre.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts under Canadian law. Exporters of software engineering services may retain export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency, may choose their own clearing banks, and are not required to repatriate or convert hard currency. Cross-border EFTs of C$10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — no standalone foreign exchange administration exists; FINTRAC administers cross-border EFT reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium — feasible only as an ancillary or disbursement rail, not as the primary corporate treasury. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer are not authorized banks or credit unions in Canada; Canadian operations must be registered as money services businesses with FINTRAC and generally operate through partner-bank deposit arrangements. They can receive and settle CAD invoices, but they cannot issue commercial LCs, provide committed credit, or replace a Schedule I bank relationship for statutory capital escrow and SWIFT-backed liquidity management.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if transactions with non-resident related parties exceed CAD 1 million for services or CAD 5 million for goods; Master File required if total group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and the Canadian entity's transactions exceed CAD 5 million. Documentation must be prepared by the filing due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act applies to transactions lacking economic substance. For offshore development centers, the CRA may scrutinize whether the Canadian entity has real business operations, employees, and risk management to avoid being deemed a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including WHT) under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they fail to remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to reallocate income under transfer pricing rules (Section 247) and apply GAAR to deny tax benefits; penalties up to 10% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential for criminal charges under the Criminal Code for fraud.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with AML obligations.
