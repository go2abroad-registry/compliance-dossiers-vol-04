Minimum nominal share capital of CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value per share, and not less than CHF 20,000 in aggregate, must be paid up before registration; the funds must be held in a blocked Swiss bank account and released only upon proof of Commercial Register registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the cantonal fire prevention authority; structural load verification for racking and storage areas from a licensed engineer; workplace clearance under the Swiss Labour Code and Labour Inspection Ordinance; employees registered with SUVA (Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung / Administration fédérale des contributions; ESTV/AFC), with the official e-service portal estv.admin.ch and the central UID registry at uid.admin.ch.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities apply to ESTV/AFC for Swiss VAT registration on the official form or via the secure FTA e-portal once annual taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 250,000 or voluntary registration is chosen. The application must include: constitutive documents and current foreign company registry extract, proof of foreign tax identification, Swiss address/service address or appointed representative, sector and turnover declaration, and expected Swiss supplies. After validation, the FTA assigns the Swiss business identification number (UID/IDE/IVH) and the separate Swiss VAT number (MWST/TVA/IVA). The UID is recorded in the public UID register and is the applicable Swiss tax identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities apply to ESTV/AFC for Swiss VAT registration on the official form or via the secure FTA e-portal once annual taxable turnover in Switzerland exceeds CHF 250,000 or voluntary registration is chosen. The application must include: constitutive documents and current foreign company registry extract, proof of foreign tax identification, Swiss address/service address or appointed representative, sector and turnover declaration, and expected Swiss supplies. After validation, the FTA assigns the Swiss business identification number (UID/IDE/IVH) and the separate Swiss VAT number (MWST/TVA/IVA). The UID is recorded in the public UID register and is the applicable Swiss tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No United-States-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch classification election exists in Swiss federal tax law; an Aktiengesellschaft is mandatorily taxable at entity level. Relevant statutory elections affecting indirect taxation include the Swiss VAT group election (Art. 21 Federal Act on VAT) for closely integrated persons and the right to elect voluntary VAT registration below statutory thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 (for Swiss-resident businesses: annual worldwide taxable turnover; for businesses established abroad: annual turnover derived from supplies taxable in Switzerland); voluntary registration is available even below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is eligible for 0% Swiss VAT if the goods are physically exported from Switzerland in the course of the taxable person's commercial activity, title passes to a foreign recipient, and the export is proven to the customs authorities, normally through a customs export declaration (e-dec Export). Offshore services supplied by a Swiss AG to a foreign recipient may be outside Swiss VAT or zero-rated under place-of-supply rules to the extent the recipient and use/benefit are abroad; the taxpayer must document the business relationship and destination. Subscription-box exports are zero-rated only when the goods leave Swiss customs territory and the shipment documentation, customer order file, and transport evidence form a complete proof chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrdeklaration / déclaration en douane d'exportation) generated via e-dec Export
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / Air Waybill / postal dispatch certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swiss-bank evidence of foreign receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice stating seller, buyer, destination, Incoterms and export dispatch
  • Subscription order/contract or ERP sales record identifying the foreign customer and delivery address

Switzerland does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API, or digital stamping mandate for VAT invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted if authenticity and integrity are preserved; ordinary Swiss VAT invoice content rules apply. The Swiss QR-bill (QR-Rechnung) is introduced for machine-readable payment slips, but it is not a tax pre-clearance requirement. No invoice must be pre-approved by ESTV before issuance.

ESTV and the Swiss Federal Customs Administration can audit export proofs and trace shipment and payment chains. If zero-rating is claimed without documentary evidence, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at the standard rate (currently 8.1%) and the input VAT deducted against the export supply is clawed back. Section 70 VATA empowers ESTV to assess tax evaded with interest; fines may reach four times the evaded VAT and criminal prosecution is possible in aggravated tax fraud cases. Ordinary limitation period is generally five to ten years depending on tax evasion or omission, with retrospective reassessment for the full limitation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CHF is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. Switzerland is an IMF Article VIII member; there are no foreign exchange licensing regimes, no prior approval mandates for inward or outward transfers, and no capital controls. Profit and dividend remittance from a Swiss AG to foreign shareholders is unrestricted; the only friction is Swiss federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35% on distributed dividends, which is reduced or refunded under Switzerland's extensive double taxation treaty network or domestic participation relief. The sole formal obligation is SNB statistical reporting: any resident company with aggregate foreign assets/liabilities or annual international payment flows exceeding CHF 10 million equivalent must file (or instruct its bank to file) the SNB International Payments Report; the reporting is accomplished via the banking relationship and does not constitute a convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Swiss law imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. A Swiss AG may freely retain export receivables in USD, EUR, CNY, or other currencies, hold them in foreign-currency accounts, and convert at market rates or execute hedges without SNB approval. For the subscription-box business, cross-border card settlement flows are credited in original currency to the operational account and may be swept to CHF at the company's discretion. The only ancillary duty is accurate declaration of goods in Swiss Customs (e-dec/eTAS) and correct payment-purpose labelling for SNB statistics; no statutory deadline for conversion exists.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) / Schweizerische Nationalbank (SNB), Bern and Zurich; administered jointly with the Federal Department of Finance. SNB is the sole monetary authority and custodian of Swiss franc (CHF) external stability, but prudential banking supervision resides with FINMA. SNB also operates the Swiss Interbank Clearing (SIC/SIC4) system and enforces statistical reporting on international payments and cross-border positions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary card-collection or multi-currency settlement node; LOW as the primary treasury institution. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Paysera) are not FINMA-licensed banks and may not lawfully accept deposits from the Swiss public under Art. 1 Banking Act. They operate under UK EMI or EU PSD2 licences, typically holding client funds in pooled safeguarding accounts at partner banks rather than segregated Swiss bank accounts. For a subscription-box AG, an EMI may be used for customer card settlement and cross-border supplier payouts, provided: (i) the EMI is fully licensed in its home EEA/UK jurisdiction, (ii) the company's principal operating account and statutory capital deposit remain at a FINMA-regulated bank, and (iii) balances are swept daily to the Swiss account to avoid unlicensed deposit exposure and opaque payment-purpose classification. EMIs may not be used for payroll, Swiss tax, or share-capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory share capital (minimum CHF 100,000 total, with at least CHF 50,000 paid in at incorporation) is routed via SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 from the shareholder's overseas bank using purpose code 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARES' and referencing the notarial deed and the pending Commercial Register filing. The receiving Swiss bank credits the funds to a blocked capital escrow account (Sperrkonto); release occurs only upon registration of the AG in the Commercial Register. For a subsequent capital increase, the same protocol applies with reference to the notarial capital-increase deed and shareholder resolution. EUR contributions from EEA shareholders may use SEPA Credit Transfer; all other inbound flows must be SWIFT. Each inward transfer above CHF 15,000 triggers an automatic request for source-of-funds documentation; the mandatory rule is that funds must originate from the declared shareholder's own account and never from a third party, otherwise the deposit may be treated as unlicensed public deposit-taking or trigger an AML investigation.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA suppliers and international freight, with purpose code 'TRADE PAYMENT / PURCHASE OF GOODS' and reference to the commercial invoice; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU/EEA suppliers in EUR; (3) SIC4 instant CHF payments for domestic logistics, payroll, and recurring Swiss-service invoices; (4) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and documentary collections (URC 522) for high-value inventory sourcing above USD 100,000, quoted via a Swiss confirming bank with a cash margin of typically 10–30%. Each outward disbursement must be matched with the underlying supplier invoice, customs declaration, and proof of delivery to satisfy FINMA AML audit and SNB statistical reporting; no pre-approval from FINMA or SNB is required for outbound wires. For subscription-box customer refunds, use the card-processor reversal rail first; bank-level wires are reserved for B2B or regulatory refunds.Outright FX Forwards (USD/CHF, EUR/CHF, CNY/CHF) with tenors up to 12 months under ISDA or a Swiss Master Agreement, available from UBS, ZKB, and BCV without SNB approvalFX Swaps (near-leg/far-leg) for rolling the currency mismatch between card-settlement USD balances and CHF operational liquidityEuropean-style vanilla FX Options (calls and puts) and zero-premium option collars to protect the EBIT margin of subscription-box revenue against EUR/USD and CHF appreciationCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) to hedge long-term mismatches between foreign-currency cash holdings and CHF-denominated borrowingsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on exotic sourcing currencies (TRY, INR, IDR) where onshore deliverability is restricted; NDFs on CHF are unnecessary because CHF is fully convertibleOTC FX limit-order sweeps for daily conversion of customer card settlement balances into CHF at best execution, executed via the bank's e-banking treasury module
