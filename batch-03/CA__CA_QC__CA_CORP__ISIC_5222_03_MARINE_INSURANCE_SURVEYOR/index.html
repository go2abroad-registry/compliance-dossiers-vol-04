Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; for Québec provincial sales tax, Revenu Québec at https://www.revenuquebec.ca

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident corporation doing business in Canada obtains a 15-character Business Number (BN), which serves as the TIN. Steps: (1) incorporate federally or provincially, or register extra-provincially with Corporations Canada or the Registraire des entreprises du Québec; (2) register for CRA tax accounts through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or Form RC1, supplying corporate registration documents and directors' information; (3) where the corporation carries on a Québec commercial activity, also obtain the Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ) and register for GST/HST and QST accounts with Revenu Québec via 'Register for Tax Services' in the Québec enterprise register; (4) open CRA My Business Account and Revenu Québec clic ReQ UT account; (5) keep BN and NEQ references on all invoices and customs documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. A Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Quebec Business Corporations Act is inherently classified as a taxable corporation, and entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available elections include the non-resident corporation's subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election for net Canadian property income, section 85 rollover elections for reorganization transactions, and certain provincial election mechanisms, but none changes the default corporation classification or grants pass-through eligibility. Hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act sections 12.4 and 18.4 are mandatory statutory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective classification regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies by a non-resident carrying on business in Canada in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four quarters; QST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Québec for the QST small-supplier threshold. Services supplied to non-resident insurers may nevertheless be zero-rated, but registration remains advisable to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A marine claims survey service is zero-rated as an exported service only when the recipient is a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is not supplied in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or intangible personal property for use in Canada. If the vessel or cargo is physically present in Canadian territory at the time of inspection, the supply is ordinarily taxable at 5% GST and 9.975% QST unless a specific statutory exception applies. The commercial documentation must establish the foreign ownership of the insured risk, the non-resident status of the insurer, and the absence of a Canadian situs for the property surveyed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System record) where goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading or original shipping evidence for vessel or cargo movements
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire confirmation showing foreign-currency receipt within the contractual period
  • Executed marine claims survey agreement and survey report identifying the insured object, survey location, and cause of damage
  • Certificate of non-resident status or declaration from the foreign underwriter or reinsurer
  • GST/HST and QST invoice with zero-rating notation and supporting place-of-supply analysis

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate is in force at either the federal or Québec level. CRA accepts electronic invoices containing the prescribed statutory information under Excise Tax Act section 230; electronic GST/HST return filing is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1,500,000. Revenu Québec requires electronic filing of QST returns and prescribed invoices, but does not operate a government pre-approval or digital stamping clearance system for routine invoices.

CRA and Revenu Québec may reassess invalid zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, or indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is present. A reassessment will claw back claimed input tax credits, charge GST/QST, and impose interest. Under Excise Tax Act section 285.1, the civil penalty is the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax amount for false statements; gross negligence penalties can also apply; fraudulent conduct carries criminal liability with significant fines and imprisonment. Revenu Québec applies parallel QST penalties, including interest and penalties under the Québec Taxation Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital-account transactions; Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and does not impose capital controls, exchange-rate ceilings, or limitations on profit remittance; dividends, management fees, and loan repayments may be transferred freely, subject only to normal tax withholding and anti-money-laundering verification

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts; there is no obligation to repatriate, surrender, or convert foreign-currency proceeds; Canadian exporters may retain funds in foreign-currency bank accounts, and all conversion decisions are commercial

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC; Canada maintains no central foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium — European EMIs and offshore digital payment platforms are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act; they cannot accept deposits or issue Canadian bank accounts without operating as registered money-service businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC; cross-border payment fintech (including Wise) may be used as payment corridors for low-value transfers, but major corporate treasury functions must remain with a Tier-1 Canadian chartered bank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under federal Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. No separate Quebec provincial dividend withholding tax is applied on the same remittance because Part XIII is a federal tax. Treaty relief can reduce the rate to 5% for an U.S. parent owning at least 10% of voting shares, 15% for portfolio investors, and analogous rates under other comprehensive bilateral DTAs (e.g., 15% for Germany, 10% for France under varying conditions).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Both T2 and CO-17 must be filed no later than six months after the corporate fiscal year-end. A Quebec business corporation resident in Quebec is required to file both returns. Payments of instalments for Quebec and federal corporate taxes generally follow quarterly/ monthly rules based on prior-year tax.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after fiscal year-end for both federal and Quebec returns. If a corporation carries on no activity, a nil return is still due within six months of the year-end. Quebec corporations must also file the separate Registre des entreprises (REQ) annual declaration under the Business Corporations Act.

For aggressive evasion, gross-negligence penalty is 50% of understated tax.
CO-17 Section A – Identification Quebec enterprise registration number (NEQ), business number, business address, sector code, and fiscal period. A non-resident corporation with a PE in Quebec must obtain an NEQ before filing and indicate the Quebec establishment address. Foreign directors acting as Quebec registrants may require a Quebec-issued authorization. Late filing in Quebec: 5% of the balance due plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; repeat failures attract 20% for chronic non-compliance; interest compounded daily at Quebec's prescribed redemption rate. Willful omission carries civil penalties of up to C$50,000 and penal fines under the Tax Administration Act.
CO-17 Part 2 – Taxable Income Compute Quebec taxable income from Canadian and Quebec active business income, adjusting for provincial differences (e.g., Quebec-specific political contributions, specialized loss carryovers). A non-resident should allocate income to the Quebec PE following arm's-length internal pricing. Cross-border marine survey fees charged by a non-resident head office must satisfy Part XIII withholding and Quebec transfer-pricing documentation. Late filing in Quebec: 5% of the balance due plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; repeat failures attract 20% for chronic non-compliance; interest compounded daily at Quebec's prescribed redemption rate. Willful omission carries civil penalties of up to C$50,000 and penal fines under the Tax Administration Act.
CO-17 Lines 224–251 – Tax Rates and Credits Quebec general corporate rate of 11.5%; small business deduction and investment tax credits are computed on Schedule P. Treaty relief claimed federally on Form NR301 is also respected by Quebec only if the treaty applies under Quebec domestic adoption. Non-resident corporations should attach the federal T2 and Schedule 1 to substantiate the consistency. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Form T106 reporting applies to reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents that exceed C$1,000,000 in aggregate per year. Local File and Master File documentation must be prepared contemporaneously when an audit-level risk of transfer-pricing adjustments exists; CbC notification is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue over C$750 million. Documentation must include functional analysis, economic analysis, comparable searches, and prices/margins for every controlled cross-border service or reinsurance arrangement. Marine survey corp. commonly transacts with foreign insurers, underwriters, or affiliated survey companies and must satisfy these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies OECD/G20 economic substance principles through statutory GAAR (section 245 Income Tax Act) and the transfer-pricing recharacterization power (section 247). No standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) registry exists in Quebec, but a corporate entity with no real or qualified personnel, minimal presence, and no board or management activity in Quebec may be disregarded as a conduit; treaty benefits can be denied under 'beneficial owner' and 'permanent establishment' clauses. The CRA and Revenu Québec assess commercial reality, local decision-making, personnel, assets, office premises and business purpose. A marine insurance surveyor must maintain an operational office in Quebec or, if foreign-owned, demonstrate meaningful and substantive survey activity inside Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and forfeiture of property related to the offence. Civil gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of understated tax; interest on unpaid balances. Quebec penalties up to C$50,000 on the corporation and the registration may be cancelled by Revenu Québec.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and section 1018 of the Quebec Taxation Act for corporate tax, interest, and penalties when they direct, authorize, or acquiesce in false filings. Directors can face personal criminal conviction, travel restrictions, asset seizure, and veil-piercing in Quebec civil proceedings where the corporation is an instrumentality of shareholder fraud.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Shell Conduit

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-assess at the arm's-length price and also impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net understatement where the amount understated exceeds C$5 million or reasonable basis requirement is not met. GAAR can deny the tax benefit and levy penalties of up to 50% of the benefit. Additionally, denial of Part XIII exemption for foreign-source allocations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham transfer-pricing arrangement risk civil joint liability for re-assessed tax, plus criminal exposure for tax evasion if false documentation is presented. Canadian courts may pierce the corporate veil to attach personal assets where the company is a mere facade and used for fraud.
Failure to Withhold, Remit, and Report Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the unremitted withholding amount, plus daily interest and a 20% civil penalty for repeated failure. Non-remittance is a criminal offence under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, with fines up to C$25,000 and imprisonment up to two years. Quebec QST and payroll remittances carry distinct penal assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes; no defence of good faith is available unless they exercised due diligence to prevent the failure. CRA issues director liability certificates that become judicial judgments, allowing garnishment of personal bank accounts and real property.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Shell Company Framework

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to C$1 million for corporations for AML/beneficial ownership omissions; criminal sanctions and fines for willful contravention. Revenu Québec can strike the company from the register, rendering contracts unenforceable; directors then face civil liability for corporate obligations.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing occurs in Quebec under civil law when the company is created to commit fraud or misuse corporate personality. Directors may be ordered to personally restore assets misappropriated, receive travel bans, and are amenable to extradition for cross-border tax fraud or money laundering. Individual officers can be imprisoned for up to 10 years under the Income Tax Act after indictment.
