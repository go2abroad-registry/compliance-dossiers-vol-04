Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Federal registration portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and 'Business Registration Online (BRO)' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance handles provincial employer health tax and Ontario-specific tax accounts; the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch registers the limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Act (Form 3) as a non-tax statutory prerequisite.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Ensure the Ontario limited partnership (LP) is validly constituted: deliver Form 3 (Declaration) to the Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch and pay the registration fee. Step 2 – Obtain the federal Business Number (BN): complete Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts' and select program account 'RT' for GST/HST. Filing channels: Business Registration Online (BRO) for representatives with an access code; by mail/fax to the CRA (address prescribed for non-resident applicants); or by telephone (1-800-959-5525 inside Canada / 1-800-665-0354 from the United States, or the applicable non-resident line). A non-resident partnership may use its foreign head-office address and, if applicable, a Canadian agent's address. Step 3 – CRA issues the BN and RT account; the GST/HST account effective date is the earlier of (a) the date registration is requested and (b) the date the LP ceases to be a 'small supplier' (i.e., exceeds CAD 30,000 in aggregate taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, in a single quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters). Step 4 – Open an RP (payroll) program account if the LP employs individuals working in Ontario, and register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for employer health tax if the Ontario payroll exceeds the annual exemption. Step 5 – File annual T5013 Partnership Information Return with the CRA regardless of the partners' residence, reporting income allocations to all limited and general partners. Step 6 – Non-resident corporate or individual limited partners obtain or confirm their own Canadian tax identifiers and file Part I returns (T2/T1) on their allocated shares of Canadian-source business income; partnership business income allocated to non-resident partners is taxed on a net basis in Part I (no Part XIII withholding applies to such allocations).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal and Ontario income tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level but is taxed only at the partner level (Income Tax Act, s. 96). The LP itself is not a taxpayer for income tax. A limited partner's deductible losses are restricted to the amount that the partner has at risk in the partnership (ITA s. 96(2.1)). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal and Ontario income tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level but is taxed only at the partner level (Income Tax Act, s. 96). The LP itself is not a taxpayer for income tax. However, for GST/HST purposes, the Excise Tax Act (ETA) deems the partnership to be a separate 'person', so the LP is the registrant and must charge, collect, and remit HST on taxable Canadian supplies; the partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's net tax. A limited partner's deductible losses are restricted to the amount that the partner has at risk in the partnership (ITA s. 96(2.1)). A non-resident limited partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP remains taxable in Canada under Part I on its allocated share, subject to any applicable income tax treaty.

Available election forms

Not applicable — Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' or comparable elective classification regime; entity classification follows statutory legal form. Relevant elections available to an Ontario LP include: (i) the partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4), whereby the LP (through a designated member) may elect a fiscal period that differs from the default calendar year, filed with the annual T5013; (ii) voluntary registration as a GST/HST registrant by a non-resident small-supplier partnership under ETA s. 240(1), which permits immediate ITC recovery on Canadian inputs; (iii) a documented partner allocation agreement in the limited partnership agreement that satisfies CRA's reasonable allocation requirements. Hybrid-mismatch arrangements (e.g., an Ontario LP that is fiscally transparent in Canada but opaque in a foreign partner's jurisdiction) are addressed automatically by ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5, which disallow deductions to the extent of double-dip or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; these provisions are mandatory, not elective, and must be considered when the LP's partners include US or other foreign entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person is a 'small supplier' and is not required to register unless the total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or in a single calendar quarter. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Zero-rated export revenues count toward the threshold. A small supplier may voluntarily register; once registered, the registrant must generally remain registered for at least one year. For the Ontario LP monetizing hyper-casual games through AdMob/AppLovin, the CAD 30,000 threshold will almost always be crossed, making registration mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% HST rate (zero-rating) applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 10 where the Ontario LP supplies a service or intangible personal property to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST under Part IX of the ETA, provided the supply is not performed in Canada (in the case of services) and is not in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the non-resident recipient is not an individual present in Canada at the time the service is rendered. For a hyper-casual game publisher, the following supplies qualify as zero-rated exports: (i) the supply of ad-placement and ad-monetization services to non-resident ad networks (AdMob, AppLovin) under the network's publisher agreement; (ii) the license or transfer of digital game software/intangible property to non-resident end users or distributors, delivered or made available electronically. To claim zero-rating, the LP must verify and document the recipient's non-resident status (normal place of business outside Canada, foreign billing address, non-Canadian incorporation), issue an invoice stating 'Zero-rated supply — ETA Schedule VI, Part V', exclude HST, and retain contemporaneous export evidence. Input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian inputs are fully available to the LP because the zero-rated export supplies are 'commercial activity' under ETA s. 240; the GST/HST refund arises through the normal ITC mechanism, not a special refund program.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) — Not applicable to zero-rated digital services and electronically delivered intangibles (no physical goods cross the border); required only if the LP later exports media or hardware.
  • Bill of Lading — Not applicable to a digital services export; the functional equivalent is the platform payout/ad-serving statement issued by AdMob or AppLovin to the LP evidencing the offshore delivery of advertising inventory and the computation of publication revenue.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — CRA-expected bank record, in respect of the relevant payout to the LP, showing the aggregate foreign-currency payment credited to the LP's Canadian or offshore bank account and the conversion of ad-network proceeds (USD or other FX) into CAD.
  • Signed publisher service agreement or platform terms executed with AdMob/AppLovin identifying the non-resident counterparty and the service scope (ad mediation, placement, bid stream access).
  • GST/HST-compliant invoices issued to the non-resident ad networks, marked with the LP's BN/RT account, describing the zero-rated supply, and stating 'Zero-rated: ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V'.
  • Monthly ad-network payout reports and bank deposit slips reconciled to the LP's sales ledger (the electronic invoice/clearance substitute for a customs declaration).
  • Non-resident status evidence for each ad-network counterparty: certificate of incorporation, foreign business registry extract, or written confirmation of the network's principal place of business outside Canada.

Not applicable — Canada has no mandatory real-time tax clearance, fiscal QR/stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. The CRA's national e-invoicing framework is voluntary (announced 2023 rollout based on the Peppol PINT standard). A GST/HST registrant must issue and retain invoices containing the prescribed particulars under ETA s. 169(1) and the Invoicing Requirements Regulations, including the registrant's BN/RT number, the date of supply, a description of the service, the consideration, and a statement that the supply is zero-rated where applicable. GST/HST returns (GST34-1) must be filed electronically by all registrants through GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or EDI; paper filing is available only in narrow exceptions. No API-based clearance or pre-approval of transactions is imposed.

CRA applies heightened scrutiny to zero-rated export claims in the digital-services sector. Where the export documentation is not contemporaneous or the ad-network counterparty's non-resident status is not substantiated, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable in Canada by applying 13% HST on the consideration, with retroactive effect from the date of supply, plus statutory interest (daily compound) under the Income Tax Act (s. 161) and the Excise Tax Act. Each GST/HST return that omits HST on a supply later determined to be taxable may be reassessed within the normal 4-year reassessment period; misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default extends the window to 7 years. Penalties include: ETA s. 280(1) gross-negligence penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated); ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty for income-tax understatement (50% of the understated tax) where export income is improperly excluded or zero-rated without basis; and late-filing penalties under ETA s. 280.1 and ITA s. 162 for failure to file T5013 or GST/HST returns. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is applied by the CRA as an evidentiary screen: export receivables remaining unpaid beyond 180 days may be treated as lacking economic substance and the zero-rating may be denied, leading to the full 13% HST clawback, interest, and penalties. The LP must therefore enforce payment terms with foreign ad networks, document collection efforts, and recognize receivables in accordance with ITA s. 12(2) timing rules to avoid recharacterization of the export consideration as a taxable Canadian-source financing or service amount.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There is no statutory exchange control, no general requirement for government approval to remit dividends, interest, royalties, or profits, and no restriction on reinvestment or withdrawal of foreign direct investment. Non-resident partners in a Canadian limited partnership are subject to Canadian withholding tax on certain passive payments (e.g., Part XIII for amounts deemed to be interest, rents, or royalties) and to ordinary income tax on business carried on within Canada; however, partnership allocations of active business profits to non-residents may be taxable as Canadian-source income, requiring careful treaty analysis. Profit remittance is operationally unrestricted, provided the partnership has sufficient after-tax cash and Canadian tax clearance where mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export-proceeds surrender requirement in Canada. Exporters and service providers, including a hyper-casual games limited partnership receiving AdMob and AppLovin USD payouts, may retain export receipts in any currency, hold them in foreign-currency bank accounts, or convert them at market rates. Cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, but no conversion ratio or mandatory deposit timeline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks; FINTRAC administers anti-money laundering and cross-border transaction reporting. Canada operates no foreign-exchange control agency and leaves FX transactions to the market within the Financial Administration Act framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium legal viability with significant regulatory constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., non-Canadian e-money institutions) are not permitted to provide payment services to Canadian residents without compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) as a foreign money services business (MSB) or by partnering with a Schedule I Canadian bank. They cannot serve as the sole clearing rail for partnership revenue because Canadian-dollar settlement must occur through a recognized payment system. Feasibility rating: 'Medium' for supplementary treasury purposes, provided the EMI is registered with FINTRAC, demonstrates a Canadian agent/partnership, and does not replace a full-service Tier I bank where director liability for funds segregation is appetite-expanding.

Capital contributions from foreign limited partners must be wired via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent using the partnership's cash letter of instructions from its Tier I bank. Since Canada has no mandatory purpose-code framework, the remittance information field must state 'LIMITED PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — LP NAME' to enable internal categorization. The incoming wire amount must match the capital schedule in the LP agreement; the bank may require are Note-Copy of the LP agreement and capitalization table. All cross-border transactions greater than CAD 10,000 will be reported to FINTRAC. For hyper-casual games, capital bases are normally small (under CAD 1 million) but the same verifiability applies. Where the contribution is derived from platform profits earned offshore, the source-of-funds evidence includes the offshore parent's bank statement and board resolution designating the remittance as partnership capital.Primary clearing is via SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, contractor disbursements, and periodic distributions to non-resident limited partners. CAD payments are settled through Lynx (Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system) or via Automated Clearing Settlement System for low-value items. Trade settlements for overseas services or licensors use Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) plus MIRO/MT400 acceptance advice where the gaming publisher requires documentary assurance. For USD disbursements, the partnership uses a Tier I bank's US-dollar correspondent bank (typically JP Morgan or BNY Mellon for Canadian banks) to avoid double conversion; both parties settle on CLS where relevant for FX. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium legal viability with significant regulatory constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., non-Canadian e-money institutions) are not permitted to provide payment services to Canadian residents without compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) as a foreign money services business (MSB) or by partnering with a Schedule I Canadian bank. They cannot serve as the sole clearing rail for partnership revenue because Canadian-dollar settlement must occur through a recognized payment system. Feasibility rating: 'Medium' for supplementary treasury purposes, provided the EMI is registered with FINTRAC, demonstrates a Canadian agent/partnership, and does not replace a full-service Tier I bank where director liability for funds segregation is appetite-expanding.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% combined general rate (federal Part I 15% + Ontario general corporate 10%); the Ontario Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent, with income allocated to partners who bear tax at their respective marginal rates

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders/partners; for the Ontario LP, distributions of Canadian-source business or property income to non-resident limited partners are generally subject to 25% Part XIII withholding, subject to a 15% withholding regime under subsection 218.1(1) for non-resident individuals earning business income through the partnership, and further subject to treaty reduction

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 2,000,000 in aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length transactions requiring Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents); CAD 1,000,000 threshold for outstanding loans or advances requiring T106 disclosure; CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold for Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4643) filing by the ultimate parent of an MNE group

Economic substance scope: Canada has no jurisdiction-specific Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE; however, CRA applies economic-substance analysis through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), treaty anti-abuse provisions (beneficial ownership, limitation-on-benefits), and the OECD BEPS framework; a no-substance Ontario LP without an active office, employees, or real decision-making functions for game-publishing operations is vulnerable to GAAR recharacterization, transfer-pricing adjustments under section 247, and mandatory disclosure regime penalties; Bill C-59 amendments (in force for transactions entered into on or after January 1, 2025) impose a 50% GAAR penalty on abusive avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Section 239 Income Tax Act: summary conviction - fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; indictable conviction - fine up to 200% of tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under subsection 163(2); failure-to-report-income penalty of 10% of unreported amounts (20% on second occurrence); compound interest charged on all outstanding balances; CRA may reopen returns beyond the normal 3-year reassessment period (10 years for misrepresentation, unlimited for fraud)

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; subsection 160 joint liability applies to non-arm's length transfers of property made to avoid tax; a limited partner who participates in management or control of the LP forfeits limited-liability protection under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act; criminal prosecutions target directing minds with personal fines, confiscation orders, travel-ban bail conditions, passport surrender, and extradition exposure
Transfer Pricing and Non-Arm's Length Transactions

Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) discretionary transfer-pricing adjustments; penalty under subsection 247(7) of 10% of the net adjustment (reduced by the CAD 10,000 tolerance) where documentation is not contemporaneously prepared; CbC-report failure penalties of CAD 2,000 per month; general foreign-exchange and compound-interest exposure; new 50% GAAR penalty for abusive avoidance transactions entered into after 2024

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or implemented the non-arm's length arrangement may be assessed under subsection 160 for property transferred without fair-market consideration; CRA may freeze corporate bank accounts and seek court orders compelling payment; a corporate general partner's directors may be personally liable for the LP's unremitted payroll and HST amounts; criminal charges may follow where documentation is intentionally falsified
Shell Company, Sham, and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 denies the tax benefit and, for transactions entered into on or after January 1, 2025, imposes a 50% penalty on the tax benefit; the common-law sham doctrine recharacterizes facade transactions with retroactive effect; mandatory disclosure regime (sections 237.3-237.6) imposes penalties up to CAD 100,000 for failing to report reportable or notifiable transactions; partnership allocations lacking economic substance may be ignored and reallocated by CRA

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the veil where the LP is a pure facade or agent of its general partner; the general partner (and its directors when the general partner is a corporation) becomes personally liable for the LP's tax debts; officers may face criminal fraud charges (Criminal Code section 380) where the arrangement defrauds the Crown; travel bans, passport revocation, and arrest warrants may issue, and Ontario courts may grant anti-dissipation injunctions freezing personal assets
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation (up to CAD 1,000,000 for certain repeat violations); criminal conviction for operating an unregistered MSB carries fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; CRA and FINTRAC joint enforcement may result in asset seizure as proceeds of crime

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be subject to FINTRAC administrative penalties personally for failing to implement a compliance program; knowingly facilitating money laundering creates personal criminal liability under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years; personal assets traceable to unlawful proceeds are subject to restraint orders and forfeiture; courts may impose travel restrictions and require surrender of passports pending prosecution
