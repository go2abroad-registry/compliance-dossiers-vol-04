Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal Business Registration Online (BRO) portal for Business Number (BN) issuance; Revenu Québec (RQ) — 'clics affaires' portal and the Registre des entreprises du Québec (REQ) for the NEQ (Numéro d'entreprise du Québec) and QST account administration. RQ administers both the federal GST (TPS) and the provincial QST under a collection and harmonization agreement with the CRA.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine small supplier status — an LP whose total worldwide taxable supplies are $30,000 CAD or less per calendar quarter and per previous four quarters is exempt from mandatory registration. Step 2: Non-resident LPs apply to CRA via Form RC3911 'GST/HST and QST Registration for Non-Resident Persons' or through BRO; CRA issues a BN with program account suffix 'RT' for the partnership as a distinct registrant 'person' under ETA s.272.1. Step 3: In Quebec, the LP must obtain an NEQ from REQ (required for any operating business), then register for QST by linking the QST account to the federal BN (suffix 'TQ'), through RQ's clics affaires. Step 4: If permanent establishment exists in Quebec, the LP must also file provincial income tax administrative records; non-resident partners without a PE file directly with CRA. Step 5: Use RQ's CLIC registration package to elect electronic returns and designate a representative in Quebec (mandatory for non-resident registrants).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA s.96, the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax: no entity-level federal or Quebec income tax; each partner includes allocated profit/loss on a calendar-year or partnership fiscal-year basis. For GST/HST/QST, the LP is a deemed 'person' that is a separate registrant and must charge, collect, and remit tax on taxable supplies; partners do not jointly remit partnership GST. Quebec treats the LP identically for QST under the Quebec Taxation Act Art. 772 and the QST rules, preserving flow-through characterization and avoiding hybrid mismatch in the Canada-U.S./Canada-EU context.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' regime exists in Canada. Elections available: (a) partnership fiscal year end election under ITA s.249.1 to use a non-calendar year (subject to deferral rules); (b) s.216 election for non-resident partners to report rental income on a net basis; (c) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s.228.1) for eligible small suppliers, including vintage resellers, using simplified remittance on gross sales; (d) Streamlined Accounting and electronic filing election for non-resident digital service vendors (ETA s.180.1) effective July 1, 2021; (e) ETA s.273 joint election for closely related corporations/partnerships, applicable to the LP when a corporation is the general partner; (f) QST Input Tax Rebate election for resident partners in a selected financial partnership; (g) Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4–18.6 apply automatically where a partner's residence jurisdiction treats the LP as opaque — no opt-out election is available; the LP must document partner country classifications to prevent disallowed deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: 30,000 CAD small supplier threshold (per single calendar quarter and previous four consecutive quarters), measured on worldwide taxable supplies; QST: also 30,000 CAD measured on worldwide taxable supplies, with the same quarterly test. For non-resident digital platform vendors of intangible and services to Canadian consumers (as 'specified supplies'), the simplified registration regime requires registration once Canadian consumer sales exceed 30,000 CAD, with an annual return and simplified rate tables; this threshold is also applied to online consignment and secondhand luxury resale platforms operating into Quebec.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s.1–5 when the LP exports the vintage luxury goods from Canada, the goods are delivered to a non-resident consignee outside Canada, and the export is documented (proof of removal from Canada within 60 days after delivery to the carrier). Services exports: zero-rated under s.7 when (i) the service is supplied to a non-resident person, (ii) the service is not performed in Canada for a Canadian resident, and (iii) the client is not in Canada at the time the service is performed; for consignment services (listing, authentication, escrow and resale of luxury goods on behalf of foreign sellers) these criteria are met if the foreign seller/consignor is a non-resident. For a non-resident consignor, the LP may act as a commissionaire and charge its commission; the commission is zero-rated when made to the non-resident principal. Retrospective application: the zero-rated export status must be claimed at the time of the sale with cumulative documentation; failure to keep export evidence within 6 months of filing means the supply is re-characterized as taxable in Canada.

  • Commercial Invoice — full legal name of LP, buyer, unit price, quantity, HS tariff code for each luxury item (e.g., HS 42.02, 71.13, 91.01), and declaration 'Exported from Canada for consumption outside Canada'.
  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Customs Coding Form B13A (paper) or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) / Electronic Export Reporting via the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), filed for shipments above the $2,000 or $2,500 exemption threshold; date and reference number must match invoice.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — through carrier bill showing the LP as exporter/consignor, the foreign buyer as consignee, and a clearly designated Canadian port of loading and foreign port of discharge.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Payment Evidence — SWIFT MT103, Escrow.com settlement, or Payment processor deposit record, demonstrating the funds received in the LP's Canadian or foreign bank account from the non-resident buyer; note that the $30,000 CAD small supplier threshold applies to turnover, and repatriation of proceeds is mandatory within the fiscal year.
  • Packing List — showing carton marks, weights, serial numbers for luxury watches/handbags, and destination address.
  • For exported services: signed service agreement, purchase order, and invoice with delivery address outside Canada and a statement of the non-resident recipient's role (universal no-placement rules).
  • Carrier's proof of removal: leaving certificate or final delivery receipt signed by the foreign buyer or customs clearing broker abroad.

Canada has no mandatory government-issued digital clearance, API stamping, or real-time fiscal control regime. The CRA and RQ operate a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing infrastructure using the PEPPOL network; adoption is optional but encouraged for large retailers. RQ mandates that all invoices issued in Quebec be drawn up in French (Charter of the French Language), and must contain: the legal full name of the LP, the trading name if different, the BN and GST/HST/QST account numbers, date, description of the supply, the amount of consideration, and the tax amount charged (or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated or exempt). For QST purposes, the invoice must state 'TPS 5% / TVQ 9.975%' on separate lines; where a customer is non-resident and the supply is zero-rated, the invoice must state '0% TPS / 0% TVQ' with the export documentation cross-referenced.

CRA and RQ jointly audit export zero-rating claims under ETA s.296; normal reassessment period is 4 years from the filing date, extended indefinitely to 7 years where misrepresentation or negligence is shown. If the documentary bundle is incomplete or the 60-day export rule is breached, the zero-rating is invalidated: the LP is assessed output GST/HST and QST at the standard rate (14.975%) on the full sales price, plus compound daily interest (s.280). Penalties: gross negligence under s.285.1 is 50% of the amount of tax that was sought to be avoided or incorrectly refunded; evasion under s.327 (willful fraud) carries a fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment of up to 5 years. In Quebec, RQ may also issue director/partner liability notices, revoke the LP's registration, and add a 25% administrative fine for false QST claims. Any refund obtained through fraudulent export zero-rating is clawed back with retroactive effect, and the exporter may be excluded from PEPPOL e-invoicing and export tariff benefits. For consignment resale, if the LP claims zero-rating on goods that are not actually exported (e.g., delivered to a warehouse in Quebec for later sale to a Canadian resident buyer), the full tax applies, and the LP is liable for penalties and foreign exchange translation losses based on the CRA monthly average exchange rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) enjoys full de jure and de facto convertibility on both the current account and the capital account, with no statutory requirement for Central Bank approval of cross-border remittances or capital transactions. Canada maintains an independent floating exchange rate regime; the Bank of Canada does not intervene in foreign exchange markets and no quantitative convertibility ceilings exist. For the Quebec Limited Partnership (CA_LP), profit and capital distributions to non-resident limited partners are freely remittable in CAD or USD. A non-resident partner of a Canadian partnership remains taxable in Canada on its share of Canadian-source business income under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and Part IV of the Taxation Act (Quebec), except where a tax treaty limits Canadian taxing rights. Distributions representing return of contributed capital and, in most cases, allocated business income are not subject to Part XIII withholding; Part XIII withholding applies only to enumerated 'passive' payments (e.g., rents, royalties, management-fee income, and certain interest) sourced to Canada. Practical banking constraints are limited to institution-level documentary due diligence and FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more, not to any restriction on capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or on any foreign currency earned by Canadian residents. A resident exporter, such as the CA_LP engaged in cross-border online consignment resale, may retain US dollars and other foreign currency indefinitely in bank accounts at Canadian banks or in foreign correspondent accounts. The only enforceable obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting of all cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and associated regulations, which the reporting bank discharges, and (ii) the declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency of physical import/export of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or greater. No export documentation other than the standard commercial invoice, customs declaration, and shipping records is required for FX purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and the sole issuer of Canadian bank notes under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2). Canada has not maintained a separate foreign exchange control administration since the abolition of exchange controls on December 14, 1951. The Department of Finance Canada and Global Affairs Canada jointly administer cross-border financial sanctions and trade controls; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) is the financial intelligence unit for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing reporting, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federal Schedule I banks. The Quebec jurisdiction adds the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) for securities and provincial financial-sector conduct, and Revenu Québec for provincial tax and QST (Quebec Sales Tax) compliance, although banking and FX are federally regulated.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an auxiliary payment rail; LOW as a primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or any European-licensed e-money institution) may lawfully serve as collection/disbursement accelerators for the CA_LP, but they cannot replace a Canadian Schedule I bank account because the partnership must hold its corporate operating and escrow liquidity with a prudentially regulated and FINTRAC-reporting Canadian financial institution to support the commercial payment rails (Stripe and Escrow_Pay) and to preserve clean audit trails. Legal constraints include: (i) any EMI processing CAD or holding accounts for a Canadian resident entity must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, or operate through a Canadian-registered partner; (ii) unregistered foreign EMIs are prohibited from maintaining 'customer payment accounts' for Canadian-domiciled merchants; (iii) the CA_LP must not use an EMI as a pass-through for customer settlement funds because commingling would trigger a bank compliance rejection of the merchant's striped settlement reserves. Wise Business and Payoneer are acceptable as sub-rails for small-value foreign payouts, provided the principal treasury ledger remains at a Tier-1 Canadian bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). The limited partnership is fiscally transparent; no entity-level tax is imposed on the LP itself. A corporate partner pays the combined rate on its allocable share of net business income; individual partners pay graduated personal rates (2024 top combined marginal rate in Quebec approx. 53.31%). Foreign partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are subject to Part I tax on the Canadian-source business income attributable to the LP's Quebec permanent establishment.
