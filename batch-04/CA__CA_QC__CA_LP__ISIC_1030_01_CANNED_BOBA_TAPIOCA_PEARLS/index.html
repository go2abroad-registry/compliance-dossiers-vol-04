Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow/deposit account is required for a Quebec limited partnership. The Declaration of Registration must state the amount of money or the value of other property contributed or agreed to be contributed by each general partner; the liability of limited partners is capped at their stated subscribed contribution. Contributions may be by cash, assets or services, and must be objectively quantified in the partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Quebec Fire Safety Act and the Fire Safety Code, including an inspection by the municipal fire prevention service before operation; structural load inspection and building/occupancy permit by the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ) for the canning and aseptic tubing lines; and workplace clearance from the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST) covering machine guarding, emergency exits, ammonia refrigeration safety where used, and the employer's prevention program (programme de prévention).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow/deposit account is required for a Quebec limited partnership. The Declaration of Registration must state the amount of money or the value of other property contributed or agreed to be contributed by each general partner; the liability of limited partners is capped at their stated subscribed contribution. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) (Agence du revenu du Canada) — federal GST/HST and income tax; Revenu Québec — Québec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial registration; portals: CRA My Business Account / Revenu Québec Mon dossier d'entreprise

E-government filing portal

Stage 1: Register the limited partnership (société en commandite) with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the Québec enterprise number (NEQ). Stage 2: Apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account using Form RC1 or online through My Business Account; select 'Partnership' type and file for each partner where required. Stage 3: Register with Revenu Québec for a QST account (form LM-1-V) using the NEQ/BN; the registration is mandatory when the partnership makes taxable supplies in Québec and exceeds the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold. Stage 4: Obtain a Canada customs client identifier (CCN) via CBSA for export clearance if not already held.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the Québec L.P. is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; the partnership files an information return (T5013) and partners include their shares of income/loss). Entity_Level_Taxation_Status: the partnership itself is not a taxpayer but is a separate registrant for GST/HST/QST. Territorial_Scope: a partnership carrying on business in Canada is subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source business income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income under Part I/Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to treaty relief. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian federal law; classification of a partnership as flow-through is mandatory. Non-resident partners may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act for certain Canadian-source property income. Hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act may apply to deny deductions for cross-border hybrid arrangements, and no election avoids that outcome. For GST/HST, the partnership may elect under section 156 with closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as nil for certain internal transactions, and for QST under the corresponding Québec provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST and QST 'small supplier' threshold); a non-resident partnership that 'carries on business in Canada' may be required to register even below the threshold if it makes taxable supplies in Canada or imports goods into Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada if the supplier (partnership) has evidence of export and the goods are delivered outside Canada as one of the qualifying export modalities (e.g., commercial shipment, household effects, or personal export). The supply must be made by way of sale and actual export must occur in accordance with the Customs Act. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if they are 'offshore services' under section 5 of Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act and are for consumption wholly outside Canada, with performance outside Canada or in some circumstances performed in Canada solely for the non-resident.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / electronic CERS report)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air) or international airway bill
  • Commercial invoice
  • Proof of export (CBSA stamp, terminal receipt, or carrier certificate)
  • Wire/remittance evidence showing payment from the foreign buyer (inward remittance record)

No real-time fiscal clearance or permanent digital stamping is mandated in Canada. E-invoicing is permitted and increasingly adopted via the PEPPOL network, but only paper/electronic invoices that contain the prescribed information (supplier/recipient identification, GST/HST/QST registration numbers, tax amounts) satisfy the legal requirements. CBSA requires electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for controlled goods and for non-controlled exports exceeding CAD 2,000.

CRA and Revenu Québec may deny the zero-rating on audit where documentation is not retained. Fraudulent or gross-negligent claims attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act: ETA s. 285(1) (false statements) up to 25% of the tax amount at issue, ETA s. 280(1) (gross negligence) up to 50% of the refund/credit claimed, plus interest and reassessment within the normal four-year period (not subject to limitation if misrepresentation is attributable to neglect or fraud). Revenu Québec imposes analogous QST penalties and interest under the Taxation Act and QSTA, and criminal prosecution is possible for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance from a Quebec limited partnership is structurally pass-through: the partnership files T5013; non-resident partners are taxable under Income Tax Act Part I on allocated Canadian-source business income. Passive remittances (interest, royalties, deemed dividends or similar payments) attract Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25% rate, reduced by applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US Convention: 15% generally and 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder; Canada-China treaty: 10% dividends, 10% interest and royalties). Treaty relief is obtained via CRA forms NR301/NR302 series; no prior regulatory approval is required for outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed wartime exchange-control regulations in the 1950s; there is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts. Exporters may retain and hold foreign currency freely in domestic or foreign-currency bank accounts. Relevant compliance obligations: (i) CBSA B13A export declaration and CARM accounting where goods are exported, with thresholds applying to goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled/regulated products; (ii) FINTRAC report obligations for large-cash transactions and suspicious transactions; (iii) CBSA cross-border currency reporting where CAD 10,000 or more in currency or monetary instruments is physically transported; (iv) sanctions and denied-party screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act, and applicable United States OFAC rules where US clearing correspondents are involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the exclusive monetary and exchange-rate authority under the Bank of Canada Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. B-2; prudential oversight of banks, FX activities and clearing settlement is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering compliance is enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Quebec market conduct, including money-services business licensing, is administered by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Such entities are not acceptable for escrow-grade capital custody and must be limited to ancillary FX conversion or minor trade disbursements; the primary treasury relationship must remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and Quebec statutory headline rate of 26.5% (federal 15% plus Quebec 11.5%) for a Quebec limited partnership allocated taxable income of Canadian-controlled private corporation or non-resident corporate partner; passive investment income allocation can trigger a higher Quebec rate of 16.7% plus federal 38.7% Part I refundable tax mechanics.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under the Income Tax Act (Canada) for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident partner or corporate shareholder; for a Quebec limited partnership, allocated income is not legally repatriated as a dividend because the partnership is transparent. Instead, profit distributions to a non-resident limited partner are generally not subject to Canadian withholding tax if the partner is not a non-resident of Canada in respect of the partnership income; if the partner is a non-resident, the distribution of Canadian-source partnership income may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% unless treaty-reduced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 3,000,000 in aggregate gross revenue for the fiscal year and transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeding CAD 3,000,000 trigger mandatory contemporaneous documentation; Master File is required when total gross revenue exceeds CAD 500,000,000 and cross-border related-party transactions exceed CAD 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the OECD Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) used in zero-tax jurisdictions, but the CRA can recharacterize transactions under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and treaty anti-abuse rules. A Quebec limited partnership must demonstrate real business presence, operational decision-making in Canada, and substantive manufacturing activity for canned boba tapioca pearls to avoid GAAR reassessment and treaty-shopping denial. The partnership must maintain payroll, physical premises, and production records in Quebec.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the federal tax evaded plus 50% of Quebec tax evaded, gross negligence penalty of $100 or 50% of understated tax, plus interest; criminal prosecution carries a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years under section 239 of the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they knew or were grossly negligent; CRA can issue a director's liability assessment, freeze personal bank accounts, impose travel bans under tax collection procedures, and pursue civil restitution.
Failure to File or Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months (federal); repeat failure penalty of 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months; Quebec imposes parallel penalties and interest; failure to file a T5013 can attract a $25/day penalty per partner with a maximum of $2,500.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to cause the partnership to file or who sign false statements can be subject to penalties for false statements under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act and personal fines up to $100,000 plus imprisonment of up to two years; Quebec penal provisions mirror these under the Taxation Act.
Transfer Pricing and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA can recharacterize the transfer price and assess additional tax plus 10% to 25% transfer pricing penalty on the adjustment amount; the penalty is 10% for non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 3,000,000 and 25% if a late or inadequate documentation request was made. Treaty relief can be denied and transactions subject to GAAR reassessment with a 50% penalty.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors and officers who sign transfer pricing self-assessment declarations that are knowingly false face penalties of up to $100,000 and imprisonment up to two years; they may also be personally assessed for the penalties if they authorized the false transfer pricing documentation.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership allocation under GAAR and attribute the partnership income to the non-resident partner directly; penalties for false statements and GST/HST can apply. Canada can lift the treaty benefit and impose a 25% Part XIII withholding tax on deemed distributions. The Quebec business registration can be cancelled for failure to maintain a head office or place of business.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell corporate general partner are personally liable for corporate and partnership tax debts if they signed the returns or exercised control; courts can impose civil restitution, freeze assets, and ban directors from serving as directors in Canada for up to five years; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment of up to five years.
AML and Cross-Border Remittance Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals per violation; failure to register as a money services business when providing payment services can lead to criminal fines and imprisonment up to five years. FINTRAC can disclose information to CRA.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct, authorize, or participate in AML violations can be personally criminally liable and subjected to fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years; civil restitution to the Crown for unreported taxes can be enforced against personal assets.
