E-government filing portal

Foreign corporations file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and CRA Program Accounts) or use the CRA My Business Account online registration with a Canadian director/agent. The entity is assigned a Business Number (BN) and a 9-digit GST/HST program account suffix 'RT'. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada may register voluntarily. The BN must be quoted on all GST/HST returns, import/export documents, and fiscal correspondence. Evidence of foreign incorporation and Ontario extra-provincial registration is required.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada imposes no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no Central Bank approval for the repatriation of capital or the remittance of profits. Dividends paid by an Ontario corporation to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement; franchise royalties and technical services fees are likewise subject to 25% statutory withholding, reducible to 10% under treaty where the beneficial owner qualifies. No prior exchange-control authorization is required for profit repatriation, but the withholding tax must be remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) on behalf of the payee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada maintains no statutory timeline for the repatriation, surrender, or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. An Ontario bakery exporter or central-kitchen operator may retain foreign-currency balances in any Canadian chartered bank account without restriction. For treasury compliance, export receipts of CAD 30 or more per transaction must be reported to FINTRAC as large cross-border currency transactions, and exporters must maintain supporting GST/HST zero-rating documentation for exported goods. There is no requirement to convert export proceeds into CAD within any fixed period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange control administration is also exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign investment review is administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (15% federal general rate + 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII Canadian withholding tax) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced or exempted under an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due no later than 6 months after fiscal year-end; tax balance due within 2 months after year-end (3 months for CCPCs claiming the small business deduction)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1A : Gross Sales, Services, and Rentals Aggregate gross revenue from bakery, cafe, franchise royalties, central kitchen deliveries, and all Canadian and foreign-source business revenue attributable to the Canadian establishment. Non-resident corporations report only Canadian-source gross revenue, excluding intercompany amounts; apportion by permanent establishment / Canadian business presence. Non-resident corporations generally cannot access the small business rate; they must apply the full 15% federal rate on Canadian-source active business income. Late filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum 17%); interest on arrears at prescribed rate; failure to file T106 or schedules may incur separate penalties of C$100 per day up to C$25,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File monetary thresholds exist; Canadian CRA's Information Circular 87-2R requires contemporaneous TP documentation. A T106 Information Return is mandatory for non-arm's length transactions with non-residents aggregating C$1 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and the transfer-pricing 'real substance' requirement under s. 247 of the Income Tax Act may disregard transactions lacking business purpose. A permanent establishment and substantive Canadian operations (employees, assets, management) determine treaty protection and source taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of additional tax under the Income Tax Act (subsection 163(2)); criminal prosecution under s. 327 leading to fines up to C$200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated or acquiesced; CRA can obtain a court order freezing assets, prohibiting travel abroad, and imposing restitution. Criminal convictions can trigger forfeiture of personal property.
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) denies the tax benefit and reassesses tax plus interest; a 50% gross negligence penalty may apply. The CRA may also impose transfer pricing adjustments with a 10% civil penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the artificial arrangement are jointly and severally liable under statute; courts can lift the corporate veil and impose personal restitution; a criminal referral can lead to imprisonment.
AML / FINTRAC Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to C$500,000 per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; possible cease-and-desist orders and revocation of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally exposed to penalties if they knowingly failed to establish or implement required AML controls; travel bans and personal fines may be ordered for egregious misconduct.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: An adjustment to arm's length pricing can lead to additional tax, interest, and a 10% transfer-pricing penalty (or 25% for false documentation). Document failure penalties apply under T106 rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign defective T106 returns or knowingly participate in false pricing may be individually liable for penalties; CRA may seek director liability for unremitted taxes and criminal charges for falsifying corporate records.
Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: CRA can assess the full 25% withholding tax against the Canadian payer, plus late payment interest and penalties. For willful default, a 50% penalty under s. 163(2) may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutorily liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s. 227.1; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and pursue personal bank accounts, travel bans, or criminal charges for failure to remit trust funds.
