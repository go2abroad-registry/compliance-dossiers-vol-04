Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online service and My Business Account portal, for federal business numbers, GST/HST accounts, payroll and information-return accounts; and Revenu Québec, including the clicSÉQUR–Entreprises portal and My Account for Businesses, for Quebec sales tax (QST) registration, filings and payments. A Quebec limited partnership must also maintain its civil and enterprise-registration records with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ), which is separate from tax registration.

E-government filing portal

1. Obtain or confirm the Quebec limited partnership's Québec enterprise number (NEQ) through the REQ. 2. Obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online, the CRA non-resident registration process, or an authorized representative; foreign partners may be required to provide formation documents, partnership agreement, registered-office information, responsible-person details and identity documentation. 3. Register the applicable CRA program accounts, principally RT for GST/HST, and any required payroll, import-export or information-reporting accounts. 4. Register for QST with Revenu Québec where the partnership is carrying on business in Quebec or is otherwise required to collect QST, including the applicable rules for non-resident and specified digital-service suppliers. 5. Register for import-export privileges with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), including a RM account where commercial imports or exports require one. 6. Establish electronic filing access, appoint a Canadian or Quebec tax representative where appropriate, and retain records supporting residency, partnership classification, taxable supplies, customer location and export status. A foreign entity does not receive a single Canadian VAT number automatically; the BN, GST/HST account number and QST identification number are separate identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: the partnership computes income and allocates it to partners under the Income Tax Act, while partners generally report their allocated shares. Canada has no United States-style check-the-box election for Canadian partnerships. Classification of a foreign partnership for Canadian purposes is determined under Canadian law and relevant treaty principles, not by an automatic elective form. Partnerships may make or participate in specific statutory elections where applicable, including elections concerning allocation, disposition or treatment of partnership property and income, but these do not convert the partnership into a general corporate taxpayer. Canada has enacted hybrid-mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act; no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification election is available. Foreign partners must separately assess Canadian filing, withholding, treaty, permanent-establishment and section 116 or other source-tax obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, calculated under the applicable small-supplier rules over the prescribed preceding four-quarter period or in the current quarter, is the principal GST/HST registration threshold. Quebec generally uses a CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for QST purposes, subject to specific rules for non-resident and specified digital-service suppliers. Registration can be required earlier where the business ceases to be a small supplier, voluntarily registers, imports taxable commercial goods, or falls within a special digital-economy or non-resident registration rule. Taxable digital subscriptions and app-based reading access supplied to customers in Canada or Quebec may create collection obligations based on customer-location evidence, contractual supply structure and the role of Apple or Google.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated for GST/HST when the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the supplier retains evidence that the goods were exported or delivered for export. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the specific exported-service rules apply, including the general rule for services supplied to a non-resident and performed in relation to matters outside Canada, subject to exclusions and customer-status requirements. Electronically supplied web-fiction subscriptions, app access and interactive reading services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the supplier is Canadian or payment is received from abroad. The supplier must establish customer location, contractual recipient, place of performance and applicable exclusions. Services supplied to Canadian consumers are generally taxable; QST and GST/HST treatment must be analyzed separately, including specified digital-product and non-resident supplier rules.

  • For exported goods: Canadian Export Declaration filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where required
  • Commercial invoice or equivalent accounting document identifying exporter, consignee, description, value, currency, origin and destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record or other carrier evidence showing shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Purchase order, sales contract and delivery terms supporting export eligibility
  • Proof of delivery, customs import record or foreign receipt where needed to substantiate that the goods left Canada
  • For exported services or digital supplies: non-resident customer evidence, billing address and payment data, IP or geolocation evidence where relevant, service agreement, usage records, evidence of offshore performance or foreign recipient status, and documentation supporting the applicable zero-rating provision
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory Canadian GST/HST or QST document; bank payment records may be retained as corroborating evidence but do not independently establish zero-rating

Canada and Quebec do not generally impose a universal real-time e-invoice clearance, government API pre-approval or digital tax-stamp mandate for ordinary GST/HST or QST invoices. Registrants must issue and retain records containing the prescribed supplier identity, applicable GST/HST or QST registration number where required, consideration, tax rate and tax amount, and must retain reliable electronic records for the statutory retention period. CRA and Revenu Québec may accept electronic records if they remain accessible, legible, complete and auditable. GST/HST and QST returns and payments are generally filed electronically where required or where the registrant uses the relevant online portal. CERS electronic export declarations apply to prescribed commercial exports, but they are customs filings rather than VAT-clearance invoices. Apple App Store and Google Play billing may operate as principal, marketplace or payment intermediary depending on contractual facts; the partnership must document whether the platform or the LP is the supplier for indirect-tax purposes.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export zero-rating may be denied or retrospectively assessed where the statutory conditions, customer status, export evidence or service classification are not met. The supplier may become liable for uncollected GST/HST or QST, arrears interest and applicable administrative penalties. False statements, omissions, deliberate tax evasion or fraudulent input-tax-credit or rebate claims may attract civil penalties, including gross-negligence penalties generally calculated by reference to the understated net tax or improperly claimed amount, and criminal prosecution under applicable federal or Quebec law. Customs violations may result in penalties under the Administrative Monetary Penalty System, seizure or forfeiture, reassessment and interest. Voluntary disclosure, correction and documentary remediation should be considered before audit contact where eligibility or filing errors are identified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts; no approval or quota applies to profit remittance, partnership distributions, or repatriation of non-resident capital. For a Quebec limited partnership (CA_LP) with non-resident partners, the only conversion frictions are tax-driven: (i) a 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Canadian Income Tax Act applies to Canadian-source amounts allocated or paid to non-resident partners, reducible to treaty rates (e.g., 15% or 5% under Canada-US Article XI, or 0% for literary royalty flows under Article XII of the Canada-US Treaty); (ii) a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is subject to Part I tax on its Canadian-source business income and must file an NR4/T1044 non-resident return; (iii) USD revenue from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing may be retained in a USD concentration account or converted to CAD without any mandatory surrender. For tax measurement in Quebec, USD receipts are translated into CAD at the Bank of Canada noon exchange rate as of the relevant fiscal date; unrealized FX gains on USD cash balances at year-end are recognized as ordinary income under ITA s.39(2) and do not qualify for capital-gains treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for digital content receipts. Apple and Google IAP settlement proceeds may be held in USD indefinitely or converted at bank-quoted market rates. The sole reporting requirement is informational: any Qualifying Electronic Fund Transfer (QEFTR) in or out of Canada equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour period must be reported by the financial institution to FINTRAC; this reporting does not restrict convertibility or settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian legal tender, while the Department of Finance Canada and FINTRAC administer the complementary regulatory perimeter under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration and issues no FX permits under IMF Article VIII; all current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange-control authorization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) are feasible only as secondary collection or vendor-payout utilities and are not viable as the primary clearing rail for a Quebec LP. Legal constraints: (a) only Payments Canada members, being Schedule I/II banks and select credit unions, may clear Canadian-dollar obligations through Lynx (high-value) and ACSS (retail); an EMI holds no Payments Canada clearing membership; (b) EMI balances constitute e-money obligations, not bank deposits, and are not CDIC-insured; (c) a Quebec entity that operates an FX or electronic-funds remittance ledger, including through an offshore EMI, must register as an RSVM (money-services business) with Revenu Quebec and as a money-services business with FINTRAC; failure to register exposes the CA_LP to administrative monetary penalties; (d) routing Apple/Google IAP proceeds through an EMI before a corporate bank account may cause re-characterization as escrow or agent arrangements for GST/QST and FATCA/CRS purposes; (e) incoming USD to an EMI from non-resident partners may trigger additional FINTRAC QEFTR duplication. Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a collection sweeper and vendor-payout rail, LOW as the sole treasury clearing facade for regulatory resilience.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

CONTRIB. CA_LP NEQ [registration number]'; funds settle into the LP's CAD operating account through Lynx (high-value) or ACSS (retail). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal and Quebec headline corporate rate (15% federal general rate + 11.5% Quebec general rate) for taxable corporations; the CA_LP is a limited partnership and is fiscally transparent, so the partnership itself is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII gross dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. The limited partnership itself makes no dividend withholding because it is pass-through; however, distributions of Canadian-source business profits to non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on a net-basis unless exempted or reduced by tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return on Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory when the aggregate value of transactions between the partnership and a non-arm's length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; CRA also expects contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation for taxpayers with gross revenue of at least CAD 50,000,000 or assets of CAD 250,000,000, and country-by-country reporting (CbCR) applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada and Quebec have no stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as seen in offshore financial centres. However, the Income Tax Act section 245 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) and Quebec's Tax Administration Act section 53 apply a substance-over-form doctrine; CRA and Revenu Québec will disregard artificial filing structures where the LP lacks real employees, physical presence, decision-making authority, and genuine economic risk in Quebec, especially where revenue from Apple/Google digital sales is diverted to a shell foreign entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Blindness (Digital Revenue Suppression)

Statutory sanction: CRA gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest; criminal prosecution under s.239(1) of the Income Tax Act is punishable by a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; Revenu Québec can impose an additional 50% penalty under the Tax Administration Act and seize assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally and severally liable under s.227.1 ITA and section 103 of the Quebec Tax Administration Act for unremitted source deductions, QST, and Part XIII withholdings; where evasion is proven, courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution to CRA/Revenu Québec, issue travel bans under CRA's international enforcement program, and impose imprisonment.
Statutory Filing Failure / Non-resident Partner Oversight

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties of CAD 100 per day to a maximum of CAD 2,500 plus per-slip penalties of CAD 500 for each missing Relevé 15; the partnership may lose the ability to claim expenses or be denied allocation of losses to partners; a non-resident partner who fails to file is subject to the gross-negligence penalty and, for repeated non-compliance, criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner who approved or participated in causing false filings are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid amounts deemed withheld or required to be reported; the deregistration of the LP does not extinguish director liability, and CRA may issue a federal lien or Revenu Québec a legal mortgage over personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Profit Shifting without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassesses to arm's length prices under s.247(2), adds a 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3), and charges prescribed-rate interest compounded daily; expenses may be disallowed outright, and redirected amounts to a foreign parent company may be recharacterized as a taxable dividend subject to 25% Part XIII withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or transmit false transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to gross-negligence penalties under s.163.2; in egregious cases CRA can assess directors jointly and severally for the foreign parent's Canadian tax liability on the basis of de facto control and piercing the corporate veil, and obtain seizure orders against personal bank accounts and real property.
AML / Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLFTA), the entity can be assessed monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for failure to maintain required records; provincial beneficial-ownership violations carry up to CAD 100,000 fines; tax-return disallowance may follow where the true economic owner cannot be demonstrated; in criminal calibrations, digital assets and revenue flows may be restrained or forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for penalties and restitution where the LP is found to be a facade for money laundering; a criminal conviction under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime carries imprisonment up to 10 years; travel bans and asset freezes are regularly issued in multi-jurisdictional extradition proceedings; corporate-veil protection is completely inapplicable to illegal activity.
