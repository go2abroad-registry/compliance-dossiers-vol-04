Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — federal tax authority for corporate income tax, GST/HST and payroll; online portals: CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) and CRA 'My Business Account'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or a Quebec provincial companies statute is a separate taxable 'person' under the Income Tax Act (Part I), the Excise Tax Act (Part IX, GST/HST), the Quebec Taxation Act and the QST Act. It is taxed at the corporate level on worldwide income if resident in Canada (or on Canadian-source income if non-resident), files its own corporate income tax returns (T2 and Quebec CO-17), and is a distinct GST/HST and QST registrant. It is not a pass-through entity. The VC fund is typically a limited partnership (income-tax flow-through) or trust, but the GP corporation is taxed at entity level; carried interest allocated to the GP corporation is corporate income, and management fees charged by the GP to the fund are taxable supplies for GST/HST and QST purposes.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' regime; every corporation is per se a separate taxpayer. Statutory elections available to the GP structure include: (1) ITA s. 216 election — a non-resident corporation may elect to file a Canadian return and be taxed on a net basis on Canadian rental/royalty income (limited relevance to a VC GP); (2) ITA s. 250(2) election — a foreign corporation may elect to be deemed resident in Canada; (3) ETA s. 273 election — closely related corporations may elect to treat taxable supplies made between them as being for nil consideration (GST/HST simplification), mirrored for QST by QST Act s. 264; (4) ETA s. 156 joint venture election — co-venturers may elect one 'operator' to account for GST/HST on a joint venture; (5) Hybrid mismatch rules under Part XII.7 of the ITA apply by statutory default rather than by election, with exclusions and ordering rules that may be documented/electively applied to prevent adjustment where 'dual inclusion income' exists; (6) Quebec: parallel elections under the QST Act and the Taxation Act (Quebec), including nil-consideration elections for closely related persons and the Quebec equivalent of the s. 216 election. Pass-through tax treatment for the fund itself is achieved only by structuring as a limited partnership or trust; no election can convert the GP corporation into a flow-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 240(1): mandatory registration applies to any person (including a non-resident) making or intending to make taxable supplies in Canada unless total worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters plus the current quarter are C$30,000 or less (special attribution rules apply to non-resident suppliers of intangibles and digital services). The QST registration threshold under the QST Act is aligned at C$30,000. A VC GP earning management fees above C$30,000 on a rolling four-quarter basis is required to register for GST/HST and QST; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is also required to obtain a corporate income tax account irrespective of the small supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods: A supply of goods is zero-rated for GST/HST under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA where the supplier exports the goods, delivers them to a common carrier for export, or the non-resident purchaser exports them within 60 days as part of the supply (ETA Sch. VI Pt. V ss. 1-8); the QST Act contains mirror zero-rating provisions (QST Act s. 194 et seq.). The exporter must retain proof of physical export. (2) Services: Advisory, consulting, management and research services supplied by a GP to a non-resident fund are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI Pt. V s. 7 where the recipient is a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST, the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the service is acquired for consumption wholly outside Canada; a parallel rule exists under the QST Act. (3) The GP must obtain and retain 'non-resident status' evidence (foreign tax residence certificate, articles of incorporation, certificate of good standing) and documents demonstrating offshore performance and consumption (contracts, engagement letters, invoices, time records, emails, board materials). (4) Input tax credits (GST/HST) and QST input tax refunds may be claimed on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports. (5) Management fees charged to a Canadian-resident fund (including a Canadian-resident limited partnership) remain taxable at the combined 14.975% rate and cannot be zero-rated merely because the fund's investors are non-resident.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-authorization of invoices exists at the federal or Quebec level. Invoices must satisfy documentary evidence rules (ETA s. 169/286 and QST Act s. 482) but may be issued on paper, PDF, EDI (EDIFACT) or XML. Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian e-Invoice standard based on the European PEPPOL framework on a voluntary basis; Revenu Quebec accepts PEPPOL and interoperable e-invoicing formats. Mandatory electronic filing: GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA 'My Business Account' / GST/HST NETFILE where annual gross revenue exceeds C$1 million; QST returns must be filed electronically through Revenu Quebec's e-service portal where QST turnover exceeds C$1 million. Electronic payment of GST/HST and QST is mandatory for all registrants. No invoice-level government clearance or cryptographic stamping is imposed.

The CRA and Revenu Quebec may reassess a claimed zero-rated export within the 4-year normal reassessment period (ETA s. 298 and parallel QST Act rules), with no limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is established. If the exporter fails to substantiate export within the required time (e.g., goods not exported within 60 days or deficient documentation), the zero-rating is clawed back and the GP is assessed for unremitted GST/HST and QST at the combined 14.975% rate on the export consideration, plus daily compounding arrears interest. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285 — false statements/omissions — penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net-tax understatement; ETA s. 285.1 — gross negligence — penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; parallel penalty rules apply under the QST Act. Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and QST (ETA s. 323 and the analogous QST Act director-liability provision) where the corporation failed to exercise due diligence. In cases of deliberate fraud, the Criminal Code (s. 380 — fraud against Her Majesty) applies; CRA criminal investigation may lead to prosecution fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment, in addition to the full retrospective clawback and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely floating; there are no general exchange-control restrictions, no central-bank permission for inward capital calls, and no blanket profit-remittance licensing. Regulatory hurdles are confined to anti-money-laundering reporting, sanctions screening, and tax compliance. Cross-border dividends, interest, and management fees paid by a Quebec corporation may be subject to non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Part XIII) and, where applicable, reduced by the Canada-Quebec treaty network or the Canada-U.S. treaty in the case of U.S. investors. Capital repatriation and loan repayments are unrestricted aside from treaty and anti-avoidance provisions such as thin capitalization and general anti-avoidance rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export proceeds or foreign receipts. Exporters and fund managers may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. Nevertheless, all international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial intermediary to FINTRAC; the entity should maintain underlying export, investment, or capital-call documentation to demonstrate the commercial or investment purpose of each forex flow.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF surveillance and international electronic funds transfer reporting. Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange control ministry; exchange-control functions are absorbed into the federal banking and payments architecture.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Offshore digital EMI (electronic money institution) accounts cannot serve as primary capital-call or escrow rails for a Quebec venture-capital general partner because they are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions and do not benefit from Canadian deposit insurance or OSFI prudential oversight. Such platforms may be used for non-core disbursement support only if they are registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) and meet Quebec's virtual-currency and payment-provider licensing expectations. The principal legal constraints are the requirement for a Canadian domestic bank account for subscription escrow, the prohibition on accepting unlicensed deposit business, FINTRAC registration, sanctions compliance, and the inability of an offshore EMI to issue a Canadian clearing-routing number (CAD) or provide a SWIFT correspondent relationship that satisfies institutional limited-partner expectations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Venture capital general partner with non-resident limited partners and no prior Canadian banking relationship

Root cause: Venture capital general partner with non-resident limited partners and no prior Canadian banking relationship

Defense evidence: Certified certificate of incorporation or continuance, Quebec register extract (Registraire des entreprises), full constitutional documents, partnership agreement, limited-partner subscription agreements, fund term sheet, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth letters, investor beneficial-ownership waterfall, audited or reviewed financial statements, org chart identifying all directors and ultimate beneficial owners, sanctions/PEP rationalization, and legal opinion from Quebec counsel confirming the GP's authority to open accounts and receive capital calls.
Capital calls and subscription wires from foreign-currency jurisdictions routed through correspondent banks

Root cause: Capital calls and subscription wires from foreign-currency jurisdictions routed through correspondent banks

Defense evidence: Capital-call notices matched to LP commitments, bank statements showing upstream source account in the investor's name, SWIFT MT103 with clear payment purpose narrative, investor-side AML/KYC packs, investor-country tax identification/CRS/FATCA self-certifications, and the fund's closing statement or notarized closing index identifying each subscription.
Third-party payments, management fee rebates, carried-interest distributions, or payments from pooled fund accounts

Root cause: Third-party payments, management fee rebates, carried-interest distributions, or payments from pooled fund accounts

Defense evidence: Executed partnership agreement and GP compensation schedule, Limited Partnership Act-compliant capital account statements, fund administrator's NAV report, distribution waterfall model, board resolutions approving each disbursement, and copy of the GP's written AML policy for third-party payment screening and travel-rule compliance.
High-risk jurisdiction exposure or non-resident ultimate beneficial owner

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction exposure or non-resident ultimate beneficial owner

Defense evidence: Enhanced due diligence file including biographical information, independent source-of-wealth report, corporate registry and corporate-listing search results, tax residence certificates, criminal-record/sanctions screening, transaction rationale, economic-substance documentation, and a written approval from the bank's Compliance Officer for high-risk client acceptance.
Statutory licensing and regulatory registration as a Quebec exempt-market dealer or portfolio manager

Root cause: Statutory licensing and regulatory registration as a Quebec exempt-market dealer or portfolio manager

Defense evidence: Current certificate of registration issued by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF), NI 31-103 registration or exemption documentation, notice of exempt market dealer registration, chief compliance officer attestation, Form 33-109F6 records, and confirmation that the GP is permitted to solicit accredited investors under applicable securities legislation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital calls should be routed via SWIFT MT103 through the Quebec GP's domestic Canadian clearing account held with a Schedule I bank. Payment instructions must identify the Canadian beneficiary GP, the fund's capital-call account, and the LP investor's name and account. Although Canada does not maintain a centralized payment-purpose coding system, each MT103 narrative must state the commercial purpose clearly, e.g., 'Capital contribution to [Fund] per call notice dated [date]'. The Canadian receiving bank will perform sanctions and FINTRAC reporting for wires of CAD 10,000 or greater; the GP should pre-submit the capital-call notice, subscription agreement excerpt, and beneficial-ownership data to the relationship manager before the first wire to avoid extended KYC holds. Multi-currency inbound flows may be converted into CAD or held in USD sub-accounts; conversion is unrestricted but should be documented at the then-prevailing market rate.Outbound settlements for investment disbursements and fund expenses should be executed either through SWIFT MT103 for direct cash transfers or through commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) where a financing structure requires a bank guarantee. For VC-stage investments, standard outbound rails include: (i) domestic wire via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for CAD payments; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD/EUR/CAD wires; and (iii) Secure Payment of trade-related milestones using a commercial LC only when the underlying investment agreement contains documentary delivery conditions. All outbound wires require the GP's executed disbursement resolution, amended capital call, or investment committee approval, and must pass FINTRAC's $10,000 international EFT reporting threshold, the receiving bank's AML screening, and Quebec's securities and privacy-law constraints on disclosing LP information to the receiving bank.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (forwards traded with OSFI-regulated Canadian chartered banks)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic or restricted currencies, executed with an authorized bank or prime brokerCross-currency swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps) for matching capital-call currency and investment-currency exposurePlain-vanilla FX options (call/put options) used to hedge committed capital inflows and future transaction settlements

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Offshore digital EMI (electronic money institution) accounts cannot serve as primary capital-call or escrow rails for a Quebec venture-capital general partner because they are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions and do not benefit from Canadian deposit insurance or OSFI prudential oversight. Such platforms may be used for non-core disbursement support only if they are registered with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) and meet Quebec's virtual-currency and payment-provider licensing expectations. The principal legal constraints are the requirement for a Canadian domestic bank account for subscription escrow, the prohibition on accepting unlicensed deposit business, FINTRAC registration, sanctions compliance, and the inability of an offshore EMI to issue a Canadian clearing-routing number (CAD) or provide a SWIFT correspondent relationship that satisfies institutional limited-partner expectations.
